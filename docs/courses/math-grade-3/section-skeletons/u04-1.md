## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.OA.A.2** (days 62, 63, 64, 65, 66): Interpret whole-number quotients of whole numbers, e.g., interpret 56 ÷ 8 as the number of objects in each share when 56 objects are partitioned equally into 8 shares, or as a number of shares when 56 objects are partitioned into equal shares of 8 objects each. For example, describe a context in which a number of shares or a number of groups can be expressed as 56 ÷ 8.
- **3.OA.B.6** (days 62, 63, 64, 65, 66): Understand division as an unknown-factor problem. For example, find 32 ÷ 8 by finding the number that makes 32 when multiplied by 8.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 62, 63, 64, 65, 66): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 62, 63, 64, 65, 66): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- skill m3-division-meaning (grade 3)
- skill m2-arrays-equal-addends (grade 2, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill m3-mult-fluency (grade 3)
- skill m3-add-sub-1000 (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 62:** Invented for practice: arrange 18 paper squares into packets of three. How many packets can we make? Allow eight minutes for attempts, then pose the compelling question before retrieval.
> **Supporting questions:** What does the quotient count (day 65)? How can a product find a quotient (day 67)? Why do table patterns continue (day 70)? Why do rectangle parts preserve a product (day 71)?
> **Teaching:** Model sharing on day 63 and equation connections on day 67. Step back for story comparisons on day 65 and explanations on day 70. Day 71 connects tiled rectangle splitting to division: divide each partial area by the common side length, then add the quotients to recover the whole length. Within the existing rectangle work, day 72 independently practises split-product and split-dividend equations on ungridded rectangles. Establish zero and one cases on day 67 and the nines strategy on day 70 before day 74 practice.
>
> **Balance:** Understanding receives 480 minutes, fluency 240 and application 120. Major work accounts for one of one units, fourteen of fourteen teaching lessons, and 755 of 840 instructional minutes, 89.9 percent. Daily computation takes 70 additional-work minutes; retrieval takes fifteen more, five each on days 62, 69 and 70. Other minutes develop major work. No supporting cluster is assigned here.
> **Carried:** Restore unit 2's array, tie rows to division expressions on day 64, connect missing factors on day 67 and withdraw supplied arrays on day 69. Restore its area model with tiles and equations on day 71, remove grids on day 72 and withdraw supplied rectangles on day 74. Student drawings remain available for explanation and repair; day 76 requires concrete tiling.
