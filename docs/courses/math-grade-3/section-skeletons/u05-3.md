## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.OA.A.2** (days 87, 88): Interpret whole-number quotients of whole numbers, e.g., interpret 56 ÷ 8 as the number of objects in each share when 56 objects are partitioned equally into 8 shares, or as a number of shares when 56 objects are partitioned into equal shares of 8 objects each. For example, describe a context in which a number of shares or a number of groups can be expressed as 56 ÷ 8.
- **3.OA.B.6** (days 87, 88): Understand division as an unknown-factor problem. For example, find 32 ÷ 8 by finding the number that makes 32 when multiplied by 8.
- **3.OA.A.3** (days 87, 88, 89, 90, 91): Use multiplication and division within 100 to solve word problems in situations involving equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities, e.g., by using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem.
- **3.OA.A.4** (days 87, 88, 89, 90, 91): Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division equation relating three whole numbers. For example, determine the unknown number that makes the equation true in each of the equations 8 × ? = 48, 5 = _ ÷ 3, 6 × 6 = ?.
- **3.NBT.A.3** (days 87, 90, 91): Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90 (e.g., 9 × 80, 5 × 60) using strategies based on place value and properties of operations.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 87, 88, 89): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 87, 88, 89): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **3.OA.D.8** (days 89, 90, 91): Solve two-step word problems using the four operations. Represent these problems using equations with a letter standing for the unknown quantity. Assess the reasonableness of answers using mental computation and estimation strategies including rounding.

> **Supporting questions:** Why does splitting a factor preserve the product (day 80)? What quantity is unknown (day 83)? Which operation comes first (day 85)? How can multiplication explain a larger quotient (day 87)?
> **Teaching:** Model tens on day 78 and matching rectangle parts to partial products on day 80. Step back for strategy choices on day 81 and unknowns on day 83. Model operation order on day 85, including same-priority operations from left to right. Connect both division meanings to decomposed dividends on day 87 before day 88's practice.
>
> - **3.NBT.A.3:** Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90 (e.g., 9 × 80, 5 × 60) using strategies based on place value and properties of operations. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
>
> **Practices:** MP1 develops through day 83's Equation Positions: identify what the unknown counts and check it in the original situation. MP4 develops through day 89's Two-Step Record: draw quantities, write letter equations and judge whether the solution fits the story.
> **Fluency:** Maintain 3.OA.C.7 and 3.NBT.A.2 through one shared five-minute rotation of spaced mixed facts and written computation each teaching day, with private feedback and no time pressure. Days 81, 83 and 88 apply established strategies; fact memory remains a year-end goal.
> **Carried:** Restore unit 4's arrays and area model; day 80 ties rectangle partitions to equations, day 81 removes grids, and day 83 withdraws supplied arrays. Base-ten drawings connect tens to products on day 78, fade on day 79, return for partitioning on day 80 and division on day 87, then withdraw on day 88. Tape diagrams tie groups to equations on day 79, carry two-step quantities on day 84 and withdraw as supplied scaffolds on day 89. Student drawings remain available for explanation and repair.
