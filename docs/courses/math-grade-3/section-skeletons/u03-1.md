## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 39, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 39, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.OA.D.9** (days 40, 43): Identify arithmetic patterns (including patterns in the addition table or multiplication table), and explain them using properties of operations. For example, observe that 4 times a number is always even, and explain why 4 times a number can be decomposed into two equal addends.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 39:** In eight minutes, compare 400 + 80 + 6 with 400 + 70 + 16. Can both represent one number? Draw and explain before introducing the compelling question and retrieval.
> **Supporting questions:** Why do addition methods agree (day 43)? What changes when a hundred is decomposed (day 48)? Which multiple is nearer (day 51)? How does an equation tell a story (day 56)?
> **Teaching:** Model exchanges alongside written records on days 42 and 46. Step back for method critiques on days 43 and 48; connect equivalent records afterward. Establish distances on days 50 and 51; teach rounding and exact ties on day 52 before day 53 fluency practice.
>
> - **3.OA.C.7:** Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
>
> **Practices:** MP3 develops through day 43's comparison of expanded and compact addition and day 48's critique of subtraction across zero. MP6 develops on day 52 when students label adjacent multiples and justify distances, distinguishing exact and approximate values.
> **Fluency:** 3.NBT.A.2 receives independent written practice on days 41, 44, 45, 48 and 49. Use varied numbers, method choice and feedback without timed recall. Five-minute multiplication practice on days 39 to 57 and 59 maintains understood products for 3.OA.C.7; record memory, efficient strategy or counting privately.
> **Limits:** Computation stays within 1000. Standard algorithm mastery waits for grade 4. Fluency days practise established ideas. Unit 4 completes division strategies and both table types; year-end checks establish full fact fluency. Unit 5 completes four-operation two-step problems. Days 40, 43 and 54 to 57 provide formative evidence of these begun parts.
>
> **Balance:** Understanding receives 540 minutes, fluency 360 and application 240. This unit chiefly teaches additional work, so its major-work unit count is zero of one. Four of nineteen teaching lessons chiefly teach major work, 21.1 percent. Major work receives 295 of 1140 instructional minutes, 25.9 percent: five daily fact minutes, ten extra pattern minutes on days 40 and 43, and 45 extra problem-solving minutes on days 54 to 57. Additional computation and rounding serve major problem solving there.
> **Carried:** Tie base-ten drawings and the place value chart to addition on day 42; withdraw supplied drawings on day 44. Restore them for subtraction on day 46, fade through day 48 and withdraw on day 49. Withdraw supplied charts on day 49. Tie open-line jumps to sums on day 41, withdraw on day 44, restore for subtraction on day 45 and withdraw on day 49. Tie distances to rounding on day 50; withdraw supplied lines on day 52. Reconnect tapes to equations on day 54, label quantities on day 55 and withdraw supplied tapes on day 56. Student drawings remain available for explanation and repair.
