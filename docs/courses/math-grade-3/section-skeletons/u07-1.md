## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.MD.B.4** (days 117, 118, 119, 120, 121): Generate measurement data by measuring lengths using rulers marked with halves and fourths of an inch. Show the data by making a line plot, where the horizontal scale is marked off in appropriate units - whole numbers, halves, or quarters.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 117, 118, 119, 120, 121): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 117, 118, 119, 120, 121): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **3.NF.A.3a** (days 119): Understand two fractions as equivalent (equal) if they are the same size, or the same point on a number line.
- **3.NF.A.3b** (days 119): Recognize and generate simple equivalent fractions, e.g., 1/2 = 2/4, 4/6 = 2/3. Explain why the fractions are equivalent, e.g., by using a visual fraction model.
- **3.NF.A.3c** (days 119): Express whole numbers as fractions, and recognize fractions that are equivalent to whole numbers. Examples: Express 3 in the form 3 = 3/1; recognize that 6/1 = 6; locate 4/4 and 1 at the same point of a number line diagram.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 117:** Invented for practice: a paper strip ends between two whole-inch marks. Can whole inches tell its length precisely? Discuss for five minutes, then pose the compelling question. Adapt source lesson 1's need for fractional measurements.
> **Supporting questions:** What do ruler spaces measure (day 118)? How can a plot preserve every measurement (day 121)? Which unit fits this quantity (day 124)? How can a line show passing time (day 126)? What does each number mean (day 128)?
> **Teaching:** Model equal ruler intervals on days 117 to 118, scale readings on days 122 and 124, and moving clock hands on day 125. Step back for independent measuring on day 121 and operation choices on days 129 to 131. Ask students to check answers against their quantities.
>
> - **3.MD.B.4:** Generate measurement data by measuring lengths using rulers marked with halves and fourths of an inch. Show the data by making a line plot, where the horizontal scale is marked off in appropriate units - whole numbers, halves, or quarters. Parts, by verb: (a) Generate data by measuring lengths with half-inch and quarter-inch rulers; (b) show the data on a line plot with an appropriate whole-inch, half-inch or quarter-inch scale. Taught here on days 117 to 121.
> - **3.NF.A.3c:** Express whole numbers as fractions, and recognize fractions that are equivalent to whole numbers. Examples: Express 3 in the form 3 = 3/1; recognize that 6/1 = 6; locate 4/4 and 1 at the same point of a number line diagram. Parts, by verb: (a) Express whole numbers as fractions; (b) recognize fractions equivalent to whole numbers. Maintained from unit 6 on day 119 through whole-inch locations and fraction names.
>
> **Practices:** MP5 develops on day 121 through Fractional Length Record: choose a half-inch or quarter-inch ruler, justify its precision and align zero. MP2 develops on day 128 through Match the Measurement: match situation cards, tapes and equations, explaining each quantity and operation; revisit reasonableness on day 130.
> **Fluency:** Maintain 3.OA.C.7 and 3.NBT.A.2 through spaced mixed facts, related quotients and written sums with private feedback, without a timer. Reuse earlier individual records. Day 119 practises established ruler readings, equivalent fractions and whole-number fraction names, with no new content.
> **Balance:** Understanding takes 420 minutes, fluency 60 and application 420. By units, one of one has a major-work majority; ten of fifteen teaching lessons primarily address major work, 66.7 percent. Major time is 590 of 900 minutes, 65.6 percent. The five-minute rotation gives facts three minutes and written computation two. Days 117, 118, 120 and 121 add two fact minutes; day 119 adds seventeen fraction minutes. Days 122 to 131 give measurement fifty-two minutes and retrieval or transitions three. Supporting plots practise fraction locations and equivalence without counting all plot time as major.
> **Carried:** Keep unit 6's number line and tape diagram; retire supplied fraction strips and area models. Tie lines to ruler readings on day 117 and interval equations on day 126; withdraw supplied lines on day 127. Tie tapes to measurement equations on day 128; withdraw supplied tapes on day 131. Students may draw either. Tie each plot mark to one measurement on day 120; withdraw the plot template on day 121. Restore models for day 133 repairs.
