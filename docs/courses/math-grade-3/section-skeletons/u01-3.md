## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.OA.A.1** (days 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14): Interpret products of whole numbers, e.g., interpret 5 × 7 as the total number of objects in 5 groups of 7 objects each. For example, describe a context in which a total number of objects can be expressed as 5 × 7.
- **3.OA.A.3** (days 12, 13, 14): Use multiplication and division within 100 to solve word problems in situations involving equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities, e.g., by using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem.
- **3.OA.A.4** (days 12, 13, 14): Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division equation relating three whole numbers. For example, determine the unknown number that makes the equation true in each of the equations 8 × ? = 48, 5 = _ ÷ 3, 6 × 6 = ?.
- skill m3-equal-groups (grade 3)
- skill m2-one-step-problems (grade 2, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill m3-mult-div-problems (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions:** What does one picture represent (day 3)? How can a scale preserve counts (day 6)? What do the factors count (day 11)? Why does turning an array preserve its total (day 20)?
>
> **Teaching:** Model a new key, scale or symbol before guided practice. Step back for scale defenses on day 6 and array explanations on day 20; connect students' methods afterward. On day 11, model describing an equal-group situation for 3 times 5, then guide students to create one for 4 times 2, naming each factor's role. On day 19, students independently describe a situation for 5 times 6 and show it with an array and equation. Label these situations "invented for practice".
>
> - **3.OA.C.7:** Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: (a) fluently multiply within 100; (b) fluently divide within 100; (c) know all one-digit products from memory by year end. This unit begins multiplication strategies for factors 2, 5 and 10; unit 4 completes strategy fluency, and year-end checks establish full fact fluency.
> - **3.OA.B.5:** Apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide. Examples: If 6 × 4 = 24 is known, then 4 × 6 = 24 is also known. (Commutative property of multiplication.) 3 × 5 × 2 can be found by 3 × 5 = 15, then 15 × 2 = 30, or by 5 × 2 = 10, then 3 × 10 = 30. (Associative property of multiplication.) Knowing that 8 × 5 = 40 and 8 × 2 = 16, one can find 8 × 7 as 8 × (5 + 2) = (8 × 5) + (8 × 2) = 40 + 16 = 56. (Distributive property.) Parts, by verb: apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide. This unit begins commutativity through turned arrays; unit 4 completes the properties and their use in multiplication and division.
>
>   - bar-graph: introduced or first used here: Unit 1 (m3-scaled-graphs, scale of 2, 5 or 10)
>   - tape-diagram: reactivated here (from an earlier grade: m2-one-step-problems, m2-two-step-problems, m2-length-problems): Unit 1 day 10 equal-group pictures (retrieved); again unit 3's two-step lessons; carried by rows from unit 5 (m3-mult-div-problems, m3-two-step-problems)
>
> **Fluency:** 3.OA.C.7 begins here. Days 15 and 18 practise understood products with factors 2, 5 and 10 without new content. Days 16 to 22 reserve five minutes for mixed retrieval and feedback, except assessment day 21. Record memory, efficient strategy or counting privately; do not time recall.
>
> **Balance:** The 20 teaching days give understanding 600 minutes, fluency 120 and application 480. Major work occupies 12 teaching days, with 710 of 1,200 minutes (59.2 percent); ten minutes within those days retrieve earlier work. This is one mixed unit. On day 9, reinterpret scaled pictures as equal groups; supporting graph work prepares multiplication without entering its focus count.
>
> **Carried:** Tie bar intervals to written counts on day 5; remove supplied scale labels on day 6, keeping student graphs for comparisons. Tie tape parts to expressions on day 11 and equations on day 13; withdraw supplied tapes on day 14. Tie array rows to products on day 16; withdraw counters on day 18 and supplied arrays on day 19. Students may still draw models; day 21 requires their own array.
