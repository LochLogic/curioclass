## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.G.A.2** (days 92, 93, 94): Partition shapes into parts with equal areas. Express the area of each part as a unit fraction of the whole. For example, partition a shape into 4 parts with equal area, and describe the area of each part as 1/4 of the area of the shape.
- **3.NF.A.1** (days 92, 93, 95, 96): Understand a fraction 1/b as the quantity formed by 1 part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts; understand a fraction a/b as the quantity formed by a parts of size 1/b.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 92, 93, 94, 95, 96, 97): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 92, 93, 94, 95, 96, 97): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **3.NF.A.2a** (days 97): Represent a fraction 1/b on a number line diagram by defining the interval from 0 to 1 as the whole and partitioning it into b equal parts. Recognize that each part has size 1/b and that the endpoint of the part based at 0 locates the number 1/b on the number line.
- **3.NF.A.2b** (days 97): Represent a fraction a/b on a number line diagram by marking off a lengths 1/b from 0. Recognize that the resulting interval has size a/b and that its endpoint locates the number a/b on the number line.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 92:** Invented for practice: show equal rectangles, one divided into two equal areas, another into two unequal areas. Can every piece be called a half? Allow eight minutes, then pose the compelling question. Adapt the source lesson 1 partition discussion.
> **Supporting questions:** What makes parts equal (day 94)? How does a length become a number (day 97)? Can one point have several names (day 105)? Why does a larger denominator sometimes mean a smaller fraction (day 109)?
> **Teaching:** Model equal-area partitions on day 92, unit-fraction iteration with paper tapes (fraction strips) and a/b notation on day 95, and endpoints on day 97. Step back for reconstruction on day 102. Model repartitioning on day 104, then ask partners to defend comparisons on days 108 to 111.
>
> **Fluency:** Maintain 3.OA.C.7 and 3.NBT.A.2 in the shared five-minute rotation, with spaced mixed practice, private feedback and no timer. Day 108 supplies fresh computation evidence for items 9 and 10. Days 96, 106 and 108 practise established fraction ideas, including whole-number notation taught on day 101. Introducing the strip on day 96 means reusing day 95's paper tape and its written fractions, with no new representation.
> **Balance:** Understanding takes 780 minutes, fluency 180 and application 300. Major work occupies one of one units, eighteen of twenty-one teaching lessons and 1,055 of 1,260 teaching minutes, 83.7 percent. Days 92 to 94 use supporting shape partitions to prepare unit fractions; their minutes retain the recorded budgets.
> **Carried:** Tie area partitions to 1/b on day 93; withdraw supplied shapes on day 95, restoring them for day 112. Tie tapes (fraction strips) to a/b on day 95; practise that link on day 96 and replace tapes with number lines on day 99. Remove supplied strips on day 102, restore for equivalence and withdraw on day 111. Day 97 ties endpoints to fractions; withdraw prepartitioned lines on day 108. Students still draw lines and choose repair models.
