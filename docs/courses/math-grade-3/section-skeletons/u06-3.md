## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.NF.A.3a** (days 104, 105): Understand two fractions as equivalent (equal) if they are the same size, or the same point on a number line.
- **3.NF.A.3b** (days 104, 105): Recognize and generate simple equivalent fractions, e.g., 1/2 = 2/4, 4/6 = 2/3. Explain why the fractions are equivalent, e.g., by using a visual fraction model.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **3.NF.A.3c** (days 106): Express whole numbers as fractions, and recognize fractions that are equivalent to whole numbers. Examples: Express 3 in the form 3 = 3/1; recognize that 6/1 = 6; locate 4/4 and 1 at the same point of a number line diagram.
- **3.NF.A.3d** (days 107, 108, 109): Compare two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator by reasoning about their size. Recognize that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. Record the results of comparisons with the symbols >, =, or <, and justify the conclusions, e.g., by using a visual fraction model.
- skill m3-equivalent-fractions (grade 3)
- skill m3-mult-fluency (grade 3)
- skill m3-add-sub-1000 (grade 3)
- skill m3-compare-fractions (grade 3)

> **Supporting questions:** What makes parts equal (day 94)? How does a length become a number (day 97)? Can one point have several names (day 105)? Why does a larger denominator sometimes mean a smaller fraction (day 109)?
> **Teaching:** Model equal-area partitions on day 92, unit-fraction iteration with paper tapes (fraction strips) and a/b notation on day 95, and endpoints on day 97. Step back for reconstruction on day 102. Model repartitioning on day 104, then ask partners to defend comparisons on days 108 to 111.
>
> **Practices:** MP3 develops through day 109's Same Count task: defend a same-numerator comparison with part sizes and critique a partner's reason. MP5 develops through day 110's Unequal Wholes task: choose strips or number lines, explain the choice and test the claim. Both return in Fraction Claims.
> **Fluency:** Maintain 3.OA.C.7 and 3.NBT.A.2 in the shared five-minute rotation, with spaced mixed practice, private feedback and no timer. Day 108 supplies fresh computation evidence for items 9 and 10. Days 96, 106 and 108 practise established fraction ideas, including whole-number notation taught on day 101. Introducing the strip on day 96 means reusing day 95's paper tape and its written fractions, with no new representation.
