## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we find how many without counting one by one?"

**Hook, day 1:** In eight minutes, compare a supplied picture graph with its bar graph: drawing 6, building 4, puzzles 8, cards 2. Ask what each mark counts and what question the graphs answer. Label this case "invented for practice".

**Supporting questions:** What does one picture represent (day 3)? How can a scale preserve counts (day 6)? What do the factors count (day 11)? Why does turning an array preserve its total (day 20)?

**Closing claim:** "I can count equal groups, explain each factor, and represent and compare category counts." The day 6 graph constructions and array-story tasks supply evidence on day 21.

**Teaching:** Model a new key, scale or symbol before guided practice. Step back for scale defenses on day 6 and array explanations on day 20; connect students' methods afterward. On day 11, model describing an equal-group situation for 3 times 5, then guide students to create one for 4 times 2, naming each factor's role. On day 19, students independently describe a situation for 5 times 6 and show it with an array and equation. Label these situations "invented for practice".

<!-- skel -->
- **3.MD.B.3:** Draw a scaled picture graph and a scaled bar graph to represent a data set with several categories. Solve one- and two-step “how many more” and “how many less” problems using information presented in scaled bar graphs. For example, draw a bar graph in which each square in the bar graph might represent 5 pets. Parts, by verb: (a) draw a scaled picture graph; (b) draw a scaled bar graph; (c) solve one-step more and fewer problems; (d) solve two-step more and fewer problems. All finish here.
- **3.OA.A.1:** Interpret products of whole numbers, e.g., interpret 5 × 7 as the total number of objects in 5 groups of 7 objects each. For example, describe a context in which a total number of objects can be expressed as 5 × 7. Parts, by verb: (a) interpret a whole-number product as equal groups; (b) describe a context for a product. Both finish here.
- **3.OA.A.3:** Use multiplication and division within 100 to solve word problems in situations involving equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities, e.g., by using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: use multiplication and division within 100 to solve equal-group, array and measurement word problems, representing them with drawings and equations with an unknown. This unit begins multiplication with unknown products in equal-group and array contexts; units 4 and 5 complete division, other unknowns and measurement contexts.
- **3.OA.A.4:** Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division equation relating three whole numbers. For example, determine the unknown number that makes the equation true in each of the equations 8 × ? = 48, 5 = _ ÷ 3, 6 × 6 = ?. Parts, by verb: determine an unknown whole number in a multiplication or division equation. This unit begins unknown products; units 4 and 5 complete unknown factors and division equations.
- **3.OA.C.7:** Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: (a) fluently multiply within 100; (b) fluently divide within 100; (c) know all one-digit products from memory by year end. This unit begins multiplication strategies for factors 2, 5 and 10; unit 4 completes strategy fluency, and year-end checks establish full fact fluency.
- **3.OA.D.9:** Identify arithmetic patterns (including patterns in the addition table or multiplication table), and explain them using properties of operations. For example, observe that 4 times a number is always even, and explain why 4 times a number can be decomposed into two equal addends. Parts, by verb: (a) identify arithmetic patterns; (b) explain them using properties of operations. This unit begins patterns in products of 2; unit 3 returns to addition-table patterns and unit 4 completes property explanations.
- **3.OA.B.5:** Apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide. Examples: If 6 × 4 = 24 is known, then 4 × 6 = 24 is also known. (Commutative property of multiplication.) 3 × 5 × 2 can be found by 3 × 5 = 15, then 15 × 2 = 30, or by 5 × 2 = 10, then 3 × 10 = 30. (Associative property of multiplication.) Knowing that 8 × 5 = 40 and 8 × 2 = 16, one can find 8 × 7 as 8 × (5 + 2) = (8 × 5) + (8 × 2) = 40 + 16 = 56. (Distributive property.) Parts, by verb: apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide. This unit begins commutativity through turned arrays; unit 4 completes the properties and their use in multiplication and division.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Counts pictures or bar intervals as single objects despite a larger scale. | The unit represented by one mark is lost. Repair: expand each mark into its stated count. | from memory |
| B | Uses unequal numerical increments at equally spaced bar ticks. | Equal spatial steps do not yet mean equal numerical steps. Repair: rebuild one axis with a repeated increment. | from memory |
| C | Adds two compared counts to answer how many more. | A comparison is treated as a total. Repair: align two bars and isolate the unmatched amount. | from memory |
| D | Adds the factors to find an equal-group total. | Multiplication is treated as adding two quantities. Repair: write one repeated addend per group. | from memory |
| E | Labels the number of groups as the group size. | The factors' roles are reversed in the stated representation. Repair: point to all groups, then count within one. | from memory |
| F | Draws unequal rows as equal groups. | The equal-group condition is missing. Repair: redistribute the same count into equal rows. | from memory |
