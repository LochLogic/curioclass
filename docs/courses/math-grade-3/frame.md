# Course frame: Math Grade 3

Course id math-grade-3, grade 3, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8, one for each of the base text's first seven units, with its fourth unit (division) split in two;
  its eighth unit (a year review) becomes course-level review days.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 144 in units and 16 at course level (3 course-level checks on 4 days, 4 respond
  days, 8 year-review days). Math is daily: one lesson of about 60 minutes every school day, five a week (the base
  text's own lesson length: a warm-up of 5 to 10 minutes that mixes earlier work, the activities, a synthesis and
  our own exit question). 160 days is the full-year yardstick. The base text's year is 143 lessons (136 required)
  with 2 assessment days a unit; the units here keep 120 lesson days (two optional lessons kept, the other five cut,
  three subtraction lessons merged into two, two design lessons cut) and add 8 days of our own where the rows' load
  notes ask for them (fact strategies, measurement-quantity and two-step problems, fractions greater than 1 and the
  comparison error). Each unit's days are its lessons, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day.

- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, grade 3 (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt),
  taught in full, every lettered part placed (3.MD.C.5a and b, 3.MD.C.7a to d, 3.NF.A.2a and b, 3.NF.A.3a to d).
  The 24 progression rows are the contract; each unit names the rows it owns. There is no check set: the standard
  is used as written. The unit plan checks each unit against the Progressions documents (Common Core Standards
  Writing Team), which the rows cite by page; the "Check set adds" column names the candidate check for each unit.
  Eureka Math is a reference for sequence and models only, never copied.
- **Major work.** The major clusters (3.OA.A to D, 3.NF.A, 3.MD.A, 3.MD.C) hold 6 of 8 units (units 1, 2, 4, 5, 6,
  7; 75 percent by units) and about 109 of 160 days (68 percent by days, counting a unit's lessons whose main
  standard is major and splitting its assessment and respond days in the same share). By time it is higher, since
  every warm-up after unit 4 practises the multiplication facts. The line is 65 percent (RUBRIC-MATH-K8). The
  supporting work practises the major work on purpose: scaled graphs read with multiplication (unit 1), partitioned
  shapes as unit fractions (unit 6), line plots in halves and fourths as fractions on a number line (unit 7).
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, grade 3 (CC BY 4.0), adapted into our days with a
  credit line on each (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the store is S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\grade-3).
  Its order is kept; the source map at the end places every lesson. Only the free public pages are used: cool-downs,
  answer keys, section checkpoints and end-of-unit assessments sit behind a login and are not taken, so every exit
  question, end-of-unit assessment and course-level check is our own. The 2nd edition (CC BY-NC) is never used.
- **Fluency.** 3.OA.C.7 (multiply and divide within 100; all products of two one-digit numbers from memory by the
  end of the year) is taught as strategies in unit 4 and kept up every day after that in the warm-up: short,
  spaced, mixed practice with feedback. No countdown timer is ever shown to a student; a paper sprint, where used,
  is compared only with the student's own last score and never graded or posted (APPROACH-MATH). 3.NBT.A.2 (add and
  subtract within 1,000) is the year's other fluency, taught in unit 3 and kept up the same way.
- **Practices.** Each unit names one or two Standards for Mathematical Practice in the C3 column (C3 itself is a
  social studies framework and does not apply) and the unit plan names the task where students do them.
- **Readiness.** Each unit opens with a short readiness check on the grade 2 rows it builds on (listed under Seams);
  a student who misses one gets a ten-minute reteach with the grade 2 model before the new work.
- **Paper path.** Every manipulative has a paper form (fraction strips cut from paper, square tiles and grid paper,
  base-ten drawings, paper rulers marked in halves and fourths of an inch, a paper clock), and every practice
  generator prints. No student account.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. No unit needs a community or qualified
  reader: the contexts are classroom, food, games, measurement and building. Word problems use a range of names and
  never ask about family money, body weight or home life.
- **Reading load.** Word problems are read aloud on request; the words of a problem are taught as math words (the
  rows' words column), so reading level never blocks the math.

## For the lead

1. **Time a day (audience).** The frame sets 160 days of about 60 minutes, the base text's lesson length. Schools
   with a 45-minute block need about 20 percent more days or shorter lessons. Pick: keep 60 minutes; the scope and
   sequence marks which activity in each lesson can move to the next day.
2. **The base text's unit 8 as course-level review (structure).** Its lessons place no new standard, so they are
