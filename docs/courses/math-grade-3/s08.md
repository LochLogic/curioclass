## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What makes a shape belong to a group, and how far is it around?"

**Hook, day 134:** Invented for practice: sort a tilted square, a long rectangle, a triangle and an irregular quadrilateral. Can one shape belong to several groups? Discuss for five minutes, then pose the compelling question. Adapt source lesson 1's attribute sort.
**Supporting questions:** What do all quadrilaterals share (day 135)? Why is a square a rectangle and a rhombus (day 137)? What does the boundary measure (day 139)? Can equal boundaries enclose different areas (day 144)? Can equal areas have different boundaries (day 145)?
**Closing claim:** "I can justify shape categories, find perimeter and unknown lengths, and show that equal perimeter does not force equal area or the reverse." Category Record, Rectangle Comparisons and Boundary Problems provide evidence on day 147.
**Teaching:** Model attribute tests on days 134 to 135. On day 137, contrast square inclusion with drawing a quadrilateral outside the named groups. Trace length units on day 139. On day 140, label equal opposite rectangle sides before day 141's practice; connect unknown lengths to subtraction on day 142. Model both rectangle comparisons on day 143 before independent work on days 144 to 145. Step back for day 146's design.

- **3.G.A.1, parts by verb:** (a) understand shared attributes across categories and how they define a larger category; (b) recognize rhombuses, rectangles and squares as quadrilaterals; (c) draw quadrilaterals outside those subcategories.
- **3.OA.C.7, parts by verb:** (a) fluently multiply within 100; (b) fluently divide within 100; (c) know all products of two one-digit numbers from memory by year end.
- **3.NBT.A.2, parts by verb:** (a) fluently add within 1000; (b) fluently subtract within 1000, using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations or the addition-subtraction relationship.
- **3.MD.D.8, parts by verb:** (a) solve real world and mathematical polygon perimeter problems given side lengths; (b) find an unknown side length; (c) exhibit rectangles with equal perimeter and different areas; (d) exhibit rectangles with equal area and different perimeters.
- **3.MD.C.7a, parts by verb:** (a) find rectangle area by tiling with unit squares; (b) show that the tile count equals the side-length product.
- **3.MD.C.7b, parts by verb:** (a) multiply whole-number side lengths to find rectangle areas in real world and mathematical problems; (b) represent whole-number products as rectangular areas.
- **3.MD.C.7d, parts by verb:** (a) recognize area as additive; (b) find rectilinear areas by decomposing into non-overlapping rectangles and adding their areas; (c) apply decomposition and addition to real world problems.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Rotation changes a shape category. | Uses appearance rather than attributes. Repair: rotate one card and recount unchanged sides and corners. | from memory |
| B | A square is not a rectangle. | Treats rectangle membership as excluding equal adjacent sides. Repair: test all four square corners against the rectangle condition. | from memory |
| C | A square is not a rhombus. | Treats rhombus membership as excluding right angles. Repair: check the square's four equal sides against the rhombus condition. | from memory |
| D | Four sides alone make a rectangle. | Omits the right-angle condition. Repair: compare an irregular quadrilateral with a rectangle using a paper corner. | from memory |
| E | Multiply adjacent rectangle sides to find perimeter. | Uses area reasoning for boundary length. Repair: contrast tracing the boundary with covering the interior in squares. | from memory |
| F | Omit boundary sides from the perimeter sum. | Does not account for the complete boundary. Repair: label and trace every exterior side once before adding. | from memory |
| G | Add the known side subtotal to the total perimeter to find a missing side. | Reverses the part-whole relationship. Repair: remove known lengths from a strip representing the total. | from memory |
| H | Equal perimeter forces equal area. | Treats boundary length as determining coverage. Repair: compare counted squares in two rectangles with equal checked boundaries. | from memory |
| I | Equal area forces equal perimeter. | Treats coverage as determining boundary length. Repair: rearrange a fixed set of squares into two rectangles and trace their boundaries. | from memory |
| J | Count an interior seam in the perimeter. | Includes a segment outside the exterior boundary. Repair: trace only the exposed edge of a joined paper figure. | from memory |
