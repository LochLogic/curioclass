## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does a fraction live on the number line?"
**Hook, day 92:** Invented for practice: show equal rectangles, one divided into two equal areas, another into two unequal areas. Can every piece be called a half? Allow eight minutes, then pose the compelling question. Adapt the source lesson 1 partition discussion.
**Supporting questions:** What makes parts equal (day 94)? How does a length become a number (day 97)? Can one point have several names (day 105)? Why does a larger denominator sometimes mean a smaller fraction (day 109)?
**Closing claim:** "I can build fractions from equal parts, locate them, explain equivalent names and justify comparisons for the same whole." The Annotated Design and Fraction Claims tasks provide evidence on day 113.
**Teaching:** Model equal-area partitions on day 92, unit-fraction iteration with paper tapes (fraction strips) and a/b notation on day 95, and endpoints on day 97. Step back for reconstruction on day 102. Model repartitioning on day 104, then ask partners to defend comparisons on days 108 to 111.

- **3.G.A.2:** Partition shapes into parts with equal areas. Express the area of each part as a unit fraction of the whole. For example, partition a shape into 4 parts with equal area, and describe the area of each part as 1/4 of the area of the shape. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.1:** Understand a fraction 1/b as the quantity formed by 1 part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts; understand a fraction a/b as the quantity formed by a parts of size 1/b. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.OA.C.7:** Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NBT.A.2:** Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.2a:** Represent a fraction 1/b on a number line diagram by defining the interval from 0 to 1 as the whole and partitioning it into b equal parts. Recognize that each part has size 1/b and that the endpoint of the part based at 0 locates the number 1/b on the number line. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.2b:** Represent a fraction a/b on a number line diagram by marking off a lengths 1/b from 0. Recognize that the resulting interval has size a/b and that its endpoint locates the number a/b on the number line. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.3c:** Express whole numbers as fractions, and recognize fractions that are equivalent to whole numbers. Examples: Express 3 in the form 3 = 3/1; recognize that 6/1 = 6; locate 4/4 and 1 at the same point of a number line diagram. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.3a:** Understand two fractions as equivalent (equal) if they are the same size, or the same point on a number line. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.3b:** Recognize and generate simple equivalent fractions, e.g., 1/2 = 2/4, 4/6 = 2/3. Explain why the fractions are equivalent, e.g., by using a visual fraction model. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.NF.A.3d:** Compare two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator by reasoning about their size. Recognize that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. Record the results of comparisons with the symbols >, =, or <, and justify the conclusions, e.g., by using a visual fraction model. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
