## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Which way to add or subtract is best for these numbers?"
**Hook, day 39:** In eight minutes, compare 400 + 80 + 6 with 400 + 70 + 16. Can both represent one number? Draw and explain before introducing the compelling question and retrieval.
**Supporting questions:** Why do addition methods agree (day 43)? What changes when a hundred is decomposed (day 48)? Which multiple is nearer (day 51)? How does an equation tell a story (day 56)?
**Closing claim:** "I can choose an accurate written method and explain a rounded estimate." Day 58's computation and rounding tasks check this. Day 57's letter-equation record shows developing application.
**Teaching:** Model exchanges alongside written records on days 42 and 46. Step back for method critiques on days 43 and 48; connect equivalent records afterward. Establish distances on days 50 and 51; teach rounding and exact ties on day 52 before day 53 fluency practice.

**Item 1 look-fors:** Item 1 meets when all four calculations are accurate, the written methods preserve place value, an exchange in each operation is explained and the inverse check agrees. ## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Omits a newly composed place value unit in addition. | The written record loses value. Repair: circle ten ones or ten tens and record the new unit in its destination column. | From memory. |
| B | Subtracts the smaller digit from the larger in each column. | Treats subtraction as reversible within a column. Repair: decompose the first number and subtract each requested quantity in its given order. | Base lesson 9, activity 1 narrative. |
| C | Keeps a donor unit after decomposing it. | The renamed number has increased. Repair: cross out the donated unit and verify that the new expanded form equals the original. | From memory. |
| D | Rounds to a different place from the one requested. | Uses the wrong bounding multiples. Repair: underline the requested place and label its two adjacent multiples before locating the number. | From memory. |
| E | Always selects the lower bounding multiple when rounding. | Does not compare distances. Repair: count the distance to both endpoints and select the nearer one. | From memory. |
| F | Calls the upper multiple closer at an exact midpoint. | Does not recognize equal distances. Repair: mark both equal distances and explain why either neighbor is allowed. | From memory. |
