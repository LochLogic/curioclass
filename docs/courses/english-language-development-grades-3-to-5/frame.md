# Course frame: English Language Development, grades 3 to 5

Course `english-language-development-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `eld-3-5`), grades 3, 4 and 5, a band course of
three grade years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 from the pilot (the K to 2 frame, its two reads and its fix
report); the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time. Each year's part of this file (its units,
its end-of-unit assessments, its two course-level checks) is that grade year's frame, which meets STANDARD.md
section 2 ("one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 25: 8 in grade 3 (units 1 to 8), 9 in grade 4 (units 9 to 17), 8 in grade 5 (units 18 to 25).
  One unit per content row of the progression (grade 3 8, grade 4 9, grade 5 9, where grade 5 pairs its science
  Explain and Argue rows on one shared host, unit 19, as the progression review's pace finding 9 allows). The four
  SI rows of each year ride inside the content unit whose key language use they share. Each unit is built around
  one or two key language uses, in both modes. SI in every unit (bar item 1; STANDARD section 3): the unit's own SI
  row, or one SI use named as review in What students learn (units 2, 5, 6, 7, 10, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19, 21, 24 and
  25), each with its talk move on the host content and its look-for, chosen so that every key language use returns
  within its year.
- **Days.** 480: 160 a year for three years. Each year: 154 days in units and 6 for two course-level checks (two
  check days and one respond day each). One lesson of 30 minutes a day, five a week, 32 weeks, the protected daily
  ELD period settled in STANDARD.md section 2 and APPROACH.md "Pacing" (80 hours a year). It is not ELA time and does
  not stand in for ELA (bar item 8). The 30 minutes hold the talk set-up, handing out and collecting cards and the
  exit ticket; there is no lab or field clean-up, since ELD runs no investigation. Every unit's end-of-unit
  assessment is two days (a sitting with unseen items: an interpretive part on new content of the same kind, then a
  talk task heard by the teacher and a writing task, at each entry point), followed by one respond day inside the
  unit. No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment day. Days follow the host windows first: each
  unit runs in the weeks its host is taught, and the two checks sit in fixed weeks, so a unit's length is mostly the
  gap between hosts and checks. Within those windows the load sets the rest: an LA row (a host passage taken apart
  and an own piece built, in both modes) weighs most, a content row next, an SI row least, since it rides on the same
  unit's talk. Five units are tight for what they hold, and say what they leave to the respond day: unit 5 (16 days,
  two math hosts, before the mid-year check), unit 9 (17 days, two rows and the year's new talk routine, with its
  math host opening on day 9), unit 16 (16 days, before the year-end check), unit 17 (15 days, the year's last host
  window) and unit 24 (15 days, before the year-end check). Each keeps its Fluency days to the forms
  it owns and leaves review forms to the respond day's groups; the scope and sequence may move days within a year,
  never across a check. Years run in school order: grade 3 is band days 1 to 160, grade 4 band days 161 to 320,
  grade 5 band days 321 to 480; days below are given within the year (grade 4 day 72 is band day 232). These are
  course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places them on school days and weeks, and at five a
  week a year's course day d falls on school day d, in week d divided by 5, rounded up.
- **Standard.** English language development standards (WIDA 2020, data/standards/wida-2020.txt), ids and our own
  words only: no Language Expectation, proficiency level descriptor or other framework wording appears in any page
  (STANDARD.md section 1); public pages say "English language development standards" with the id. The band's 38
  rows own 42 ids. Grade 3 owns 20: the four SI K-3 ids (Narrate, Inform, Explain, Argue) and the 16 cluster 2-3 ids
  of its content rows (LA Narrate and Inform, MA, SC and SS Explain and Argue, each in both modes), the ids the K to 2
  band's grade 2 carried. Grade 4 owns the four SI 4-12 ids; its content rows carry the cluster 4-5 ids (owner year
  grade 5), and each unit that carries one names it in What students learn with its owner. Grade 5 owns the 18
  cluster 4-5 ids (LA Narrate, Inform and Argue, MA, SC and SS Explain and Argue, each in both modes) and carries the
  SI 4-12 ids owned in grade 4 (the owner rule of STANDARD section 2). Ids in full: every part of an owned id's
  expectations is taught in its unit, checked against the framework's own lists for the id (not the code alone) and
  said in our words (units 1, 7 and 23 name theirs); the framework's own lists were not opened for this frame, so
  that check is For the lead, item 7. Check set: bar part 10 items 1 to 14 (ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS), the PRIME 2020
  criteria in our words (docs/RUBRIC-ELD.md, a planning reference only; Curio Class claims no alignment) and the
  California criteria map rows of STANDARD section 4, which covers grades 4 to 8 and so applies to grades 4 and 5
  directly. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
- **Language through content.** Every unit names its host: the core unit of the same grade that the class studies
  that month (STANDARD section 3), cited with the host frame's own course days (science meets four days a week, so its
  course days differ from school days; its weeks are given beside them). The host column of the Units table (headed
  Host unit and key language uses) gives it. Where a row names two hosts, the unit uses the one that runs alongside it
  and retrieves the other where it was taught earlier; a host taught later in the year is not used, and the unit says
  so (unit 10: social studies grade 4 unit 4's primary sources; unit 19: science grade 5 unit 4's gravity). Some units
  run just after their host (unit 3's last nine days after social studies grade 3 unit 2, unit 6 after science grade 3 unit 4, unit 7 after math grade 3 unit 6, unit 21's last
  ten days after social studies grade 5 unit 5, unit 25's first six days after social studies grade 5 unit 9) or a
  few days ahead (units 2, 9 and 11, each with an "If later" line): these are the nearest fits that keep every row in
  host order with two checks a year. Each host is checked against the host row's standard, not its title. Content is
  the grade's own, made reachable with help; it is never swapped for an easier text. For an LA unit the host is the
  ELA unit it pairs with (STANDARD section 5): ELD takes one page or passage of the ELA text apart and names its
  language, and the reading and writing lessons stay in ELA.
- **Key language uses as the spine.** Each year builds all four uses, and each returns with a harder text or task
  (bar item 13). Grade 3: inform (units 1, 5), explain (2, 4, 5, 6), argue (3, 6, 7), narrate (2, 8). Grade 4:
  argue (9, 10, 14, 16), inform (10, 11, 15), narrate (12, 16), explain (13, 15, 17). Grade 5: narrate (18, 25),
  explain (19, 21, 22), argue (19, 23, 24, 25), inform (20, 21, 24). Inside each year every key language use returns
  in a later unit, through the unit's own row or its SI review use, and what gets harder is named in that unit:
  grade 3 narrate 2 to 8 (a recount of how the town's land changed, in order, grows to a story with dialogue and an
  ending and a school event retold by the listener), inform 1 to 5 (a sharing time on a class topic grows to the
  facts of a problem shared before it is solved), explain 2 to 4 and 5 (what a level of government does grows to a
  life cycle and a force, then the steps of a multiplication model and why each works), argue 3 to 6 and 7 (a claim
  from a source in a deliberation grows to a claim from test data, then a fraction claim with a model and a
  critique); grade 4 argue 9 to 10, 14 and 16, inform 10 to 11 and 15, narrate 12 to 16 (a scene told aloud grows to
  a recount of a group test for an absent classmate), explain 13 to 15 and 17; grade 5 narrate 18 to 25 (a novel's
  scenes grow to a recount of a research project across several days), explain 19 to 21 and 22, argue 19 to 23, 24
  and 25, inform 20 to 21 and 24. Grade 3 carries the most weight, because it is the owner year for every id the K to
  2 band's grade 2 carried.
- **Every unit, the same moves** (bar items 9 to 12; APPROACH, "Across the unit"): the key language use taught
  openly from a model taken apart, built with the teacher, then made alone; forms taught directly at the text, the
  sentence and the word (each form named with its job); structured talk every day with frames, before any writing;
  both modes (interpretive: listening, viewing and reading; expressive: speaking and writing) and all four domains in
  every unit (California 1.26); one change of audience or purpose; one moment where students name a form and its job
  and judge their own language on a self-check page they keep (California 3.5); the host-teacher note (bar item 14:
  the content used and the language taught, two lines).
- **The talk routine.** One named routine a year, the same every week, each growing from the one before: grade 3
  "tell, ask, retell" (grade 2's "tell, answer, retell", renamed because the partner now asks the question, and run by the students themselves: one tells, the partner asks one question,
  a third retells, and the roles turn), grade 4 "plan, check, report" (a group of four with named roles agrees its
  plan, checks it with "Do you mean ___?", and one member reports it to the class; SI 4-12 moves the talk from pairs
  to groups), grade 5 "report, sum up, answer" (a group reports, another group sums it up in two sentences and asks
  "why not ___?", the first group answers). A class that never had the earlier years learns the routine in its year's
  first unit (units 1, 9 and 18, days 1 to 3).
- **Entry points.** Three, by proficiency (STANDARD section 2): levels 1 and 2, 3 and 4, 5 and 6. Every entry point
  does the same task on the same content at the same thinking demand; levels change the help, never the task (bar
  item 2). Help is withdrawn on the progression's default days: for 5 and 6 the frame comes off after the first
  application day; for 3 and 4 before the end-of-unit writing or telling task; 1 and 2 keep the word bank and lose the
  cloze before the check. Grade 3 removes the K to 2 photo and picture cards for levels 3 and 4; grade 4 removes the
  grade 3 clozes named in each row for levels 3 and 4; grade 5 removes the grade 4 ones (California 4.1). A student
  moves to the next entry point when the teacher's paper sheet shows the unit's look-fors "seen" in the end-of-unit
  assessment and in one course-level check, or on the school's own progress check (bar item 7). Placement and exit
  use the school's proficiency score; Curio Class gives no placement test and keeps no scores. Every unit has notes
  for newcomers, long-term English learners (who first appear in numbers in grades 4 and 5: students in English
  programs for six years or more, fluent in social talk, who need the academic forms named openly) and English
  learners with disabilities, and a line on telling a language need from a learning need (California 3.3;
  APPROACH, "Sensitive ground": a gap in English is never read as a disability).
- **Newcomer strand.** A 20-day on-ramp beside each year, outside its 160 days (STANDARD section 2; Desk
  q-fix-eld-1): grade 3 works the SI K-3 ids and both WIDA.ELD-LA.2-3.Inform ids (Interpretive and Expressive) only, since cluster 2-3 has no MA, SC or SS
  Inform id; grades 4 and 5 work the SI 4-12 ids and both WIDA.ELD-LA.4-5.Inform ids (Interpretive and Expressive) only, for the same reason in cluster
  4-5. Its content is the school day itself (names, routines, the room, asking for help, the timetable) and the
  current host unit's pictures. A newcomer arriving at any point takes it, then joins the class's current unit at
  entry points 1 and 2. A newcomer in grades 3 to 5 who needs letters and sounds uses the ELA K to 2 foundational
  rows with a note (STANDARD section 5); ELD does not teach phonics. The strand is written with the year's scope and
  sequence and gets its own sensitivity read before use.
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day in ELD": Understanding is
  the first teaching of a form, a text's structure or a model (the form shown in the host text, named, explained);
  Fluency practices forms already taught (oral rehearsal with the frame, sentence building from cards, word work and
  cognate sorts) and drafts nothing; Application uses the forms in talk and then in writing on the content (every
  day that drafts or revises is Application); Check is an end-of-unit assessment or course-level check day; Respond
  reteaches the forms the sheet shows "not yet". Fluency is about a fifth of teaching days in grades 3 to 5, less than
  in K to 2, since more of the practice now happens inside Application talk.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every grade 3 row assumes its grade 2 row in the K to 2 band, and every grade 4 and 5
  row the row of the year before; each opens as a retrieval task inside a day of the unit that first needs it (day
  2, after day 1's hook), in the same words as sequence-write rule 2; no unit needs a readiness check. The count
  starts from the student's own grade year. A student who joins without the earlier year meets the retrieval task at
  entry points 1 and 2 with the earlier year's model on the card, and a newcomer takes the strand first. A host's
  earlier unit is retrieved the same way (named in each unit). A mixed-grade group uses the year of its youngest
  grade, and the teacher page says so (STANDARD section 2).
- **Base text.** None of its own (docs/reviews/eld-open-material-2026-11-16.md: no open ELD course exists). Texts are
  the host units' own: the ELA grades 3 to 5 anchors (Curio Class's own books and stories, public-domain federal
  pages and records, and the base text's CC BY 4.0 texts, credited through the ELA courses) and the science, social
  studies and math units' Curio Class pages, records and photographs. ELD adapts EL Education's Language Dive routine
  (EL Education K-5 Language Arts, 2017 edition, CC BY 4.0; a sentence from the text taken apart and rebuilt),
  credited once a day where used. Curio Class writes the scaffolds (word banks, cards, the frames per level, the
  model page each unit builds with the teacher) and each end-of-unit assessment's new content of the same kind,
  named per unit. The unit writer searches docs/ and data/ before writing new content, so an unseen text is never a
  page the class has met in a host unit. No source map: ELD places no base units.
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs on paper, cards and talk. Any tool a unit names (a model-sentence replay tool:
  hear and replay the day's model sentence) has a paper path beside it (the teacher reads the model aloud) and needs
  no student account. No tool records a student's voice or writing, scores pronunciation or places a student at a
  level (APPROACH, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared display is optional for the model page and the replay tool. No owned
  code needs technology, so a paper-path class meets every id in full.
- **Costs.** Printing only: word banks, cards and the four model sheets on card stock, reused across a year, about $4
  a unit for a class of 30 (a planning figure, not a quote); no supplies are bought.
- **Home languages.** A help, never a ranking (bar item 6; California 5.25): talk in the home language before
  English, cognates named (and words that share a root but look different named as such), and teacher notes on how
  Spanish does the same thing (other languages when a reader exists, APPROACH "Home languages"). No student
  translates for another, for the class or for a family. Family notes are in English and Spanish.
- **Safety.** ELD runs no investigation and no field work. Students talk from the host's own records, photographs and
  class charts; where a teacher repeats a moment from the host (a ball rolled down a ramp, a pan of sand tipped to
  show erosion), it is the host's own step under the host unit's hazards and controls (the host frame's safety sheet
  and its safety read), never a new test, and nothing is heated, tasted or shone in eyes.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`, community: multilingual learners and immigrant families), and every Spanish note and family
  note gets a `spanish` read (APPROACH, "Sensitive ground", Cory 2026-11-21). Before reading, the agent studies the
  APPROACH page's Sensitive ground rules, the federal English learner toolkit (US Department of Education, Office of
  English Language Acquisition, free online, https://www.ncela.ed.gov/educator-support/toolkits/english-learner-toolkit)
  and the National Academies 2017 report on children learning English (free to read online). Flagged units name
  more below: unit 18 (a family that loses its home and starts over in farm work), units 10 and 17 (Canada and
  Mexico; natural disasters, which some students have lived through), unit 25 (civil rights and Jim Crow), unit 21
  (a war's causes), units 3 and 14 (a local question and a canal through Haudenosaunee lands), and the own-story
  prompts of units 8, 12 and 18. The newcomer strand and all family notes get the read before use. No outside human
  reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14); the course's checks get the answer-key agent's read.

## Models carried

All four models are paper sheets the student and teacher fill; they grow by stage (the progression's "Model:" lines).
The K to 2 band begins the 2 to 3 stage in grade 2; grade 3 finishes it, and grades 4 and 5 use the 4 to 5 stage.

| Model | Where it comes from | Used here | How it grows |
|---|---|---|---|
| text map | The K to 2 band (k-si-inform, model_out), at the 2 to 3 stage from grade 2 | Units 1 and 5 (grade 3: grouped parts with headings; the facts of a problem); units 10, 11, 15 and 14's model_in (grade 4: a plan's steps; a text's structure with a defined term; facts feeding the opinion); units 20, 21 and 24 (grade 5: two texts side by side; a progress report) | 2 to 3 stage: main idea and details in grouped boxes with headings; 4 to 5 stage: subtopics with a defined term and the text's structure named, two texts side by side in grade 5 |

**Weeks.** After-core checks do not take a core check week (docs/pipeline/briefs/frame-write.md, "After-core
courses"). In the calendar the core frames give today (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md), the core course-level check weeks
are: grade 3 9, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 20, 21, 23, 24, 25, 28, 30, 31 and 32; grade 4 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 16, 19, 21, 22,
23, 27, 28, 30, 31 and 32; grade 5 10, 11, 12, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29, 30, 31 and 32. So the ELD checks sit in grade 3
weeks 19 and 27, grade 4 weeks 15 and 29, and grade 5 weeks 18 and 28: in each year, a free week near the middle that
follows a unit's end, and a late free week with one host-ordered unit left after it (grade 3's
weeks 26 and 27 are the only free weeks between core weeks 25 and 28; grade 4's 29 sits between core weeks 28 and 30;
grade 5's 28 is the last free week before core weeks 29 to 32). That is why each year's last unit (8, 17 and 25)
follows its year-end check: it is taught and assessed in full, and its end-of-unit record is passed on unscored.
The cost is stated plainly: no course-level check samples grade 3's LA Narrate ids and SI K-3 Narrate as its own row
(unit 8; SI Narrate is heard once, as unit 2's review use, in the mid-year talk task) or its MA Argue ids (unit 7,
carried unscored); grade 4's SC Argue and SS Explain rows (units 16 and 17; ids owned in grade 5, where the year's
SC and SS units are checked); grade 5's MA Argue and SS Argue ids (units 24 and 25). Each is checked by its
end-of-unit assessment, and that record and the teacher's sheet go on to the next teacher. CALENDAR.md rule 2
(one-to-one checks kept apart) is a K to 2 rule and does not bind here. `frame.py calendar` lists these six weeks beside
other after-core checks in the same weeks (For the lead, item 5); the weeks stay until the lead's calendar pass across
the after-core frames.

**How a check runs in 30 minutes.** A read or heard part (the teacher reads or plays the new text twice; students
also have it on paper) and the writing task go to the whole class at once; the talk task is heard one student or
one group at a time, about a minute a student, while the others work, over the two days. Each student is heard once
in a check. The respond day groups students by form and by entry point (at most four groups; the others rehearse in
pairs from the frame). No scores are stored; the teacher's paper sheet stays with the teacher (promise 2).
