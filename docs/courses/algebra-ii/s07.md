## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Treats a convenient sample as representative of the whole population. | Misses selection bias. Repair: compare roster coverage with a convenience list. | Base lesson 3. |
| B | Treats a sample statistic as an exact population parameter. | Loses sampling uncertainty. Repair: compare statistics from repeated samples. | Base lesson 9. |
| C | Treats random assignment as random population selection. | Confuses two randomization jobs. Repair: label separately who enters and who gets each treatment. | Base lesson 2. |
| D | Claims causation from an observational association. | Does not account for confounding. Repair: draw one plausible third-variable explanation. | From memory. |
| E | Fits a normal curve to every data shape. | Uses numerical summaries without checking shape. Repair: contrast a bell with a skewed or two-peaked distribution. | Base lesson 5. |
| F | Reads a normal curve height as a data percentage. | Confuses density with area. Repair: shade an interval and compare its area with total area one. | From memory. |
| G | Uses spread of individual responses as spread of sample statistics. | Confuses two distributions. Repair: label a response plot and a repeated-means plot beside their different spreads. | From memory. |
| H | Calls a simulation-based margin a guarantee. | Overstates repeated-sampling evidence. Repair: identify occasional simulated samples outside the typical range. | From memory. |
| I | Resamples experimental outcomes with replacement in a reassignment. | Changes the fixed experimental outcomes. Repair: shuffle all outcome cards and preserve both group sizes. | Base lesson 13. |
| J | Counts only extremes in the A minus B direction when judging how unusual a difference is. | Drops the opposite direction of treatment difference. Repair: shade both tails beyond the observed magnitude. | Base lesson 13. |
| K | Says an unsurprising result proves no treatment effect. | Confuses insufficient evidence with proof of equality. Repair: rewrite the conclusion as insufficient evidence under this design. | From memory. |
| L | Simulates a different chance model from the one being tested. | Replaces the assumption before testing it. Repair: match each simulated outcome probability with the stated model. | From memory. |
| M | Divides a survey count by only nonzero responses. | Changes the sampled denominator. Repair: include every sampled response before forming the proportion. | From memory. |
| N | Says the mean resists a large outlier. | Misreads center sensitivity. Repair: move one extreme value and recalculate mean and median. | From memory. |
| O | Records different frequencies in a plot from those in the data. | Loses observations while representing them. Repair: tally every observation before plotting. | From memory. |
