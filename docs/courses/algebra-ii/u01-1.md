# Section 1.1: From repeated steps to functions

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/algebra-ii/1/s/1.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py algebra-ii u01-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Students read and write at this grade. Evidence is written work on paper, marked against an exact worked key. Every invented context says "invented for practice".

## 1. Standards and practices


- **F-IF.A.3, this section's share.** "Recognize that sequences are functions, sometimes defined recursively, whose domain is a subset of the integers." Days 1, 2, 3 and 5 build the idea: a term depends on earlier terms (day 1), each position gives exactly one term (day 2), only integer positions are inputs (day 5), and a rule with two seeds is still a function (day 5). The scored attempt is day 13 item 1, owned by section 1.3.
- **F-BF.A.2, this section's share.** "Write arithmetic and geometric sequences both recursively and with an explicit formula". Day 2 gives the explicit geometric form, day 5 the recursive form of an arithmetic sequence. The explicit arithmetic form is first taught on day 8, so day 7 asks for the recurrence only. "Translate between the two forms" waits for days 8 and 9 (section 1.2).
- **F-LE.A.2, this section's share.** "Construct ... given a graph, a description of a relationship, or two input-output pairs (include reading these from a table)". Days 3 to 5 read tables and dot graphs and continue them. Construction from two pairs waits for day 8.
- **Limits that bite here.** Integer inputs only. Differences and ratios are compared over equal input steps only; unequal spacing waits for day 8. Fractional ratios are allowed. No series totals. Function notation is Algebra I review: it is retrieved, not taught as new.
- **Connections.** F-LE.A.1 (linear against exponential, from Algebra I), day 3: a constant difference or a constant ratio decides the type. F-IF.A.2 (function notation), the warm-ups of days 2 to 5.
- **Practices.** MP8: day 2, notice that each fold doubles the layers and use that to write one rule for any fold. MP7: day 3, look for the structure that stays constant across equal steps.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That a sequence is a function whose inputs are the integers from a stated start, and its graph is separate dots, never a line. That "recursive" and "explicit" are two ways to specify one function. Why a recurrence needs its starting value or values (error D). Why 2^0 is 1, so that a count of zero folds is one layer. That a rule using two earlier terms still gives one output for each input, so it is a function (error I). That the disk puzzle's move counts are a recurrence the class discovers, not a formula to memorise.

## 2. Where it sits


This section opens the unit and the course, with no earlier Algebra II section. It uses all three start-of-year items on day 1 (s01 field 4, each serving section 1.1): item 1 on notation, item 2 on difference against ratio, item 3 on the exponential rule. Results are recorded by item and shape day 2, whose first ten minutes repair the gaps. Its own exit results go to the record sheet and set the opening of the next day.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Items 1 to 3 belong to section 1.3 and item 4 to section 1.2 (s01 field 5). Nothing from days 1 to 5 is scored.

**Exit questions.** Five minutes at the end of each day, on paper, unaided. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example (field 4).
- **E1 (day 1).** "(a) Chain 1 has 8 beads. Each chain has 9 more beads than the chain before it (invented for practice). How many beads does chain 4 have? Say which earlier chain you used. (b) Write what someone needs to know to rebuild every chain." Answer: (a) 17, 26, 35, so chain 4 has 35; chain 3 was used. (b) The first chain has 8 beads, and each next chain has 9 more than the one before. Wrong: 44, from 8 + 4(9) (C); "each chain has 9 more than the one before" with no first chain (D).
- **E2 (day 2).** "A design (invented for practice) has 12 squares at stage 0 and 5 times as many squares at each next stage. (a) Write the rule S(n) for the number at stage n. (b) Find S(3) and say which number is the position and which is the term." Answer: S(n) = 12(5)^n; S(3) = 12(125) = 1500; position 3, term 1500. Wrong: S(3) = 27, from 12 + 5 + 5 + 5 (B); S(3) = 300, from 12(5)^2 (C).
- **E3 (day 3).** "Table P gives 90, 30, 10 for n = 0, 1, 2. The dots of graph Q are (0, 47), (1, 41) and (2, 35). Each continues with the same pattern over equal steps. For each, say whether the same amount is added or the same factor multiplies, then give the term at n = 3." Answer: P multiplies by 1/3 (30/90 = 10/30), so P(3) = 10/3. Q adds -6, so Q(3) = 29. Wrong: P(3) = -10, from subtracting the last change of 20 (B).
- **E4 (day 4).** "b(0) = 100 and each next term is 0.8 times the term before it. (a) Find b(1), b(2), b(3). (b) A classmate plotted (1, 80), (2, 64), (3, 48). Which point is wrong, and what is the right one?" Answer: b(1) = 80, b(2) = 64, b(3) = 51.2. The point (3, 48) is wrong; it should be (3, 51.2). Wrong: 100, 80, 60, 40 or accepting (3, 48), both from subtracting 20 or 16 each time (B); b(3) = 64, one step short (C).
- **E5 (day 5).** "A bus loop (invented for practice) has 70 riders aboard at stop 0, and 8 riders get off at each later stop. (a) Define v(n), the riders after stop n, with its starting value, its recurrence and its domain (the stops that make sense). (b) Find v(4). Does v(2.5) mean anything here?" Answer: (a) v(0) = 70, v(n) = v(n-1) - 8 for integers n from 1 through 8 (only 6 riders remain after stop 8). (b) v(4) = 38; no, there is no stop 2.5, so only integers are allowed. Wrong: v(n) = v(n-1) - 8 with no starting value (D); "v(2.5) = 50, so yes" (A); a domain that runs past stop 8 (no letter).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The function table, tied to notation on day 2 (inputs and outputs labeled, L(3) read as position 3 and term 8), and the discrete graph, tied to ordered pairs on day 3 (each dot is (n, term)). Both are connected to recurrences on day 5. Pairs hold the table and graph; the teacher holds the notation. Nothing is withdrawn here: repair of mismatches is day 6 and withdrawal is day 9.
- **Key moment 1, day 3: dots, not a line.** Ideal instrument: labeled axes on which students place integer-input points and see one set bend and one stay straight. Spec: fits, as plain graph paper or a light coordinate graph; the paper route loses nothing. Gaps and asks: none, so no TOOL-ASKS.md entry.
- **Key moment 2, day 4: a rule repeated.** Ideal instrument: a table that applies a rule to its own output. Spec: fits, with a calculator's table feature (light) or a hand table. Optional; the paper path gives the same evidence.
- **A room with nothing.** Scrap paper cut or torn into three sizes serves as disks. Fold a strip of scrap paper for day 2. Hand tables and graph paper do days 3 to 5. Without a calculator, use hand arithmetic; day 4's decimals are kept to one place.

## 6. Days

## Day 1


- **8 min. Hook.** Three drawn spaces and paper disks of three sizes: move one disk at a time and never place a larger disk on a smaller one. Pairs try one disk, then two, then three, and record the moves (1, 3, 7). No legend or story is told.
- **12 min. Worked example: the smaller puzzle helps.** Show three disks as "move the top two, move the big disk, move the two again", so the moves for three disks are twice the moves for two, plus one. Build the table of disks and moves, and say the rule in words: each term uses the one before it.
- **10 min. Pairs: four disks.** Predict the moves for four disks from the table before touching a disk (15), then check by moving. Each pair says which earlier row it used, then fixes one wrong prediction for five disks alone, in one minute.
- **5 min. Talk.** "What did you need before you could find the next one?" Write one sentence from the class.
- **10 min. Start-of-year check.** Items 1 to 3 of s01 field 4, unaided, after the hook. Practice only, no score.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E1.

Who holds what is carried: the pair holds the table; the teacher holds the rule in words. Tool: None. By hand: moving paper disks, the table, the exit. Evidence: E1; the check is practice and shapes day 2.

## Day 2


- **10 min. Warm-up and repair.** Five minutes of retrieval: for q(n) = 2n + 5 evaluate q(3) and say what q(3) = 11 means; for v(n) = 6(2)^n evaluate v(0) and v(2). Then five minutes repair the gaps from the day 1 check: the whole class if more than a third missed an item, with one board example for each error, otherwise a small group while the rest begin folding. Each student who missed an item does its retry alone in the first six minutes of the pairs part.
- **12 min. Worked example: folding a strip.** Fold a strip of paper in half again and again. Build a table of folds n = 0, 1, 2, 3 against layers L(n) = 1, 2, 4, 8, then write L(n) = 2^n. Read L(3) = 8: the input 3 is the position, the output 8 is the term. Then change the start: 4 squares at stage 0, doubling, gives 4, 8, 16, 32 and the rule 4(2)^n, with the frame term = start x factor^position.
- **15 min. Pairs: tables and notation.** Pairs fold their own strip four times (the fifth row is filled by doubling) and label inputs and outputs. Then they take a printed pattern with 3 squares at stage 0 that doubles each stage (invented for practice), write P(n) = 3(2)^n and read P(2) aloud as position and term.
- **8 min. Talk.** Sort four statements into "position" and "term" (for example, in P(2) = 12, which number is which). Revisit the notation error G if it appears.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E2.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the table, with inputs and outputs labeled; the teacher holds the notation. Tool: None. By hand: folding, the table, the rule, the exit. Evidence: E2.

## Day 3


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: q(3) + 1 against q(4) for q(n) = 2n + 5, and one more value of v(n) = 6(2)^n.
- **10 min. Worked example: two tables.** Table X, n = 0 to 3: 6, 10, 14, 18. Differences are 4, 4, 4; ratios 10/6, 14/10 and 18/14 differ. Table Y: 6, 12, 24, 48. Ratios are 2, 2, 2; differences 6, 12, 24 differ. Always compare over equal input steps.
- **10 min. Plot both by hand.** Place (n, term) for X and for Y on labeled axes. Dots only, no line drawn. X lies on a straight line; Y rises faster each step. Tool: coordinate graph (light), plotting, show why, 10 minutes.
- **15 min. Pairs: sort six cards.** Each card is a table or a dot graph read from a grid. Decide difference or ratio, justify with a calculation and write the next term. MP7.
- **5 min. Talk.** "How do you know it is a difference and not a ratio?"
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E3.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the table and the dot graph; the teacher holds the test (difference or ratio). Tool: coordinate graph (light), plotting, show why, 10 minutes; paper route equal. By hand: calculating differences and ratios, plotting, the exit. Evidence: E3.

## Day 4


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: evaluate a given notation value and say what it means; one value from a growth rule.
- **8 min. Worked example: one rule, repeated.** Start at 20 and add 6 each time: 20, 26, 32, 38. Start at 3 and multiply by 4: 3, 12, 48, 192. Plot the first points of each and test whether (2, 48) lies on the second graph.
- **25 min. Practice with fading prompts.** Six rules, each a start and a repeated step. The first two come with a table frame, the next two with the start and rule only, the last two with the rule given in words. The set holds a decimal ratio (start 200, times 0.9) and a negative change (start 15, subtract 4, how many terms are positive), and gives a plot scale where the numbers are large. Students build the table and plot. Tool: graphing calculator (light), table and plot check, practice with feedback, 10 minutes inside this part. No timing and no speed scores.
- **7 min. Check and fix.** Swap with a partner, check cards 3 and 6, find one wrong value or point and fix it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E4.

Who holds what is carried: the student holds the table and plotted points. Tool: graphing calculator (light), checking, practice with feedback, 10 minutes; hand table and plot are the paper path. By hand: building tables, plotting, the exit. Evidence: E4.

## Day 5


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval on function notation and an already used growth rule.
- **10 min. Worked example: starting term and recurrence.** m(1) = 4, m(n) = m(n-1) + 6 for integers n at least 2 gives 4, 10, 16, 22. Name the parts: starting value, rule, domain.
- **8 min. Why the start matters.** The same rule with m(1) = 20 gives a different sequence (error D). Two sequences are written side by side.
- **8 min. Domain.** Mark the allowed inputs on an input line. m(1.5) and m(0) are outside the domain (error A).
- **7 min. Two seeds.** w(0) = 2, w(1) = 3, w(n) = w(n-1) + w(n-2) for integers n at least 2 gives 5, 8, 13. The teacher lists the index and term pairs: each index has one term, so it is a function (error I).
- **7 min. Pairs write.** Each pair defines one sequence from a situation card (the written task, field 7).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the written definition; the teacher holds the three parts (start, rule, domain). Tool: None. By hand: definitions, the input line, the exit. Evidence: E5; the written task is judged by the look-fors in field 7.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions:** day 1, "What did you need before you could find the next one?"; day 3, "How do you know it is a difference and not a ratio?"; day 5, "Why is 2.5 not an input here?"
- **Written task, day 5:** define the sequence on your situation card with its starting value or values, its recurrence and its domain, then write one sentence saying why a different starting value would give a different sequence. A short task; this is a concept section.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) the starting value is stated with its index; (2) the recurrence uses earlier terms and states the integer domain; (3) the sentence says that the rule alone does not fix the sequence.

## 8. Practice that returns


- **Warm-up.** Five minutes on days 2 to 5, from the unit retrieval table: function notation (q(n) = 2n + 5) and the exponential rule (v(n) = 6(2)^n), with changed inputs each day. Practice only, never check evidence.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Day 4 builds accuracy with fading prompts and no timer.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure.** Day 4 starts with a table frame and fades to a rule in words, one step at a time. Students check their own points against a calculator or a partner and fix them, so accuracy comes from feedback, not speed.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sequence, term, position, rule, starting value, difference, ratio, input, output. Frames: "The term at position __ is __." "Each term is __ more than the term before." "Each term is __ times the term before." Accept a home-language word with the English beside it.
- **Access.** Raised-line or tactile graph axes and a verbal description of dots. A scribe or partner may write the table. Fold-free alternative on day 2: a partner folds or the class uses a drawn table. Large print tables.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each exit aloud; a scribe or dictated reasoning is allowed. Judge the mathematics separately from the English.
- **Reading load.** One short paragraph alone at a time; situation cards are read aloud first.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find two different rules that begin 1, 2, 4 and give the next term of each, then say what extra information would settle which one is meant.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Move three paper disks together on day 1 and write the move counts in a table; fold a strip on day 2; on day 3 plot two tables as dots and ask "difference or ratio?"; on day 5 write the starting value, the rule and the allowed inputs of one situation. Do the exit on paper without help, then go over it together.
- **One projector.** Show the disk table, the dot graphs and the worked examples on one screen, but each student predicts the next term on paper before it is revealed. Students keep their own tables.
