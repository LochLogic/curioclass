## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does it mean to take the cube root of a number, or the square root of a negative one?"

**Hook, day 46, eight minutes:** Compare 3^4 divided by 3^2, 3^4 times 3^2 and 3^4 divided by 3^6, each as one power. Reconstruct the quotient rule from factors. Ask how laws could survive new exponents, leading to the compelling question. Adapt base lesson 1.
**Supporting questions:** What must a fractional exponent mean (day 49)? Does squaring preserve every solution (day 54)? What can square to a negative number (day 58)? When are quadratic solutions non-real (day 67)?
**Closing claim:** Students extend number meanings while preserving laws and justify solution sets. Day 68 checks this through the equation audit, rewrites, arithmetic, quadratic methods and inverse construction.
**Teaching:** Day 48 uses cube-root notation only; rational exponents wait for day 49. Day 46 practices structural rewrites with variable powers and domain restrictions. ## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Treats a negative exponent as a negative result. | Confuses a reciprocal with an additive inverse. Repair: multiply the positive power by its proposed reciprocal and require product one. | From memory. |
| B | Treats a fractional exponent as multiplication by that fraction. | Does not preserve exponent laws. Repair: raise the proposed value to the denominator power and compare with the base. | From memory. |
| C | Omits one of two distinct square-equation solutions. | Confuses a principal root with a complete solution set. Repair: substitute both signed roots. | From memory. |
| D | Gives a principal square-root expression two values. | Confuses a notation convention with solving an equation. Repair: compare sqrt(25)=5 with the two solutions of x^2=25. | From memory. |
| E | Accepts an extraneous candidate created by squaring. | Assumes every transformation is reversible. Repair: substitute every candidate into the original equation. | From memory. |
| F | Accepts a candidate where the original denominator is zero. | Ignores the original domain. Repair: mark forbidden inputs before clearing denominators. | From memory. |
| G | Replaces i^2 with 1. | Loses the defining relation. Repair: replace each pair of i factors with -1 before collecting terms. | From memory. |
| H | Combines real and imaginary terms as like terms. | Ignores the distinct parts of a+bi. Repair: collect real coefficients and imaginary coefficients in separate columns. | From memory. |
| I | Completes a square on one side without balancing the other. | Changes the equation's solutions. Repair: write the added square on both sides. | From memory. |
| J | Divides only the radical term by 2a in the formula. | Misreads the fraction's scope. Repair: enclose the whole numerator before dividing. | From memory. |
| K | Writes a reciprocal instead of an inverse function. | Confuses undoing a rule with dividing one by its output. Repair: reverse an input-output arrow and test the recovered input. | From memory. |
| L | Calls a rational plus an irrational number rational. | Does not use rational closure to justify classification. Repair: assume the sum is rational and subtract the rational addend to obtain a contradiction. | From memory. |
