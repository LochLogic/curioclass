## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How long until an amount doubles, and how can we find an exponent we do not know?"
**Hook, day 73, eight minutes:** A square photograph (invented for practice) has side 15 centimetres. Repeated enlargements multiply its side by 1.1; reductions multiply it by 0.9. Predict after three changes and explain why neither adds a fixed length. Adapt base lesson 1.
**Supporting questions:** What factor belongs to half an interval (day 75)? What number names an unknown exponent (day 81)? What does continuous growth mean (day 85)? Why does an intersection solve an equation (day 90)?
**Closing claim:** Students choose and interpret equivalent forms, construct models and justify logarithmic solutions. Day 94 checks this through the signal investigation, rate rewrite, arch analysis and intersection panels.
**Teaching:** Model fractional-period rewrites on day 75 before day 76 practises them. Introduce logarithms on day 81 before days 82 to 84 practise translation and estimation. Day 85 includes ten minutes comparing compounding-table trends; day 86 distinguishes continuous rate from effective growth. Day 87 compares quadratic forms. Day 88 models exponential inequalities and doubling time before guided growth and decay practice; justify solution intervals by monotonicity. Day 90's last 15 minutes bracket an absolute value and rational intersection, retrieving day 37. On day 91 students choose quantities, state assumptions, fit decay data, test a withheld reading, revise and report limits. Independently construct and solve a fresh threshold inequality, justifying its interval by monotonicity.
