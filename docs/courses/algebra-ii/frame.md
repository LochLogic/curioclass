# Course frame: Algebra II

Course `algebra-ii` (catalogue id `algebra-2`), grade 11 (data/courses.csv lists grades 10 to 11; the accelerated path is parked, see For the lead), draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) and days.csv now set every day in order; this frame keeps the course allocations.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7 units, in the base text's own order (IM Algebra 2, units 1 to 7).
- **Days.** 160 course days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks at five lessons a week, one 50-minute lesson a day: a 5-minute opening of spaced, mixed practice on earlier work, then the base lesson (written for a class period of at least 45 minutes). Each unit's days are its base lessons, plus one modeling-prompt day, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day after it. Unit 2, the one unit over 25 days, also holds a mid-unit assessment of about 20 minutes inside a lesson after base lesson 15 (polynomials), its respond work in the openings of the next two lessons; no day moves. Days follow load: units 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 each carry two more days, named in the unit (unit 2 owns 13 codes across seven rows; unit 4 owns ten codes across four rows; units 3, 6 and 7 each carry a contribution or rebuild the rows ask for). Units 1 and 5 (11 base lessons each) are the shortest. Count by grade number: a row last taught or assessed in grade g gets retrieval in grade g+1 and readiness in grade g+2 or later; passing retrieval never resets last use. Readiness occupies the final ten minutes of the preceding respond day, except unit 1 after its day 1 hook. The scope and sequence lists every prerequisite and actual last use.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, high school (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt), is the whole standard: every code a row owns sits in one unit below, and each unit names every lettered part it teaches. The Owned standards cell contains only codes owned by the unit's rows, separated with semicolons. Contributions and revisits are named in the learning description; no tilde notation is used. Math has no check set. The C3 column names the unit's practices; the "Check set adds" column names, as candidates, the Progressions check for the unit (the high school progressions of the Common Core Standards Writing Team) and extensions. The 53 (+) codes are deferred to the electives (data/progressions/math-excluded.csv), with one exception kept from the progression: F-IF.C.7d (unit 2, see For the lead).
- **The last core course.** Algebra II ends every core math thread. The Seams section says where each ends and what Precalculus, Statistics and Calculus (electives in data/courses.csv, with no progression rows) would pick up.
- **Focus, coherence and rigor.** The scope and sequence states the single planned-minute focus calculation, using the published high school prerequisite table and excluding assessment and respond days. Base-lesson alignment counts are descriptive, not instructional-time percentages. No K to 8 percentage threshold applies. Trigonometric graphs practice earlier function transformations and feature interpretation; statistics connects displays, spread and function models. Units deliberately build understanding, fluent procedures and applications, with the actual minute allocation recorded in days.csv. A separate high school rubric remains a shared-document task, not a reason to postpone this calculation.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Algebra 2 (CC BY 4.0; S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\hs-algebra-2): 7 units, 120 lessons, placed in the Source map at the end. Credit line on adapted days, in the form docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md sets: "Adapted from Illustrative Mathematics K-12 Math, Algebra 2, unit <n>, lesson <n>, CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Changed by Curio Class." The CC BY-NC edition is not used, and no login-gated material is: cool-downs, answer keys, unit assessments and modeling-prompt sample responses are gated, so Curio Class writes its own exit tickets, unit assessments, answer keys and modeling rubrics.
- **Readiness after a gap.** The verified prerequisite ledger in the scope and sequence controls treatment. Most Algebra I rows remain last taught in grade 9, even where Geometry retrieves them. Completing the square is taught on Geometry days 110 to 111 and assessed on day 125, so it is retrieved here before complex quadratics. Geometry's current day 101 explicitly assesses piecewise and absolute-value graphs, so that row also receives retrieval. All six Geometry-owned handoffs receive retrieval. Later work taught or assessed earlier in Algebra II receives retrieval rather than another readiness check. Older integer-exponent properties receive readiness on day 45. Readiness occupies the last ten minutes of days 14, 45, 72, 95 and 136, immediately before the next unit; unit 1 uses the last ten minutes of day 1 after its hook. Results target reminders and parallel support without replacing the next hook.
- **Practices.** All eight are placed (C3 column): MP1 (unit 4), MP2 (units 2, 4), MP3 (units 3, 7), MP4 (units 1, 4, 5, 6, 7), MP5 (units 6, 7), MP6 (unit 3), MP7 (units 1, 2, 3, 5, 6), MP8 (units 1, 2, 5).
- **Fluency without a timer.** The procedures this course expects to be fluent: polynomial arithmetic (A-APR.A.1, unit 2); rewriting rational expressions (A-APR.D.6, unit 2); seeing structure to rewrite expressions (A-SSE.A.2, unit 2); transformations of graphs (F-BF.B.3, unit 5). Each is built in the units named and kept up in the daily opening for the rest of the year, beside the Algebra I skills Geometry does not use (factoring and solving quadratics, linear and exponential equations and their graphs), which the opening keeps up from the first week. No countdown timer is ever shown to a student. The list rests on memory of the high school publishers' criteria and is confirmed when docs/RUBRIC-MATH-HS.md is written.
- **Personal finance, lightly.** Unit 4's compound and continuous growth (alg2-number-e-continuous) is read on given savings and loan amounts, the course's light personal finance touch (Cory, 2026-11-14: no course of its own, Economics teaches it); it uses given amounts, never a student's own family money, and adds no day.
- **Modeling.** Each unit has one modeling-prompt day, using the base text's public modeling prompts (the sample responses are gated, so the rubric is ours).
- **Technology and the paper path.** Graphing, simulation and normal-curve areas use technology. Each has a paper path: grid paper, printed tables (a normal table included), coins, cards and dice for simulation, a basic scientific calculator. Any electronic tool is a free one that needs no student account, named in no student page.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). Unit 7's health and experiment contexts are health content: before reading them the agent studies a national public health body's plain-language pages on clinical trials and on reading study results, a health journalists' association guide on reporting studies and risk, and a disability and illness language guide, and checks that the contexts use given data, show the people in a study as people, and never ask a student to share their own health data. No other unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content that needs a named community study. The answer-key agent reads every check, assessment, modeling rubric and key, solving each item cold beside the script check (BASELINE rule 27), before the course is called finished. Word problems use a range of names and settings; the unit plan lists the people and places its problems show.
