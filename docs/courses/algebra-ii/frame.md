# Course frame: Algebra II

Course id `algebra-2`, grade 11 (data/courses.csv lists grades 10 to 11; grade 10 on the accelerated path), draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7 units, in the base text's own order (IM Algebra 2, units 1 to 7).
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks at five lessons a week, one lesson of 45 to 50 minutes a day (the base text's lessons are written for a class period of at least 45 minutes). Each unit's days are its base lessons, plus one readiness day, one modeling-prompt day and one end-of-unit assessment day. Days follow load: units 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 each carry two more days, named in the unit (unit 4 owns the most codes of the year, ten; unit 2 has seven rows; units 3, 6 and 7 each carry a contribution or rebuild the rows ask for). Units 1 and 5 (11 base lessons each) are the shortest.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, high school (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt), is the whole standard: every code a row owns sits in one unit below, and each unit names every lettered part it teaches. A tilde code in a row is one owned elsewhere that the row revisits or contributes to (named in the unit). There is no check set in math, so the "Check set adds" column names candidate extensions only. The 53 (+) codes are deferred to the electives (data/progressions/math-excluded.csv), with one exception kept from the progression: F-IF.C.7d (unit 2, see For the lead).
- **The last core course.** Algebra II ends every core math thread. The Seams section says where each ends and what Precalculus, Statistics and Calculus (electives in data/courses.csv, with no progression rows) would pick up.
- **The bar, applied by analogy.** docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md covers kindergarten to grade 8 only. This frame applies it to high school by analogy. (1) Focus: the K-8 line (at least 65 percent of days on major work) becomes the share of unit days on rows the progression tags "Widely applicable prerequisite". Algebra II: about 86 of 151 unit days (57 percent: units 1 and 2 fully, units 3 and 4 three rows of four, unit 6 one row of four; units 5 and 7 are not tagged). Across the three-course sequence: about 234 of 453 unit days (52 percent). (2) Coherence: the statistics and trigonometry units practise the function work (unit 7 fits normal curves with unit 5's transformations; unit 6 graphs with unit 5's moves), and every seam below is written at both ends. (3) Rigor: each unit names its concept work, its procedures and its applications (the modeling-prompt day). The missing high school bar is under For the lead.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Algebra 2 (CC BY 4.0; S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\hs-algebra-2): 7 units, 120 lessons, placed in the Source map at the end. Credit line on adapted days: "Based on IM K-12 Math by Illustrative Mathematics, CC BY 4.0." The CC BY-NC edition is not used, and no login-gated material is: cool-downs, answer keys, unit assessments and modeling-prompt sample responses are gated, so Curio Class writes its own exit tickets, unit assessments, answer keys and modeling rubrics.
- **Readiness after a gap.** Geometry (grade 10) carries no algebra or functions unit, so almost every Algebra I skill this course uses returns after a year away. Each opens with a readiness check in the first unit that uses it (named in the unit); a student who misses it gets a brief reminder with the earlier model, drawn from the matching Algebra 1 supports lesson.
- **Modeling.** Each unit has one modeling-prompt day, using the base text's public modeling prompts (the sample responses are gated, so the rubric is ours).
- **Technology and the paper path.** Graphing, simulation and normal-curve areas use technology. Each has a paper path: grid paper, printed tables (a normal table included), coins, cards and dice for simulation, a basic scientific calculator. Any electronic tool is a free one that needs no student account, named in no student page.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. No unit needs a community or qualified reader. Unit 7's health and experiment contexts use given data; no student shares their own health data.
