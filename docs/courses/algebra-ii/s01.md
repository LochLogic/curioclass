# Unit 1: Sequences and functions

Course `algebra-ii` (Algebra II). **Days 1 to 14** (14 course days; school days 1 to 14, weeks 1 to 3). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 5); 1.2 (days 6 to 10); 1.3 (days 11 to 14). Days by kind: understanding 6, fluency 3, application 3, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1.1: From repeated steps to functions. Students name inputs and outputs.
- Section 1.2: Representing and predicting terms. Students explain equivalent rules.
- Section 1.3: Totals and model limits. Students justify predictions with assumptions.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can a pattern that steps from term to term become a function we can predict with?"

**Hook, day 1, eight minutes:** Move graduated paper disks between three drawn spaces, one disk at a time, never placing a larger disk on a smaller one. Try two disks, then three. How can the earlier solution help with another disk? Use the base lesson's puzzle, without its legend.
**Supporting questions:** What stays constant, a difference or a ratio (day 3)? Which integers are allowed inputs (day 5)? Why does starting at zero change a formula (day 8)? When does a prediction fail (day 12)?
**Closing claim:** Students can define a sequence as a function, translate its rules, and defend a contextual prediction. Day 13 checks this through recurrence recognition, rule translations, a tile model and construction from graphs and pairs.
**Teaching:** On day 1, show how the smaller puzzle supports the next step. Model domain and initial conditions on day 5, including a two-seed recurrence. Derive direct formulas on day 8. Step back during day 7's missing-information exchange. Day 8 derives differences and ratios from terms several steps apart. Days 4, 6 and 9 practice already taught representations; they introduce no new mathematics.


**Practices:** MP7: day 3, compare differences and ratios over equal input steps. MP8: day 8, turn repeated operations into indexed formulas. MP4: day 12, choose quantities, model fictional message sharing, compute, compare with withheld observations, revise assumptions and report limits.
**Balance and focus:** Follow the frame: six understanding, three fluency and three application days, a 50, 25 and 25 percent balance by teaching-day count. Using the course's conservative exclusion of day 1, days 13 and 14, count 465 of 550 minutes, 84.55 percent, on high school prerequisites, matching the days.csv focus minutes. Each day 2 to 12 includes five qualifying retrieval minutes. Main tasks contribute 30 minutes each on days 2, 4, 5 and 6 for domain and representation reasoning; 45 each on days 3, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12 for growth comparison or contextual rule construction; and 20 on day 9 for rule interpretation. Supporting sequence work practices F-IF.A.3 and F-BF.A.1a within these tasks; translation mechanics outside these allocations do not count.

**Fluency:** No owned standard sets a fluency expectation here. Days 4, 6 and 9 build independent accuracy through mixed representations, worked examples and fading prompts, without speed scores.
**Limits:** Use integer inputs, fractional differences and ratios, and simple other recurrences. General series formulas wait until unit 2. F-LE.A.2 is begun here through sequence construction from descriptions, graphs and pairs; retain day 13 evidence for unit 4's completion on day 94. F-BF.A.1a finishes here; the parent F-BF.A.1 closes with part b in unit 5. No composition is claimed.
**Carried:** Tie function tables to notation on day 2 and discrete graphs to ordered pairs on day 3. Connect both to recurrences on day 5; repair mismatches on day 6. Withdraw supplied tables and graphs on day 9. Students construct their own afterward; keep both available for later units.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Allows noninteger inputs for a sequence indexed by counted stages. | Confuses the sequence with a continuous extension. Repair: mark the allowed integers on an input line. | From memory. |
| B | Uses addition for a constant-ratio pattern. | Confuses the two growth structures. Repair: compare consecutive differences with consecutive ratios. | From memory. |
| C | Counts transitions incorrectly from the initial index. | Miscounts changes from the starting position. Repair: mark zero changes at the given initial index, then count n minus that index transitions. | From memory. |
| D | Gives a recurrence without an initial value. | Does not define a unique sequence. Repair: generate two sequences from different starting values under the same recurrence. | From memory. |
| E | Keeps a model after contradictory new data. | Treats an assumption as a fact. Repair: compare prediction and observation before revising the assumption. | From memory. |
| F | Gives the last contribution as the accumulated total. | Confuses a term with a sum. Repair: label and add the separate contributions. | From memory. |
| G | Reads f(n) as f multiplied by n. | Misreads function notation. Repair: trace a numerical input through a rule to one output. | From memory. |
| H | Uses the output change without dividing by the input change. | Ignores unequal input spacing. Repair: divide the output change by the number of input steps. | From memory. |
| I | Says a sequence whose rule uses two earlier terms is not a function. | Treats needing earlier terms as having more than one output. Repair: compute u(2), u(3), u(4) from two seeds, then list index and term pairs to show one term for each index. | From memory. |
| J | Uses the ratio over several input steps as the ratio for one step. | Ignores unequal input spacing for ratios. Repair: write the multiplications between the two inputs, then find the one-step factor. | From memory. |
