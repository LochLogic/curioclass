# Section 1.2: Representing and predicting terms

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/algebra-ii/1/s/1.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py algebra-ii u01-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Students read and write at this grade. Evidence is written work on paper, marked against an exact worked key. Every invented context says "invented for practice".

## 1. Standards and practices


- **F-IF.A.3, this section's share.** "Recognize that sequences are functions, sometimes defined recursively, whose domain is a subset of the integers." Days 6 and 10 return to it: a recurrence, its table and its dot graph describe one function (day 6), and the domain is the positions the situation allows (day 10).
- **F-BF.A.2, this section's share.** "Write arithmetic and geometric sequences both recursively and with an explicit formula ... and translate between the two forms." Days 8 and 9 carry the explicit formula and the translation, in both directions, for both types. Day 7 uses both forms on a situation.
- **F-LE.A.2, this section's share.** "Construct ... given a graph, a description of a relationship, or two input-output pairs (include reading these from a table)." Day 8 builds a rule from two pairs that are several steps apart; days 6 and 10 construct from a graph and from a description. Item 4 of the assessment samples the sequence-construction part only.
- **F-BF.A.1a, this section's share.** "Determine an explicit expression, a recursive process, or steps for calculation from a context." Days 7 and 10 write the rule from a situation. The standard is finished here and in section 1.3.
- **Limits that bite here.** Integer inputs; a stated starting index. Differences and ratios are fractional where needed. Unequal input spacing appears from day 8. Totals wait for day 11.
- **Connections.** F-IF.A.2 (function notation, from Algebra I), the warm-ups. F-LE.A.1, day 8: the difference or ratio over several steps divided back to one step.
- **Practices.** MP8: day 8, turn repeated operations into an indexed formula and see why the formula changes when counting starts at 0. MP7: day 6, use the repeated structure of a rule to check a table and a graph against each other.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the formula changes when the first index changes (the number of steps from the start is n minus the first index). That a rule over several input steps gives a difference or ratio for all those steps together, and must be divided or rooted to get one step (a positive ratio is assumed). That a recurrence is better when the next term is the question, and an explicit rule when a far term is. Why a stack of cups has no half cups.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 4 (F-LE.A.2, sequence construction from graphs, a table and a description), on the unit plan (s01 field 5, owned here). Items 1 to 3 stay with section 1.3. Nothing from days 6 to 10 is scored.

**Exit questions.** Five minutes at the end of each day, on paper, unaided. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example (field 4).
- **E6 (day 6).** "s(0) = 4 and s(n) = 3 s(n-1) for integers n at least 1. A table printed for n = 0, 1, 2, 3 reads 4, 12, 36, 100. (a) Which entry does not match the recurrence, and what should it be? (b) At what point do you plot the corrected entry?" Answer: the entry 100 for n = 3 should be 3(36) = 108; the point is (3, 108). Wrong: the entry 36 should be 20, since the pattern adds 8 (B); the point is plotted at (4, 108) (C).
- **E7 (day 7).** "A garden club (invented for practice) plants 3 more rows each week than the week before. You may ask one question before writing a rule. (a) What do you ask? (b) The answer is that week 1 has 6 rows. Write the recurrence for week n." Answer: (a) How many rows are planted in the first week, and which week counts as week 1? (b) r(1) = 6, r(n) = r(n-1) + 3 for integers n at least 2. Wrong: r(n) = r(n-1) + 3 with no first value (D); r(1) = 6 with the rule for integers n at least 1, a domain that reaches back before week 1 (A).
- **E8 (day 8).** "A geometric sequence with a positive ratio has d(1) = 9 and d(3) = 81, with integer inputs. (a) Write d(n). (b) The same terms are numbered from 0 instead, so e(0) = 9. Write e(n)." Answer: two steps multiply by 9, so one step multiplies by 3; d(n) = 9(3)^(n-1) for integers n at least 1; e(n) = 9(3)^n for integers n at least 0. Wrong: d(n) = 9(9)^(n-1), using the two-step ratio for one step (J); d(n) = 9(3)^n, or e(n) = 9(3)^(n-1) (C).
- **E9 (day 9).** "t(n) = 150 - 6(n-1) for integers n at least 1. (a) Write t as a recurrence. (b) Which form do you use to find t(20), and what is t(20)?" Answer: (a) t(1) = 150, t(n) = t(n-1) - 6 for integers n at least 2. (b) The explicit form, since twenty steps by recurrence is long; t(20) = 150 - 6(19) = 36. Wrong: t(n) = t(n-1) - 6 with no t(1) (D); t(20) = 150 - 6(20) = 30 (C).
- **E10 (day 10).** "A stack of trays (invented for practice) is 13 cm tall with 1 tray, and each extra tray adds 4 cm. (a) Write a rule b(n) for the height of n trays. (b) Does b(4.5) mean anything? Say why." Answer: (a) b(n) = 13 + 4(n-1) = 4n + 9 for integers n at least 1. (b) No; there is no half tray, so only whole numbers from 1 are allowed. Wrong: b(n) = 13 + 4n (C); "b(4.5) = 27 cm, so it is allowed" (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The function table and discrete graph, from section 1.1: day 6 connects a recurrence to its table and graph and repairs mismatches (supplied by the teacher); on day 9 the supplied tables and graphs are withdrawn, so students build their own to answer the questions, and they stay available afterward. The teacher holds the repair routine; pairs hold the card sets; each student holds their own rule on day 9.
- **Key moment 1, day 6: three representations of one sequence.** Ideal instrument: a set of matched recurrence, table and dot graph, with one deliberately wrong. Spec: fits, as printed cards and graph paper or a light coordinate graph (the paper route loses nothing). Gaps and asks: none, so no TOOL-ASKS.md entry.
- **Key moment 2, day 10: stack heights.** Ideal instrument: real nested cups or a drawn stack, with a ruler. Spec: fits, as paper cups or a drawing. No tool is named.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand tables, graph paper and a ruler; paper cups, or scrap paper rolled into cones, for the stack.

## 6. Days

## Day 6


- **10 min. Return and repair.** The day 5 written task comes back for a revision note (3 minutes). Then the most common of errors A, D or I from the day 5 exit is repaired for the whole class (7 minutes). Retrieval of notation and the growth rule, with new inputs, is folded into the worked example.
- **8 min. Worked example: three views of one sequence.** c(0) = 30, c(n) = c(n-1) - 5 gives the table 30, 25, 20, 15 and the dots (0, 30), (1, 25), (2, 20). A second set: e(0) = 1, e(n) = 3 e(n-1) gives 1, 3, 9, 27. Show a graph drawn from (1, 30) and repair it.
- **23 min. Pairs: find the mismatch.** Six card sets, each with a recurrence, a table and a dot graph; one view in each set is wrong. Find it, say what is wrong and fix it. Tool: coordinate graph (light), drawing the dots, practice with feedback, 10 minutes. No new content is taught.
- **4 min. Talk.** Two repairs shared on the board.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E6.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the card sets; the teacher holds the repair routine. Tool: coordinate graph (light), practice with feedback, 10 minutes; hand plotting is the paper route. By hand: comparing, plotting, the exit. Evidence: E6.

## Day 7


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: one value from a notation rule and one from the growth rule.
- **8 min. Worked example: what to ask.** A bakery (invented for practice) bakes 24 loaves on day 1 and 5 more each day than the day before. Model the question "What is the first value, what changes each time and which day is first?" Write the recurrence only; the explicit rule is first taught on day 8.
- **25 min. Partner exchange.** Each partner holds half of a situation card; one half hides the starting value or the change. Each student must ask for the missing item, then both define a sequence by its recurrence. The teacher steps back and answers only a student's question to the other student.
- **7 min. Compare.** Two pairs compare rules and the questions that got them there.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7.

Who holds what is carried: each partner holds half the information; the teacher holds nothing. Tool: None. By hand: asking, writing, the exit. Evidence: E7.

## Day 8


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: a growth rule and an input to evaluate.
- **12 min. Worked example: direct formulas.** a(1) = 10, adding 4 each step: a(n) = 10 + 4(n-1) = 4n + 6 for integers n at least 1. A geometric sequence starting 80 and halving gives 80(1/2)^(n-1). MP8: why the exponent is n - 1.
- **10 min. Terms several steps apart.** p(2) = 17 and p(6) = 37 are arithmetic: 20 over 4 steps is 5 a step, p(n) = 5n + 7. q(2) = 12 and q(5) = 96 are geometric: the ratio over 3 steps is 8, so one step is 2, q(n) = 6(2)^(n-1).
- **8 min. Counting from zero.** The terms of a renumbered from 0 give b(0) = 10, b(n) = 10 + 4n. Compare the two formulas and say why they differ.
- **10 min. Pairs.** Write the direct formula from two terms several steps apart and check a third term.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E8.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the one-step difference or ratio; the teacher holds the check. Tool: None. By hand: dividing out the steps, writing formulas, the exit. Evidence: E8.

## Day 9


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: one value from each of two rules.
- **8 min. Worked example: both directions.** L(1) = 12 and L(n) = L(n-1) + 5 matches L(n) = 12 + 5(n-1) = 5n + 7. M(0) = 2 and M(n) = 4 M(n-1) matches M(n) = 2(4)^n.
- **20 min. Practice with fading prompts.** Translate eight rules in both directions: the first two with a half-written frame, the next two with the first value only, the last four unaided. Students build their own tables when needed. No timing and no speed scores.
- **12 min. Choose the form.** Which form for "which term of L is 112?" (explicit: 5n + 7 = 112, n = 21) and for "what follows M(5) = 2048?" (recurrence: 4 times 2048 = 8192)? Students sort six questions by the form that answers them fastest.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E9.

Who holds what is carried: each student holds their own table and rule; supplied tables and graphs are withdrawn. Tool: None. By hand: translating, choosing, the exit. Evidence: E9.

## Day 10


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: evaluate a rule and say what the result means.
- **8 min. Worked example: nested cups.** One cup is 3 cm tall, two nested cups are 5 cm and three are 7 cm; each extra cup adds 2 cm (invented for practice). Write h(n) = 3 + 2(n-1) = 2n + 1 for integers n at least 1 and say why there is no h(2.5).
- **15 min. Stack table.** In pairs, extend the table for 4 to 8 cups, then find the height of 12 cups with the rule and check it against the table.
- **12 min. Choose a model.** Each pair gets a new stack story, chooses a rule, states the allowed positions, and writes two or three sentences saying which positions the rule allows and what one value outside them would mean (the written task).
- **5 min. Compare.** Two pairs compare models and domains.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E10.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the model; the teacher holds the domain question. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the rule, the exit. Evidence: E10; the written task by the look-fors in field 7.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions:** day 7, "What do you need to know before you can write the rule?"; day 8, "Why does the formula change if we count from zero?"; day 9, "When would you pick the recurrence, and when the explicit rule?"
- **Written task, day 10:** in two or three sentences say which positions your rule allows and what one value outside them would mean.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) the allowed positions are stated as integers from a stated start; (2) the outside value is explained from the story, for example no half tray; (3) the rule reproduces the given heights.

## 8. Practice that returns


- **Warm-up.** Five minutes on days 6 to 10, from the unit retrieval table: function notation and the exponential rule, with new inputs each day, including a growth case and a decay case already used in Algebra I. Practice only, never check evidence.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure.** Days 6 and 9 use sets of cards and eight rules with fading prompts, and students check each answer by recomputing one term or a graph point. No clock, no speed score.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: recurrence, explicit, direct formula, domain, translate, starting index. Frames: "To find the term at position __, I use __ because __." "The rule allows __ because __." Accept a home-language word with the English beside it.
- **Access.** Tactile or verbally described graphs, enlarged cards, a scribe for formulas. In the day 7 exchange a student may ask in writing. Large-print tables.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each exit aloud; a scribe or dictated reasoning is allowed. Judge the mathematics separately from the English.
- **Reading load.** One short paragraph alone at a time; situation cards are read aloud first.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write the rule for a sequence given by terms several steps apart whose positive ratio is a fraction, then say how the rule changes if the first index is 2.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 6: match a rule, a table and dots, and find the one that does not fit. Day 7: ask the parent for the missing fact before writing. Day 8: write a formula from two terms and check a third. Day 9: change a rule into the other form. Day 10: stack real cups or books, then decide which counts make sense. Do each exit on paper alone, then go over it together.
- **One projector.** Show cards, graphs and the cup stack on one screen, but each student predicts on paper before the reveal. Students keep their own tables, and the day 7 exchange works with printed half cards.
