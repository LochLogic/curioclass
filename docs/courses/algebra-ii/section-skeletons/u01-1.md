## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **F-IF.A.3** (days 1, 2, 3, 5): Recognize that sequences are functions, sometimes defined recursively, whose domain is a subset of the integers. For example, the Fibonacci sequence is defined recursively by f(0) = f(1) = 1, f(n+1) = f(n) + f(n-1) for n ≥ 1.
- **F-BF.A.2** (days 2, 3, 4, 5): Write arithmetic and geometric sequences both recursively and with an explicit formula, use them to model situations, and translate between the two forms.★
- **F-LE.A.2** (days 3, 4, 5): Construct linear and exponential functions, including arithmetic and geometric sequences, given a graph, a description of a relationship, or two input-output pairs (include reading these from a table).
- skill alg2-sequences (grade 11)
- skill alg1-function-notation (grade 9, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill alg1-linear-vs-exponential (grade 9, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill alg1-exponential-functions (grade 9, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 1, eight minutes:** Move graduated paper disks between three drawn spaces, one disk at a time, never placing a larger disk on a smaller one. Try two disks, then three. How can the earlier solution help with another disk? Use the base lesson's puzzle, without its legend.
> **Supporting questions:** What stays constant, a difference or a ratio (day 3)? Which integers are allowed inputs (day 5)? Why does starting at zero change a formula (day 8)? When does a prediction fail (day 12)?
> **Teaching:** On day 1, show how the smaller puzzle supports the next step. Model domain and initial conditions on day 5, including a two-seed recurrence. Derive direct formulas on day 8. Step back during day 7's missing-information exchange. Day 8 derives differences and ratios from terms several steps apart. Days 4, 6 and 9 practise already taught representations; they introduce no new mathematics.
>
> - **F-IF.A.3:** Recognize that sequences are functions, sometimes defined recursively, whose domain is a subset of the integers. For example, the Fibonacci sequence is defined recursively by f(0) = f(1) = 1, f(n+1) = f(n) + f(n-1) for n ≥ 1. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
>
> - **Earlier-grade rows in the days.csv skills column (retrieval or readiness, not owned; field 4):** alg1-function-notation (grade 9); alg1-linear-vs-exponential (grade 9); alg1-exponential-functions (grade 9).
> **Practices:** MP7: day 3, compare differences and ratios over equal input steps. MP8: day 8, turn repeated operations into indexed formulas. MP4: day 12, choose quantities, model fictional message sharing, compute, compare with withheld observations, revise assumptions and report limits.
> **Balance and focus:** Follow the frame: six understanding, three fluency and three application days, a 50, 25 and 25 percent balance by teaching-day count. Using the course's conservative exclusion of day 1, days 13 and 14, count 435 of 550 minutes, 79.09 percent, on high school prerequisites. Day 4 (unlisted codes) is excluded. Each day 2 to 12 includes five qualifying retrieval minutes. Main tasks contribute 30 minutes each on days 2, 4, 5 and 6 for domain and representation reasoning; 45 each on days 3, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12 for growth comparison or contextual rule construction; and 20 on day 9 for rule interpretation. Supporting sequence work practises F-IF.A.3 and F-BF.A.1a within these tasks; translation mechanics outside these allocations do not count.
>
> **Fluency:** No owned standard sets a fluency expectation here. Days 4, 6 and 9 build independent accuracy through mixed representations, worked examples and fading prompts, without speed scores.
> **Carried:** Tie function tables to notation on day 2 and discrete graphs to ordered pairs on day 3. Connect both to recurrences on day 5; repair mismatches on day 6. Withdraw supplied tables and graphs on day 9. Students construct their own afterward; keep both available for later units.
