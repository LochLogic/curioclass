**s03-X102.** Reference: g4-u3-08 g4-u3-07, Anansi paragraphs 2 and 3 first read here; Heracles paragraph 7 is familiar. Ask: "How are the two trials alike in what the characters must do to succeed?"

Key: Both must meet conditions set for them by others. Anansi tricks the bees into a jar for Nyankupon. Heracles has to bring back the guardian without weapons and return him afterward. Accept a comparison supported by both episodes.
Wrong: "Both heroes trap bees to win stories." (W).
Shape: Compare a trial pattern using the two tellings.

**s03-X103.** Reference: g4-u3-09 g4-u3-07, Momotaro paragraph 4 first read here; Heracles paragraphs 5 and 6 are familiar. Ask: "How well does Herculean describe what Momotaro does in this passage?"

Key: Herculean suggests extraordinary strength or effort. Momotaro stops quarrels and gets his companions to cooperate. That differs from Heracles taking the weight of the sky. Accept another interpretation if the child explains what shows extraordinary effort.
Wrong: "It means telling people what to do." (X).
Shape: Apply an allusion to an action in context.

**s03-X104.** Reference: g4-u3-02 g4-u3-03, M3 complete financing paragraph, heard before print here; Count the Cost borrowing paragraph beginning Next. Ask: "How do the writers differ about paying for the canal in these two passages?"

Key: The memorial projects income from salt works, state land values or tolls to pay interest or debt. The speech asks what will cover interest before expected income arrives. Accept a comparison grounded in both passages.
Wrong: "The memorial talks about money. The speech talks about borrowing." (AD). "Tolls had already paid all the interest." (B).
Shape: Compare positions on financing using both texts.
