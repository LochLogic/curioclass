**s01-X23, day 23.** g4-u1-01, January 17: the four-step invitation and the final two-line sign, paired for the first time as an informative writing plan. Withhold this pair from models and practice.

Reader: "I have never visited Ben's display."
Ask: "Draft an introduction explaining the sign's change for this reader."

Key: Name the display and introduce the directions/invitation change for an unfamiliar reader. Example: "Ben changed his display sign. This explanation compares its directions with its invitation to choose." Unexplained he is AE; invented ticket price O. The plan remains unjudged.
Shape: One reader-oriented introduction from a new source pairing. Practice inspects missing information; own plans and parallel introductions remain unjudged.

**s01-X24, day 24.** g4-u1-01, September 7, from "On the way home I tried" through "falling where the floor was already dry". This observation-to-image passage is excluded from all current practice and models.

Ask: "Develop a paragraph explaining Ben's umbrella image. Use an event and an exact quotation."

Key, open: he hears a wet umbrella shaken beside the bakery door, then imagines "a small indoor rain". The image turns its falling water into a little rain scene. Meets: "Someone shook a wet umbrella beside the bakery door. Ben imagines 'a small indoor rain,' making its water visible." Accept correctly paired single or double quotation marks around exact words. Wrong: invented outdoor storm (O); ungrouped word list (N); unclosed quote (W); missing introducing comma where required by the chosen sentence (AG).
Shape: develop an explanation from an event and exact literary words. Avoid complete fact/quotation paragraphs in models or judged practice; inspect one source or punctuation fault. Own body paragraph stays unjudged.

**s01-X25, day 25.** g4-u1-01, February 14, the three-line visitor card beginning "The door closes". Print those lines exactly. A separate supplied draft is deliberately weak: "The poem has things. The things keep going."

Ask: "Develop this paragraph with a fitting link and precise words from the poem."

Key, open: "The door closes. However, the hall keeps talking, so its sounds seem to continue outside." Other accurate developments work; a replacement must identify the hall or its voices rather than repeat things. Wrong: a false cause about who built the door (P); unchanged vague things (Q); a new event absent from the card (O).
Shape: develop a weak paragraph through a logical link and precise literary language. Avoid paragraph-rewriting models/practice and this door-card case; inspect a link or noun in an unrelated isolated sentence. Own second body paragraph stays unjudged.

**s01-X26-1, day 26, one conclusion exit.** g4-u1-07, Frost paragraph 3, excluding it from earlier practice. Supply this short explanation: "The speaker pauses near woods. Snow falls, and the horse shakes its bells. Then the speaker remembers obligations." A separate weak close says: "Now this essay will explain garden tools."

Ask: "Write a conclusion related to this explanation."

Key: A related close connects the quiet pause with choosing whether to continue. Example: "The quiet stop draws attention to the speaker's choice about continuing." An unrelated lunch ending is R.
Shape: One composed close from the reserved Frost paragraph. Practice inspects misplaced body details, never produces a full conclusion.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview the words needed for the source, then remove meaning hints from independent work. Practice frame: "This choice fits because ___." Use movable statements and content scribing; never supply the child's decoding or spelling. A private exchange replaces public speech. Extend by challenging another detail's relevance on the same standard.

Give every child a source copy and paper response. Reading leaves and tasks follow DEFAULTS.md. Preserve source punctuation. If an existing core reading exceeds its sentence cap, return that issue to the text owner; the day writer cannot rewrite it. Directions below are plain sentences of at most 15 words. Reading is about 120 words per minute, with help recorded.

One whole poet profile is 380 to 401 words, about four reading minutes. Selected independent passages are under 120 words. The 740 to 950 figure is an unmeasured difficulty target, not length. Preview pet death in the notebook, Frost's father's death, Hughes's Black identity, Rossetti's religion/illness/death, and Dickinson's publication without permission. No personal parallel or racial role play.
