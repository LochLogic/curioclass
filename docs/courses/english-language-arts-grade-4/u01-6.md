## 3. Checks, written first


All records use one scale per component: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record actual work and support, without numeric points. Own plans/drafts/revisions remain unjudged until the named assessment collection. Judged practice finds or fixes a supplied weakness. **s01-X23.**

**s01-X23.** Poet Kez collects words heard near a stairwell. His poem repeats footsteps in short lines.
Reader: I have never read Kez's poems.
Write an introduction naming the subject and what your essay will explain.

Key: Kez turns stairwell sounds into poems. This essay explains his collected words and repeated footsteps.

Wrong answers: He does that. (AE); Kez won a biography prize. (O)
Shape: produce an introduction for a new reader from a plan. Avoid completed introductions/models; inspect one opening's missing information. Own introduction is unjudged folder work.

**s01-X24.**

**s01-X24.** Mara's note: I watched the rooftop after sunset.
Poem line: Silver threads stitch the dark.
Develop a paragraph about Mara's night image using a fact and the exact poem words.

Key: Mara watched the rooftop after sunset. Her poem states, "Silver threads stitch the dark." The sewing image helps readers imagine light against darkness. Other supported development works.

Wrong answers: Mara watched shooting stars. (O); Rooftop, sunset, silver, threads. (N); Her poem states, "Silver threads stitch the dark. (W); Her poem states "Silver threads stitch the dark." (AG)
Shape: produce a developed fact/quotation paragraph from fresh sources. Avoid complete paragraph models or judged production practice; inspect the relevance or quotation boundary of one supplied sentence. Own body paragraph stays unjudged.

**s01-X25.**

**s01-X25.** The poem repeats lantern at each ending. The things help readers expect a return.
Revise this paragraph with a fitting link and a precise poetry word.

Key: The poem repeats lantern at each ending. These repeated endings help readers expect a return. Other logical links and precise poetry nouns work.

Wrong answers: Because the poem repeats lantern, the writer was born here. (P); The things help readers expect a return. (Q)
Shape: revise a supplied paragraph for linking and precision. Avoid paragraph rewriting in practice/models; judge one bad link or vague word in isolated sentences. Own body drafting stays unjudged.

**s01-X26, two paper items.**

**s01-X26-1.** Oren's poem names a kettle's click and a spoon's ring. These kitchen sounds form a beat.
Write a conclusion that follows the explanation.

Key: Oren makes a kitchen into a source of rhythm. Other related closing statements work.

Wrong answers: Shopping is fun. (R)

**s01-X26-2.** Oren's poem names a kettle's click and a spoon's ring. These kitchen sounds form a beat.
Draft ending: Tomorrow the school bus arrives early.
Explain why the draft ending fails the topic look-for.

Key: Bus arrival does not conclude the explanation of the poem's kitchen sounds and beat.

Wrong answers: The bus sentence explains the kitchen rhythm. (O)
Shape: produce a related close and critique a supplied ending. Avoid conclusion-production models or whole-ending critique practice; inspect one misplaced body detail. Own conclusion stays unjudged.

**Unit assessment owned: D27a**, shared 40-minute revision, with retained convention slips. Student directions: "Use yesterday's feedback to revise your essay for an unfamiliar reader." "Keep your plan, feedback, earlier draft and revision together." Components: introduction/grouping/useful presentation eight minutes; development ten; links six; precise language five; conclusion six; process explanation five. These are planning allowances, not forced stops. Audience fit and convention slips add no sitting. No fresh answer model during collection. Score retained version day 36; do not score new day 27 feedback.

Cold-solved look-fors: topic named for an unfamiliar reader; two coherent aspects; source-true facts and exact quotations; logical links; accurate poetry words; related close; a meaning change responding to day 26 feedback. Williams sample in s01 is defensible: profile paragraph 1 supports medicine/writing; paragraph 2 supports experimentation; poem supports split compounds/colors. Neither proves a patient caused the poem. Meeting process sample: "I moved the line-break detail beside the other poem details after the note." Weak: "I copied it in green" (S). An invented patient cause is O; missing topic AE; unsorted aspects N; false because P; unexplained thing Q; unrelated close R. Each is a separate response, not a combined letter.
Shape: independent revision of an extended source-based essay. Avoid producing/modeling the same complete task or rehearsing assessed wording; use faulty-component inspection. Own drafting is unjudged preparation expressly retained for this process assessment.

**D27a convention components**, separate slips first collected days 24/25: a new complete sentence, one fragment and one fused sentence; opening/name/title capitals; speech and text quotations; reference-checked spelling. Unit sample keys cold-solved: "Because the lines are short, each object receives attention." "He writes poems. He treats patients." "William Carlos Williams wrote poems." **D27 RF record:** purpose/meaning, successive prose and poetry, accuracy/rate/expression, actual letter/context confirmation or correction. Five due children at two minutes each within fifteen, with matching ten-day records reused. Meeting: all printed parts decoded, question phrased meaningfully, rereading checks sense. Weak: familiar-word substitution (H), meaning-obscuring phrasing (I), unsupported interpretation (A), separately.
Shape: actual reading behavior; no rehearsed reserved performance, staged mistake or second class screen.

## 6. Days

Pairs work concurrently. Allow about 45 seconds per spoken ask per child; hear only the named sample. Everyone else hands back paper. No teacher repair group runs during teacher listening. Tools: None every day. Children hold sources and dated folders; teacher holds actual-observation records. Hand work includes source tags, practice fixes and the named evidence. A five-minute Opening repairs one earliest unmet component; others use Choice workshop. More than ten misses means whole-class repair. Prepare individual written feedback after class, not thirty conferences inside a feedback slot.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 23: "Which reader information is missing here?" Day 25: "Does this link state the relationship?" Day 26: "Which detail cannot be found in its source?" Judge one supplied weakness with feedback. Own essay is unjudged until collection; retain individual convention slips and all dated stages. A peer note never substitutes for teacher written feedback.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 23 reviews aspect grouping and reader/purpose/form; day 24 reviews both quotation uses and capitals; day 25 returns complete thoughts/reference spelling; day 26 returns evidence-discussion moves; day 27 silently retrieves familiar patterns. Days 23 to 25 apply schwa/known bases. Day 26 retrieves stress/related words modeled day 22, not first teaching during application. Week 6 words: run-on, feedback, edit, contrast, synonym, antonym, idiom, adage. Establish any planned-only terminology in section 1.5 instruction before these application days.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview the section words in taught examples. Practice frame: "This choice fits because ___." Provide enlarged movable cards, tactile line separators, signing or content scribing; record help. Independent decoding/spelling stays the child's. Speak directions without supplying an independent reading item's words. A private live exchange replaces public sharing. Extend by challenging another supplied detail on the same standard.

Paginate whole readings across leaves, each at most 350 words including labels/directions, in 14 point type. Task sheets meet that cap too. Our stems and original stimuli use sentences of at most 15 words; preserve source punctuation. Current whole poet profiles are 380 to 401 words, about four reading minutes, not 740-word readings. The 740 to 950 range is an unmeasured difficulty target. Assign one whole profile, not the entire collection. Preview pet illness/death in Ben's notebook; Frost's father's death; Hughes's Black identity; Rossetti's religion, illness/death; Dickinson's publication without permission. No personal parallels or racial role play.

## 10. Two rooms

At home use 35 minutes: five retrieve/repair, ten read together, ten inspect, five respond, five reread. The adult takes the partner role and keeps dated work without scores. Complete whole readings across sittings if needed. Cover reserved cases until response time. One projector shows taught sources and isolated features; everyone responds on paper. Hand-copied short cards and a board replace the screen. No visitor or recording is required.
