A line break can focus attention without demanding a pause; free verse need not have regular meter. This section contributes to the standards, without completing the whole reading range.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Review grade 3 u01-1's "discussion moves chart: mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer" and "reading log: date, title, my purpose, one detail." Children keep logs; teacher keeps the named-child record. Use facilitator, evidence-finder and questioner as the three roles. Everyone also answers and reflects; these are moves, not extra roles. In paired rehearsals, children swap questioner and evidence-finder; the teacher models the facilitator cue. Group discussion assigns all three roles, with repeated roles where needed. A role does not require another full speech.

Review grade 3 u01-1's "sound and spelling wall: the week's irregular words marked by the class." Familiar syllable patterns are review. Day 3 reviews grade 3 u01-2's "story map with a message box: who, want, events joined by arrows, message." Attach dated references; keep through day 13. This is the frame's story-map/narrative-arc work in familiar words.

Ideal instruments: complete reading copy, separate short task sheet, paper log and actual oral record. Print fits; no key tool or unbuilt spec. Without print, share taught readings orally and copy the independent stimulus separately. Decoding evidence still needs accessible printed or tactile words.

Word-card meanings for new vocabulary: poet, a writer of poems; verse, writing arranged in deliberate lines; rhythm, the pattern of sounds and stresses heard while reading; image, words that help a reader imagine a sensory detail. Infer means work out an unstated idea using text details. Summary means a brief account of important events without personal opinion. Review detail, evidence, poem, setting and event in their written ledger senses. Review the written five-type chart from grade 1 u04-8 day 154, not its planned day 150 predecessor.

## 6. Days

Pairs work concurrently. A teacher hears only the named children; everyone else returns dated paper. A two-child exchange takes two minutes, not two minutes per child. Six pairs take twelve minutes. This allows about 45 seconds each plus a short follow-up. A five-minute repair group runs outside teacher listening. Return all inspected work with the next named exit hand-back; unjudged drafts stay in the folder. Role feedback from days 1, 4, 8 and 10 returns in day 12 Choice workshop; day 12 targets return in day 13 Launch.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview poem, poet, detail, evidence, line, verse, image and rhythm in taught reading. Frame: "These words fit this claim because ___." Sketching can precede an unjudged notice. Extend by defending another supported reading of the same taught line.

Spoken directions, large print, tactile line separators, signing and a scribe provide access without supplying an answer. Record help. Independent decoding and spelling remain the child's. Caption live presentations and supply their printed directions. Public speaking is optional; offer a private live exchange. Never infer oral skill from paper.

Use complete on-file core readings as separately identified reading copies. Keep the task sheet, including its reserved exit passage, within DEFAULTS.md. A typical first-read novel part is about 1,050 words, nine minutes at 120 words per minute. Shared reading with tracking fits the named reading part; independent practice uses at most 120 words at once. Do not shorten the whole text to fit the task sheet. Explain this distinction on the teacher page. Preserve source punctuation; our directions and new directions use sentences of 15 words or fewer. No stripped punctuation.

## 10. Two rooms

At home use the 30 to 40 minute default: five minutes retrieve/repair, twelve read together, eight inspect a choice, five respond and five reread. The adult is the partner and keeps dated work without scores. Read assigned parts whole, sharing the load. Cover reserved stimuli until response time. One projector displays taught texts and individual feature demonstrations only; everyone has paper for the response. A board and hand-copied short cards replace the screen. No visitor or device is required.
