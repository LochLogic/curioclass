## 1. Standards and practices


W.4.4, "Produce clear and coherent writing," starts an image for an absent reader on day 1. W.4.10, "Write routinely," starts dated brief work, not proof of an extended process. RL.4.10 and RI.4.10, "read and comprehend," launch literary/informational choices on days 1 and 2, not year-end mastery. RL.4.1, "Refer to details and examples," and W.4.9a, "Apply grade 4 Reading standards to literature," connect words and interpretations on days 2 and 3. RL.4.2, "Determine a theme" and "summarize," and RL.4.3, "Describe in depth," begin with thoughts, actions, setting and results on day 3. RL.4.4, "Determine the meaning," RL.4.5, "Explain major differences," and L.4.5a, "Explain the meaning," begin contextual images, verse and rhythm on day 4; complete forms come in section 1.2 and allusions later.

RF.4.3a, "Use combined knowledge," combines letters, syllables and familiar parts before context. L.4.2d, "Spell grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references as needed," uses those patterns. RF.4.4a to c, "Read," "Read" and "Use context," records purpose, meaning, successive readings, accuracy, phrasing and confirmation. SL.4.1a and b, "Come to discussions prepared" and "Follow agreed-upon rules," retrieves the five discussion moves and adds roles on day 1. Teach every move needed by application day 10 now: linked question, response and closing reconsideration included.

Teacher explanation: a speaker need not be the author; details limit an inference. Review message and character actions before introducing inference and summary as terms. Review grade 3 u03-4's "theme: a message that comes back in more than one book"; now extend that familiar message language to a supported theme in one text. A line break can focus attention without demanding a pause; free verse need not have regular meter. This section contributes to the standards, without completing the whole reading range.

## 3. Checks, written first


All new exit stimuli below are Curio Class originals, invented for assessment. The text owner must register each short companion and place it on file before day writing. They are separate from the core novel. Keep them off models, practice cards and home sheets. Use one scale throughout these records: shown, shown with help, not yet seen, with separate component columns. Blank evidence means not yet seen, not an inferred error. Paper responses reach all 30 children. Multiple answers are allowed only where stated.

Shape notes supersede the immutable practice column wherever it duplicates an exit or check. Judged practice locates, matches, selects between supplied interpretations, or fixes a weak response. No model demonstrates a complete check response. Own drafts with an assessed form remain unjudged folder work. A returned assessment is corrected individually, never displayed as a practice model. First collection is diagnostic; retain dated written feedback and a later independent revision.

Ownership: no unit assessment sitting here. Reading records contribute to days 35 and 36; word-analysis records lead to later probes. Never practice the day 29 probe words or any A-item vocabulary.

**s01-X01, day 1, W.4.4.** Student copy: A paper kite is caught in a fence. Describe it for someone who cannot see it.
Answer: Many answers work: a coherent specific image suited to a reader who cannot see the scene. Samples: The torn kite slaps the fence. Its red tail catches on a nail. | A kite is there. | The thing is nice.
Shape: independently compose a specific image from a situation. Judged practice only selects, locates or repairs an isolated weakness on another taught case. Own parallel drafts remain unjudged.

**s01-X02, day 2, RL.4.1.** Student copy: I rubbed the word smooth off my stone poem. Under my thumb, the stone had sharp ridges. I wrote rough instead. /  / Explain the boy's thought using a detail.
Answer: Changing smooth to rough suggests that he wants the words to match the texture. Accept other interpretations grounded in the printed detail. Samples: He changed smooth to rough after feeling sharp ridges. He wants his description to match. | He feels ridges, but I cannot tell what he thinks. | He hates writing because stones are hard.
Shape: produce a detail-to-thought explanation. Judged practice only selects, locates or repairs an isolated weakness on another taught case. Own parallel drafts remain unjudged.

**s01-X03, day 3, RL.4.1.** Student copy: My paint runs out. / I drop my brush. / My picture waits / beside the sink. / A scrap of cloth / catches my eye. / I glue its stripes / where paint should go. /  / Explain the painter's first feeling using a detail.
Answer: Frustration or disappointment fits dropping the brush when the paint runs out. Accept another feeling supported by the poem. Samples: The painter feels frustrated and drops the brush when the paint runs out. | Happy because everyone loves painting.
Shape: infer a feeling from a detail; separately summarize events. Judged practice only selects, locates or repairs an isolated weakness on another taught case. Own parallel drafts remain unjudged.

**s01-X03b, day 3, RL.4.2.** Student copy: Use the painter poem. Summarize its important events.
Answer: The paint runs out. The painter drops the brush, notices striped cloth, and glues it onto the picture instead. Samples: The paint runs out. The painter finishes part of the picture with striped cloth. | I like painting.
Shape: infer a feeling from a detail; separately summarize events. Judged practice only selects, locates or repairs an isolated weakness on another taught case. Own parallel drafts remain unjudged.

**s01-X04, day 4, RL.4.5.** Student copy: Tap, tap, tap, / the loose lid sings. / Drip, drip, drip, / the gutter rings. /  / Explain how the repeated sounds create this poem's image and rhythm.
Answer: Tap and drip suggest repeated striking sounds, such as rain on the lid and gutter. Their repeated three-part pattern creates a beat. Sings and rings suggest sounds, not literal voices. Accept other supported sound interpretations. Samples: I imagine little strikes on metal. The three taps and three drips make a repeating beat. | Tap makes a sound, but I cannot explain the pattern. | The lid has a singing mouth.
Shape: explain the sound-image/rhythm link; separate paper Exit box explains a line ending. Judged practice only selects, locates or repairs an isolated weakness on another taught case. Own parallel drafts remain unjudged.

**s01-P4-X1, day 4, RL.4.5.** Student copy: Use the exit poem. Explain what one line ending helps you hear.
Answer: A supported account of one line ending: for example, a pause after the three taps groups the sounds before the lid is named. Other supported phrasing effects count. Samples: I wait after the three taps before finding out what makes them. | The first line ends after tap, but I do not explain its effect. | Every line must be read with the same long pause.
Shape: explain the sound-image/rhythm link; separate paper Exit box explains a line ending. Judged practice only selects, locates or repairs an isolated weakness on another taught case. Own parallel drafts remain unjudged.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview poem, poet, detail, evidence, line, verse, image and rhythm in taught reading. Frame: "These words fit this claim because ___." Sketching can precede an unjudged notice. Extend by defending another supported reading of the same taught line.

Spoken directions, large print, tactile line separators, signing and a scribe provide access without supplying an answer. Record help. Independent decoding and spelling remain the child's. Caption live presentations and supply their printed directions. Public speaking is optional; offer a private live exchange. Never infer oral skill from paper.

Use complete on-file core readings as separately identified reading copies. Keep the task sheet, including its original exit stimulus, within DEFAULTS.md. A typical first-read novel part is about 1,050 words, nine minutes at 120 words per minute. Shared reading with tracking fits the named reading part; independent practice uses at most 120 words at once. Do not shorten the whole text to fit the task sheet. Explain this distinction on the teacher page. Preserve source punctuation; our directions and original stimuli use sentences of 15 words or fewer. No stripped punctuation.

## 10. Two rooms

At home use the 30 to 40 minute default: five minutes retrieve/repair, twelve read together, eight inspect a choice, five respond and five reread. The adult is the partner and keeps dated work without scores. Read assigned parts whole, sharing the load. Cover reserved stimuli until response time. One projector displays taught texts and individual feature demonstrations only; everyone has paper for the response. A board and hand-copied short cards replace the screen. No visitor or device is required.
