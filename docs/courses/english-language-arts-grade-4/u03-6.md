## 3. Checks, written first


**Administer:** D108a RI.4.8 (12 minutes), b SL.4.3 (10), c W.4.9b (13), unit field 5. Also teach toward D116c and D119f, collected in 3.8.

**Writing-transfer exits.** The following supplied draft faults are assessment tasks based on registered texts, not new core readings or cold comprehension checks. Their new writing situations differ from the worked components. The supplied draft is task copy, not a separate text_id. Since these answers require source details, use needs_text yes and the named registered source id. Own matching-form composition stays unjudged. **s03-X105.** Reference: g4-u3-03, Count the Cost main argument, opening and its three reason paragraphs. Ask: "The opinion is to wait. The plan lists two reasons, delay and wait. Replace them with two supported reasons."

Key: Accept two different reasons that support delay, with a textual detail for each. For example, the estimate may miss route obstacles, and interest could be due before projected income arrives. The unfinished crossing can support a different reason about how much of a route has to work. Meets: "Costs may change because this route has its own obstacles. Payments may come due before canal income arrives." Wrong: Reason one is wait. Reason two is do not start yet. (J); Wait because canals are dull and stories about boats are boring. (A).

Shape: One repair of repeated reason labels into supported groups. Practice locates one detail without completing a two-group plan.

**s03-X106.** Reference: g4-u3-02 g4-u3-03, M2 page 415 and Count the Cost first reason beginning First. Use M2 for both responses. The opposing speech supplies context. Ask: "A note says the estimate proves there will be no unexpected costs. What reason do the memorial writers actually give for using the estimate?"

Key: The writers are guided by experience with comparable projects. This provides a basis for estimating costs without guaranteeing them.
Wrong: "Their reason is five million dollars." (AM).
Shape: Identify the reasoning behind the estimate.

**s03-X106-detail.** Ask: "How does the commissioners' estimate support the memorial's argument?"

Key: The commissioners estimated that the work would cost no more than five million dollars. That figure gave lawmakers a basis for judging affordability before the work began.
Wrong: "Five million dollars was the full amount paid." (B).
Shape: Explain how a reported estimate supports a plan.

**s03-X107.** Reference: g4-u3-03, Count the Cost opening and final paragraph before P99. Ask: "The draft begins, ‘This shows it.’ Write a clear introduction for a reader new to the issue."

Key: The introduction identifies the proposed canal and the decision whether the state should commit now or wait for clearer answers. It states the position taken. Accept another supported position if a new reader can identify the issue and opinion. Meets: "Should the state commit to building the canal now? I would wait for clearer construction reports." Wrong: This shows it. We should wait. (AN); People have questions about the canal. (Z).

Shape: One brief opinion opening for an unfamiliar reader. Collect located evidence separately. Keep matching own drafts unjudged.

**s03-X109.** Reference: g4-u3-03, Count the Cost main argument, especially the incomplete-crossing paragraph beginning In addition. Keep the source-based reason and ending on separate lines. The punctuation line supplies its own sentences. Ask: "The draft says every finished section settles every route question. Revise this reason using the speech."

Key: A working section can show planners how part of the route works, but incomplete sections can leave route questions unanswered.
Wrong: "Every finished section settles every route question." (S).
Shape: Revise one reason using the speech.

**s03-X109-ending.** Ask: "Write an ending that follows the speech's reasoning about a partly finished route."

Key: For example, The state should wait for clearer reports on the full route before committing to the whole project.
Wrong: "The canal has many kinds of boats." (AC).
Shape: Write a conclusion that follows source reasoning.

**s03-X109-comma.** Ask: "Some crews have finished their work. Other crews still have work to do. Join these sentences into one compound sentence."

Key: Some crews have finished their work, but other crews still have work to do. Accept a fitting coordinating conjunction with a comma between the two complete clauses.
Wrong: "Some crews have finished their work, but still have work to do." (T).
Shape: Join the supplied independent clauses with a comma and coordinating conjunction.

Separate evidence: day 105 Opinion retrieval uses its final minute for s03-R105-opinion. Ask: "Name the writer’s opinion." Key: delay full commitment until costs and payment are clearer; topic without position is Z. Shape: a single position label, separate from the reason-group repair.

Day 107 Own evidence folder uses its final three minutes for s03-R107-notes. Ask: "Record two reasons for your position. Label where each reason comes from." Key: two distinct, grounded reasons and a traceable location for each. Unsupported reasons are A; missing source is K. Shape: located source notes, separate from an opening. Collect before X107. Return each with its day’s exit in the next named repair slot.
