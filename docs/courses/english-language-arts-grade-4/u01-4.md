**s01-X14, day 14, RL.4.1.** Student copy: I found three chalk circles where yesterday's hopscotch had been. / A shoe crossed them. I began a poem about games that leave traces. /  / Write an evidence note explaining what sparked this boy's poem.

**D17a, 35 minutes, canonical N9/N13 only.** Ask: "Explain what inspires Ben's writing for someone unfamiliar with his notebook." Add: "Write one paragraph using details from both entries." ## 9. Supports and extension

Preview spark, inspiration, detail, organize and conclusion in taught examples. Judgment-practice frame: "This sentence belongs here because ___." Offer enlarged movable statements and content scribing while retaining independent choice. Extend by challenging a different detail's relevance in the same source.

Spoken directions, large print, tactile line separators, signing and a scribe provide access without supplying an answer. Record help. Independent decoding and spelling remain the child's. Caption live presentations and supply their printed directions. Public speaking is optional; offer a private live exchange. Never infer oral skill from paper.

Use complete on-file core readings as separately identified reading copies. Keep the task sheet, including its original exit stimulus, within DEFAULTS.md. A typical first-read novel part is about 1,050 words, nine minutes at 120 words per minute. Shared reading with tracking fits the named reading part; independent practice uses at most 120 words at once. Do not shorten the whole text to fit the task sheet. Explain this distinction on the teacher page. Preserve source punctuation; our directions and original stimuli use sentences of 15 words or fewer. No stripped punctuation.

## 10. Two rooms

At home use the 30 to 40 minute default: five minutes retrieve/repair, twelve read together, eight inspect a choice, five respond and five reread. The adult is the partner and keeps dated work without scores. Read assigned parts whole, sharing the load. Cover reserved stimuli until response time. One projector displays taught texts and individual feature demonstrations only; everyone has paper for the response. A board and hand-copied short cards replace the screen. No visitor or device is required.

Preview plainly that Tansy becomes ill and dies. Offer supported private reading and a quiet return. Writing can use gate/display/kitchen entries instead; no family loss story is requested.
