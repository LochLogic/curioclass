**s03-X110.** Reference: g4-u3-01, Canal: Gains and Costs chapter 4 final two paragraphs and chapter 5 paragraphs 3 and 5. Ask: "The opinion is to count more than money. Plan two reasons supported by the readings."

Key: A court or payment record cannot show everything about a Nation's relationship with its homeland. A wage figure cannot show whether workers were safe or recovered from injuries. Accept two distinct reasons with relevant source details.
Wrong: "Reason one is wages. Reason two is money paid to workers." (J).
Shape: Plan two distinct supported reasons.

**s03-X110-sources.** Ask: "List a source location for each reason in your plan."

Key: Accept a traceable location matching each reason. For example, chapter 4's final paragraph describes the two kinds of records, and chapter 5 paragraph 5 explains what wages cannot show.
Wrong: "Source: that canal thing we read." (K).
Shape: Record traceable source locations for the planned reasons.

**s03-X111.** Reference: g4-u3-02, M3 page 418, predicted income, borrowing and tolls. Ask: "The reader knows canals. The reader has not seen the funding plan. Write an introduction that states your opinion and signals your reasons."

Key: Name the question of relying on expected income to pay borrowing costs. State a position and signal distinct reasons, such as expected revenue and timing of interest. Either position can meet if it preserves the difference between projected and received money. Meets: "Should the state borrow based on expected canal income? I would wait until predicted income and interest payments are clearer." Wrong: They should say yes to it for those reasons. (AN); The memorial has a funding plan. (Z); The state should borrow because it already got all the promised income. (B).

Shape: One introduction for a new reader’s funding question. Keep full introductions and this audience case out of models.

**s03-X112.** Reference: g4-u3-01 g4-u3-03, Canal: Gains and Costs chapter 5, especially paragraphs 1 and 3; Count the Cost first reason. Ask: "The draft treats the imagined speaker as a witness to workers’ dangers. Write a reason paragraph that corrects the source mistake."

Key: The reconstruction raises questions about a cost estimate and possible obstacles. The history describes actual labor risks such as accidents or illness. Explain how a sourced danger and a planning concern support a reason about evaluating construction costs or worker safeguards. Meets: "Plans should account for worker safety. The speech raises questions about costs. The history describes accidents and illnesses workers faced." Wrong: The speaker watched workers get sick. (AA); Plans should be different because I say so. (A).

Shape: One source-corrected reason paragraph. Keep the false labor-testimony attribution and complete two-source paragraph out of practice.

**s03-X113.** Reference: g4-u3-01, Canal: Gains and Costs chapter 6 paragraphs 2 and 4; skip the diagram when numbering paragraphs. Use the two prose paragraphs for the reason and place phrase. The punctuation line supplies its own sentences. Ask: "The draft says one design solves every problem. Revise this reason using details from the reading."

Key: For example, a lock changes the level of the water, while an aqueduct carries canal water over a stream. That is why planners need different designs for different obstacles.
Wrong: "One kind of design is best because I like it." (A).
Shape: Link engineering details to a revised reason.

**s03-X113-phrase.** Ask: "Describe where the stream passes at the crossing, using a complete place phrase."

Key: For example, The smaller stream passes beneath the aqueduct. Accept a complete phrase that puts the stream beneath the crossing.
Wrong: "The stream passes beneath." (U).
Shape: Use a complete place phrase grounded in the source.

**s03-X113-comma.** Ask: "The canal followed one route. The road took another. Join these sentences into one compound sentence."

Key: The canal followed one route, and the road took another. Accept a fitting coordinating conjunction with a comma between the two complete clauses.
Wrong: "The canal followed one route, and took another." (T).
Shape: Join two supplied independent clauses with a comma and coordinating conjunction.

**s03-X114.** Reference: g4-u3-02 g4-u3-03, M3 paragraph and Count the Cost borrowing paragraph. Ask: "The draft ends by saying all payments arrived. Revise this ending using one financing idea from the readings."

Key: A related conclusion can recommend waiting or put a condition on borrowing because interest can come due before revenue arrives. Accept a closing judgment grounded in a financing idea from the readings.
Wrong: "Go ahead because the expected income has all arrived." (B). "The canal has many kinds of boats." (AC).
Shape: Revise one conclusion using the financing evidence.

s03-R114-question uses each child’s existing first 45-second turn in Formal critique and revision. Ask: "Ask a question about one source detail in your partner’s draft." Key: a relevant question tied to an actual source detail and the partner’s point. An unrelated question is M. Shape: one live question about an actual peer draft. Keep it separate from the supplied X114 ending. Return the recorded observation in day 115 Written feedback. Use genuinely heard questions for D119c.
