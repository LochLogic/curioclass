## The daily reading and skills rhythm

Each 90-minute day has a 60-minute unit lesson and a 30-minute skills block. Long-word reading and aligned spelling receive 10 minutes daily. Monday, Wednesday and Friday add 20 minutes of independent reading with logs and teacher conferences; Tuesday and Thursday add a 5-minute grammar warm-up retrieving already taught grammar and 15 minutes of partner rereading with feedback. Reading conferences sample literary and informational band texts. Course-level oral-reading rotations replace the usual conference/rereading work on their two check days. The teacher holds the watch privately.

The first row of each five-day week in days.csv specifies its word-analysis focus and eight vocabulary targets. The focus runs from retrieved syllable types through three- and four-syllable division, schwa and stress, combined affixes, and Greek/Latin roots, then increasingly unfamiliar transfer. Each day reads and spells words in isolation, sentences and the current passage. Short pattern probes sit in that block on days 14, 29, 44, 59, 74, 89, 104, 119, 134 and 149, with a final mixed check on days 157 to 158. The next day's 10 minutes first corrects the observed pattern, then continues or extends it. Students read letters and word parts before using context to confirm; pictures and context never replace decoding. Fluency attends to purpose, accuracy, expression and self-correction, not speed alone, with brief teacher listening every week and fall, winter and spring records.

Vocabulary targets are commissioning requirements for the original texts, not a claim that unwritten texts have been examined. The text writer must verify each target occurs meaningfully in the named week's reading, or substitute an equally useful word actually there. Introduce meaning before the relevant passage, inspect its use while reading, then require it in talk and writing after reading. Repeated targets such as evidence, source, precise, revise and conclusion deliberately connect units. No new list is added to a cold check; week 16 and week 32 lists retrieve familiar words. Direct word-strategy teaching remains in unit 2 even though grade 3 strategies support unit 1 reading.

Every reading day includes partner talk and a short written exit ticket needing that day's text. W.4.10 is daily; W.4.9a is taught on the literary texts in unit 1 and W.4.9b on informational research in unit 2. Unit 1 biography responses use RI.4.1 and the grade 3 evidence routine without claiming the later W.4.9b step. Formal discussions are placed about every ten course days, including days 10, 20, 26, 33, 40, 50, 60, 68, 90, 99, 104, 114, 128, 134, 140, 145 and 155, with additional assessed discussions. Models precede drafting, and each unit includes planning, peer critique, teacher feedback, revision, editing and publication.

## Assessment evidence and response

A code on a `Check` or `assess=end` row is an evidence commitment. The unit plan must ask about every part of that code listed on that row, using a short prompt per part or the named product/performance. Parent RF codes on day 35 include their lettered parts. The daily RF.4.3a and L.4.2d skills-block work on assessment days includes a brief read/spell transfer sample; the W.4.10 record uses accumulated short and extended writing rather than an extra essay. A polished product alone does not prove revision, spelling-reference use, all grammar forms, or digital collaboration, so those have explicit samples or process records.

End-of-unit assessment components span final days to avoid two full performances in one block. Portfolio scoring uses products already made, not additional on-demand versions. Discussion and recording tasks use small groups. A named 15-minute feedback/response slot belongs to that assessment once; later practice/publication applies that feedback without adding another full response allocation. Where a multi-day task delays feedback until its second day, the CSV says so. Mid-unit feedback changes the next day's opening or practice group.

Grammar ownership stays in unit 1 with teaching continued by part: L.4.1f there; c and d in unit 2; b, e and g in unit 3; a in unit 4. L.4.2a, b and d are taught in unit 1, c in unit 3. Later end components assess their continued parts, rather than pretending they were completed in unit 1.

Day 37 responds and rehearses. Day 38 presents the original poem and inspiration with a visual and a small-group discussion, followed by feedback inside the day.
