## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Carry section 1.1's log, written discussion moves and sound/spelling wall. Roles are planned only for the corrected three-role design. Independently explain: facilitator invites, evidence-finder locates, questioner asks. Everyone answers and reflects. Review grade 3 u02-1's "stanza: a group of lines in a poem." Add verse/rhythm and meter as recurring stress pattern; Williams is free verse. Context-clues reviews grade 3 u01-4's sentence clue. Figurative-language adds simile/metaphor names to literal/nonliteral review. Keep each word's sense stable.

Day 8 ideal instrument: complete print versions beside actual oral reading and staging. A teacher and willing reader fit; a transcript alone partly fits because it cannot show voice/action. Live performance is the fallback for missing recording equipment. Use a floor-level space, paper and soft taps, never a raised step. No key digital tool or unbuilt spec.

Word-card meanings: meter, a recurring stress pattern; simile, a comparison using like or as; metaphor, a comparison stating one thing as another; synonym, a word with a similar meaning; antonym, a word with an opposite meaning. Idiom means an expression understood beyond its separate words. Adage and proverb name customary sayings offering wisdom or guidance; their categories can overlap. Prose uses sentences and paragraphs; drama gives a scene for performance. Dialogue is the characters' spoken words. Review the written stanza and stage-direction meanings; first person uses a participant's telling voice, while third person refers to characters by name or he, she or they. Teach close as familiar through a friendship example, distinct from shut. The pocket passage remains reserved.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview metaphor, simile, prose, drama and narrator with separately labeled examples. Gloss historical Rossetti vocabulary before rereading, without treating glosses as decoding evidence. Frame: "This feature belongs here because ___." Extend with another supported nuance, not allusions.

Spoken directions, large print, tactile line separators, signing and a scribe provide access without supplying an answer. Record help. Independent decoding and spelling remain the child's. Caption live presentations and supply their printed directions. Public speaking is optional; offer a private live exchange. Never infer oral skill from paper.

Use complete on-file core readings as separately identified reading copies. Keep the task sheet, including its reserved exit passage, within DEFAULTS.md. A typical first-read novel part is about 1,050 words, nine minutes at 120 words per minute. Shared reading with tracking fits the named reading part; independent practice uses at most 120 words at once. Do not shorten the whole text to fit the task sheet. Explain this distinction on the teacher page. Preserve source punctuation; our directions and new directions use sentences of 15 words or fewer. No stripped punctuation.

## 10. Two rooms
