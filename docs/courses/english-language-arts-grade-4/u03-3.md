**X92.** First read g4-u3-05, scene 1's closing sequence from "Ada picks up the basket" onward. Teacher's seated performance changes only Ben's last action: he keeps holding the stake. Ask: "Compare Ben's final direction with the performed action. Explain how the difference changes the scene." Key: the script has Ben withdraw his hand and pick up the boat; holding the stake instead makes him disregard Mother's request. Accept another supported effect of that precise mismatch. Wrong: the changed action follows the direction faithfully (AI). **Shape:** script/performance comparison with its effect; avoid a full written comparison and this closing sequence. Practice labels one feature or repairs one separate action.

**X93.** First read g4-u3-05, scene 2 from "I was thinking about a journey" to its final direction. Ask: "Describe Ben's response using words and actions. Include one present progressive sentence." Key: Ben recognizes the start of the journey matters and questions his imagined loads; he is setting one block beside the boat. Equivalent evidence-based descriptions accepted. Wrong: "He is nice" alone (F); only "He set a block aside" for the required tense (G). **Shape:** character response with a progressive sentence; avoid combined character/tense production and this final counting decision. Practice fixes one auxiliary separately from a trait/evidence match.

**X94.** First read g4-u3-05, scene 3 from "A gust lifts one corner" through the end. Ask: "Explain how the wind affects Ada's actions. Include one complete prepositional phrase from this passage." Key: the gust lifts the map, so Ada catches it with both hands; "with both hands" is complete. Other accurate phrases are accepted if attached to a supported explanation. Wrong: "It is a nice place" (F); "with" without its object (U). **Shape:** setting/action explanation with a complete phrase; avoid that combined product and the gust/catch case. Practice supplies a missing object in an earlier fence sentence.

**X95.** First read g4-u3-05, scene 4 from "Ada slides a blank sheet" onward. Teacher performs Ben placing one apple beside the sheet instead of two. Ask: "Explain one mismatch between the reading and script."

Key: The script gives two apples; performance gives one. Claiming these match is AI.
Shape: One script/performance mismatch using the reserved scene ending. Practice inspects a separate feature; no apple/sheet rehearsal.

**X96.** Hear g4-u3-02, M3's first sentence and final three sentences, first here. Identify the omitted middle explicitly; do not present the selections as continuous. Gloss interest, augmented, subscribed and transit duties without explaining the argument. Ask: "Explain how the speaker supports funding the canal. Use a reason and a specific detail." Key: the memorial expects future revenues to cover debt; pledged land, reported as 106,632 acres, is a specific supporting detail. Tolls or increased salt-work revenues can also support a correctly explained connection. The speaker is the adult reading the actual petition, not an invented historical witness. Wrong: only the wish to build (H); future toll receipts already arrived (B). **Shape:** explain a heard point through its reason and detail; avoid a complete support response and the pledged-land/toll case. Models correct one mislabeled entry from already read history. Read twice; collect every paper before print. Ten minutes include glossing, listening and writing.

C95-ownership is a separate independent record in the last two minutes of Word work: "Correct the ownership word: There map remains beside the basket." Key: Their map remains beside the basket; possessive there V. Shape: one fresh ownership correction, never a complete paired performance/grammar response. Return it with X95 in the next named repair slot.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: cast, dialogue, direction, response, scene, progressive, phrase. Call the modern opposition an invented argument; reserve P119's complete decoding words from vocabulary cards and word practice. Frames: "This action suggests ___." "This phrase locates ___." Direction here means stage instruction, unlike map direction. Response means a character reaction, not a child's answer. Read directions aloud; scribe content without supplying reasoning. Keep independent reading independent. Read listening twice, collect paper before revealing print. Describe display layout without interpreting it. Public passing is allowed; every child returns paper. Enlarge source labels/directions and provide bilingual glossaries. One marking scale throughout: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keep criteria separate; no points or totals.

Printed directions, new cards and exit stems have punctuated sentences of at most fifteen words. Each student page and each separate reading leaf stays within 350 words including its stimulus. Core texts use separate reading leaves from registered texts, divided at coherent boundaries; older primary quotations stay intact with shared reading support.
