**X92.** Cast: Ina and messenger. Setting: landing steps. "[Ina hurries down the steps, waving a letter.] INA: Wait! This must reach my brother today! [The messenger turns back.]" Presentation: "Ina sits still and whispers. The messenger keeps walking." Ask: "Explain what this script reveals through its drama form. How do these performance choices change that meaning?" Key: urgent movement/speech and response; cast/setting/dialogue/directions guide reading; stillness/whispering reduce urgency and walking removes response. Wrong: calls contradictory staging faithful (AI). **Shape:** drama features plus script/performance meaning; avoid full written comparison and letter case. Practice repairs one feature label, then one action separately.

**X93.** "[Ben moves his boat aside so Ada can write.] BEN: I want your questions on the list." Ask: "Describe his response with evidence. Use present and future progressive sentences." Key: "He is making space for Ada. He will be helping her record concerns." Future is a supported possibility, not known later fact. Wrong: "He helped Ada" for progressive (G); generic nice (F). **Shape:** evidence-based response in two progressive forms; avoid combined character/progressive production. Practice fixes one auxiliary; matches an action/inference separately.

**X94.** "[By the kitchen window, Ada unfolds a map beside a bowl of apples. A route stake is visible outside.]" Ask: "How does this setting make the event matter? Name a phrase precisely locating the map." Key: home/apples/stake relate route to livelihood; beside a bowl of apples locates map. Equivalent accurate location accepted. Wrong: "Nice place" (F); "beside" without object (U). **Shape:** setting significance plus precise phrase; avoid that combination and window/bowl/map arrangement. Practice repairs a missing object elsewhere.

**X95.** "[Ben waits beside Ada.] BEN: Their tree stands there." Performance: "Ben interrupts from the doorway." Ask: "Explain one performance difference from the script. Correct this sentence: There tree stands there." Key: waiting versus interrupting or beside versus doorway, either explained difference; Their tree stands there. Wrong: interruption treated as waiting faithfully (AI); possessive There retained (V). **Shape:** performance difference plus ownership correction; avoid paired task and doorway/tree case. Practice inspects a missing feature separately from word meaning.

**X96.** Heard first: "We should ask about digging expenses. A transport estimate measures carrying goods. The memorial's rate comparison concerns a load and distance." Ask: "Name the point, reason and example. Explain how the example supports the point." Key: ask about digging expense; carriage expense differs; load/distance comparison illustrates that distinction, leaving digging unanswered. Wrong: speaker's wish alone (H); rate proves digging expense (C). **Shape:** extract spoken support and explain connection; avoid full three-box answer and cost-comparison card. Model one incorrect chart label, not a complete response.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: cast, dialogue, direction, response, scene, progressive, phrase, reconstruction. Frames: "This action suggests ___." "This phrase locates ___." Direction here means stage instruction, unlike map direction. Response means a character reaction, not a child's answer. Read directions aloud; scribe content without supplying reasoning. Keep independent reading independent. Read listening twice, collect paper before revealing print. Describe display layout without interpreting it. Public passing is allowed; every child returns paper. Enlarge source labels/directions and provide bilingual glossaries. One marking scale throughout: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keep criteria separate; no points or totals.

Printed directions, new cards and exit stems have punctuated sentences of at most fifteen words. Each student page and each separate reading leaf stays within 350 words including its stimulus. Core texts use separate registered reading leaves, divided at coherent boundaries; older primary quotations stay intact with shared reading support. No invented summary replaces a core text. Extension: support another interpretation using the same text.

Scenes 1 to 4 are about 200 to 300 words each, two to three reading minutes, with glossing/rereading inside fifteen. Day 95 verse is the unit-named Another Missing Line, previously assessed: retrieval, never cold. Register its literary text row if absent. Day 96 uses M2, about fifty source words plus glossary, shared reading for long clauses.
