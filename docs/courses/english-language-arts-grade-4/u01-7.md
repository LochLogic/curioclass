**s01-X28.** Fresh invented observation cards: "A tin button clicked against a cup. Its edge flashed." "A leaf pressed against glass. Its shadow trembled." Ask: "Choose a subject and plan a poem with an image and a deliberate line break." Key sample plan: button; clicking/flash; break after clicked to let the sound stand alone. Other coherent plans work when the child names the sensory image and where/why the line ends. Weak: things with no identifiable subject/image (Q); page spacing alone offered as the plan (E).
Shape: produce an image/line-break plan from a fresh chosen observation. Avoid completed poem plans or judged planning practice. Choose between supplied weak/strong images instead; own plan stays unjudged.

**s01-X29.** Fresh fictional moment: "A drawer slides shut. One wooden bead rolls into the light." Ask: "Write a short poem showing this moment through images and deliberate line breaks." Key sample: "A drawer shuts. / One bead rolls / into the light." The break delays the bead's arrival in light; another supported image/line choice works. Weak: "Things do stuff" (Q); claims an unmentioned storm occurs (O). Ask no additional explanation on the student sheet; teacher notes whether the lines create a discernible focus.
Shape: produce a fresh short poem. Avoid complete poem-production models or judged drafting practice; inspect one weak image/line choice. Own full poem stays unjudged preparation for D30a.

**s01-X31.** Fictional Rae poem: "A pebble hides / a stripe of gold / until I turn it." Plan: noticing hidden details; reader has never read Rae's poem. Ask: "Draft an introduction explaining this poem's inspiration for the reader." Key sample: "Rae's poem shows a detail revealed by turning a pebble. Noticing something hidden inspired this poem." Accept qualified inferences, not claims about a real poet. Weak: unexplained "It came from that" (AE); a prize caused writing (O).
Shape: produce an introduction from a new poem/plan. Avoid complete introduction models/cards; inspect missing topic or sequence in a supplied oral presentation. Own introduction stays unjudged.
