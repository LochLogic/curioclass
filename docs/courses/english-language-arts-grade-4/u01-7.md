**s01-X28.**

**s01-X28.** Observation A: A tin button clicked against a cup. Its edge flashed.
Observation B: A leaf pressed against glass. Its shadow trembled.
Choose one observation for a poem plan with an image and a deliberate line break.

Key: Button; clicking and flash; break after clicked to let the sound stand alone. Either observation works with a sensory image and purposeful break.

Wrong answers: Things happen. (Q); Spaces alone make it a poem. (E)
Shape: produce an image/line-break plan from a fresh chosen observation. Avoid completed poem plans or judged planning practice. Choose between supplied weak/strong images instead; own plan stays unjudged.

**s01-X29.**

**s01-X29.** A drawer slides shut. One wooden bead rolls into the light.
Write a short poem showing this moment through images and deliberate line breaks.

Key: A drawer shuts. / One bead rolls / into the light. Breaks focus movement and arrival; other discernible image/line choices work.

Wrong answers: Things do stuff. (Q); A storm tears the house apart. (O)
Shape: produce a fresh short poem. Avoid complete poem-production models or judged drafting practice; inspect one weak image/line choice. Own full poem stays unjudged preparation for D30a.

**s01-X31.**

**s01-X31.** Rae's poem: A pebble hides / a stripe of gold / until I turn it.
Plan: noticing hidden details. Reader: someone new to Rae's poem.
Draft an introduction explaining this poem's inspiration for the reader.

Key: Rae's poem shows a detail revealed by turning a pebble. Noticing something hidden seems to inspire this poem. Qualified inferences work.

Wrong answers: It came from that. (AE); A prize caused Rae to write. (O)
Shape: produce an introduction from a new poem/plan. Avoid complete introduction models/cards; inspect missing topic or sequence in a supplied oral presentation. Own introduction stays unjudged.

**s01-X32.**

**s01-X32.** Source poem: The zipper bites / the scarf's loose end.
Response poem: My stapler snaps / its shiny teeth.
Explain a connection between the poems using a detail from each.

Key: The zipper bites; the stapler has teeth. Both make an everyday fastener seem alive.

Wrong answers: Both are nice. (A); They describe the same object. (O)
Shape: written source-to-poem evidence connection. Avoid completed connection models or judged connection-writing; inspect an unrelated supplied detail. Own explanation stays unjudged until its named revision collection.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview the section words in taught examples. Practice frame: "This choice fits because ___." Provide enlarged movable cards, tactile line separators, signing or content scribing; record help. Independent decoding/spelling stays the child's. Speak directions without supplying an independent reading item's words. A private live exchange replaces public sharing. Extend by challenging another supplied detail on the same standard.

Paginate whole readings across leaves, each at most 350 words including labels/directions, in 14 point type. Task sheets meet that cap too. Our stems and original stimuli use sentences of at most 15 words; preserve source punctuation. Current whole poet profiles are 380 to 401 words, about four reading minutes, not 740-word readings. The 740 to 950 range is an unmeasured difficulty target. Assign one whole profile, not the entire collection. Preview pet illness/death in Ben's notebook; Frost's father's death; Hughes's Black identity; Rossetti's religion, illness/death; Dickinson's publication without permission. No personal parallels or racial role play.
