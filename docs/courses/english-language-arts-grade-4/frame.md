# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 4

Course `english-language-arts-grade-4` (catalogue id `ela-k12`), grade 4, draft frame, written 2026-11-14; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text's grade 4 year, in the base text's order; unit 3 keeps the
  module's design (a history set, a short play, a piece urging a side, a two-sided discussion) on a new event.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 153 in units and 7 at course level (4 for two course-level checks, 3 respond days).
  One day is one literacy block of about 90 minutes, five days a week: the unit lesson (60 minutes: the base
  text's module lesson kept whole) and the skills block (30 minutes). The skills block's week, 150 minutes: the
  long-word and spelling sequence 10 minutes every day (50); on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, independent reading
  for 20 minutes with the teacher's conferences inside it (60); on Tuesday and Thursday, a 5-minute grammar warm-up
  on a sentence from the anchor (10) and 15 minutes of partner rereading for fluency with the teacher's feedback
  (30). Grammar is taught in the module lessons where the base text teaches it; the warm-ups retrieve it. This split
  is provisional until a representative day is timed in the probe. The base text plans 153 module lessons plus a
  second 60-minute block with five parts (independent reading; additional work with complex text; fluency with
  grammar, usage and mechanics; writing practice; word study and vocabulary). Curio Class keeps the module lesson
  whole, so 153 lessons become 153 unit days (unit 1 adds 1 for the start-of-year screens, the writing sample, since the long-word and oral reading screens run in the skills block as grade 3 set them up, unit 3 swaps 3 lessons
  for 3 myth days inside its 39, unit 4 merges 2 lessons into 1); it folds the second block's word study, fluency,
  grammar practice and independent reading into the 30 minutes and leaves out its additional complex-text work and
  writing practice, which the module lessons' close reading and daily writing carry (For the lead 1). Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and its mid-unit
  assessments: the base text's assessments for the module's first two base units are the unit's mid-unit
  assessments, and its third base unit's assessment with the performance task is the end-of-unit assessment (source
  map). A row last taught or assessed in grade g is retrieved in grade g+1 and checked for readiness in grade g+2 or later. A needed readiness check is given in the preceding unit's last ten minutes or an intervening respond day, at most three days ahead; unit 1 uses the last ten minutes of day 1 after its hook. This course's confirmed grade 3 assumptions all receive retrieval.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 4 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt), all
  30 progression rows of grade 4 (data/progressions/ela.csv), every lettered part taught in the unit named below.
  The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (grades 3 to 5, all three gateways, Gateway 1 in full: Cory's yes, 2026-11-12) and
  the approach is docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) governs every unit.
- **Base text.** The 2017 K to 5 language arts curriculum recorded in docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md (CC BY 4.0; copy
  in S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017), grade 4, modules 1 to 4 (the 2017 curriculum is CC BY 4.0, the
  store's terms excerpt licence/eleducation-terms-of-use-excerpt-2026-11-12.txt; only the 2024 Skills Block is CC
  BY-NC). Its lesson design, sequence, protocols
  and assessments are adapted; its trade books are replaced by Curio Class texts (BASELINE rule 29: newer texts,
  mostly original Curio Class texts written to the grade 4 text spec, each read by the text agent before use,
  docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); its own CC BY texts may be adapted; its third-party excerpts are replaced; public-domain texts may stay.
  Its name is never used on a Curio Class page; one credit line per adapted day. The 2025 edition and the 2024
  skills block are never used. The source map closes this frame.
- **Which row sits where.** A row sits in the unit that first teaches its grade 4 step (the part new this year),
  which is where the kept base lessons first teach and assess it. Where a row's lettered parts come in later units,
  the owning unit says which part waits and the later unit names it as continued. Before a row's unit, a unit uses
  the grade 3 form of the skill, and the first unit that leans on a grade 3 skill retrieves
  it. Later units practise every placed row again on new texts; the table names those returns as review.
- **Reading level.** Anchors sit in the grades 4 to 5 band (a quantitative reading measure of about 740 to 1010),
  most in its lower half with scaffolding at the high end (e4-reading-range); a few poems and the play sit below the
  band by measure but above it in meaning, placed by what makes them hard and by reader and task, not by the
  number. About half the anchors are literary and half informational. The measures named in the units are targets
  the commissioned texts must meet; each anchor gets a stable id and a text record (measure, what makes it hard
  under the four headings, the reader and task, why here, rights, whole or excerpt) in
  the Course text record section of scope-and-sequence.md. Entries with unwritten or unchosen texts remain provisional: the text owner completes exact selections/manuscripts, lengths, measures where meaningful, qualitative demands, reader/task fit, supports, vocabulary/features and rights before the relevant unit plan. Anchor balance counts anchors only, once per work or unified set: two of four are informational (50 percent).
- **Word lists.** About 8 to 10 words a week from the unit's texts, met before, during and after reading and met again in later units; the scope and sequence sets each list.
- **Long texts.** Two read whole: an original verse novel (unit 1) and an original chapter book (unit 4).
- **Words, read and never guessed.** The long-word sequence (e4-long-words) runs every day from day 1 with a check
  every few weeks; spelling follows it.
- **Writing.** Every reading day ends in a written exit ticket that needs the day's text (W.4.9, W.4.10). Each unit
  takes at least one piece through the whole process. The year keeps the grades 3 to 5 shares: about 30 percent
  opinion, 35 informative, 35 narrative. Research runs in unit 2 and again in unit 4.
- **Talk.** Partner talk daily; a formal text-based discussion about every two weeks, with roles from unit 1.
- **Paper path and devices.** Every anchor has a print copy and every task a paper route. Keyboarding, publishing,
  the internet exchange of drafts, the poem recordings and the announcement happen in the school day on a shared classroom device rotation or a
  lab session, with no student account; home practice is optional. If no device route exists by year end, W.4.6
  is recorded unfinished for that class (e4-writing-process).
- **Record sheet, family note and sub-ready day.** One per base unit (three per Curio Class unit, 12 a year), since a unit here is a whole base module of eight weeks; the unit stays the base module, which carries the compelling question and the performance task.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 3 (Haudenosaunee lands taken for the canal; Irish and other
  laborers' hard and dangerous work): before it reads, the agent studies the Haudenosaunee Confederacy's own
  published pages and the histories the Onondaga, Oneida, Seneca and other Nations publish on land loss in New York,
  scholarship by Haudenosaunee historians and by historians of the New York land purchases, and a Native journalists'
  association style guide, and checks the history and any Haudenosaunee voice against them. Unit 4 (racism inside the
  suffrage movement; the exclusion of Black women): it studies Black women suffragists' own writing of the time,
  scholarship by Black women historians of the vote, and a national African American history museum's pages on
  the vote.
