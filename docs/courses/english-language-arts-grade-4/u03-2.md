**X87.** "A purchase record names the state and a price. A Nation's account explains land loss. The record alone cannot describe every experience of losing that land." Ask: "Name this passage's main idea with support. Name one limit of the record." Key: a single record cannot tell the whole history; a price cannot describe every experience. Wrong: "Land" as main idea (D); "Price tells everyone's feelings" (C). **Shape:** supported main idea plus source limit; avoid that combined task. Practice removes a mismatched detail, without a complete supplied answer.

**X88.** A, A Landing Proposal: "Build a landing near our orchard. Boats could collect apples there. Growers would not need to carry them to the next town. That shorter cart journey could leave more daylight for harvesting." B, After the Landing Opened: "Growers beside the new landing carted apples only a short distance. Growers across the ridge still took the long road around it. The new landing changed some journeys while leaving others long." Ask: "Which statement is a prediction? What does B add when interpreting it?" Key: A predicts shorter cart journeys; B qualifies who gained, near versus across the ridge. Any quoted prediction with its accurate qualification accepted. Wrong: "A reports completed results" (B); disconnected lists (AD). **Shape:** distinguish prediction and qualify it across texts; avoid paired prediction/outcome sorting. Practice inspects one unsupported certainty, never orchard/landing/ridge.

**X89.** A, A Journey: "At sunrise I boarded a boat. At midday we passed a stone bridge. By evening we reached a warehouse. There I watched sacks being unloaded." B, Digging the Channel: "Crews used picks to loosen hard ground and shovels to lift it. Each load had to be raised by hand. Clearing the channel required repeated physical effort." Ask: "Explain each passage's structure. Which gives evidence about digging conditions?" Key: A orders travel observations through time; B explains method and resulting effort; B provides digging evidence. Wrong: "The traveler watched digging" (AH); signal words without relationship (AG). **Shape:** two structures plus relevant witness; avoid complete paired structure/witness analysis. Practice corrects one source/date label.

**X90.** Cards: "A merchant praises shipments reaching buyers." "A history describes crews digging with picks and shovels." Ask: "Explain one gain beside one cost from these cards. Write a specific partner question about their connection." Key: trade access beside strenuous hand digging; a relevant question qualifies the judgment or asks what remains unknown. Multiple linked questions accepted. Wrong: "Boats; shovels" with no relationship (AD); lunch question (M). **Shape:** integrated explanation plus linked question; avoid that whole product. Discussion inspects weak comparisons; own notes remain unjudged.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: memorial, petition, record, prediction, interpretation, Nation, labor. Frame: "This source describes ___; it cannot establish ___." Hall's improvement means growth/clearing, not our approval. Native in Hall means locally born, not a Native Nation. Read directions aloud; scribe content without supplying reasoning. Keep independent reading independent. Read listening twice, collect paper before revealing print. Describe display layout without interpreting it. Public passing is allowed; every child returns paper. Enlarge source labels/directions and provide bilingual glossaries. One marking scale throughout: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keep criteria separate; no points or totals.

Printed directions, new cards and exit stems have punctuated sentences of at most fifteen words. Each student page and each separate reading leaf stays within 350 words including its stimulus. Core texts use separate registered reading leaves, divided at coherent boundaries; older primary quotations stay intact with shared reading support. No invented summary replaces a core text. Extension: support another interpretation using the same text.

Chapter 4 about 650 words, six reading minutes. H89-A/B plus chapter 5 about 900 words, eight minutes plus glossing. Chapters 6 to 8 about 1,200 words, ten minutes on 90. H91 about 420 words, four check minutes. Never display Hall's full file before 116.
