**s02-X63, The Porch Fork.** Text: "A note-carrier chooses a route to a porch. Covered path: go to 2A. Open path: go to 2B. 2A. The note reaches the porch dry. End. 2B. Rain soaks the note before it reaches the porch. End." Ask: "Trace each possible path in one sentence." Key/sample: "The covered path brings a dry note to the porch. The open path brings a wet note there." Require both choice/outcome correspondences, not both routes consecutively. Wrong: covered then open (AA); dry in both (A).
Shape: two route sentences from written navigation. Avoid note-carrier/porch cases and full route-summary models; identify a faulty navigation instruction.

**s02-X64, Hooks and Covering.** Fact card: "A decorator crab has hooks on its shell. Attached seaweed helps it blend with its surroundings." Scene: "A shadow crosses a rocky shore." Ask: "Plan a new story situation using this real defense." Key/sample: "A decorator crab rests among seaweed as a fish passes. Its attached seaweed makes its outline harder to see." Require participant, setting/problem and plausible camouflage, without guaranteed escape. Other defensible situations work. Wrong: becomes seaweed (AK); unrelated fact list with no situation (Z).
Shape: a new encounter constrained by applied covering. Avoid decorator crabs and complete story-plan models; inspect a mismatch in a taught action. Own plans stay unjudged.

**s02-X65, Under the Log.** Card: "Pill bugs live in damp places under logs. Hard overlapping plates cover their bodies. They can curl into a ball when disturbed." Ask: "Develop a narrator or character using one located detail from this card." Key/sample: "Under the damp log, a pill bug's plates brushed a leaf as it moved." Require identifiable participant/view and concrete action/setting shaped by a card detail. Wrong: "It was nice" (AB); flies on wings (AK). No animal speech required.
Shape: develop characterization from a located body/habitat detail. Avoid pill bugs and full portraits; identify a generic phrase on taught material.

**s02-X66, Scattered Pages.** Text: "A gust scatters Imani's numbered story pages. One page lands behind a closed gate." Ask: "Write a problem and three connected events in a natural order." Key/sample: "Imani cannot recover her ending. She asks the caretaker to open the gate. She gathers the missing page. She sorts all pages by number." Other safe coherent sequences work. Wrong: sorts the missing page before recovery without explanation (AA); unrelated lunch ending (W).
Shape: originate a problem/causal sequence, not sort supplied events. Avoid missing-page cases and full problem/three-event models; inspect an impossible dependency.

**s02-X67, A Closed Ball.** Card: "A southern three-banded armadillo can curl into a ball. Its head, legs and tail tuck inside its armor." Scene: "A nearby animal approaches. The armadillo curls up. The other animal waits, then leaves." Ask: "Plan an ending that follows these events and preserves the real defense." Key/sample: "After the other animal leaves, the armadillo uncurls. Its limbs are no longer tucked inside." Require outcome tied to departure and protective position. Wrong: flies into a tree (AK); wins an unrelated race (W).
Shape: continue a supplied encounter using anatomical constraints. Avoid this species/scene and full endings. Never transfer curling to the nine-banded expert animal.

**s02-X68, The Unfinished Banner.** Card: "Ari hangs a paper banner. Its center sags. Ari adds a center support before hanging it again." Weak plan: "Hang the banner again. Add the support. Notice the sag." Ask: "Critique this plan's organization using a detail from the card." Key/sample: "The support belongs before rehanging; the card says Ari adds it first." Require defect plus located justification. Wrong: "It needs work" (AJ); claims paint caused the sag (A).
Shape: source-justified critique of fresh event organization. Avoid banner/support cases and complete critique-response models; practice marks only the defective component.
