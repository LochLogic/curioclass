**s02-X69, day 69.** Sources: g4-u2-03, C, A noticeable coat, paragraph 2, and g4-u2-01, Poison, A warning is not a wall, paragraph 2. The empty-den encounter is invented.

Ask: “A skunk waits beside an empty den at dusk. The opening says, "It waited there." Expand the opening so a new reader knows who is there and what is happening.”

Key, open: At dusk I saw a striped skunk by an empty den. It raised its tail as another animal drew near. Look for an identifiable narrator or characters, a setting and a situation supported by the named passages. Accept a participant or outside narrator. Wrong: Z: It waited there for them. AB: The skunk was in a nice place.
Shape: expand a bare opening from a fresh source pairing and situation. Avoid this den encounter and complete opening exemplars; own openings remain unjudged.

**s02-X71, day 71.** Source context: g4-u2-04, 2B, paragraph beginning For a moment, Zara saw only the spreading cloud. The supplied line tests punctuation and can be judged without the passage.

Ask: “Punctuate the line below. Eli said I cannot see her now”

Key, open: Eli said, "I cannot see her now." Look for the comma after said, quotation marks around the speech and the final period inside the quotation marks. The passage supplies scene context. Punctuation can be judged from the supplied line. Wrong: AO: Eli said, I cannot see her now.
Shape: Punctuate one complete, unseen line of direct speech. Practice locates an isolated boundary.

**s02-X72, day 72.** Source: g4-u2-04, 2A, paragraph beginning Zara almost added another line. Use that paragraph with the invented visitor and Eli's new line.

Ask: “A visitor thinks Pip left the ledge. Eli says, "Did you bring lunch?" Revise Eli's line to show a feeling and how he answers the visitor.”

Key, open: Eli said, "I am unsure where the rock ends. Look closely where the outline breaks." Accept a plausible feeling and a response grounded in the outline becoming hard to see. Record a punctuation error separately from a response that ignores the scene. Wrong: AG: Eli said, "Did you bring lunch?" AO: Eli said, It might still be against the rock.
Shape: Replace irrelevant dialogue with a response grounded in a source scene. Hold back the visitor and new line. Keep own dialogue unjudged.

**s02-X73, day 73.** Sources: g4-u2-04, 2B, first paragraph, and g4-u2-03, E, How skin changes appearance, paragraph 3. The supplied description is invented for the task.

Ask: “A pale broad patch lay beside Pip. Everything looked nice. Revise the description using the usual adjective order and a detail you could see.”

Key, open: Pip's outline stood out against the broad pale patch. Look for size before color and a concrete description supported by the two named passages. Wrong: AF: Pip's outline stood out against the pale broad patch. AB: Everything looked nice beside Pip.
Shape: Revise sensory description with conventional adjective order. Practice inspects word suitability. Hold back broad and pale together and keep the separate grammar sample reserved.

These are new writing/editing tasks on registered sources, not additional reading companions. Keys were solved against the named paragraphs. No complete exit or end-item response enters practice, a model or a home page.
