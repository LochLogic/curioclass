# The Curio Class approach to teaching mathematics

This page says how mathematics carries out the shared principles in [APPROACH.md](APPROACH.md). It does not
repeat that page; principle numbers point back to it. The same rules hold: nothing here has been tried with
children, every claim rests on a source read for this page, and the rules are rules for section plans. Evidence
ratings (strong, moderate, minimal) are the federal practice guides' own. Study detail is in
[APPROACH-SOURCES.md](APPROACH-SOURCES.md), "Evidence notes".

Second draft, 2026-11-11, revised after the curriculum expert's cold read. Not yet approved.

**The role of the standards, and focus, coherence, rigor.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics set
what is learned and leave the teaching to us (shared core). From the K-8 Publishers' Criteria: most of the year
goes to the grade's major work (at least 65 percent of class time), each topic is tied to earlier grades and to
the major work, and understanding, fluency and application are each pursued. The scope and sequence carries the first two.
The section plan carries the third.

**The kinds of day (principle 2).** A math day is marked understanding, fluency or use. Understanding, fluent
procedures and quick recall of facts support each other (National Mathematics Advisory Panel; *Adding It Up*
says the same of its five strands).

**The progressions are the backbone (principle 1).** The standards were cut from written accounts of how each
topic grows across grades. A section plan reads the progression first. Four that matter for grade 4:

- *Fractions are numbers.* Grade 3 builds every fraction from unit fractions and places it on the number line.
  Grade 4 rests on this for equivalent fractions, which are hard because the numerals get bigger while the
  parts get smaller.
- *Place value.* A digit is worth ten times what it would be one place to the right.
- *Multiplicative comparison* ("times as many") first appears in grade 4. The language is the hard part; tape
  diagrams help.
- *Strategies before standard algorithms.* Students first compute with place value strategies, drawings and
  area models and explain them. Fluency with the standard algorithm is expected for addition and subtraction in
  grade 4, multiplication in grade 5 and division in grade 6. So grade 4 multiplies and divides with strategies
  and area models, on purpose.

**How a lesson opens and teaches (principles 3 and 4).** Teaching first is the default for anything new in
grades 2 to 5. A lesson may open with a "what do you notice" task of ten minutes at most when students can
attempt it and the goal is an idea. Explicit teaching has consistently positive effects for students with math
difficulties (National Mathematics Advisory Panel, finding 27).

**Concrete, then pictures, then symbols (principle 6).** New ideas start with objects or drawings and are tied,
step by step, to the written form. Connecting concrete and abstract forms is rated moderate (Pashler et al.
2007); for students who struggle, well-chosen concrete and drawn representations are rated strong (WWC 2021,
recommendation 3). What we read does not show that a fixed three-stage order is needed or that more
representations are better. Objects are temporary, chosen for a reason, and too many at once can confuse (EEF
maths report); an idea learned only with objects can stay stuck to them. A manipulative must be true to the
mathematics and tied to a written method; "fluently" never means with blocks (Publishers' Criteria). We have
not yet read the direct studies of this sequence, or of manipulatives; they are named in the evidence notes.

**The number line.** It is the one picture that holds whole numbers, fractions and decimals together. As the
central picture for fractions it is rated moderate, with much of the evidence from whole numbers and decimals
(WWC fractions guide, recommendation 2); for students who struggle, strong (WWC 2021, recommendation 4). The
grade 4 scope and sequence carries one number line model through its fraction and decimal units.

**Practice and fluency (principle 7).** A five-minute warm-up opens each day, drawn from printable generators,
mixing earlier work. Fluent means accurate and reasonably fast, with a method that is understood (Publishers'
Criteria). Automatic recall of facts takes enough practice over time (National Mathematics Advisory Panel,
finding 11). On timing: short timed activities of one to five minutes are rated strong as one part of taught
small-group help for students who struggle, on material already taught, with feedback, never as bare timed
sheets (WWC 2021, recommendation 6). The evidence does not tell us whether a whole class needs a clock. We
wanted to say fluency is best built without time pressure. We could not source that, so we do not say it.
*Rule (Cory, 2026-11-11):* no tool shows a
countdown timer to a student. Tools build fluency with short, spaced, mixed practice and immediate feedback. A
teacher or parent may run a short paper sprint on facts already taught, the student comparing only with their
own last score, never graded or posted.

**The daily evidence (principle 8).** Each day ends with an exit question. Regular formative assessment helps
in the elementary grades (National Mathematics Advisory Panel, finding 25). Many errors are sensible ideas used
where they no longer hold, such as "multiplication makes bigger"; a section plan lists them in advance (EEF maths
report) and puts a wrong worked example beside a right one once the right one is known (Deans for Impact).

**Word problems.** For grades 4 to 8: teach students to monitor and reflect (strong), use visual
representations such as tape diagrams (strong), compare more than one strategy (moderate), connect problems to
the concepts under them (moderate) (WWC 2012). Teaching word problem types is rated strong for students who
struggle (WWC 2021, recommendation 5). Contexts are how students learn to apply math, which the standards
require. We do not expect a context to teach the idea by itself: a small number of studies found real-world
problem teaching helped on similar problems only (National Mathematics Advisory Panel, finding 26).

**Practices and discussion (principle 5).** Each unit names the one or two Standards for Mathematical Practice
it builds and the task where students do them. A discussion is planned: anticipate, watch, choose whose work to
show and in what order, connect (the "5 Practices", as in the Illustrative Mathematics design principles).
Comparing strategies is the part with rated evidence; the wider case rests on professional guidance (NCTM).

**Students who are behind (principle 9).** They stay on grade-level work; sorting into lower sets tends to
widen gaps (EEF maths report). The readiness check finds the gap, the section plan gives a ten-minute reteach and
the earlier-grade tool, and respond days give time. The grade 4 scope and sequence has no reteach time beyond its four
respond days, and Curio Class has no small-group program. Teachers who need one should read the federal guide
*Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics* (WWC 2021), whose six recommendations are all rated strong.

**What a math tool is for (principle 6).** It shows why (move a point on a number line, cut a strip, build an
array), gives practice with feedback, or checks unaided. An on-screen manipulative meets the same bar as a real
one: true to the math, tied to the written method, dropped when no longer needed. On whether on-screen
manipulatives teach as well as real ones, we have read nothing. That is the first gap to close.

## What math adds to the unit and section plan fields

Field numbers are those of [courses/UNIT-PLAN.md](courses/UNIT-PLAN.md), fixed 2026-11-11.

- Unit plan, field 3 (where it sits): the progression is cited.
- Section plan, field 1: whether the standard asks for strategies or the standard algorithm; the one or two
  practices and their task.
- Field 3: the exit question for every day, in full.
- Field 4: the wrong worked example used for each expected error.
- Field 5: the representation carried through the unit, the day it is tied to the written method and the day
  it is withdrawn.
- Field 6: each day marked understanding, fluency, use or check.
- Field 8: the fluency target, if the standard sets one, and where it is practised.
