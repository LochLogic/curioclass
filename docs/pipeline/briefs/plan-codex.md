[COURSE] = {course}. You fill in the plan for unit {unit} of the social studies course {course}: docs/courses/{course}/s{uu}.md only; other units are written in their own runs, one after another. Where an earlier unit plan of this course exists, read it first, keep its shape and voice, and keep or change its carried list. The file already exists as a skeleton made by scripts/unit_skeleton.py: the mechanical fields are filled and correct; replace every `TODO(writer)` line with the content it asks for, as the curriculum expert (docs/agents/EXPERT.md), following docs/pipeline/briefs/plan.md and docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md, to the bar: the social studies part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md with the content standard docs/courses/social-studies/STANDARD.md. Change a mechanical field only if it is wrong, and then say so under "For the lead". Then run `python -X utf8 scripts/unitplan.py check {course}` until s{uu}.md passes (later units may still be skeletons; ignore their failures).

Style: a teacher reads this quickly. Write plain full sentences. No slash chains (write "map and photograph", not "map/photograph"), no telegraphic shorthand, no abbreviations a teacher would have to decode. Keep within the word cap by choosing less, not by compressing words.

Settled (apply in every unit you write; do not raise):
- Write the file as UTF-8 with LF line endings. Never let an apostrophe, quote or accented letter turn into "?"; the gate fails it.
- An exit ticket is one question with at most two parts; any invented case is printed "invented for practice". Kindergarten to grade 2: every child marks a picture on paper while the teacher reads the prompt aloud, and the spoken reason is heard from a rotating group of about six a day.
- Give each assessment item its minutes in the assessment table and keep the total inside the day's real minutes; a part made earlier on a product day comes off the sitting, and is scored only if it had written feedback and a chance to revise first; work from the day a topic is first taught is never a scored part. Work made, given feedback, revised and scored all in one period is not a scored part: scored work is made on an earlier day than the score. No reteaching on an assessment day.
- Error register: one misconception per letter, with one repair; never join two claims in one letter. File each wrong answer only under a letter whose text describes it.
- Sources (field 10): verified or none. Name where a quoted text's words can be checked for free (a primary-text archive, a government page, the publisher's page); plan no passage that cannot be checked and no paraphrase of a book we have not read.
- A day exit is answered from a source or case the students have not just worked through that day, and it measures every named part of the day's goal.
- Check days.csv before naming a day in prose; a day number in the plan matches days.csv.
- No pipeline notes in the plan (skeleton, gate, run or reviewer remarks); they go in your last message.
- A fact that can change (a living institution or leader, a renamed place, a court ruling, a current figure) gets a dated teacher background line ("As of November 2026: ...") with its official source where one exists, after a news search of the last 24 months, deaths included.
- The readiness check is given before the unit opens, in the last ten minutes of the previous unit's last day or of the respond day between the units, a few days ahead at most; day 1 opens on the hook and the compelling question (settled 2026-11-14, see DESK-ANSWERS). Unit 1's check, where unit 1 has readiness rows, takes the last ten minutes of day 1 after the hook, and days.csv marks day 1 `readiness`; unit 1's plan defines what it asks and how the teacher uses it (lead 2026-11-14, see DESK-ANSWERS).
- Grades 6 to 12 periods are 50 minutes. A timer appears only where it has a grounded purpose, such as a pace cue, a movement game or classroom management (settled 2026-11-14, see DESK-ANSWERS). Kindergarten to grade 2 assume at most one shared classroom device, always with a paper path.
- No qualified outside readers exist: agent reads take their place (the answer-key read that solves every item cold, the safety read against published school guidance, the expert's text read, and the sensitivity agent, docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). Name, per unit, what each read that applies must study; where the unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, faith, health or disability content, the community's own sources come first.
- No day added beyond the frame's totals.
- Kindergarten to grade 2 exits in full: the picture mark (point, circle, place a token or draw one mark) comes in the last two minutes. The rotating group is heard during supported work, so each child is heard about once a week, and the record sheet shows the rotation. Never ask a whole class of pre-readers for individual spoken reasons in two minutes.
- Spread spoken and observed evidence across the unit's days; never load every child's spoken evidence into the one assessment day.
- An observation never takes the only teaching minutes of a new skill: teach the skill first (on that day or an earlier one), observe after.
- Size the end-of-unit assessment to the day's real minutes for the grade: count the items and the time each takes (a built map or a written paragraph takes several minutes; a picked answer about one). Where the scope and sequence asks for a short assessment, keep it short.
- Start-of-year check (unit 1 only): there are no school days before day 1, so never place a sitting "before day 1". Where unit 1 has readiness rows (last used two or more grades back), the start-of-year check is those items, given as above; its reteach sits in day 2 or 3 inside existing time. Where unit 1 has none (all retrieval), write "Start-of-year check: none needed; retrieval only" and give no extra items.
- Every unit keeps a daily exit table. A source withheld for an assessment is never used on a teaching day; check every day's note and exit before you finish.
- A reteach station never takes over a day's teaching or product time: it runs inside a set number of minutes the day already has.
- Check holiday, symbol and civic facts against current official sources (for example the national bird law of 2024, current federal holidays and their names in our states); say what you checked.
- Check current events that touch the unit (court rulings, deaths or changes of named people) on the web before writing, and say what you checked. Where 2025 or 2026 events change what a unit teaches (rulings, laws, agency changes, elections), the dated background line cites an official source; for a court ruling, give the vote and what was decided (a stay is not a ruling on the merits).
- Cite documents exactly (article and section numbers, census versus city estimates); check each citation against the source.
- Name the measure behind any population figure: who counts (census, survey or estimate), which year, and what is counted (for example people who practice a faith, or who identify with it).
- Never use a campaign page or party material as a source; for a living public official use official, nonpartisan pages, and prefer people whose story is settled history. No living officeholder is the subject of a biography day.
- Do not label a person by identity unless their own words or a reliable source do.
- Where a course names Native nations, do not let one nation carry several units: vary the nations, each from its own sources, and never list a nation's present-day home as a place "around the world".
- The sensitivity study list in field 10 names at least every source the frame and the scope and sequence name for the unit's topics, then adds the community's own sources. Study every source the frame names for the unit, and record each as reached or not reached.
