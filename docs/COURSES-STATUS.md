# Course status: which layers exist for every course

Generated by scripts/course_status.py on 2026-11-13. Do not edit by hand; run the script again.

**What this is.** A status table, not a plan. It shows the gap between what exists and a finished course. Every course on it is to be finished to the bar (Cory, 2026-11-11). The plan with sizes and dates is written after the grade 4 math pilot unit shows what one finished unit costs (ROADMAP step 4f).

**How courses are counted.** As a school would schedule them: math, English language arts, science and social studies by grade and then by named high school course; every other subject by discipline and grade band; world languages by language. The catalog has 93 pages (a subject at a grade or band), which is a different count: some pages hold several courses and some hold none.

**The layers** (docs/ROADMAP.md, "The layers"). Standards are loaded for every subject, the shared approach (docs/APPROACH.md) is a draft that serves every course, and every catalog page is shaped, so those have no column. The columns are the layers that differ by course:

- **Rubric file**: our own-words file of what a published review rubric asks. Where none is written, the bar for that course is only the general one in docs/ADOPTION-BAR.md, and for subjects with no published rubric the bar still has to be stated.
- **Approach**: the subject page that says how the shared approach is carried out.
- **Progression**: what the grade inherits and hands on.
- **Scope and sequence**: every teaching day of the year in order. "draft" means written and read by the expert; see ROADMAP for which have Cory's yes.
- **Plans**: unit plans and section plans on file, as a count.
- **Topics** and **Tools**: what the catalog holds. Tools are counted as spec / built / live. A spec or a built tool is not yet tied to a section plan anywhere except the trial sections.

No course has lessons, print, checks with keys or teacher pages yet. That is layer 7 and starts with the pilot unit.
