Eight hazard research cards

Shaking 1

Shaking can push a building sideways. Bracing strengthens vulnerable walls against that movement.

Source: bracing guidance, page 1, opening.

Shaking 2

Braces must connect parts securely to support the building. Our cardboard model cannot show whether a real building is safe.

Source: bracing guidance, page 1, anchoring tips.

Volcano 1

Scientists compare a volcano's new readings with its quieter readings. Changes can help them judge possible eruption activity.

Source: monitoring, background-measurement paragraph.

Volcano 2

Past eruptions and current monitoring help scientists consider possible outcomes. A forecast has uncertainty; monitoring does not stop an eruption.

Source: monitoring, history and final forecast paragraphs.

Tsunami 1

A tsunami warning tells people to act. Moving to a safe place can reduce exposure while waves continue.

Source: alerts, Warning paragraph.

Tsunami 2

A usable route leads outside the hazard zone, inland or to high ground. Strong natural warnings may leave no time to wait for an official message.

Source: preparation, Plan for Evacuation; alerts, natural-warning paragraph.

Flood 1

A diversion takes water from its usual route toward another route or storage. It can reduce floodwater reaching an exposed place.

Source: diversion guidance, definition and purpose.

Flood 2

Raising a building can reduce its exposure to floodwater. Walls can fail or be overtopped; protection depends on conditions.

Source: flood measures, Building Elevation and Ringwalls.
