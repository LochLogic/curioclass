Community and wetland source cards

Card S: dated community account

Seattle Public Utilities, April 8, 2026. Our Water Supply This Spring: What You Need to Know Source row: S06-Seattle. Original summary; municipal copyright remains.

S1. The city's water comes from the Cedar and Tolt River watersheds. S2. Reservoir supplies depend on rain and gradually melting mountain snow. S3. The April account expected enough summer water for people and river fish flows. S4. It supported wise water use while continuing to watch weather and reservoir levels. These are dated statements, not a report about today's supply.

Card W: scientific account

United States Environmental Protection Agency. Why are Wetlands Important? Source row: S06-wetlands. Original summary; no source passage or image copied.

W1. Wetlands can store water and release it more slowly. W2. Wetland plants and soil interact with water moving through the watershed. W3. Wetlands provide habitat and can improve water quality. Location: introduction, Wetlands and Nature, and Flood Protection. These functions do not establish drinking safety.

Words

A watershed is land draining to a common outlet. Wetlands have water covering or saturating soil for some or all of the year. Definition source: What is a Wetland?, S06-wetland-definition.
