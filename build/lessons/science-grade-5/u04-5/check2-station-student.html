Day 82 teacher-only station guide

Keep this guide and all answers off student pages. Read it before class.

Prepare and pretest

Make six trays before the day. Each holds six small plastic cups, three sealed 1 g salt doses, six 10 mL room-temperature water portions, stirrers and a spill tray. Provide one ruler with 1 mm marks, a scale reading whole grams, a magnet, a smooth metal piece, a clean teacher-checked soft mineral and a sealed liquid sample. Use known classroom materials, no unidentified found powders. Keep salt doses sealed until mixing; no tasting, smelling, warming or electrical testing. Conduction is supplied evidence only. Screen allergies before food salt. No dusty scratch tests today.

Goggles for everyone near the tray; observers stay beyond splashing distance. Two sinks, soap, towels and wipes are ready. Each child has their own goggles. Pretest the small trials and the walking, scale and recording routine with the actual equipment. If four minutes does not hold a group's acts safely, stop that visit and record the unfinished act as not yet observed; never rush or add a later meeting. Keep all safe actual results, including grains that fail to dissolve.

Students may choose a ruler measurement of the mineral or metal, a whole-gram scale reading of a contained sample, or water volume with the marked measure. Reading resolution stays explicit: ruler to nearest 1 mm or one tenth cm; scale to 1 g; water to 1 mL marks. Each child obtains one actual measurement and one qualitative property. Mass/weight distinction and density are outside this task. The teacher keeps actual sample identities and privately verifies observations against the specimens.

Four observed minutes per group

Waiting children independently write their proposed comparisons and reference identifications. They are not rehearsed as a complete sample inventory.

  • 30 seconds: five children reconcile independent plans. Agree on salt addition versus water controls, equal water amounts, identical treatment salt doses, matched stirring/interval and three repeated pairs. Teacher intervenes for safety only. An unsafe or confounded proposal is retained, then supplied safe steps allow conducting with help. Do not award an independent planning result from the supplied plan.
  • 100 seconds: five personal measuring/property turns, twenty seconds each. Observe zero/alignment or tare, sample reading and property note. Record the child's act, unit and assistance. Each child signs their own measurement.
  • 75 seconds: three consecutive 25-second paired trials, with prepared equal water portions. Children 1/2 handle treatment/control for pair 1; children 3/4 for pair 2; children 5/1 for pair 3. For each: the named treatment child adds the dose and stirs twenty seconds; the control child stirs its water for the same twenty seconds. All observe five seconds afterward. The named actors record their own role. The clock standardizes the investigation interval, not children's work speed.
  • 20 seconds: each child marks all six resulting cells using their shared observations; complete longer notes afterward.
  • 15 seconds: verify every child's name, measuring act and mixing action, then close salt containers.

Total: 240 seconds. Three sixty-second comparisons would not fit this window; the 60-second powder test printed on side 1 is a separate invented reference record, never the live trial interval. If a child needs longer for access, preserve the actual unfinished status and use the paper route without personal-practical credit.

Six visits use 24 minutes. Four minutes total transitions allow tray changes and wiping. Finished groups wash at two sinks during the next group's visit: five children in three twenty-second waves, one minute per group. Final group washes in the cleanup block; earlier groups have already washed. Two further cleanup minutes close/empty cups, wipe surfaces and clean goggles. Adult empties the small salt-water amounts. Keep sink paths clear of station paths.

Failure and no-kit fallback

Retry a failed reading once within its turn. Use a larger contained portion if the scale shows zero and time permits. Keep the failed attempt; if still unreadable, use a ruler route or record missing. Never write a finer value than an instrument shows.

Announce: "These supplied readings are invented for practice." F82: sample P is white grains, 9 g on a whole-gram scale; mineral R is dull and 5 cm long on a 1 cm ruler. Three pairs start with clear water, with visible white grains added only to the salt cups. After twenty seconds, all three treatment and control cups look clear. These are constructed records, not expected class results or evidence that a child handled equipment. Personal measurement and conducting stay not yet observed.

The independent follow-up in c2-E82-claim is invented, separate from the live trials. No drying occurs during the station. Complete causal answers keep class results and the follow-up distinct. Equal recovery properties are consistent with retained salt, not a universal rule that a reversible change cannot form new material.

What the teacher records

Use check2-record.md: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Observe actions rather than hear thirty explanations. Each child returns independent written reasoning. Public talk is optional; no serial oral turns. Missing units receive a descriptive note; error U applies only if a copied reading is claimed as personal evidence.

A new reference inventory is printed on the student page. Reference letters A to H identify materials within the invented set. Matching two properties justifies the match in that set, without proving a unique identity among every possible substance. Do not supply material names from color alone.
