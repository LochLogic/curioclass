warm-ups05-P79-1MA.USI.T5.3 (whole)Name one place where the Emancipation Proclamation freed no one at once, and say why.A loyal slave state (Kentucky, Maryland, Missouri, Delaware), Tennessee, which the Proclamation never named, or a named exception (13 Louisiana parishes, West Virginia, 7 Virginia counties): it applied only to areas in rebellion.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. a correct place
  2. the reason: only areas in rebellion were covered
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Kentucky, because it stayed in the Union and the Proclamation covered only states in rebellion.
  • Georgia, because Lincoln forgot it.
Georgia, because Lincoln forgot it.: error C, lincoln freed everyone immediately
examples05-K79-1MA.USI.T5.4 (whole)Worked example: Grant at Vicksburg. Use Source C and Source F to name Grant's decision, its immediate effect and its lasting effect.Decision: move his army down the river and inland, away from his supply line. Immediate: the city and its army surrendered on July 4, 1863. Lasting: with Port Hudson's surrender on July 9, 1863, the Union held the Mississippi and split the Confederacy.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. Read the Vicksburg row of Source F: the city stands on the Mississippi River
  2. Read Grant's sentence and its date: he wrote it in 1885
  3. Name the decision
  4. Name the same-month effect
  5. Name the effect that lasted to the end of the war
  6. Limit: a memoir written years later is the general's own view
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Grant gave up his supply line to get behind Vicksburg; the city surrendered on July 4, 1863; after Port Hudson fell on July 9 the Union held the river.
  • Vicksburg ended the war.
practices05-P79-2MA.USI.T5.4 (whole)Leaders and battles organizer, alone: for each of Davis, Lee and Grant, one decision and its result; for each of Antietam, Gettysburg and Vicksburg, one immediate and one lasting effect.Davis: the 1862 draft law; Lee: invasions of Maryland and Pennsylvania; Grant: the Vicksburg campaign. Each battle with a same-season and a longer effect.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. a decision for each leader
  2. immediate and lasting effects for all three battles
  3. no battle claimed to end the war alone
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Davis drafted men in 1862 to keep armies full; Lee invaded twice and was turned back twice; Grant took Vicksburg. Antietam: invasion stopped, then preliminary emancipation. Gettysburg: invasion stopped, Lee never invaded the North again. Vicksburg: the river held after Port Hudson fell on July 9, the Confederacy split.
  • All three battles were turning points that won the war.
exit (paper)s05-X79MA.USI.T5.4 (whole)Name where one decision of Davis, Lee or Grant played out, and say what the decision was. Compare one immediate effect of two of the three battles. Then read Source I, a clause you have not seen: explain how it extended federal power, and use Source H to say whose land it was.A leader tied to a decision and a named place; two battle effects compared; the Pacific Railway Act's grant (Source I) shown giving a company federal land along the line, across land Native nations still held, with the United States' promise to extinguish their titles (Source E), and at least one nation named from Source H.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. a leader tied to a decision and a named place
  2. two battles compared by immediate effect
  3. Source I shown extending federal power
  4. the land named as a named nation's land (Source H), not empty land
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Lee chose to enter Maryland in September 1862, and his army was turned back at Antietam. Antietam turned back Lee's invasion at once; at Vicksburg the city and its army surrendered on July 4. In Source I Congress grants the company every other section of public land along the line, so the federal government decided who would own that land. The Pacific Railway Act gave the railroad companies land and a right of way and promised to extinguish the "Indian titles", so the federal government planned to take land that the Cheyenne, Arapaho and Shoshone still held, as Source H and its gloss show.
  • All three battles ended the war; the government gave away empty land.
One battle ended the war.: error M, gettysburg ended the war; Antietam and Gettysburg had the same effect.: error N, the three battles were interchangeable, with the same effect; The government gave away empty land.: error K, western land was empty when the government gave it away (Native nations erased from the land the laws granted)
