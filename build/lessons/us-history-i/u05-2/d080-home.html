Day 80: Service and the limits of a source

At home

GoalDocument the men and women who fought or served, the United States Colored Troops among them, from diaries, newspapers, photographs and cartoons

The questions

WhereQuestionAnswer
Worked example 2 (side 1)Worked example: Source A, Frederick Douglass, Men of Color, To Arms! (1863). Who made it, when, for whom and why? What service does it ask for, and what can it not tell us?Douglass, a formerly enslaved abolitionist, March 1863, to Black men in the free states, to recruit them for the 54th Massachusetts; it asks for armed service; it cannot show who joined or how they were treated.
  1. Who made it
  2. When, and what had just happened (the Proclamation, January 1863)
  3. For whom and why: a recruiting call
  4. What kind of source: a call to act, not a record of events
  5. What it shows about service
  6. What it cannot tell us
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Douglass wrote it in March 1863 to recruit Black men after the Proclamation opened the army; it shows what he hoped service would win, not what soldiers met.
  • It proves every Black man joined the army.
Four-source organizer 3 (side 2)Four-source organizer: for each of Sources B, C, D and E, write who made it, when and for whom; one kind of service it shows; and one thing it cannot tell you.B journal, Cumming, published 1866 about 1862, nursing; C letter, Tubman, dictated June 1863 for friends in the North, the Combahee raid she guided and the men who then joined; D photograph, between 1863 and 1866, a Black infantry company; E cartoon, Nast, 1865, a Black veteran's claim to vote. Limits: one nurse's view, written up for readers after the war; Tubman's own claim to credit, written to win support; a posed photograph; an argument, not a record.
Look-fors:
  1. maker, date and audience for all four
  2. a kind of service for each
  3. a limit tied to the source for each
  4. the cartoon read as an argument
Samples (ours, untested):
  • For all four: the maker, date and audience, the service shown, and a limit tied to who made it, when or why; the cartoon is Nast arguing for the vote.
  • The photograph shows what the soldiers felt.
the ticket questionOpen Source F, a letter you have not seen. Use it and one of today's other sources: give one detail from each that shows how men or women served or what they served for, and name the kind of source each is. Then give one thing Source F cannot tell you, and say why.Two cited details of service, one from Source F and one from another source, each with its kind; one limit of Source F tied to who made it, when or why.
Look-fors:
  1. a detail from Source F, cited
  2. a detail from another source, cited
  3. the kind of each source named
  4. one limit of Source F tied to the maker, the date or the purpose
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Spotswood Rice, a private in a Black infantry regiment, writes to his daughters in 1864 that he will free them if it costs his life, so he served to win his family's freedom. Tubman's letter says she and the soldiers brought 756 people away on the Combahee and most able men joined the army. Rice's letter cannot tell us whether he got his daughters back, because it was written before he tried.
  • The cartoon shows exactly what happened to the soldier, so it is a record.
