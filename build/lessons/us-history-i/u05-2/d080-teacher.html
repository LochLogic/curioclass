Creditours

Before class

Print the listed pieces; fold Source F under on every sheet. Sort day 79's exit tickets by look-for. Write comments on day 79's organizers for day 82. Finish written feedback on essay 1 draft 1 for day 81.

The day

5 min. Warm-up

Return day 79's exit; read two answers to day 79's s05-P79-1 aloud and judge them together. Students answer Ask. Which 1863 event split Confederate territory, and which ended Lee's second invasion of the North? [Vicksburg split the Confederacy; Gettysburg ended the second invasion.] alone. Expect Vicksburg, then Gettysburg. If more than a third of the exits showed error D, M or N, use this warm-up to sort the three offices and put the battles in order from day 79's Source F. Error K goes to a small group during the pairs part: read day 79's Sources E and H, Source E's "Indian titles" beside Source H and its gloss; name two nations whose land the 1862 railway grant crossed, and say which source shows each. The worked example keeps its ten minutes.

10 min. Worked example

Read Source A and its source line aloud. Students attempt Ask. Worked example: Source A, Frederick Douglass, Men of Color, To Arms! (1863). Who made it, when, for whom and why? What service does it ask for, and what can it not tell us? [Douglass, a formerly enslaved abolitionist, March 1863, to Black men in the free states, to recruit them for the 54th Massachusetts; it asks for armed service; it cannot show who joined or how they were treated.] before the model:

  1. Who made it
  2. When, and what had just happened (the Proclamation, January 1863)
  3. For whom and why: a recruiting call
  4. What kind of source: a call to act, not a record of events
  5. What it shows about service
  6. What it cannot tell us

Douglass, who had escaped slavery himself, spoke in March 1863 to Black men in the free states, after the Proclamation opened the army to them. Point to the words that recruit. A call to serve cannot show who joined or how they were treated. Students fill the frame on side 1.

20 min. Pairs: the four-source organizer

Read Sources B and C aloud with their glosses; show D and E with their descriptions. Content note: wounded men, and a veteran who lost a leg. Ask no one to share family stories. Cumming nursed Confederate wounded; the army she served fought for slavery. Source C is Tubman's own letter: she guided Colonel Montgomery's Black soldiers of the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers up the Combahee, and 756 people came away to freedom. She asks credit for "we colored people." United States Colored Troops was the army's name for Black regiments. Black soldiers were paid less than white soldiers until Congress made pay equal in 1864, and captured Black soldiers faced death or enslavement, as at Fort Pillow in 1864.

Pairs complete Ask. Four-source organizer: for each of Sources B, C, D and E, write who made it, when and for whom; one kind of service it shows; and one thing it cannot tell you. [B journal, Cumming, published 1866 about 1862, nursing; C letter, Tubman, dictated June 1863 for friends in the North, the Combahee raid she guided and the men who then joined; D photograph, between 1863 and 1866, a Black infantry company; E cartoon, Nast, 1865, a Black veteran's claim to vote. Limits: one nurse's view, written up for readers after the war; Tubman's own claim to credit, written to win support; a posed photograph; an argument, not a record.]: one partner writes rows B and C, the other D and E; then each copies the partner's two rows and checks one limit with them. Cumming's journal describes 1862 but was published in 1866: give both dates. The photograph shows a posed company, not feelings. The cartoon argues for the vote; it is not one man's record. Collect the organizers after the share.

5 min. Share

Ask. Which of Sources B to E answers something Source A cannot show? Expect: Source C and Source D show Black men did serve; Source E asks what their service should earn. Ask. What can the photograph still not tell you? Expect: why they joined or what they felt. Two pairs read one limit each; the class sorts it into who made it, when, or why.

10 min. Exit ticket

Students unfold Source F, a letter they have not seen. Read its source line and first sentence aloud, neutrally; it keeps Rice's spelling. Students answer Open Source F, a letter you have not seen. Use it and one of today's other sources: give one detail from each that shows how men or women served or what they served for, and name the kind of source each is. Then give one thing Source F cannot tell you, and say why. [Two cited details of service, one from Source F and one from another source, each with its kind; one limit of Source F tied to who made it, when or why.] alone on the ticket. Accept dictated answers, scribed without hints. Collect tickets for day 82.

Other rooms

Paper is the main path. Without a printer, copy the passages and the organizer onto the board, and read the image descriptions aloud. One projector can show the two images large.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: one pair reports in the share; keep the model, all four organizer rows and the exit. Longer: set Source A's promise beside Source E's question: by 1865, what had service earned?

Words and supports

Gloss franchise as the right to vote, and sourcing as asking who made it, when, for whom and why. Read in chunks. Frame: "This source shows , but it cannot show because (who made it, when, or for whom)." Take the frame away for the exit. Accept spoken reasons, so a language gap is not marked as error O.

Tomorrow

Tick the exit's four look-fors. Day 81 is a writing day: its warm-up judges Which 1863 event split Confederate territory, and which ended Lee's second invasion of the North? [Vicksburg split the Confederacy; Gettysburg ended the second invasion.] aloud, then essay 1 draft 2. The organizer, s05-P80-2, and this exit come back in day 82's warm-up. Flag error O there (feelings read from a picture, the cartoon or Rice's letter read past what it shows) and error E (women's service left out); if more than a third show error O, that warm-up sorts what Source D shows and cannot show, then Source B.
