Day 81: Revise the reasoning

At home

GoalEssay 1, draft 2: revise from written feedback
Time40 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No printer: read the model pair aloud from one copy.

Skip. The warm-up.

  • 5 min. Model. Read the model pair together. Say. "What changed in meaning, not just in words?" Expect: the claim says who, when and why it matters.
  • 30 min. Writing. The child works alone on Writing: draft 2, question 2 (side 1) with draft 1, the feedback and the packet open. Help only when asked. Say. "What can your evidence not show?" Expect: a limit tied to a source.
  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone. Keep everything in the folder.

The questions

Writing: draft 2, question 2 (side 1)Essay 1, draft 2: revise from your written feedback. Change your claim and your counterclaim, not only the words. Keep draft 1. Then write a revision note: what you changed, which evidence made you change it, and one limit that remains.A revised draft with claim and counterclaim changed in substance, draft 1 kept, and a revision note that names the evidence and a limit.
Look-fors:
  1. D3.4 claim: more precise about who, when and what, tied to evidence, and why it matters to the question
  2. D3.4 counterclaim: stated at its strongest, then answered with evidence
  3. D3.4 limits: what each side cannot show
  4. D4.1: a counterclaim answered in each section
  5. D3.3: the inconsistency the feedback named is dealt with
  6. revision note names the evidence for each change
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Claim now names leaders' fear for slavery's future in the territories, from the declaration; counterclaim says the election set the timing and is answered in each section; note names the declaration and the map and says official sources speak for leaders, not every resident.
  • Spelling fixed, two quotations added, claim unchanged.
the ticket questionCopy one sentence of your claim and one of your counterclaim from draft 1 and from draft 2. Say how the feedback and the evidence changed their precision, their limits and why the claim matters to the question.A real change to both claim and counterclaim, explained by evidence, with a limit of each; draft comparison and feedback kept.
Look-fors:
  1. both claim and counterclaim changed in substance
  2. the feedback and the evidence behind each change named
  3. a limit of each stated
  4. why the claim matters to the question
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Draft 1 said the war was about slavery. Draft 2 says leaders seceded to protect slavery's future in the territories and their power in Washington, from the Mississippi declaration; it cannot show what every voter wanted. My counterclaim now says the election set the timing, which is true, but not the cause.
