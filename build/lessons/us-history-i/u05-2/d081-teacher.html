Before class

Write feedback on each draft 1 that names four things, one line each: the claim, the counterclaim, the inconsistency to deal with, and why the claim matters to the question. Print the model draft pair. Lay out the essay 1 folders.

The day

5 min. Warm-up

Read two answers to day 80's s05-P80-1 aloud and judge them together. Day 80's organizer and exit stay with you for day 82. Return essay 1 draft 1 from day 77 with its feedback. Students answer Ask. Read the written feedback on your draft 1. Copy the one sentence the feedback questions (claim or counterclaim), and name the evidence it asks for. [The questioned sentence copied and the evidence named.] alone. Expect the sentence the feedback questions, copied, and the evidence it asks for, not praise. Note who needs a conference.

5 min. What revision is

Show the model draft pair. It answers the day 75 question about South Carolina's declaration, not essay 1. Ask. Which changes are in meaning, and which are in words only? Expect: draft 2 names who, when and why it matters, states the counterclaim at its strongest and names a limit; "seceded from the Union" to "left the Union" is words only. Changing words alone is error I. Put the pair away before writing.

30 min. Writing: draft 2

Students work alone on Ask. Essay 1, draft 2: revise from your written feedback. Change your claim and your counterclaim, not only the words. Keep draft 1. Then write a revision note: what you changed, which evidence made you change it, and one limit that remains. [A revised draft with claim and counterclaim changed in substance, draft 1 kept, and a revision note that names the evidence and a limit.], with draft 1, the feedback and the P1 packet open, on lined paper. They use only the packet's sources, cite each by id and detail, and paraphrase the historians with their names, never quoting them.

Confer first with the students you noted. Ask each to point to the feedback, the evidence and the sentence it changes. Expect a claim more exact about who, when and what, and why it matters to the question; a counterclaim at its strongest, answered in each section; the inconsistency the feedback named, dealt with; and a limit of each side. Slavery is named plainly; an election's timing does not replace it. A counterclaim is not a Lost Cause claim to balance.

Students write the revision note in the box on their page as they go. For error I, show the model pair again in a conference, then send the student back to their own sentence. Do not supply a claim.

10 min. Exit ticket

Students answer Copy one sentence of your claim and one of your counterclaim from draft 1 and from draft 2. Say how the feedback and the evidence changed their precision, their limits and why the claim matters to the question. [A real change to both claim and counterclaim, explained by evidence, with a limit of each; draft comparison and feedback kept.] alone, with both drafts and the feedback open. They copy four sentences, the claim and counterclaim from each draft, and say what changed and why. Accept dictated answers. Collect draft 2, the revision note and the exit together; draft 1, the feedback and Read the written feedback on your draft 1. Copy the one sentence the feedback questions (claim or counterclaim), and name the evidence it asks for. [The questioned sentence copied and the evidence named.] stay in the essay folder.

Other rooms

Paper is the main path; the P1 packet is already in each folder. Without a printer, read the model pair aloud and write its two claims on the board.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: name one change in the model pair, not all of them; keep the writing time and the exit. Longer: tie a second source detail to a changed sentence in the same draft.

Words and supports

Precision means saying who, when and what. A counterclaim is the strongest other explanation; a limit is what the evidence cannot show; significance is why the claim matters to the question. In conferences, use: "Evidence changes my claim by ; my counterclaim explains but cannot explain ." Read the feedback aloud and allow dictation.

Tomorrow

Tick the exit's four look-fors and W81's six on the record sheet; flag error I when only words change. Day 82 returns day 80's organizer and exit in its warm-up, then tests the Lost Cause against the wartime record. Write feedback on draft 2 and the revision note during days 82 and 83; it comes back in day 84's warm-up.
