Day 81: one draft pair, two kinds of change

This pair answers the day 75 question about South Carolina's declaration of December 24, 1860. It is not about your essay 1 question. Read draft 1, then draft 2, and mark every change: M for a change in meaning, W for a change in words only.

Draft 1

The South left the Union because of slavery. South Carolina's declaration talks about slavery a lot, which proves it. Some people say the election caused secession, but they are wrong because the declaration is about slavery. So slavery was the cause of the war and that is why South Carolina seceded from the Union in 1860.

Draft 2

South Carolina's convention left the Union in December 1860 to protect slavery's future, and its own declaration says so: it names the Northern states' hostility to slavery as its grievance. Some argue Lincoln's election caused secession, and the timing supports them, since the convention met weeks after the vote. But the declaration treats the election as the last proof of a danger to slavery, not as the danger itself. The declaration speaks for the convention's delegates, not for every South Carolinian, so it shows the leaders' reasons, not the whole state's.

What changed. The claim now says who (the convention), when (December 1860) and why it matters (it is the seceders' own reason). The counterclaim is stated at its strongest before it is answered. A limit is named. One change is words only: "seceded from the Union" became "left the Union."
