Day 78: How emancipation changed the Union's war aims: key

Each answer is worked out in full. The last column names the wrong answers to expect and the error each one shows.

warm-ups05-P78-1MA.USI.T5.3 (whole)What did the Union say it was fighting for in 1861? Was it the same in 1863? Give one reason.In 1861, restoring the Union; by 1863 freedom in areas in rebellion had become a war aim, pushed by people who escaped slavery, Black leaders and the needs of the war.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. the 1861 aim: the Union
  2. a change by 1863, with one reason
Samples (ours, untested):
  • In 1861 the North said it fought to restore the Union; by 1863 freeing people in the rebel states was a war aim, because people who escaped to Union lines forced the question.
  • It was always to end slavery, from the first day.
It was always to end slavery, from the first day.: error J, the Union's war aims never changed: it fought to end slavery from the first day, or never did
examples05-K78-1MA.USI.T5.3 (whole)Worked example: Source B, Lincoln to Horace Greeley, August 1862. What does Lincoln say his aim is, what does his closing sentence add, and what did he not say in the letter?His public aim, his official duty, is saving the Union, by freeing all, some or none; his closing sentence keeps his personal wish that all men everywhere could be free; he did not say he had already drafted an emancipation order.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. Read the source line: who, when, to whom, published where
  2. Find the aim sentence
  3. Name the three choices he lists
  4. Read the closing sentence: official duty against personal wish
  5. Ask what the reader could not know: the draft order of July 1862
  6. Limit the claim: the letter shows his public stance in August 1862, not his whole view
Samples (ours, untested):
  • Lincoln says his official aim is the Union and he would free all, some or none to save it, but he ends by saying his personal wish is that all men everywhere could be free; the letter was public, written a month after he had shown his cabinet a draft order, so it shows his public position, not his plan.
  • Lincoln did not care about slavery at all.
practices05-P78-2MA.USI.T5.3 (whole)Place Sources A, B, C and D on the timeline from April 1861 to January 1863. Beside each, write what the Union was fighting for at that point and who was pushing for a change.A blockade, April 1861: restore the Union by war on Confederate trade. D Fortress Monroe, May 1861: people escaping slavery force the question. B Greeley letter, August 1862: Union first, freeing used as a means. C Proclamation, January 1863: freedom in areas in rebellion and Black men into the army.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. all four placed in order
  2. an aim beside each
  3. at least one push from enslaved people or Black leaders
Samples (ours, untested):
  • A then D then B then C; the aim moves from restoring the Union to freedom as a war measure, pushed by people who fled to Union lines.
  • All four say the same thing: free the slaves.
exit (paper)s05-X78MA.USI.T5.3 (whole)Use Source E (Lincoln to James Conkling, 1863), Source A and what you learned today to connect the blockade, the change in Union war aims and emancipation. Then name one place the Proclamation did not reach, and one obstacle Lincoln faced.The blockade and emancipation were both war measures against the Confederacy; by 1863 Black soldiers and freedom were part of the Union's aims; the Proclamation covered only areas in rebellion and needed armies to enforce it, and opposition and uncertain battles constrained Lincoln.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. the blockade named as a war measure
  2. the change in aims from 1861 to 1863, tied to Source E
  3. emancipation linked to Black military service
  4. one place the Proclamation did not reach, with the reason
  5. one obstacle Lincoln faced
Samples (ours, untested):
  • In 1861 Lincoln blockaded Southern ports to choke the Confederacy and said his aim was the Union. By 1863 he writes that Black soldiers fight for freedom and the promise must be kept, so ending slavery had become a war aim. The Proclamation did not free people in Kentucky, a loyal slave state, because it covered only areas in rebellion. Conkling's meeting shows the obstacle: many Union voters disliked the Proclamation.
  • On January 1, 1863, Lincoln freed every enslaved person in the country by his order.
Lincoln freed every enslaved person in the country on January 1, 1863.: error C, lincoln freed everyone immediately; military victory alone explains emancipation; The Union fought to end slavery from the first day.: error J, the Union's war aims never changed: it fought to end slavery from the first day, or never did
