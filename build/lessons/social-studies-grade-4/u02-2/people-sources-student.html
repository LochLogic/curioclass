People and languages: short source cards

Shared introduction, teacher reads

First Nations, Inuit and Métis are living, distinct Indigenous peoples in Canada. The Haida Nation's council describes its work protecting its culture today. An Inuit organization describes its work for Inuit rights and interests. Mexico has many Indigenous peoples and languages. A binational Indigenous organization describes rights and language work in Mexico and the United States. Mexico's constitution recognizes Afro-Mexican peoples and communities.

Credits: original summaries of the named peoples' organizations and Mexico's constitution, Article 2. No words, images or cultural material are copied.

Canada research card, print for this path

Maker: Statistics Canada. Census year: 2021. Kind: printed digital summary. About 237,420 Indigenous people reported speaking an Indigenous language well enough for conversation. This is a language finding, not Canada's total population.

Credit: Indigenous languages, 2023, facts paraphrased.

Mexico research card, print for this path

Maker: INEGI. Census year: 2020. Kind: printed digital summary. The count was 7,364,645 residents aged three or older speaking an Indigenous language. The language institute names Zapotec languages in Oaxaca and southern Veracruz. These examples do not describe everyone in Mexico.

Credits: 2020 census; language institute. Facts paraphrased.
