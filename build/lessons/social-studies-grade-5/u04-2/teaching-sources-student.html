Section 4.2 teaching selections

Day 47: dated action fragments

Trenton, New Jersey, December 1776: Washington's forces surprised Hessian troops fighting for Britain. The victory renewed American prospects after setbacks. Saratoga, New York, 1777: American forces compelled a British surrender. This encouraged the formal French alliance in 1778; some French help came earlier. Read each record separately beside its labeled teaching map.

Day 48: separate logistics

Valley Forge, Pennsylvania, winter 1777 to 1778: soldiers faced shortages and illness. Training helped the army operate more effectively. Yorktown, Virginia, 1781: allied land forces and French naval support surrounded Cornwallis. His force surrendered; peace followed in 1783. Read each logistics note separately, not as a combined response.

Credits: s04-T-valley; s04-T-york; s04-T-french; s04-T-jadams. Facts paraphrased; wording ours.

Day 49: resource inventory

French assistance preceded the formal 1778 alliance. Dutch merchants connected American buyers with arms supplies; bankers supplied finance. In 1779, cattle from Texas fed Spanish troops fighting Britain along the Gulf. Britain faced an Atlantic supply journey and a widening war. Record resource, origin, and date separately. Do not show the held route card.

Credits: s04-T-french; s04-T-dutch; s04-T-spanish. Facts paraphrased; wording ours.

Day 50: separate choices

Loyalists supported Britain. Neutral people did not join either side; motives need evidence. The Onondaga timeline records Haudenosaunee neutrality agreed at Fort Pitt in 1775. The Oneida Indian Nation describes help at Fort Stanwix in 1777. Free and enslaved Black people participated on both sides; service did not guarantee freedom. Nations continue today; a national alliance never proves every individual's choice. Made-up household: a notice records no enlistment. It gives no reason. Do not invent its motive or assign a student's identity to it.

Credits: s04-T-oneida; s04-T-onondaga; s04-T-black. Facts paraphrased; household invented.

Day 51: eight separate profiles

John Adams argued forcefully for independence in Congress. Thomas Jefferson drafted the Declaration with a committee. Benjamin Franklin sought French assistance for the American cause. George III opposed independence; Parliament also shaped British policy. George Washington led the army through setbacks. Lafayette provided muskets to soldiers in need. Alexander Hamilton urged another convention after Annapolis in 1786. James Madison helped propose a trade meeting in 1786. Keep each historical action on a separate label; do not compare the held pairs.

Made-up model: a writing team clarifies directions; a supply team distributes paper. One makes the message usable; the other makes materials available.
