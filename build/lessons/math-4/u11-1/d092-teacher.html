Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips and the key. Have the 36-foot string loop and grid paper out. Put the display page on the projector. Write the two warm-up problems on the board.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: two perimeters

Alone, on the back of the grid paper. A rectangle is 264 feet long and 129 feet wide. Find its perimeter. [786 feet] and A rectangle is 2 3/5 inches long and 1 4/5 inches wide. Find its perimeter. [8 4/5 inches; 7 9/5 is also right]. Students add the length and the width, then add the sum to itself, as on day 91. Go over both at 5 minutes. A sum not doubled (393, or 4 2/5) is error B: trace the four sides on the board.

20 min. The dog pen, then teaching first

10 minutes: the task, in pairs (A dog pen is a rectangle. It uses all 36 feet of fence. Which pen gives the dog the most room? [9 feet by 9 feet: 81 square feet. Every pen's length plus width is 18]). Read the task aloud once. Two volunteers hold the string loop as a long thin pen, then a square one: same string, which dog has more room? Pairs sketch pens on grid paper and fill the table on side 1: length, width, length plus width, room inside. Push for whole feet only. Watch for pairs who change the fence while changing the pen (a 10 by 10 pen): count its sides with them.

Close it whole class, from the pairs' tables, smallest pen first:

  1. Every pen uses all 36 feet: two lengths and two widths.
  2. So one length and one width use half the fence: 18 feet.
  3. Every pen has length + width = 18: 1 and 17, 2 and 16, and so on up to 9 and 9.
  4. Room for the dog is the inside: the area, length times width.
  5. 1 × 17 = 17, 5 × 13 = 65, 8 × 10 = 80, 9 × 9 = 81.
  6. The 9 by 9 pen gives the most room: 81 square feet.

Ask. "Every pen used 36 feet of fence. What is the same about the length plus the width of each? Why?" Expect: "they all make 18"; then "the fence is two lengths and two widths, so one of each is half". Take the first, then press for the second.

Ask. "Two rectangles have the same perimeter. Must they have the same area?" Expect "no": point at 1 by 17 (17 square feet) and 9 by 9 (81).

10 minutes: two worked examples, whole class. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go.

Example 1, times as long (A rug is 7 feet wide. Its length is 2 times its width. How much floor is under the rug? [Length 2 × 7 = 14 feet; area 14 × 7 = 98 square feet]).

  1. Sketch the rug: 7 feet wide, the length unknown.
  2. "2 times its width" means 2 × 7, not 7 + 2. The rug is 14 feet long.
  3. The floor under the rug is the inside of the rectangle: the area.
  4. Area is length times width: 14 × 7 = 98.
  5. The rug is on 98 square feet of floor. Square feet, because it is area.
  6. Check: 14 is 2 sevens, and 98 is 14 rows of 7.

"2 times as long" is 2 × 7, not 7 + 2: the same as stretch 10's comparing. A child who writes 9 is adding.

Example 2, two steps (A chicken run takes up 72 square feet of the yard. It is 8 feet wide. How much fence does it need? [8 × ? = 72, so 9 feet long; fence 9 + 8 + 9 + 8 = 34 feet]).

  1. Sketch the run: 8 feet wide, the length unknown, 72 square feet inside.
  2. Step 1: the 72 square feet is the inside, so 8 × ? = 72. 8 × 9 = 72: the run is 9 feet long.
  3. Step 2: fence runs along the edge of the run. That is the perimeter.
  4. 9 + 8 + 9 + 8 = 34, or 2 × (9 + 8) = 34.
  5. The run needs 34 feet of fence. Feet, because it is a length.

Say the question in each step: "What is this number, the inside or the edge?"

20 min. Six problems by hand

Alone, side 2. Read each problem aloud once, all six at the start, then students work. For each, students write "area" or "perimeter" first, sketch on the grid paper, then the answer with its unit. None of the six says "covers" or "around": the story tells which. Problems 3 and 5 are "times as long". A kitchen floor is 9 feet long and 6 feet wide. Each tile is 1 foot by 1 foot. How many tiles fill the floor? [9 × 6 = 54 tiles (area, 54 square feet)] Zoe makes a rectangle out of tape on the gym floor. It is 14 feet long and 9 feet wide. How much tape does she use? [14 + 9 + 14 + 9 = 46 feet (perimeter)] A bench seat is 2 feet wide. It is 6 times as long as it is wide. Mom paints the top of the seat. How much does she paint? [Length 6 × 2 = 12 feet; 12 × 2 = 24 square feet (area)] A dog run is 6 feet wide. It has 42 square feet of ground. How much fence closes it in? [6 × ? = 42, so 7 feet long; fence 7 + 6 + 7 + 6 = 26 feet] A flag is 9 inches wide. It is 2 times as long as it is wide. Gold trim is sewn along each side. How much trim is used? [Length 2 × 9 = 18 inches; 18 + 9 + 18 + 9 = 54 inches (perimeter)] Kai bends 24 inches of wire into a rectangle. It is 7 inches long. How wide is it? [7 + ? + 7 + ? = 24; 5 inches (the wire is the perimeter)]

After 15 minutes, pairs swap and check each answer against the story: "Is your number the inside or the edge?" A mismatch gets a question mark.

Watch for: a "times as long" length found by adding (8 for the bench, 11 for the flag); the wrong measure (30 tiles, 126 feet of tape, 162 inches of trim: error A); "54 feet" of tiles (error E); stopping at the side (7 for the dog run). If many choose the wrong measure: each pair sketches problem 1 and shades what the story asks for, the inside or the edge. If many add for "times as long": draw 2 bars of 9 for the flag.

5 min. Written task

Alone, the box at the foot of side 2: Look at problem 2. Did it need area or perimeter? How did you know? [Perimeter, 46 feet: the tape makes the sides, not the floor inside (look-fors and samples on the teacher page)]. Students may answer aloud to you instead (a scribe is allowed). Collect them; read after class.

Four look-fors, each ticked alone: (1) says what is measured, the inside or the edge; (2) names the measure; (3) the unit matches, feet for perimeter; (4) the reason comes from the situation, not from one word.

Meets (ours, untested): "Perimeter. The tape makes the lines of the rectangle. It does not fill the floor. 14 + 9 + 14 + 9 = 46 feet." Does not meet: "Area, because it says floor. 14 × 9 = 126 square feet." That answer rests on one word and measures the inside. A child who writes "perimeter because it is tape" has look-fors 2 and 3 only: ask "what does the tape do?"

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Which problem was hardest to decide? What in the story told you?" Expect the dog run or the flag. Show a problem where the answer came from the story first, then one from a word. Connect them: ask "inside or edge?" before any number.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper: A patio is 4 yards wide. Its length is 3 times its width. (a) Ana paints the whole patio floor. How much floor is that? (b) She hangs a string of lights along all four edges. How long is the string? Write the units. [48 square yards; 32 yards]. Read it aloud once. Write the results on the record sheet under E92.

Other rooms

  • No string loop. Draw a long thin pen and a square one on the board with 36 squares of edge each.
  • One projector. As in the defaults; the class writes each answer on side 2 before you show it.
  • One child at home. Make the 36-foot pens from a 36-inch string on a table, with paper squares for the room inside. Then do problems 1 to 3 together and 4 to 6 alone.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: stop the task's table at 6 pens and close it from there; drop the partner check (students check their own). Never drop the written task or the reading aloud. Longer: the challenge (A dog pen is 10 feet long and 2 feet wide. Draw a pen with more room but less fence. [For example 5 by 5: 25 square feet and 20 feet of fence. The 10 by 2 pen has 20 square feet and 24 feet of fence]), on the back of the grid paper.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square foot, times as long. Frames on the display: "This problem needs because the story asks about the ." "The length is times , so it is feet."

Tomorrow

Day 93 is the end check. On the slip, a length of 7 (so 28 and 22) is adding for "times as long": those students go to stretch 10's comparing help in the respond half. Swapped measures (32 for the floor, 48 for the string) is error A; "48 yards" is error E: both groups take their error's "if many" action. Hand back the written tasks at the door, each with its look-fors ticked; no class time is taken.
