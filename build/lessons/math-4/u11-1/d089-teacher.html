Day 89: Both measures of one rectangle

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the facts check part 1 sheet, the exit tickets and the key. Count 15 tiles or paper squares into a cup for each student. Have unit 10's end-of-unit assessments ready to hand back, items 7 and 8 marked. Put the display page on the projector.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 1

Alone. The 36 products from unit 1, untimed. Collect at 5 minutes; an item not reached is left blank, not marked wrong. Mark the sheets after class. Students under 32 of 36 keep their 9 by 9 table out from day 90.

5 min. Day 88's items 7 and 8

Hand back day 88's end-of-unit assessments. Show a "meets" answer for each. Item 7: "b = 20 + 4 × 20. 4 × 20 = 80. 20 + 80 = 100 beads." Item 8: "18 is about 20, and 7 × 7 is about 50, so about 70. 175 is far too big. s = 67." Students fix their own in pen beside the first try. Collect them for the record sheet. Many 80 on 7 (F): underline the question and ask "is 80 what it asked?" Many 175 on 8 (H): write Ravi's steps as two equations.

20 min. Teaching first: one rectangle

12 minutes together: build one rectangle (Build a rectangle 5 tiles long and 3 tiles wide. Find its area and its perimeter. [5 × 3 = 15 square units; 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units, or 2 × (5 + 3) = 16]). Each student builds along with tiles on grid paper and fills the frame on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. In the box on side 1, they sketch the 5 by 3 and label its sides. The display shows the figure, then each step.

  1. Build it: a row of 5 tiles, then 2 more rows of 5 under it. Each tile is one square unit.
  2. Area is how many squares cover the inside. Count by fives: 5, 10, 15.
  3. That is 3 rows of 5. Write it with the sides: A = l × w, so 5 × 3 = 15 square units.
  4. Perimeter is the length all the way around the edge. Run a finger along it and count the unit sides: 16.
  5. Write it side by side: P = l + w + l + w, so 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units.
  6. Stop halfway around: one length and one width, 5 + 3 = 8. The other half is the same 8.
  7. So P = 2 × (l + w): 2 × (5 + 3) = 2 × 8 = 16 units. The same answer.

Say as you point: area covers the inside, perimeter goes around the edge. Leave A = l × w, P = l + w + l + w and P = 2 × (l + w) on the board.

For you: 2 × l + 2 × w = 2 × (l + w) is the distributive property (section opener); students show it on the tiles, not by name.

Watch for a student who counts the squares along the edge instead of the unit sides (12, not 16: a corner square has two sides on the edge). Have them mark each unit side with a tick and count the ticks.

8 minutes in pairs. "Make any other rectangle from your 15 tiles. Each of you writes A = and both forms of P; swap and check that both forms give the same number."

15 min. On paper: three rectangles

"Three rectangles", side 1 (Draw a rectangle 6 units long and 2 units wide. Write both equations. [6 × 2 = 12 square units; 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16 units, or 2 × (6 + 2) = 16], Draw a rectangle 9 units long and 5 units wide. Write both equations. [9 × 5 = 45 square units; 9 + 5 + 9 + 5 = 28 units, or 2 × (9 + 5) = 28], Draw a rectangle 10 units long and 3 units wide. Write both equations. [10 × 3 = 30 square units; 10 + 3 + 10 + 3 = 26 units, or 2 × (10 + 3) = 26]). Alone. Students draw each one on grid paper, shade the inside, trace the edge, then write both equations with the units. For each perimeter they write the four sides first, then the 2 × form. Tiles stay on the desk only to check the 6 by 2.

Watch for a perimeter that is one length and one width (8, 14 or 13: error B), answers in the wrong rows (error A) and edge squares counted (24 or 22: error F). Ask: "Did you count the squares or the edge? Which one did the question ask for?" If many give one side pair: trace all four sides together and write l + w + l + w. If many swap them: sort the three drawings' numbers into "covers" and "goes around" on the board.

If the readiness check showed no gap (fewer than a third missed item 1 and fewer than a third missed item 2), this part is one rectangle (Draw a rectangle 6 units long and 2 units wide. Write both equations. [6 × 2 = 12 square units; 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16 units, or 2 × (6 + 2) = 16]), 5 minutes. Then "Missing side" on side 2 (A tray covers 42 square inches. It is 6 inches wide. How long is it? [6 × ? = 42; 7 inches]), alone, checked by multiplying back, 5 minutes. Then pairs start the planned question below on their own rectangle, 5 minutes; the class finishes it in "Pull it together".

5 min. On paper: fix two

"Fix it", side 2 (Ava says: "A rug is 6 feet long and 5 feet wide. Its area is 22 square feet." [6 × 5 = 30 square feet. Ava added the four sides: 22 feet is the perimeter], Ben says: "A card is 10 inches long and 7 inches wide. Its perimeter is 17 inches." [10 + 7 + 10 + 7 = 34 inches. Ben added one length and one width]). Pairs for one minute on Ava's, then the class. The display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Ava added the four sides: that is the edge, not the inside. Ben stopped halfway around.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Is 2 × (l + w) always the same as l + w + l + w? How do you know?" Expect, and show in this order: "It worked for every pair's" (true for those; ask "and one we have not drawn?"); "you go one length and one width, then the same again" (trace the tile rectangle to the halfway corner and stop); "it is two lengths and two widths either way". Close on the second: halfway around is l + w, and the other half matches.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, the exit ticket (A rectangle is 8 meters long and 3 meters wide. Find the area and the perimeter. Write the equation and the units for each. [8 × 3 = 24 square meters; 8 + 3 + 8 + 3 = 22 meters (2 × (8 + 3) is also right)]). Students write both equations and the units. Record on the record sheet under E89.

Other rooms

  • No tiles. Students shade 15 squares on grid paper instead; the build is the same.
  • One projector. You build at the front; the class builds first, then checks against yours.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the third rectangle and the pairs minute on Ava's. Never drop the four-side equation before the 2 × form, or the fixes. Longer: "Missing side" (A tray covers 42 square inches. It is 6 inches wide. How long is it? [6 × ? = 42; 7 inches]), then the challenge (Draw two rectangles with an area of 20 square units. Give them different perimeters. Find each perimeter. [Two of: 1 by 20, perimeter 42; 2 by 10, perimeter 24; 4 by 5, perimeter 18]).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square unit, formula, equation, each on a labelled rectangle. Frames on the display: "The area is square units because rows of cover it." "The perimeter is units because + + + = ." Access: large-square grid paper; tiles for touch.
