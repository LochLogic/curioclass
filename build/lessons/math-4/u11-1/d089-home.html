Day 89: Both measures of one rectangle

At home

GoalI can find both measures of one rectangle and write each as a formula
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. Tiles: 15 coins or paper squares. Grid paper: squares ruled on plain paper. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The facts check, day 88's items, rectangle 3 and the challenge.

  • 12 min. Build one rectangle. Together, do Build it (side 1) with the squares and fill its frame. Count the squares for the area; run a finger round the edge and count the sides for the perimeter: 16, not 12.

    Say. "Is 2 × (5 + 3) the same as 5 + 3 + 5 + 3? How do you know?" Expect: yes, one length and one width, then the same again.

  • 10 min. Two rectangles. The child makes another rectangle from the 15 squares and writes both measures. Then Three rectangles 1 (side 1) and 2 alone, four sides first.

  • 8 min. Fix two. Fix it 1 (side 2), then 2.

    Say. "Did Ava find the inside or the edge?" Expect: the edge, so 22 feet is the perimeter.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone. Keep it with the student page.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Build it (side 1)Build a rectangle 5 tiles long and 3 tiles wide. Find its area and its perimeter.5 × 3 = 15 square units; 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units, or 2 × (5 + 3) = 16
  1. Build it: a row of 5 tiles, then 2 more rows of 5 under it. Each tile is one square unit.
  2. Area is how many squares cover the inside. Count by fives: 5, 10, 15.
  3. That is 3 rows of 5. Write it with the sides: A = l × w, so 5 × 3 = 15 square units.
  4. Perimeter is the length all the way around the edge. Run a finger along it and count the unit sides: 16.
  5. Write it side by side: P = l + w + l + w, so 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units.
  6. Stop halfway around: one length and one width, 5 + 3 = 8. The other half is the same 8.
  7. So P = 2 × (l + w): 2 × (5 + 3) = 2 × 8 = 16 units. The same answer.
Three rectangles 1 (side 1)Draw a rectangle 6 units long and 2 units wide. Write both equations.6 × 2 = 12 square units; 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16 units, or 2 × (6 + 2) = 16
Three rectangles 2 (side 1)Draw a rectangle 9 units long and 5 units wide. Write both equations.9 × 5 = 45 square units; 9 + 5 + 9 + 5 = 28 units, or 2 × (9 + 5) = 28
Fix it 1 (side 2)Ava says: "A rug is 6 feet long and 5 feet wide. Its area is 22 square feet."6 × 5 = 30 square feet. Ava added the four sides: 22 feet is the perimeter
Fix it 2 (side 2)Ben says: "A card is 10 inches long and 7 inches wide. Its perimeter is 17 inches."10 + 7 + 10 + 7 = 34 inches. Ben added one length and one width
the ticket questionA rectangle is 8 meters long and 3 meters wide. Find the area and the perimeter. Write the equation and the units for each.8 × 3 = 24 square meters; 8 + 3 + 8 + 3 = 22 meters (2 × (8 + 3) is also right)
