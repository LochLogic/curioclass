Before class

Print what the header lists (the facts check part 2 only if day 90 ran the reteach). Put the display page on the projector. Put the grid paper away: from today a rectangle is a sketch with labelled sides.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: four sums

Alone, top of side 1: two multi-digit sums and two fraction sums (386 + 386 = ? [772], 419 + 275 = ? [694], 2 3/5 + 2 3/5 = ? [5 1/5; 4 6/5 is also right], 3 5/8 + 1 7/8 = ? [5 4/8; 4 12/8 and 5 1/2 are also right]). Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes. Denominators added (4 6/10 for the third) is a fraction slip. Those students, and those day 90 flagged ("counted", or shading square by square), sit near you for the examples. If day 88's item 8 showed many with no estimate: write "about " before each sum.

If day 90 ran the reteach: the facts check part 2 is the warm-up instead, untimed, collected at 5 minutes. The four sums are dropped; the examples carry the same moves.

If many made error C or D on day 90's exit: in "Fix it", take fix 3 (C) or fix 4 (D) first, whole class, with one minute's turn-and-talk. If day 90 sent A here (day 89's ticket), Ava's rug is the warm-up. The facts check, when it runs, has the whole warm-up.

10 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Students fill the two frames on side 1 as you go. No grid: each sketch is a rectangle with all four sides labelled. The display shows each figure, then each step on its own screen.

Example 1 (A school field is 264 feet long and 158 feet wide. How much fence goes all the way around? [264 + 158 = 422; 422 + 422 = 844; 844 feet]).

  1. Sketch the field: a rectangle, 264 feet on both long sides and 158 feet on both short sides. No grid, just labels.
  2. The fence goes all the way around: 264 + 158 + 264 + 158.
  3. Length plus width first: 264 + 158 = 422. That is half the fence.
  4. The other half is the same, so add the sum to itself: 422 + 422 = 844.
  5. The fence is 844 feet. It goes around, so the unit is feet, not square feet.
  6. Check: 264 + 264 = 528 and 158 + 158 = 316. 528 + 316 = 844.

Example 2 (A picture frame is 6 3/4 inches long and 2 3/4 inches wide. Find its perimeter. [6 3/4 + 2 3/4 = 8 6/4; 8 6/4 + 8 6/4 = 16 12/4 = 19; 19 inches (16 12/4, 18 4/4 and 76/4 are also right)]).

  1. Sketch the frame: 6 3/4 inches on both long sides, 2 3/4 inches on both short sides.
  2. Length plus width: add the wholes, 6 + 2 = 8, and the fourths, 3/4 + 3/4 = 6/4. That is 8 6/4.
  3. Add the sum to itself: 8 6/4 + 8 6/4. Wholes: 8 + 8 = 16. Fourths: 6/4 + 6/4 = 12/4.
  4. 12/4 is 3 wholes, because 4/4 + 4/4 + 4/4 = 12/4. So 16 + 3 = 19.
  5. The perimeter is 19 inches.
  6. Check: four sides have 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16 wholes and four 3/4, which is 12/4, or 3. 16 + 3 = 19.

Accept 16 12/4, 18 4/4 or 76/4.

Say it twice: length plus width is halfway around, so add it to itself.

12 min. Fix it: five wrong answers

Side 2 (Lia says: "A hall floor is 60 feet long and 9 feet wide. Its area is 138 square feet." [60 × 9 = 540; 540 square feet. Lia found the edge (error A)], Ben says: "A track is 274 feet long and 189 feet wide. Its perimeter is 463 feet." [274 + 189 = 463; 463 + 463 = 926; 926 feet. Ben added one length and one width (error B)], Kai says: "A patio covers 72 square feet. It is 8 feet wide, so it is 64 feet long." [8 × ? = 72; 9 feet. Kai subtracted (error C)], Rosa says: "A goat pen has 58 feet of fence all the way around. It is 17 feet long, so it is 24 feet wide." [17 + ? + 17 + ? = 58; 12 feet. Rosa did not halve (error D)], Nia says: "A stage floor is 50 feet long and 3 feet wide. Its area is 150 feet." [50 × 3 = 150; 150 square feet. Nia named a length unit (error E)]), one for each error A to E. Pairs, about 2 minutes each: sketch, say what went wrong, write the right working. Then the display shows the wrong working beside the right one.

Lia found the edge for a floor (A): ask "does the floor cover, or go around?" Ben stopped halfway around (B). Kai subtracted from an area (C): "8 times what is 72?" Rosa did not halve (D). Nia's number is right and her unit is wrong (E): "is 150 feet a line or 150 squares?"

22 min. The generator sheet

Alone for 18 minutes, then 4 minutes checking in pairs. The sheet (sheet-d091) holds twelve mixed problems with no pictures. Every answer is a sketch with labelled sides, an equation and a unit. Most students finish 8 to 10 in 18 minutes. That is fine: the pairs check what is done, and the rest go home or into tomorrow's spare minutes.

At 18 minutes, partners swap and compare; where they disagree, both redo it. Then put the sheet's key on the display, equations and all. Students mark each answer with a tick, or a question mark and the right working.

Watch for: half-size perimeters (B); swapped measures (A); no unit, or feet for an area (E); a side from an area by taking away (C); a fence side not halved (D); a lost regrouped ten; denominators added. Ask: "Does this cover or go around? So which unit?"

If many make B: write 274 + 189 + 274 + 189 on the board beside 463 + 463 and show they match. If many make E: the eight answers with no units from the section opener, each written as feet or square feet.

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "When the sides are big, why add length plus width first?" Expect: "fewer numbers to add"; "the other half is the same". Show the first, then the second, then connect: 2 × (l + w) means the sum twice.

Ask. "With no picture, how do you know whether to write feet or square feet?" Expect: area covers, so it counts squares; perimeter goes around, so it is a length.

7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, three questions (A hall is 40 feet long and 6 feet wide. Area? [240 square feet], A park is 235 yards long and 148 yards wide. Perimeter? [766 yards], A card is 3 1/10 inches long and 2 4/10 inches wide. Perimeter? [11 inches; 10 10/10 is also right]); students may sketch. On the record sheet under E91: a tick if all three are right; otherwise the letter of each error, and a dot for a number slip.

Other rooms

  • The sheet not printed. Write the six problems from the sheet page's "Without a printer" list on the board; 12 minutes alone, then the key on the board.
  • One projector. The display page runs the examples and the fixes; the class writes before each step shows.
  • One child at home. Measure a door and a table top to the nearest foot with a tape measure or a ruler; find both measures of each, with units. Then do the sheet together.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the pairs' check on the sheet (students check their own against the key) and the second Ask. Never drop the fixes, the sketches or the units, and never hand back the grid. Longer: the challenge on the board (A rectangle has an area of 60 square feet and a perimeter of 32 feet. How long are its sides? [6 feet and 10 feet]), then the extension (Find every rectangle with whole-number sides and an area of 60 square feet. Find the perimeter of each. [1 by 60, perimeter 122; 2 by 30, 64; 3 by 20, 46; 4 by 15, 38; 5 by 12, 34; 6 by 10, 32]).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: perimeter, area, square foot, mixed number, length, width. Frames on the display: "Length plus width is , so the perimeter is + = ." "It covers, so the unit is square ." "It goes around, so the unit is ." A student who needs touch traces the four sides of the sketch with a finger while saying each number.
