Day 93: Show what I know

At home

GoalI can show what I know and fix what I missed
Time45 minutes, one parent and one child (an assessment day)
Printthe student page; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: mark from the key. Grid paper: paper squares or a string.

Skip. The groups and the challenge.

  • 30 min. End-of-unit assessment. Items 1 to 7, alone, with no squares or times table. Read each item aloud once. Not reached is not wrong.

  • 15 min. Go over it, one to one. Mark items 1 to 4 and 7 together from the key. Fix each missed one in pen beside the first try: item 1 (side 1) with 28 squares in rows of 4; item 2 (side 1) by filling in 16 + + 16 + = 50.

    Say. For item 6 (side 2): "Do your two sides add to 19? Why does that keep the perimeter the same?" Expect: length plus width is half the fence.

    Read items 5 and 6 after, ticking each look-for on the key alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
item 1 (side 1)A closet floor covers 28 square feet. It is 4 feet wide. How long is it? Write an equation with ? for the unknown side.4 × ? = 28 (also right: ? × 4 = 28, 28 = 4 × ?, 28 ÷ 4 = ?); 7 feet
item 2 (side 1)Mia uses all 50 feet of her fence to make a rectangular dog pen. The pen is 16 feet long. How wide is it? Show your steps.9 feet
item 3 (side 1)A rectangle is 238 inches long and 118 inches wide. Find its perimeter.7 inches; 6 8/8 and 56/8 are also right
item 4 (side 1)A rectangle is 318 meters long and 147 meters wide. Find its perimeter.930 meters
item 5 (side 2)A sandbox is 5 feet wide. It is 4 times as long as it is wide. (a) A plastic sheet lies flat on top and just fits. How much plastic is that? (b) A wooden border runs along all four sides. How long is the border? Write the units.100 square feet; 50 feet
Look-fors:
  1. finds the length, 4 × 5 = 20
  2. uses area for the sheet and perimeter for the border (part e)
  3. writes square feet for the sheet and feet for the border
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Length 4 × 5 = 20. Sheet 5 × 20 = 100 square feet. Border 20 + 5 + 20 + 5 = 50 feet.
  • partly: 5 + 4 = 9. Sheet 45 square feet, border 28 feet. (Look-for 2 is met; the length goes to the comparing help: 4 bars of 5.)
  • not: Length 20. Sheet 20 + 5 + 20 + 5 = 50 feet. Border 20 × 5 = 100 feet.
item 6 (side 2)A garden is 11 feet long and 8 feet wide. Write the length and width of a different rectangle with the same perimeter. How can you tell the perimeters match without adding all four sides?For example 12 by 7; length plus width is still 19
Look-fors:
  1. gives two sides that add to 19 and are not 11 and 8 (fractions count, and so does 9 1/2 by 9 1/2)
  2. gives a reason that rests on length plus width, on half the perimeter, or on one side 1 longer and the other 1 shorter (part f)
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: 12 by 7, because 11 + 8 = 19 and 12 + 7 = 19, and the perimeter is double that.
  • partly: 10 by 9. I added 10 + 9 + 10 + 9 and got 38. (Look-for 1 only.)
  • not: 22 by 4, because 22 × 4 = 88 and 11 × 8 = 88.
item 7 (side 2)A rectangle is 70 centimeters long and 8 centimeters wide. Find its area.560 square centimeters
