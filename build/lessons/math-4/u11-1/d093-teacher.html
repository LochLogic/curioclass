Before class

Print the end-of-unit assessment both sides, the key and the record sheet. Open the display; show the answers only at the marking. Put grid paper out for the groups.

The day

30 min. End-of-unit assessment

Alone, unaided: no grid paper, tiles or times table. Read each item aloud once. Early finishers check each section. At 30 minutes, pencils down. An item not reached is recorded as not reached, not wrong.

20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

Mark, 5 minutes. Pencils away; show the answers to 1 to 4 and 7. Students tick or circle each in pen or colored pencil. Items 5 and 6 come back in the last ten minutes.

Groups, 15 minutes. Hands up by item sets the groups. A student who missed several joins the group for the first one missed. Each group fixes its item in pen beside the first try, then checks it: put the side back in the equation, or add the four sides again. Each group's task is on the display.

  • Item 1 (error C): rows of 4 on grid paper until the 28 squares are used.
  • Item 2 (error D): fill in 16 + + 16 + = 50 together.
  • Items 3 and 4 (error B): trace and name all four sides, then write l + w + l + w. A right setup with a wrong sum is an adding slip: redo the sum only.
  • Item 7 (error A or E): sketch it, shade the inside, trace the edge in another color. Area is the shaded part, in square centimeters. A student whose day 92 written task missed look-for 1 or 4, or whose E92 ticket swapped the two measures, joins this group.

Students who missed nothing do the challenge (A rectangle has an area of 90 square feet and a perimeter of 38 feet. How long are its sides? [9 feet and 10 feet]), then the extension (Find every rectangle with whole-number sides and an area of 42 square feet. Find the perimeter of each. [1 by 42, 86; 2 by 21, 46; 3 by 14, 34; 6 by 7, 26]), then check a group's fixes. Collect the checks.

10 min. Items 5 and 6 together

Hand back the checks. Pairs read each other's items 5 and 6 and ask the two questions on the display. Students add to their own answers in pen; you judge the pencil first try after class.

Ask. "Which part asked for the inside, and which for the edge?"

Ask. "Do your two sides add to 19? Why does that make the perimeter the same?"

When unit 12's readiness check exists, it takes these ten minutes and items 5 and 6 return on day 94 instead.

Other rooms

  • No projector. Write the answers and the group tasks on a covered part of the board.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the groups to ten minutes, never the check or the marking. Longer: the extension (Find every rectangle with whole-number sides and an area of 42 square feet. Find the perimeter of each. [1 by 42, 86; 2 by 21, 46; 3 by 14, 34; 6 by 7, 26]) for everyone who finishes a fix.

Words and supports

The defaults hold: items read aloud once, large print offered, a scribe or an oral answer on items 5 and 6, a sketch drawn from a student's words.

Tomorrow

About 40 minutes after class: record 1 to 4 and 7 from the pencil first try, then judge 5 and 6, ticking each look-for alone.

Item 5:

  1. finds the length, 4 × 5 = 20
  2. uses area for the sheet and perimeter for the border (part e)
  3. writes square feet for the sheet and feet for the border

Item 6:

  1. gives two sides that add to 19 and are not 11 and 8 (fractions count, and so does 9 1/2 by 9 1/2)
  2. gives a reason that rests on length plus width, on half the perimeter, or on one side 1 longer and the other 1 shorter (part f)

A length of 9 ("4 more"): draw 4 bars of 5 beside one bar of 5. "20 by 5" for (a) is asked again: "How much plastic is that?"
