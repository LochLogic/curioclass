practices09-W76v24.NBT.B.4 (b), MP.8Kim is finding 7,000 − 2,358. She renames 7,000 as 6 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones. Tell a friend, in two sentences, why two zeros became 9 and one became 10.For example: there are no hundreds or tens to ungroup, so one thousand is ungrouped and passed down. The hundreds and the tens each pass one on and keep 9; the ones are the place that needed ones, so they keep all 10.
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. says one thousand was ungrouped and passed down
  2. says the hundreds and tens each passed one on and kept 9, and the ones kept 10 because they needed them
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: There are no hundreds or tens, so one thousand is ungrouped. The hundreds and the tens each pass one on and keep 9, but the ones needed them, so they keep 10.
  • partly: You cross out the 7 and make it 6, and the zeros turn into 9s and a 10. (Look-for 1 only, said as marks: ask which place needed the ones.)
  • not: All the zeros become 9s, because you borrow. (Every zero made 9: error I.)
"the zeros become 10s": error I, across zeros, renames a zero's place wrongly: leaves it 10 when it passed one on, or makes every zero 9, even the place that needed ones
