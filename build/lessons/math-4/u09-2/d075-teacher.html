Before class

Print everything on the print line. Look at readiness items 3 and 4 (R3, R4) and decide: whole class, or a small group; only one branch below runs. Mark the E74 tickets and sort them: F (smaller digit from the larger) and G (tens left as they were). Put the place name cards up and the display page on the projector. Write the warm-up on the board.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: one ungrouping

Alone, on grid paper: 8,436 − 3,271 [5,165], 6,952 − 4,618 [2,334], then 47,381 − 15,243 [32,138] if there is time. Read the answers aloud. If only a few missed readiness items 3 and 4, do the reteach below with them now.

5 min. Readiness reteach, or E74 back

Hand back the day 74 tickets.

If many missed readiness items 3 and 4: write 734 − 258 in expanded form, rewrite 700 + 30 + 4 as 600 + 120 + 14, and subtract each place; then the same in columns. Then say 402 as 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones before writing. Name E74's error inside the worked difference that follows.

If not, and day 74's tickets showed many F (3,336: the smaller digit taken from the larger): F's wrong example again, 5,842 − 1,718 shown as 4,136 beside 4,124. Ask "can I take 8 from 2?" in every column, then ungroup.

If many G (3,334, or "8 tens and 13 ones": the 1 written, nothing traded): G's wrong example again, 9,372 − 4,156 shown as 5,226 beside 5,216, in expanded form beside the algorithm. Ask what the 7 tens became.

If both: F first; then G inside the worked difference, asking at each ungrouping what the place became.

If neither: one student reads their E74 difference, the place they ungrouped and what it became.

7 min. Teaching first: three ungroupings

Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go. No expanded form today; the place names stay up.

Example (52,413 − 17,256 [About 52,000 − 17,000 = 35,000; 35,157; check 35,157 + 17,256 = 52,413]). Estimate, subtract, check by adding.

  1. Estimate first. 52,413 is about 52,000, and 17,256 is about 17,000. 52,000 − 17,000 = 35,000.
  2. Say both numbers aloud. Line them up by place: ones under ones.
  3. Find every ungrouping first, right to left. Ones: can I take 6 from 3? No. Ungroup 1 ten: 1 ten becomes 0 tens, and 3 ones become 13 ones.
  4. Tens: there are 0 tens now. Can I take 5 from 0? No. Ungroup 1 hundred: 4 hundreds become 3 hundreds, and 0 tens become 10 tens.
  5. Hundreds: can I take 2 from 3? Yes. Thousands: can I take 7 from 2? No. Ungroup 1 ten thousand: 5 ten thousands become 4, and 2 thousands become 12 thousands. Ten thousands: can I take 1 from 4? Yes.
  6. The new top number is 4 ten thousands, 12 thousands, 3 hundreds, 10 tens, 13 ones. It is still 52,413.
  7. Now subtract each place: 13 − 6 = 7, 10 − 5 = 5, 3 − 2 = 1, 12 − 7 = 5, 4 − 1 = 3. The difference is 35,157.
  8. Is it near the estimate? 35,157 is close to 35,000. Yes.
  9. Check by adding: 35,157 + 17,256 = 52,413. That is the top number, so the difference is right.

Say the point once: find every ungrouping first, write the new top number, then subtract. The estimate catches a big mistake, and adding back catches a small one. Either order is right if the add-back works.

14 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, problems 1 to 3 (7,834 − 2,569 [5,265. Estimate about 8,000 − 3,000 = 5,000 (any close estimate). Check 5,265 + 2,569 = 7,834], 63,152 − 28,497 [34,655. Estimate about 63,000 − 28,000 = 35,000 (any close estimate). Check 34,655 + 28,497 = 63,152], 91,736 − 8,358 [83,378. Estimate about 92,000 − 8,000 = 84,000 (any close estimate). Check 83,378 + 8,358 = 91,736]), on the column sheet; add back on its back. Each: estimate, subtract, add back. Any close estimate is right; it only has to catch a big mistake. Walk with one question: "Can you take that digit from the one above it?" Watch for:

  • 5,335, 45,345 or 97,422: the smaller digit taken from the larger (error F).
  • 5,275, 34,665 or 83,388: an ungrouping written but the place not made smaller (error H, or G if nothing was traded). Ask them to add back; it will not reach the top number.
  • 8,156 on problem 3: 8,358 lined up from the left (error B). Ask for the estimate first.

At 12 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own and fix in pen. If many miss problem 2, redo its tens and hundreds together on the board.

Check: Did your check by adding come back to the top number?

13 min. Set 2: place names down

Take the place name cards down. Alone, problems 4 to 6 (245,817 − 129,563 [116,254. Estimate about 250,000 − 130,000 = 120,000 (any close estimate). Check 116,254 + 129,563 = 245,817], 573,264 − 86,791 [486,473. Estimate about 570,000 − 90,000 = 480,000 (any close estimate). Check 486,473 + 86,791 = 573,264], 831,425 − 362,758 [468,667. Estimate about 830,000 − 360,000 = 470,000 (any close estimate). Check 468,667 + 362,758 = 831,425]), on grid paper, adding back under each difference. Same routine.

Watch for 513,533 or 531,333 (F) and an answer too big by exactly 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 or 100,000 (H). At 11 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own.

Check: Is each difference near its estimate? Does adding back give the top number?

6 min. Fix it: Ty and Nia

Side 2, problems 7 and 8 (Ty says: "45,206 − 3,874 = 6,466." [About 45,000 − 4,000 = 41,000, so 6,466 is far too small; 41,332. Ty lined up from the left (error B)], Nia says: "82,415 − 36,278 = 56,137." [56,137 + 36,278 = 92,415, not 82,415; 46,137. Nia forgot the 8 ten thousands became 7 (error H)]). One minute alone on each, then the class; the display shows each working beside the right one.

Ty (error B): ask for the estimate before any column. About 41,000 says 6,466 cannot be right; then line up the ones. Nia (error H): add back first. 92,415 is not 82,415, so a place went wrong; find the place that was ungrouped but not made smaller: the 8 ten thousands should be 7.

5 min. Pull it together

Ask. "How do you know 52,918 − 4,375 is not 9,168?" Expect: "about 53,000 − 4,000 is about 49,000".

Ask. "Why does adding back prove the difference?" Expect: the difference and the number taken away make the top number again.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Estimate first, then subtract: 524,316 − 187,452. [336,864; about 500,000 − 200,000 = 300,000, or 520,000 − 190,000 = 330,000]). Place names stay down. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E75.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The example and six problems on the board, one at a time; columns on lined paper turned sideways, the place names written at the top of set 1 only.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: in set 2 do problems 4 and 6 only. Never drop the estimate, the adding back, or the fixes. Longer: the challenge (Find the missing digit: 8?,526 − 34,178 = 50,348. [4. Add to find the top number: 50,348 + 34,178 = 84,526]): adding back finds the missing digit, so the check becomes the method.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: difference, ungroup, estimate, check, and the place names to hundred thousands. Frame on the display and side 1: "1 is 10 ." Large-square grid paper and typed columns for a student who cannot write small digits.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 336,964, 337,864, 346,864 or 436,864 (error H: an ungrouped place not made smaller) or 463,144 (error F: the smaller digit taken from the larger): day 76's first three minutes show H's wrong example again, ungrouping everything first (H), or the ticket's own difference, "can I take 2 from 6?" in every column (F). If neither, one student reads their estimate and difference. A ticket with no estimate counts as half; say so when it goes back. Then day 76 teaches subtracting when the top number has zeros.
