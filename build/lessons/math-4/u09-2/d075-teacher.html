Before class

Print the student page both sides, the column sheets, the exit slips and the key. Look at readiness items 3 and 4 from day 74 and decide: whole class, or a small group. Mark the E74 slips and sort them: F (smaller digit from the larger) and G (tens left as they were). Put the place name cards up. Write the warm-up on the board.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: one ungrouping

Alone, on grid paper: 8,436 − 3,281 [5,155], 6,952 − 4,617 [2,335], then 47,381 − 15,243 [32,138] if there is time. Read the answers aloud. If only a few missed readiness items 3 and 4, do the reteach below with them now.

5 min. Readiness reteach, or E74 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 74 slips.

If many missed readiness items 3 and 4: write 734 − 258 in expanded form, rewrite 700 + 30 + 4 as 600 + 120 + 14, and subtract each place; then the same in columns. Then say 402 as 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones before writing. Name E74's error inside the worked difference that follows.

If not, and day 74's slips showed many F (3,336: the smaller digit taken from the larger): F's wrong example again, 5,842 − 1,718 shown as 4,136 beside 4,124. Ask "can I take 8 from 2?" in every column, then ungroup.

If many G (3,334, or "8 tens and 13 ones": the 1 written, nothing traded): G's wrong example again, 9,372 − 4,156 shown as 5,226 beside 5,216, in expanded form beside the algorithm. Ask what the 7 tens became.

If both: F first; then G inside the worked difference, asking at each ungrouping what the place became.

If neither: one student reads their E74 difference and says what the 8 tens and 3 ones became.

5 min. Teaching first: three ungroupings

Whole class. Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go. No expanded form today; the place names stay up.

Example (52,413 − 17,256 [About 52,000 − 17,000 = 35,000; 35,157; check 35,157 + 17,256 = 52,413]). Estimate, subtract, check by adding.

  1. Estimate first. 52,413 is about 52,000, and 17,256 is about 17,000. 52,000 − 17,000 = 35,000.
  2. Say both numbers aloud. Line them up by place: ones under ones.
  3. Ones: can I take 6 from 3? No. Ungroup 1 ten. 1 ten becomes 0 tens, and 3 ones become 13 ones. 13 − 6 = 7.
  4. Tens: can I take 5 from 0? No. Ungroup 1 hundred. 4 hundreds become 3 hundreds, and 0 tens become 10 tens. 10 − 5 = 5.
  5. Hundreds: can I take 2 from 3? Yes. 3 − 2 = 1.
  6. Thousands: can I take 7 from 2? No. Ungroup 1 ten thousand. 5 ten thousands become 4, and 2 thousands become 12 thousands. 12 − 7 = 5.
  7. Ten thousands: 4 − 1 = 3. The difference is 35,157.
  8. Is it near the estimate? 35,157 is close to 35,000. Yes.
  9. Check by adding: 35,157 + 17,256 = 52,413. That is the top number, so the difference is right.

Say the point once: the estimate catches a big mistake, and adding back catches a small one.

14 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, problems 1 to 3, the columns on the column sheet. Each: estimate, subtract, add back. Walk with one question: "Can you take that digit from the one above it?" Watch for:

  • 5,335, 45,345 or 97,422: the smaller digit taken from the larger (error F). Ask the question column by column.
  • 5,275, 34,665 or 83,388: an ungrouping written but the place not made smaller (error H, or G if nothing was traded). Ask them to add back; it will not reach the top number.
  • 8,156 on problem 3: 8,358 lined up from the left (error B). Ask for the estimate first.

At 12 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own and fix in pen. If many miss problem 2, redo its tens and hundreds together on the board.

Check: Did your check by adding come back to the top number?

15 min. Set 2: place names down

Take the place name cards down. Alone, problems 4 to 6, on grid paper. Same routine. Early finishers do the challenge.

Watch for 513,533 or 531,333 (F) and an answer off by exactly 100, 1,000 or 10,000 (H). At 13 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own.

Check: Is each difference near its estimate? Does adding back give the top number?

6 min. Fix it: Ty and Nia

Side 2, problems 7 and 8 (Ty says: "45,206 − 3,874 = 6,466." [About 45,000 − 4,000 = 41,000, so 6,466 is far too small; 41,332. Ty lined up from the left (error B)], Nia says: "82,415 − 36,278 = 56,137." [56,137 + 36,278 = 92,415, not 82,415; 46,137. Nia forgot the 8 ten thousands became 7 (error H)]). One minute alone on each, then the class; the display shows each working beside the right one.

Ty (error B): ask for the estimate before any column. About 41,000 says 6,466 cannot be right; then line up the ones. Nia (error H): add back first. 92,415 is not 82,415, so a place went wrong; find the ten thousands that were already made smaller.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Without subtracting, how do you know 45,206 − 3,874 is not 6,466?" Expect: "about 45,000 take away about 4,000 is about 41,000"; "you only take away a few thousand, so the answer is still over 40,000".

Ask. "Why does adding back prove the difference?" Expect: the difference and the number taken away make the top number again.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper (Estimate first, then subtract: 524,316 − 187,452. [336,864; about 500,000 − 200,000 = 300,000, or 520,000 − 190,000 = 330,000]). Place names stay down. Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E75.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The example and six problems on the board, one at a time; columns on lined paper turned sideways, the place names written at the top of set 1 only.
  • One child at home. Do the example together, then problems 1, 2 and 4. The child says "can I take it?" aloud in every column, and adds back after each one.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: in set 2 do problems 4 and 6 only. Never drop the estimate, the adding back, or the fixes. Longer: the challenge (Find the missing digit: 8?,526 − 34,178 = 50,348. [4. Add to find the top number: 50,348 + 34,178 = 84,526]): adding back finds the missing digit, so the check becomes the method.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: difference, ungroup, estimate, check, and the place names to hundred thousands. Families may say "borrow" or "regroup"; all are welcome. Frame on the display and side 1: "1 is 10 ." Large-square grid paper and typed columns for a student who cannot write small digits.

Tomorrow

If neither, one student reads their estimate and check. A slip with no estimate counts as half; say so when it goes back. Then day 76 teaches subtracting when the top number has zeros.
