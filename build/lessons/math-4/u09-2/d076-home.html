Day 76: Subtracting across zeros

At home

GoalI can subtract when the top number has zeros
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1. Grid paper: lined paper turned sideways.

Skip. The warm-up, problems 5 and 6, the written task and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Example 1 (side 1), then 2. Say the renamed top number together before writing: "6,005 is 5 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones."

    Say. "In 500 − 263, why does the tens zero become 9 but the ones zero become 10?" Expect: the tens passed one on; the ones were the place that needed it.

  • 5 min. Fix it. Fix it (side 1). The child adds Kai's answer back: 3,276 + 1,836 is 5,112, not 5,002.

  • 15 min. Four differences. Six differences 1 (side 2), 2, 3, 4: rename the top number in one line, subtract, then add to check. If the check misses, find the mistake together.
  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)Subtract 500 − 263. Then add to check.237; 237 + 263 = 500
  1. Say the top number by its places: 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones. Can I take 3 ones from 0 ones? No. Are there tens to ungroup? No, the tens place is 0.
  2. Go to the hundreds. Ungroup 1 hundred into 10 tens: 4 hundreds, 10 tens, 0 ones.
  3. Now ungroup 1 of those tens into 10 ones: 4 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones. The tens zero passed one on and kept 9. The ones needed ones, so they have all 10.
  4. Expanded form: 400 + 90 + 10. Still 500.
  5. Write the rename over the top: 4, 9 and 10.
  6. Ones: 10 − 3 = 7. Tens: 9 − 6 = 3. Hundreds: 4 − 2 = 2. The difference is 237.
  7. Add to check: 237 + 263 = 500.
Example 2 (side 1)Subtract 6,005 − 2,347. Then add to check.3,658; 3,658 + 2,347 = 6,005
  1. Say the top number by its places: 6 thousands, 0 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones. Can I take 7 ones from 5 ones? No. No tens, no hundreds to ungroup.
  2. Go to the thousands. 1 thousand is 10 hundreds. 1 of those hundreds is 10 tens. 1 of those tens is 10 ones.
  3. Rename in one line: 5 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones. Each zero passed one on and kept 9.
  4. Expanded form: 5,000 + 900 + 90 + 15. Still 6,005.
  5. Write the rename over the top: 5, 9, 9 and 15.
  6. Ones: 15 − 7 = 8. Tens: 9 − 4 = 5. Hundreds: 9 − 3 = 6. Thousands: 5 − 2 = 3. The difference is 3,658.
  7. Add to check: 3,658 + 2,347 = 6,005. It comes back, so 3,658 is right.
