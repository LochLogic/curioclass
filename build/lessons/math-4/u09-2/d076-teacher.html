Before class

Print everything on the print line. Mark the E75 tickets and sort them: H (one ungrouping not taken away), F (smaller digit from the larger). From the readiness check, list who missed item 4. Write the warm-up on the board. Put the display page on the projector. Put out grid paper or the blank column sheet.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: differences and tens

Alone, on grid paper: 8,231 − 4,768 [3,463], 63,512 − 28,739 [34,773], 8 × 70 [560], 9 × 40 [360]. The two differences are checked by adding; the two products are read aloud. Watch for a smaller digit taken from a larger one (F): ask "can I take 8 from 1?"

3 min. E75 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 75 tickets.

If day 75's tickets showed many H (such as 336,964): H's wrong example, 82,415 − 36,278 = 56,137 beside 46,137; ungroup everything first, then subtract.

If many F (463,144): the ticket's own difference on the board, "can I take 2 from 6?" asked in every column, then ungroup.

Otherwise, one student reads their estimate and difference. A ticket with no estimate counts as half; say so as it goes back.

10 min. Teaching first: zeros in the top number

Expanded form comes back today, for zeros only. Students who missed readiness item 4 sit near you for both examples. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go. Place names stay over the columns.

Example 1 (Subtract 500 − 263. Then add to check. [237; 237 + 263 = 500]).

  1. Say the top number by its places: 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones. Can I take 3 ones from 0 ones? No. Are there tens to ungroup? No, the tens place is 0.
  2. Go to the hundreds. Ungroup 1 hundred into 10 tens: 4 hundreds, 10 tens, 0 ones.
  3. Now ungroup 1 of those tens into 10 ones: 4 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones. The tens zero passed one on and kept 9. The ones needed ones, so they have all 10.
  4. Expanded form: 400 + 90 + 10. Still 500.
  5. Write the rename over the top: 4, 9 and 10.
  6. Ones: 10 − 3 = 7. Tens: 9 − 6 = 3. Hundreds: 4 − 2 = 2. The difference is 237.
  7. Add to check: 237 + 263 = 500.

Say why this is honest: 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones and 4 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones are the same number. One hundred became ten tens, and one ten became ten ones. Nothing was added or lost. A zero between keeps 9: it took ten and passed one on. The place that needed ones keeps all 10.

Example 2 (Subtract 6,005 − 2,347. Then add to check. [3,658; 3,658 + 2,347 = 6,005]).

  1. Say the top number by its places: 6 thousands, 0 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones. Can I take 7 ones from 5 ones? No. No tens, no hundreds to ungroup.
  2. Go to the thousands. 1 thousand is 10 hundreds. 1 of those hundreds is 10 tens. 1 of those tens is 10 ones.
  3. Rename in one line: 5 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones. Each zero passed one on and kept 9.
  4. Expanded form: 5,000 + 900 + 90 + 15. Still 6,005.
  5. Write the rename over the top: 5, 9, 9 and 15.
  6. Ones: 15 − 7 = 8. Tens: 9 − 4 = 5. Hundreds: 9 − 3 = 6. Thousands: 5 − 2 = 3. The difference is 3,658.
  7. Add to check: 3,658 + 2,347 = 6,005. It comes back, so 3,658 is right.

The habit for the day: rename the whole top number in one line before any column is subtracted. Adding the answer to the number taken away is a full check: subtracting asks "what joins 2,347 to make 6,005?", so the two parts must add back to the whole.

5 min. Fix it: Kai across zeros

Side 1 (Kai says: "5,002 − 1,836 = 3,276." [3,166; Kai made each zero 10 and left it 10; 3,166 + 1,836 = 5,002]). Two minutes alone: what went wrong, and Kai's add-back. Then the class does the right working on the display. Kai's check is the clue: 3,276 + 1,836 is 5,112, not 5,002. Kai turned 5,002 into 4 thousands, 10 hundreds, 10 tens, 12 ones, and that is 5,112.

20 min. Six differences across zeros

Alone, on grid paper, side 2 (803 − 457 [346; 346 + 457 = 803], 9,004 − 3,678 [5,326; 5,326 + 3,678 = 9,004], 60,500 − 27,846 [32,654; 32,654 + 27,846 = 60,500], 30,007 − 12,469 [17,538; 17,538 + 12,469 = 30,007], 100,000 − 46,183 [53,817; 53,817 + 46,183 = 100,000], 407,020 − 158,364 [248,656; 248,656 + 158,364 = 407,020]). For each: rename the top number in one line, subtract, then add to check. Everyone finishes 1 to 4; 5 and 6 are for those who are ready.

Walk with one question: "What did that zero become, and why?" Watch for:

  • 356, 5,436, 18,648, 33,664, 64,927 or 258,756: each zero made 10 and left 10 (error I). Say the rename aloud: "9,004 is 8 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 14 ones."
  • 32,653, 53,816 or 248,655: every zero made 9, even the place that needed ones (error I). Ask: which place needed the ones?
  • 454, 6,674, 22,462, 47,346, 146,183 or 351,344: smaller digit taken from the larger (error F). Ask "can I take 7 from 3?"
  • An adding check that does not come back to the top number, left as it is. The check found a mistake; find it.

At 12 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own and fix in pen. If many wrote 10 over a zero, rename Example 2 again with the class, in words before digits.

Check: Add your answer to the number taken away. Do you get the top number?

7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 (Kim is finding 7,000 − 2,358. She renames 7,000 as 6 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones. Tell a friend, in two sentences, why two zeros became 9 and one became 10. [For example: there are no hundreds or tens to ungroup, so one thousand is ungrouped and passed down. The hundreds and the tens each pass one on and keep 9; the ones are the place that needed ones, so they keep all 10.]). Two sentences; a student may dictate them to you. Collect them: they are judged after class. Read the look-fors on the key.

5 min. Pull it together

Ask. "In 500 − 263, why does the tens zero become 9 but the ones zero become 10?" Expect: "the tens gave one away"; "the ones were the place that needed it". Take a marks-only answer first ("you cross out and write 9"), then one that says what was ungrouped. Show Example 1's rename beside 400 + 90 + 10 = 500.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Subtract 40,060 − 17,384. Then add to check your answer. [22,676; 22,676 + 17,384 = 40,060]). Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E76.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Examples and the six differences on the board; all work on grid paper or lined paper turned sideways.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: problems 1 to 4. Never drop the rename, the adding check or the fix. Longer: the challenge (Mia subtracted and got 4,268. She took away 3,739. What number did she start with? Then subtract to check. [8,007; 8,007 − 3,739 = 4,268]): add to find the start, then subtract across zeros to check.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: ungroup, rename, difference, check, zero, and the place names. Frames, printed on the student page: "1 is 10 ." " renamed: thousands, hundreds, tens, ones."
