Before class

Print everything on the print line. Have the day 71 readiness sheets. Mark the E73 tickets (circle a lost carry) and the day 73 written tasks; note B on the tickets and E in practice sets 1 and 2. Put the display page on the projector. Write the warm-up on the board. Draw the example table on the board, top two rows filled. Put the place names over the columns on the board: thousands, hundreds, tens, ones.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: sums and facts

Alone, then whole class: 3,867 + 2,475 [6,342], 48,159 + 26,374 [74,533], 6 × 9 [54], 8 × 7 [56].

5 min. Readiness items 3 and 4

Hand back the day 71 readiness sheets; read items 3 and 4 aloud once. Alone, no drawings. Collect them. Mark them tonight with the E74 tickets: R3 and R4 on the record sheet (tick, F, G, I or x). Nothing moves today: the results go to day 75's first five minutes.

3 min. E73 back

Hand back the day 73 tickets and the written task (the small 1), marked against its look-fors. A lost carry (A) is circled on the ticket only.

If day 73's tickets showed many B (lined up from the left, 819,627): show 6,285 + 947 = 15,755 beside the estimate of 7,200. Say both numbers aloud, then line them up under the place names.

If day 73's practice sets 1 and 2 showed many E (the last carry lost): show 64,859 + 47,463 = 12,322 beside 112,322. The estimate says the sum must pass 100,000.

If neither: one student reads their estimate and sum.

10 min. Teaching first: one ten ungrouped

Whole class. Students fill the side 1 table as you go, not ahead of you: expanded form on the left half, columns on the right.

Example (9,372 − 4,156 [5,216; 7 tens and 2 ones became 6 tens and 12 ones]).

  1. Say both numbers aloud. Line them up under the place names: thousands, hundreds, tens, ones.
  2. Expanded form: 9,372 is 9,000 + 300 + 70 + 2. 4,156 is 4,000 + 100 + 50 + 6.
  3. Start with the ones. Can I take 6 ones from 2 ones? No. I need more ones.
  4. Ungroup one ten. 7 tens and 2 ones become 6 tens and 12 ones. In expanded form, 70 + 2 becomes 60 + 12.
  5. Nothing was taken away yet. 9,000 + 300 + 60 + 12 is still 9,372. Ungrouping is carrying run backwards.
  6. In the columns: cross out the 7 and write 6 above it. Write a small 1 by the 2: 12 ones.
  7. Ones: 12 − 6 = 6. Tens: 6 − 5 = 1. Hundreds: 3 − 1 = 2. Thousands: 9 − 4 = 5.
  8. Expanded form gives 5,000 + 200 + 10 + 6. The columns give 5,216. Both ways, the difference is 5,216.

Point to both halves at each step. Ungrouping is carrying run backwards. The top number keeps its value; only how it is written changes.

10 min. Both ways, in pairs

Pairs, problems 1 and 2 (8,926 − 3,751 [5,175; one hundred ungrouped], 7,562 − 2,138 [5,424; one ten ungrouped]). Both partners fill the whole table. Partner A says each expanded-form step, partner B the matching column step; they swap for problem 2. Problem 1 ungroups a hundred: "1 hundred is 10 tens."

Watch for a pair that fills the right half and copies it into the left. Ask what 9 hundreds 2 tens became. Expect 800 and 120.

Check: Do your two halves give the same answer as the screen?

12 min. One ungrouping each, on grid paper

Alone, side 2, problems 3 to 8 (5,947 − 1,382 [4,565; one hundred ungrouped], 9,618 − 4,275 [5,343; one hundred ungrouped], 7,254 − 3,821 [3,433; one thousand ungrouped], 46,385 − 12,149 [34,236; one ten ungrouped], 83,617 − 51,904 [31,713; one thousand ungrouped], 72,594 − 38,253 [34,341; one ten thousand ungrouped]), on grid paper, one digit a square; 4 and 7 are for early finishers. After each difference, a student whispers to a partner which place they ungrouped, then writes it in the table.

Walk with one question: "Can I take this digit from that one?" Watch for:

  • 4,645, 5,463 or 4,633: the smaller digit taken from the larger (error F). Point to the top digit first.
  • 4,665, 5,443 or 4,433: the small 1 written, the next place left as it was (error G). Ask what that place became.

When done, students check their own and fix in pen. If many miss one, redo it on the board.

Check: Is your difference the same as the screen? Did you write the place you ungrouped?

5 min. Fix it: Kai and Mia

Side 2, problems 9 and 10 (Kai says 5,842 − 1,718 = 4,136. [4,124. Kai took 2 from 8 in the ones. He needed to ungroup one ten: 3 tens and 12 ones.], Mia says 6,594 − 2,368 = 4,236. [4,226. Mia made 14 ones but left the 9 tens as 9. They became 8 tens.]). Two minutes alone (the right difference only if time), one minute of turn-and-talk, then the class: the display shows their working beside the right working. Kai took the smaller digit from the larger (error F). Mia wrote the small 1 but traded nothing (error G). If many still copy Mia: write Mia's difference in expanded form, and ask where the extra ten came from.

5 min. Pull it together

On the board, write 9,372 − 4,156 = 5,226 beside the right working, 5,216.

Ask. "Where did the 12 ones come from, and what happened to the 7 tens?" Expect: "one ten was ungrouped into ten ones"; "10 and 2 make 12"; "the 7 tens became 6 tens". Then: "So which answer is right?" 5,216: the 5,226 kept a ten that was already traded away.

Ask. "Is 9,000 + 300 + 60 + 12 the same number as 9,372?" Expect: "yes, 60 + 12 is 72"; "nothing was taken away, it was only written another way".

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Subtract 6,483 − 3,159 in columns. Which place did you ungroup? What did it become? [3,324; one ten: 8 tens and 3 ones became 7 tens and 13 ones]). Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E74.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Draw the table for the example on the board; students rule it on grid paper. Problems 1 to 10 go on the board, a few at a time.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: in the grid set do problems 3, 5, 6 and 8 (a different place ungrouped in each). Never drop the left half of the table, the fix or saying the ungrouped place. Longer: the challenge (One digit is hidden: 6,4□5 − 2,173 = 4,262. What is the hidden digit? [3 (6,435 − 2,173 = 4,262: a hundred was ungrouped into the tens, 13 − 7 = 6)]): the hundreds column shows a hundred was ungrouped.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: difference, ungroup, thousands, hundreds, tens, ones. Large-square grid paper or the columns sheet (its dashed row is for the ungrouped digit) for any student who needs it. Frames on the display: "1 is 10 ." " tens and ones became tens and ones."

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 3,324 and "one ten: 7 tens and 13 ones" are right. 3,336 (error F: the smaller digit from the larger) or 3,334, or "8 tens and 13 ones" (error G: nothing traded): day 75 opens with that wrong example again, F with "can I take 8 from 2?" asked in every column, G in expanded form beside the columns. If many missed readiness items 3 and 4, day 75's first five minutes reteach those first; item 4 misses also go on day 76's list. Then day 75 teaches several ungroupings in one difference.
