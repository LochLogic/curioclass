Day 75: Ungroup more than once, then check by adding

At home

GoalI can ungroup more than once, in numbers up to six digits, and check by adding
Time39 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1. Column sheet or grid paper: lined paper turned sideways. Place name cards: write the place names over the columns for problems 1 and 2 only.

Skip. The warm-up, problems 3 and 5, and the challenge.

  • 8 min. The example. Example (side 1) together: estimate, ungroup, subtract, then add back. The child fills the frame on side 1.

    Say. "Why does adding back prove the difference?" Expect: the difference and the number taken away make the top number again.

  • 10 min. Set 1. Set 1, question 1 (side 1), question 2. The child says "Can I take it?" aloud in every column and adds back after each one.

  • 10 min. Set 2. Set 2, question 4 (side 2), question 6, no place names, the same way.

  • 6 min. Fix it. Fix it 7 (side 2) estimate first; Fix it 8 (side 2) add back first.

    Say. "How do you know Ty's 6,466 is wrong?" Expect: about 45,000 − 4,000 is about 41,000.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

Example (side 1)52,413 − 17,256About 52,000 − 17,000 = 35,000; 35,157; check 35,157 + 17,256 = 52,413
  1. Estimate first. 52,413 is about 52,000, and 17,256 is about 17,000. 52,000 − 17,000 = 35,000.
  2. Say both numbers aloud. Line them up by place: ones under ones.
  3. Find every ungrouping first, right to left. Ones: can I take 6 from 3? No. Ungroup 1 ten: 1 ten becomes 0 tens, and 3 ones become 13 ones.
  4. Tens: there are 0 tens now. Can I take 5 from 0? No. Ungroup 1 hundred: 4 hundreds become 3 hundreds, and 0 tens become 10 tens.
  5. Hundreds: can I take 2 from 3? Yes. Thousands: can I take 7 from 2? No. Ungroup 1 ten thousand: 5 ten thousands become 4, and 2 thousands become 12 thousands. Ten thousands: can I take 1 from 4? Yes.
  6. The new top number is 4 ten thousands, 12 thousands, 3 hundreds, 10 tens, 13 ones. It is still 52,413.
  7. Now subtract each place: 13 − 6 = 7, 10 − 5 = 5, 3 − 2 = 1, 12 − 7 = 5, 4 − 1 = 3. The difference is 35,157.
  8. Is it near the estimate? 35,157 is close to 35,000. Yes.
  9. Check by adding: 35,157 + 17,256 = 52,413. That is the top number, so the difference is right.
