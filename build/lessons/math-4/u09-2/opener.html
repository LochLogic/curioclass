Section 9.2: The standard algorithm for subtracting (days 74 to 77)

The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NBT.B.4, its second part: "Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm." Section 9.1 taught adding. This section subtracts, across zeros included, and ends with the end-of-unit assessment on day 77.

Ungrouping is carrying run backwards. In adding, ten of a unit become one of the next. In subtracting, one of a unit becomes ten of the next smaller unit. In 9,372 − 4,156 the ones cannot give 6 from 2, so one of the 7 tens is ungrouped: 7 tens and 2 ones are rewritten as 6 tens and 12 ones. Then 12 − 6 = 6, 6 tens − 5 tens = 1 ten, and on: 5,216. Families may say "borrow" or "regroup"; the move is the same, and nothing is borrowed back.

The top number keeps its value. 7 tens and 2 ones is 72; 6 tens and 12 ones is also 72. Ungrouping rewrites the top number and never changes it. That is why the answer is still right, and it is what a student says aloud on day 74: what the 7 tens became. A student may ungroup every column first and then subtract, or ungroup column by column as they go; both are the standard algorithm.

Across zeros, a zero place passes one on and keeps nine. 402 − 178 needs ones, but there are no tens to ungroup. So one hundred is ungrouped into 10 tens, and one of those tens into 10 ones: 402 is 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones. 6,005 is 5 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones. On day 76 the top number is renamed in one line like this before any column is subtracted. A zero between keeps 9: it took ten and passed one on. The place that needed ones keeps all 10 (MP.8).

Adding back is a full check. A difference is the number that, added to the number taken away, gives the top number. So 5,216 + 4,156 must come back to 9,372. From day 75 every difference is checked this way, and an estimate is made first (4.NBT.A.3): it catches a difference that is far off.

Limits. Whole numbers to 1,000,000; a difference is never below zero. No blocks. No time target is set and no clock is shown.

Expanded form beside the algorithm, then the renaming

  • Day 74. The teacher shows 9,372 − 4,156 both ways, side by side, under place names. Pairs do two differences both ways, then four- and five-digit differences on grid paper, one ungrouping each, the ungrouped place said aloud.
  • Day 75. Expanded form is gone. Several ungroupings, to six digits; an estimate before each and a check by adding after. Place names come down before E75.
  • Day 76. Expanded form comes back for ten minutes, for zeros only: 402 − 178, then 6,005 − 2,347, each top number renamed in one line first. Then differences with one, two and three zeros, and from 100,000.
  • Day 77. The check: a mixed set, the end-of-unit assessment, then groups by error.

The place value columns sheet (u09-1/columns.md) has a dashed row above the top number for ungrouped digits.

Why this model

The same expanded form that showed where a carry comes from shows where an ungrouped ten goes, so subtracting reads as the same idea run backwards. Renaming across zeros in one line, before any column, keeps the student from switching between ungrouping and subtracting, which is where H and I start. No tool fits days 74 to 76. Road Trip Logbook's Trip ledger fits day 77's ten mixed minutes, but the tool is spec only, so the day runs its paper path (practice-d077.md) in the same minutes.

The reteach (readiness items 3 and 4)

Items 3 and 4 are given in day 74's first five minutes and marked that night, R3 and R4 ticked F, G, I or x (u09-1/readiness.md). If more than a third of the class missed one, day 75's first five minutes are this reteach for the whole class; the warm-up still runs. Otherwise run it with the students who missed it during day 75's warm-up, and the five minutes go to E74.

Item 3, subtracting within 1,000.

  1. Write 734 as 700 + 30 + 4 and 258 under it as 200 + 50 + 8.
  2. Ask "Can I take 8 ones from 4 ones?" Ungroup a ten: 700 + 20 + 14.
  3. Ask "Can I take 5 tens from 2 tens?" Ungroup a hundred: 600 + 120 + 14.
  4. Subtract each place, then do the same difference in columns beside it.

Item 4, across a zero. Say 402 as 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones before writing anything, and check that it is still 402. Every student who missed item 4 sits with the teacher for day 76's teaching (both examples).

Supplies for the section

  • Per student: grid paper, or lined paper turned sideways; the place value columns sheet, one per student on day 75, for any student who wants it on other days; the day 77 mixed set and the end-of-unit assessment; a pencil.

  • Place name cards from ones to hundred thousands, on paper, for the board (days 74 to 76).

  • Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board with place names over the columns.
  • A room with nothing: differences copied from the board; lined paper turned sideways.

Words

Difference, ungroup, check, and the place names, each on the worked example at first use. Families may say "borrow" or "regroup"; all are welcome. Frame: "1 is 10 ." A student may say an ungrouping in a home language first, then in the frame.

The end of the unit

Day 77: the mixed set (10 minutes), then the end-of-unit assessment (30 minutes, unaided; 9.2a, 9.2b and 9.2c are this section's), then the respond half by error group. E76's tickets go back when pencils go down and join the I and F groups. A student in an adding group and a subtracting group goes to the subtracting one. Items 9.1c and 9.2c are judged after class and handed back on day 79, after Check 2 (day 78), which holds unit 9's items C2.1 and C2.2 and is not ready until units 6 to 8 add theirs. Day 79 responds to both. Everything goes on unit 9's record sheet (u09-1/record.md).
