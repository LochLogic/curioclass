Day 88: Show what I know

At home

GoalI can show what I know about "times as many" and two-step problems
Time50 minutes, one parent and one child (an assessment day)
Printthe student page; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: mark from the key. No grid paper: squares ruled on plain paper.

Skip. The groups and the challenges.

  • 30 min. End-of-unit assessment. Items 1 to 8, alone, with no strip sheet, cubes or times table. Read each item aloud once. Not reached is not wrong.

  • 10 min. Go over it, one to one. Mark items 1 to 6 together from the key; you judge 7 and 8 from its look-fors. Fix each missed one in pen beside the first try: item 3 (side 1) by drawing the cat as an 8-strip and the dog as copies under it; item 4 (side 1) by asking "who is taller?" first.

    Say. For item 7 (side 2): "What does your letter stand for? Did you answer what was asked?" Expect: all the beads in both jars; b = 20 + 4 × 20, so 100.

  • 10 min. Unit 11's readiness check. Alone, with grid paper for item 4. Read each item aloud once.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
item 1 (side 1)54 = 6 × 9. Write two sentences this equation tells you. Use the words 'times as many as' in each.54 is 6 times as many as 9. 54 is 9 times as many as 6.
item 2 (side 1)Write a multiplication equation for this sentence: 210 is 7 times as many as 30.210 = 7 × 30, or 7 × 30 = 210
item 3 (side 1)A cat weighs 8 pounds. A dog weighs 5 times as much as the cat. How much does the dog weigh? Write an equation with a letter. Then solve it.d = 5 × 8; 40 pounds
item 4 (side 1)A tree is 32 feet tall. It is 4 times as tall as a bush. How tall is the bush? Write an equation with a letter. Then solve it.4 × b = 32, or b = 32 ÷ 4; 8 feet
item 5 (side 2)A red ribbon is 27 inches long. A blue ribbon is 3 inches long. The red ribbon is how many times as long as the blue ribbon?9 times
item 6 (side 2)Sam has 6 cards. Lee has 3 times as many cards as Sam. Kim has 3 more cards than Sam. How many cards does Lee have? How many does Kim have?Lee 18, Kim 9
item 7 (side 2)A small jar holds 20 beads. A big jar holds 4 times as many beads as the small jar. Nia fills one small jar and one big jar. How many beads does she use? Write one equation with a letter for the unknown. Then solve it.b = 20 + 4 × 20; 100 beads
Look-fors:
  1. writes one equation, or two steps joined, with a letter that stands for all the beads (part g)
  2. does both steps: 4 × 20 = 80, then 20 + 80; or 1 + 4 = 5 jars of 20, then 5 × 20 (part f)
  3. finds 100 beads
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: b = 20 + 4 × 20. 4 × 20 = 80. 20 + 80 = 100 beads. (Also meets: 4 × 20 = 80, 80 + 20 = b, b = 100.) (Also meets: 1 + 4 = 5 small jars. b = 5 × 20 = 100 beads.)
  • partly: 4 × 20 = 80. 80 + 20 = 100 beads. (Look-fors 2 and 3: no letter, error G; ask what a letter would stand for.)
  • not: 4 × 20 = 80 beads. (One step only, error F.)
item 8 (side 2)A family buys an 18-dollar parking pass and 7 tickets. Each ticket costs 7 dollars. Ravi wrote s = 18 + 7 × 7 and got s = 175. Use an estimate to explain why 175 cannot be right. Then find s.18 is about 20, and 7 × 7 is about 50, so about 70; 175 is far too big. s = 67
Look-fors:
  1. estimates with easy numbers from the story (about 50, about 20)
  2. compares the estimate with 175 and says why 175 cannot be right
  3. finds s = 67
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: 18 is about 20, and 7 × 7 is about 50, so about 70. 175 is far too big. s = 67.
  • partly: 7 × 7 = 49 and 49 + 18 = 67, so Ravi is wrong. s = 67. (Look-for 3 only: no estimate. If many: days 91 and 92 start each warm-up sum with 'about __'.)
  • not: 175 is about 200, so it is close enough. s = 175. (It rounds the answer, not the story's numbers.)
