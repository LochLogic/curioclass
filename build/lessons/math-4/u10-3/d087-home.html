Day 87: Two steps, one equation, one estimate

At home

GoalI can solve a two-step problem with one equation and a letter, and check my answer with an estimate
Time36 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: read the answers from the key.

Skip. The warm-up, problems 2, 3 and 5, the three to judge, and two of the fixes.

  • 8 min. One story, one equation. Example (side 1) together: Ben's strip, 4 copies for Amy, 135 marked off, p on the rest.

    Say. "Could the answer be more than 1,000? How do you know?" Expect: the book has only 280 pages.

  • 12 min. Three problems. Item 1 (side 1), 4 (side 2), 6. Before each, the child tells the story back, says what the letter stands for and guesses about how many. Then one equation, solve, estimate.

  • 5 min. Fix one. Read it aloud: Lena has 40 dollars and Omar has 2 times as much; Pia says they have 80 dollars together.

    Say. "Is 80 the answer to the question that was asked?" Expect: no, that is Omar alone; together 40 + 2 × 40 = 120.

  • 4 min. Mia's estimate. Mia (side 2), written alone, then read aloud to you.

    Say. "What should Mia do next?" Expect: 12 and 120 are far apart, so she checks her working and her estimate again.

  • 7 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example (side 1)Ben's book has 70 pages. Amy's book has 4 times as many pages. Amy has read 135 pages. How many pages does she have left to read?p = 4 × 70 − 135; 145 pages; estimate about 280 − 140 = 140
  1. Read it twice. Underline the question: how many pages are left?
  2. Name the letter in words first: p is the pages Amy has left.
  3. Draw it: a strip for Ben's 70 pages, then 4 copies of it for Amy's book. Mark 135 read and p left at the end.
  4. Two equations: 4 × 70 = 280 pages in Amy's book. Then 280 − 135 = p.
  5. One equation: p = 4 × 70 − 135. We multiply first, then subtract.
  6. Solve: 4 × 70 = 280, and 280 − 135 = 145. p = 145. Amy has 145 pages left.
  7. Estimate: 4 × 70 is 280, and 135 is about 140. 280 − 140 = 140. 145 is close to 140, so it makes sense.
item 1 (side 1)Jade has 60 stamps. Her aunt has 5 times as many stamps. Her aunt gives away 115 of them. How many stamps does her aunt have now?a = 5 × 60 − 115; 185 stamps; about 300 − 120 = 180
item 4 (side 2)A box holds 8 rows of 50 tiles. Mr. Diaz uses 286 tiles. How many tiles are left in the box?t = 8 × 50 − 286; 114 tiles; about 400 − 290 = 110
item 6 (side 2)Sam has 463 dollars. He buys 7 tickets that cost 40 dollars each. How much money does he have left?m = 463 − 7 × 40; 183 dollars; about 460 − 280 = 180
Mia (side 2)Mia says the answer to a problem is 12. Her estimate was 120. What should Mia do next, and why?The answer and the estimate are far apart, so one is wrong; Mia checks her working and her estimate again
Look-fors:
  1. says the two do not agree, so one of them is wrong
  2. says to check both the working and the estimate, not just pick one
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: 12 and 120 are far apart, so one is wrong. Mia should work the problem again and check her estimate too.
  • partly: Mia should do it again. (Look-for 2 in part; look-for 1 not said: ask why.)
  • not: Mia should change her answer to 120. (Picks one without checking.)
the ticket questionA school has 158 pencils. It gives 3 classes 30 pencils each. How many pencils are left? Write one equation with a letter. Say what the letter stands for. Solve it. Then estimate to show your answer makes sense.p = 158 − 3 × 30; 68 pencils; about 160 − 90 = 70
Look-fors:
  1. one equation with a letter that stands for the pencils left (part g)
  2. both steps: 3 × 30 = 90, then 158 − 90 = 68 (part f)
  3. an estimate from the story's numbers that lands near 68 (part h)
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: p = 158 − 3 × 30. p is the pencils left. 3 × 30 = 90, and 158 − 90 = 68. About 160 − 90 = 70, and 68 is close.
  • partly: 158 − 90 = 68 pencils. 68 is about 70. (Look-for 2 only: no letter, and the estimate comes from the answer, not the story.)
  • not: 3 × 30 = 90 pencils. (One step only, error F.)
