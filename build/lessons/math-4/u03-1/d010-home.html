Day 10: One amount, more than one name

At home

GoalI can show that one amount can have more than one fraction name
Time31 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No strip sheet: cut strips of one length from plain paper. Fold one in 2, again and again for halves, fourths and eighths. Roll one into an S and press flat for thirds; fold thirds in 2 for sixths. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The warm-up, Fold and record 2, and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Example 1 (side 1): you fold a strip, the child folds one too and fills the frame. Then Example 2 (side 1) the same way.

    Say. "Lay the folded strip on one not folded. Did the shaded part change length?" Expect: no; more parts, each smaller.

  • 8 min. Fold and record. Fold and record 1 (side 1), then 3. The child folds, draws the cuts, then writes.

  • 8 min. Fix two. Fix it 1 (side 2), then 2.

    Say. "Lu cut the shaded third too. How many parts are shaded now?" Expect: 2, so 1/3 = 2/6.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

Example 1 (side 1)A strip is cut in halves, and 12 is shaded. Fold each half into 2 equal parts. Then fold each part in 2 again. Name the shaded part each time.1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8; each fold made 2 times as many parts
  1. Hold up the halves strip: one whole, 2 equal parts, 1 shaded. That is 1/2.
  2. Fold each half into 2 equal parts, the shaded half and the other half. Open it. Count the parts in the whole: 4. Count the shaded parts: 2. So 1/2 = 2/4.
  3. Lay it on a strip that is not folded. The shaded part reaches the same place. Each part is half as big, and there are twice as many.
  4. Fold each part in 2 again. Now there are 8 parts in the whole and 4 are shaded. 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8.
  5. Every part was cut, the shaded ones too. So both numbers changed, and the shaded length stayed the same.
Example 2 (side 1)A strip is cut in thirds, and 13 is shaded. Cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the new name.1/3 = 2/6; each part was cut into 2, so there are 2 times as many parts
  1. Hold up the thirds strip: one whole, 3 equal parts, 1 shaded. That is 1/3.
  2. Fold the thirds strip on its two lines, like a Z. Fold the stack in half and open it: every third has a crease in its middle, the shaded one too.
  3. Count the parts in the whole: 6. Count the shaded parts: 2. So 1/3 = 2/6.
  4. Lay it on the sixths strip. Two sixths reach the end of the shaded third. Same length, new name.
