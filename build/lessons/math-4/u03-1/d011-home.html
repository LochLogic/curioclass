Day 11: The strip on the line

At home

GoalI can find a fraction's other names on a number line, past 1 too, and say why the point stays put
Time37 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No strip sheet: cut strips of one length from plain paper and fold them; for fifths and tenths, rule a 10 inch strip every 2 inches, then every inch. No number lines sheet: lay a strip along a ruled line and mark 0 and 1 at its ends. No projector: point at the page.

Skip. The warm-up, the readiness fix, questions 6 and 9, and the challenge.

  • 8 min. The strip on the line. Read Example 1 (side 1) together; lay and mark as it says.

    Say. "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" Expect: more parts, each smaller; the point stayed put.

  • 7 min. Up to 1. The child does Up to 1, question 4 (side 1) and question 5, counting parts from 0 aloud.

  • 5 min. Fix Ana's count. Fix it 7 (side 1).

    Say. "Where would 6/6 be?" Expect: at 1, so her point is 5/6.

  • 8 min. Past 1. Read Example 2 (side 2) together on line 4, then the child does Past 1, question 8 (side 2).

  • 4 min. Fix Max's line. Fix it 10 (side 2). Cover the line past 1 with your hand.

    Say. "How many fourths make 1?" Expect: four, so the point is 5/4.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)The line from 0 to 1 is marked in fourths. A strip 14 long lies on it from 0. Cut each fourth into 2 equal parts. What is the new name for the strip's end?2/8; the point did not move
  1. Lay the fourths strip on line 1, its left end at 0, and mark where each fourth ends. The first fourth ends at the point 1/4.
  2. Lay the eighths strip on the same line and mark where each eighth ends. Every fourth is now cut into 2 equal parts.
  3. Now 8 equal parts fill the line from 0 to 1. Each part is an eighth.
  4. Count the parts from 0 to the strip's end, not the marks: 1, 2. The point is 2/8.
  5. The strip did not change, so the point did not move. Each fourth was cut into 2, so 1/4 = 2/8.
Up to 1, question 4 (side 1)A line from 0 to 1 is in fourths. A point is at 24. Cut each fourth into 2. New name?4/8
Up to 1, question 5 (side 1)A line from 0 to 1 is in fifths. A point is at 45. Cut each fifth into 2. New name?8/10
Fix it 7 (side 1)Ana marked sixths on a line from 0 to 1. She counted every mark from the mark at 0 to her point: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. She named the point 66.5/6. Ana counted the mark at 0 as one
Example 2 (side 2)The line from 0 to 2 is marked in halves. A point is at 32. Cut each half into 4 equal parts. What is the point's new name?12/8; the point did not move
  1. On line 4, count halves from 0: one half, two halves, three halves. Two halves reach 1, so the point is one half past 1.
  2. Cut each half into 4 equal parts: mark the middle of each half, then the middle of each new part.
  3. 8 parts now fill 0 to 1, so each part is an eighth.
  4. Count the parts from 0 to the point: 12. The point is 12/8.
  5. The length from 0 did not change, so the point stayed put. Each half was cut into 4, so 3/2 = 12/8.
  6. Check: eight eighths reach 1, and four more eighths reach the point.
Past 1, question 8 (side 2)A line from 0 to 2 is in fifths. A point is at 75. Cut each fifth into 2. New name?14/10
Fix it 10 (side 2)Max's line from 0 to 2 is marked in fourths. His point is on the fifth mark after 0. He counted all 8 parts and named it 58.5/4. Max took 0 to 2 as the whole
the ticket questionBoth lines run from 0 to 2. (a) On the fourths line, put a point at 34. Cut each fourth into 2 equal parts. What is the point's new name? Did the point move? (b) On the fifths line, a point is on the eighth mark after 0. Name the point.(a) 6/8; no, the point did not move (b) 8/5
