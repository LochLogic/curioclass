Before class

Print the student page both sides, the number lines sheets, the exit tickets and the key. Check every envelope holds the fourths, fifths, eighths and tenths strips. Sort day 9's readiness checks by item and day 10's tickets by error. Fold day 10's plain long strip in fourths. Draw a line from 0 to 2 on the board, each whole as long as a long strip; mark 0, 1 and 2. Put the display page on the projector.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: a sum, a difference and a fact

Alone, top of side 1 (465 + 278 = ? [743], 802 − 357 = ? [445], 8 × 7 = ? [56]). Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes; students mark their own. Students whose day 10 ticket said 4/6 with no cuts drawn draw the cuts on it first.

A few who missed a readiness item sit with you instead: item 1 or 4, lay the fourths strip on line 1, count four fourths to 1, then slide it past the end, "five fourths"; item 2, fold a half strip again; item 3, a thirds strip beside an eighths strip.

5 min. Reteach, or day 10's error

If many missed a readiness item (more than a third): the warm-up and these 5 minutes are that item's fix, whole class. Day 10's error then goes into Ana's fix, one minute.

Otherwise, if day 10's tickets showed many A (2/6 or 4/3): show "1/3 = 1/6" beside the two strips; fold thirds into sixths again and count the shaded parts aloud. Many B (4/5): lay the 2/3 strip beside the 4/5 strip. Adding 2 to both numbers changed the length.

If neither, everyone does item 1's fix: four fourths land on 1.

5 min. Teaching first: the strip on the line

Whole class. Hold the long fourths strip on the board line from 0; fold it again for eighths. Students lay their strips on line 1 of the number lines sheet and copy each move.

Example 1 (The line from 0 to 1 is marked in fourths. A strip 1/4 long lies on it from 0. Cut each fourth into 2 equal parts. What is the new name for the strip's end? [2/8; the point did not move]).

  1. Lay the fourths strip on line 1, its left end at 0, and mark where each fourth ends. The first fourth ends at the point 1/4.
  2. Lay the eighths strip on the same line and mark where each eighth ends. Every fourth is now cut into 2 equal parts.
  3. Now 8 equal parts fill the line from 0 to 1. Each part is an eighth.
  4. Count the parts from 0 to the strip's end, not the marks: 1, 2. The point is 2/8.
  5. The strip did not change, so the point did not move. Each fourth was cut into 2, so 1/4 = 2/8.

Every time: the point is where the strip ends; count parts, not marks.

10 min. Cut and rename, up to 1

Alone, side 1 (A line from 0 to 1 is in fourths. A point is at 2/4. Cut each fourth into 2. New name? [4/8], A line from 0 to 1 is in fifths. A point is at 4/5. Cut each fifth into 2. New name? [8/10], On the fifths line, a point is at 1. Name it in fifths, then in tenths. [5/5; 10/10] for early finishers), 8 minutes. Mark the line with the matching strip, then lay the eighths or tenths strip on it and mark its parts.

Watch for one part too many, 5/8, 9/10, or 6/5 and 11/10 (error C: the mark at 0 counted), and only the denominator changed, 2/8 or 4/10 (error A). Ask: "Where does your strip end? How many parts from 0?"

Then 2 minutes in pairs: partners swap and lay a strip on each point. A mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Does your strip end at the point? Did you count parts, not marks?

5 min. Fix it: Ana's count

Ana marked sixths on a line from 0 to 1. She counted every mark from the mark at 0 to her point: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. She named the point 6/6. [5/6. Ana counted the mark at 0 as one], her line printed. Pairs, one minute, then the class. Ask: "Where would 6/6 be?" Lay the strip from 0 again: the first part ends at the first mark after 0.

If many still count marks, every student lays the sixths strip on line 1 and touches each part, saying "one sixth, two sixths..."

5 min. Past 1: the second whole

Whole class. Lay a second long strip after the first on the board line: it runs to 2. Students use line 4, printed in halves, and fill the frame under Example 2.

Example 2 (The line from 0 to 2 is marked in halves. A point is at 3/2. Cut each half into 4 equal parts. What is the point's new name? [12/8; the point did not move]).

  1. On line 4, count halves from 0: one half, two halves, three halves. Two halves reach 1, so the point is one half past 1.
  2. Cut each half into 4 equal parts: mark the middle of each half, then the middle of each new part.
  3. 8 parts now fill 0 to 1, so each part is an eighth.
  4. Count the parts from 0 to the point: 12. The point is 12/8.
  5. The length from 0 did not change, so the point stayed put. Each half was cut into 4, so 3/2 = 12/8.
  6. Check: eight eighths reach 1, and four more eighths reach the point.

10 min. Past 1, alone

Alone, side 2 (A line from 0 to 2 is in fifths. A point is at 7/5. Cut each fifth into 2. New name? [14/10], A line from 0 to 2 is in fourths. A point is at 2. Name it in fourths. Then cut each fourth into 2 and name it again. [8/4; 16/8]), on lines 5 and 6: to cut each printed part in 2, mark its middle. Early finishers: the challenge.

Watch for 7/10 and 14/20 for the first (error A, then error K: the whole line taken as the whole), and 8/8 for the point at 2 (error K). Ask: "Where is 1 on your line? How many parts make one whole?"

5 min. Fix it: Max's line

Max's line from 0 to 2 is marked in fourths. His point is on the fifth mark after 0. He counted all 8 parts and named it 5/8. [5/4. Max took 0 to 2 as the whole], his line printed. Pairs, one minute, then the class. Cover the line past 1 with a sheet of paper: four parts make 1. Uncover it and count on past 1: "five fourths."

If many said 8/8 for the point at 2, cover line 6 the same way.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" Expect, in this order: "there are more parts"; "each part is smaller"; last, "the point stayed in the same place, so it is the same amount with a new name". Students finish the frame on side 2.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The exit ticket (Both lines run from 0 to 2. (a) On the fourths line, put a point at 3/4. Cut each fourth into 2 equal parts. What is the point's new name? Did the point move? (b) On the fifths line, a point is on the eighth mark after 0. Name the point. [(a) 6/8; no, the point did not move (b) 8/5]). Both lines are printed on the ticket. Collect at 5 minutes.

Other rooms

  • No number lines sheet. Lay a whole strip along lined paper and mark 0 and 1 at its ends; for 0 to 2, rule 0, 1 and 2 evenly and measure the parts.
  • No strip sheet. Cut strips 10 inches long from scrap paper: rule fifths every 2 inches, tenths every inch; fold the rest.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop questions 6 and 9 and the partner check (students lay their own strip on each point). Never drop the two fixes or the planned question. Longer: the challenge (Can 3/8 be written in fourths? Why or why not? [No: 3/8 is between 2/8 and 4/8, that is 1/4 and 2/4, so no mark in fourths lands on it]).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: number line, point, equivalent, numerator, denominator. The frame is on side 2 and the display. A cut may be ruled, not folded.

Tomorrow

Day 12 opens its task with these tickets. Many 7/8 (error C, marks counted): day 12 shows a line where 0 is counted as "one", and the class lays the strip from 0 again. Many 3/8 (error A): fold fourths into eighths and count the shaded parts aloud. "Yes, it moved": lay the fourths strip on the line before and after the cut. Many 8/10 on part (b) (error K): cover the line past 1, count the parts in one whole, then count on. Results go on the record sheet under E11.
