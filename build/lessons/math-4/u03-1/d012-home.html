Day 12: The same amount, or not? Prove it

At home

GoalI can tell if two fractions are the same amount and prove it with a drawing
Time37 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: read each step from the key and point at side 1. No strips: fold a paper strip to check.

Skip. The warm-up, pairs 3 and 4, and the challenge.

  • 5 min. The question. The question (side 1), on the line stack.

    Say. "Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" Expect: 5/10, yes; no third is at the 1/2 point.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Read Example 1 (side 1) with its steps from the key; the child marks the lines and fills the frame. Then Example 2 (side 1).

  • 5 min. Two pairs. Pair 1 (side 1) and 2 (side 2). Check each by laying a pencil straight down from one point.

  • 6 min. Jo and Kai. Jo's claim (side 2), then Kai's claim (side 2).

    Say. "Jo added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" Expect: no; 5/6 is farther from 0.

  • 6 min. Written task. Written task (side 2). The child may draw.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone on the ticket's line, strips away.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
The question (side 1)Can 12 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?Tenths: yes, 1/2 = 5/10, one point on the lines. Thirds: no, 1/2 falls between 1/3 and 2/3
Example 1 (side 1)Are 34 and 912 the same amount? Prove it on the lines.Yes: each fourth cut into 3 makes twelfths, and 3/4 = 9/12, one point
Example 2 (side 1)Are 25 and 512 the same amount? Prove it on the lines.No: 2/5 is between 4/12 and 5/12, so the points do not line up
Pair 1 (side 1)Are 56 and 1012 the same amount?The same: one point
Pair 2 (side 2)Are 310 and 512 the same amount?Not the same: 3/10 is closer to 0
Jo's claim (side 2)Jo says 34 = 56 because she added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?No: 3/4 and 5/6 are at two points; 5/6 is farther from 0. Adding moved the point
Kai's claim (side 2)Kai says 25 and 410 are different amounts, because 4 and 10 are bigger.Not right: each fifth cut into 2 makes tenths, and 2/5 = 4/10, one point
Written task (side 2)Explain why 34 and 68 are the same amount. You may draw.Each fourth is cut into 2: twice as many parts in the whole and in the shaded piece, each half the size, so the length is the same
the ticket questionNo strips. "The line from 0 to 1 is marked in twelfths. Mark 23 and 45 as well as you can. Are they the same amount? Then write another name for 23."No: 2/3 is at 8/12 and 4/5 falls between 9/12 and 10/12; 4/6 or 8/12
