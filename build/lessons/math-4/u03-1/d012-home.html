Day 12: The same amount, or not? Prove it

At home

GoalI can tell if two fractions are the same amount and prove it with a drawing
Time37 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1. No strips: fold a paper strip to check.

Skip. The warm-up, pairs 3 and 4, and the challenge.

  • 5 min. The question. The question (side 1), on the line stack.

    Say. "Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" Expect: 5/10, yes; no third is at the 1/2 point.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Read Example 1 (side 1) together: mark 3/4, draw 2 marks in each fourth, count 9 twelfths, check on the twelfths line. Then Example 2 (side 1).

  • 5 min. Two pairs. Pair 1 (side 1) and 2 (side 2). Check each by laying a pencil straight down from one point.

  • 6 min. Jo and Kai. Jo's claim (side 2), then Kai's claim (side 2).

    Say. "Jo added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" Expect: no; 5/6 is farther from 0.

  • 6 min. Written task. Written task (side 2). The child may draw.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone on the ticket's line, strips away.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
The question (side 1)Can 12 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?Tenths: yes, 1/2 = 5/10, one point on the lines. Thirds: no, 1/2 falls between 1/3 and 2/3
Example 1 (side 1)Are 34 and 912 the same amount? Prove it on the lines.Yes: each fourth cut into 3 makes twelfths, and 3/4 = 9/12, one point
  1. Mark 3/4 on the fourths line: 3 fourths from 0.
  2. Cut each fourth into 3 equal parts. 4 × 3 = 12, so the whole now has 12 parts: twelfths.
  3. The 3 fourths are now 3 × 3 = 9 twelfths. The same point is named 9/12.
  4. Check on the twelfths line, right below: 9/12 lines up with 3/4.
  5. 3 times as many parts, each a third the size, and the length did not change. 3/4 = 9/12.
Example 2 (side 1)Are 25 and 612 the same amount? Prove it on the lines.No: 6/12 is halfway and 2/5 is short of halfway, so the points do not line up
  1. Mark 2/5 on the fifths line: 2 fifths from 0.
  2. Mark 6/12 on the twelfths line, right below: 6 twelfths from 0. 6 is half of 12, so 6/12 is halfway.
  3. Lay a pencil straight down from 2/5. Do the points line up? No: 2/5 is closer to 0.
  4. Why: half of 5 fifths is 2 and a half fifths. 2 fifths is short of halfway.
  5. The points do not line up, so 2/5 and 6/12 are not the same amount.
Pair 1 (side 1)Are 56 and 1012 the same amount?The same: 5/6 and 10/12 are one point, on the fifth sixths mark and the tenth twelfths mark
Pair 2 (side 2)Are 310 and 512 the same amount?Not the same: 3/10 (third tenths mark) is closer to 0 than 5/12 (fifth twelfths mark)
Jo's claim (side 2)Jo says 34 = 56 because she added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?No: 3/4 and 5/6 are at two points; 5/6 is farther from 0. Adding moved the point
Kai's claim (side 2)Kai says 25 and 410 are different amounts, because 4 and 10 are bigger.Not right: each fifth cut into 2 makes tenths, and 2/5 = 4/10, one point
Written task (side 2)Explain why 34 and 68 are the same amount. You may draw.Each fourth is cut into 2: twice as many parts in the whole and in the shaded piece, each half the size, so the length is the same
Look-fors:
  1. says how each part was cut
  2. says there are more parts and each is smaller
  3. says the length or the point is the same
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: I cut each fourth into 2. Now there are 8 parts and 6 are shaded. The parts are smaller, but the shaded length is the same.
  • partly: They are at the same point on the line. (Look-for 3 only: ask how each part was cut.)
  • not: 6/8 is more, because 6 and 8 are bigger. (Error D.)
the ticket questionThe line from 0 to 1 is marked in twelfths. Mark 23 and 45 on it as well as you can. Are they the same amount? Write another name for 23.No: 2/3 is on 8/12, and 4/5 is past 8/12 and short of 1 (it falls between 9/12 and 10/12); 4/6 or 8/12
