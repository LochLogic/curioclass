Before class

Print the student page, the exit tickets and the key. Print the strip sheets one-sided ahead: students cut out the strips into their envelopes as morning work, or hand them out pre-cut. Cut three long strips of one length; mark one in halves, one in thirds; shade 1/2 and 1/3. Put the display page on the projector. Have day 9's readiness results and assessments (items 3 and 5 ticked) beside you; write the "meets" answers to 3 and 5 on a covered board.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: add, subtract, two facts

Alone, then whole class: 618 + 257 [875], 804 − 368 [436], 7 × 6 [42], 9 × 8 [72]. Meanwhile hand back day 9's assessments. At the answers, uncover the "meets" answers to items 3 and 5; students fix theirs in pen. More numbers as the reason: one jump in dot pairs, then "It must keep going because ".

If the readiness check showed gaps: nothing moves today. Gaps on items 1, 3 and 4 go to day 11's warm-up and first five minutes. Item 2 (1/2 as fourths) is today's work: seat those students where they can see your strips, and look at their folds first.

15 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. You fold the long strips, held up high; students fold a blank strip from page 2 of the sheet along with you and fill the frames on side 1. The display shows each step on its own screen. Read every fraction as words: "one half", "two fourths", never "one over two".

Example 1 (A strip is cut in halves, and 1/2 is shaded. Fold each half into 2 equal parts. Then fold each part in 2 again. Name the shaded part each time. [1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8; each fold made 2 times as many parts]).

  1. Hold up the halves strip: one whole, 2 equal parts, 1 shaded. That is 1/2.
  2. Fold each half into 2 equal parts, the shaded half and the other half. Open it. Count the parts in the whole: 4. Count the shaded parts: 2. So 1/2 = 2/4.
  3. Lay it on a strip that is not folded. The shaded part reaches the same place. Each part is half as big, and there are twice as many.
  4. Fold each part in 2 again. Now there are 8 parts in the whole and 4 are shaded. 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8.
  5. Every part was cut, the shaded ones too. So both numbers changed, and the shaded length stayed the same.

Say: 1/2 and 2/4 name the same amount. Fractions like that are equivalent. Put up the card.

Example 2 (A strip is cut in thirds, and 1/3 is shaded. Cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the new name. [1/3 = 2/6; each part was cut into 2, so there are 2 times as many parts]).

  1. Hold up the thirds strip: one whole, 3 equal parts, 1 shaded. That is 1/3.
  2. Fold the thirds strip on its two lines, like a Z. Fold the stack in half and open it: every third has a crease in its middle, the shaded one too.
  3. Count the parts in the whole: 6. Count the shaded parts: 2. So 1/3 = 2/6.
  4. Lay it on the sixths strip. Two sixths reach the end of the shaded third. Same length, new name.

Say the reason in full once: when every part is cut into 2, there are twice as many parts in the whole and twice as many in the shaded piece, each half the size, so the length does not change. Do not give a rule about multiplying; section 3.2 writes it.

18 min. Fold and record

Alone, then pairs. Side 1, "Fold and record" (Shade 1/2 of a strip. Lay it on the sixths strip. 1/2 = /6 [1/2 = 3/6], Shade 2/5 of the fifths strip. Fold each fifth in 2. Draw your cuts. 2/5 = /10 [2/5 = 4/10], Shade 5/6 of the sixths strip. Cut each sixth into 2 equal parts. Draw your cuts. 5/6 = /12 [5/6 = 10/12]), 14 minutes. Shade blank strips only; printed strips are laid, never shaded. On items 2 and 3, shade and draw each cut on the strip printed on the page, then write the name. Fifths and sixths are too small to fold: mark each middle in pencil, then check with the tenths or twelfths strip. Item 2 is the first time two shaded parts are cut: count the shaded fifths, then the shaded tenths, aloud. Then 4 minutes in pairs: partners lay two strips side by side and check that the shaded lengths match.

Check: Lay the strips together. Does the shaded part reach the same place?

Watch for a new name with only one number changed (error A: 1/6 on the first, 2/10 or 4/5 on the second, 5/12 or 10/6 on the third), and for the same number added to both (error B: 5/6, 7/10, 11/12). Ask as you walk: "How many parts are in the whole now? How many are shaded?" If many make A, fold thirds into sixths again with the class and count the shaded parts aloud.

12 min. Fix two wrong answers

"Fix it", side 2 (Lu shaded 1/3 of a strip. She cut each third into 2 equal parts. She wrote 1/3 = 1/6. [1/3 = 2/6. Lu cut the shaded third too, so 2 parts are shaded, not 1], Mo shaded 3/6 of a strip. He cut each sixth into 2 equal parts. He wrote 3/6 = 6/6. [3/6 = 6/12. Mo counted the new shaded parts but not the new parts in the whole]). Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then the class. The display shows each wrong picture beside the right one. Lu cut the shaded third too, so it is 2 parts: 1/6 beside 1/3 is plainly shorter. Mo recounted the shaded parts but not the whole: 6/6 is the whole strip, and only half is shaded.

If many still keep one number, every student lays their thirds strip on the sixths strip and counts aloud: "one sixth, two sixths".

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class, at your long halves strip.

Ask. "1/2 and 4/8 have different numbers. How can they be the same amount?" Expect: "the parts are smaller, but there are more of them"; "the shaded part is the same length". Take the length answer first, then the parts.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Shade 2/3 of the strip. Cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the new name: 2/3 = /. [2/3 = 4/6, with every third cut (6 parts) and 4 shaded]). Read it aloud once. Strips stay in the envelope; the ticket's strip is drawn on. A tick needs the cuts drawn (6 parts, 4 shaded) as well as 4/6. Record results on the record sheet under E10. Collect the envelopes for day 11.

Other rooms

  • No strip sheet. Students cut eight strips of one length from scrap paper and fold them as At home's Swap says (twelfths: the sixths in 2 again). Fifths and tenths: rule a 10 inch strip every 2 inches, then every inch.
  • No printer. Write the three "Fold and record" questions and the two fixes on the board; students answer in their notebooks.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop Fold and record 3 and the challenge. Never drop the fixes or the strips. Longer: the challenge (Lay the fourths, sixths, eighths and twelfths strips under the halves strip. Write every name for 1/2 you find. [2/4, 3/6, 4/8, 6/12]). The search ends when all four strips are laid.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: equivalent, numerator, denominator, each on a labelled strip. Frames, to take or leave: "Each part was cut into . There are times as many parts." "Each part is smaller. The length is the same, so = ." Access: pre-cut, wide strips; a fold may be ruled with a pencil line instead.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 2/6 or 4/3 (error A, one number changed): day 11 shows Lu's 1/3 = 1/6 beside the right strips, and the class folds thirds into sixths again, counting the shaded parts aloud. 4/5 (error B, 2 added to both): day 11 lays the 2/3 strip beside the 4/5 strip, which is plainly longer. 4/6 with no cuts drawn: these students draw their cuts first in day 11's warm-up. Bring back the envelopes: day 11 lays the strips on the number line.
