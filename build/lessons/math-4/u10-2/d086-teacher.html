Day 86: Which kind of comparison?

Before class

Print the student page both sides, about 8 strip sheets, the exit tickets and the key. Write the warm-up problem on the board. Put the display page on the projector.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: one grade 3 two-step problem

Alone, in notebooks. Pia has 7 packs of 8 stickers. She gives away 9 stickers. How many stickers does she have left? [s = 7 × 8 − 9; 47 stickers] Students write two short equations (56 stickers, then 47), then one with a letter, and say what the letter stands for. Watch for 56, one step only: ask "Is that what the question asked?"

3 min. Day 85's exit back

Whole class. If day 85's tickets showed many C ("Jon read 4 times as many"): say the answer as a full sentence, "Pat read 4 times as many pages as Jon", and test it: draw Jon's 5-strip, then four copies. The long strip is Pat's, so Pat's name goes first. Otherwise one student reads the right sentence and points to the longer strip. Students who wrote 15 or 100 sit near you for the sort.

5 min. Teaching first: two problems that look alike

Whole class. Students fill the two frames on side 1 as you go. Ask "Copies or extra?" before any working.

Example 1 (Nora has 8 marbles. Owen has 6 times as many marbles as Nora. How many marbles does Owen have? [Times as many; o = 6 × 8; Owen has 48 marbles]).

  1. Read it. Who has more? Owen.
  2. Copies or extra? 6 times as many means 6 copies of Nora's 8.
  3. Stuck? Draw an 8-strip for Nora and 6 copies of it for Owen. Put o under Owen's strips.
  4. Equation: o = 6 × 8.
  5. o = 48. Owen has 48 marbles.
  6. Reread: is 48 six copies of 8? 6 × 8 = 48. Yes.

Example 2 (Nora has 8 marbles. Owen has 6 more marbles than Nora. How many marbles does Owen have? [More than; o = 8 + 6; Owen has 14 marbles]).

  1. Read it. Who has more? Owen.
  2. Copies or extra? 6 more than means Nora's 8 and 6 extra.
  3. Stuck? Draw an 8-strip for Nora. For Owen, the same 8-strip and a short piece of 6. Put o under Owen's strip.
  4. Equation: o = 8 + 6.
  5. o = 14. Owen has 14 marbles.
  6. Reread: is 14 six more than 8? Yes.

Say steps 2, 4 and 5 only; the display shows them all.

Ask. "Both problems use 8 and 6. Why are the answers so different?" Expect: "times as many is 6 copies of 8"; "more than is 8 and 6 extra".

Say: from today, draw the strip only when you are stuck. When you are sure, say what it would look like.

18 min. Sort cards on paper (Growth Spurt, Which one?, when it exists)

Alone. The eight cards on side 1 (Leo has 5 stickers. Mia has 7 times as many stickers as Leo. How many does Mia have? [Times as many; m = 7 × 5; 35 stickers], A rope is 9 feet long. A hose is 7 feet longer than the rope. How long is the hose? [More than; h = 9 + 7; 16 feet], A big box holds 36 crayons. That is 4 times as many as a small box holds. How many does the small box hold? [Times as many; 4 × s = 36, or s = 36 ÷ 4; 9 crayons], A dog weighs 56 pounds. A cat weighs 7 pounds. The dog weighs how many times as much as the cat? [Times as many; n × 7 = 56, or n = 56 ÷ 7; 8 times], Ivy read 43 pages. That is 9 more pages than Sam read. How many pages did Sam read? [More than; s + 9 = 43, or s = 43 − 9; 34 pages], A short ribbon is 8 inches long. A long ribbon is 9 times as long. How long is the long ribbon? [Times as many; r = 9 × 8; 72 inches], Tia has 49 beads. Max has 7 beads. How many more beads does Tia have than Max? [More than; 7 + b = 49, or b = 49 − 7; 42 beads], Kim picked 45 apples. Gus picked 5 apples. Kim picked how many times as many apples as Gus? [Times as many; n × 5 = 45, or n = 45 ÷ 5; 9 times]). For each card: who has more? What does the story say or ask: times as many (copies), or more than (extra)? The student says what the strip would look like, then writes the card number in the sort table. A student who cannot say it draws it on the strip sheet. Nobody solves yet.

At 12 minutes, read the kinds aloud from the key, card by card; students tick or fix their own. Cards 2, 5 and 7 are "more than". On card 7 say: "49 is 7 copies of 7, and also 7 and 42 extra. The question decides: it asks how many more."

Check (sort): Listen to each kind. Tick it or fix it.

Watch for card 7 sorted "times as many": 49 and 7 look like a division. Ask "Does it ask how many more, or how many times?" On card 2, ask "Longer by copies, or by 7 feet?" If many miss cards 5 and 7, draw card 7 both ways on the board and ask which picture the words make.

12 min. Solve four cards, then a partner check

Alone, 9 minutes: cards 3, 4, 5 and 7 on side 2, each with a letter equation and a sentence answer. Then 3 minutes in pairs: partners swap and check by rereading the story with the answer in it ("Is 34 pages 9 fewer than 43?"). A mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer.

Watch for 144 on card 3 (error B): ask "Who has more? The answer must fit the longer strip." On 392 for card 4, ask "392 times? Is the dog as heavy as 392 cats?" Watch for 52 on card 5 (error C): Sam read fewer pages than Ivy. If many miss card 3, draw 36 as four equal pieces with s under one piece.

10 min. Written task: two stories, the same children

Alone, 7 minutes: Write one story where the answer is 2 × 9. Write another where the answer is 2 + 9. Use the same two children in both. [For example: Ali has 9 shells and Bea has 2 times as many, so Bea has 18; Ali has 9 shells and Bea has 2 more, so Bea has 11] Look-fors:

  1. the first story says "times as many" or "times as much"
  2. the second story says "more than"

Then 3 minutes, whole class: two students read theirs; the class says which is which.

Ask. "How did you make the 2 mean copies in one story and extra in the other?" Expect: "times as many as" in one, "more than" in the other.

Read the rest after class against the key's samples.

7 min. Exit ticket

Alone. A pumpkin weighs 24 pounds. That is 4 times as much as a melon weighs. A squash weighs 4 pounds more than the pumpkin. How much does the melon weigh? How much does the squash weigh? [Melon 6 pounds; squash 28 pounds] Read it aloud once. A strip only if stuck, on the back. Collect the tickets for tomorrow.

Other rooms

  • No devices, or the tool not yet built. The sort cards are the paper path. When it exists: Which one? for the same 18 minutes, stop at 18. Students still say the strip in words before they tap a kind.
  • One projector. Read one card at a time aloud; students hold up one finger for times as many, two for more than, then show the key screen.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: solve cards 3 and 7 only; keep the whole sort, the written task and the exit. Longer: the challenge (Ada has 8 shells. Bo has 3 times as many shells as Ada. Cy has 7 more shells than Bo. How many shells does Cy have? [Bo has 3 × 8 = 24; Cy has 24 + 7 = 31 shells]), then a card of the student's own for a partner to sort.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many, times as much, more than, longer than, how many times. Frames, printed on side 2: " has times as many as ." " has more than ." Read any card aloud when asked; a student may retell it in a home language first.

Tomorrow

Day 87 opens with readiness item 3's reteach if many missed it; otherwise its first 5 minutes go to this exit. On the ticket, melon 96 (error B), or melon 20 or squash 96 (error A, the two kinds mixed): day 87 redraws the pumpkin as 4 copies of the melon, then the squash as the pumpkin plus a piece of 4. Squash 10: the 4 went on the melon; reread whose weight. The two-stories task goes back in day 87's first five minutes. Both stories one kind (error A): those students join the A redraw. Check first, in day 87's problems, the students who drew most strips today.
