Day 85: How many times as many?

Before class

Print the student page, the exit tickets and the key. Cut a few strip sheets into strips. Sort day 84's tickets: right, 147, and 14 or 28. Write the warm-up problem on the board. Put the display page on the projector. Have one index card per pair.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: one grade 3 two-step problem

Alone, in notebooks, untimed: the crayon problem on the board (A box has 50 crayons. 3 children each take 8 crayons. How many crayons are left? Write an equation with a letter. [c = 50 − 3 × 8; 26 crayons]). Pattern Painter (grade 3) may stand in when it exists. Watch for 24, the crayons taken (error F), and 376, worked left to right (error H). At 5 minutes one student reads the equation and the class checks: 3 × 8 = 24, 50 − 24 = 26.

3 min. Day 84's tickets back

Whole class, tickets handed back. Students whose ticket had no letter or no strips sit near you through the teaching.

If day 84's tickets showed many 147 (multiplied, error B) or 14 or 28 (error A): before any numbers, ask "Who has more, the big tank or the small one?" Draw the small tank's strip, then the big tank's as seven copies of it. The answer must fit the short strip: 3 fish.

Otherwise, one student with a right ticket draws the two tank strips and says the equation.

5 min. Teaching first: the number of times unknown

Whole class. Yesterday the routine found a missing amount. Today both amounts are given and the number of times is missing. Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go.

Example (Gus picked 36 apples. Nell picked 4 apples. Gus picked how many times as many apples as Nell? [n × 4 = 36, or n = 36 ÷ 4; 9 times]).

  1. Read it. Who has more? Gus, so Gus gets the long strip.
  2. Draw Nell's strip and write 4 on it.
  3. Draw Gus's strip under it, as long as 36. We do not know how many copies of 4 fit, so write n on it.
  4. Equation: n × 4 = 36. It says the same thing as n = 36 ÷ 4.
  5. Count by fours: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36. That is 9 copies, so n = 9.
  6. Answer in a sentence: Gus picked 9 times as many apples as Nell.
  7. Reread the story with the answer in it: 9 copies of 4 make 36. It fits.

The word says times; the strips say divide.

18 min. Six problems: the unknown in all three places

Alone, then pairs for the last 3 minutes (Rosa has 8 marbles. Teo has 7 times as many marbles as Rosa. How many marbles does Teo have? [t = 7 × 8; 56 marbles], Pia has 42 stickers. That is 6 times as many stickers as Sam has. How many stickers does Sam have? [6 × s = 42, or s = 42 ÷ 6; 7 stickers], A tall jar holds 48 buttons. A small jar holds 8 buttons. The tall jar holds how many times as many buttons as the small jar? [n × 8 = 48, or n = 48 ÷ 8; 6 times], A whale is 72 feet long. That is 9 times as long as a dolphin. How long is the dolphin? [9 × d = 72, or d = 72 ÷ 9; 8 feet], A puppy weighs 9 pounds. A big dog weighs 10 times as much as the puppy. How much does the big dog weigh? [d = 10 × 9; 90 pounds], A tower is 45 feet tall. A shed is 9 feet tall. The tower is how many times as tall as the shed? [n × 9 = 45, or n = 45 ÷ 9; 5 times]). Problems 1 to 3 are one of each kind; everyone finishes those. 4 to 6 as time allows; problem 6 is drawn for all in the discussion.

Ask "Who has more?" before you look at any numbers. Watch for 252 or 648 on problems 2 and 4 (error B), a difference such as 36 or 40 (error A), 64 or 99, one copy too many (error D): count the copies aloud, and a sentence that gives the times to the smaller amount (error C). If many miss problems 2 and 4, stop and redraw the whale: the long strip is 72, so the dolphin's must be shorter.

At 15 minutes partners check each other's. Check: Read the story again with your answer in it. Does it fit the strips?

7 min. Discussion: two pictures, one equation

Whole class. Draw problem 6 (A tower is 45 feet tall. A shed is 9 feet tall. The tower is how many times as tall as the shed? [n × 9 = 45, or n = 45 ÷ 9; 5 times]) twice on the board. Left: the shed's 9-strip, and under it five 9-strips, every copy shown, bracketed and labelled 45. Right: the shed's 9-strip, and under it one long strip labelled 45, with "5 times" over it. Write n × 9 = 45 under both, then n = 5.

Ask. "Where is the 5 in each picture? The 9? The 45?" Expect: "five boxes on the left"; "the label on the right"; "the 9 is the shed".

Ask. "Which picture would you draw for 9 times? Why?" Expect: the long strip; nine copies take a while.

8 min. Pairs write a problem

Pairs. Each pair writes one "how many times as many" problem on a card, with numbers from the times tables, and solves it on the back. Pairs swap, solve with strips and an equation, then check against the back. A question that gives the times away ("Ana has 3 times as many") is a larger-amount problem: ask them to hide the times.

5 min. Fix it: who has more?

Pairs for one minute, then the class (Ann has 24 pens. Raj has 3 pens. Ali says: "Raj has 8 times as many pens as Ann." [Ann has 8 times as many pens as Raj; 24 = 8 × 3. Ali swapped the two children]). Ask "Who has more? Whose strip is long?" The left picture is Ali's sentence drawn: Raj's strip as 8 copies of Ann's 24. Raj has only 3 pens. Ann has more, so Ann has 8 times as many. The bigger amount stands alone in 24 = 8 × 3. If many still swap: everyone says problem 3's answer as a full sentence and points to the long strip.

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "The question said 'how many times'. Did we multiply? How did you know?" Expect: "we divided, because both amounts were given"; "we counted copies of the short strip". Take the first, then the second.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The exit ticket (Pat read 20 pages. Jon read 5 pages. Pat read how many times as many pages as Jon? Write an equation with a letter. Solve it, and answer in a sentence. [n × 5 = 20, or n = 20 ÷ 5; 4 times; Pat read 4 times as many pages as Jon]). Write the result on the record sheet under E85.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The problems on the board two at a time; strips in notebooks.
  • One projector. One problem at a time; the class holds up the equation before you show it.
  • Strips to move. A student who cannot draw the copies lays paper strips.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: the pairs part becomes 4 minutes, each pair solving its own card. Never drop the discussion, the fix or the sentence answers. Longer: the challenge (Lin has 2 beads. Mae has 3 times as many beads as Lin. Ned has 3 times as many beads as Mae. Ned has how many times as many beads as Lin? [Mae 6, Ned 18; Ned has 9 times as many as Lin]), then a problem whose answer is "10 times".

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, times as long as, how many times. Frames on the display: " has times as many as ." "The long strip is copies of the short strip." Students retell each story before drawing, in a home language if they wish.

Tomorrow
