Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips and the key. Have day 79's readiness results and the E82 slips in hand. Draw the Bo table on the board: "Bo has", "Cy has 3 more", "Cy has 3 times as many", four empty rows. Put the display page on the projector.

The day

5 min. Warm-up

Alone. Two sentences from unit 10.1 turned into equations, and times tables.

If the readiness check showed gaps (more than a third missed item 2): the next five minutes are the unit opener's reteach for the whole class, and E82's wrong example goes beside the strips in Teaching first. If fewer missed it, those students do the reteach with you now: two strips, one above the other, and they mark the part of the longer strip that sticks out.

5 min. Readiness item 2, or day 82's exit back

Whole class. If many of day 82's slips wrote an addition, 2 + 7 = 9 or 10 + 6 = 16 (error A): A's wrong example again, "8 times as many as 3 is 11", beside eight copies of the 3-strip. If many wrote 16 × 2 = 32 for the ropes (error B): reread Kai's sentence and ask "who has more?" before any numbers. If neither, one student reads an equation; the class says its sentence.

8 min. Teaching first: one story, told two ways

Whole class. Students draw in the boxes on side 1 as you go. Draw both strips on the board, one under the other, and leave them up.

Example 1 (Ada has 6 shells. Eli has 2 more shells than Ada. How many shells does Eli have? [6 + 2 = 8; 8 shells]).

  1. Draw Ada's strip: 6 shells long.
  2. Draw Eli's strip under it, starting at the same place. Eli has all that Ada has.
  3. Add a short piece to Eli's strip for the 2 more. That piece sticks out.
  4. 6 + 2 = 8. Eli has 8 shells.
  5. Say it: 8 is 2 more than 6.

Example 2 (Ada has 6 shells. Eli has 2 times as many shells as Ada. How many shells does Eli have? [2 × 6 = 12; 12 shells]).

  1. Draw Ada's strip again: 6 shells long.
  2. Eli's strip is 2 copies of Ada's strip, end to end. Count the copies: one, two.
  3. The 2 tells how many copies, not how many extra shells.
  4. 2 × 6 = 12. Eli has 12 shells.
  5. Say it: 12 is 2 times as many as 6.

Ask. "In which story does the 2 tell you how many copies?" Expect: "the times story; in the more story the 2 is a short piece". The piece that sticks out is the readiness picture: that is what "more than" looks like.

15 min. Growth Spurt, Growing: the Bo table on paper

Growing, with its comparison switch, is spec only. When it exists: students drag the bean and flip between "times as tall" and "taller by" for these 15 minutes. Until then, the Bo table does the same job.

Alone, then whole class. Students write each row on side 1 before you fill it in, in this order:

  • Bo has 1 stamp. [3 more: 4; 3 times as many: 3]
  • Bo has 4 stamps. [3 more: 7; 3 times as many: 12]
  • Bo has 5 stamps. [3 more: 8; 3 times as many: 15]
  • Bo has 10 stamps. [3 more: 13; 3 times as many: 30]

For each row one student says an answer as a strip: "a piece of 3 on Bo's strip", or "3 copies of Bo's strip".

Watch for the same number in both columns (error A), and for 16 or 20 in the times column, Bo's strip then three more copies (error D): count the copies aloud, Bo's strip as copy one.

Ask. "If Bo had 10 stamps, which gives Cy more: 3 more, or 3 times as many? What if Bo had 1?" Expect: "times as many, 30 beats 13"; "at 1, 3 more wins". Then: "Which column grows faster?" The 3 more column keeps its gap of 3; the times column pulls away.

12 min. Four pairs, each drawn both ways

Pairs. Side 2 (Kit has 8 marbles. Rue has 3 more marbles than Kit. Sol has 3 times as many marbles as Kit. How many do Rue and Sol each have? [Rue 11 (8 + 3); Sol 24 (3 × 8)], Max has 9 cards. Nia has 5 more cards than Max. Oz has 5 times as many cards as Max. How many do Nia and Oz each have? [Nia 14 (9 + 5); Oz 45 (5 × 9)], A green ribbon is 6 inches long. A red ribbon is 7 inches longer. A blue ribbon is 7 times as long as the green one. How long are the red and blue ribbons? [Red 13 inches (6 + 7); blue 42 inches (7 × 6)], Ida has 70 buttons. Jo has 2 more buttons than Ida. Kai has 2 times as many buttons as Ida. How many do Jo and Kai each have? [Jo 72 (70 + 2); Kai 140 (2 × 70)]). Each partner draws both strips for every story, then they compare. The strip comes before the number.

Ask as you walk: "Is the small number a piece, or a number of copies?" If many give both children the same number: back to Ada and Eli on the board; point to the piece, then to the copies.

5 min. A phrase we will not use

Whole class, a talk, not a correction (error E, a first idea). Say once, aloud: "Some people say '3 times more than 4 dollars'. Is that 12 dollars, or 16?" Write only 12 and 16 on the board, never the phrase. Let both be argued: 12 is 3 times as many; 16 is the 4 with 12 more on top. "People mean different things, so we say 'times as many' or 'times as much'." Nothing is marked.

5 min. Fix it

Alone 2 minutes, then whole class (Bo has 4 stamps. Cy has 10 times as many stamps as Bo. Lu says Cy has 14. [10 × 4 = 40; Cy has 40 stamps. Lu added]). The display shows Lu's working beside the right working. The 10 is how many copies of Bo's strip, not a piece to add. If many miss it: error A's action from the unit opener, "8 more than 3" and "8 times as many as 3" drawn side by side.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone. The slip (Ty has 4 toy cars. Mo has 2 more toy cars than Ty. Di has 2 times as many toy cars as Ty. Draw a strip picture for Mo and one for Di. How many cars does each have? [Mo 6 (a 4-strip and a 2-piece); Di 8 (two 4-strips)]), read aloud once. Results go on the record sheet under E83.

Other rooms

  • No devices, or Growth Spurt not yet built. The paper path above is the day.
  • One projector. Once the tool exists, you drag the bean; the class writes the next height on paper first.
  • One child at home. Beans: you set out 4; the child sets out 3 more, then 3 times as many.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs 1 and 3 only. Never cut the strips, the talk or the fix. Longer: the challenge (Pat has some cards. Sam has 2 more cards than Pat. Tia has 2 times as many cards as Pat. Sam and Tia have the same number. How many cards does Pat have? [2 cards: 2 more than 2 is 4, and 2 times as many as 2 is 4]), then "Find a start where 4 more beats 4 times as many."

Words and supports

Word Wall cards, each with its strip picture: more than, times as many, times as much, copies. A student may say a comparison in a home language first. Frames on the display: " is more than ." " is times as many as ."
