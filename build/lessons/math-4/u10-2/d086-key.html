Day 86: Which kind of comparison?: key

Each answer is worked out in full. The last column names the wrong answers to expect and the error each one shows.

Any equal form of an equation is right: the two factors in either order, either side of the equals sign, any letter, or the matching division (n × 9 = 36, 9 × n = 36, 36 = 9 × n and 36 ÷ 9 = n all say the same).

practices10-W864.OA.A.2 (e), MP.2Write one story where the answer is 2 × 9. Write another where the answer is 2 + 9. Use the same two children in both.For example: Ali has 9 shells and Bea has 2 times as many, so Bea has 18; Ali has 9 shells and Bea has 2 more, so Bea has 11
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. the first story says "times as many" or "times as much"
  2. the second story says "more than"
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Ali has 9 shells. Bea has 2 times as many shells as Ali. Bea has 18. Then: Ali has 9 shells. Bea has 2 more shells than Ali. Bea has 11.
  • partly: Bea has 2 times as many shells as Ali's 9, so 18. Bea has 2 times more, so 11. (Look-for 1 only: the second story does not say "more than".)
  • not: Ali has 9 shells and Bea has 2 more, so 18. (The words say more than; the number is times as many: error A.)
both stories say "times", or both say "more": error A, mixes the two comparisons: reads 'times as many' as 'more than' (5 times as many as 3 is 8), or multiplies in a 'more than' story (3 more than 5 is 15)
