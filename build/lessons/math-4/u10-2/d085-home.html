Day 85: How many times as many?

At home

GoalI can find how many times as many one amount is as another
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1. No index card: any scrap of paper. Coins stand in for paper strips.

Skip. The warm-up, problems 4 and 5, and the challenge.

  • 6 min. Count the groups. Example (side 1): lay out 4 coins, then 36; the child counts the groups of 4 and fills the frame on side 1.

  • 12 min. Three problems. Problem 1 (side 1), 2, 3. Ask "Who has more?" before any number.

    Say. "Read the story again with your answer in it. Does it fit?" Expect: for the jars, 6 × 8 = 48, so 6 times fits.

  • 5 min. Two pictures. Draw Problem 6 (side 2) twice: five 9-strips, then one long strip marked "5 times".

  • 7 min. Fix it, then write one. Fix it (side 2). Then the child writes a "how many times as many" problem for you to solve.

    Say. "Who has more? Whose strip is long?" Expect: Ann; Ann has 8 times as many pens as Raj.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example (side 1)Gus picked 36 apples. Nell picked 4 apples. Gus picked how many times as many apples as Nell?n × 4 = 36, or n = 36 ÷ 4; 9 times
  1. Read it. Who has more? Gus, so Gus gets the long strip.
  2. Draw Nell's strip and write 4 on it.
  3. Draw Gus's strip under it, as long as 36. We do not know how many copies of 4 fit, so write n on it.
  4. Equation: n × 4 = 36. It says the same thing as n = 36 ÷ 4.
  5. Count by fours: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36. That is 9 copies, so n = 9.
  6. Answer in a sentence: Gus picked 9 times as many apples as Nell.
  7. Reread the story with the answer in it: 9 copies of 4 make 36. It fits.
Problem 1 (side 1)Rosa has 8 marbles. Teo has 7 times as many marbles as Rosa. How many marbles does Teo have?t = 7 × 8; 56 marbles
Problem 2 (side 1)Pia has 42 stickers. That is 6 times as many stickers as Sam has. How many stickers does Sam have?6 × s = 42, or s = 42 ÷ 6; 7 stickers
Problem 3 (side 1)A tall jar holds 48 buttons. A small jar holds 8 buttons. The tall jar holds how many times as many buttons as the small jar?n × 8 = 48, or n = 48 ÷ 8; 6 times
Problem 6 (side 2)A tower is 45 feet tall. A shed is 9 feet tall. The tower is how many times as tall as the shed?n × 9 = 45, or n = 45 ÷ 9; 5 times
Fix it (side 2)Ann has 24 pens. Raj has 3 pens. Ali says: "Raj has 8 times as many pens as Ann."Ann has 8 times as many pens as Raj; 24 = 8 × 3. Ali swapped the two children
the ticket questionPat read 20 pages. Jon read 5 pages. Pat read how many times as many pages as Jon? Write an equation with a letter. Solve it, and answer in a sentence.n × 5 = 20, or n = 20 ÷ 5; 4 times; Pat read 4 times as many pages as Jon
