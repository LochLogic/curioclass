Day 84: Which amount is missing?

At home

GoalI can solve a comparison problem when the bigger or the smaller amount is missing
Time33 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at the printed strips on side 1. No strip sheet: squares in a row on plain paper.

Skip. The warm-up, problems 4 to 6, and the challenge.

  • 10 min. One story, the letter moved. Example 1 (side 1), then 2; the child fills the frames under the strips. Same strips, only the letter moved: on the long strip, multiply; on the short one, divide.

  • 12 min. Three problems. Six problems 1 (side 2), 2 and 3: two strips with the letter on the unknown, an equation, a sentence. Act one out with the child's toys if it helps.

    Say. "Who has more? Is your letter on the long strip or the short one?" Expect: for the rope, the long rope has more; j is on the short strip, so divide: 7 feet.

  • 6 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 1).

    Say. "Who has more, Jo or Max?" Expect: Jo; Max's strip is the short one, so 24 cannot be right. Max has 6.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)A kitten weighs 7 pounds. A dog weighs 8 times as much as the kitten. How much does the dog weigh?d = 8 × 7; 56 pounds
  1. Read it. Who weighs more? The dog: it weighs 8 times as much as the kitten.
  2. Draw the kitten's strip: one box. Write 7 in it.
  3. Draw the dog's strip under it: 8 boxes, each the same size as the kitten's box.
  4. The dog's weight is the unknown. Bracket the whole dog strip and write d.
  5. Equation: d = 8 × 7.
  6. Solve: 8 × 7 = 56, so d = 56.
  7. Answer in a sentence: The dog weighs 56 pounds.
  8. Reread the story with 56 in it. The dog's strip is the longer one, and 56 is the bigger number. It fits.
Example 2 (side 1)A dog weighs 56 pounds. That is 8 times as much as a kitten weighs. How much does the kitten weigh?8 × k = 56, or k = 56 ÷ 8; 7 pounds
  1. Read it. Who weighs more? The dog. "That is 8 times as much" says the dog is 8 copies of the kitten.
  2. Draw the kitten's strip: one box. The kitten's weight is the unknown now, so write k in it.
  3. Draw the dog's strip under it: 8 boxes, each the same size as k. Bracket the whole dog strip and write 56.
  4. Equation: 8 × k = 56. Eight copies of k make 56.
  5. Solve: 8 times what is 56? 8 × 7 = 56, so k = 7. The same as k = 56 ÷ 8.
  6. Answer in a sentence: The kitten weighs 7 pounds.
  7. Reread the story with 7 in it. The kitten's strip is the shorter one, and 7 is less than 56. It fits.
Six problems 1 (side 2)Lin has 9 stickers. Gus has 4 times as many stickers as Lin. How many stickers does Gus have?g = 4 × 9; 36 stickers
Six problems 2 (side 2)A rope is 42 feet long. That is 6 times as long as a jump rope. How long is the jump rope?6 × j = 42, or j = 42 ÷ 6; 7 feet
Six problems 3 (side 2)Zoe picked 5 times as many apples as Ben. Zoe picked 45 apples. How many apples did Ben pick?5 × b = 45, or b = 45 ÷ 5; 9 apples
Fix it 1 (side 1)Lu says: "Jo has 12 shells. That is 2 times as many as Max has. So Max has 24 shells."2 × m = 12, or m = 12 ÷ 2; 6 shells. Lu multiplied because the story says times
the ticket questionA big tank holds 7 times as many fish as a small tank. The big tank holds 21 fish. How many fish does the small tank hold? Draw strips, write an equation with a letter, and solve.7 × s = 21, or s = 21 ÷ 7; 3 fish
