Day 25: A fraction as a sum of pieces

At home

GoalI can write a fraction as a sum of unit fractions, past 1 too
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No pieces: cut paper strips of one length into thirds, fourths, fifths and sixths, two strips of each. No line page: draw a line from 0 to 2. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The warm-up, day 24's hand-back, the tenths fix and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Example 1 (side 1), then 2: you lay the pieces, the child lays them too, writes one unit fraction for each piece and fills the frame.

    Say. "How many pieces, and how big is each one?" Expect: seven, each one fourth.

  • 12 min. Board paths. Board paths 1 (side 1), 2, 3 (side 2): one piece a board, then the sum, then more or less than 1.

    Say. "Where is 1 on your row?" Expect: after six sixths on path 3; one more sixth is past it.

  • 6 min. Fix it. Fix it 5 (side 2): the child lays a fourth third-piece and says where it ends.

  • 7 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)Lay fifth-pieces in a row to make 35. Write 35 as a sum of unit fractions.1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/5
  1. A fifth-piece is one of 5 equal parts of a whole strip. Its name is 1/5, one fifth.
  2. Lay one piece. Write 1/5. Say: one fifth.
  3. Lay a second piece touching it. Write + 1/5. Say: two fifths.
  4. Lay a third piece. Write + 1/5. Say: three fifths.
  5. Three pieces, each of size one fifth: 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/5.
  6. The bottom number names the size of each piece. The top number counts the pieces. Laying pieces together does not change their size, so the bottom stays 5.
Example 2 (side 1)Lay fourth-pieces in a row, then mark 74 on the line from 0 to 2. Write 74 as a sum of unit fractions.1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 7/4; 7/4 is more than 1
  1. Each piece is one fourth, 1/4. Lay the first piece on the desk. Say: one fourth.
  2. Lay pieces end to end in a row: two fourths, three fourths, four fourths. Four fourths make 1, one whole strip long.
  3. The count does not stop at 1. Keep going: five fourths, six fourths, seven fourths. Stop at 7/4.
  4. Write one 1/4 for each piece: 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 7/4.
  5. On the line from 0 to 2, count seven fourths from 0 and mark the end: 7/4.
  6. Is 7/4 more or less than 1? More. Four fourths make 1, and there are 3 fourths more.
Board paths 1 (side 1)Path 1 is 46 meter long. Each board is 16 meter. Write the path as a sum of boards. Is it more or less than 1 meter?1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 4/6; less than 1 meter: six sixths make 1, and there are only 4
Board paths 2 (side 1)Path 2 is 53 meters long. Each board is 13 meter. Write the path as a sum of boards. Is it more or less than 1 meter?1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 5/3; more than 1 meter: three thirds make 1, and there are 2 thirds more
Board paths 3 (side 2)Path 3 is 76 meters long. Each board is 16 meter. Write the path as a sum of boards. Is it more or less than 1 meter?1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 7/6; more than 1 meter: six sixths make 1, and there is 1 sixth more
Fix it 5 (side 2)Ada counted third-pieces: 13, 23, 33. She says there is no 43, because 33 is all of it.There is a 4/3: 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 4/3. The count goes on past 1
the ticket questionWrite 65 as a sum of unit fractions. Is 65 more or less than 1? How do you know?1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 6/5; more: five fifths make 1, and there is one fifth more. A reason says five fifths make 1 and there is one fifth more, or points past 1 on a line; "the top is bigger" alone is not a reason
