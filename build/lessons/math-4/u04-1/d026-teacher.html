Day 26: One amount, more than one way

Before class

Print the student page, strip sheets, tickets and key. Students cut the eighths and twelfths strips into pieces for the day 25 envelope, as morning work (or hand out pre-cut). Have day 25's tickets and the readiness marks (R1 to R4) beside you.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: facts within 20

Alone (Find each: 9 + 6, 14 − 8, 8 + 6, 16 − 9, 4 + 9, 11 − 3 [15, 6, 14, 7, 13, 8]). Students who missed readiness item 4 sit with you and say how they found each fact ("9 and 1 make 10, and 5 more"); they keep the facts warm-ups through day 31. At 4 minutes, show the answers.

Check: Is yours the same? Fix it in pen.

5 min. Readiness gaps and day 25's tickets

Whole class for what more than a third showed; otherwise the students named, at your table, while the rest check their envelopes, thirds to twelfths.

  • If day 25's tickets showed many B (five 1/5s, or "less, it is a fraction"), or readiness item 1: show "There is no 4/3: 3/3 is all of it" beside third-pieces counted aloud past 1 on line 1, thirds marked by eye.
  • If many A (a sum = 6/30): show "1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 = 4/40" beside four tenth-pieces, said as "four tenths".
  • Five 1/6s: those students read 6/5 aloud as "six fifths" and lay six fifth-pieces with a partner.
  • Readiness item 2: four fourth-pieces under each of two whole strips, counted. Item 3: unit 3's fold, one third into two sixths.

5 min. Teaching first: one amount, ringed two ways

Whole class. You lay pieces under the camera; students lay theirs and ring Example 1's two strips. Read every fraction as words.

Example 1 (Make 5/6 with sixth-pieces. Group the pieces two different ways. Write an equation for each way. [2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6 and 1/6 + 1/6 + 3/6 = 5/6]).

  1. Lay five sixth-pieces end to end on line 3, from 0. Say: one sixth, two sixths, up to five sixths.
  2. Push the pieces into two groups: 2 pieces, then 3 pieces. On the first strip, ring 2 parts, then 3 parts. Two sixths and three sixths: 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6.
  3. Slide the pieces back together. Group them again: 1 piece, 1 piece, 3 pieces. Ring them on the second strip: 1/6 + 1/6 + 3/6 = 5/6.
  4. Both ways use the same five sixth-pieces. Grouping adds no piece, takes none away and does not change their size. So each way makes 5/6, and every bottom stays 6.

Ask. After step 3: "Is 2/6 + 3/6 the same way as 1/6 + 1/6 + 3/6? How do you know?" Expect: no; the rings hold 2 and 3 pieces, then 1, 1 and 3.

20 min. Recipe Scaler: building rounds, or the same on paper

When it exists and its building rounds open by themselves: alone, Party kitchen, Building, from Grandpa Lou's round (Lou's model: oil, 2/4 cup, with the 1/4 cup scoop. [1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4]); each second way needs its "because" sentence. Stop at 20 minutes.

Until then, on paper. Alone, 15 minutes: "Building rounds", side 1 (Building: milk, 3/4 cup, with the 1/4 cup scoop. Write it as a sum of unit fractions, then a second way. [1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 3/4; a second way: 1/4 + 2/4], Building: cornmeal, 2/3 cup, with the 1/3 cup scoop. Write it as a sum of unit fractions. [1/3 + 1/3 = 2/3], Building: butter, 3/8 cup, with the 1/8 cup scoop. Write it as a sum of unit fractions, then a second way. [1/8 + 1/8 + 1/8 = 3/8; a second way: 1/8 + 2/8], Building: sugar, 6/8 cup, with the 1/8 cup scoop. Write it as a sum of unit fractions, then a second way. [1/8 six times = 6/8; a second way: 3/8 + 3/8, 2/8 + 4/8 or 1/8 + 5/8]), one piece a scoop. Lay the pieces, write the unit fractions, then ring a second way and write it. Then 5 minutes, partners swap pages.

Check: Do both ways make the same amount? Are the rings a new set of sizes?

Watch for a second way that is the first written again (error D) and two fourth-pieces written 2/8 (error A). Ask as you walk: "How many pieces in each ring?" Cornmeal has no second way: it waits for the end. Rounds 5 and 7 to 9 wait for the tool: 5/8 is the fix, and wholes are day 27's.

4 min. Fix it: a new order

Pairs, one minute, then the class. "Fix it", side 2 (Kai wrote 5/8 two ways: 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8 and 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8. [Not two ways: 3/8 + 2/8 is the same parts in a new order. A second way: 1/8 + 4/8 = 5/8, or 1/8 + 1/8 + 3/8 = 5/8]).

Ask. "Is 3/8 + 2/8 a new way if you already wrote 2/8 + 3/8?" Expect: no; the same two rings, 2 pieces and 3, in a new order.

  1. Kai's working: 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8, then 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8. Both times the rings hold 2 pieces and 3 pieces.
  2. Right working: ring the five eighth-pieces in groups of new sizes, 1 piece and 4 pieces: 1/8 + 4/8 = 5/8.
  3. Or ring 1, 1 and 3 pieces: 1/8 + 1/8 + 3/8 = 5/8. A new way needs a new set of ring sizes, not the same sizes in a new order.

If many still count a swap: ring five eighth-pieces under the camera in sizes not used yet.

10 min. Three ways each, on the line

Alone, pieces on the line page. "Three ways", side 2 (Make 6/10 with tenth-pieces on line 4. Write it three different ways. Shade the strip and ring one way. [Any three different sums of tenths that make 6/10, such as 1/10 + 5/10, 2/10 + 4/10 and 1/10 + 2/10 + 3/10, with 6 parts shaded and ringed for one way], Make 9/12 with twelfth-pieces on line 5. Write it with four parts, then three, then two. [Any sums of twelfths that make 9/12, such as 1/12 + 2/12 + 3/12 + 3/12, 2/12 + 3/12 + 4/12 and 4/12 + 5/12]): 6/10 on line 4, then the strip; 9/12 on line 5. Every way is an equation.

Watch for 2/5 + 4/5 for 6/10 (error A) and a two-part way that swaps the first (error D). Ask: "What size is each piece in this ring?" If many split the bottom: say each ring in words, "two tenths"; the pieces are still tenths.

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Cornmeal, 2/3, had only one way. Can 1/10 be taken apart into other tenths?" Expect: no; 1/10 is one piece, and a smaller piece is not a tenth. 2/10 has one way; from 3/10 up there are more.

Ask. "Why does each of your ways make 6/10?" Expect: each groups the same six tenth-pieces; grouping adds none, takes none away and does not change their size. Take "the tops add to 6", then ask why the bottom stays 10.

7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Write 7/12 as a sum of twelfths in two different ways. Shade a strip for one of them. [Any two different sums that make 7/12, such as 3/12 + 4/12 and 1/12 + 1/12 + 5/12, with a strip of 12 parts, 7 shaded and grouped]). Read it aloud once. Pieces stay in the envelope. A tick needs two different sums that make 7/12 and a strip with 7 parts shaded and ringed. Record under E26.

Other rooms

  • No strip sheet. Fold scrap strips of one length: eighths, in half three times; twelfths, in thirds, then in half twice.
  • No printer. The rounds, the fix and the two amounts on the board; notebooks and hand-ruled strips.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop cornmeal, 9/12's four-part way and the challenge; never the fix, 6/10 or the exit. Longer: the challenge (Find every way to write 7/10 as a sum of two tenths. Then find every way with three. How do you know you have them all? [Each way makes 7/10. Two parts, 3 ways: 1/10 + 6/10, 2/10 + 5/10, 3/10 + 4/10. Three parts, 4 ways: 1/10 + 1/10 + 5/10, 1/10 + 2/10 + 4/10, 1/10 + 3/10 + 3/10, 2/10 + 2/10 + 3/10. List each way smallest part first, each part no smaller than the one before: the first part can go no higher than 3/10 for two parts and 2/10 for three]). The search ends: write each way smallest part first, no part smaller than the one before; the first part stops at 3/10 for two parts, 2/10 for three.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, equation, unit fraction. Frame, to take or leave: " = + , and also = + + ." Access: a different coloured pencil for each ring; a helper rings as the student points.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, a split bottom such as 3/6 + 4/6 (error A): day 27 opens on A's wrong example, said in words beside its pieces. The same two parts swapped (error D): day 27 opens on D's wrong example beside rings of new sizes. A sum making 6/12 or 8/12: those students recount with twelfth-pieces and a partner. Pieces stay in envelopes; day 27 lays whole strips under them.
