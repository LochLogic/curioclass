Day 28: The missing part

At home

GoalI can find the missing part of a sum quickly, without pieces
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1. No pieces needed today: draw a strip and split it when you disagree.

Skip. The warm-up, day 27's tickets, the second fix and the challenge.

  • 8 min. Two examples. Example 1 (side 1), then 2: read each step; the child says the size first.

    Say. "What is 1 in twelfths?" Expect: 12/12, so 8 more than 4/12.

  • 4 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 1).

    Say. "What size are the parts?" Expect: tenths, so the answer is 7/10, not 7/0.

  • 10 min. Make it true. Make it true. Set A 3 (side 1), then Set B 4 (side 2). The child says the first step aloud.

  • 6 min. True or false. True or false 5 (side 2).

    Say. "Which can you tell at a glance?" Expect: (b): sixths joined stay sixths.

  • 7 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

Example 1 (side 1)Fill in the blank: 108 = 38 + __7/8, since 3/8 + 7/8 = 10/8
  1. First step: look at the bottoms. Every part is an eighth, so the missing part is eighths too.
  2. Count up from 3 eighths to 10 eighths: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10. That is 7 more.
  3. So the missing part is 7/8.
  4. Check by joining: 3/8 + 7/8 = 10/8. True.
Example 2 (side 1)Fill in the blank: 1 = 412 + __8/12, since 1 = 12/12 and 4/12 + 8/12 = 12/12
  1. First step: the other part is twelfths, so write 1 as twelfths. A whole cut in 12 equal parts has 12 of them: 1 = 12/12.
  2. Now it reads 12/12 = 4/12 + __.
  3. Count up from 4 twelfths to 12 twelfths: 8 more. The missing part is 8/12.
  4. Check by joining: 4/12 + 8/12 = 12/12, one whole. True.
