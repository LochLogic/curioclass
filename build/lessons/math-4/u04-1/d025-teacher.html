Before class

Print the student page, line pages, tickets, key and family note. Students cut the strip sheet's thirds to tenths strips into single pieces, one envelope each, as morning work (or hand out pre-cut). Have day 24's assessments and the marked readiness checks beside you. Put the display page on the projector.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: count by halves

Whole class, aloud. Count together by halves from one half to eight halves (Count by halves from 1/2 to 8/2. Write the counts that are whole numbers. [2/2 = 1, 4/2 = 2, 6/2 = 3, 8/2 = 4]); write each count on the board as it is said. Then, alone, students write the counts that are whole numbers.

Ask. "Which counts are whole numbers? Why every second one?" Expect: 2/2, 4/2, 6/2, 8/2 are 1, 2, 3, 4; two halves make each new whole. Then: "Did the count stop at 1?" Expect: no, it went on to 8/2.

The facts within 20 start on day 26, with the readiness small group.

5 min. Day 24's items 2 and 5 back

Alone, in pen. Hand back day 24's assessments, look-fors ticked; students fix items 2 and 5 beside the first try. Agreed with Rosa (unit 3's D): lay two sixths beside four twelfths from the envelope. Part size alone (unit 3's J): say each as "how many parts, of what size". Item 5 with unit 3's D or G: one line from 0 to 1, both points, read the sign aloud.

15 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. You lay pieces under the camera; students lay their own and fill the frames on side 1. Read every fraction as words: "three fifths", never "three over five".

Example 1 (Lay fifth-pieces in a row to make 3/5. Write 3/5 as a sum of unit fractions. [1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/5]).

  1. A fifth-piece is one of 5 equal parts of a whole strip. Its name is 1/5, one fifth.
  2. Lay one piece. Write 1/5. Say: one fifth.
  3. Lay a second piece touching it. Write + 1/5. Say: two fifths.
  4. Lay a third piece. Write + 1/5. Say: three fifths.
  5. Three pieces, each of size one fifth: 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/5.
  6. The bottom number names the size of each piece. The top number counts the pieces. Laying pieces together does not change their size, so the bottom stays 5.

Ask. After step 4: "How many pieces, and how big is each one?" Expect: three, each one fifth.

Example 2 (Lay fourth-pieces along a line from 0 to 2. Stop at 7/4. Write 7/4 as a sum of unit fractions. [1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 7/4; 7/4 is more than 1]). Pairs pool their fourth-pieces: one envelope holds only four.

  1. Each piece is one fourth, 1/4. Lay the first piece from 0. Say: one fourth.
  2. Lay pieces end to end: two fourths, three fourths, four fourths. Four fourths reach 1.
  3. The count does not stop at 1. Keep going: five fourths, six fourths, seven fourths. Stop at 7/4.
  4. Write one 1/4 for each piece: 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 7/4.
  5. Is 7/4 more or less than 1? More. Four fourths make 1, and there are 3 fourths more.

Ask. After step 4: "Is 7/4 more or less than 1? How do you know?" Expect: more; four fourths make 1, and three more are past it.

Seat students who stopped at 4/4 on readiness item 1 where they see the camera.

14 min. Board paths

Pairs, pieces pooled. "Board paths", side 2 (Path 1 is 4/6 meter long. Each board is 1/6 meter. Write the path as a sum of boards. Is it more or less than 1 meter? [1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 4/6; less than 1 meter: six sixths make 1, and there are only 4], Path 2 is 5/3 meters long. Each board is 1/3 meter. Write the path as a sum of boards. Is it more or less than 1 meter? [1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 5/3; more than 1 meter: three thirds make 1, and there are 2 thirds more], Path 3 is 7/6 meters long. Each board is 1/6 meter. Write the path as a sum of boards. Is it more or less than 1 meter? [1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 7/6; more than 1 meter: six sixths make 1, and there is 1 sixth more]). For each path, students lay one piece a board, write one unit fraction a piece, then answer more or less than 1 with a reason. Then they mark where the path ends on the line page: line 3 (sixths) for paths 1 and 3; line 1 for path 2, after marking thirds by eye, two marks in each whole.

Watch for a sum with the bottoms added (5/15 on path 2: error A) and a count that stops at 6/6 on path 3 (error B). Ask as you walk: "How does your row of pieces show the sum? Where is 1 on your row?"

8 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then the class. "Fix it", side 2 (Bo laid 4 tenth-pieces in a row. He wrote 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 = 4/40. [4/10. Each piece is a tenth, and laying pieces together does not change their size. Bo added the bottoms too], Ada counted third-pieces: 1/3, 2/3, 3/3. She says there is no 4/3, because 3/3 is all of it. [There is a 4/3: 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 4/3. The count goes on past 1]). The display shows each wrong picture beside the right one.

Ask. "Bo's row is 4 tenth-pieces. Is 4/40 that long?" Expect: no; 4/40 is 4 tiny parts.

  1. Bo's working: 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 = 4/40. He added the tops to get 4 and the bottoms to get 40.
  2. Right working: each piece is one tenth. Four pieces of size one tenth are four tenths: 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 = 4/10.
  3. 4/40 is 4 parts of a whole cut into 40: far shorter than Bo's row of 4 tenth-pieces. The size of the pieces did not change, so the bottom stays 10.

Ask. "Ada says 3/3 is all of it. Lay a fourth third-piece. Where does it end?" Expect: past 1, at 4/3.

  1. Ada's working: 1/3, 2/3, 3/3, then stop. She takes 3/3, one whole, as the end of the numbers.
  2. Right working: lay a fourth third-piece after the first three. It ends past 1. The count is four thirds: 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 4/3.
  3. 1 is one point on the line, not its end. The count goes on past 1, and past 2 too.

If many still add the bottoms: say each sum in words, "four tenths", and lay the row again. If many still stop at 1: lay third-pieces along line 1 past 1 and keep counting aloud.

6 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Five thirds, path 2: how many pieces, and how big is each one?" Expect: five pieces, each one third; it is past 1 because three thirds make 1. Take "five pieces" first, then the size.

Ask. "In 5/3, which number tells the size of a piece, and which counts them?" Expect: the 3 names the size, thirds; the 5 counts the pieces.

7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Write 6/5 as a sum of unit fractions. Is 6/5 more or less than 1? How do you know? [1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 6/5; more: five fifths make 1, and there is one fifth more]). Read it aloud once. Pieces stay in the envelope. A tick needs the sum and "more" with a reason. Record under E25 on the record sheet. Send the family note home.

Other rooms

  • No strip sheet. Students fold scrap strips of one length into thirds, fourths, fifths and sixths and cut them; tenths: rule a 10 inch strip every inch.
  • No printer. Write the three paths and the two fixes on the board; students answer in notebooks and draw a line from 0 to 2 by hand.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop path 1 and the challenge. Never drop the fixes, path 3 or the exit. Longer: the challenge (Ren also has long boards. Each is 3/6 meter. Make a 7/6 meter path with long and short boards. Write the sum. [3/6 + 3/6 + 1/6 = 7/6, or 3/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 7/6]). Then: how many different paths can Ren make from the two kinds of boards? A new order is not a new way. The search ends at three: no, one or two long boards, since three would pass 7/6.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: unit fraction, sum, equation, whole, each on a labelled piece. Frame, to take or leave: " is pieces of size , so = + + ..." Access: wide pre-cut pieces; a helper lays pieces as the student points.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, five 1/5s or "less, it is a fraction" (error B): day 26 shows "There is no 4/3: 3/3 is all of it" beside third-pieces counted past 1 on a line to 2, in its first five minutes. A sum = 6/30 (error A): day 26 shows "1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 = 4/40" beside the four tenth-pieces. Five 1/6s (top and bottom read the other way): in the same minutes, those students read 6/5 aloud as "six fifths" and lay six fifth-pieces with a partner. Readiness gaps go to day 26's warm-up and first five minutes, as the section opener says. Pieces stay in envelopes for day 26's rings.
