Day 27: Wholes are parts too

At home

GoalI can take apart a fraction bigger than 1 using wholes, and show why it is right
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No pieces: draw three strips of one length, each split into as many equal boxes as the item's bottom number (6, 3 or 4), and shade instead of laying pieces. No line page: draw a line from 0 to 3. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The warm-up, day 26's tickets, Example 3, questions 4 and 5, and the challenge.

  • 8 min. Wholes as sixths. Example 1 (side 1), then 2: the child lays or shades the pieces and writes each sum.

    Say. "Why can we write 1 as 6/6 but not as 6/5?" Expect: six sixths fill one strip exactly; six fifths go past it.

  • 5 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 1).

    Say. "How many whole strips does 3/3 fill? How many does 2 1/3 need?" Expect: one; two and a third more, so 3/3 + 3/3 + 1/3 = 7/3.

  • 8 min. Two on your own. Wholes and parts 2 (side 2), then 3.

  • 7 min. Lee's sum. Lee's sum (side 2): the child draws, then says why.
  • 7 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)Lay sixth-pieces on a whole strip until it is covered. Write 1 as sixths.1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 6/6, so 1 = 6/6
  1. Lay a whole strip on the table. It is one whole, 1.
  2. Lay sixth-pieces on it end to end. Count: one sixth, two sixths, up to six sixths.
  3. Six sixth-pieces cover the strip: no gap, and nothing past the end. So 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 6/6.
  4. 6/6 covers the same strip as 1, so 1 = 6/6. A whole cut in 6 equal parts has 6 of them.
Example 2 (side 1)Write 216 as a sum that uses 66 for each whole. Then write it as one fraction.6/6 + 6/6 + 1/6 = 13/6, so 2 1/6 = 13/6
  1. 2 1/6 is 2 wholes and 1 sixth more.
  2. Lay two whole strips end to end. Cover each with six sixth-pieces. Each whole is 6/6.
  3. Lay one more sixth-piece past the second strip. Write one whole at a time: 6/6 + 6/6 + 1/6.
  4. Count the sixths: 6 and 6 is 12, and 1 more is 13. So 2 1/6 = 13/6. The bottom stays 6: every piece is a sixth.
  5. On line 9, count sixths from 0 and mark the end: six reach 1, six more reach 2, and 13/6 is one more, past 2.
Fix it 1 (side 1)Jo wrote 213 as one fraction. Jo put the 2 and the 1 on top: 213 = 33.7/3. Each whole is 3/3, so 2 1/3 = 3/3 + 3/3 + 1/3 = 7/3. Jo's 3/3 is only one whole
Wholes and parts 2 (side 2)Lay fourth-pieces on a whole strip, then 3 more. Write 134 as a sum that uses 44 for the whole. Then write it as one fraction.4/4 + 3/4 = 7/4
Wholes and parts 3 (side 2)Write 226 as a sum that uses 66 for each whole. Then write it as one fraction. Mark it on line 9.6/6 + 6/6 + 2/6 = 14/6, marked past 2 on line 9
Lee's sum (side 2)Lee says 124 = 14 + 24. Is Lee right? Show with a drawing.Not right: the 1 is a whole, four fourths, so 1 2/4 = 4/4 + 2/4 = 6/4; Lee's sum is only 3/4
Look-fors:
  1. says no
  2. shows the whole as four fourths
  3. gives 4/4 + 2/4 or 6/4
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: No. I drew one whole strip in fourths and 2 more fourths. The 1 is 4 fourths, so it is 4/4 + 2/4 = 6/4.
  • partly: No, because 1/4 + 2/4 is only 3/4. (Look-for 1 only: ask what the 1 is in fourths, and to draw it.)
  • not: Yes, the 1 goes on the 1/4. (Error C: the whole read as one part.)
the ticket questionWrite 2310 as a sum that uses 1010 for each whole. Then write it as one fraction. Draw strips to show it.10/10 + 10/10 + 3/10 = 23/10, with two whole strips in tenths and 3 tenths more
