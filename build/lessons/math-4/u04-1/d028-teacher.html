Day 28: The missing part

Before class

Print the student page, tickets and key. Have day 27's tickets and Lee's sums beside you, look-fors ticked, and the facts group's names. Envelopes of pieces stay in the cupboard today.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: equal names

Whole class, in their heads (Fill in each blank. (a) 1/2 = /8 (b) 3/4 = /12 (c) 2/5 = 4/ [(a) 4, so 1/2 = 4/8 (b) 9, so 3/4 = 9/12 (c) 10, so 2/5 = 4/10]). Side 1 face down; write one blank at a time on the board; hands up when ready. After (c), students turn the sheet over and write the three answers. The facts group (readiness item 4) does Find each: 6 + 8, 15 − 7, 9 + 4, 12 − 5, 7 + 7, 11 − 6 [14, 8, 13, 7, 14, 5] on side 2 alone first, then joins for (c).

Ask. After (a): "Is 4/8 a new number, or a new name for 1/2?" Expect: a new name; both sit at the same point on the line.

3 min. Day 27's tickets and written task

Hand back Lee's sums, look-fors ticked. Whole class for what more than a third showed; otherwise the students named, at your table, while the rest read side 1.

  • If day 27's tickets showed many C (5/10, the 2 and the 3 put on top): write "3 1/5 = 4/5: the 3 goes on top" beside a sketch of three whole strips in fifths and one fifth more, 5/5 + 5/5 + 5/5 + 1/5 = 16/5. Ask how many strips 5/5 fills. A "yes" on Lee's sum is C too.
  • 13/10 (one whole counted): those students sketch two whole strips in tenths with a partner and count.

8 min. Teaching first: count up from the part you have

Whole class; students fill the frames on side 1. No pieces: say the parts in words.

Example 1 (Fill in the blank: 10/8 = 3/8 + [7/8, since 3/8 + 7/8 = 10/8]).

Ask. "Before we count: what size will the missing part be?" Expect: eighths; joining eighths cannot make another size.

  1. First step: look at the bottoms. Every part is an eighth, so the missing part is eighths too.
  2. Count up from 3 eighths to 10 eighths: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10. That is 7 more.
  3. So the missing part is 7/8.
  4. Check by joining: 3/8 + 7/8 = 10/8. True.

Ask. After step 3: "Is there a faster way than counting one at a time?" Expect: 3 and 7 make 10, so 7 eighths: the tops are a fact.

Example 2 (Fill in the blank: 1 = 4/12 + [8/12, since 1 = 12/12 and 4/12 + 8/12 = 12/12]).

Ask. "What is the first step here, and why?" Expect: write 1 as twelfths, 12/12, so all three numbers count the same parts. Take "count up from 4" and ask: count up to what?

  1. First step: the other part is twelfths, so write 1 as twelfths. A whole cut in 12 equal parts has 12 of them: 1 = 12/12.
  2. Now it reads 12/12 = 4/12 + .
  3. Count up from 4 twelfths to 12 twelfths: 8 more. The missing part is 8/12.
  4. Check by joining: 4/12 + 8/12 = 12/12, one whole. True.

6 min. Fix it: Max and Kit

Pairs, two minutes, then the class. "Fix it", side 1 (Max filled in 11/10 = 4/10 + . Max wrote 7/0: 11 take away 4 is 7, and 10 take away 10 is 0. [7/10. Every part is a tenth, so count up from 4 tenths to 11 tenths: 7 more], Kit filled in 1 = 3/8 + . Kit wrote 4/8: the 1 goes on top with the 3. [5/8. The 1 is a whole, 8/8, so count up from 3 eighths to 8 eighths: 5 more]).

Ask. "Max wrote 7/0. How big is one zeroth?" Expect: there is no such part; the parts are tenths, so the missing part is tenths.

  1. Max's working: 11 − 4 = 7 on top, 10 − 10 = 0 on the bottom, so 7/0.
  2. Right working: every part is a tenth, so the missing part is tenths too. The bottom stays 10.
  3. Count up from 4 tenths to 11 tenths: 7 more. The missing part is 7/10.
  4. Check by joining: 4/10 + 7/10 = 11/10. Max's 7/0 names no size of part at all.

Ask. "Kit's 1 went on top. What is 1 in eighths?" Expect: 8/8, so count from 3 to 8.

  1. Kit's working: 1 + 3 = 4 on top, so 4/8.
  2. Right working: the 1 is one whole. In eighths, 1 = 8/8.
  3. Count up from 3 eighths to 8 eighths: 5 more. The missing part is 5/8.
  4. Check by joining: 3/8 + 5/8 = 8/8, one whole. Kit's 3/8 + 4/8 is only 7/8.

If many still take the bottoms away: say the sum in words, "four tenths and how many tenths make eleven tenths?"

18 min. Make it true

Alone, then pairs. "Make it true": set A on side 1 ((a) 5/6 = 1/6 + (b) + 1/5 = 4/5 (c) 57/100 = 20/100 + (d) 13/8 = 5/8 + [(a) 4/6 (b) 3/5 (c) 37/100 (d) 8/8, which is 1]), then set B on side 2 ((a) 1 = 3/4 + (b) 1 = + 7/10 (c) 2 = 5/3 + (d) 1 = 30/100 + [(a) 1/4 (b) 3/10 (c) 1/3, since 2 = 6/3 (d) 70/100]). Under set B each student writes the first step for (c) in a sentence. Then partners compare: did you start the same way? Where answers differ, each sketches a strip for that one in the box on side 2. Then the display shows both keys.

Check: Is the bottom of each missing part the same as the others? Do your part and the given part join to make the sum?

Watch for a 0 on the bottom, 4/0 or 3/0 on set A (error A); 4/4 on set B (a), the 1 put on top (error C); and 77/100 on set A (c), the numbers added. Ask as you walk: "How many fourths fill one whole?" For A (d), 8/8 and 1 are both right. For B (c), 2 is 6/3 first. A (c): by tens from 20/100 to 50/100 is 30/100, then 7 more: 37/100. B (d): by tens from 30/100 to 100/100, 7 tens, 70/100.

If many miss set B: write 1 = 4/4, 1 = 10/10, 2 = 6/3 on the board and have them say each aloud.

8 min. True or false

Pairs (True or false? Write T or F on each line. (a) 1 = 5/8 + 3/8 (b) 4/6 + 2/6 = 6/12 (c) 7/5 = 3/5 + 3/5 (d) 1 = 6/10 + 4/10 (e) 2 = 4/3 + 2/3 (f) 9/12 = 5/12 + 3/12 [(a) true (b) false: 4/6 + 2/6 = 6/6, which is 1 (c) false: 3/5 + 3/5 = 6/5 (d) true (e) true: 6/3 = 2 (f) false: 5/12 + 3/12 = 8/12]). Sort each sum without working every one out. Then the class.

Ask. "Which could you tell at a glance? How?" Expect: (b), the bottom changed to twelfths, but sixths joined stay sixths; (a) and (d), the tops add to the bottom, so the sum is one whole.

Check: (a) true, (b) false, (c) false, (d) true, (e) true, (f) false. Fix any in pen.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "When the whole was 1, what did you do first? Did your partner start the same way?" Expect: wrote 1 as the same parts as the other number, 1 = 4/4 or 1 = 10/10. Some counted up, some thought of a fact: both work.

7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Fill in each blank. (a) 8/6 = 5/6 + (b) 1 = 2/6 + (c) 13/100 = 6/100 + [(a) 3/6 (b) 4/6 (c) 7/100]). No pieces; a sketch is allowed. Record under E28.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The examples, the fixes, both sets and the sort on the board; notebooks; the sketch in the margin.
  • No projector. Write each step on the board; read the keys aloud.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop True or false (e) and (f) and the Pull it together Ask; never the fixes, set B or the exit. Longer: the challenge (Fill in with eighths: 1 = 1/8 + + . Find every way. How do you know you have them all? [1/8 + 1/8 + 6/8, 1/8 + 2/8 + 5/8 and 1/8 + 3/8 + 4/8. The two blanks make 7/8. The smaller blank can be 1/8, 2/8 or 3/8; past that the same parts come back in a new order]). The search ends: the blanks make 7/8, the smaller is 1/8, 2/8 or 3/8; past that, the same parts come back in a new order (error D).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, whole, missing part. Frame, on side 2: "My first step for (c): ." Say it as "I wrote as because ." Access: a student may sketch every item; a helper may write the sentence the student says.

Tomorrow

On (b), 3/6 (error C, 1 not seen as 6/6): if many, day 29 opens with 1 = 5/10 + answered 6/10, "the 1 goes on top with the 5", beside 1 = 10/10, counted up from 5 tenths: 5/10. On (a) or (c), 3/0 or 7/0 (error A, the bottoms taken away too) is met in day 29's teaching; if many, point at it there. 13/6 or 19/100: those students read the blank aloud ("five sixths and how many sixths make eight sixths?") and check by joining; day 30's check by adding meets it again.
