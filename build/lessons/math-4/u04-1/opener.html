Section 4.1: A fraction as a sum, taken apart more than one way (days 25 to 28)

The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NF.B.3: "Understand a fraction a/b with a > 1 as a sum of fractions 1/b." Part 3b asks students to "decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator in more than one way, recording each decomposition by an equation", and to justify it with a drawing. This section is that: a fraction taken apart and put back together, with no adding rule yet.

A fraction counts parts of one size. The bottom number names the size of the part; the top number counts them. So 3/5 is three pieces of size one fifth: 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/5. The size does not change when pieces are laid together, which is why the bottom number stays 5. Students say it in words before they write it: "three fifths is three pieces of one fifth".

Past one whole. The count does not stop at 1. Lay fourth-pieces in a row: four fourths are one whole strip long, and the fifth piece runs past it, at 5/4. A fraction past 1 is the same kind of count. The pieces are longer than the spaces on the line pages, so pieces lie in a row on the desk and the line is only to count and mark the end.

More than one way. Any grouping of the pieces is a sum that makes the fraction: 5/8 = 1/8 + 4/8 = 1/8 + 1/8 + 3/8. The same parts in a new order are not a new way (our key rule, error D): 2/8 + 3/8 and 3/8 + 2/8 are one way.

Wholes are parts too. One whole is b/b: 1 = 6/6. So a whole and some parts can be written all as parts: 2 1/6 = 6/6 + 6/6 + 1/6 = 13/6. This is the standard's own example form, and it sets up mixed numbers in section 4.3, where the word "mixed number" first appears.

Limits. Denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 100. No simplest form. No "times the bottom, add the top" rule: a whole is written as a sum of parts first.

The pieces and the line

  • Day 25. Unit-fraction pieces from unit 3's strip sheet, one addend a piece, laid down as the sum is written. Then fourth-pieces in a row, counted past 1, the end marked on a line from 0 to 2. The whole, eighths, twelfths and blank strips go in the envelope uncut.
  • Day 26. The eighths and twelfths strips cut into pieces. Pieces and rings: one amount ringed two ways. Recipe Scaler's building rounds, Lou's model to sugar (key, show why, 20 min) where the class has devices and the tool's parts open one at a time; otherwise the four building amounts on paper with pieces and rings.
  • Day 27. Blank whole strips from the envelope laid under the pieces: 1 = 6/6, then wholes and parts as one sum.
  • Day 28. Pieces withdrawn: a quick sketch only, to settle a partner disagreement. Short keyed sets.

The number line pages (number-lines.md): lines 1 to 6 from 0 to 2, blank and in fifths, sixths, tenths and twelfths; lines 7 to 12 from 0 to 5, for section 4.3. Print both sides, one a student, for day 25; keep it for the unit.

Why this model

A piece in the hand is one addend, and a row of pieces is the sum: the equation is read off the table. Rings show two groupings of the same row, so "another way" is a new grouping, not a new order. The line carries the count past 1, where pieces alone suggest the whole is the end. Recipe Scaler (spec only) builds amounts in a cup and asks for second ways; it fits day 26 as a key tool, but its picture is a cup, so the line stays on paper.

The errors to expect, in plain words

What students do What it shows What to do when many do it
A Add the tops and add the bottoms: 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/15 A fraction read as two whole numbers Say the sum in words: three fifths; the part size does not change
B Stop at one whole: "there is no 6/4" The whole taken as the end of the number Lay the pieces in a row past a whole strip and keep counting; mark the end on a line from 0 to 2
C Put the whole number on top: 2 1/3 = 3/3 A whole not yet seen as n parts Write the whole as a sum first, 2 = 3/3 + 3/3, then count thirds
D Swap the order and call it a new way One split, not two Ring the pieces in groups of a size not used yet

Wrong examples: A and B on day 25, D on day 26, C on day 27. B and C are the readiness signs of items 1 and 2.

The reteach (readiness items 1 to 4)

The readiness check (readiness.md) is given in the last ten minutes of day 24 and marked before day 25. Day 25's first five minutes go to unit 3's judged items, so the reteach is day 26's warm-up and first five minutes: for the whole class if more than a third missed an item, otherwise for the students who missed it.

  1. Item 1, counting on past 1. Pairs pool their fourth-pieces and lay them in a row past a whole strip, saying "one fourth, two fourths, ... five fourths", then mark 5/4 on a line from 0 to 2. The count does not stop at 1.
  2. Item 2, a whole number as fourths. Pairs pool their fourth-pieces and lay four under each of their two whole strips, counting eight fourths.
  3. Item 3, an equal name. Unit 3's fold: one third folded into two sixths. The unit 3 record sheet names who.
  4. Item 4, facts within 20. A small group only, whatever the count: facts in the warm-ups of days 26 to 31 and a facts sheet at home. The class keeps the lesson.

Supplies for the section

  • Per student: unit-fraction pieces cut from unit 3's strip sheet (thirds to twelfths) in an envelope, with the whole and blank strips kept uncut (day 27); the number line pages; a pencil and a pen.
  • Per class: the projector for the display pages; pieces under the camera, or drawn on the board.
  • A room with nothing: pieces cut from scrap strips, all one length; lines ruled by hand.

Words

Unit fraction, sum, equation, whole, each on a labelled piece on the wall. Read "six fifths", never "six over five". Frame, to take or leave: " is pieces of size , so = + ."

How the unit is checked

Readiness check, day 24 (readiness.md); exits on days 25 to 37, on paper; written tasks on days 27, 30, 34 and 37, judged with look-fors; the end-of-unit assessment on day 38, where items 4.1a, 4.1b and 4.1c are this section's. All of it goes on one record sheet for the unit (record.md), by error letter. The family note (family.md) goes home on day 25.
