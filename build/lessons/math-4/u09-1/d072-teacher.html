Day 72: What is the small 1 worth?

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the column sheets, the exit tickets, the key and the family note. Put the place name cards over the board, ones on the right. Put the display page on the projector. Have the marked readiness items 1 and 2 from day 71 beside you: list who missed item 1 (adding within 1,000) and who missed item 2 (writing a number in digits). Seat the item 1 list where you can reach them in the pairs part.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: within 1,000, and times tables

Alone, side 1, then whole class: 573 + 248 [821], 806 − 462 [344], 6 × 8 [48], 9 × 4 [36]. Read the answers; students fix in pen.

Check: the four answers are on the screen.

Readiness items 1 and 2: nothing moves today. Keep both lists: day 73 opens with their reteach.

15 min. Teaching first: two ways, side by side

Whole class. Students fill the two frames on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. Write expanded form on the left of the board, the columns on the right, under the place name cards.

Example 1 (Add 3,746 + 2,538. Use expanded form and the standard algorithm. [6,284. Small 1s: one ten, one thousand]).

  1. Write both numbers in expanded form: 3,000 + 700 + 40 + 6, and 2,000 + 500 + 30 + 8. Beside them, write the numbers in columns under the place names.
  2. Expanded form, place by place: 5,000 + 1,200 + 70 + 14. That is the sum, but not yet written as one number.
  3. Columns, ones first: 6 ones and 8 ones is 14 ones. 14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones. Write 4 under the ones. Write the 1 small, over the tens. One what? One ten.
  4. Tens: 4 tens, 3 tens, and the 1 ten we made: 8 tens. Write 8 under the tens. In expanded form, 70 + 10 = 80.
  5. Hundreds: 7 hundreds and 5 hundreds is 12 hundreds. 12 hundreds is 1 thousand and 2 hundreds. Write 2. Write the small 1 over the thousands. One what? One thousand.
  6. Thousands: 3 thousands, 2 thousands, and the 1 thousand we made: 6 thousands. Write 6.
  7. Both ways give 6,284. Expanded form: 6,000 + 200 + 80 + 4. The small 1s are the 14 ones and the 12 hundreds, written short.

Point from the 14 in the expanded form to the 4 and the small 1 in the columns. Say it plainly: the standard algorithm is expanded form written short. Each column is one place, and ten of a place are written as one of the next place, in the next column.

Example 2 (Add 1,895 + 4,368. Use expanded form and the standard algorithm. [6,263. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand]). The class says each step with you. At every small 1, ask the room "One what?" before you show the next screen.

  1. Expanded form: 1,000 + 800 + 90 + 5, and 4,000 + 300 + 60 + 8. Place by place: 5,000 + 1,100 + 150 + 13.
  2. Ones: 5 + 8 is 13 ones. 13 ones is 1 ten and 3 ones. Write 3, and a small 1 over the tens. One what?
  3. One ten. Tens: 9 + 6 + 1 is 16 tens. 16 tens is 1 hundred and 6 tens. Write 6, and a small 1 over the hundreds. One what?
  4. One hundred. Hundreds: 8 + 3 + 1 is 12 hundreds. 12 hundreds is 1 thousand and 2 hundreds. Write 2, and a small 1 over the thousands. One what?
  5. One thousand. Thousands: 1 + 4 + 1 is 6 thousands. Write 6. The sum is 6,263.
  6. Expanded form gives the same: 5,000 + 1,100 + 150 + 13 = 6,263.

10 min. Pairs: both ways

Pairs, side 2 (5,189 + 2,647 [7,836. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred], 3,578 + 4,293 [7,871. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred]). One partner writes expanded form, the other the columns. They swap for the second sum. Then they compare sums.

Check: Do both ways give the same sum? Is each small 1 named?

Walk with one question: "Point to that small 1. One what?" Go first to the item 1 list. If a pair's two sums differ, do 5,189 + 2,647 [7,836. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred] with them in expanded form, each carry said by its value. Watch for a sum that differs by exactly 10 or 100 (error A: a carry lost); the expanded form side shows the ten that went missing. Watch for a column holding two digits, as 13 under the ones (error D): ask how many digits one place can hold. If many pairs get different sums, stop and do 5,189 + 2,647 [7,836. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred] together, both ways.

15 min. Columns alone: name every small 1

Alone, on the column sheet, side 2 (6,358 + 1,794 [8,152. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand], 4,926 + 3,587 [8,513. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand], 27,465 + 18,379 [45,844. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one ten thousand], 53,618 + 29,754 [83,372. Small 1s: one ten, one thousand, one ten thousand], 36,847 + 45,286 [82,133. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand, one ten thousand]). The algorithm only now. Beside each sum on the student page, students write what each small 1 is worth: "1 ten, 1 thousand". The last three are five-digit sums: the ten thousands column works the same way. Items 10 and 11 are for fast finishers.

At 10 minutes, students check the sums they have done against the screen and fix in pen.

Check: Is your sum the same as the screen? Did you name every small 1?

Watch for "a 1" written as the worth (error C). Ask "One what?" and point to the column it sits over. If many write "a 1", the class names the small 1s of 6,358 + 1,794 [8,152. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand] aloud together before going on.

5 min. Fix it: Kai and Mia

Side 2 (Kai added 3,746 + 2,538 and got 6,274. [6,284. Kai lost the small 1 over the tens: 14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones], Mia added 3,746 + 2,538 and got 512714. [6,284. Mia wrote 14 and 12 whole: each column holds one digit]). One minute alone on each, then the class: the display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Kai wrote the 4 but lost the ten: 14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones. Mia wrote 14 and 12 whole: one place holds one digit, so 14 ones is written as 4, and the ten moves on.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class, at Example 1 on the board.

Ask. "Where are the 14 ones in the standard algorithm?" Expect, in this order: "the 4 under the ones"; "the small 1 over the tens is the ten"; "14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones, so it is split between two columns". Show the first answer, then point to the small 1 until someone gives the second. Then ask the same of the 12 hundreds.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Add 4,563 + 2,871 with the standard algorithm. Circle the small 1 you wrote when you added the tens. What is it worth? [7,434; one hundred (13 tens is 1 hundred and 3 tens)]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E72.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The warm-up and the sums on the board, one set at a time; columns on lined paper turned sideways.
  • One child at home. Do Example 1 at the table, both ways, side by side. The child says each small 1 by its worth. Then 5,189 + 2,647 [7,836. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred] and 6,358 + 1,794 [8,152. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand] to 27,465 + 18,379 [45,844. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one ten thousand]; stop when they are right.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: in the columns part, do 6,358 + 1,794 [8,152. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one thousand] to 27,465 + 18,379 [45,844. Small 1s: one ten, one hundred, one ten thousand] only. Never drop naming the small 1s, Mia's fix or the pairs part. Longer: the challenge (Add 2,758 + 1,469 + 3,684. What is the small number over the tens worth? [7,911; 2 tens (the ones make 21: 2 tens and 1 one)]), on grid paper: three numbers make a column of 21, so the small number is a 2. Ask what it is worth.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, regroup (families may say "carry"; both are welcome), expanded form, standard algorithm, and the place names. A student who cannot write small digits may type columns or say each small 1 aloud. Frames on the display: " ones is ten and ones." " tens is 1 and tens."

Tomorrow
