Day 73: Line them up, carry, and check

At home

GoalI can line up numbers of different lengths, carry more than once, and check with an estimate
Time39 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: check answers in this view. Column sheet or grid paper: lined paper turned sideways.

Skip. The warm-up, set 3, the written task and the challenge.

  • 8 min. The example. Example (side 1) together: estimate first, then line up the ones.

    Say. "Without adding, how do you know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" Expect: 947 is under 1,000, so the sum is under 8,000.

  • 10 min. Set 1. Three sets 1 (side 2), 2, 3, with the place names written over the columns. The child says an estimate aloud before each sum, then checks the sum against it.

  • 10 min. Set 2. Three sets 4 (side 2), 5, 6, with no place names.

  • 6 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 1), then 2.

    Say. "Mia got 12,322. Should the sum pass 100,000?" Expect: yes, about 112,000, so a small 1 was lost; 112,322.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone. The estimate is part of the answer.

Example (side 1)6,285 + 9477,232; estimate about 6,300 + 900 = 7,200
  1. Estimate first. 6,285 is about 6,300, and 947 is about 900. About 6,300 + 900 = 7,200.
  2. Say both numbers: six thousand, two hundred eighty-five; nine hundred forty-seven. 947 has no thousands.
  3. Line them up by place, ones under ones: the 7 under the 5, the 4 under the 8, the 9 under the 2.
  4. Ones: 5 + 7 = 12. 12 ones is 1 ten and 2 ones. Write 2, and a small 1 over the tens.
  5. Tens: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13 tens. 13 tens is 1 hundred and 3 tens. Write 3, and a small 1 over the hundreds.
  6. Hundreds: 2 + 9 + 1 = 12 hundreds. 12 hundreds is 1 thousand and 2 hundreds. Write 2, and a small 1 over the thousands.
  7. Thousands: 6 + 1 = 7 thousands. Write 7.
  8. The sum is 7,232. The estimate was about 7,200. It fits.
