Day 73: Line them up, carry, and check

GoalI can line up numbers of different lengths, carry more than once, and check with an estimate

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips, the column sheets and the key. Mark the E72 slips and sort them: A (a carry lost), D (a two-digit sum in one column), C (the small 1 called "a 1"). Count who missed readiness items 1 and 2. Write place names over four columns on the board, and the warm-up.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: a sum and three facts

Alone, then whole class: 3,958 + 2,764 = ? [6,722], 6 × 70 = ? [420], 9 × 80 = ? [720], 4 × 600 = ? [2,400]. If only a few missed readiness items 1 or 2, sit with them now: 467 + 285 in expanded form on a place value chart, then in columns; then 306,042 read by its comma groups.

5 min. Readiness reteach, or E72 back

Whole class. If many missed readiness items 1 and 2: the reteach above, with everyone; the E72 slips go back at the door. Otherwise hand back the day 72 slips now.

If day 72's slips showed many A or D (a carry lost, or "613712"): put day 72's fix back up, 3,746 + 2,538 shown as 6,274 and as "512714", beside 6,284. Say each carry by its value: "14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones."

If many C ("a 1", or "one hundred"): students name every carry by its value, aloud, all day.

If both: talk E72's error through inside today's worked sum. If neither: one student says what each small 1 in their E72 sum is worth. Students whose E72 lost every carry sit near you for set 1.

5 min. Teaching first: different lengths

Whole class. Expanded form is gone from today. Students fill side 1's estimate and columns as you go, not ahead of you.

Example (6,285 + 947 [7,232; estimate about 6,300 + 900 = 7,200]).

  1. Estimate first. 6,285 is about 6,300, and 947 is about 900. About 6,300 + 900 = 7,200.
  2. Say both numbers: six thousand, two hundred eighty-five; nine hundred forty-seven. 947 has no thousands.
  3. Line them up by place, ones under ones: the 7 under the 5, the 4 under the 8, the 9 under the 2.
  4. Ones: 5 + 7 = 12. 12 ones is 1 ten and 2 ones. Write 2, and a small 1 over the tens.
  5. Tens: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13 tens. 13 tens is 1 hundred and 3 tens. Write 3, and a small 1 over the hundreds.
  6. Hundreds: 2 + 9 + 1 = 12 hundreds. 12 hundreds is 1 thousand and 2 hundreds. Write 2, and a small 1 over the thousands.
  7. Thousands: 6 + 1 = 7 thousands. Write 7.
  8. The sum is 7,232. The estimate was about 7,200. It fits.

Saying both numbers first lets students hear that 947 has no thousands.

8 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, side 2, on the column sheet: 5,867 + 739 [6,606; estimate about 5,900 + 700 = 6,600 (any close estimate)], 37,594 + 8,867 [46,461; estimate about 38,000 + 9,000 = 47,000 (any close estimate)], 83,746 + 29,385 [113,131; estimate about 84,000 + 29,000 = 113,000 (any close estimate)]. An estimate first, every time. Walk with one question: "One what?" at any small 1. Watch for:

  • 13,257 on problem 1: lined up from the left (error B). Ask for both numbers aloud.
  • 13,131 on problem 3: the last small 1 lost (error E). Ask: does the estimate pass 100,000?
  • A sum short by 10, 100 or 1,000: a carry lost (error A). Ask what that column made.

Then the display shows set 1's answers. Students mark their own in pen and count the right-first-try sums.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Fix any column in pen.

Take the place names off the board now.

7 min. Set 2: place names down

Alone, on grid paper with no place names: 249,683 + 7,548 [257,231; estimate about 250,000 + 8,000 = 258,000 (any close estimate)], 9,476 + 92,857 [102,333; estimate about 9,000 + 93,000 = 102,000 (any close estimate)], 516,394 + 68,729 [585,123; estimate about 520,000 + 70,000 = 590,000 (any close estimate)]. Problem 5 puts the shorter number first. If many lined up from the left in set 1, say problem 4's numbers aloud together first.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Count your right-first-try sums.

7 min. Set 3: one more set

Alone: 375,968 + 427,456 [803,424; estimate about 380,000 + 430,000 = 810,000 (any close estimate)], 46,785 + 897 [47,682; estimate about 46,800 + 900 = 47,700 (any close estimate)], 691,547 + 208,693 [900,240; estimate about 690,000 + 210,000 = 900,000 (any close estimate)]. Problem 9 carries in five columns in a row. If many miss it, work it together, one column at a time, before the check.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Compare your count with set 1's count, not a friend's.

6 min. Fix it: Kai and Mia

Side 1 (Kai added 6,285 + 947 and got 15,755. Estimate. Then find the right sum. [about 6,300 + 900 = 7,200, so 15,755 is far too big; 7,232. Kai lined up from the left], Mia added 64,859 + 47,463 and got 12,322. Estimate. Then find the right sum. [about 65,000 + 47,000 = 112,000, so the sum must pass 100,000; 112,322. Mia lost the last small 1]). Two minutes alone on Kai's, an estimate first; then the display shows Kai's working beside the right working. The same for Mia's. Each estimate does the catching: about 7,200 is far from 15,755, and about 112,000 passes 100,000. If many miss Mia's: "11 ten thousands is 1 hundred thousand and 1 ten thousand," said aloud.

7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 (Tell a third grader, in two sentences, what the small 1 means. [For example: when a column makes ten or more, ten of that place are traded for one of the next place. The small 1 is that one, so over the tens it is one ten.]). A student may say it first, then write. Collect the student pages at the door; read the task tonight against its look-fors on the key.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Without adding, how do you know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" Expect: "947 is under 1,000, so the sum is under 8,000"; "Kai put the 9 under the thousands, but 947 has no thousands". Show the estimate answer first.

Ask. "Why do expanded form and the standard algorithm give the same sum?" Expect: "the columns are the places"; "the small 1 is the ten we traded, written short". If no one says the second, show day 72's two methods side by side.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper (Estimate first, then write in columns and add: 286,947 + 53,268. [340,215; about 290,000 + 50,000 = 340,000]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E73. The estimate is half the question.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The sums on the board, a set at a time; lined paper turned sideways, place names for set 1 only.
  • One child at home. Six of the nine sums, an estimate before each, said aloud. Once they are right, stop explaining; keep the count.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 3 and the second Ask; never the estimates, the fixes or the exit. Longer: the challenge, 27,486 + 9,758 + 63,679 [100,923; estimate about 27,000 + 10,000 + 64,000 = 101,000]: three numbers, so a column may carry 2.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, estimate, regroup, and the place names to hundred thousands. "Carry" is welcome too. Frame: " ones is ten and ones." Large-square grid paper or typed columns for a student who cannot write small digits.

Tomorrow

On the slip, 819,627 (error B: lined up from the left): day 74's E73 back shows B's wrong example beside its estimate. If many in sets 1 and 2 lost the last small 1 into a new place (13,131, error E), day 74 shows E's instead. On a slip short by 10, 100 or 1,000, or with no estimate, circle the place before it goes back. The written task goes back with the E73 slips on day 74.
