Day 73: Line them up, carry, and check

Before class

Print everything on the print line. Mark the E72 tickets and sort them: A (a carry lost), D (a two-digit sum in one column), C ("a 1", or "one ten"). Count who missed readiness items 1 and 2. Write place names over six columns on the board, hundred thousands to ones, and the warm-up.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: a sum and three facts

Alone: 3,958 + 2,764 = ? [6,722], 6 × 70 = ? [420], 9 × 80 = ? [720], 8 × 30 = ? [240]; the display shows the answers at 4 minutes. If only a few missed readiness items 1 or 2, sit with them now: 467 + 285 in expanded form on a place value chart, then in columns; then 306,042 read by its comma groups.

Check: the four answers are on the screen.

5 min. Readiness reteach, or E72 back

Whole class. If many missed readiness items 1 and 2: the reteach above, with everyone; the E72 tickets go back when pencils go down, a circle on any lost carry, and the C line below still runs. Otherwise hand back the day 72 tickets now.

If day 72's tickets showed many A or D (a carry lost, or "613134"): put day 72's fix (3,746 + 2,538) back up, shown as 6,274 and as "512714", beside 6,284. Say each carry by its value: "14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones."

If many C ("a 1", or "one ten"): students name every carry by its value, aloud, all day.

If neither: one student says what each small 1 in their E72 sum is worth. Students whose E72 lost every carry sit near you for set 1.

5 min. Teaching first: different lengths

Whole class. No expanded form today. Students fill side 1 as you go.

Example (6,285 + 947 [7,232; estimate about 6,300 + 900 = 7,200]).

  1. Estimate first. 6,285 is about 6,300, and 947 is about 900. About 6,300 + 900 = 7,200.
  2. Say both numbers: six thousand, two hundred eighty-five; nine hundred forty-seven. 947 has no thousands.
  3. Line them up by place, ones under ones: the 7 under the 5, the 4 under the 8, the 9 under the 2.
  4. Ones: 5 + 7 = 12. 12 ones is 1 ten and 2 ones. Write 2, and a small 1 over the tens.
  5. Tens: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13 tens. 13 tens is 1 hundred and 3 tens. Write 3, and a small 1 over the hundreds.
  6. Hundreds: 2 + 9 + 1 = 12 hundreds. 12 hundreds is 1 thousand and 2 hundreds. Write 2, and a small 1 over the thousands.
  7. Thousands: 6 + 1 = 7 thousands. Write 7.
  8. The sum is 7,232. The estimate was about 7,200. It fits.

Say both numbers first: 947 has no thousands. Then say what the estimate cannot do: 7,222, a lost small 1, is close to 7,200 too. The estimate catches a wrong place; the columns catch a lost 1.

8 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, side 2, on the column sheet: 5,867 + 739 [6,606; estimate about 5,900 + 700 = 6,600 (any close estimate)], 37,594 + 8,867 [46,461; estimate about 38,000 + 9,000 = 47,000 (any close estimate)], 83,746 + 29,385 [113,131; estimate about 84,000 + 29,000 = 113,000 (any close estimate)]. Walk with one question: "One what?" at any small 1. Watch for:

  • 13,257 on problem 1: lined up from the left (error B). Ask for both numbers aloud.
  • 13,131 on problem 3: the last small 1 lost (error E). Ask: does the estimate pass 100,000?
  • A sum short by 10, 100 or 1,000: a carry lost (error A). Ask what that column made.

After each set, the display shows its answers; students mark their own in pen and count the right-first-try sums.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Did you write every small 1?

Take the place names off the board now.

7 min. Set 2: place names down

Alone, on grid paper with no place names: 249,683 + 7,548 [257,231; estimate about 250,000 + 8,000 = 258,000 (any close estimate)], 9,476 + 92,857 [102,333; estimate about 9,000 + 93,000 = 102,000 (any close estimate)], 516,394 + 68,729 [585,123; estimate about 520,000 + 70,000 = 590,000 (any close estimate)]. Problem 5 puts the shorter number first. If many lined up from the left in set 1, say problem 4's numbers aloud together first.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Count your right-first-try sums.

7 min. Set 3: one more set

Alone, on the grid paper: 375,968 + 427,456 [803,424; estimate about 380,000 + 430,000 = 810,000 (any close estimate)], 46,785 + 897 [47,682; estimate about 46,800 + 900 = 47,700 (any close estimate)], 691,547 + 208,693 [900,240; estimate about 690,000 + 210,000 = 900,000 (any close estimate)]; problem 8 is for fast finishers. Problem 9 carries in five columns in a row. If many miss it, work it together, one column at a time.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Compare your count with set 1's count, not a friend's.

6 min. Fix it: Kai and Mia

Turn back to side 1 (Kai added 4,738 + 965 and got 14,388. Without adding, how can you tell Kai is wrong? Then find the right sum. [About 4,700 + 1,000 = 5,700 (or: 965 has no thousands, so the sum is under 6,000), so 14,388 is far too big; 5,703. Kai lined up from the left], Mia added 64,859 + 47,463 and got 12,322. Estimate. Then find the right sum. [about 65,000 + 47,000 = 112,000, so the sum must pass 100,000; 112,322. Mia lost the last small 1]). Two minutes alone on Kai's; then the display shows Kai's working beside the right working. The same for Mia's. Each estimate does the catching: about 5,700 is far from 14,388, and 965 has no thousands; about 112,000 passes 100,000. If many miss Mia's: "11 ten thousands is 1 hundred thousand and 1 ten thousand," said aloud.

7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 (Tell a third grader, in two sentences, what the small 1 means. [For example: when a column makes ten or more, ten of that place are traded for one of the next place. The small 1 is that one, so over the tens it is one ten.]). A student may say it first, then write. Collect the student pages at the door; read the task tonight against its look-fors on the key.

5 min. Pull it together

Ask. "Without adding, how do you know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" Expect: "947 is under 1,000, so the sum is under 8,000"; "lined up from the left, the 9 goes under the thousands, but 947 has no thousands".

Ask. "Problem 9 carried in five columns in a row. What happened the same way every time?" Expect: "ten of one place made one of the next"; "ten hundreds made a thousand, ten thousands made a ten thousand". If no one says the first, put two of the columns side by side.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Estimate first, then write in columns and add: 286,947 + 53,268. [340,215; about 290,000 + 50,000 = 340,000]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E73. The estimate is half the question.

Other rooms

  • No printer. The sums on the board, a set at a time; lined paper turned sideways, place names for set 1 only.
  • One child at home. Six of the nine sums, an estimate before each, said aloud.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 3 and the second Ask; never the estimates, the fixes or the exit. Longer: the challenge, 27,486 + 9,758 + 63,679 [100,923; estimate about 27,000 + 10,000 + 64,000 = 101,000], on grid paper: three numbers, so a column may carry 2.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, estimate, regroup, and the place names to hundred thousands. Frame: " ones is ten and ones." Large-square grid paper or typed columns for a student who cannot write small digits.

Tomorrow
