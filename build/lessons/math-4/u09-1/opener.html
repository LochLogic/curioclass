Section 9.1: The standard algorithm for adding (days 72 to 73)

The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NBT.B.4, its first part: "Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm." This section adds; section 9.2 subtracts. It is the grade's one fluency standard, so it is begun here and carried by the daily warm-up to day 150.

The standard algorithm is expanded form written short. In grade 3, students added within 1,000 by place (3.NBT.A.2). 3,746 + 2,538 in expanded form is 3,000 + 700 + 40 + 6 and 2,000 + 500 + 30 + 8. Add like units: 14 ones, 7 tens, 12 hundreds, 5 thousands. 14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones, so the ones place holds 4 and the ten joins the tens: 8 tens. 12 hundreds is 1 thousand and 2 hundreds, so the hundreds place holds 2 and the thousand joins the thousands: 6 thousands. The sum is 6,284. The standard algorithm does the same work in columns, right to left, with each ten of a unit written as a small 1 over the next column.

The small 1 always has a worth. Over the tens it is one ten; over the thousands it is one thousand. It is "one" only as a digit. Students say each carry by its value ("14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones") on day 72, so the algorithm stays tied to place value. Families may say "carry"; "regroup" is the same move.

Ten of any unit make one of the next. The same rule runs in every column, all the way to a million: ten hundred thousands make one million. So nothing new is needed for six-digit numbers, only care. This is MP.8, using what repeats.

An estimate catches what the columns hide. Lining up numbers of different lengths is where most wrong sums start. Before each sum on day 73, students round and add (4.NBT.A.3, from day 71): 6,285 + 947 is about 6,300 + 900 = 7,200, so any answer near 15,000 is wrong before it is checked. Saying both numbers aloud by their comma groups first (4.NBT.A.2) puts each digit in its place.

Limits. Whole numbers; sums under 1,000,000. No blocks: fluency here means accurate and reasonably fast, with a method that is understood. No time target is set and no clock is shown.

Expanded form beside the algorithm

The model the section carries, under place names over the columns.

  • Day 72. The teacher shows both, side by side, for 3,746 + 2,538 and 1,895 + 4,368, saying each carry by its value. Pairs do two sums both ways, then four- and five-digit sums with the algorithm alone, each small 1 labelled with its worth.
  • Day 73. Expanded form is gone. Place names stay up for the first set, then come down before E73. An estimate is written before every sum.

The place value columns sheet (columns.md) gives place names to millions and a dashed row for small digits. It is for any student who needs the columns ruled, on both days and on day 77.

Why this model

Expanded form shows where every carry comes from: the 14 ones are on the page before they are written as 4 and a small 1. Putting it beside the algorithm on the same sum, under the same place names, lets a student match each step. Taking it away on day 73 is the point: the algorithm is the fast form, and the estimate takes over as the check. No tool fits days 72 and 73. Road Trip Logbook (spec only) asks only grade 3 sizes and mixes in subtracting, so the section runs on paper with a key.

The errors to expect, in plain words

What students do What it shows What to do when many do it
A Drop a carry: usually one, rarely all Each column is added as its own small sum One sum in expanded form beside the algorithm; say each carry by its value
B Line numbers up from the left Digits are matched, not places Place names over the columns; say both numbers aloud first
C Call every carry "one" The place value is gone Students name each carry aloud for a day
D Write a two-digit sum in one column A place is not seen as holding one digit The same sum in expanded form
E Lose the last carry into a new place Stops at the longer number's last digit Estimate first: the sum must pass the next place

The day each wrong example is shown: A, C and D on day 72, B and E on day 73. B comes back in section 9.2, on a difference. Each day's teacher page names the wrong answer that signals each error on that day's exit.

The reteach (readiness items 1 and 2)

Items 1 and 2 are given in the last ten minutes of day 71 and marked that night (readiness.md). If more than a third of the class missed one, day 73's warm-up and first five minutes are this reteach for the whole class. Otherwise run it with the students who missed it during day 73's warm-up, and the five minutes go to E72.

Item 1, adding within 1,000.

  1. Draw a place value chart: hundreds, tens, ones.
  2. Write 467 and 285 in it as 400 + 60 + 7 and 200 + 80 + 5.
  3. Add each place: 12 ones, 14 tens, 6 hundreds. Ask "12 ones is how many tens and ones?" and trade.
  4. Then "14 tens is how many hundreds and tens?" and trade. Write the same sum in columns beside it.

Item 2, a number in digits. Read "three hundred six thousand" and write 306 before the comma; read "forty-two" and fill the last three places, a zero in the hundreds. Students read two more numbers by their comma groups.

Supplies for the section

  • Per student: the place value columns sheet on day 72 (and for anyone who wants it on day 73); grid paper on day 73; a pencil.
  • Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board with place names written over the columns.
  • A room with nothing: sums copied from the board; lined paper turned sideways so its lines make columns.

Words

Sum, regroup, estimate, and the place names, each taught on the worked sum at first use. Families may say "carry"; both words are welcome. Frame: "__ ones is __ ten and __ ones." A student may say a carry in a home language first, then in the frame.

How the unit is checked

Readiness: four items, two on day 71 and two on day 74 (readiness.md). Exits: one each on days 72 to 76, on paper, and E79. Written tasks on days 73 and 76, judged with look-fors. End-of-unit assessment: day 77, six items, unaided; 9.1a, 9.1b and 9.1c are this section's. Check 2 on day 78 holds two more (C2.1 and C2.2). All of it goes on one record sheet for the unit (record.md), by error letter. The family note (family.md) goes home on day 72.
