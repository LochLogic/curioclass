Day 3: Factor pairs with no tiles: key

Each answer is worked out in full. The last column names the wrong answers to expect and the error each one shows.

Any equal form of an equation is right: the two factors in either order, either side of the equals sign, any letter, or the matching division (n × 9 = 36, 9 × n = 36, 36 = 9 × n and 36 ÷ 9 = n all say the same).

practices01-W34.OA.B.4 (a), MP.8Write how you know when to stop looking for factor pairs of 64.Try 1, 2, 3 and on, in order. At 8, 8 × 8 = 64. Any number after 8 goes with a number under 8, which was already tried, so stop at 8
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. says the numbers were tried in order
  2. says the pairs turn around: 8 × 8, or the next pair is one already found
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: I go 1, 2, 3 in order. When I get to 8 × 8 = 64, the next ones would be the same pairs turned around, so I stop.
  • partly: I stop at 8 because 8 × 8 = 64. (Look-for 2 only: ask what was tried before 8.)
  • not: I stop when I get to 64. (Searches to the number itself: error C.)
stops at 64, or tries every number to 64: error C, counts a turned pair as new, or keeps searching past the turn; "when I can't think of any more": error A, lists some factor pairs and stops; often leaves out 1 × n (no order to the search)
