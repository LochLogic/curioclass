Day 3: Factor pairs with no tiles

At home

GoalI can find all the factor pairs of any number to 100 with no tiles, even pairs that are not in my times tables
Time34 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: read the examples from side 1. The 9 by 9 table: print one, or write out the times table the child asks for.

Skip. The facts check, 90, the practice sheet and the challenge. No coins from today.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Example (side 1) together, writing the list as it grows. Test 3 and 4 from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30, 18 more is 3 × 6, so 3 × 16. Then Then (side 1).
  • 8 min. Every shape: 72. Alone, in the 72 column of the side 1 table: "fits, with " or "no" for each row from 1, the turn marked, then the pairs. Check: 1 × 72, 2 × 36, 3 × 24, 4 × 18, 6 × 12, 8 × 9.

  • 5 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 2).

    Say. "How can 3 be a factor of 42 when 42 is not in the 3 times table?" Expect: 3 × 14 = 42; 3 × 10 is 30, then 12 more.

  • 6 min. When to stop. Write 2 (side 2), alone. The child may say it while you write it down.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers ticket question 1 and question 2 alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example (side 1)Find all the factor pairs of 48 with no tiles.1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8
  1. Start at 1 and go in order. 1 fits: 1 × 48 = 48.
  2. 2 fits: 48 is even, and half of 48 is 24. So 2 × 24 = 48.
  3. 3: 48 is not in the 3s I know. Start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30. 18 more is 3 × 6. So 3 × 16 = 48. It fits.
  4. 4: start from ten times: 4 × 10 = 40. 8 more is 4 × 2. So 4 × 12 = 48. It fits.
  5. 5: the 5s go 40, 45, 50. They skip 48, so no.
  6. 6: 6 × 8 = 48. It fits.
  7. 7: 7 × 6 = 42 and 7 × 7 = 49. No. And 7 × 7 is already past 48, so any new pair would be one we have, turned. Stop.
  8. The factor pairs of 48: 1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8.
Then (side 1)Is 3 a factor of 81? Start from ten times.Yes: 3 × 27 = 81 (3 × 20 = 60, and 21 more is 3 × 7)
  1. Start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30. 51 more is still past the 3s I know.
  2. Take another ten: 3 × 20 = 60.
  3. 21 more is 3 × 7.
  4. 20 and 7 make 27, so 3 × 27 = 81. It fits.
  5. Check: 60 + 21 = 81.
Fix it 1 (side 2)Rosa says the factor pairs of 42 are 1 × 42, 2 × 21 and 6 × 7. She says she is done.1 × 42, 2 × 21, 3 × 14, 6 × 7. Rosa missed 3 × 14 because it is not in the times tables (error B)
Write 2 (side 2)Write how you know when to stop looking for factor pairs of 64.Try 1, 2, 3 and on, in order. At 8, 8 × 8 = 64. Any number after 8 goes with a number under 8, which was already tried, so stop at 8
Look-fors:
  1. says the numbers were tried in order
  2. says the pairs turn around: 8 × 8, or the next pair is one already found
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: I go 1, 2, 3 in order. When I get to 8 × 8 = 64, the next ones would be the same pairs turned around, so I stop.
  • partly: I stop at 8 because 8 × 8 = 64. (Look-for 2 only: ask what was tried before 8.)
  • not: I stop when I get to 64. (Searches to the number itself: error C.)
