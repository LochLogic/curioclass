Day 2: Search in order

Before class

Print everything on the print list. Sort the marked E1 tickets: C (one rectangle counted twice), a short last row, A (only 2 by 5). From the readiness sheets, list who missed item 2, or both item 3 and E1. Cut a 2 by 5 and a 4 by 7 rectangle from grid paper. Write the warm-up on the board.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: add and subtract

Alone, then whole class: 563 + 278 [841], 702 − 345 [357], 419 + 386 [805], 830 − 467 [363].

Students who missed readiness item 2, or missed both item 3 and E1, if fewer than a third, sit with you instead. Item 2: three rows of 8 dots, read as 3 × 8 = 24 and 24 ÷ 3 = 8. Item 3: build 14 with paper squares in every rectangle; a turned one is the same rectangle.

If more than a third missed item 2 or 3, the next 5 minutes are that reteach for the whole class, and E1's error is taken inside the worked search.

5 min. E1 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 1 tickets.

If day 1's tickets showed many C (2 by 5 and 5 by 2 counted as two): cut a 2 by 5 rectangle from grid paper and turn it. "Did it change? One rectangle, two names."

If many drew a short last row for 3 rows: build 10 in 3 rows with tiles. Rows of 3 leave 1 over; rows of 4 need 12. The last row is short, so 3 rows do not fit.

If neither: one student shows both rectangles. Students who drew only 2 by 5 (A) sit near you for Teaching first: the search in order answers them.

8 min. Teaching first: one search, 24

Whole class. Students fill the table on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. Pairs build each row count with 24 tiles. The display shows each step on its own screen.

Example (24 squares. Try 1 row, 2 rows, 3 rows and on, in order. Which numbers of rows fit with none left over? [1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8, 4 × 6; 5 rows: no; 6 rows of 4 is 4 rows of 6 turned, so stop]).

  1. 1 row: 1 row of 24. Fits, with 24. Write 1 × 24 = 24.
  2. 2 rows: 2 × 12 = 24. Fits, with 12.
  3. 3 rows: 3 × 8 = 24. Fits, with 8.
  4. 4 rows: 4 × 6 = 24. Fits, with 6.
  5. 5 rows: 5 rows of 4 is 20, and 4 are left. No. And 5 × 5 = 25 is more than 24.
  6. 6 rows: 6 rows of 4. Fits, but 6 × 4 is 4 × 6 turned: the same rectangle. Draw a line under 4 rows. That is the turn.
  7. Stop. When rows × rows is more than the number, every pair left is one we have, turned. 5 × 5 = 25 is past 24.
  8. The factor pairs of 24 are 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8 and 4 × 6. Four pairs.

Why stop: once rows × rows is more than the number, any new fit would need fewer in each row than the rows, and those were tried. Show it with the tiles: 5 rows of 5 would need 25.

20 min. Egg Carton Factory, Pack Station: on paper

Alone, on the rows table page; grid paper to check. When it exists: Pack Station, the same table filled beside the cartons, stopped at 20 minutes. Until then, the paper path: 36 squares. Fill the rows table from 1 row. [1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12, 4 × 9, 6 × 6; 5 rows: no; 7 × 7 = 49 is past 36, stop after 6 rows (either order in each pair)], 30 squares. Fill the rows table from 1 row. [1 × 30, 2 × 15, 3 × 10, 5 × 6; 4 rows: no; 6 × 6 = 36 is past 30, stop after 5 (either order in each pair)], 49 squares. Fill the rows table from 1 row. [1 × 49, 7 × 7; 2 to 6 rows: no; 8 × 8 = 64 is past 49, stop after 7 (either order in each pair)], 60 squares. Fill the rows table from 1 row. [1 × 60, 2 × 30, 3 × 20, 4 × 15, 5 × 12, 6 × 10; 7 rows: no; 8 × 8 = 64 is past 60, stop (either order in each pair)].

For each number of rows from 1 up, students write "fits, with " or "no" before checking it by a fact or by shading rows on grid paper. They draw a line under the last pair. After each table, the display shows its key.

Check: Does every row match? Is your line under the same pair? Fix a row in pen.

Walk with one question: "How many rows did you just try? What comes next?" Watch for:

  • A turned pair written again, such as 6 × 5 on 30 (error C). Point to the line.
  • A table that starts at 2 rows, so no 1 × 36 (error A). "Start with 1 row."
  • A row skipped because the fact is not one they know, such as 2 × 18 (error B). Shade it on grid paper. No shortcut today; that is day 3.
  • 49: a student who stops at 6 rows. 7 rows of 7 fits. 60: stop at 8 rows.

If many miss the same row of 36, shade it together, then go on.

7 min. Fix it: Mia and Kai

Side 2 (Mia says: "The factor pairs of 20 are 2 × 10 and 4 × 5. Done." [1 × 20, 2 × 10, 4 × 5. Mia did not go in order, so she missed 1 × 20], Kai says: "28 has five factor pairs: 1 × 28, 2 × 14, 4 × 7, 7 × 4, 14 × 2." [3 pairs: 1 × 28, 2 × 14, 4 × 7. 7 × 4 and 14 × 2 are 4 × 7 and 2 × 14 turned]). Three minutes alone, one in pairs, three for the class: the display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Mia used facts she knew, in no order, so she could not know she was done. Kai kept going past 4 × 7. Turn the cut 4 by 7 rectangle.

6 min. On your own: 40

Alone, side 2, no tiles (Find all the factor pairs of 40. [1 × 40, 2 × 20, 4 × 10, 5 × 8 (either order in each pair)]); grid paper only to check a row. Four minutes, then partners swap and check.

Check: Does your partner start at 1 row? Is every pair there once? Is there a line under the last pair?

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "You found 6 × 6 for 36. Why is every fit after that one you already have?" Expect: "7 rows would need fewer than 6 in each row, and I already tried those"; "they are the ones before, turned"; "7 × 7 = 49 is past 36". Hold up the cut 4 by 7 rectangle and turn it.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Find all the factor pairs of 50. How do you know you have them all? [1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10. After 5, neither 6 nor 7 works, and 8 × 8 = 64 is already more than 50, so any new pair would be one of these turned. Also right: "the next number that works is 10, and I have it."]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E2.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Students rule their own rows table: "rows" and "fits?". The fixes and 40 on the board.
  • No tiles. Squares cut from grid paper.
  • One projector. For each row count the class writes "fits, with " or "no" before you show it.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop 49 from the rows tables and the partner check (students check their own). Never drop the search in order, the fixes or the stop. Longer: the challenge (Which number from 41 to 49 has the most factor pairs? List its pairs. [48: 1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8]).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: factor, factor pair, row, product. Anchor chart: the table for 24 with its line. Students say each row aloud with a partner, in a home language if they wish.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 10 × 5 as a fourth pair (error C): day 3's E2 back, 2 minutes after its facts check, crosses out the turned pairs in one list together. "I just know", or no reason: day 3 reads one reason that names the stop. No 1 × 50 (A) or no 2 × 25 (B): day 3's search from 1, testing from ten times, answers them. Students who listed fewer than three pairs sit near you for day 3's Teaching first.
