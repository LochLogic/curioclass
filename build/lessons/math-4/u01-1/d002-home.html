Day 2: Search in order

At home

GoalI can find all the factor pairs of a number in order, and say how I know I am done
Time38 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. Tiles: 24 coins or paper squares. Grid paper: squares ruled on plain paper. Rows table page: rule two columns, "rows" and "fits?". No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The adding warm-up, 49 and 60, and the challenge.

  • 3 min. Day 1's ticket. Check it with its answer. If 2 by 5 and 5 by 2 were drawn as two, cut a 2 by 5 from paper and turn it.

  • 8 min. One search, 24. Example (side 1) with 24 coins: 1 row, 2 rows, 3 rows, in order. The child writes "fits, with " or "no" in the side 1 table before building.

    Say. At 6 rows: "Have we made this before?" Expect: yes, 4 rows of 6 turned, so we stop.

  • 10 min. Rows tables. Rows tables 1 (side 1), then 2, alone, rows from 1 up, a line under the last pair.

  • 7 min. Fix two. Fix it 5 (side 2), then 6.

    Say. "Why could Mia not know she was done?" Expect: she did not go in order from 1, so she missed 1 × 20.

  • 5 min. On your own. On your own 7 (side 2), no coins.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example (side 1)24 squares. Try 1 row, 2 rows, 3 rows and on, in order. Which numbers of rows fit with none left over?1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8, 4 × 6; 5 rows: no; 6 rows of 4 is 4 rows of 6 turned, so stop
  1. 1 row: 1 row of 24. Fits, with 24. Write 1 × 24 = 24.
  2. 2 rows: 2 × 12 = 24. Fits, with 12.
  3. 3 rows: 3 × 8 = 24. Fits, with 8.
  4. 4 rows: 4 × 6 = 24. Fits, with 6.
  5. 5 rows: 5 rows of 4 is 20, and 4 are left. No. And 5 × 5 = 25 is more than 24.
  6. 6 rows: 6 rows of 4. Fits, but 6 × 4 is 4 × 6 turned: the same rectangle. Draw a line under 4 rows. That is the turn.
  7. Stop. When rows × rows is more than the number, every pair left is one we have, turned. 5 × 5 = 25 is past 24.
  8. The factor pairs of 24 are 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8 and 4 × 6. Four pairs.

the ticket questionFind all the factor pairs of 50. How do you know you have them all?1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10. After 5, neither 6 nor 7 works, and 8 × 8 = 64 is already more than 50, so any new pair would be one of these turned. Also right: "the next number that works is 10, and I have it."
Look-fors:
  1. lists 1 × 50, 2 × 25 and 5 × 10, with no turned copies
  2. says the numbers were tried in order
  3. says why it stops: the next pair would be one already found, or 8 × 8 is more than 50
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: 1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10. I tried 1 to 7 in order. 6 and 7 do not fit, and 8 × 8 is too big, so the next ones are turned.
  • partly: 1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10. I tried them in order. (No reason to stop: ask what comes after 7.)
  • not: 1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10, 10 × 5. I just know. (A turned pair and no reason: error C.)
