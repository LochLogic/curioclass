Day 1: Every rectangle for a number

At home

GoalI can build every rectangle for a number and write each one as a multiplication
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. Tiles: 24 coins or paper squares. Grid paper: squares ruled on plain paper. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The readiness sheet, 15 and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Build 12. Together, build Example 1 (side 1), then 2 and 3 with 12 coins; the child fills each line on side 1, then the Words line: 3 and 4 are factors, 12 is the product.

    Say. Turn the 3 by 4 a quarter turn: "Is this a new rectangle?" Expect: no, the same one turned; 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 are one factor pair.

  • 20 min. 18, 14 and 16. Every rectangle 1 (side 2), 2, then 3, alone: build each, draw it on the squared paper with its multiplication inside, then write them on side 2. Ask: "Are all your rows full?"

    Say. "Can all the coins go in one row?" Expect: yes, 1 by 18 counts. A square counts too.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone, drawing on squared paper, no coins. Read it aloud once.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)Build 3 rows of 4 tiles. How many tiles is that? Write the multiplication inside.12 tiles; 3 × 4 = 12 (4 × 3 = 12 is also right)
Example 2 (side 1)Use the same 12 tiles. Can they make 2 equal rows? Can they make 5 equal rows?2 rows: yes, 6 in each row, 2 × 6 = 12. 5 rows: no, the last rows are short
Example 3 (side 1)Put all 12 tiles in 1 row. Is it a rectangle? Write the multiplication.Yes: 1 row of 12, 1 × 12 = 12
Every rectangle 1 (side 2)Build every rectangle you can with 18 tiles.1 by 18, 2 by 9, 3 by 6: 1 × 18 = 18, 2 × 9 = 18, 3 × 6 = 18 (a turned one, such as 6 by 3, is the same rectangle)
Every rectangle 2 (side 2)Build every rectangle you can with 14 tiles.1 by 14, 2 by 7: 1 × 14 = 14, 2 × 7 = 14 (a turned one, such as 7 by 2, is the same rectangle)
Every rectangle 3 (side 2)Build every rectangle you can with 16 tiles.1 by 16, 2 by 8, 4 by 4: 1 × 16 = 16, 2 × 8 = 16, 4 × 4 = 16 (the 4 by 4 square is a rectangle too)
the ticket questionOn your grid paper, draw every rectangle with area 10. Write a multiplication equation inside each one. Can 10 squares make a rectangle with 3 rows? Show why or why not.1 by 10 and 2 by 5, with 1 × 10 = 10 and 2 × 5 = 10. No: 3 rows of 3 is 9 and 3 rows of 4 is 12
