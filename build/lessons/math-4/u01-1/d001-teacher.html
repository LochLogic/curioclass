Before class

Print the readiness sheet, the student page both sides, the exit slips and the key. Cut a quarter sheet of grid paper for each slip. Count 24 tiles into a bag for each pair. Put the display page on the projector. Write "rows" and "in each row" on the board with a small 2 by 3 sketch of tiles beside them, the words labelled.

The day

15 min. Readiness sheet

Alone, on paper, no tiles. It is the first day of the year: say the sheet is to see what they bring, not a test, and that a blank is fine. Read each item aloud once. Collect it; mark it after class. Nothing today waits on it.

10 min. Teaching first: the rectangles for 12

Whole class. Build with tiles at the front; pairs build along with their own tiles. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on its own screen.

Example 1 (Build 3 rows of 4 tiles. How many tiles is that? Write the multiplication inside. [12 tiles; 3 × 4 = 12 (4 × 3 = 12 is also right)]).

  1. Lay 4 tiles in a row, edges touching, no gaps.
  2. Build 2 more rows of 4 under it. Every row is full.
  3. Count by fours: 4, 8, 12. That is 12 tiles.
  4. Write 3 × 4 = 12 inside it: 3 rows, 4 tiles in each row.
  5. This is a rectangle: every row is the same length, with no gaps and no tile sticking out.

Example 2 (Use the same 12 tiles. Can they make 2 equal rows? Can they make 5 equal rows? [2 rows: yes, 6 in each row, 2 × 6 = 12. 5 rows: no, the last rows are short]).

  1. Take the rectangle apart. Deal the 12 tiles into 2 rows, one tile to each row in turn.
  2. Each row has 6, and both rows are full. It is a rectangle. Write 2 × 6 = 12 inside.
  3. Take it apart. Deal the 12 tiles into 5 rows the same way.
  4. Two rows have 3 tiles and three rows have 2. The last rows are short.
  5. Short rows make no rectangle. 12 tiles cannot make 5 equal rows.

Example 3 (Put all 12 tiles in 1 row. Is it a rectangle? Write the multiplication. [Yes: 1 row of 12, 1 × 12 = 12]).

  1. Lay all 12 tiles in one long row.
  2. There is one row and it is full. It is a rectangle, a long thin one.
  3. Write 1 × 12 = 12 inside: 1 row, 12 tiles in it.

Now name the words. 3 and 4 are factors of 12: they multiply to make it. Together they are a factor pair. 12 is the product. Point to the 3 rows, the 4 in each row, and the 12 tiles as you say each word.

Turn the 3 by 4 rectangle a quarter turn so it stands as 4 rows of 3.

Ask. "Is a 3 by 4 rectangle a different rectangle from a 4 by 3? Is it a different factor pair?" Expect first: "yes, it has 4 rows now". Then: "no, it is the same tiles, just turned"; "4 × 3 and 3 × 4 are the same pair". Turn it back and forth until the class agrees: one rectangle, one factor pair, written either way.

Do not show how to find them all or when to stop. That is day 2.

15 min. Rectangles for 18 and 20

Pairs, then alone. Side 2, Build every rectangle you can with 18 tiles. [1 by 18, 2 by 9, 3 by 6: 1 × 18 = 18, 2 × 9 = 18, 3 × 6 = 18 (a turned one, such as 6 by 3, is the same rectangle)] and Build every rectangle you can with 20 tiles. [1 by 20, 2 by 10, 4 by 5: 1 × 20 = 20, 2 × 10 = 20, 4 × 5 = 20 (a turned one, such as 5 by 4, is the same rectangle)]. Pairs build each rectangle with tiles; then each student draws it on grid paper and writes the multiplication inside. Each student writes every multiplication on side 2.

Walk with one question: "Are all your rows full?" Watch for:

  • A shape with a short last row: not a rectangle. Ask the pair to count each row aloud.
  • 2 by 9 and 9 by 2 drawn as two (error C). Have them lay one drawing on the other and turn it.
  • No 1 by 18 or 1 by 20 (error A). Ask "Can all the tiles go in one row?" Say no more; the order of the search is day 2's.

If many build only one or two rectangles, stop and ask the class for a third rectangle for 18; build it at the front. Then pairs go on.

Last minute: the display shows the answers. Each pair crosses out any drawing that is another one turned.

Check: Count your rectangles. A turned one is the same rectangle.

15 min. Rectangles for 16 and 15

Pairs, then alone, as before. Side 2, Build every rectangle you can with 16 tiles. [1 by 16, 2 by 8, 4 by 4: 1 × 16 = 16, 2 × 8 = 16, 4 × 4 = 16 (the 4 by 4 square is a rectangle too)] and Build every rectangle you can with 15 tiles. [1 by 15, 3 by 5: 1 × 15 = 15, 3 × 5 = 15 (a turned one, such as 5 by 3, is the same rectangle)]. 16 makes a square, 4 by 4. If a pair says a square is not a rectangle, ask: "Are all its rows full? Are its corners square?" A square is a rectangle with all sides the same. 15 makes only two rectangles; let pairs argue whether they have missed one, and leave it open.

Pairs who finish try the challenge (Find a number of tiles that makes exactly 2 rectangles. One of them must be a square. [9: 1 by 9 and 3 by 3 (4, 25 and 49 also work)]).

Last minute: the display shows the answers; each pair checks their own, as before.

Check: Count your rectangles. A square counts. A turned one is the same rectangle.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper (On the grid, draw every rectangle with area 10. Write a multiplication equation inside each one. Can 10 squares make a rectangle with 3 rows? Show why or why not. [1 by 10 and 2 by 5, with 1 × 10 = 10 and 2 × 5 = 10. No: 3 rows of 3 is 9 and 3 rows of 4 is 12]). Each student draws on the quarter sheet of grid paper and writes the 3 rows answer on the slip. Read it aloud once. No tiles. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E1.

Other rooms

  • When the start-of-year check exists. It takes the first 30 minutes in place of the readiness sheet, and the 16 and 15 part is dropped. Teaching first, the 18 and 20 part and the exit stay as written.
  • No tiles. Pairs cut squares from grid paper, 24 a pair, in the first minute of Teaching first.
  • No printer. The readiness items and the four numbers go on the board. Students draw on grid paper or ruled squares, and write the multiplications under each drawing.
  • One child at home. Use 24 coins or paper squares. Build 12 together, then let the child find the rectangles for 18 alone. Ask the turn question and wait.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop problem 4, never the drawing on grid paper or the multiplication inside each rectangle. Longer: the challenge (Find a number of tiles that makes exactly 2 rectangles. One of them must be a square. [9: 1 by 9 and 3 by 3 (4, 25 and 49 also work)]); it needs the square from 16.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: rectangle, row, factor, factor pair, product. Point to a built rectangle for each. A student who cannot move tiles directs a partner. Retell in a home language if a student wishes. Frame on the display: " and are a factor pair of , because × = ."

Tomorrow

On the slip: 2 by 5 and 5 by 2 drawn as two (error C), a shape with a short last row for the 3 rows question, or no 1 by 10 (error A). Day 2's first five minutes answer the slip if no whole-class readiness reteach is needed: for C, a rectangle cut from grid paper is turned; for a short last row, the exit's 10 is built in 3 rows and the last row is looked at. The readiness sheet, marked tonight, says whether day 2 opens instead with the reteach on item 2 or item 3. Day 2 teaches the search in order.
