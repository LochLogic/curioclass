Day 3: Factor pairs with no tiles

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the facts check part 1 sheet, the practice sheet, the exit slips and the key. Mark the E2 slips and sort them: a turned pair listed as new (C), no reason given, and the rest. Put the display page on the projector and 9 by 9 tables where students can reach them. Seat students whose E2 list showed no order near you for Teaching first.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 1

Alone, on the facts check sheet: the 36 products, untimed, no clock shown. Collect at 5 minutes; not reached is not wrong. After class, record each student under 32 and the facts missed. A 9 by 9 table stays on hand all lesson for anyone who asks.

2 min. E2 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 2 slips.

If day 2's slips showed many C (10 × 5 listed as a fourth pair): write 1 × 32, 2 × 16, 4 × 8, 8 × 4, 16 × 2 on the board and cross out the turned pairs together. "8 × 4 is the 4 by 8 rectangle turned."

If many gave no reason: read one slip whose reason names the turn: "8 × 8 is past 50, so any new pair would be one I have, turned."

If neither: no 2 × 25 (B) or no 1 × 50 (A) is answered by today's search.

8 min. Teaching first: 48 with no tiles

Whole class, lists only: no tiles or grid from today. Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step; write the list on the board as it grows.

Example (Find all the factor pairs of 48 with no tiles. [1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8]).

  1. Start at 1 and go in order. 1 fits: 1 × 48 = 48.
  2. 2 fits: 48 is even, and half of 48 is 24. So 2 × 24 = 48.
  3. 3: 48 is not in the 3s I know. Start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30. 18 more is 3 × 6. So 3 × 16 = 48. It fits.
  4. 4: start from ten times: 4 × 10 = 40. 8 more is 4 × 2. So 4 × 12 = 48. It fits.
  5. 5: the 5s go 40, 45, 50. They skip 48, so no.
  6. 6: 6 × 8 = 48. It fits.
  7. 7: 7 × 6 = 42 and 7 × 7 = 49. No. And 7 × 7 is already past 48, so any new pair would be one we have, turned. Stop.
  8. The factor pairs of 48: 1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8.

Point to 3 × 16 and 4 × 12: not in the tables, both found from ten times. A factor fits with none left; it need not be a fact you know. Leave "start from ten times" on the board.

10 min. Every shape on paper: 72 and 90 (Egg Carton Factory, when it exists)

Alone, side 1 (Find all the factor pairs of 72. Write fits or no for each number of rows. [1 × 72, 2 × 36, 3 × 24, 4 × 18, 6 × 12, 8 × 9], Find all the factor pairs of 90. Write fits or no for each number of rows. [1 × 90, 2 × 45, 3 × 30, 5 × 18, 6 × 15, 9 × 10]). For each number of rows from 1 up, students write "fits, with " or "no", mark the turn and list the pairs. At 5 minutes the display shows 72's list; students tick or fix in the second color. At 10 minutes, 90's list.

Watch for 72 with no 3 × 24 or 4 × 18, and 90 with no 5 × 18 or 6 × 15 (error B): ask "what is 3 × 10? How far is that from 72?" Watch for 9 × 8 or 10 × 9 added (error C): ask "which pair is that, turned?"

When it exists: cards 4a and 4b (Every shape), stopped at 10 minutes. Students write "fits" or "no" on paper before each tap of Pack, since Pack does the dividing.

7 min. Practice set 1: 44 and 63

Alone, on the practice sheet (Find all the factor pairs of 44. [1 × 44, 2 × 22, 4 × 11], Find all the factor pairs of 63. [1 × 63, 3 × 21, 7 × 9]). About 5 minutes, then the check screen; students tick a right list and fix a wrong one in the second color.

Check: Did you start at 1? Did you test the numbers not in your tables from ten times? Did you stop at the turn?

If many miss 4 × 11 or 3 × 21, test 3 on 63 together from ten times before set 2.

8 min. Practice set 2: 75 and 66

Alone, the same way (Find all the factor pairs of 75. [1 × 75, 3 × 25, 5 × 15], Find all the factor pairs of 66. [1 × 66, 2 × 33, 3 × 22, 6 × 11]). Walk first to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Did you start at 1? Did you test the numbers not in your tables from ten times? Did you stop at the turn?

Watch for 75 with no 3 × 25, and 66 with no 3 × 22 (error B).

5 min. Fix it: Rosa's list

Side 2 (Rosa says the factor pairs of 42 are 1 × 42, 2 × 21 and 6 × 7. She says she is done. [1 × 42, 2 × 21, 3 × 14, 6 × 7. Rosa missed 3 × 14 because it is not in the times tables (error B)]). Two minutes alone, one minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class: the display shows Rosa's working beside the right working. If many still skip 3: test 3 and 4 on 45 and 56 from ten times.

6 min. Written task: when to stop

Alone, side 2 (Write how you know when to stop looking for factor pairs of 64. [Try 1, 2, 3 and on, in order. At 8, 8 × 8 = 64. Any number after 8 goes with a number under 8, which was already tried, so stop at 8]). It may be dictated. Collect it; read it after class against the look-fors:

  1. says the numbers were tried in order
  2. says the pairs turn around: 8 × 8, or the next pair is one already found

It goes back on day 4.

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "How can 3 be a factor of 42 when 42 is not in the 3 times table you learned?" Expect: "3 × 14 = 42"; "start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30, then 12 more"; "a factor fits with none left, even if it is not a fact I know".

Ask. "We stopped 48 at 7. Why not try every number up to 48?" Expect: "7 × 7 is past 48"; "every pair after that is one we have, turned".

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper (No grid. Find all the factor pairs of 98. [1 × 98, 2 × 49, 7 × 14], Is 6 a factor of 98? Show how you know. [No: 6 × 16 = 96 and 6 × 17 = 102]). Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E3.

Other rooms

  • No devices, or the tool not yet built. The paper path above, in the same 10 minutes.
  • No printer. Write the numbers on the board; students list in notebooks, rows down the margin.
  • One projector. Once the tool exists, you tap Pack; the class writes "fits" or "no" first. Everyone writes the whole list, then the class checks it.
  • One child at home. Search 48 together aloud; the child then does 63 and 75 alone.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop 66 from set 2 and the challenge. Never drop the fix, the written task or the test from ten times. Longer: the challenge (Lee says 6 × 14 = 84, so 3 × 28 = 84 too. Is Lee right? How do you know? [Yes: 3 × 28 = 84. Half of 6 is 3, and double 14 is 28. Or from ten times: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 × 8 = 24, and 60 + 24 = 84]), then the unit's extension: "Which numbers to 50 have an odd number of factors? Why?"

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: factor, factor pair, product, turn. Frame on the display and on side 1: " and are a factor pair of , because × = ." A student may say a list aloud before writing it.

Tomorrow

On the slip, no 7 × 14 (error B): day 4 tests 7 on 98 from ten times. "Yes, 98 is even" (error E): day 4 says so and points ahead to its wrong example. No 1 × 98: write "start at 1" on the slip. Written tasks that stop at 64, or name no turn: day 4 reads one that names the turn. Then day 4 reads 3 × 7 = 21 three ways.
