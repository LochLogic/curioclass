Day 3: Factor pairs with no tiles

Teacher page

GoalI can find all the factor pairs of any number to 100 with no tiles, even pairs that are not in my times tables

Before class

Print everything on the print list. Mark the E2 tickets and sort them: a turned pair listed as new (C), no reason given, fewer than three pairs, and the rest. Put 9 by 9 tables where students can reach them. Seat students who listed fewer than three pairs on E2 near you for Teaching first.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 1

Alone, on the facts check sheet: the 36 products, untimed, no clock shown. Collect at 5 minutes; not reached is not wrong. After class, write the number right under F1 on the record sheet. A 9 by 9 table stays on hand all lesson for anyone who asks.

2 min. E2 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 2 tickets.

If day 2's tickets showed many C (10 × 5 listed as a fourth pair): write 1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10, 10 × 5 on the board and cross out the turned pair together. "10 × 5 is the 5 by 10 rectangle turned."

If many gave no reason: read one ticket whose reason names the turn: "8 × 8 is past 50, so any new pair would be one I have, turned."

If neither: say that today's search answers a missing 2 × 25 (B) or 1 × 50 (A).

9 min. Teaching first: 48 with no tiles, then 81

Whole class, lists only: no tiles or grid from today. Students fill the example lines on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step; write the list on the board as it grows.

Example (Find all the factor pairs of 48 with no tiles. [1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8]).

  1. Start at 1 and go in order. 1 fits: 1 × 48 = 48.
  2. 2 fits: 48 is even, and half of 48 is 24. So 2 × 24 = 48.
  3. 3: 48 is not in the 3s I know. Start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30. 18 more is 3 × 6. So 3 × 16 = 48. It fits.
  4. 4: start from ten times: 4 × 10 = 40. 8 more is 4 × 2. So 4 × 12 = 48. It fits.
  5. 5: the 5s go 40, 45, 50. They skip 48, so no.
  6. 6: 6 × 8 = 48. It fits.
  7. 7: 7 × 6 = 42 and 7 × 7 = 49. No. And 7 × 7 is already past 48, so any new pair would be one we have, turned. Stop.
  8. The factor pairs of 48: 1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8.

Point to 3 × 16 and 4 × 12: not in the tables, both found from ten times. A factor fits with none left; it need not be a fact you know. Leave "start from ten times" on the board. If what is left is still past your table, take another ten.

Example 2 (Is 3 a factor of 81? Start from ten times. [Yes: 3 × 27 = 81 (3 × 20 = 60, and 21 more is 3 × 7)]).

  1. Start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30. 51 more is still past the 3s I know.
  2. Take another ten: 3 × 20 = 60.
  3. 21 more is 3 × 7.
  4. 20 and 7 make 27, so 3 × 27 = 81. It fits.
  5. Check: 60 + 21 = 81.

10 min. Every shape on paper: 72 and 90 (Egg Carton Factory, when it exists)

Alone, side 1 (Find all the factor pairs of 72. Write fits or no for each number of rows. [1 × 72, 2 × 36, 3 × 24, 4 × 18, 6 × 12, 8 × 9], Find all the factor pairs of 90. Write fits or no for each number of rows. [1 × 90, 2 × 45, 3 × 30, 5 × 18, 6 × 15, 9 × 10]). Everyone does 72; 90 for those who finish. For each number of rows from 1 up, students write "fits, with " or "no", mark the turn and list the pairs. At 5 minutes the display shows 72's list; students tick or fix in pen. At 10 minutes, 90's list.

Watch for 72 with no 3 × 24 or 4 × 18, and 90 with no 3 × 30, 5 × 18 or 6 × 15 (error B): ask "Count 3 by tens: 30, 60. How far is 60 from 72?" Watch for 9 × 8 or 10 × 9 added (error C): ask "which pair is that, turned?"

When it exists: cards 4a and 4b (Every shape), stopped at 10 minutes. Students write "fits" or "no" on paper before each tap of Pack, since Pack does the dividing.

7 min. Practice set 1: 63, then 44

Alone, on the practice sheet (Find all the factor pairs of 63. [1 × 63, 3 × 21, 7 × 9], then Find all the factor pairs of 44. [1 × 44, 2 × 22, 4 × 11] for those who finish). About 5 minutes, then the check screen; students tick a right list and fix a wrong one in pen.

Check: Did you start at 1? Did you test the numbers not in your tables from ten times? Did you stop when rows × rows passed the number?

If many miss 4 × 11 or 3 × 21, test 3 on 63 together: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 more is 3 × 1, so 3 × 21 = 63.

8 min. Practice set 2: 75, then 66

Alone, the same way (Find all the factor pairs of 75. [1 × 75, 3 × 25, 5 × 15], then Find all the factor pairs of 66. [1 × 66, 2 × 33, 3 × 22, 6 × 11]). Walk first to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Did you start at 1? Did you test the numbers not in your tables from ten times? Did you stop when rows × rows passed the number?

Watch for 75 with no 3 × 25, and 66 with no 3 × 22 (error B).

5 min. Fix it: Rosa's list

Side 2 (Rosa says the factor pairs of 42 are 1 × 42, 2 × 21 and 6 × 7. She says she is done. [1 × 42, 2 × 21, 3 × 14, 6 × 7. Rosa missed 3 × 14 because it is not in the times tables (error B)]). Two minutes alone, one minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class: the display shows Rosa's working beside the right working. If many still skip 3: test 3 and 4 on 45 and 56 from ten times.

6 min. Written task: when to stop

Alone, side 2 (Write how you know when to stop looking for factor pairs of 64. [Try 1, 2, 3 and on, in order. At 8, 8 × 8 = 64. Any number after 8 goes with a number under 8, which was already tried, so stop at 8]). It may be dictated. Collect it; read it after class against the look-fors:

  1. says the numbers were tried in order
  2. says the pairs turn around: 8 × 8, or the next pair is one already found

It goes back on day 4.

3 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "How can 3 be a factor of 42 when 42 is not in the 3 times table you learned?" Expect: "3 × 14 = 42"; "start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30, then 12 more"; "a factor fits with none left, even if it is not a fact I know".

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (No grid. Find all the factor pairs of 98. [1 × 98, 2 × 49, 7 × 14], Is 6 a factor of 98? Show how you know. [No: 6 × 16 = 96 and 6 × 17 = 102. Also right: a test of 6 that leaves some over (6 × 16 = 96, 2 left), or 6 not in a complete list from part 1 with 6 shown tried]). Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E3.

Other rooms

  • No devices, or the tool not yet built. The paper path above, in the same 10 minutes.
  • No printer. Write the numbers on the board; students list in notebooks, rows down the margin.
  • One projector. Once the tool exists, you tap Pack; the class writes "fits" or "no" first. Everyone writes the whole list, then the class checks it.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop 66 from set 2. Never drop the fix, the written task or the test from ten times. Longer: the challenge (Lee says 6 × 14 = 84, so 3 × 28 = 84 too. Is Lee right? How do you know? [Yes: 3 × 28 = 84. Half of 6 is 3, and double 14 is 28. Or from ten times: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 × 8 = 24, and 60 + 24 = 84]), then the section's extension: "Which numbers to 50 have an odd number of factors? Why?"

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: factor, factor pair, product, turn. Frame on the display: " and are a factor pair of , because × = ." A student may say a list aloud before writing it.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, no 7 × 14 (error B): day 4 tests 7 on 98 from ten times. "Yes, 98 is even" (error E): day 4 says so and points ahead to its wrong example. No 1 × 98: write "start at 1" on the ticket. Written tasks that stop at 64, or name no turn: day 4 reads one that names the turn. Then day 4 reads 3 × 7 = 21 three ways.
