Day 23: Pick a way and prove it

Before class

Print what the header lists. On the covered part of the board, write the eight cards' signs, one proof each, from the key: that is the card key. Put the display page on the projector. Have day 22's written tasks ready to hand back, look-fors ticked, with one line on each.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: one benchmark

Alone, top of side 1. Hand back day 22's written tasks as students start. Use 1/2 to decide. Write >, = or <. 47/100 7/12 [<: 47/100 is less than 1/2 and 7/12 is more] Go over it at 5 minutes: half of 100 is 50, half of 12 is 6. A student who wrote > read 47 and 100 as big numbers (error D).

If day 22's tickets showed many D ((a) <, (b) > or (c) <): before the example, two minutes from the cards (stop them at 23 minutes). Hold up the tenths and twelfths strips and say four pairs aloud as parts of a size: 4 tenths and 4 twelfths, 3 sixths and 3 eighths, 2 fourths and 2 fifths, 5 eighths and 5 twelfths. If many J ((c) >, "bigger pieces"): say "how many parts, of what size" for 4/10 and 40/100. If many G (signs turned round): read six comparisons from day 22 aloud as sentences: 7/10 < 4/5, 3/8 > 3/10, 5/12 < 3/4, 3/10 > 25/100, 7/6 < 5/4, 7/8 < 9/10. Both: D first, G in the stories' pull-together.

5 min. Teaching first: one pair, three ways

Whole class. Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go. The 1/2 way is said, not drawn.

Example (Compare 2/10 and 3/4 three ways: with 1/2, with the same parts, and with the same count. [2/10 < 3/4: 2/10 is under 1/2 and 3/4 is over; 4/20 < 15/20; 6/30 < 6/8]).

  1. With 1/2: half of 10 is 5, and 2 is less, so 2/10 is under 1/2.
  2. Half of 4 is 2, and 3 is more, so 3/4 is over 1/2. One under, one over: 2/10 < 3/4.
  3. Same parts: 20 is a multiple of 10 and of 4. 2/10 = 4/20 and 3/4 = 15/20.
  4. The parts are the same size now, and 4 parts are fewer than 15. So 4/20 < 15/20, and 2/10 < 3/4.
  5. Same count: make both tops 6. 2/10 = 6/30 and 3/4 = 6/8.
  6. 6 parts each. Eighths are bigger than thirtieths, because the whole is cut into fewer parts. So 6/30 < 6/8, and 2/10 < 3/4.
  7. Three ways, one answer. Here 1/2 was quickest, because one is under and one is over. When both are under or both over, 1/2 cannot settle it.

Every way gives the same sign: pick the quickest, then prove it. One more way, for the written task: how far each fraction is from 1.

25 min. Cards, with a partner holding the key

Pairs. Until Fraction Wall Duel exists, the eight cards, six on side 1 and two on side 2 (5/10 7/12 [<: 5/10 is 1/2, and 7/12 is more than 1/2] to 1/4 2/12 [>: 1/4 = 2/8, and eighths are bigger than twelfths (or 3/12 > 2/12)]). Partner A says the sign and the way first, then writes the proof in the row. B checks the sign, then the proof: are the new names equal to the old ones, and does the sign follow? Another complete way is right too. A right sign with no proof is "try again". Swap for card 2, and so on; on your partner's cards, write the sign only. Not all eight are expected.

Two cards are traps; do not say which. A story is a trap only when the two fractions are of wholes that may not be the same size. Two ribbons measured in meters are not a trap: both are parts of one meter.

Watch for:

  • A > on 8/12 and 4/5, > on 9/8 and 8/6, < on 1/4 and 2/12 or < on 2/8 and 3/12: bigger numbers read as a bigger fraction (error D). Ask "how many parts, and of what size?"
  • An = on 6/8 and 8/10, "each is two parts short" (error I): ask which missing piece is longer, 2/8 or 2/10.
  • "Jay" or "Mo" (error H): ask "half of what?"
  • 1/2 tried on 8/12 and 4/5, both over: send them to same parts or same count.

If many miss the traps, hold two strips of different length and fold each in half. At 25 minutes, uncover the card key; pairs check every card against it.

20 min. Two stories, the written task, the planned question

Stories, 10 minutes: 8 alone, then 2 with a partner (Ivy's ribbon is 13/10 meters long. Max's ribbon is 5/3 meters long. Whose ribbon is longer? Write the comparison with >, = or <. [Max's: 13/10 < 5/3 (39/30 < 50/30; or 13/10 is 3/10 past 1, 5/3 is 2/3 past 1, and 3/10 < 2/3)], Noor ate 4/6 of her sandwich. Sam ate 3/5 of his sandwich. Noor says, "I ate more, because 4/6 > 3/5." Can she be sure? Tell why. [No. 4/6 > 3/5 is true (20/30 > 18/30), but the sandwiches are different wholes, so the fractions cannot tell who ate more]). Read both aloud once. (For you: the ribbons are past 1; the sandwiches are different wholes. Do not say so.) Then partners compare; a mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Max's ribbon is longer, 13/10 < 5/3. Noor cannot be sure: the sandwiches are different wholes.

Watch for "Ivy's" (D), "Max's" with 13/10 > 5/3 written (G), and "yes" for Noor (H).

Written task, 6 minutes, alone, the box at the foot of side 2: Which is greater, 5/6 or 9/10? Prove it two ways. One way may be a number line. [9/10: 25/30 < 27/30; or each is one part short of 1 and a tenth is smaller than a sixth; or both on one line. 5/6 < 9/10]. A scribe is allowed. Collect the pages; read them after class against the look-fors.

Pull it together, 4 minutes, whole class.

Ask. "Which way did you pick for 6/8 and 8/10, and why not another?" Expect: same parts or same count, "1/2 can't settle it: both are over". Take one of each, then: "For 1/4 and 2/12?" Expect same count, one rename.

Ask. "When does 'cannot tell' fit?" Expect: only when the two fractions are of wholes that may be different sizes; a meter, one trail or one ribbon is the same whole.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper: Ana and Ben walk the same trail. Ana has walked 2/5 of it. Ben has walked 3/8 of it. Who has walked farther? Write the comparison with >, = or <, and show or tell how you know. You may sketch a line. [Ana:2/5 > 3/8 (16/40 > 15/40, or 6/15 > 6/16)]. No line given; a sketch is allowed. Read it aloud once. Write the results on the record sheet under E23.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Write the eight cards on the board, numbered; pairs work in notebooks.
  • One projector. One card at a time on the screen. Every student writes the sign and the reason, then holds up a sign card, or writes "cannot tell" on the back of the = card.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop cards 1 and 8 and the partner swap after the stories. Never drop the traps, the written task or the proof on each card. Longer: "Find a fraction between 2/3 and 3/4. Prove it.", on the back of side 2.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, common denominator, common numerator, same whole. Frame on the display: " and are both ths now, and is more than , so > ." Read signs aloud as words. A student may place sign cards or say a proof aloud.

Tomorrow

Day 24 is the end-of-unit assessment; mark E23 tonight. "Ben" is error D. Ana's answer with 2/5 < 3/8 written is error G. "Cannot tell" takes the same-whole rule too far: the trail is one whole; write ct in the E23 cell. Hand back the E23 tickets and the written tasks, look-fors ticked, when pencils go down on day 24, so their D, G and "cannot tell" join the respond groups.
