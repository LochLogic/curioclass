Day 22: Pick a way, then compare

Before class

Print the student page, the exit tickets and the key. Sort day 21's tickets: right, a sign turned to "bigger numbers" (D), and 2/3 = 6/3 (A). Those who wrote a sign with no renamed fractions sit near you for the first ten minutes. Put the display page on the projector. Strip envelopes on desks.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: one equal fraction, one comparison

Alone, untimed, side 1 (Fill in the blank: 5/6 = /12. [10], Use 1/2 to write >, = or <: 6/10 3/8. [>: 6/10 is more than 1/2, and 3/8 is less]). No line is printed from today; a student may sketch one. Watch for 11 in the first blank: 6 added to both (error B). Ask "what was each sixth cut into?" At 5 minutes one student reads both answers aloud.

5 min. Teaching first: one pair, two ways

Whole class. Students fill the example frame on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its own screen.

If day 21's tickets showed many D (5/12 > 5/8, or 2/3 < 6/10): first show day 21's fix-it again, "3/12 is more than 3/10, because 12 is more than 10". Hold up one strip in tenths and one in twelfths: more parts, each smaller. Say four pairs aloud as parts of a size: 4 tenths and 4 twelfths, 3 sixths and 3 eighths, 2 fourths and 2 fifths, 5 eighths and 5 twelfths. Take the 3 minutes from set C.

If day 21's tickets showed many A (2/3 = 6/3): fold a strip in thirds, then each third into 3. Count the shaded parts aloud: 6 of 9, so 2/3 = 6/9, not 6/3. Take the 3 minutes from set C.

Example (Compare 3/5 and 6/8 two ways. Write the sign. [3/5 < 6/8: 24/40 < 30/40, and 3/5 < 3/4]).

  1. Way 1, same size parts. List the multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40. 40 is also a multiple of 5.
  2. Cut each fifth into 8 and each eighth into 5: 3/5 = 24/40 and 6/8 = 30/40.
  3. Fortieths are the same size, so compare the counts: 24 fortieths is less than 30 fortieths.
  4. Way 2, same count. Put each 2 eighths together into one fourth: 6/8 = 3/4. Now both have 3 parts.
  5. Fifths are smaller than fourths, so 3 fifths is less than 3 fourths.
  6. Both ways say the same: 3/5 < 6/8. Read it aloud: 3/5 is less than 6/8. The open side faces 6/8.
  7. Which way was quicker here? Way 2: one step, and no big numbers.

Say it plainly: both ways always work. Pick the one with less to do, then check the sign.

4 min. Fix it: the sign turned round

Pairs for one minute, then the class (Rio wrote: "2/5 = 4/10, and 3/10 is less, so 3/10 > 2/5." [3/10 < 2/5: the renaming is right, the sign is turned round]). Rio's thinking was right; only the sign was wrong (error G). The class reads Rio's sign aloud as words.

If many cannot say what went wrong: read these aloud as sentences, then turn each sign: 6/10 > 3/8, 3/5 < 6/8, 3/10 < 2/5, 1/3 > 1/8, 2/5 > 4/12, 3/4 > 7/10.

8 min. Set A

Alone, side 1 (7/10 4/5 [<: 7/10 < 8/10], 3/8 3/10 [>: same count, and eighths are bigger than tenths], 16/10 8/5 [=: 8/5 = 16/10]). No clock: a student who finishes early checks with 1/2 or 1. At 6 minutes show set A's answers; students mark their own in red and fix any wrong sign.

Check: Read each sign aloud as words. Does the open side face the greater number?

Watch for these:

  • 7/10 > 4/5, 16/10 > 8/5 or 3/8 < 3/10: bigger numbers read as a bigger fraction (error D). Ask "how big is each part, and how many are there?"
  • A right sign, no renaming, "bigger pieces" (error J): ask "how many parts?"
  • No renamed fractions and no "same count" written. Ask "which way did you pick?"

If many miss set A for D: hold up a strip in tenths beside one in twelfths, and say set A's pairs aloud as parts of a size. If many miss for G: the class reads set A's three answers aloud before set B.

9 min. Set B

Alone, side 2 (5/12 3/4 [<: 5/12 < 9/12 (and 5/12 is under 1/2, 3/4 is over)], 3/10 25/100 [>: 30/100 > 25/100], 7/6 5/4 [<: 14/12 < 15/12]). At 7 minutes show set B's answers; mark and fix in red.

Check: Read each sign aloud. Does it face the greater number?

11 min. Set C

Alone, side 2 (3/6 3/4 [<: same count, and sixths are smaller than fourths], 3/4 6/10 [>: 6/8 > 6/10 (eighths are bigger than tenths), or 15/20 > 12/20], 9/12 6/8 [=: 18/24 = 18/24, or both are 3/4], 7/8 9/10 [<: 35/40 < 36/40 (7/8 is 1/8 short of 1, 9/10 only 1/10 short)]). Two pairs are equal or past 1. At 9 minutes show set C's answers; mark and fix in red. Watch for 35/40 and 36/40 written right, then 7/8 > 9/10: the sign turned round (error G). Ask the student to read it aloud.

Check: Read each sign aloud. Does it face the greater number?

8 min. Two ways, and which was quicker

Alone, side 2 (Compare 1/4 and 2/10 two ways. Write the sign. Which way was quicker, and why? [5/20 > 4/20, and 2/8 > 2/10, so 1/4 > 2/10; either way may be the quicker, with a reason]). Look-fors: both renamings right, both ways giving 1/4 > 2/10, a reason for the choice. Either way may be the quicker, if the reason fits. Collect the pages at the end of the day and read this item after class.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Which pair today was quickest with the same count? Which needed the same size parts?" Expect: 3/8 and 3/10, or 3/6 and 3/4, already have the same count; 3/4 and 6/10 needed one cut to get there; 7/8 and 9/10 needed fortieths. Take the same-count answer first.

Ask. "How can 1/2 or 1 tell you your sign is wrong?" Expect: 5/12 is under a half and 3/4 is over, so 5/12 must be less; 7/8 and 9/10 are each one part short of 1, and a tenth is the smaller gap.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. No line given; a sketch is allowed (Write >, = or <. Use any way, and show the fractions you renamed. You may sketch a line. (a) 2/3 5/8 (b) 6/12 3/4 (c) 4/10 40/100. [(a) 16/24 > 15/24, so >; (b) 6/12 < 9/12, or 6/12 < 6/8, so <; (c) =]). Write the results on the record sheet under E22.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Copy the pairs on the board one set at a time; students work in notebooks. Read the key aloud after each set.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the third and fourth pairs of set C; keep 7/8 and 9/10 and both planned questions. Never drop the fix-it, the marking after each set or the written item. Longer: the challenge (Find a fraction in twelfths between 2/3 and 5/6. Prove it. [9/12: 2/3 = 8/12 and 5/6 = 10/12, so 8/12 < 9/12 < 10/12]).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, equal to, common denominator, common numerator. Read every sign as words. Frames on the display: " and are both ths now. is more than , so > ." and "Both have parts. ths are bigger, so > ." Read it again in the pair's order. A student may place a sign card or say a comparison aloud.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, (a) <, (b) > or (c) < with no renaming or wrong renaming (error D): day 23's warm-up holds up a tenths strip and a twelfths strip and says four pairs aloud as parts of a size. Right renamed fractions with a sign turned round (error G): day 23 reads six comparisons from day 22 aloud as sentences. (c) > with "bigger pieces" (error J): day 23 says "how many parts, of what size" for both fractions in the warm-up. Hand the written item back in day 23's first minutes, look-fors ticked.
