Day 23: Pick a way and prove it

At home

GoalI can pick a way to compare, and prove my answer
Time39 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at the page. The partner with the key is you.

Skip. The warm-up, cards 1, 7 and 8, and the extension.

  • 8 min. One pair, three ways. Read Example (side 1) together; the child fills the three lines.

  • 12 min. Cards. Cards 2 (side 1), 3, 4, 5 and 6. The child says the sign and the way first, then writes the proof. You say only "yes" or "try again".

    Say. "Which way did you pick for this pair, and why not another?" Expect: for 6/8 and 8/10, same parts or same count, because both are over 1/2.

  • 8 min. Two stories. Stories 1 (side 2), then 2.

    Say. "Can Noor be sure?" Expect: no, the sandwiches are different sizes.

  • 6 min. Prove it. Prove it (side 2). The child may say it while you write.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example (side 1)Compare 210 and 34 three ways: with 12, with the same parts, and with the same count.2/10 < 3/4: 2/10 is under 1/2 and 3/4 is over; 4/20 < 15/20; 6/30 < 6/8
  1. With 1/2: half of 10 is 5, and 2 is less, so 2/10 is under 1/2.
  2. Half of 4 is 2, and 3 is more, so 3/4 is over 1/2. One under, one over: 2/10 < 3/4.
  3. Same parts: 20 is a multiple of 10 and of 4. 2/10 = 4/20 and 3/4 = 15/20.
  4. The parts are the same size now, and 4 parts are fewer than 15. So 4/20 < 15/20, and 2/10 < 3/4.
  5. Same count: make both tops 6. 2/10 = 6/30 and 3/4 = 6/8.
  6. 6 parts each. Eighths are bigger than thirtieths, because the whole is cut into fewer parts. So 6/30 < 6/8, and 2/10 < 3/4.
  7. Three ways, one answer. Here 1/2 was quickest, because one is under and one is over. When both are under or both over, 1/2 cannot settle it.

Stories 1 (side 2)Ivy's ribbon is 1310 meters long. Max's ribbon is 53 meters long. Whose ribbon is longer? Write the comparison with >, = or <.Max's: 13/10 < 5/3 (39/30 < 50/30; or 13/10 is 3/10 past 1, 5/3 is 2/3 past 1, and 3/10 < 2/3)
Stories 2 (side 2)Noor ate 46 of her sandwich. Sam ate 35 of his sandwich. Noor says, "I ate more, because 46 > 35." Can she be sure? Tell why.No. 4/6 > 3/5 is true (20/30 > 18/30), but the sandwiches are different wholes, so the fractions cannot tell who ate more
Prove it (side 2)Which is greater, 56 or 910? Prove it two ways. One way may be a number line.9/10: 25/30 < 27/30; or each is one part short of 1 and a tenth is smaller than a sixth; or both on one line. 5/6 < 9/10
Look-fors:
  1. 5/6 < 9/10, with the sign
  2. each way is complete
  3. says, or shows on one line, that both are of the same whole
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: 5/6 = 25/30 and 9/10 = 27/30, so 5/6 < 9/10. Also on one line from 0 to 1, 5/6 is 1/6 short of 1 and 9/10 is only 1/10 short. Same whole, so 9/10 is greater.
  • partly: 25/30 < 27/30, so 9/10 is greater. (One way, no sign written: ask for a second way.)
  • not: 5/6 > 9/10, because sixths are bigger pieces. (Error J.)
