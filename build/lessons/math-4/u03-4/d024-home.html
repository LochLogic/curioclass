Day 24: Show what I know

At home

GoalI can show what I know and fix what I missed
Time45 minutes, one parent and one child (an assessment day)
Printthe student page; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No strip envelope: fold paper strips.

Skip. The groups, the challenges, and unit 4's readiness check (give it the next morning).

  • 30 min. End-of-unit assessment. Items 1 to 7, alone, no strips, each read aloud once.

  • 15 min. Go over it, one to one. Mark items 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 together from the key; judge 2 and 5 from its look-fors. Fix each missed one in pen beside the first try: item 1 (side 1) by folding a thirds strip into sixths and counting the parts from 0; item 4 (side 1) by asking how many parts each part was cut into, top and bottom alike; item 6 (side 2) by folding two strips of different length in half.

    Say. For item 7 (side 2): "Read each answer aloud as a sentence. Does the open side face the greater fraction?" Expect: 5/6 is greater than 7/10; 3/10 is less than 1/3; 7/4 equals 14/8.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
item 1 (side 1)The point on this line is at 43. Draw marks to cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the new name: 43 = __/__.4/3 = 8/6
item 2 (side 1)Rosa says 26 and 412 are different amounts, because 4 and 12 are bigger than 2 and 6. Is Rosa right? Explain with a drawing.Not right. Each sixth is cut into 2, so there are 2 times as many parts, each half the size; the length is the same
Look-fors:
  1. says how each part was cut
  2. says there are more parts and each is smaller
  3. says the length is the same
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Rosa is not right. I cut every sixth into 2. Now 4 of 12 pieces are shaded. They are smaller but the shaded part did not change.
  • partly: Rosa is not right. 2/6 and 4/12 are at the same place on the line. (Look-for 3 only: ask how each part was cut, and what happened to its size.)
  • not: She is wrong because 2 × 2 = 4 and 6 × 2 = 12. (No drawing and no cut: the rule said, not explained.)
item 3 (side 1)(i) Circle every fraction equal to 610: 35, 812, 60100, 6100. (ii) Are 104 and 156 the same number? Show how you know.(i) 3/5 and 60/100. (ii) Yes: both are 5/2 (or both are 30/12)
item 4 (side 1)Fill in each blank: 76 = __/12. 65 = 12/__. 1110 = __/100.14; 10; 110
item 5 (side 2)Use 12 to decide which is greater, 48100 or 35. Do not rename them. Write the comparison with a sign and explain. You may draw a number line.48/100 is less than 1/2 and 3/5 is more, so 48/100 < 3/5
Look-fors:
  1. compares each fraction with 1/2 correctly
  2. gives a reason: half of the denominator, or an equal name for 1/2
  3. writes 48/100 < 3/5
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Half of 100 is 50, so 48/100 is a little less than 1/2. Half of 5 is 2 and a half, so 3/5 is more than 1/2. 48/100 < 3/5.
  • partly: 48/100 is less than 1/2 and 3/5 is more than 1/2, so 3/5 is greater. (Look-for 1 only: no reason for either side, and no sign; ask how they know.)
  • not: 3/5 is bigger because fifths are bigger pieces. (Error J: the size of the parts alone.)
item 6 (side 2)Leo has read 23 of his book. Mai has read 14 of her book. Leo says, 'I have read more pages than you.' Can Leo be sure? Circle yes or no. Then finish the sentence below.No. "The wholes are the same", in any words (2/3 > 1/4 holds only for the same whole)
item 7 (side 2)Write >, = or <. Show the fractions you renamed. (a) 56 __ 710 (b) 310 __ 13 (c) 74 __ 148(a) 25/30 > 21/30, so >. (b) 3/10 < 3/9, or 9/30 < 10/30, so <. (c) 7/4 = 14/8, so =
