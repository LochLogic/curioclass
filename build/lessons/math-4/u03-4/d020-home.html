Day 20: Rename both fractions

At home

GoalI can rename both fractions when neither denominator fits the other
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The warm-up, On your own 4 and 5, and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Read Example 1 (side 1) together; the child fills the frame under the line. Then Example 2 (side 1).

    Say. "Can 2/3 and 4/5 be put in the same parts? Which parts?" Expect: fifteenths; cut each third into 5 and each fifth into 3.

  • 8 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 2).

    Say. "Ivy's sign is right. Is her working right?" Expect: no; 4/5 is 24/30, not 4/30.

  • 12 min. On your own. On your own 2 (side 2), then 3. The child lists multiples first, then checks the second pair with 1/2.

    Say. "Is 15 the only number of parts that works for thirds and fifths?" Expect: no; 30 works too, or any number in both lists.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)Compare 23 and 45. Rename both so the parts are the same size.10/15 < 12/15, so 2/3 < 4/5
  1. Thirds and fifths are not the same size, so we cannot compare the 2 and the 4 yet.
  2. List multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15. List multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15. 15 is in both lists.
  3. Cut each third into 5 equal parts. Now the whole has 15 parts. 2 thirds are 2 × 5 = 10 fifteenths, so 2/3 = 10/15.
  4. Cut each fifth into 3 equal parts. The whole has 15 parts again. 4 fifths are 4 × 3 = 12 fifteenths, so 4/5 = 12/15.
  5. Now the parts are the same size, so we compare the counts: 10 fifteenths is less than 12 fifteenths.
  6. Write it: 2/3 = 10/15 and 4/5 = 12/15, and 10/15 < 12/15, so 2/3 < 4/5. On the line, 2/3 is left of 4/5.
Example 2 (side 1)Compare 58 and 712. Rename both so the parts are the same size.15/24 > 14/24, so 5/8 > 7/12
  1. List multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24. List multiples of 12: 12, 24. 24 is in both lists. 8 × 12 = 96 works too, but 24 comes first.
  2. Cut each eighth into 3 equal parts. 5 eighths are 5 × 3 = 15 twenty-fourths, so 5/8 = 15/24.
  3. Cut each twelfth into 2 equal parts. 7 twelfths are 7 × 2 = 14 twenty-fourths, so 7/12 = 14/24.
  4. 15 twenty-fourths is more than 14 twenty-fourths: 15/24 > 14/24, so 5/8 > 7/12.
  5. Check with 1/2: both are more than 1/2, so 1/2 does not settle this pair. The renamed parts do.
