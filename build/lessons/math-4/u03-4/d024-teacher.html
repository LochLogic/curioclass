Before class

Stack the day 23 papers by name, held until pencils go down. Cover the Word Wall's comparing cards. Strip envelopes stay in desks until the groups; put plain paper out.

The day

30 min. End-of-unit assessment

Alone: no strips, cards or printed lines; a sketched line is allowed. Read each item aloud once. At 30 minutes, pencils down; not reached is not wrong.

20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

Mark, 5 minutes. Pencils away; hand back the day 23 papers; show answers to 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7. Students mark in pen, matching wrong answers to the group list. Say: "Raise a hand if your item 6 sentence differs; I will check it."

Groups, 15 minutes. Hands up sets the groups (in two: join the earlier). Fixes go in pen in the fixes box. Tasks are on the display.

  • A, C and K (item 1: 4/6, 9/6 or 8/12; item 3: 6/100; item 4: first blank 7, second 5, third 11; item 7: only the bottom renamed): fold a thirds strip into sixths; lay it on the line from 0, count one whole's parts, then on past 1. For item 7, cut one sixth into 5 and count the parts in 5/6.
  • B and E (item 1: 6/5; item 3: 8/12, 3/5 left out, or "no"; item 4: first blank 13, second 11, third 101): mark your answer and the given fraction on one line. Adding moves the point; cutting does not. (ii): name both with smaller numbers. Left out 3/5: group the tenths in twos.
  • D, G and J (item 7, or a day 23 ticket saying Ben, a turned sign, or sixths as bigger pieces): tenths beside twelfths strips: more parts, each smaller. Say each pair as parts of a size, then as a sentence; the open side faces the greater.
  • H (item 6 "yes", or a day 23 "cannot tell"): fold two strips of different length in half; lay the halves together. "Half of what?" Then the trail story: one trail, one whole, so you can tell.

Students who missed nothing do Find a fraction between 2/3 and 3/4. Prove it. [For example 17/24: 2/3 = 16/24 and 3/4 = 18/24, so 16/24 < 17/24 < 18/24. Any fraction between, such as 5/7, with a proof], then Find a fraction between 1/6 and 1/5. Prove it. [For example 11/60: 1/6 = 10/60 and 1/5 = 12/60. Or 2/11: 2/12 < 2/11 < 2/10], then check a group's fixes.

10 min. Unit 4's readiness check

Alone, on paper, no strips: unit 4's readiness check, four items, each read aloud once. Collect the sheets and the assessment pages at the end.

Tomorrow

After class: mark unit 4's readiness check (its teacher notes); record 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 from the pencil first try, with error letters; judge 2 and 5 by look-for.

Item 2:

  1. says how each part was cut
  2. says there are more parts and each is smaller
  3. says the length is the same

Item 5:

  1. compares each fraction with 1/2 correctly
  2. gives a reason: half of the denominator, or an equal name for 1/2
  3. writes 48/100 < 3/5

Day 25's second five minutes, after the warm-up, hand 2 and 5 back, look-fors ticked. Agrees with Rosa (D): two sixths beside four twelfths. Parts' size alone (J): say each as "how many parts, of what size". Item 5 D or G: one line from 0 to 1, both points, read the sign aloud.
