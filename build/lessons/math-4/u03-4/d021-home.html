Day 21: Same count, different parts

At home

GoalI can compare by making the numerators the same
Time37 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at the two printed lines on side 1. No paper strips: the two printed lines.

Skip. The warm-up, pairs 5 and 6, and the challenge.

  • 8 min. Same count. Example 1 (side 1), then 2; the child fills the frames.

    Say. "Both have 4 parts. Which parts are longer, tenths or twelfths?" Expect: tenths; the same whole is cut into fewer parts.

  • 8 min. Two ways. Example 3 (side 1): the child renames 1/3 as sixths, then both as twenty-fourths.

    Say. "Which way was quicker?" Expect: sixths; only one fraction was renamed.

  • 6 min. Fix it. Fix it 3 (side 1). The child counts 3 parts from 0 on each line.

  • 10 min. On your own. On your own 1 (side 2), 2, 3 and 4. The child reads each answer to you as a sentence.
  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.
