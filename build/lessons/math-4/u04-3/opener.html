Section 4.3: Mixed numbers (days 32 to 35)

The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NF.B.3c: "Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, e.g., by replacing each mixed number with an equivalent fraction, and/or by using properties of operations and the relationship between addition and subtraction." Both ways are taught, both operations on every day from 33, and every difference is checked by adding.

A mixed number is a sum. 2 3/4 means 2 + 3/4. Read it "two and three fourths". Each whole is four fourths, so 2 3/4 = 4/4 + 4/4 + 3/4 = 11/4. Going back, 13/4 takes out wholes of four fourths: three of them, and 1/4 left, 3 1/4. No "times the bottom, add the top" rule: the sum is the reason, and section 4.1 already wrote wholes as parts.

Way 1, all as fractions. Rename each mixed number as a fraction, add or take away the counts, rename back.

Way 2, wholes and parts. Add the wholes, add the parts, in any order (the commutative and associative properties). When the parts pass 1, b/b of them make a new whole: 1 3/4 + 1 3/4 = 2 6/4 = 2 + 4/4 + 2/4 = 3 2/4. When the parts are too few to take away, break one whole into b/b: 4 1/3 − 1 2/3 becomes 3 4/3 − 1 2/3 = 2 2/3.

Two ways, one answer. On day 33, 1 3/5 + 3 4/5 is done both ways side by side; both give 5 2/5. They must, since each way only renames the same amount. Then a difference both ways.

Limits. Like denominators; wholes to 6 in the given numbers. No simplest form. A sum such as 5 15/12 is a right sum, not yet written as a mixed number: it gets a tick, and the student joins group G on day 38.

The line and the strips

  • Day 32. The line from 0 to 5 (lines 7 to 12 of section 4.1's pages): wholes counted first, then parts. Whole strips with their parts.
  • Day 33. Both ways in two columns; hops past a whole tied to the new whole written down (error G). Recipe Scaler's double batch and test bake (light, practise with feedback, 15 min): it shows one way at a time, so the side by side stays on paper.
  • Day 34. A whole strip broken into its parts on the table, tied to the written renaming (error F).
  • Day 35. Withdrawn: a sketch only. Recipe Scaler's pantry rounds (key, practise with feedback, 20 min), every take-away checked by adding back; where the tool cannot run, the day page's paper path covers the same rounds.

Why this model

Whole strips make "one whole" a thing that can be broken or built, so regrouping is a move on the table before it is a step on paper. The line to 5 keeps every mixed number a point, which is how unit 5's line plots will use them. Two columns on one page make the reason for "the same answer" visible.

The errors to expect, in plain words

What students do What it shows What to do when many do it
A Add the tops and the bottoms inside a mixed number A fraction read as two whole numbers Say the parts in words; the part size stays
C Put the whole number on top: 3 2/5 = 5/5 A whole not yet seen as n parts Write each whole as 5/5 first, then count fifths
F Take the smaller from the larger in each place: 4 1/3 − 1 2/3 = 3 1/3 A one not broken into parts Break a whole strip on the table; write the new name before subtracting; check by adding
G Drop the new whole: 1 3/4 + 1 3/4 = 2 2/4 Regrouping done as a step without the reason Hop the parts past a whole on the line: 4/4 lands on the next whole number

Wrong examples: C again on day 32, G on day 33, F on day 34. A returns wherever a part is added.

Supplies for the section

  • Per student: three whole strips, folded into the day's parts; the number line pages (lines 7 to 12); a pencil.
  • Per class: the projector; a board split into two columns.
  • A room with nothing: whole strips cut from paper and folded; lines ruled to 5.

Words

Mixed number, whole, break apart, regroup, check. Read "two and three fourths". Frame, to take or leave: " wholes and parts of size is parts of size ."

How the unit is checked

Exits on days 32 to 35, on paper; day 34's written task, judged with look-fors and handed back on day 35; items 4.3a, 4.3b and 4.3c on the end-of-unit assessment, day 38. All of it goes on the unit's record sheet (u04-1/record.md).
