Day 9: Show what I know

Before class

Stack the day 8 written tasks by name, held until pencils go down. Squares stay away until the groups. Have day 8's tickets, unit 2's record sheet and unit 3's record sheet beside you.

The day

30 min. End-of-unit assessment

Alone, unaided: no squares, counters, dots or 1 to 100 chart. Read each item aloud once. At 30 minutes, pencils down; not reached is not wrong.

20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

Mark, 5 minutes. Pencils away; show the answers to 1, 2, 4 and 6. Students mark in pen and find their group.

Check: Start at 8. The rule is add 9. Write the first six numbers. [8, 17, 26, 35, 44, 53], Start at 12, add 11: 12, 23, 34, 45, 56. Write two things you notice that the rule did not tell you. [Any two: even and odd take turns; the ones digit goes up by 1 each time; the tens digit goes up by 1 each time; in each number the ones digit is 1 more than the tens digit; or any other true thing about all five numbers that is not the rule (if yours is not listed, ask your teacher). Only even and odd taking turns lasts for ever: the digit features stop at the 9th number, 100], Step 1 is one square sitting on a row of 5 squares. The rule: each step adds one more row of 5 underneath. Draw steps 2 and 3. Write the number of squares in steps 1 to 4. How many squares are in step 9? [Step 2 has two rows of 5, step 3 has three, each with the one square on top; 6, 11, 16, 21; step 9 is 6 + 8 × 5 = 46 (or 1 + 9 × 5 = 46)], Start at 14. The rule is multiply by 3. Write the first four numbers. What do you notice about every number? [14, 42, 126, 378; each is even (also right: each is a multiple of 14, or of 7; every number after the first is a multiple of 3)]

Groups, 15 minutes. By the marking: a wrong number on 1, 4 or 6 goes to A and B; a miss on 2, or "They triple" on 6, to C and D; in both, join the first. If day 8's tickets showed A, B or C, watch those first. Fixes go in pen in the fixes box.

  • A and B (item 1: 9, 18, 27 or 17, 26, 35; item 4: 5, 10, 15, 20 and 45, or all nine steps drawn; item 6: 42, 126, 378, or adding; day 8's written task: you drew all thirty shapes): circle the start in each stem, write it first, then jump. Item 4: build step 1 with squares, one on a row of 5, and write 6. Say step 9 in words first, "the first shape and how many jumps?": 6 + 8 × 5 = 46. Item 6: say "times 3" at each jump. Hand back the day 8 written tasks here, look-fors ticked: count by threes to 30.
  • C and D (item 2: "It goes up by 11" or "They are all even"; item 6: "They triple"): cover the rule. Write odd or even under every number of item 2 before saying anything, then list what is true of the numbers themselves. For item 6, write what is true of 14, 42, 126 and 378 that "times 3" never said.

Students who missed nothing do Start at any number and add 6. When are all the numbers even? When are they all odd? Can even and odd ever take turns? Why? [All even when the start is even; all odd when the start is odd. They never take turns: 6 is three pairs, so a jump of 6 never changes odd or even], then Make a rule whose numbers all end in 3 or 8. Write the start, the rule and six numbers. Why does it keep going? [For example start at 8, add 5: 8, 13, 18, 23, 28, 33. Any start ending in 3 or 8 works with an add that ends in 5 (they take turns) or in 0 (they all end the same). Adding a number ending in 5 is a jump of 10 every two jumps, and 10 leaves the ones digit alone], then check a group's fixes.

10 min. Unit 3's readiness check

Alone, on paper, no strips: unit 3's readiness check, four items, each read aloud once. Collect it and the assessments at the end.

Other rooms

  • No projector. Write the answers to 1, 2, 4 and 6 and the group tasks on a covered part of the board.
  • No printer. Write the six items on the board; students answer in a notebook, item 4's drawing included.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: groups ten minutes, never the assessment, marking or readiness check. Longer: the challenges.

Words and supports

Items read aloud once; large print offered; item 4 may be built with squares and drawn by a scribe; items 3 and 5 may be said aloud to a scribe.

Tomorrow

After class: record 1, 2, 4 and 6 from the pencil first try, with error letters; judge 3 and 5 by look-for. Mark unit 3's readiness check (its teacher notes) onto unit 3's record sheet, R1 to R4, so day 10 opens with the results beside you.

Item 3:

  1. says Nia is right
  2. says 8 is even or 61 is odd
  3. says why odd plus even stays odd

Item 5:

  1. the feature is not the rule
  2. the reason is about what adding 5 does, not more numbers

Hand 3 and 5 back in day 10's first five minutes, look-fors ticked, with the "meets" answers shown; students fix in pen. More numbers as the reason: a dot-pairs sketch of one jump, then "It must keep going because ". Unit 2's errors come back on Check 1 (day 42).
