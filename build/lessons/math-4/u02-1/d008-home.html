Day 8: Shape patterns, and the 10th step without drawing

At home

GoalI can follow a rule to make a shape pattern and find a later step without drawing them all
Time37 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page, the recording page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No squares: paper squares torn from scrap. No sticks: toothpicks or pencil marks. No projector: point at the page.

Skip. The warm-up, the reteach, the tapes and the repeating shapes.

  • 5 min. Fix it 1. Fix it 1 (side 1): the child writes eight numbers before saying what is true.

  • 10 min. The example. Read Example 1 (side 1) together. Build steps 1 to 3 with paper squares; the child writes each count under its step on the recording page, shape pattern 1.

    Say. "How many squares in the 10th step? Can you tell without drawing it?" Expect: 2, then 9 more twos, 20.

  • 8 min. Fix it 2. Build stick squares, steps 1 to 3, with toothpicks. Then Fix it 2 (side 2).

    Say. "How many sticks in the 10th shape? Can you tell without drawing it?" Expect: 4, then 9 more threes, 31.

  • 6 min. On your own. Plus sign (side 2), on shape pattern 3: count first, then a feature and why.

  • 3 min. Written task. Written task (side 2), alone.
  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Fix it 1 (side 1)Kai made start at 3, multiply by 2: 3, 6, 12, 24. He looked at 3 and 6. He says odd and even take turns.They do not take turns: 12 is even after 6. Eight numbers first: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 192, 384. Only 3 is odd; every number after the first is even
Example 1 (side 1)Step 1 is a column of 2 squares. Each step adds one more column of 2. How many squares are in the 10th step? What is true that the rule did not say?2, 4, 6, 8, 10; the 10th step has 2 + 9 × 2 = 20 squares. Every count is even: each step is columns of 2, so it is all pairs
  1. Build step 1: one column of 2 squares. Write 2 under step 1. The start is the first count, before any jump.
  2. Build step 2: add a column of 2, so 4. Step 3 is 6. Draw an arrow, add 2, between neighbours.
  3. The shape is now a list: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Each count sits under its step.
  4. The 10th step, without drawing: step 1, then the jumps. From step 1 to step 10 is 9 jumps, not 10. 2 + 9 × 2 = 2 + 18 = 20.
  5. A check: step 10 is 10 columns of 2, and 10 × 2 = 20. That works here only because step 1 is one jump. Most patterns start with more or less than one jump: the sticks will show it.
  6. What the rule did not say: every count is even. Why: every step is columns of 2, all pairs, and each jump adds one more pair.
Fix it 2 (side 2)Jo made stick squares in a row: 4, 7, 10, 13, 16 sticks. For the 10th step, Jo drew all ten squares and counted 31 sticks.31 is right, but drawing every step is slow and one slip spoils it. Step 1 and 9 jumps of 3: 4 + 9 × 3 = 4 + 27 = 31
Plus sign (side 2)A plus sign grows. Step 1 is 1 square. Each step adds 4 squares, one at the end of each arm. How many squares are in the 10th step? Write one thing the rule did not say. Why will it keep going?1, 5, 9, 13, 17; the 10th step is 1 + 9 × 4 = 37 squares. Every count is odd: 1 is odd, and 4 is two pairs, so the one left over never gets a partner
Written task (side 2)The triangles in circle, square, triangle are the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th shapes. Sam says the 30th shape is a triangle. Is Sam right? Write why.Sam is right: the triangles sit at the multiples of 3, and 30 = 3 × 10
Look-fors:
  1. says Sam is right
  2. says the triangles sit at the multiples of 3 and 30 is 3 × 10
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Sam is right. The triangles are every 3rd shape: 3, 6, 9. 30 is 3 × 10, so it is a triangle.
  • partly: Sam is right. I drew all 30 shapes and the last is a triangle. (Look-for 1 only, error B: ask how to know without drawing.)
  • not: No. 30 is not 3, 6, 9 or 12. (Only the listed shapes are checked: ask what comes after 12.)
the ticket questionStep 1 is a square of small squares, 3 rows of 3. The rule: each step adds one more row of 3 on top. Draw step 2. Write the number of squares in steps 1 to 4. How many squares are in the 10th step? Write one thing you notice that the rule did not say. Why does it keep going?9, 12, 15, 18; the 10th step is 9 + 9 × 3 = 36. Odd and even take turns, since adding 3 changes one to the other; or all are multiples of 3, since every step is rows of 3 (also right: the 10th is 12 rows of 3)
Look-fors:
  1. writes 9, 12, 15, 18 and 36 for the 10th step
  2. names a feature true of every count that is not the rule
  3. says what one row of 3 does to it
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: 9, 12, 15, 18; the 10th is 9 + 9 × 3 = 36. Odd and even take turns: 3 is a pair and one more, so each row turns odd to even and even to odd.
  • partly: 9, 12, 15, 18, 36. All multiples of 3, because 21 is too. (Look-fors 1 and 2 only: ask what one row of 3 does.)
  • not: 9, 12, 15, 18, 30. It adds 3. (Error A on the 10th step, error C as the feature.)
