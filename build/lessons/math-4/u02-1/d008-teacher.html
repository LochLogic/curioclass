Day 8: Shape patterns, and the 10th step without drawing

Teacher page

Day8, section 2.1, understanding day
GoalI can follow a rule to make a shape pattern and find a later step without drawing them all
Standard parts4.OA.B.4 (a) all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1 to 100; 4.OA.B.4 (c) whether a whole number in the range 1 to 100 is a multiple of a given one-digit number; 4.OA.C.5 (b) a shape pattern that follows a given rule; 4.OA.C.5 (c) features of the pattern that were not in the rule itself; 4.OA.C.5 (d) informally why a feature will keep going (the standard's own example: "explain informally why the numbers will continue to alternate")

Before class

Print everything on the print list. Put the display page on the projector. Write the two shape tapes on a covered part of the board: "Start at 3 tiles. Add 2. 3, 5, 7" and "Start at 2 tiles. Add 5. 2, 7, 12". Sort day 7's tickets by error. Lay out squares and sticks.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: factor pairs and multiples, or the reteach

Alone, top of side 1 (Find all the factor pairs of 82. [1 × 82, 2 × 41], Is 97 a multiple of 3? Show how you know. [No: 3 × 32 = 96 and 3 × 33 = 99, so 97 falls between them], Is 78 a multiple of 6? Show how you know. [Yes: 6 × 13 = 78 (6 × 10 = 60, and 3 more sixes are 18)]). At 4 minutes, show the answers screen; students check their own in pen.

Students who missed a readiness item sit with you for the opener's reteach: item 1 or 2, skip count by 6 on the 1 to 100 chart; item 3, counters put in pairs.

5 min. Reteach, or E7 back

If many missed a readiness item (more than a third): the warm-up and these 5 minutes are its reteach, whole class.

Otherwise, hand back day 7's tickets. Show the answer to E7 on the board: 29, 35, 41, 47, 53, 59, all odd, an odd number plus 6 is odd. Students fix in pen beside the first try.

If many C, D or E ("because it adds 6", "take turns", or 65 as the reason): cover the rule and list what is true of 29, 35, 41 by themselves; take turns fails at 35. Then dot pairs for one jump of 6: three pairs, so the one left over stays alone. Finish together: "It must keep going because ".

Many A (6 or 35 first): circle the start on five rules (start 5, add 2; start 7, add 10; start 4, add 6; start 10, add 3; start 6, add 4), write it first, then jump. These students build step 1 first in every shape today.

3 min. Fix it 1: two numbers are not a feature

Whole class, on day 7's doubling list (recording page, side 1, pattern 4). Kai made start at 3, multiply by 2: 3, 6, 12, 24. He looked at 3 and 6. He says odd and even take turns. [They do not take turns: 12 is even after 6. Eight numbers first: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 192, 384. Only 3 is odd; every number after the first is even]

  1. Kai's working: 3 is odd and 6 is even, so he said odd and even take turns. He named a feature from two numbers.
  2. Right working: write eight numbers before saying anything: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 192, 384. 12 is even after 6, so they do not take turns. Only 3 is odd. Every number after the first is 2 times a number, all pairs: even.

If many named a feature from two or three numbers: students look at the eight numbers already in pattern 4 and say what is true of them before anyone says odd or even.

8 min. Teaching first: columns of 2

You draw the steps on the board, and pairs build with squares. Students write the counts in shape pattern 1 on the recording page.

Example 1 (Step 1 is a column of 2 squares. Each step adds one more column of 2. How many squares are in the 10th step? What is true that the rule did not say? [2, 4, 6, 8, 10; the 10th step has 2 + 9 × 2 = 20 squares. Every count is even: each step is columns of 2, so it is all pairs]).

  1. Build step 1: one column of 2 squares. Write 2 under step 1. The start is the first count, before any jump.
  2. Build step 2: add a column of 2, so 4. Step 3 is 6. Draw an arrow, add 2, between neighbours.
  3. The shape is now a list: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Each count sits under its step.
  4. The 10th step, without drawing: step 1, then the jumps. From step 1 to step 10 is 9 jumps, not 10. 2 + 9 × 2 = 2 + 18 = 20.
  5. A check: step 10 is 10 columns of 2, and 10 × 2 = 20. That works here only because step 1 is one jump. Most patterns start with more or less than one jump: the sticks will show it.
  6. What the rule did not say: every count is even. Why: every step is columns of 2, all pairs, and each jump adds one more pair.

Ask. After step 3: "How many squares in the 10th step? Can you tell without drawing it?" Expect: 20, and "2, then 9 more twos". If you hear 22: count the jumps from step 1 to step 3, two, not three.

10 min. Rule Machine, on paper: two shape tapes

Alone, on the recording page, side 2, the shape tapes (Start at 3 tiles. Add 2 tiles each step. The tape shows 3, 5, 7. Write the next five. How many tiles are in the 10th step? [9, 11, 13, 15, 17; the 10th step is 3 + 9 × 2 = 21 tiles], Start at 2 tiles. Add 5 tiles each step. The tape shows 2, 7, 12. Write the next five. How many tiles are in the 10th step? [17, 22, 27, 32, 37; the 10th step is 2 + 9 × 5 = 47 tiles]). When it exists: Rule Machine, the shape tapes, practise with feedback. Until then, uncover tape 1 on the board. Students copy the tape, write the next five counts, then the 10th step on side 1. At 5 minutes show the answers; each checks their own in pen. Then tape 2 the same way.

Watch for 20 or 50: the start left out (error A); ask "how many tiles in step 1?" For 23 or 52, ten jumps: ask "how many jumps from step 1 to step 3?"

Stop at 10 minutes.

24 min. By hand: sticks, a plus sign, and shapes that repeat

Sticks, 7 minutes. Pairs build stick squares, steps 1 to 3, in shape pattern 2 on the recording page: 4, 7, 10.

Ask. "How many sticks in the 10th shape? Can you tell without drawing it?" Pairs write a guess first. Expect: 4, then 9 more threes, 31. Set 30 (ten threes) and 34 (ten jumps) against step 1's 4 sticks. If many still draw: say step 10 in words first, "the first shape and how many jumps?"

Then Fix it 2 on side 2 (Jo made stick squares in a row: 4, 7, 10, 13, 16 sticks. For the 10th step, Jo drew all ten squares and counted 31 sticks. [31 is right, but drawing every step is slow and one slip spoils it. Step 1 and 9 jumps of 3: 4 + 9 × 3 = 4 + 27 = 31]) as the check, pairs, then the class.

  1. Jo's working: Jo drew ten squares in a row and counted every stick, one by one. 31 is right, but it took a long time, and one slip spoils it.
  2. Right working: step 1 has 4 sticks. Each new square shares a side, so it adds 3 sticks. From step 1 to step 10 is 9 jumps. 4 + 9 × 3 = 4 + 27 = 31.

On your own, 11 minutes. Side 2, alone. The plus sign (A plus sign grows. Step 1 is 1 square. Each step adds 4 squares, one at the end of each arm. How many squares are in the 10th step? Write one thing the rule did not say. Why will it keep going? [1, 5, 9, 13, 17; the 10th step is 1 + 9 × 4 = 37 squares. Every count is odd: 1 is odd, and 4 is two pairs, so the one left over never gets a partner]): squares in hand, shape pattern 3 on the recording page. Then the repeating shapes (A pattern repeats circle, square, triangle. Draw the first 12 shapes. Which shapes are squares? [The 2nd, 5th, 8th and 11th shapes: every 3rd shape from the 2nd]), drawn in a row in the box. Watch for 40 or 41 on the plus sign, and "it adds 4" as the feature (error C): cover the rule. Squares at the 3rd, 6th: ask "which shape is 2nd?"

Written task, 6 minutes. Side 2, alone (The triangles in circle, square, triangle are the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th shapes. Sam says the 30th shape is a triangle. Is Sam right? Write why. [Sam is right: the triangles sit at the multiples of 3, and 30 = 3 × 10]). Collect it to judge after class with the key's two look-fors. A student who drew thirty shapes has the first only (error B).

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (Step 1 is a square of small squares, 3 rows of 3. The rule: each step adds one more row of 3 on top. Draw step 2. Write the number of squares in steps 1 to 4. How many squares are in the 10th step? Write one thing you notice that the rule did not say. Why does it keep going? [9, 12, 15, 18; the 10th step is 9 + 9 × 3 = 36. Odd and even take turns, since adding 3 changes one to the other; or all are multiples of 3, since every step is rows of 3 (also right: the 10th is 12 rows of 3)]). Read it aloud once. Tick each look-for alone: the four counts and 36 for the 10th step; a feature that is not the rule; a reason that says what one row of 3 does to it. Record under E8.

Other rooms

  • No squares or sticks. Squares drawn on the recording page; sticks as pencil marks, a short line a side.
  • No printer. Write the tapes, the stems and the frames on the board; students rule boxes in a notebook.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop tape 2 and the repeating shapes. Never drop Fix it 2 or the written task; every shape today ends with "the start and how many jumps". Longer: pairs make a shape rule, build steps 1 to 3, find the 10th, and swap to check.

Words and supports

Word Wall card: step. Frames, on side 1: "I notice ." "The 10th step is the start and jumps." Access: a student who cannot build directs a partner; reasons may be said aloud to you or a scribe.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 3, 6, 9 first or the 10th as 30 (error A), or all ten steps drawn (error B): watch these students on day 9's items 1, 4 and 6; a miss puts them in the A and B group. "It adds 3" (error C): the C and D group if they miss item 2. More numbers as the reason: read beside items 3 and 5. The written task is judged tonight and handed back on day 9.
