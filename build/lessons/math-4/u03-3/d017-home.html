Day 17: Half of what?

At home

GoalI can say why two fractions must be of the same whole, and use 1/2 to compare
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. Strips: cut a 10-inch and a 6-inch strip from any paper. No projector: point at the page.

Skip. The warm-up, the pizza talk, On your own 2 and the challenge.

  • 6 min. Two strips. Work Example 1 (side 1) with your strips; the child folds and lays the halves together.

    Say. "Half of this strip and half of that one: the same length? Both one half?" Expect: not the same length; both are 1/2, each of its own strip.

  • 8 min. Example 2. Read Example 2 (side 1) together on the printed line; the child fills the frame.

  • 8 min. Fix two. Fix it 1 (side 1), then 2.

    Say. "Rio says this 1/2 is greater. Half of what?" Expect: each is half of its own pizza, so 1/2 = 1/2.

  • 8 min. Sort and compare. Sort (side 2), then On your own 1 (side 2). The child says where each is beside 1/2.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)Ms. Ruiz has a 10-inch strip and a 6-inch strip. She folds each in half. How long is each half? Is each one 12?5 inches and 3 inches; each is 1/2 of its own strip, so the halves are not the same amount
  1. Fold the 10-inch strip in half: each half is 5 inches.
  2. Fold the 6-inch strip in half: each half is 3 inches.
  3. Lay the two halves together. They are not the same length.
  4. Are both one half? Yes. Each is 1/2 of its own strip.
  5. So "1/2" alone does not tell how much. Ask: half of what?
  6. Two fractions can be compared only when they are parts of the same whole.
Example 2 (side 1)Compare 25 and 58. Use 12.2/5 < 1/2 and 1/2 < 5/8, so 2/5 < 5/8
  1. Draw a line from 0 to 2. Mark 1/2 and 1. The whole is the length from 0 to 1.
  2. Is 2/5 more or less than 1/2? Half of 5 parts is two and a half parts. 2 parts is less, so 2/5 is left of 1/2. Write 2/5 < 1/2.
  3. Is 5/8 more or less than 1/2? In eighths, 1/2 is 4/8. 5/8 is one eighth more.
  4. So 5/8 is right of 1/2. Write 1/2 < 5/8.
  5. 2/5 is left of 1/2 and 5/8 is right of it, so 2/5 < 5/8.
  6. We never compared 2 with 5 or 5 with 8. We compared each fraction with 1/2.
Fix it 1 (side 1)Rio says: "Half a large pizza is more food than half a small pizza. So this 12 is greater than that 12."Each is 1/2 of its own pizza, so 1/2 = 1/2. The large half is more food because its whole is bigger. Compare fractions only of the same whole
Fix it 2 (side 1)Kim says: "410 is more than 12, because 4 and 10 are bigger."4/10 < 1/2: in tenths 1/2 is 5/10, and 4/10 is one tenth less. Kim read the numbers as whole numbers
Sort (side 2)Sort each fraction: less than 12, equal to 12, or more than 12. 110, 212, 30100, 48, 36, 712, 70100, 68, 75, 33Less: 1/10, 2/12, 30/100. Equal: 4/8, 3/6. More: 7/12, 70/100, 6/8, 7/5, 3/3
On your own 1 (side 2)Write >, = or <: 35100 __ 45.<: 35/100 < 1/2 (1/2 is 50/100) and 1/2 < 4/5 (half of 5 is two and a half)
the ticket question(a) Sam drank 13 of a small bottle of water. Tia drank 14 of a large bottle. Tia drank more water. Does that show 14 > 13? Explain in one sentence. (b) Write >, = or <: 38 __ 23. Show how you know.(a) No: the bottles are different wholes, so Tia's 1/4 can be more water than Sam's 1/3. A "no" that only says 1/4 < 1/3 is true but does not say why Tia drank more: not a tick; (b) <: 3/8 < 1/2 (1/2 is 4/8) and 2/3 > 1/2 (half of 3 is one and a half)
