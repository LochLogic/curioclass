Day 13: Cut each part, multiply both numbers

At home

GoalI can say what cutting each part into n does to both numbers, and write it as multiplying
Time33 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at side 1. No ruler: the edge of a book.

Skip. The warm-up, On your own 5 and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Read Example 1 (side 1) together; the child draws the cuts and fills the frame. Then Example 2 (side 1).

    Say. "What did the cut change, and what did it not change?" Expect: more parts, each smaller; the point stayed.

  • 10 min. Cuts. On your own 3 (side 1), then 4 (side 2): the cuts, both names, the rule.

    Say. "Count the parts from 0 to the point, then in one whole." Expect: 10 and 6, so 10/6.

  • 8 min. Fix two. Fix it 6 (side 2), then 7.

    Say. "We multiplied both numbers by 2. Is 4/10 twice as much as 2/5?" Expect: no, it is the same point.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)The line from 0 to 1 is marked in fifths, with a point at 25. Cut each fifth into 2 equal parts. What is the new name?2/5 = (2 × 2)/(2 × 5) = 4/10
Example 2 (side 1)The line from 0 to 2 is marked in halves, with a point at 32. Cut each half into 3 equal parts. What is the new name?3/2 = (3 × 3)/(3 × 2) = 9/6
On your own 3 (side 1)Two lines from 0 to 1 are marked in halves, with a point at 12. Cut each half into 3 on the first line. Cut each half into 5 on the second. Write each rule.1/2 = (3 × 1)/(3 × 2) = 3/6; 1/2 = (5 × 1)/(5 × 2) = 5/10
On your own 4 (side 2)The line from 0 to 2 is marked in thirds, with a point at 53. Cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the rule.5/3 = (2 × 5)/(2 × 3) = 10/6
Fix it 6 (side 2)Ria cut each fifth into 2 equal parts. She wrote 25 = 210. Her line shows the point at 410.2/5 = (2 × 2)/(2 × 5) = 4/10. Ria changed the bottom number only
Fix it 7 (side 2)Sam says 410 is twice as much as 25, because both numbers were multiplied by 2.Not twice: 4/10 and 2/5 are at one point. There are twice as many parts, each half as big
the ticket questionA line from 0 to 2 is marked in fourths, with a point at 74. "Each fourth is cut into 3 equal parts. Finish: 74 = (__ × 7)/(__ × 4) = __/__. What does the number in the blanks tell you about the cut?"7/4 = (3 × 7)/(3 × 4) = 21/12; the 3 says each fourth was cut into 3
