Section 3.2: Why multiplying top and bottom works, in both directions (days 13 to 16)

The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NF.A.1: "Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to a fraction (n × a)/(n × b) by using visual fraction models, with attention to how the number and size of the parts differ even though the two fractions themselves are the same size. Use this principle to recognize and generate equivalent fractions." Section 3.1 did the visual half. This section writes the rule, ties each number in it to a cut, and then uses it with no drawing.

Each number in the rule is a count. 2/5 = (2 × 2)/(2 × 5) = 4/10. The 2 under the line says each fifth was cut into 2, so the whole has 2 × 5 = 10 parts. The 2 on top says the same cut runs through the shaded piece, so it has 2 × 2 = 4 parts. The parts are tenths now, half the size of fifths. More parts, each smaller, the same point. On day 13 the rule is written under a printed line, with the old name above the line and the new one below, and students point to the cut each number counts.

Both directions. Read backwards, the rule groups parts instead of cutting them. On a line in twelfths, put the twelfths in groups of 4: each group is a third, and 8/12 = 2/3. Both numbers are divided by the same number. Day 14 teaches this, because without it a student cannot see that two fractions such as 6/4 and 9/6 are equal: neither comes from the other by one whole-number multiplier, but both are 3/2. Going through a third name, with smaller numbers or larger, is how such pairs are settled (day 16). Some fractions, such as 3/8, have no name with smaller numbers; the word "simplest form" is not used.

Why we do not say "multiply by 2/2". It is true, but it needs a fraction times a fraction, which is grade 5 (5.NF.B.4). In grade 4 the reason is the cut.

The amount does not grow. Multiplying both numbers by 2 does not make the fraction twice as big: 4/10 is not twice 2/5 (error F). Marking both on one line, one point, ends the question. Watch for "times 2" said without "each part".

Hundredths. Hundredths enter on day 14, grouped in tens; day 15 shows the cut: each tenth cut into 10, so 3/10 = 30/100. Tenths and hundredths come back in unit 7 (decimals), where this move is the whole reason 0.3 = 0.30.

Limits. Denominators from the grade's list, with hundredths from day 14. No simplest form. Never "multiplying by 1".

The line and the rule

  • Day 13. A printed line, cut by the student, with the equation written under it. Cuts: 2/5 = 4/10; 1/2 = 3/6 and 5/10; 5/3 = 10/6.
  • Day 14. Strips, then one printed line, with parts grouped and circled: 8/12 to 4/6 to 2/3 (and 8/12 to 2/3 in one step, groups of 4); 50/100 to 5/10 to 1/2; 4/10 to 2/5; 10/12 to 5/6 on a line.
  • Day 15. Each tenth cut into 10 on the board line, so 3/10 = 30/100; then no line is given. Missing-number sets, both ways, marked at once, with a count of right first tries and no clock; a sketch may check.
  • Day 16. No line except to explain. Sorting pairs as equal or not, four of them needing a third name.

Each day prints its own lines. With no printer, the number lines sheet from section 3.1 (u03-1/number-lines.md) stands in. The written form all section: 2/5 = (2 × 2)/(2 × 5) = 4/10.

Why this model

The rule is quick, and that is the danger: a student can use it with no idea why it works, and then cannot see that 4/10 is not twice 2/5 or that 6/4 and 9/6 are equal. Writing the rule under the line the student has just cut keeps every number tied to a count of parts. Then the line is withdrawn on day 15, so the rule has to stand on its own, with a sketch always allowed. Days 15 and 16 need many cases with quick feedback: short sets on paper, marked at once against the display.

The errors to expect, in plain words

What students do What it shows What to do when many do it
A Change one number only: "2/5 = 2/10" They multiply the bottom and forget the shaded parts were cut too Lay the cut line beside it: the point is at 4/10; count the shaded parts
B Add or take away the same number on top and bottom: "2/5 = 4/7", "4/8 = 2/6" They take the pattern for adding or taking away Mark both on one line: adding moves the point, cutting does not
D Think bigger numbers make a bigger fraction: "50/100 is far more than 1/2" They read the numerals as whole numbers One tenth cut into 10 on the line: more parts, each smaller
E Say two fractions are unequal when neither comes from the other by one multiplier: Lena's "6/4 and 9/6 cannot be equal" They look for one multiplier and do not go through a third name Give each fraction a name with smaller numbers, then compare the names; if they differ, put them in the same parts
F Think multiplying both numbers by n makes the fraction n times as big: "4/10 is twice 2/5" They read the rule as multiplying the amount, not as cutting the parts Mark both on one line: one point
K Take the line from 0 to 2 as the whole: "5/4 = 10/16" The whole is not yet 0 to 1 Cover the line past 1 and count one whole

The day each wrong example is shown: A and F on day 13, and B there too if many missed day 12's exit (Tam's "2/5 = 4/7"); K on day 13's lines past 1; B as taking away on day 14; D on day 15; E on day 16. Each day's teacher page names the wrong answer that signals each error on that day's exit. On a missing-number item, A shows as the given number kept (1 in 1/2 = /6) and B as the number added on.

Supplies for the section

  • Per student: the lines printed on each day's student page (days 13 and 14); a pencil; a ruler (days 13 and 14); a pen for fixing (days 15 and 16). The practice sets are on the day 15 and 16 student pages, their keys on the display. The fraction strips stay in the envelope, to check a claim.
  • Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board. There is no document camera: draw the line and each cut on the board, and have students write the new name before the cut is drawn.
  • A room with nothing: lines ruled by hand; the key read aloud.

Words

Multiply, divide, group, equal, rule, each written on a cut line at first use. Students say the cut in words before the numbers: "each fifth is cut into 2". Frame, to take or leave: "Each part was cut into , so I multiply both numbers by ." For day 14: "I put the parts in groups of , so I divide both numbers by ."

How the section is checked

Exits on days 13 to 16, on paper, written on the unit record sheet by error letter (courses/math-4/u03-1/ record.md). Day 16's written task (Mo's 5/3 = 10/6: what does each 2 mean on the line?) is judged with three look-fors on that day's key and handed back on day 17. End-of-unit assessment items 3.2a and 3.2b, on day 24, are this section's.
