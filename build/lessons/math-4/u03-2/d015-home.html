Day 15: Equal fractions with no drawing

At home

GoalI can make equal fractions with no drawing, hundredths too
Time38 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at the page. The key: read each answer from this view after every set.

Skip. The warm-up, sets 2 and 4, and the challenge.

  • 5 min. Example 1. Read Example 1 (side 1) on the line in this view; cut the first tenth into 10. The child fills the frame.
  • 4 min. Example 2. Example 2 (side 1), no drawing. The child fills the frame.

  • 7 min. Fix it. Fix it 5 (side 1), then 6.

    Say. "How many hundredths are in one tenth?" Expect: 10, so 1/10 = 10/100.

  • 12 min. Two sets. Set 1 (Missing numbers 1 (side 2), 2, 3), then check. Set 3 (Missing numbers 7 (side 2), 8, 9), then check.

    Say. "With no line, how do you find the missing number?" Expect: find what one number was multiplied by; do the same to the other.

  • 5 min. Names for 7/2. Names for one fraction (side 2).

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

Example 1 (side 1)Each tenth is cut into 10 equal parts. 310 = ____/100.3/10 = (10 × 3)/(10 × 10) = 30/100; 30
  1. The line from 0 to 1 is cut in tenths, with a point at 3/10.
  2. Cut each tenth into 10 equal parts. Now the whole has 10 × 10 = 100 parts. Each one is a hundredth.
  3. Each of the 3 tenths now holds 10 hundredths. 3 × 10 = 30.
  4. Write it: 3/10 = (10 × 3)/(10 × 10) = 30/100.
  5. The point did not move. There are more parts, and each part is smaller.
Example 2 (side 1)92 = 27/____.6: 27 = 3 × 9, so each half was cut into 3, and 3 × 2 = 6. 9/2 = 27/6
  1. The bottom is the blank, so look at the top numbers first: 9 became 27. (When the top is the blank, look at the bottom numbers first.)
  2. 27 = 3 × 9. So each half was cut into 3 equal parts.
  3. Cut the bottom the same way: 3 × 2 = 6.
  4. 9/2 = (3 × 9)/(3 × 2) = 27/6.
  5. Check: the same 3 multiplies both numbers.
