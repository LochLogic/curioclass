Day 15: Equal fractions with no drawing

Before class

Print what the header lists. Mark the E14 tickets and sort them: 4/3 or 20/10 (A), 1/9 (B), and the rest. Put the display page on the projector. Rule a long line from 0 to 1 on the board, cut into tenths, with room above it to write.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: four facts

Alone, top of side 1 (6 × 10 = ? [60], 10 × 10 = ? [100], 7 × 5 = ? [35], 9 × 6 = ? [54]). Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes. Hand back the E14 tickets.

If day 14's tickets showed many A (4/3, or 20/10): the facts are dropped. Write "2/5 = 2/10" on the board beside the cut line, where the point is at 4/10. Count the parts in the point aloud, then fix it together.

If day 14's tickets showed many B (1/9: 3 taken from both): the facts are dropped. Mark 4/12 and 1/9 on one line: taking away moves the point, grouping does not. Students who wrote 1/9 sit near you for the sets.

5 min. Teaching first: one tenth cut into 10

Whole class, at the board line. Students fill the Example 1 frame on side 1.

Example 1 (One tenth is cut into 10 equal parts. 3/10 = /100. [3/10 = (10 × 3)/(10 × 10) = 30/100; 30]).

  1. Draw a line from 0 to 1 and cut it into tenths. Mark 3/10.
  2. Cut each tenth into 10 equal parts. Now the whole has 10 × 10 = 100 parts. Each one is a hundredth.
  3. Each of the 3 tenths now holds 10 hundredths. 3 × 10 = 30.
  4. Write it: 3/10 = (10 × 3)/(10 × 10) = 30/100.
  5. The point did not move. There are more parts, and each part is smaller.

This is the last line given. From here a student may sketch one to check, but the work is written.

4 min. Example 2: no drawing

Whole class. Students fill the Example 2 frame.

Example 2 (No drawing. 9/2 = 27/. [6: 27 = 3 × 9, so each half was cut into 3, and 3 × 2 = 6. 9/2 = 27/6]).

  1. Look at the top numbers first: 9 became 27.
  2. 27 = 3 × 9. So each half was cut into 3 equal parts.
  3. Cut the bottom the same way: 3 × 2 = 6.
  4. 9/2 = (3 × 9)/(3 × 2) = 27/6.
  5. Check: the same 3 multiplies both numbers.

Leave on the board: "Find the number that multiplies. Do the same to the other number."

7 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

Side 1 (Kim says: "50/100 is far more than 1/2, because 50 and 100 are much bigger than 1 and 2." [Not more: 50/100 = 1/2. Kim read the numbers as whole numbers], Nia says: "I cut one tenth into 10 equal parts, so 1/10 = 1/100." [1/10 = 10/100. Nia changed the bottom number only]). Two minutes alone on each, then one minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class. The display shows each wrong working beside the right one.

Kim read 50 and 100 as whole numbers (error D). On the board line, cut one tenth into 10: more parts, each smaller. Five tenths hold 50 hundredths, and the point is at 1/2. Nia kept the top number (error A). Ask "how many hundredths are in the one tenth now?"

If many still say 50/100 is more: cut a second tenth into 10 and count to 20 hundredths, then 50.

24 min. Missing numbers: four sets of three

Alone, side 2. Each set is three blanks. After each set the display shows its key; students tick a right first try and fix a miss in pen. They count their ticks at the end. The count stays with them. No clock.

Set 1 cuts the parts (7/5 = /10 [14], 11/6 = /12 [22], 9/4 = 18/ [8]). Set 2 groups them (16/10 = /5 [8], 14/6 = /3 [7], 30/12 = 5/ [2]). Set 3 is tenths and hundredths (13/10 = /100 [130], 170/100 = /10 [17], 19/10 = 190/ [100]). Set 4 mixes all three (11/4 = /12 [33], 18/10 = 9/ [5], 230/100 = /10 [23]). About 6 minutes a set, checking included.

Check: Did you find the number that multiplies? Did you do the same to both numbers? Fix a miss in pen.

Watch for a blank that copies the given number (error A: 7 for 7/5, 13 for 13/10). Ask "how many parts is each one cut into?" Watch for the same number added or taken away (error B: 12 for 7/5, 11 for 16/10). Ask "what times 5 is 10?"

If many make A in set 1: write the multiplier above the equal sign for set 2. If many make B: mark 7/5 and 12/10 on the board line; the points are not the same.

6 min. Names for one fraction

Alone, side 2 (Names for 7/2: 7/2 = /4 = /6 = /8 = /10 = /12 = /100 [14; 21; 28; 35; 42; 350]), then the class reads the row aloud.

Ask. "What repeats in the names for 7/2?" Expect: "the bottom goes up 2 each time and the top goes up 7"; "each top is 7 times as many as the halves". Show the first, then the second. If no one sees 350: half of 100 is 50, and 7 × 50 = 350.

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "With no line, how do you find the missing number?" Expect: "find what one number was multiplied by, then multiply the other"; "if the numbers got smaller, divide both by the same number". Then: "What does that number mean on a line?" Expect: each part was cut into that many, or grouped by that many.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (No drawing. "Fill in each blank: 1/6 = /12. 9/10 = /100. 5/4 = 15/." [2; 90; 12]). No drawing given. Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E15.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Write each set on the board; students work in notebooks, one set at a time.
  • A room with nothing. Read each set's key aloud after the set.
  • No record sheet yet. Keep the E15 tickets in a pile by error letter until it exists.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4 and the second Ask. Never drop the fixes or the check after each set, and never give a line for the sets. Longer: the challenge (Write 11/5 in hundredths. Each fifth was cut into how many parts? [220/100; 20]), then the extension on the board (Find every name for 13/4 with a denominator from our list: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 100. [26/8, 39/12 and 325/100: the denominator must be 4 times a whole number]): the list is the stop, since only bottoms that are 4 times a whole number work.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: tenth, hundredth, equal, multiply, divide, group. Frames on the display: "Each part was cut into , so I multiply both numbers by ." "I grouped the parts by , so I divide both numbers by ." A student who wants a line may sketch one on the back; the answer is still written.

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 1, 9 or 4 (error A, one number kept): if many, day 16 opens with 1/10 = 1/100 fixed again before Lena. 7, 99 or 14 (error B, the same added to both): if many, day 16 marks 3/4 and 5/6 on one line first. A student with A or B in two sets today sits near you on day 16.
