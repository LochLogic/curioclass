Day 14: Group the parts

At home

GoalI can go the other way: group the parts and get a name with smaller numbers
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at the strips on side 1. No long strip: fold a paper strip in half, in half again, then in three, and shade 8 parts.

Skip. The warm-up, problem 2 and the challenge.

  • 10 min. Two examples. Example 1 (side 1), then 2. The child circles the groups on the strip and fills the frame.

    Say. "To make halves, how many tenths go in each group?" Expect: 5, because 10 tenths in 2 groups is 5 each.

  • 8 min. Fix it. Fix it 1 (side 1), then 2.

    Say. "Lu took 2 from both. Is 2/6 the same amount as 4/8?" Expect: no, it is less; group the eighths in twos to get 2/4.

  • 6 min. On your own. On your own 1 (side 2) and 3. The shaded part must end at the same point.

  • 6 min. Which ones? Which ones (side 2).

    Say. "Does 3/8 have a name with smaller numbers?" Expect: no; groups of 2 do not fit 3, and groups of 3 do not fit 8.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)A strip from 0 to 1 is cut in twelfths. 8 twelfths are shaded. Put the twelfths in groups of 2. Then put the sixths in groups of 2. Write each new name.8/12 = 4/6 = 2/3
Example 2 (side 1)A strip from 0 to 1 is cut in tenths. Each tenth is cut into 10 hundredths. 50 hundredths are shaded. Group the hundredths in tens. Then group the tenths to make halves. Write each name.50/100 = 5/10 = 1/2
Fix it 1 (side 1)Lu says 48 = 26. He took 2 from the top and 2 from the bottom.4/8 = 2/4 (or 1/2). Taking the same number from both moves the point
Fix it 2 (side 1)Max put the hundredths in groups of 10. He wrote 70100 = 7010.70/100 = 7/10: the 70 shaded hundredths make 7 groups, not 70
On your own 1 (side 2)The strip is cut in tenths. 4 tenths are shaded. Group the tenths in twos. Write the new name.4/10 = 2/5
On your own 3 (side 2)40 hundredths are shaded. Group the hundredths in tens. Then group the tenths to make fifths. Write each name.40/100 = 4/10 = 2/5
Which ones (side 2)Circle each fraction that has a name with smaller numbers. Write that name: 512, 612, 78, 10100.6/12 = 3/6, 2/4 or 1/2; 10/100 = 1/10. 5/12 and 7/8 have none
the ticket questionA line from 0 to 1 is marked in twelfths, with a point at 412. "(a) Put the twelfths in equal groups to make thirds. 412 = __/3. How many twelfths are in each group? (b) Write 20100 in tenths."(a) 4/12 = 1/3, groups of 4 twelfths; (b) 20/100 = 2/10
