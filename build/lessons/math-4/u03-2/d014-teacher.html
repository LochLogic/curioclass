Day 14: Group the parts

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Sort the marked E13 tickets: A (7/12), B (10/7), F ("3 times as big"), K (21/24). Fold a long strip in twelfths (in half, in half again, then in three) and shade 8. On the board, draw two lines from 0 to 1, one above the other: fifths on top, tenths below. Add sevenths only if many wrote 10/7, and a 0 to 2 line in fourths if any wrote 21/24. Display page on the projector.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: factors and multiples

Alone, then whole class: Find all the factor pairs of 8. [1 × 8, 2 × 4], Find all the factor pairs of 10. [1 × 10, 2 × 5], Is 9 a multiple of 3? Is 10 a multiple of 4? [Yes, 3 × 3 = 9; no]. Read the answers aloud; students tick their own. Say once: today a group size has to fit two numbers, so these facts come back.

7 min. Teaching first: twelfths in twos, then sixths in twos

Whole class. Hand back the E13 tickets as you start.

If day 13's tickets showed many A (7/12): before Example 1, put "2/5 = 2/10" beside the line cut in tenths, the point at 4/10. Count the shaded parts aloud: 4, not 2.

If many B (10/7): mark 2/5 on the fifths line and 4/7 on the sevenths line. Adding moved the point; cutting did not.

If many F ("3 times as big"): "4/10 is twice 2/5". Mark both on one line: one point.

A branch takes 2 minutes; take them from "On your own" (drop problem 3). Students who wrote 21/24: before Example 1, cover the 0 to 2 line past 1 and count one whole with them.

Example 1 (A strip from 0 to 1 is cut in twelfths. 8 twelfths are shaded. Put the twelfths in groups of 2. Then put the sixths in groups of 2. Write each new name. [8/12 = 4/6 = 2/3]). Hold up the strip. Students circle the groups on the strip on side 1 and fill the frame as you go.

  1. 8 of the 12 twelfths are shaded: 8/12.
  2. Circle the twelfths in groups of 2. The whole strip makes 6 groups, so each group is a sixth. The shaded part makes 4 groups: 8/12 = 4/6.
  3. Write it: 8/12 = (8 ÷ 2)/(12 ÷ 2) = 4/6. The 2 is the size of each group.
  4. Now circle the sixths in groups of 2. The whole makes 3 groups, so each group is a third. The shaded part makes 2 groups: 4/6 = (4 ÷ 2)/(6 ÷ 2) = 2/3.
  5. Each third is 2 sixths, so 4 twelfths. In one step: 8/12 = (8 ÷ 4)/(12 ÷ 4) = 2/3.
  6. Nothing was added or taken away. The shaded part still ends at the same point: fewer parts, each one bigger.

The number we divide by is the size of each group. It is the same on top and bottom, because the shaded parts and the whole are grouped the same way.

8 min. Example 2: hundredths in tens

Whole class. Hundredths are new: each tenth holds 10 hundredths, and day 15 shows the cut. Students circle on side 1 as you go.

Example 2 (A strip from 0 to 1 is cut in tenths. Each tenth is cut into 10 hundredths. 50 hundredths are shaded. Group the hundredths in tens. Then group the tenths to make halves. Write each name. [50/100 = 5/10 = 1/2]).

  1. 50 of the 100 hundredths are shaded: 50/100.
  2. Circle the hundredths in groups of 10. Each group of 10 hundredths is 1 tenth. The whole makes 10 groups and the shaded part makes 5: 50/100 = 5/10.
  3. Write it: 50/100 = (50 ÷ 10)/(100 ÷ 10) = 5/10.
  4. Now make halves. The whole must make 2 equal groups: 10 tenths in 2 groups is 5 in each. Circle the tenths in groups of 5. The shaded part makes 1 group: 5/10 = (5 ÷ 5)/(10 ÷ 5) = 1/2.
  5. 50/100, 5/10 and 1/2 are one point on the line. Only the size of the parts changed.

Stop at the halves step. Ask. "How did we know each group needs 5 tenths?" Expect: halves means the whole is 2 equal groups, and 10 tenths in 2 groups is 5 each. This is the step the exit needs: from the new name, find the group size.

Watch for 50/10: the groups in the whole were counted, the shaded ones were not (error A).

10 min. Fix it: Lu and Max

Side 1 (Lu says 4/8 = 2/6. He took 2 from the top and 2 from the bottom. [4/8 = 2/4 (or 1/2). Taking the same number from both moves the point], Max put the hundredths in groups of 10. He wrote 70/100 = 70/10. [70/100 = 7/10: the 70 shaded hundredths make 7 groups, not 70]). Two minutes alone, two minutes of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class: the display shows each wrong working beside the right one.

Lu took 2 from both (error B). Mark 4/8 and 2/6 on two strips, one above the other (the display shows them): taking away moved the point. Max kept the top number (error A): count the shaded groups aloud, "one ten, two tens ... seven tens". 70/10 is 7 wholes, far past 1.

If many still subtract: grouping never takes parts away, it only circles them. If many keep a number: count the shaded groups first, then the groups in the whole.

14 min. On your own, then a partner check

Side 2, problems 1 to 3 (The strip is cut in tenths, with 4/10 shaded. Group the tenths in twos. Write the new name. [4/10 = 2/5], The line from 0 to 1 is cut in twelfths. The point is at 10/12. Group the twelfths in twos. Write the new name. [10/12 = 5/6], The strip is cut in hundredths. 30/100 is shaded. Group the hundredths in tens. Write the new name. Can the tenths be put in equal groups again? [30/100 = 3/10; no, 3 and 10 share only 1]). Ten minutes alone: circle the groups, write the new name and the equation with ÷. Then four minutes in pairs.

Check: Does the shaded part end at the same point? Is every group the same size?

A mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer. Watch for 4/5 or 10/6 (top kept, A) and 2/8 or 8/10 (subtracted, B). If many miss problem 3, do its second question together: try 2, then 3.

6 min. Which ones can be grouped?

Pairs, then whole class. Side 2 (Circle each fraction that has a name with smaller numbers. Write that name: 5/12, 6/12, 7/8, 10/100. [6/12 = 3/6, 2/4 or 1/2; 10/100 = 1/10 (5/50 and 2/20 are also right). 5/12 and 7/8 have none]). Pairs list the factors of the top number (the warm-up's pairs) and try only those: a group size must fit the shaded parts, so it must be a factor of the top. Then check it fits the bottom.

Ask. "8/12 has a name with smaller numbers. Does 3/8? How can you tell?" Take "no, I tried" first, then the reason. Expect: no; groups of 2 do not fit 3, and groups of 3 do not fit 8; a group size must fit both numbers, and 3 and 8 share only 1. Link it to the warm-up: 3 has the factors 1 and 3; 3 does not fit 8.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "Yesterday we cut each part into equal parts. Today we grouped parts in twos. What happened to the two numbers each time?" Expect: cutting multiplies both by the same number; grouping divides both by the same number; the point never moved.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper (A line from 0 to 1 is marked in twelfths, with a point at 4/12. (a) 4/12 = /3. Each group has twelfths. (b) 20/100 = /10. [(a) 4/12 = 1/3, groups of 4 twelfths; (b) 20/100 = 2/10]). Read it aloud once if asked. Write results under E14 on the record sheet.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Draw each strip on the board; students rule strips of 12, 10 and 8 boxes and a line in twelfths on plain paper, and shade them before circling.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop problem 3 and the partner check (students check their own). Never drop the fixes or the halves step of Example 2. Longer: the challenge (Which fractions from 1/6 to 5/6 have a name with smaller numbers? Write each name. How do you know you found them all? [2/6 = 1/3, 3/6 = 1/2, 4/6 = 2/3; 1/6 and 5/6 have none. All five were checked]): the search ends when all five sixths are checked.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: group, equal groups, divide, equal, hundredth. Frames on the display: "I put the parts in groups of , so I divide both numbers by ." "To make , the whole makes groups of ."

Tomorrow

On the ticket, 4/3 or 20/10 (error A: the top number kept): day 15 opens with 20/100 = 20/10 and 4/12 = 4/3 on the board line. 10/90 (error B: 10 taken from both): day 15 opens with 4/8 and 2/6 on two strips. A ticket with 3 or no group size in (a): before day 15's set 2, "how many in each group?"
