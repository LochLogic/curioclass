Kim says: "50100 is far more than 12, because 50 and 100 are much bigger than 1 and 2."

Their working

50 is more than 1 and 100 is more than 2, so 50/100 must be more.

The right working

1/2 = 5/10. Cut each tenth into 10: the 5 tenths hold 5 × 10 = 50 hundredths. So 1/2 = 50/100. More parts, each smaller, the same point.

Nia says: "I cut one tenth into 10 equal parts, so 110 = 1100."

Their working

1/10 = 1/100. She kept the top number.

The right working

The one tenth now holds 10 hundredths. 1/10 = (10 × 1)/(10 × 10) = 10/100.
