Day 16: Equal or not?

At home

GoalI can tell if two fractions are equal, and say how I know
Time37 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: point at the page, and mark each set from the answers in this view.

Skip. The warm-up, set 3, the challenge and the count of ticks.

  • 5 min. Lena's claim. Read Lena's claim (side 1) together.

    Say. "What would you show Lena?" Expect: 6/4 is 3/2 and 9/6 is 3/2, so they are equal.

  • 8 min. The example. Read Example (side 1) together. The child checks each fraction aloud, then circles.

  • 12 min. Equal or not. Set 1, question 1 (side 1), question 2 and question 3. Mark these three, then do Set 2, question 4 (side 2), question 5 and question 6.

    Say. When the child writes "not equal": "Can you group both to smaller numbers? If the names differ, can you put them in the same parts?" Expect: for 14/4 and 21/6, both are 7/2, so equal.

  • 7 min. Mo's 2s. The child says an answer to Mo's 2s (side 2), then writes it.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

Lena's claim (side 1)Lena says 64 and 96 cannot be equal. She says nothing times 4 is 6.Lena is not right: 6/4 = 3/2 and 9/6 = 3/2, so 6/4 = 9/6
Example (side 1)Circle every fraction equal to 2210: 115, 2412, 220100, 225.11/5 and 220/100
  1. Take each fraction in turn. Can I cut or group the tenths to make it?
  2. 11/5: group the tenths in twos. 22/10 = (22 ÷ 2)/(10 ÷ 2) = 11/5. Equal: circle it.
  3. 24/12: 2 was added to the top and the bottom. Adding is not cutting. Put both in fifths: 24/12 is 2, which is 10/5, and 10/5 is not 11/5. Not equal.
  4. 220/100: group the hundredths in tens. 220/100 = (220 ÷ 10)/(100 ÷ 10) = 22/10. Equal: circle it. Bigger numbers, the same amount.
  5. 22/5: only the bottom changed. Fifths are bigger than tenths, so 22/5 is far more. Not equal.
  6. Circled: 11/5 and 220/100. One has smaller numbers and one has bigger numbers.

Mo's 2s (side 2)Mo wrote 53 = (2 × 5)/(2 × 3) = 106. What does each 2 mean on the number line?Each third was cut into 2: twice as many parts in the whole and in the fraction, and the point did not move
Look-fors:
  1. each third was cut into 2
  2. twice as many parts in the whole and in the fraction
  3. the point did not move
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Each third is cut into 2 parts. So there are 2 times as many parts in 1 whole, and 2 times as many in 5/3. The point stays where it was.
  • partly: The 2 means you cut the thirds in half. (Look-for 1 only: ask what happens to the count and to the point.)
  • not: The 2 means 10/6 is 2 times as big. (Error F.)
