Section 4.4: Fraction word problems (days 36 to 38)

The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NF.B.3d: "Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions referring to the same whole and having like denominators, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem." Now with mixed numbers, both operations, the unknown in every place (the result, the change, the start), and comparing stories.

The action sets the equation, not a key word. "Kai's pole is 5/6 m. It is 2/6 m longer than Lu's." The word "longer" pulls toward adding, but Lu's pole is the shorter one: 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 m. A tape drawn first, a bar for each pole, makes that plain before any number is chosen.

Any true equation is right. A story with the start unknown can be written as a take-away with a box first, or as an adding with the box last. The relation between adding and subtracting lets a student write a missing-addend equation and solve it by subtracting. The answer is said with its unit and checked against the story.

One whole, or not. Every story's parts come from one whole except one planned story on day 37, where the parts are of two different sandwiches and cannot be joined.

Limits. Like denominators. One step on every check; two-step stories on day 37 are practice only, since the standard does not ask for two steps.

The tape

  • Day 36. The tape diagram, drawn by the student, with a box for the unknown, before any equation. Recipe Scaler's kitchen stories (light, practise with feedback, 15 min); paper stories carry other settings. The exit takes 7 minutes: a tape, an equation and a solve. The written task: a story for 2 5/8 − 1 6/8 = □.
  • Day 37. Tapes drawn roughly now. The short strip and a whole strip are folded once more, into eighths (1 min), for E's second wrong example. Recipe Scaler's "on your own" (light, check unaided, 10 min), read beside the day's exits; where the tool cannot run, the paper copy of its items, marked by the teacher.
  • Day 38. The end-of-unit assessment, 30 min; the respond half, 20 min; unit 5's readiness check, 10 min (courses/math-4/u05-1/readiness.md, written with unit 5's plan).

The tape is not withdrawn: the standard names a visual model.

Why this model

A tape shows the story's action: two bars for a compare story, one bar cut for a separate story, a box where the unknown sits. Once the box is placed, the equation is read off the picture, and "more" or "left" stop deciding the operation. Recipe Scaler (spec only) takes any true equation and never puts the answer on the card, so it fits as light practice; its stories are all in one kitchen.

The errors to expect, in plain words

What students do What it shows What to do when many do it
E Join parts of two different wholes: "Ana ate 2/8 of her sandwich and Ben ate 5/8 of his, so they ate 7/8 of a sandwich" A fraction read as an amount of stuff Lay the short strip and a whole strip together; ask "eighths of what?"
F Take the smaller from the larger in each place A one not broken into parts Break a whole strip; check by adding
H Add on a "how much more" or a "how much longer" The operation picked from a word Draw the tape first, box the unknown, then write the equation

Wrong examples: H on day 36 (Kai's and Lu's poles), E again on day 37.

The respond half (day 38)

Mark, 5 minutes: every item but 4.1c against the key, each wrong answer matched to its letter. Item 4.1c is read after class and handed back in day 39's first five minutes, unit 5's first day. Groups, 15 minutes: by error letter, A and G first, since unit 5 adds eighths on its first day. A student who wrote 5 15/12 on 4.3b joins group G, which sketches a line from 5 to 7 in the item's part size (nothing printed). A student who missed nothing takes the extension.

Supplies for the section

  • Per student: story sheets; blank bars for tapes where a student needs them; the envelope's short strip and a whole strip (day 37, and day 38's group E); a pencil.
  • Per class: the projector; the keys; day 37's paper copy of the "on your own" items.
  • A room with nothing: tapes drawn by hand; stories read aloud.

Words

Farther, left, in all, at first, longer than, each with a tape. Frame, to take or leave: "The whole is . The part I know is . The box is ."

How the unit is checked

Exits on days 36 and 37, on paper; day 36's written task, judged with look-fors and handed back on day 37; items 4.4a and 4.4b on the end-of-unit assessment, day 38. All of it goes on the unit's record sheet (u04-1/record.md).
