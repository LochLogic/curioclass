Day 4: Is it a multiple?

At home

GoalI can tell if a number is a multiple of another number, and say how factors and multiples go together
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: read the examples from side 1, and draw the jumps on paper. The 9 by 9 table: print one for when the child asks.

Skip. The facts check, orders 3 and 4, side 2 problems 3 and 4, and the challenge.

  • 8 min. Two examples. Example 1 (side 1), then 2; the child fills the frames. A multiple is what multiplying makes.

    Say. "21 is a multiple of 7. What else does 3 × 7 = 21 tell you?" Expect: 21 is a multiple of 3; 3 and 7 are factors of 21.

  • 8 min. Egg cartons. Fill Line 1 (side 1), then 2: yes or no first, then the jumps, one long jump for each ten cartons.

  • 8 min. Fence it. Is it a multiple 1 (side 2) and 2, alone: the two products it sits between, then yes or no.

    Say. "How do you tell if a big number is a multiple of 6?" Expect: start at 6 × 10 and count on; not the last digit.

  • 6 min. Fix three. Fix it 5 (side 2), 6 and 7.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers ticket question 1 and question 2 alone.

The questions

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)3 × 7 = 21. Say it three ways.3 rows of 7 make 21; 21 is a multiple of 7 and of 3; 3 and 7 are factors of 21 (either order)
  1. Sketch the rows: 3 rows of 7 squares, none left over.
  2. Read it as rows: 3 rows of 7 make 21.
  3. Read it as a multiple: count by 7s, 7, 14, 21. The count lands on 21, so 21 is a multiple of 7.
  4. Turn the sketch: 7 rows of 3. So 21 is a multiple of 3 too.
  5. Read it as factors: 3 and 7 multiply to make 21, so 3 and 7 are factors of 21.
  6. One equation, two words. The numbers multiplied are factors. The product is a multiple of each one.
Example 2 (side 1)Is 96 a multiple of 6?Yes: 6 × 16 = 96 (6 × 10 = 60, and 36 more is 6 × 6). 96 is a multiple of 6, and 6 is a factor of 96. It is a multiple of 3 too: 3 × 30 = 90 and 3 × 2 = 6, so 3 × 32 = 96
  1. Start from ten times: 6 × 10 = 60. That is one long jump to 60.
  2. 96 is 36 more than 60. 36 is 6 × 6: 6 more jumps of 6.
  3. 10 jumps and 6 jumps make 16 jumps: 6 × 16 = 96.
  4. The jumps land on 96 with none left over. So yes, 96 is a multiple of 6.
  5. Say it both ways: 96 is a multiple of 6, and 6 is a factor of 96.
  6. Check: 60 + 36 = 96.
  7. Is 96 a multiple of 3 too? One ten of 3s is only 30, and 66 is left: past the table. Take more tens: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 × 30 = 90.
  8. 90 and 6 more is 3 × 2. So 3 × 32 = 96: 96 is a multiple of 3 and of 6.

Fix it 5 (side 2)Kai says: "The multiples of 6 are 1, 2, 3 and 6."Those are the factors of 6. The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and on (6 × 1, 6 × 2, 6 × 3, 6 × 4)

Fix it 7 (side 2)Ravi says: "68 is not a multiple of 4. The 4s stop at 40."It is a multiple: 4 × 17 = 68 (4 × 10 = 40 and 4 × 7 = 28, and 40 + 28 = 68)
