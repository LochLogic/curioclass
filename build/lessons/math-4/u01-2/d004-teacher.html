Day 4: Is it a multiple?

Before class

Print everything on the print list. Mark the E3 slips and day 3's written tasks and sort them: B (no 7 × 14), E ("98 is even"), no turn.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 2

Alone, on the facts check sheet: the 36 quotients, no clock shown. Collect at 5 minutes; an item not reached is not wrong. Mark after class.

3 min. E3 and the written task back

Whole class. Hand back the E3 slips and day 3's written tasks. From now on, a 9 by 9 table is on hand for any student who asks. Not during the facts check.

If day 3's slips showed many B (no 7 × 14): test 7 on 98 from ten times. 7 × 10 = 70; 28 more is 7 × 4.

If many E ("98 is even"): even tells you about 2, not 6. Today's Fix it has the same kind of mistake (Ivy).

If many written tasks named no turn (error C or A): read one that does, "at 8 × 8 the pairs turn round".

Otherwise one student reads a task that names the turn. Students with B sit near you for Teaching first.

10 min. Teaching first: one fact, three ways; then past ten times

Whole class; students fill the side 1 frames as you go.

Example 1 (3 × 7 = 21. Say it three ways. [3 rows of 7 make 21; 21 is a multiple of 7 and of 3; 3 and 7 are factors of 21 (either order)]).

  1. Sketch the rows: 3 rows of 7 squares, none left over.
  2. Read it as rows: 3 rows of 7 make 21.
  3. Read it as a multiple: count by 7s, 7, 14, 21. The count lands on 21, so 21 is a multiple of 7.
  4. Turn the sketch: 7 rows of 3. So 21 is a multiple of 3 too.
  5. Read it as factors: 3 and 7 multiply to make 21, so 3 and 7 are factors of 21.
  6. One equation, two words. The numbers multiplied are factors. The product is a multiple of each one.

Say "multiple" beside "multiply": a multiple is what multiplying makes.

Example 2 (Is 96 a multiple of 6? [Yes: 6 × 16 = 96 (6 × 10 = 60, and 36 more is 6 × 6). 96 is a multiple of 6, and 6 is a factor of 96. It is a multiple of 3 too: 3 × 30 = 90 and 3 × 2 = 6, so 3 × 32 = 96]).

  1. Start from ten times: 6 × 10 = 60. That is one long jump to 60.
  2. 96 is 36 more than 60. 36 is 6 × 6: 6 more jumps of 6.
  3. 10 jumps and 6 jumps make 16 jumps: 6 × 16 = 96.
  4. The jumps land on 96 with none left over. So yes, 96 is a multiple of 6.
  5. Say it both ways: 96 is a multiple of 6, and 6 is a factor of 96.
  6. Check: 60 + 36 = 96.
  7. Is 96 a multiple of 3 too? One ten of 3s is only 30, and 66 is left: past the table. Take more tens: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 × 30 = 90.
  8. 90 and 6 more is 3 × 2. So 3 × 32 = 96: 96 is a multiple of 3 and of 6.

The display has no number line yet: draw the jumps on the board as the steps come. The point: the 6s do not stop at 60, and when one ten is not enough, take more tens.

10 min. Egg Carton Factory, Fill Line: four orders on paper

When Fill Line exists, students write yes or no first, then run each order.

Alone, side 1 (84 eggs go in cartons of 7. Is every carton full? [Yes: 12 full cartons. 7 × 12 = 84, so 84 is a multiple of 7 and 7 is a factor of 84], 58 eggs go in cartons of 4. Is every carton full? [No: 4 × 14 = 56 and 4 × 15 = 60. The last carton has only 2 eggs], 75 eggs go in cartons of 5. Is every carton full? [Yes: 15 full cartons. 5 × 15 = 75, so 75 is a multiple of 5 and 5 is a factor of 75], 79 eggs go in cartons of 3. Is every carton full? [No: 3 × 26 = 78 and 3 × 27 = 81. The last carton has only 1 egg]). Yes or no first, before any drawing. Then the jumps on a number line: one long jump for each ten cartons, then one jump a carton. Landing on the order means yes; passing it means the last carton is short.

Leave the display on Example 2 until every order has its yes or no written; then show each order's answer screen as one student says where the last jump landed. Watch for "no" on 84 (the 7s stop at 70), "yes" on 58 (it is even) and "yes" on 79 (the 1 left ignored): all error E.

15 min. Is it a multiple? Fence it

Alone, side 2 (Is 86 a multiple of 6? [No: 6 × 14 = 84 and 6 × 15 = 90, and 86 is between them], Is 88 a multiple of 4? [Yes: 4 × 22 = 88 (4 × 20 is 80, and 2 more 4s is 8)], Is 69 a multiple of 3? [Yes: 3 × 23 = 69 (3 × 20 is 60, and 3 more 3s is 9)], Is 62 a multiple of 8? [No: 8 × 7 = 56 and 8 × 8 = 64, and 62 is between them]). Two products of the number tested, one at or below, one at or above, from ten times past the tables. Then yes or no. Walk with one question: "Which two products is it between?" Watch for:

  • "Yes" on 86 (ends in 6) or 62 (even): error E. Ask for the products first.
  • "No" on 88 or 69 (the table stopped short, or "it is odd"): error E. Ask "What is 4 × 20?" (for 69: "What is 3 × 20?").

At 11 minutes, partners check against the display; redo a mismatch together. If many miss 86, fence it on the board.

Check: Is your number on a product, or between two? On a product means yes.

8 min. Fix it: Kai, Ivy and Ravi

Side 2 (Kai says: "The multiples of 6 are 1, 2, 3 and 6." [Those are the factors of 6. The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and on (6 × 1, 6 × 2, 6 × 3, 6 × 4)], Ivy says: "46 is a multiple of 6, because it ends in 6." [Not a multiple: 6 × 7 = 42 and 6 × 8 = 48, and 46 is between them], Ravi says: "68 is not a multiple of 4. The 4s stop at 40." [It is a multiple: 4 × 17 = 68 (4 × 10 = 40 and 4 × 7 = 28, and 40 + 28 = 68)]). A minute alone on each, then the class. Show each screen after the minute alone: it holds the wrong working beside the right one.

Kai listed factors: they make 6; multiples are made from 6. Ivy went by the last digit. That works for 2, 5 and 10 because every ten is a multiple of them; ten is not a multiple of 4 or 6. Ravi stopped at the table.

If many still swap the words (error D): sort 1, 3, 9, 18, 27 and 54 into "factor of 9" and "multiple of 9", each with its equation. 9 goes in both: 1 × 9 = 9 makes 9 a factor and a multiple of 9.

4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "21 is a multiple of 7. What else does 3 × 7 = 21 tell you?" Expect: "21 is a multiple of 3"; "3 and 7 are factors of 21"; "3 rows of 7 make 21". One fact, read both ways.

Ask. "How do you tell if a big number is a multiple of 6?" Expect: from 6 × 10, count on, or fence it. Not the last digit.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper (5 × 11 = 55. Write one sentence about 55 with the word multiple, and one with the word factor. [55 is a multiple of 5 (or of 11, or 1, or 55); 5 (or 11, 1 or 55) is a factor of 55. Any true pair of sentences is right], Is 92 a multiple of 4? Show how you know. [Yes: 4 × 23 = 92 (4 × 20 = 80 and 4 × 3 = 12)]). Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; record under E4 on the record sheet.

Other rooms

  • No devices, or the tool not built. The paper path above is the whole job.
  • No printer. Orders and questions on the board; number lines in notebooks.
  • One projector. With Fill Line, you run it; the class writes yes or no first.
  • One child at home. 30 beans: rows of 4 with none left? Rows of 6? Both sentences for each yes.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: the first three fence questions; students check their own. Never drop yes or no first, the fixes or the two sentences. Longer: the challenge (Which numbers between 70 and 80 are multiples of 3? [72, 75 and 78: 3 × 24 = 72, 3 × 25 = 75, 3 × 26 = 78]).

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: multiple, factor, product, multiply. Students may say the sentences in a home language first. Frames on the display: " is a multiple of , because × = ." " is a factor of ."

Tomorrow

On the slip, "55 is a factor of 5", or the blanks swapped (error D): day 5's warm-up says one equation both ways, then two more like it. On 92, "no, the 4s stop at 40", or "yes, it is even" with no product (error E): day 5's warm-up fences one number past the tables. If neither, day 5's warm-up is one factor-pair list from unit 1.1. Mark the facts check tonight, under F2: students under 32 right on either part copy each missed fact, with its answer, on the back of their facts check sheet in day 5's warm-up, and keep a 9 by 9 table from then on.
