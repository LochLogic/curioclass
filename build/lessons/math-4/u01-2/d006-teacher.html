Creditours

Before class

Print everything on the print list. Open the display; show the answers only at the marking. Write the three group tasks on a covered part of the board, and, until unit 2's readiness sheet exists, its three items. Take down any chart that lists primes.

The day

30 min. End-of-unit assessment

Keep the day 5 tickets and written tasks until pencils go down. Alone, unaided: no tiles, no tool, no chart. A student below the facts mark keeps the 9 by 9 table. Read each item aloud once. At 30 minutes, pencils down; not reached is not wrong.

20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

Mark, 5 minutes. Pencils away; hand back the day 5 tickets and written tasks; show the answers to 1 to 4. Students tick or circle each in pen. You judge 5 and 6 after class.

Groups, 15 minutes. Hands up sets the groups; a student in two joins the earlier one. Each group fixes its items in pen. Tasks are on the display.

  • A to C (missed item 1): search 30 in order, saying "yes, with " or "no" for 1 to 6, and stop at the turn. Cross out any turned pair. Test 3 and 4 on 42 and 56 from ten times. Then fix item 1.
  • D and E (missed item 2 or 3): sort 1, 3, 9, 18, 27 and 54 into "factor of 9" and "multiple of 9", each said with its equation. 9 goes in both: 1 × 9 = 9 says it. Fence 58 between two products of 4, then of 6, starting from ten times. Then fix items 2 and 3.
  • F and G (missed item 4, or the day 5 ticket or written task): search 39, 57 and 97 in order to the turn. Call a number prime only when the search finds nothing. Anyone who called 1 prime or 2 composite counts the factors of 1, 2, 3 and 4. Then fix item 4 and the ticket.

Students who missed nothing do On a 1 to 100 chart, cross out 1. Cross out the multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7, but not those four. How many numbers are left? Why were the multiples of 4, 6, 8 and 9 not needed? [25 numbers are left, and every one is prime. A multiple of 4 or 8 is already crossed out as a multiple of 2; a multiple of 6 or 9, as a multiple of 3] on the 1 to 100 chart. Collect the checks.

10 min. Unit 2's readiness check

Alone, on its sheet; until the sheet exists, students write answers only: item 1, four numbers; item 3, the even numbers. Read each item aloud once.

  1. "Write the next four numbers: 6, 12, 18, , , , ."
  2. "4 × 3 = 12, 4 × 5 = 20, 4 × 7 = 28. Is 4 times a number always even? Write why, or draw."
  3. "Circle the even numbers: 3, 8, 11, 14, 15, 17, 20."

Collect them.

Other rooms

  • No projector. Answers on the board too, covered.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: groups ten minutes, never the check, the marking or the readiness check.

Words and supports

The defaults hold, as written.

Tomorrow

After class, about 40 minutes: record 1 to 4 from the pencil first try, judge 5 and 6 from the pencil first try, each look-for alone, on unit 1's record sheet. On item 4, a prime with nothing tested is x ("no search"). Mark the readiness items tonight: on unit 2's record sheet when it exists, until then on the back of unit 1's.

Item 5:

  1. says Ana is not right
  2. gives a factor pair of 99 other than 1 × 99
  3. says a prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself

Item 6:

  1. says Maya is right
  2. says 7 goes with 5, a pair she has (or that any number past 5 needs a partner she has already tried)
  3. says every pair after that repeats one she has

Day 7 opens with items 5 and 6, 5 minutes: hand them back, show the "meets" answers of both, and students fix their own in pen.
