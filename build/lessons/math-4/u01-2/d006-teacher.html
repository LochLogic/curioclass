Day 6: Show what I know

Creditours

Before class

Print everything on the print list. Open the display; show the answers only at the marking. Until stretch 2's readiness sheet exists, write its three items on a covered part of the board. Take down any chart that lists primes.

The day

30 min. End check

Keep the day 5 slips and written tasks until pencils go down. Alone, unaided: no tiles, no tool, no chart. A student below the facts mark keeps the 9 by 9 table. Read each item aloud once. At 30 minutes, pencils down; not reached is not wrong.

20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

Mark, 5 minutes. Pencils away; hand back the day 5 slips and written tasks; show the answers to 1 to 4. Students tick or circle each in pen. You judge 5 and 6 after class.

Groups, 15 minutes. Hands up sets the groups; a student in two joins the earlier one. Each group fixes its items in pen. Tasks are on the display.

  • A to C (missed item 1): search 30 in order, saying "yes, with " or "no" for 1 to 6, and stop at the turn. Cross out any turned pair. Test 3 and 4 on 42 and 56 from ten times. Then fix item 1.
  • D and E (missed item 2 or 3): sort 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12, 24, 36, 60 and 72 into "factor of 12" and "multiple of 12", each said with its equation. Fence 58 between two products of 4, then of 6, starting from ten times. Then fix items 2 and 3.
  • F and G (missed item 4, or the day 5 slip or written task): test 2, 3, 5 and 7 in order on 39 and 57. Call a number prime only when the search finds nothing. Write the factors of 1, 2, 3 and 4, and count them. Then fix item 4 and the slip.

Students who missed nothing do On a 1 to 100 chart, cross out 1. Cross out the multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7, but not those four. How many numbers are left? Why were the multiples of 4, 6, 8 and 9 not needed? [25 numbers are left, and every one is prime. A multiple of 4 or 8 is already crossed out as a multiple of 2; a multiple of 6 or 9, as a multiple of 3] on grid paper, ruling a 10 by 10 square and numbering it first. It waits until now because it gives away every prime. Collect the checks.

10 min. Stretch 2's readiness check

Alone, on its sheet; until the sheet exists, on lined paper from the board. Read each item aloud once.

  1. "Write the next four numbers: 6, 12, 18, , , , ."
  2. "4 × 3 = 12, 4 × 5 = 20, 4 × 7 = 28. Is 4 times a number always even? Write why, or draw."
  3. "Circle the even numbers: 3, 8, 11, 14, 15, 17, 20."

Collect them.

Other rooms

  • No projector. Answers and group tasks on a covered part of the board.
  • One child at home. The defaults, then the three readiness items aloud.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: groups ten minutes, never the check, the marking or the readiness check.

Words and supports

The defaults hold; items 5 and 6 may be answered aloud.

Tomorrow

After class, about 40 minutes: record 1 to 4 from the pencil first try, judge 5 and 6 from the pencil first try, each look-for alone, on stretch 1's record sheet. Mark the three readiness items tonight; they go on stretch 2's record sheet when it exists, and until then on the back of stretch 1's.

Item 5:

  1. says Ana is not right
  2. gives a factor pair of 99 other than 1 × 99
  3. says a prime has only 1 and itself as factors

Item 6:

  1. says Maya is right
  2. says 7 × 5 is 5 × 7 turned
  3. says every pair after that repeats one she has

Day 7 opens with items 5 and 6, 5 minutes: hand them back, show the "meets" answers of both, and students fix their own in pen.
