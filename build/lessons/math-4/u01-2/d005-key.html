Each answer is worked out in full. The last column names the wrong answers to expect and the error each one shows.

Any equal form of an equation is right: the two factors in either order, either side of the equals sign, any letter, or the matching division (n × 9 = 36, 9 × n = 36, 36 = 9 × n and 36 ÷ 9 = n all say the same).

practices01-W54.OA.B.4 (d), MP.3Ben says 91 is prime. Write to Ben. Is he right? Prove it.Ben is not right: 7 × 13 = 91, so 91 is composite
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. says 91 is composite
  2. gives 7 × 13
  3. says a prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Ben, you are not right. 7 × 13 = 91, so 91 is composite. A prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself.
  • partly: Ben is wrong, 7 × 13 = 91. (Look-fors 1 and 2; ask what a prime has.)
  • not: Ben is right, 91 is odd and it is not in the tables. (Error F.)

challenges01-C5-14.OA.B.4 (d), MP.3Can a multiple of 7 be prime? Explain.Only 7 itself, since 1 × 7 = 7. Every other multiple, such as 2 × 7 = 14, has a pair other than 1 × itself, so it is composite
Look-fors, each ticked alone:
  1. says only 7 itself
  2. gives another multiple with its pair, such as 2 × 7 = 14
  3. says that pair proves the multiple composite
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: Only 7. 7 is 1 × 7. Any other multiple, like 14 = 2 × 7, has a pair, so it is composite.
  • partly: No. 14 = 2 × 7, so it is composite. (Misses 7 itself: ask if 7 is a multiple of 7.)
  • not: Yes, 21 is odd, so it is prime. (Error F.)
"yes", with an odd multiple such as 21: error F, odd, or "not in my tables", means prime
