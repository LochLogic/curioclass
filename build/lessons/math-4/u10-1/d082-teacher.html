Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets, about 6 strip sheets and the key. Put the display page on the projector. Have day 81's exit tickets sorted: one sentence only, roles swapped (error C), 'more than' or strips with no copies (error A), and the rest.

The day

5 min. Warm-up

Alone, on the board: 702 − 456 = ? [246], 6 × 6 = ? [36], 7 × 6 = ? [42], 8 × 7 = ? [56], 8 × 8 = ? [64], 6 × 30 = ? [180], 8 × 40 = ? [320]. Answers on the display at 4 minutes.

3 min. Day 81's exit back

Whole class. Hand back day 81's tickets. If day 81's tickets showed many C or many with one sentence only: draw a 7-strip with four copies under it and read 28 = 4 × 7 both ways. Point to the longer strip: "Which amount is bigger? It stands alone on one side." Then show C's wrong example, 3 = 4 × 12, beside 12 = 4 × 3. Tickets with one copy too many (error D): count the copies aloud with those students. Otherwise one student reads both sentences for their strip, and the class names the bigger number each time.

5 min. Teaching first: three sentences into equations

Whole class. Students write each equation on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on its own screen. Draw a strip only if a sentence is hard.

Sentence 1 (35 is 7 times as many as 5. [35 = 7 × 5, or 7 × 5 = 35 (the two factors in either order)]).

  1. Read it aloud: 35 is 7 times as many as 5.
  2. Which amount is bigger? 35. The bigger amount stands alone on one side.
  3. "Is" becomes the equals sign, with 35 alone on its side.
  4. "7 times as many as 5" becomes 7 × 5.
  5. 35 = 7 × 5.
  6. Check: 7 × 5 is 35, so 35 is 7 copies of 5.

Sentence 2 (10 times as many as 8 is 80. [80 = 10 × 8, or 10 × 8 = 80 (the two factors in either order)]).

  1. Read it aloud: 10 times as many as 8 is 80. The sentence starts with the times.
  2. Which amount is bigger? 80.
  3. "10 times as many as 8" becomes 10 × 8.
  4. "Is 80" becomes = 80.
  5. 10 × 8 = 80. The bigger amount is still alone, on the right this time.
  6. Check: 10 copies of 8 make 80.

Sentence 3 (A whale is 48 feet long. It is 6 times as long as an 8-foot dolphin. [48 = 6 × 8, or 6 × 8 = 48 (the two factors in either order)]).

  1. Read it aloud. Two lengths: the whale and the dolphin.
  2. Which is longer? The whale, 48 feet.
  3. Strip picture, if it helps: one strip for the dolphin, 8 feet; 6 copies of it for the whale.
  4. "It is 6 times as long as the 8-foot dolphin" becomes 48 = 6 × 8.
  5. We do not multiply 48 by 6: the 48 is already the whale.
  6. Check: 6 × 8 is 48. The equation has no feet in it; the sentence tells what each number counts.

Say the rule once and leave it on the board: the bigger amount stands alone; the times and the smaller amount are multiplied. Either side of the equals sign, and either order of the two factors, is fine. If time runs short, take sentence 2 in one screen: read it, write 10 × 8 = 80, point to the 80.

7 min. Sentence set 1

Alone. Either reading of an equation is right; early finishers write the second one too: 45 is 5 times as many as 9. [45 = 5 × 9, or 5 × 9 = 45 (the two factors in either order)], 56 = 7 × 8 [56 is 7 times as many as 8, or 56 is 8 times as many as 7], A flagpole is 50 feet tall. It is 10 times as tall as a 5-foot fence. [50 = 10 × 5, or 10 × 5 = 50 (the two factors in either order)], 42 = 6 × 7 [42 is 6 times as many as 7, or 42 is 7 times as many as 6]. Go first to the students day 81 marked A; draw a strip with each on their first measured sentence.

About 4 minutes, then the check screen; read each answer from the key. Students tick a right first try and fix a wrong one in the second color, comparing only with their own last set; no counts aloud.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

Watch for 45 = 5 + 9 (error A); 9 = 5 × 45 (error C); and on a measured sentence, 50 × 10 (error B). If many miss one item, draw its strip on the board before set 2: one strip for the smaller amount, copies for the bigger.

7 min. Sentence set 2

Alone, the same way: 10 times as many as 7 is 70. [70 = 10 × 7, or 10 × 7 = 70 (the two factors in either order)], 72 = 8 × 9 [72 is 8 times as many as 9, or 72 is 9 times as many as 8], A bag weighs 36 pounds. That is 4 times as much as a 9-pound box. [36 = 4 × 9, or 4 × 9 = 36 (the two factors in either order)], 60 = 3 × 20 [60 is 3 times as many as 20, or 60 is 20 times as many as 3]. Its first sentence starts with the times, like sentence 2.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

7 min. Sentence set 3

Alone: 120 = 10 × 12 [120 is 10 times as many as 12, or 120 is 12 times as many as 10], 9 times as many as 2 is 18. [18 = 9 × 2, or 9 × 2 = 18 (the two factors in either order)], 200 = 10 × 20 [200 is 10 times as many as 20, or 200 is 20 times as many as 10], A pool is 90 feet long. It is 3 times as long as a 30-foot boat. [90 = 3 × 30, or 3 × 30 = 90 (the two factors in either order)]. Two equations are ten times a two-digit number. Walk to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

7 min. Sentence set 4

Alone: A train is 400 feet long. It is 8 times as long as a 50-foot bus. [400 = 8 × 50, or 8 × 50 = 400 (the two factors in either order)], 280 = 4 × 70 [280 is 4 times as many as 70, or 280 is 70 times as many as 4], A farm has 240 hens. That is 6 times as many as its 40 cows. [240 = 6 × 40, or 6 × 40 = 240 (the two factors in either order)], 300 = 5 × 60 [300 is 5 times as many as 60, or 300 is 60 times as many as 5]. The section's target (ours, untested) is at least 7 of the 8 in sets 3 and 4, right on the first try. No time target.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

If many are still under 3 of 4 in set 4, the strip sheet comes out for the written task.

9 min. Written task: is Jo right?

Alone, side 2 (Jo says 15 is 5 times as many as 3. Max says 15 is 3 times as many as 5. Who is right? Draw a picture and write an equation to show it. [Both are right: five 3-strips, or three 5-strips, make 15; 15 = 5 × 3 shows both]). Read after class with the tickets. Look-fors, each ticked alone:

  1. a strip picture of equal copies (five 3s or three 5s)
  2. one equation, 15 = 5 × 3 or 15 = 3 × 5
  3. says both are right, and why

Watch for a 3-strip and a 5-strip side by side with 3 + 5 = 8 (error A): ask "how many copies of 3 are there?"

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "A ten is how many times as much as a one?" Expect: 10 times; 10 = 10 × 1. Then tie it to sentence 2: 80 is 10 times as many as 8, and each ten is 10 times a one.

Ask. "In every equation today, where did the bigger number go?" Expect: alone on one side of the equals sign.

5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, no strip sheet, read aloud once (Write a multiplication equation: 14 is 2 times as many as 7. [14 = 2 × 7, or 2 × 7 = 14 (the two factors in either order)], Write a multiplication equation: 10 times as many as 6 is 60. [10 × 6 = 60, or 60 = 10 × 6 (the two factors in either order)], Kai's rope is 16 feet long. That is 2 times as long as Lu's 8-foot rope. Write a multiplication equation. [16 = 2 × 8, or 2 × 8 = 16 (the two factors in either order)]). Write the results on the record sheet under E82.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Write each set of four on the board as it starts; students write on lined paper and draw a line under each set for the count. The exit: read the three aloud; students write on the back of their lined paper.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4's last two items and the second Ask. Never drop the checks between sets, the written task or the exit. Longer: the challenge (Write every "times as many" sentence that 24 can show. [24 is 3 times as many as 8; 24 is 8 times as many as 3; 24 is 4 times as many as 6; 24 is 6 times as many as 4 (2 and 12 is extra)]), on lined paper; then three sentences of their own with ten times a two-digit number, swapped with a partner.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, times as long as, times as tall as, equation. Frames on the display: " is times as many as ." " = × ". The frame from day 80 stays on the wall over its strip picture.
