Day 82: Sentences into equations

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips, the strip sheets and the key. Put the display page on the projector. Have day 81's exit slips sorted: one sentence only, roles swapped (error C), and the rest.

The day

5 min. Warm-up

Alone, on the board: one difference with the standard algorithm, and six facts from the 6, 7 and 8 tables, two of them a one-digit number times a multiple of 10.

3 min. Day 81's exit back

Whole class. Hand back day 81's slips. If day 81's slips showed many C or many with one sentence only: draw the C example again, "12 is 4 times as many as 3" written as 3 = 4 × 12, beside 12 = 4 × 3. Point to the longer strip first: "Which amount is bigger? It stands alone on one side." Otherwise one student reads both sentences for their strip, and the class names the bigger number each time.

5 min. Teaching first: three sentences into equations

Whole class. Students write each equation on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on its own screen. Draw a strip only if a sentence is hard.

Sentence 1 (35 is 7 times as many as 5. [35 = 7 × 5, or 7 × 5 = 35]).

  1. Read it aloud: 35 is 7 times as many as 5.
  2. Which amount is bigger? 35. The bigger amount stands alone on one side.
  3. "Is" becomes the equals sign, with 35 alone on its side.
  4. "7 times as many as 5" becomes 7 × 5.
  5. 35 = 7 × 5.
  6. Check: 7 × 5 is 35, so 35 is 7 copies of 5.

Sentence 2 (10 times as many as 8 is 80. [80 = 10 × 8, or 10 × 8 = 80]).

  1. Read it aloud: 10 times as many as 8 is 80. The sentence starts with the times.
  2. Which amount is bigger? 80.
  3. "10 times as many as 8" becomes 10 × 8.
  4. "Is 80" becomes = 80.
  5. 10 × 8 = 80. The bigger amount is still alone, on the right this time.
  6. Check: 10 copies of 8 make 80.

Sentence 3 (A whale is 48 feet long. It is 6 times as long as an 8-foot dolphin. [48 = 6 × 8, or 6 × 8 = 48]).

  1. Read it aloud. Two lengths: the whale and the dolphin.
  2. Which is longer? The whale, 48 feet.
  3. Strip picture, if it helps: one strip for the dolphin, 8 feet; 6 copies of it for the whale.
  4. "It is 6 times as long as 8" becomes 48 = 6 × 8.
  5. We do not multiply 48 by 6: the 48 is already the whale.
  6. Check: 6 × 8 is 48. The equation has no feet in it; the sentence tells what each number counts.

Say the rule once and leave it on the board: the bigger amount stands alone; the times and the smaller amount are multiplied. Either side of the equals sign is fine.

7 min. Sentence set 1

Alone. Each set mixes both ways, things counted and things measured. For an equation either reading is right; a student who finishes early writes the second one too: 45 is 5 times as many as 9. [45 = 5 × 9, or 5 × 9 = 45], 56 = 7 × 8 [56 is 7 times as many as 8, or 56 is 8 times as many as 7], A flagpole is 50 feet tall. It is 10 times as tall as a 5-foot fence. [50 = 10 × 5, or 10 × 5 = 50], 42 = 6 × 7 [42 is 6 times as many as 7, or 42 is 7 times as many as 6].

About 4 minutes to write, then the check screen, and you read each answer from the key. Students tick a right first try and fix a wrong one in the second colour. A student compares only with their own last set; no counts aloud.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

Watch for an addition, 5 + 9 or 45 = 5 + 9 (error A); the bigger number inside the multiplication, 9 = 5 × 45 (error C); and on a measured sentence, the two big numbers multiplied, 50 × 10 (error B). If many miss one item, draw its strip on the board before set 2: one strip for the smaller amount, copies for the bigger.

7 min. Sentence set 2

Alone, the same way: 10 times as many as 7 is 70. [70 = 10 × 7, or 10 × 7 = 70], 72 = 8 × 9 [72 is 8 times as many as 9, or 72 is 9 times as many as 8], A bag weighs 36 pounds. That is 4 times as much as a 9-pound box. [36 = 4 × 9, or 4 × 9 = 36], 60 = 3 × 20 [60 is 3 times as many as 20, or 60 is 20 times as many as 3]. Its first sentence starts with the times, like sentence 2.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

7 min. Sentence set 3

Alone: 120 = 10 × 12 [120 is 10 times as many as 12, or 120 is 12 times as many as 10], 18 is 9 times as many as 2. [18 = 9 × 2, or 9 × 2 = 18], 200 = 10 × 20 [200 is 10 times as many as 20, or 200 is 20 times as many as 10], A pool is 90 feet long. It is 3 times as long as a 30-foot boat. [90 = 3 × 30, or 3 × 30 = 90]. Two equations are ten times a two-digit number. Walk to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

7 min. Sentence set 4

Alone: A train is 400 feet long. It is 8 times as long as a 50-foot bus. [400 = 8 × 50, or 8 × 50 = 400], 280 = 4 × 70 [280 is 4 times as many as 70, or 280 is 70 times as many as 4], A farm has 240 hens. That is 6 times as many as its 40 cows. [240 = 6 × 40, or 6 × 40 = 240], 300 = 5 × 60 [300 is 5 times as many as 60, or 300 is 60 times as many as 5]. The unit's target (ours, untested) is at least 5 of 6 right on the first try by today: read it from sets 3 and 4 together. No time target.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

If many are still under 3 of 4 in set 4, the strip sheet comes out for the written task.

9 min. Written task: is Jo right?

Alone, side 2 (Jo says 15 is 5 times as many as 3. Draw a picture and write an equation to show whether Jo is right. [Jo is right: five copies of a 3-strip make 15; 15 = 5 × 3]). The picture is a strip picture; the strip sheet is there for anyone who wants it. You read these after class with the slips. Look-fors, each ticked alone:

  1. five equal copies of 3
  2. the equation 15 = 5 × 3

Watch for a 3-strip and a 5-strip side by side with 3 + 5 = 8 (error A): ask "how many copies of 3 are there?"

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

Ask. "A ten is how many times as much as a one?" Expect: 10 times; 10 = 10 × 1. Then tie it to sentence 2: 80 is 10 times as many as 8, and each ten is 10 times a one.

Ask. "In every equation today, where did the bigger number go?" Expect: alone on one side of the equals sign.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, no strip sheet, read aloud once (Write a multiplication equation: 14 is 2 times as many as 7. [14 = 2 × 7], Write a multiplication equation: 10 times as many as 6 is 60. [10 × 6 = 60], Kai's rope is 16 feet long. That is 2 times as long as Lu's 8-foot rope. Write a multiplication equation. [16 = 2 × 8]). Write the results on the record sheet under E82.

Other rooms

  • No printer. Write each set of four on the board as it starts; students write on lined paper and draw a line under each set for the count.
  • One child at home. Say each sentence aloud and have the child write the equation, then turn it round. Ten right is enough: stop at ten if all ten are right, and skip the count.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4's last two items and the second Ask. Never drop the checks between sets, the written task or the exit. Longer: the challenge (Write every "times as many" sentence that 24 can show. [24 is 3 times as many as 8; 24 is 8 times as many as 3; 24 is 4 times as many as 6; 24 is 6 times as many as 4 (also 2 and 12, and 1 and 24)]), on lined paper; then three sentences of their own with ten times a two-digit number, swapped with a partner.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, times as long as, times as tall as, equation. Frames on the display: " is times as many as ." " = × ". The frame from day 80 stays on the wall over its strip picture.

Tomorrow

On the slip, an addition, 2 + 7 = 9 or 14 = 2 + 7, or 10 + 6 = 16 (error A): day 83's five minutes after the warm-up show A's wrong example again, unless the readiness reteach needs them. Kai's rope as 16 + 8 = 24 or 16 × 2 = 32 (error B): day 83 rereads the sentence and asks who has more. 7 = 2 × 14 (error C): day 83 has no slot for it, so write "Which is bigger?" on those slips before they go back. A written task with 3 + 5 = 8 joins the A group.
