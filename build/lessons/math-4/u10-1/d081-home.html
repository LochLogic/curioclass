Day 81: One equation, two comparisons

At home

GoalI can read a multiplication equation as a comparison, two ways
Time35 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No grid paper or scissors: 12 coins or paper squares. No projector: point at side 1.

Skip. The warm-up, the cutting, half the six equations, and the challenge.

  • 8 min. Coins both ways. Lay 12 coins in 3 rows of 4, then in 4 rows of 3. Then Example 1 (side 1) together.

    Say. "What do you notice about 3 rows of 4 and 4 rows of 3?" Expect: both are 12; the 3 and the 4 swap jobs.

  • 7 min. Read it both ways. Read Example 2 (side 1) aloud together. The child does Equation cards 1 (side 1) and 2; check from the key that each sentence starts with the bigger number.

  • 10 min. Three equations. Six equations 1 (side 2), 2 and 4: both sentences, then one strip picture each.

  • 5 min. Fix it. Fix it (side 2): the child writes what went wrong and both sentences.

    Say. "Which amount is bigger? Where does it go?" Expect: 12; first in the sentence.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers the ticket question alone.

The questions

The child works on the printed page; the answers are for you.

WhereQuestionAnswer
Example 1 (side 1)12 = 3 × 4. What two comparisons does it show?12 is 3 times as many as 4; 12 is 4 times as many as 3
  1. Lay one 4-strip on its own above the 3 strips of 4, and one 3-strip above the 4 strips of 3. Both rows make 12 squares.
  2. Look at the 3 strips of 4. That is 3 copies of a 4-strip, so 12 is 3 times as many as 4.
  3. Look at the 4 strips of 3. That is 4 copies of a 3-strip, so 12 is 4 times as many as 3.
  4. The 12 stands alone in the equation. It is the bigger amount: all the copies together.
  5. Either factor can be the number of copies. So 12 = 3 × 4 says two things.
Example 2 (side 1)42 = 6 × 7. Finish both sentences.42 is 6 times as many as 7; 42 is 7 times as many as 6
  1. The 42 stands alone, so 42 is the bigger amount.
  2. 6 copies of a 7-strip make 42. So 42 is 6 times as many as 7.
  3. 7 copies of a 6-strip make 42. So 42 is 7 times as many as 6.
