Day 82: Sentences into equations

At home

GoalI can write the equation for a "times as many" sentence, quickly and correctly
Time31 minutes, one parent and one child (a teaching day)
Printthe student page and one exit ticket; this page and its answers are for you

Swap. No projector: read each answer from the key.

Skip. The warm-up sums, sets 2 and 4, and the challenge.

  • 6 min. Three sentences. Read the three Teaching first sentences on side 1 aloud; the child writes each equation as you go. The bigger number stands alone on one side: 35 = 7 × 5, 80 = 10 × 8, 48 = 6 × 8.

  • 6 min. Set 1. Sentence sets 1 (side 1), 2, 3, 4. Read each answer from the key; the child ticks a right first try and fixes a wrong one in the second color.

    Say. "Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?" Expect: for the first, yes: 45 = 5 × 9.

  • 7 min. Set 3. Sentence sets, continued 9 (side 2), 10, 11, 12, checked the same way.

  • 7 min. Is Jo right? Jo and Max (side 2), with a strip picture.

    Say. "How many copies of 3 make 15? How many copies of 5?" Expect: 5 and 3, so both are right.

  • 5 min. Exit ticket. The child answers ticket question 1, question 2 and question 3 alone.

Look-fors:
  1. a strip picture of equal copies (five 3s or three 5s)
  2. one equation, 15 = 5 × 3 or 15 = 3 × 5
  3. says both are right, and why
Samples (ours, untested):
  • meets: five 3-strips, 15 = 5 × 3, both are right because 5 × 3 = 3 × 5.
  • partly: 15 = 5 × 3, Jo is right. (Look-for 3 missing: Max not judged.)
  • not: 3 + 5 = 8, so both are wrong. (Error A.)
