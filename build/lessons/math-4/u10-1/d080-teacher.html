Day 80: Times as many

Before class

Print the student page both sides, the strip sheet, the exit slips and the key. Count 30 cubes into a bag for each pair. Write the frame card " is times as many as " with a 3-strip and its 4 copies under it, and put it on the wall. Put the display page on the projector. Have day 79's readiness results beside you, with the names of students who missed item 1.

The day

5 min. Warm-up: count by threes and fours

Whole class, then alone. Count aloud by threes to 30, then by fours to 40. Students write each count on side 2. Example 1's copies are counted by threes.

If the readiness check showed gaps on item 1 (the 30 chairs in 5 rows): nothing moves today. If many missed it, day 81 opens with the whole-class reteach in the unit opener. If a few did, list them for a small group in day 81's warm-up. Today, pair each of them with a partner who got item 1 right. Gaps on items 2 and 3 wait for days 83 and 87.

10 min. Teaching first: 4 times as many, beside 4 more

Whole class, the teacher building at the front. Students fill the two frames on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its own screen.

Example 1 (Pia has 3 cubes. Build 4 times as many cubes. How many is that? [12 cubes; 12 is 4 times as many as 3]).

  1. Snap 3 cubes into a strip. This is the 3-strip, Pia's cubes.
  2. 4 times as many means 4 copies of the 3-strip. Build them under it, counting copies aloud: one, two, three, four.
  3. In the bottom row, the first strip is copy one. Nothing else goes in that row.
  4. Count by threes: 3, 6, 9, 12.
  5. Say it in the frame: 12 is 4 times as many as 3.
  6. Draw it: a strip of 3 squares, and under it 4 strips of 3 squares, end to end.

Example 2 (Pia has 3 cubes. Now build 4 more cubes than Pia has. How many is that? [7 cubes; 7 is 4 more than 3]).

  1. Put out Pia's 3-strip again.
  2. 4 more means 4 single cubes on the end, not 4 copies.
  3. Build a strip of 3, then snap 4 cubes after it: 3 + 4 = 7.
  4. Say it: 7 is 4 more than 3.
  5. Lay it beside the 12. In one, the 4 counts copies. In the other, the 4 counts cubes.

Leave both builds side by side all day. Say once, plainly: "Times also means occasions, as in three times a day. Here it means copies." A student may say the comparison in a home language first.

25 min. Build, draw, say: six comparisons

Pairs with cubes for the first three, then alone, drawing only. The six are on side 2 (Build a strip of 2 cubes. Build 3 times as many cubes. How many is that? [6; 6 is 3 times as many as 2], Build a strip of 1 cube. Build 7 times as many cubes. How many is that? [7; 7 is 7 times as many as 1], Build a strip of 5 cubes. Build 3 times as many cubes. How many is that? [15; 15 is 3 times as many as 5], Draw a strip for 5. Draw 7 times as many. How many is that? [35; 35 is 7 times as many as 5], Draw a strip for 6. Draw 8 times as many. How many is that? [48; 48 is 8 times as many as 6], Draw a strip for 4. Draw 9 times as many. How many is that? [36; 36 is 9 times as many as 4]).

For 1 to 3, one partner builds the small strip, the other builds the copies under it, counting copies aloud. Items 1 and 2: both draw the two strips on the strip sheet, a square for each cube. From item 3 on, each copy is a box with its number inside. Then fill the frame. Swap jobs each time. Cubes go back in the bag after 3. Leave the equation line blank today; write the frame sentence on the answer line.

For 4 to 6 there are too many to build. Students draw each copy as a box, as in item 3, and count by that number. This is the strip picture the stretch keeps. At 20 minutes, partners swap and check 4 to 6 against the screen.

Check: Count the copies in the bottom row. Is the first strip copy one?

Watch for a small strip plus single cubes on the end (error A: 5 for the first), and one copy too many (error D: 8 for the first). Ask as you walk: "Which strip is one copy? How many copies did you make?" If many make D, the class counts copies aloud with you, starting with the first strip as copy one.

10 min. Fix two wrong answers

"Fix it", side 1 (Ray says: "8 times as many as 3 is 11." He drew a 3-strip and 8 more squares. [8 copies of the 3-strip; 24. 24 is 8 times as many as 3. Ray added 8], Lin drew 4 times as many as 3. In one row, she drew a 3-strip, then 4 more 3-strips. She got 15. [4 copies of the 3-strip in all; 12. Lin drew one copy too many]). Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then the class. The display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Ray added 8 squares: 8 times as many is 8 copies. Lin drew the 3-strip and then four more in one row: in the long row, the first strip is copy one.

If many still add, pairs build both with cubes, "8 more than 3" and "8 times as many as 3", and say which is which.

5 min. Pull it together

Whole class, at the two builds from teaching first.

Ask. "Where is the 4 in your cubes? Where is the 3?" Expect: "the 3 is the cubes in one strip"; "the 4 is how many copies". Then point to the 7: "Where is the 4 here?" Expect: "4 single cubes on the end". Show the copies first, then the single cubes.

5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper (Draw a strip for 2 cubes. Under it, draw a strip that shows 5 times as many cubes. How many cubes is that? Finish: is times as many as . [Five copies of the 2-strip; 10 cubes; 10 is 5 times as many as 2]). Read it aloud once. A student may build it with cubes first, then draw. Record results on the record sheet under E80. Cubes are put away for the stretch.

Other rooms

  • No cubes or counters. Students draw squares on the strip sheet, or cut paper squares; the pairs work stays the same, one drawing the small strip and one the copies.
  • One projector, no camera. Build at the front, then draw the same strips on the board.
  • One child at home. Beans, coins or bricks. You set out 3; the child sets out 4 times as many, then 4 more, and says which is which and draws both. Then swap roles.

Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop comparison 6 and the challenge. Never drop the fixes, the frames or the cubes for 1 to 3. Longer: the challenge (A strip has 7 squares. Draw 8 times as many squares. How many is that? [56; 56 is 8 times as many as 7]), drawn with each copy as a box marked 7.

Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, copy, strip, more than. Frames: " is times as many as ." " is more than ." Access: cubes that snap, sentences read aloud, frames to fill, not copy.

Tomorrow

On the slip, 7 (error A): the strips show a 2 and a 5 added. 12, or six copies drawn (error D): one copy too many. "2 is 5 times as many as 10" (error C): roles swapped. Day 81 opens with the readiness reteach if many missed item 1. Otherwise its first five minutes show the commoner of A and D beside the right one. If both are needed, that error is talked through inside day 81's notice task. C is fixed on day 81 anyway. Collect the strip sheets with the slips.
