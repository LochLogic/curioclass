# 1. Story Mountain: The Enormous Turnip
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell the beginning, middle and end of a story and find the most exciting part.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to The Enormous Turnip. Fill in the story mountain: the beginning at the bottom, the middle at the top, the end on the way down. Then circle the part with the most excitement for an audience.
mistakes: Students often put "they pulled" in every part. The beginning sets up the problem (the turnip is too big), the top of the mountain is the biggest moment (everyone pulls, even the mouse), and the end is how it turns out (pop, everyone falls). Some think the beginning is the most exciting because it comes first.
notes: The Enormous Turnip is a Russian folktale, retold here in Curio Class's own words (about 110 words). Read it aloud twice, then let small groups act it out in a line, each actor holding the waist of the one in front. Ask the audience when they leaned forward: that is the top of the mountain.

## Listen
passage: The Enormous Turnip
  Grandpa planted a turnip seed. "Grow, little turnip! Grow big and sweet!" The turnip grew and grew until it was enormous.
  Grandpa pulled and pulled, but he could not pull it up. So he called Grandma. They pulled and pulled. Still the turnip stayed in the ground.
  Grandma called the girl. The girl called the dog. The dog called the cat. They all pulled and pulled. Still it stayed!
  At last the cat called a tiny mouse. The mouse pulled the cat, the cat pulled the dog, the dog pulled the girl, the girl pulled Grandma, and Grandma pulled Grandpa. Pop! Out came the enormous turnip, and everyone fell down in a heap.

## Main: Story mountain
write: Bottom of the mountain (the beginning): What happens first?
  lines: 2
  key: Grandpa plants a turnip seed, and it grows enormous. He cannot pull it up.
write: Top of the mountain (the middle): What is the biggest moment?
  lines: 2
  key: Grandpa calls for help again and again: Grandma, the girl, the dog, the cat, and at last the tiny mouse. They all pull together.
write: Down the mountain (the end): How does it turn out?
  lines: 2
  key: Pop! The turnip comes out, and everyone falls down in a heap.

## Circle the most exciting part
choice: (Read aloud.) Which part has the most excitement for an audience?
  - The beginning: Grandpa plants a seed.
  * The top: everyone, even the mouse, pulls together. Will it come out?
  - The end: the turnip is out.

## Stretch
write: How could actors make the top of the mountain exciting for the audience?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary: pull harder and harder, make straining faces and grunting sounds, slow down before the pop, count "one, two, three, pull!" together, fall down with a big tumble.

# 7. Pantomime Puzzle: What Am I Holding?
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can figure out an invisible object from how an actor moves, and pantomime one of my own.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each clue card tells how an actor moves with an invisible object. Write what the object is and draw it in the box. Then invent your own pantomime clue for a partner to solve.
mistakes: Students often name the action ("brushing") instead of the object ("a toothbrush"). Ask, "What is in the actor's hand?" Some clues fit two objects (a broom or a mop); accept either if the student can point to the clue words that fit.
notes: Act out each clue after students solve it so they see how the hands show the object's size, shape and weight. In pantomime, the hands keep the object the same size every time they hold it. Answers: 1 toothbrush, 2 kite (the string), 3 basketball, 4 broom, 5 egg, 6 book. For the stretch, partners trade clue cards, solve them, and then act them out.

## Example
example: Clue: The actor holds something small near her ear, waits, nods, says "Hello?" and smiles. What is she holding? **A phone.**

## Main: Solve the clue cards
table: Clue card | The object is... | Draw the invisible object in this box
  height: 0.95
  - 1. The actor holds something thin. He moves it back and forth in front of his teeth, then rinses and spits in a sink. | {a toothbrush} |
  - 2. The actor holds a string with both hands, looks up high, runs, and pulls when the wind tugs. | {a kite} |
  - 3. The actor bounces something round on the floor, then pushes it up high toward a hoop. | {a basketball} |
  - 4. The actor holds a long handle with two hands and pushes it side to side across the floor. | {a broom} |
  - 5. The actor taps something small on the edge of a bowl and pulls it into two halves. | {an egg} |
  - 6. The actor holds something flat in both hands, looks down, and turns a page. | {a book} |

## Stretch: Invent one
write: Write a clue card for your own invisible object. Tell how the actor moves, but do not name the object.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary, for example: The actor holds something round and cold in one hand, licks the top, and catches a drip before it falls. (an ice cream cone)
draw: Draw your object here, then fold this box back so your partner cannot see it. | 1.2

# 20. Puppet Moves Color by Code
minutes: 25
materials: crayons or colored pencils: yellow, blue, green, red
ican: I can tell how a puppet feels from the way it moves.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each puppet move. Decide the feeling it shows and write it with its color. Then color each square of the picture by its number to find the hidden picture.
mistakes: Students may guess the feeling from one word ("turns" means shy) without reading the whole move; a puppet that turns its back and crosses its arms is mad, not shy. Check the 8 rows before students start coloring: one wrong feeling changes a whole part of the picture.
notes: Feelings and colors: happy is yellow (moves 1 and 5), sad is blue (2 and 6), shy is green (3 and 7), mad is red (4 and 8). The hidden picture: a yellow sun in the top right corner of a blue sky, and a red flower in green grass. Act out a few moves with a puppet first and have the class name the feeling.

## Warm up: The color key
table: Feeling | Color
  - happy | yellow
  - sad | blue
  - shy | green
  - mad | red

## Main: What does each move show?
table: Number | Puppet move | Feeling | Color
  - 1 | The puppet bounces quickly with its arms up high. | {happy} | {yellow}
  - 2 | The puppet's head tilts down and its body droops low. | {sad} | {blue}
  - 3 | The puppet turns away and hides its face. | {shy} | {green}
  - 4 | The puppet stomps and shakes its fists. | {mad} | {red}
  - 5 | The puppet does a big spin and claps. | {happy} | {yellow}
  - 6 | The puppet walks slowly, slumps and sighs. | {sad} | {blue}
  - 7 | The puppet peeks out, then quickly hides again. | {shy} | {green}
  - 8 | The puppet turns its back, crosses its arms and huffs. | {mad} | {red}

## Color the picture
table: A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I
  height: 0.42
  - 6 | 2 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 1 | 1
  - 2 | 2 | 6 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 5 | 1
  - 2 | 2 | 2 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 6
  - 6 | 2 | 2 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 6 | 2
  - 3 | 3 | 4 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7
  - 7 | 4 | 8 | 4 | 3 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7
  - 7 | 3 | 3 | 7 | 3 | 7 | 3 | 7 | 7
fill: The hidden picture is a {sun}, a sky and a {flower} in the grass.

## Stretch
write: Make up a new puppet move that shows "excited." Tell what the head, arms and body do.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary: the puppet jumps up and down fast, waves both arms, and its head pops up and looks all around.

# 21. What Do the Puppets Like? Bar Graph
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can make a bar graph and answer questions about it.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the chart about 4 puppets. Make a bar graph of how many times each puppet played its favorite game. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students may color one box too many by starting at 1 instead of at the bottom line, or color the space between numbers. Show them that each bar starts at 0 and stops on the line for its number. Watch for "How many more" answered with the bigger number (8) instead of the difference (5).
notes: The graph grid numbers the puppets 1 to 4 along the bottom; students draw each bar as a box centered on its number (the key shows the bars). Before graphing, have students point to each puppet's number in the chart. Puppet names and data are made up.

## Read the chart
table: Puppet | Likes to eat | Likes to play | Times it played this week
  - 1 Fox | berries | tag | 6
  - 2 Frog | crackers | hopscotch | 8
  - 3 Owl | carrots | hide and seek | 3
  - 4 Bear | apples | catch | 5

## Warm up
fill: Owl likes to eat {carrots}.
fill: Bear likes to play {catch}.

## Main: Make a bar graph
graph: Puppet (1 Fox, 2 Frog, 3 Owl, 4 Bear) | 0 5 1 | Times played | 0 10 1
  key: 0.7,0 0.7,6 1.3,6 1.3,0 1.7,0 1.7,8 2.3,8 2.3,0 2.7,0 2.7,3 3.3,3 3.3,0 3.7,0 3.7,5 4.3,5 4.3,0

## Questions
choice: Which puppet played its game the most times?
  - Fox
  * Frog
  - Owl
  - Bear
choice: Which puppet played its game the fewest times?
  - Fox
  - Frog
  * Owl
  - Bear
fill: How many more times did Frog play than Owl? {5} more times

## Stretch
write: Plan a puppet picnic scene. Which snacks should go on the prop table so every puppet gets the food it likes? Use the chart.
  lines: 2
  key: Berries for Fox, crackers for Frog, carrots for Owl and apples for Bear.

# 33. Lost Traveler Map
minutes: 30
materials: pencil; for the drama: a hat, a bag or a map prop for the teacher in role
ican: I can use a map to help a character in a drama.
directions: (Read aloud.) Your teacher will play a lost traveler who just got off the train. In role as town helpers, use the map. Answer the questions, draw the traveler's route from the Train Station to the Library, and label 3 landmarks on the way.
mistakes: Students may draw a route through the Pond or jump across squares. The route moves one square at a time: up, down, left or right. Some name squares backward (1A instead of A1); the letter is the column and the number is the row.
notes: Teacher in role: put on a hat, hold a suitcase, and say, "Excuse me! I'm lost. I need to return a book to the Library before it closes. Can you help me?" Let helpers give directions using the map ("Go right to the Bakery, then down to the Park..."). Act confused if directions are unclear, so students learn to name landmarks. Model route: A1, B1 (Bakery), B2 (Park), C2 (Park), C3 (Fountain), D3, E3 (Toy Shop), E4 (Library). Other routes that go one square at a time and skip the Pond are correct; landmarks labeled must be on the route the student drew.

## The town map
table: Row | A | B | C | D | E
  height: 0.6
  - 1 | Train Station | Bakery | | Fire Station | Pond
  - 2 | | Park | Park | School |
  - 3 | Post Office | | Fountain | | Toy Shop
  - 4 | Garden | | Bridge | Café | Library
text: Map rule: walk one square at a time, up, down, left or right. Do not walk through the Pond.

## Warm up: Read the map
fill:
  - The traveler gets off the train in square {A1}.
  - The Library is in square {E4}.
  - The Bakery is just to the {right} of the Train Station.
  - The landmark just above the Library is the {Toy Shop}.
  - The Fountain is in square {C3}.

## Main: Guide the traveler
text: Draw the traveler's route on the map with a line and arrows. Then label 3 landmarks the traveler passes.
write: Name 3 landmarks on your route, in order.
  lines: 1
  key: Model route: Bakery, Park, Fountain (or Toy Shop). Accept any 3 landmarks that are on the route the student drew.
write: In role, tell the traveler how to get to the Library. Use 2 landmarks.
  lines: 3
  key: Model: Go right to the Bakery. Walk down through the Park to the Fountain. Then go right to the Toy Shop and down one square. The Library is right there!

## Stretch
write: The traveler asks for the Garden next. Write directions from the Library to the Garden.
  lines: 2
  key: Model: Go left past the Café and the Bridge, then keep going left to the Garden (E4, D4, C4, B4, A4).
