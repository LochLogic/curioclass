# 1. Body Parts That Move
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can name body parts and move them one at a time.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the body part I name. Circle it on the child. Write the first letter. Then stand up and wiggle that part!
mistakes: Children often circle the whole arm when you say hands, or the whole leg for feet: point to where the arm ends and the hand begins. Back is the hardest: on the picture it is the middle of the body, so have children reach around and pat their own back before they circle.
notes: Name the parts in the order on the sheet (top left, then across): head, arms, hands, legs, feet, back. After each one, pause for a 5-second wiggle, then freeze like a statue. Wiggles stay in each child's own space. The first letters are a bonus; a child who only circles has done the dance part.

## Warm up: Wiggle and freeze
example: I say "hands." You circle the hands, write **h**, then wiggle your hands and freeze!

## Main: Circle, write, wiggle
picword:
  - child | {h}ead
  - child | {a}rms
  - child | {h}ands
  - child | {l}egs
  - child | {f}eet
  - child | {b}ack

## Stretch: Your turn
draw: Draw a dancer wiggling one body part. | 1.6

# 7. My Space Bubble
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can dance in my own space bubble and keep my friends safe.
directions: (Read aloud.) Draw a dancer inside a big space bubble. Then look at each picture. Is it a safe way to move near friends? Write O for safe or X for not safe.
mistakes: Children may mark the two children dancing far apart as unsafe because they are "not playing together"; explain that each child has a bubble and the bubbles do not touch. Some children think pushing is fine if it is gentle; keep the rule simple: hands stay in your own bubble.
notes: Before the sheet, have children stand, stretch their arms all the way around them and "paint" the inside of their bubble, then walk slowly around the room without popping anyone's bubble. Read each picture aloud: (1) two friends dancing, each in their own bubble; (2) two friends bumping heads; (3) a child pushing a friend; (4) a child walking slowly and looking where she is going. Children who cannot yet write O and X can circle the safe pictures and cross out the others.

## Warm up: Your bubble
draw: Draw a big bubble. Draw a dancer inside it, arms out wide. | 2.2

## Main: Safe or not safe?
example: Safe: write O. Not safe: write X.
picword:
  - kids-space | {O}
  - kids-bump | {X}
  - kids-push | {X}
  - walk | {O}

## Stretch: Show me
text: Stand up. Make your bubble big, then small. Dance in your bubble without popping a friend's bubble!
note: Watch for children who can make the bubble smaller when a friend comes close; that is the skill.

# 20. Puppy Dance: Start, Middle, End
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can make a dance with a start, a middle and an end.
directions: (Read aloud.) We will make a dance about a happy puppy. Look at the pictures. Write O under the moves a happy puppy might do and X under the sleepy one. Then draw your puppy dance in 3 boxes: a starting shape, a middle move and an ending shape.
mistakes: Children often draw three moving pictures. Point out that the start and end are shapes you hold still (a freeze), and only the middle moves. Some children forget the dance is about a happy puppy; ask, "How does a happy puppy hold its body?"
notes: Read the pictures aloud: jump, run, sleeping cat. Dance it first, then draw: start in a puppy shape (crouched, ready to pounce, or sitting with a wagging "tail"), dance the middle (bouncing, chasing a ball, spinning after a tail) for 8 counts, then freeze in an end shape (curled up for a nap, or rolled over for a tummy rub). Any stick figure or scribble that shows a held shape and a move is a success.

## Warm up: Happy puppy moves
picword:
  - jump | {O}
  - run | {O}
  - cat-sleeping | {X}

## Main: My puppy dance
draw: 1. Start: a still puppy shape. | 1.35
draw: 2. Middle: a happy puppy move. | 1.35
draw: 3. End: a still puppy shape. | 1.35
note: Check that boxes 1 and 3 show held shapes and box 2 shows a move (motion lines, bent legs, a ball).

# 21. Melting Snowman Story Strip
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can show a beginning, a middle and an end with my body.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the 3 pictures. Write 1, 2 or 3 to show what happens first, next and last. Cut them out and glue them in order. Then dance the story!
mistakes: Some children put the puddle first because it is the simplest picture: ask "What did the snowman look like before the sun came out?" Children may melt to the floor in one fast drop; coach them to melt slowly, a little at a time, so the middle lasts.
notes: Tell the story first: "A snowman stands tall and proud. The sun comes out and the snowman starts to drip and droop. At the end there is only a puddle with a hat in it." Dance it together: stand tall and frozen (beginning), sink slowly while the arms droop (middle), lie or sit flat and spread out like a puddle (end). Melting stays in each child's own space; sitting flat is fine for children who prefer not to lie down.

draw: 1. Beginning: glue here. | 1.15
draw: 2. Middle: glue here. | 1.15
draw: 3. End: glue here. | 1.15

# 33. Dance Places Map
minutes: 20
materials: scissors, glue stick, crayons, a classroom world map or globe
ican: I can match dances to the places they come from.
directions: (Read aloud.) Here are 4 dances from around the world. Find each place on our big world map. Cut out the dance pictures and glue each one under its place.
mistakes: Children may match by guessing from the pictures alone. Show each place on the world map or globe first and say its dance name. Watch for the lion dance glued under Mexico or Ireland because "lions live far away"; it is a dance with dancers inside a lion costume, from China.
notes: The sheet has no printed map; use the classroom world map or a globe: point to each place, then children glue the matching picture under its name. Pictures, read aloud: hula, a dance from Hawaii that tells stories with the hands; Irish step dance, from Ireland, with fast feet and arms held still at the sides; the lion dance, from China, with two dancers inside one lion costume, often at the New Year; and folklorico, dances from Mexico with wide, swirling skirts. Optional: after gluing, children put a sticky dot on the big map at each place.

## Warm up: Meet the dances
picword:
  - hula | hula
  - irish-dance | step dance
  - lion-dance | lion dance
  - folklorico | folklorico
note: Point to Hawaii, Ireland, China and Mexico on the world map as you read each name.
