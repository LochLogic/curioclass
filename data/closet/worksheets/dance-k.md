# 1. Body Parts That Move
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can name body parts and move them one at a time.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the body part I name. Circle it on the child. Write the first letter. Then stand up and wiggle that part!
mistakes: Children often circle the whole arm when you say hands, or the whole leg for feet: point to where the arm ends and the hand begins. Back is the hardest: on the picture it is the middle of the body, so have children reach around and pat their own back before they circle.
notes: Name the parts in the order on the sheet (top left, then across): head, arms, hands, legs, feet, back. After each one, pause for a 5-second wiggle, then freeze like a statue. Wiggles stay in each child's own space. The first letters are a bonus; a child who only circles has done the dance part.

## Warm up: Wiggle and freeze
example: I say "hands." You circle the hands, write **h**, then wiggle your hands and freeze!

# 21. Melting Snowman Story Strip
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can show a beginning, a middle and an end with my body.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the 3 pictures. Write 1, 2 or 3 to show what happens first, next and last. Cut them out and glue them in order. Then dance the story!
mistakes: Some children put the puddle first because it is the simplest picture: ask "What did the snowman look like before the sun came out?" Children may melt to the floor in one fast drop; coach them to melt slowly, a little at a time, so the middle lasts.
notes: Tell the story first: "A snowman stands tall and proud. The sun comes out and the snowman starts to drip and droop. At the end there is only a puddle with a hat in it." Dance it together: stand tall and frozen (beginning), sink slowly while the arms droop (middle), lie or sit flat and spread out like a puddle (end). Melting stays in each child's own space; sitting flat is fine for children who prefer not to lie down.
