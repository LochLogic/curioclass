# 1. Anatomy of a Food Label
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, highlighter or crayon
ican: I can find and name the parts of a food label.
directions: Read the pretend cereal label. Write the name of each labeled part using the word bank. Then use the label to answer the questions.
pairs: label-detective
mistakes: Students mix up serving size (how much is one serving) and servings per container (how many servings are in the whole box). Watch for students who think the first ingredient is the one there is least of: ingredients are listed from the most (by weight) to the least.
notes: This is part of a pretend label made for Curio Class; it leaves out some lines (such as total fat and protein) to focus on six parts. The numbers follow the FDA Nutrition Facts rules: 8 g of added sugars is 16% of the 50 g Daily Value, 190 mg of sodium is 8% of 2,300 mg, and 4 g of fiber is 14% of 28 g. Keep talk on reading the label, not on any child's own eating.

## Warm up: Word bank
text: Word bank: **serving size**, **servings per container**, **added sugars**, **sodium**, **dietary fiber**, **ingredients list**

## Main: Label the parts
table: Part | What the pretend Oat Rings label says | Name of the part
  - A | 8 servings per container | {servings per container}
  - B | Serving size 1 cup (39 g) | {serving size}
  - C | Sodium 190 mg, 8% | {sodium}
  - D | Dietary Fiber 4 g, 14% | {dietary fiber}
  - E | Includes 8 g Added Sugars, 16% | {added sugars}
  - F | Ingredients: whole grain oats, sugar, corn starch, salt, vitamin E | {ingredients list}

## Use the label
fill:
  - How much cereal is in one serving? {1 cup}
  - How many servings are in the whole box? {8}
  - Which ingredient is there the most of? {whole grain oats}
  - Theo eats 2 cups of Oat Rings. How many grams of added sugars is that? {16} grams
choice: Salt is a kind of sodium. Which part of the label tells how much sodium is in one serving?
  - Ingredients list
  * Sodium line (part C)
  - Servings per container

## Stretch
write: Why do you think the label lists ingredients from most to least?
  lines: 2
  key: So shoppers can see what the food is mostly made of; the first few ingredients make up most of the food.

# 5. Snack for the Big Game
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can use food labels and a choice chart to pick a snack that helps my body.
directions: Lucia has soccer practice in one hour and needs a snack. Read the four pretend labels and answer the label questions. Then use the 3-step choice chart to pick the best snack for Lucia and explain your choice.
pairs: label-detective
mistakes: Students pick the snack with the fewest calories, thinking fewer is always better. Before running and kicking, Lucia's body needs energy; the question is where the energy comes from. Watch for students who read grams and percent as the same thing: 20 g of added sugars is 40% of the Daily Value.
notes: The labels are pretend, written for Curio Class, with real Daily Values (added sugars 50 g, fiber 28 g, sodium 2,300 mg). Sports nutrition advice for kids is simple: a snack 1 to 2 hours before activity with carbohydrates for energy (fruit, whole grains), a little protein, not much added sugar, and water before, during and after. Either A or C is a strong answer if the reasons fit; B and D are weaker because their energy comes mostly from added sugar or from salty, low-fiber processed corn. Keep the talk on Lucia, not on any child's own eating.

## The pretend labels (one serving)
table: Snack | Calories | Added sugars | Dietary fiber | Protein | Sodium
  - A. Banana (1 medium) | 105 | 0 g (0%) | 3 g (11%) | 1 g | 0 mg (0%)
  - B. Pretend Frosted Snack Cakes (2 cakes) | 230 | 20 g (40%) | 0 g (0%) | 2 g | 200 mg (9%)
  - C. Pretend Whole-Grain Crackers and Cheese | 200 | 1 g (2%) | 3 g (11%) | 9 g | 380 mg (17%)
  - D. Pretend Cheese Puffs (1 bag) | 160 | 0 g (0%) | 0 g (0%) | 2 g | 290 mg (13%)

## Warm up: Read the labels
fill:
  - Which snack has the most added sugars? {B}
  - Which snack has the most protein? {C}
  - Which two snacks have dietary fiber? {A} and {C}
  - If Lucia ate two servings of B (4 cakes), she would get {40} grams of added sugars, which is {80}% of the Daily Value.
choice: Using the 5-20 rule (5% DV or less is low, 20% or more is high), snack C's sodium is
  - low
  * in between
  - high

## Main: The 3-step choice chart
text: **Step 1. Options:** what are the choices? **Step 2. What helps:** what does Lucia's body need before practice? **Step 3. Choose:** which snack, and why?
chart: My thinking
  labels: Step 1. Options; Step 2. What helps; Step 3. Choose
  height: 1.1
  key: Step 1: the banana, the snack cakes, the crackers and cheese, and the cheese puffs. Step 2: energy from fruit or whole grains, a little protein, not much added sugar or salt, and water to drink. Step 3: A or C. Model: "I choose C, crackers and cheese, because the whole grains give energy and fiber, the cheese gives 9 g of protein, and it has almost no added sugar. She should drink water too."
write: Why is snack B the weakest choice for Lucia, even though it has the most calories?
  lines: 3
  key: Most of its energy comes from added sugars (20 g, 40% of the Daily Value in one serving), and it has no fiber and little protein, so the energy does not last through practice.

## Stretch
write: Lucia's teammate is allergic to milk. Which snack would you pick for him, and what else should he drink?
  lines: 2
  key: A, the banana: it has no milk (C has cheese and D is cheese-flavored), it gives quick energy from fruit and has no added sugars. He should drink water before, during and after practice.

# 15. Germ Stopper Moves
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can choose the right move to stop germs from spreading.
directions: Match each germ stopper move to how it works. Then read each classroom scenario and write the germ stopper move that fits best. Use the move bank. One move is used twice.
mistakes: Students write "wash hands" for everything. It is often helpful, but ask which move stops this germ at this moment: a sneeze needs covering first, a fever means staying home, a shared cup is a mouth-to-mouth path. Watch for "cover your mouth with your hand"; the elbow or a tissue keeps germs off hands.
notes: Guidance behind the key (CDC): wash hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, especially after the bathroom and before eating; cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow; stay home when sick, and come back after the fever has been gone for at least 24 hours without fever medicine (or as your school nurse says); do not share cups, water bottles or utensils; clean surfaces many hands touch. Keep scenarios about characters; never ask students about their own illnesses.

## Warm up: How each move works
match: Match each move to the way it stops germs.
  - Wash your hands = Soap and water lift germs off your skin and rinse them away.
  - Cover your cough or sneeze = Your elbow or a tissue catches the drops before they fly.
  - Don't share a cup = Germs from one mouth cannot reach another mouth.
  - Stay home when sick = A sick person cannot pass germs to the class.
  - Wipe a surface = Germs that many hands left behind are cleaned away.

## Main: Pick the move
text: Move bank: **wash hands**, **cover**, **don't share a cup**, **stay home**, **wipe the surface**
fill:
  - Ana feels a big sneeze coming while she reads at her desk. {cover}
  - Ben woke up with a fever and a sore throat on a school day. {stay home}
  - At lunch, Carla's friend asks for a sip from Carla's water bottle. {don't share a cup}
  - Dev just used the bathroom and is heading back to class. {wash hands}
  - The art table is sticky, and the whole class touched it this morning. {wipe the surface}
  - Eli played with the class pet and now it is snack time. {wash hands}

## Stretch
write: Ben feels a little better by lunch. Why should he still stay home that day?
  lines: 3
  key: He can still pass germs to classmates while he is sick, and his body needs rest to get better; he should wait until the fever has been gone for at least 24 hours without fever medicine, or follow the school nurse's rule.

# 21. Theo's Hygiene Routine
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can plan a hygiene routine that keeps my body clean and healthy.
directions: Theo wants a routine he can follow every day. Choose steps from the bank and write them in order in Theo's morning plan and evening plan. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students sometimes put brushing teeth in only one plan. Brushing is a twice-a-day habit, so it belongs in both. Watch for washing hands left out after using the toilet. Order can vary (a shower can be morning or evening), so accept any order with a sensible reason.
notes: Facts from mainstream guidance: brush teeth twice a day for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth once a day (American Dental Association); wash hands with soap for at least 20 seconds (CDC). School-age children need 9 to 12 hours of sleep (CDC). Theo is a story character; keep the talk on his routine, not on any child's home.

## Warm up: Step bank
text: Step bank: **brush teeth for 2 minutes**, **floss**, **wash face**, **use the toilet and wash hands**, **get dressed in clean clothes**, **comb or brush hair**, **take a shower or bath**, **put on pajamas**, **lay out clothes for tomorrow**, **put dirty clothes in the hamper**, **go to bed on time**

## Main: Theo's plans
chart: Theo's morning plan (in order) | Theo's evening plan (in order)
  rows: 6
  key: A sample plan. Morning: use the toilet and wash hands; wash face; brush teeth for 2 minutes; get dressed in clean clothes; comb or brush hair. Evening: take a shower or bath; put dirty clothes in the hamper; put on pajamas; brush teeth for 2 minutes; floss; lay out clothes for tomorrow; go to bed on time. Any sensible order works if brushing is in both plans.

## Check the plan
fill:
  - Theo should brush his teeth {2} times a day.
  - Each time, he should brush for {2} minutes.
  - He should clean between his teeth by flossing {1} time a day.
  - He should scrub his hands with soap for at least {20} seconds.
choice: Which step is in both of Theo's plans?
  - Lay out clothes for tomorrow
  * Brush teeth for 2 minutes
  - Put on pajamas

## Stretch
write: Theo is in a rush one morning. Which two steps should he never skip? Explain why.
  lines: 3
  key: Brushing his teeth (it removes plaque that causes cavities) and washing his hands after the toilet (it stops germs from spreading). Accept other choices with a health reason.

# 26. Jonah at the Lunch Table
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can choose safe upstander actions and name trusted adults to tell when I see bullying.
directions: Read the story. Highlight the sentences that show the teasing happened more than once. Then answer the questions about what Jonah could do.
mistakes: Students often say Jonah should "fight back" or say something mean to the boys. Upstander actions are safe and do not make things worse: not laughing along, saying something calm, sitting with the person, and telling an adult. Watch for students who think telling is tattling: telling to get someone help or to stop someone getting hurt is reporting, not tattling.
notes: Bullying is unwanted, aggressive behavior that is repeated or likely to be repeated, often with an imbalance of power (StopBullying.gov). The story shows repetition (Friday, yesterday, today) and a group against one new student. Characters are invented. If a student shares something that is happening to them, follow your school's reporting procedure.

## Read
passage: Jonah at the Lunch Table
  On Tuesday, Jonah carried his tray to his usual seat in the cafeteria. At the end of the table sat Amir, a new boy who had joined the class two weeks ago. Amir opened his lunch box and took out a container of rice and lentils that his grandmother had made.
  Two boys across the table leaned over. "Ew, what is that smell?" one of them said loudly. The other boy held his nose and laughed. Kids at the next table turned to look. Amir closed the lid and stared at his tray. He did not eat another bite.
  Jonah felt his stomach tighten. This was not the first time. Yesterday the same boys had called Amir's lunch "weird," and on Friday they had picked up their trays and moved away when he sat down.
  Jonah thought about laughing along so the boys would not tease him next. Then he remembered what Ms. Ortiz had taught the class: an upstander is someone who sees unkind behavior and does something safe to help.
  Jonah took a deep breath, picked up his tray, and walked to the end of the table.

## Questions
choice: What is an upstander, according to the story?
  - Someone who stays out of other people's problems
  * Someone who sees unkind behavior and does something safe to help
  - Someone who teases back to make it fair
choice: Which detail shows that the teasing has happened more than once?
  - Amir opened his lunch box.
  - Kids at the next table turned to look.
  * Yesterday the same boys had called Amir's lunch "weird."
choice: At lunch, which adult could Jonah tell right away?
  * The lunch aide or teacher on duty in the cafeteria
  - An older student in the hallway
  - Nobody, because it would make the boys angry
write: Jonah walks to the end of the table. Name two safe things he could do or say when he gets there.
  lines: 3
  key: He could sit next to Amir and talk to him ("Want to sit together? That looks good."); he could calmly tell the boys "That's not cool, cut it out"; he could invite Amir to play at recess. Any two safe, kind actions.
write: Jonah also wants to tell an adult. Is that tattling? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: No. Tattling is trying to get someone in trouble for something small. Reporting is telling an adult so someone who is being hurt gets help. The teasing keeps happening to Amir, so an adult like the lunch aide or Ms. Ortiz needs to know.

## Stretch
write: How might Amir feel when Jonah sits down next to him? Use a detail from the story to explain.
  lines: 2
  key: Relieved or less alone, because the story says he stopped eating and stared at his tray, which shows he felt embarrassed and alone; having someone sit with him shows not everyone agrees with the teasing.

# 36. Kitchen Safety Sweep
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayon or highlighter
ican: I can find hazards in a kitchen and tell how to make them safe.
directions: Look at the 10 kitchen pictures. Circle the 8 hazards, the things that could cause a burn, cut, fall, fire or poisoning. Then write a fix for each hazard in the chart.
mistakes: Students circle every picture, including the apples and the clock. A hazard is something that could hurt someone. Watch for fixes that are only "be careful"; a fix changes the kitchen: lock it up, turn the handle in, wipe the spill, move the towel. For medicine and cleaners the fix is "tell a grown-up," not "put it away myself" if it is open or spilled.
notes: Fixes follow common home safety advice: keep medicine and cleaners up high, closed and locked; turn pot handles toward the back of the stove; keep towels and paper away from the stove; never leave a candle burning alone; wipe spills right away; use a step stool, not a chair, to reach high shelves. The Poison Help line in the United States is 1-800-222-1222. Kitchen tasks with heat or knives always need a grown-up.

## Make it safe
table: Hazard | My fix
  height: 0.5
  - Medicine left open | {Tell a grown-up; the grown-up closes it and puts it up high or locked away.}
  - Cleaner with no cap | {Tell a grown-up; cap it and store it away from food, up high or locked.}
  - Pan handle sticking out | {A grown-up turns the handle in toward the back of the stove.}
  - Knife at the edge | {A grown-up moves it to the back of the counter or puts it away.}
  - Spilled juice | {Wipe it up right away so no one slips.}
  - Candle burning alone | {A grown-up blows it out; never leave a candle burning in an empty room.}
  - Standing on a chair | {Get down; ask a grown-up or use a sturdy step stool with a grown-up.}
  - Towel by the burner | {Move the towel away from the stove so it cannot catch fire.}

  - Give him a big glass of juice
