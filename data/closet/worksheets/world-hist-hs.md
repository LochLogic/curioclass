# 1. From Copernicus to Newton
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can sequence key discoveries of the Scientific Revolution and explain how each challenged an older authority.
directions: Place the letter of each discovery on the timeline. Match each discovery to the older authority or idea it challenged. Then explain how the Scientific Revolution changed the way Europeans decided what was true.
mistakes: Students often say the Scientific Revolution was "science versus religion." Most of these thinkers were religious, and the deeper change was in method: trusting observation, experiment and mathematics over ancient texts (Aristotle, Ptolemy, Galen) and inherited authority. Watch for students who credit Galileo with proposing that Earth orbits the Sun; Copernicus did, in 1543, and Galileo's telescope gave new evidence for it.
notes: Dates are publication dates (Tycho's is the year he observed the new star; he published in 1573; Leeuwenhoek's is his 1676 letter to the Royal Society). Kepler is the stretch on purpose: his first two laws of planetary motion appeared in 1609, one year before Galileo's telescope report, which is too close to show on this timeline. Galileo was tried by the Roman Inquisition in 1633 for his 1632 Dialogue and spent his last years under house arrest.

## Warm up: The old authorities
match: Match each ancient authority to the field Europeans followed him in for centuries.
  - Galen = Medicine and the human body
  - Aristotle = Physics and the nature of the heavens and Earth
  - Ptolemy = Astronomy: an Earth-centered universe
  - Euclid = Geometry, which the new scientists kept and built on

## Main: Place 10 discoveries
timeline: 1540 1690 10
  - 1543 | Copernicus publishes On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres: the Sun, not Earth, is at the center
  - 1572 | Tycho Brahe observes a new star in the sky
  - 1600 | William Gilbert publishes On the Magnet: Earth itself is a giant magnet
  - 1610 | Galileo reports seeing moons orbiting Jupiter through his telescope
  - 1620 | Francis Bacon publishes the New Organon, calling for knowledge built from experiments
  - 1628 | William Harvey shows that the heart pumps blood around the body in a circle
  - 1637 | René Descartes publishes Discourse on Method: doubt everything until reason proves it
  - 1665 | Robert Hooke's Micrographia names "cells" seen under a microscope
  - 1676 | Antonie van Leeuwenhoek reports tiny living things in a drop of water
  - 1687 | Isaac Newton's Principia: one law of gravity rules Earth and the heavens
match: Which older idea did each discovery challenge?
  - Copernicus's Sun-centered system = Ptolemy's teaching that Earth sits still at the center
  - Tycho's new star = Aristotle's idea that the heavens are perfect and never change
  - Galileo's moons of Jupiter = The belief that everything in the sky circles Earth
  - Harvey's circulation of blood = Galen's teaching that the liver makes blood that the body uses up
  - Bacon's method = Relying on Aristotle's logic and ancient books instead of experiment
  - Newton's universal gravity = Aristotle's idea that the heavens follow different laws than Earth

## Explain
write: Choose two discoveries from the timeline. For each, name the authority it challenged and the kind of evidence it used (observation, experiment or mathematics).
  lines: 5
  key: Galileo (1610) challenged Ptolemy and Aristotle using observation through a telescope: moons circling Jupiter proved not everything orbits Earth. Harvey (1628) challenged Galen using experiment and measurement: the amount of blood pumped each hour was far more than the body could make, so it must circulate.
write: Why might the Catholic Church and many university scholars have resisted some of these ideas?
  lines: 4
  key: Their teaching, and their authority, rested on combining Scripture with Aristotle and Ptolemy; a moving Earth seemed to contradict some Bible passages and undercut who decided truth. Galileo's 1633 trial shows the conflict, though many scientists were devout and saw their work as studying God's creation.

## Stretch: Where does Kepler go?
write: In 1609 Johannes Kepler showed that planets move in ellipses (ovals), not perfect circles. Where would Kepler fall on the timeline, and what older idea did he challenge, even among Copernicans?
  lines: 3
  key: Between Gilbert (1600) and Galileo (1610), just before Galileo. He challenged the ancient belief, kept by Ptolemy and even Copernicus, that heavenly motion must be in perfect circles; his laws were built from Tycho's careful observations of Mars.

# 25. The Great Depression Worldwide
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, calculator, colored pencils
ican: I can use economic data to explain how the Great Depression spread from country to country.
directions: Study the two data tables. Answer the questions, graph world trade, and write an explanation of how the crash spread, citing numbers.
mistakes: Students often compare raw numbers across countries that were counted differently: the German figure is a count of registered unemployed people, not a percentage, and the British and American rates come from different sources. Ask them to compare change over time within each country. Another trap: reading the trade spiral as a fall in the amount of goods only; it is value in gold dollars, so falling prices make up part of the drop.
notes: Sources. US unemployment, percent of the civilian labor force: Stanley Lebergott's estimates (Historical Statistics of the United States). UK unemployment, percent of the workforce: Bank of England "A millennium of macroeconomic data," via FRED series UNRTUKA (insured-worker rates of the time were higher, about 22 percent in 1932). Germany, registered unemployed, yearly average in millions, rounded (the official count peaked at about 5.6 million in 1932). World trade: total imports of 75 countries in January of each year, millions of gold dollars, from Charles Kindleberger, The World in Depression, 1929 to 1939. Change 1929 to 1932: Blum, Cameron and Barnes, The European World (1970), as reported in many textbooks. Teach France as the exception: unemployment there stayed low in percentage terms even as trade fell.

## Read the data
fill:
  - In which year was US unemployment highest? {1933}
  - In which year did unemployment in the UK peak? {1932}
  - By how many percentage points did US unemployment rise from 1929 to 1933? {21.7}
  - By how many million did registered unemployment in Germany rise from 1929 to 1932? {3.7}
  - About what percent did world imports fall from January 1929 to January 1933? (Round to the nearest whole percent.) {67}%
  - Which country's foreign trade fell the most from 1929 to 1932? {United States}

## Explain: How the crash spread
choice: Which statement does the data support best?
  - The Depression stayed inside the United States.
  * Trade and production shrank in every country shown and unemployment rose wherever it is reported, so the crisis was worldwide.
  - Trade fell, but unemployment did not change.
  - Germany recovered by 1930.
write: Explain how a stock market crash in New York could cost jobs in Britain and Germany. Use at least two numbers from the tables and the ideas of loans, trade and tariffs.
  lines: 6
  key: After the 1929 crash, American banks stopped lending abroad and called in loans; Germany, which depended on those loans, saw registered unemployment rise from 1.9 to 5.6 million by 1932. Countries raised tariffs to protect their own industries (the US Smoot-Hawley Tariff of 1930), so trade collapsed: world imports fell from 2,998 to 992 million gold dollars by 1933, and US foreign trade fell 70 percent. Fewer sales abroad meant factories in Britain and Germany cut workers, so UK unemployment doubled from 7.2 to 15.4 percent.

## Stretch: Politics
write: Germany's unemployment peaked in 1932, and Hitler became chancellor in January 1933. Using the data, explain one way an economic crisis can weaken a democracy. What other causes would a historian also want to examine?
  lines: 5
  key: Mass unemployment (5.6 million registered in 1932) made many voters lose faith in the Weimar Republic and turn to extreme parties that promised jobs and blamed others. A historian would also examine the Treaty of Versailles, the 1923 inflation, political violence, the choices of conservative elites who brought Hitler into government, and the Nazis' use of propaganda.
