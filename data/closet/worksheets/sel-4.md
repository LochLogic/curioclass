# 1. Think, Feel, Do Triangle
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain how what someone thinks connects to how they feel and what they do.
directions: Read each scene. One corner of the triangle is filled in for you. Fill in the other two: what the character thinks, feels or does. Make sure all three corners fit together.
mistakes: Students write a feeling in the Thinks box ("He thinks sad"). A thought is a sentence the character might say inside their head; a feeling is one word. Also watch for actions that do not match the feeling (a nervous character who "cheers loudly").
notes: The scenes are original to Curio Class. Each triangle is printed as a three-box row: Thinks, Feels, Does, which are the three corners; draw the triangle on the board and fill scene 1 together. Answers vary; the key gives one model per box. Accept any answer that fits the scene and links to the other corners. The stretch shows the big idea: change the thought and the feeling and action change too.

## Warm up
example: Scene: Lucy's friend walks past without saying hi. **Thinks:** "Maybe she didn't see me." **Feels:** calm. **Does:** waves and calls her name.
text: The three corners connect: what you think changes how you feel, and how you feel changes what you do.

## Main: Fill the triangles
text: **1. Priya.** The art teacher hangs up everyone's paintings except Priya's, which is still drying on the rack. Priya stares at the wall and stops talking.
table: Thinks | Feels | Does
  - {"Maybe my painting isn't good enough."} | {disappointed or left out} | stares at the wall and stops talking
text: **2. Marcus.** The teacher asks Marcus to read his paragraph out loud to the class.
table: Thinks | Feels | Does
  - "Everyone will laugh if I mess up." | {nervous} | {reads very quietly or asks to go last}
text: **3. Ana.** Ana's soccer team plays in the final game on Saturday.
table: Thinks | Feels | Does
  - {"We can win this!"} | excited | {practices extra kicks and talks about the game all week}
text: **4. Dev.** Dev's little brother knocks over the block tower Dev spent an hour building.
table: Thinks | Feels | Does
  - "He did that on purpose." | {angry or furious} | {yells at his brother or stomps away}

## Stretch: Change the thought
write: In scene 4, what if Dev thought, "He's little. It was an accident"? How might Dev feel, and what might he do?
  lines: 3
  key: Dev might feel annoyed or disappointed but calmer, not furious. He might take a breath, tell his brother to be careful, and start rebuilding, maybe with his brother's help.

# 21. Coach Comment Makeover
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can turn a fixed-mindset comment into a growth-mindset comment.
directions: Read the story about the Comets soccer team. Then rewrite each fixed-mindset comment as something a growth-minded coach would say. Look at the example first.
mistakes: Students often just flip the comment to praise ("You're a fast runner!"), which is still about talent. A growth comment names effort, a strategy or a next step, and often uses the word "yet." Also watch for rewrites that are unkind or sarcastic.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class. A strong makeover has two parts: it is honest about where the player is now, and it names a way to grow (practice, a strategy, help, time). Praise for being "smart" or "a natural" is a fixed-mindset message even though it sounds nice (item 6). Answers vary; the key gives one model for each.

## Read
passage: A Rough Season for the Comets
  The Comets had lost four games in a row. At Tuesday's practice, everyone was grumpy.
  Their coach, Ms. Rivera, was home sick, so a helper named Mr. Sloane ran practice. He meant well, but his comments did not help.
  When Jamal fell behind in a sprint, Mr. Sloane said, "You're just not a fast runner. Some kids are, and some aren't." When Maya scored, he said, "Maya is a natural. She never has to practice."
  Talking about Saturday's game, Mr. Sloane sighed, "We lost again. We're a losing team."
  The comments kept coming. "Don't kick with your left foot. You're bad at it." "You missed the goal, so you're not a striker." "Great goal! You're so talented." "Goalies are born, not made." "That drill is too hard for you. Just skip it."
  By the end of practice, nobody was talking. Jamal sat on the bench and tied his shoes very slowly.
  The next week, Ms. Rivera came back. She read the list of comments her players had heard and frowned. "Let's give these a makeover," she said. "Every one of you can get better. That's what practice is for."

## Warm up: Spot the growth comment
choice: Which comment is growth-focused?
  - You're the best player we have.
  * You kept trying that pass until it worked. Nice effort.
  - Some people just can't kick with their left foot.
choice: Which comment is fixed-mindset?
  * You'll never be a goalie. You don't have it.
  - Let's figure out what went wrong and try again.
  - Your passing has improved a lot this month.

## Main: Comment makeover
example: Fixed: "You can't dribble." Growth: "You can't dribble past defenders **yet**. Let's practice with the cones ten minutes a day."
write: "You're just not a fast runner. Some kids are, and some aren't."
  lines: 2
  key: You're not as fast as you want to be yet. Let's do sprint drills and time ourselves to see you improve.
write: "Maya is a natural. She never has to practice."
  lines: 2
  key: Maya has practiced her shooting a lot, and it shows. What could you practice to improve your shot?
write: "We lost again. We're a losing team."
  lines: 2
  key: We lost this game. Let's look at what worked, pick one thing to practice, and try it next game.
write: "Don't kick with your left foot. You're bad at it."
  lines: 2
  key: Your left foot is still learning. Take five left-foot shots every practice and it will get stronger.
write: "You missed the goal, so you're not a striker."
  lines: 2
  key: That shot missed. Let's work on aiming low and to the corners, and you'll get more chances.
write: "Great goal! You're so talented."
  lines: 2
  key: Great goal! You saw the open space and shot quickly. All that practice paid off.
write: "Goalies are born, not made."
  lines: 2
  key: Good goalies learn by practicing. Let's work on diving and catching drills each week.
write: "That drill is too hard for you. Just skip it."
  lines: 2
  key: That drill is hard right now. Try it slowly first, then speed up as you get the hang of it.

## Stretch
write: Pick one makeover you wrote. How might the player feel and act differently after hearing it?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. For example: Jamal might feel hopeful instead of embarrassed, and he might ask for sprint drills and keep trying instead of sitting on the bench.
