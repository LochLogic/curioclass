# 1. Half Note: Hold for Two
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can read and clap rhythms with quarter notes, eighth notes and half notes.
directions: (Read aloud.) Ta is a quarter note: 1 beat. Ti-ti is two eighth notes: 2 sounds in 1 beat. Ta-a is a half note: 1 sound held for 2 beats. Clap each rhythm while you count. Then write the beat counts.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: The most common slip is clapping ta-a as two claps. A half note is one clap, then hold: have students clap and then slide their hands apart for the second beat. Watch for counts that restart in the middle of a measure, and for "&" written after ta (only ti-ti gets an "&").
notes: Rhythms are written in rhythm syllables so the sheet prints without music fonts; write the same rhythms in notation on the board (quarter, beamed eighths, half note) and have students point to each as they read. Counts: a quarter note gets its beat number, ti-ti gets the beat number and "&," and a half note gets both beat numbers joined by a dash (3-4). A slash ( / ) is a bar line: counting starts over at 1 after it.

## Warm up: How long?
example: ta  ta  ta-a   counts: 1  2  3-4
  ta-a starts on beat 3 and holds through beat 4, so it is counted 3-4.
fill:
  - Ta (a quarter note) lasts {1} beat.
  - Ti-ti (two eighth notes) fits in {1} beat.
  - Ta-a (a half note) lasts {2} beats.

## Main: Clap and count
table: Rhythm | Say and clap | Write the counts
  - 1 | ta  ta  ta-a | {1  2  3-4}
  - 2 | ta-a  ta  ta | {1-2  3  4}
  - 3 | ti-ti  ta  ta-a | {1 &  2  3-4}
  - 4 | ta  ti-ti  ta-a | {1  2 &  3-4}
  - 5 | ta-a  ti-ti  ta | {1-2  3 &  4}
  - 6 | ti-ti  ti-ti  ta-a | {1 &  2 &  3-4}
  - 7 | ta-a  ta-a / ta  ti-ti  ta-a | {1-2  3-4 / 1  2 &  3-4}
  - 8 | ti-ti  ta  ta-a / ta-a  ti-ti  ti-ti | {1 &  2  3-4 / 1-2  3 &  4 &}

## Stretch: Write your own
choice: Which rhythm has 4 beats?
  - ta-a  ta-a  ta
  * ta  ti-ti  ta-a
  - ta-a  ta-a  ti-ti
write: Write your own 4-beat rhythm with one ta-a in it. Write the counts under it. Clap it for a partner.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Check that the rhythm adds up to exactly 4 beats (ta = 1, ti-ti = 1, ta-a = 2), for example ti-ti ta-a ta, counted 1 & 2-3 4.

# 7. Pat-Clap-Snap Ostinato
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can perform and write a body percussion ostinato.
directions: (Read aloud.) An ostinato is a short pattern that repeats again and again. Learn the ostinato from the picture key. Then write a new 2-measure ostinato with the same symbols and perform it.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: Children often speed up each time the ostinato repeats, or pause at the end of measure 2 before starting again. Keep a steady beat going with a drum and have them say the symbols out loud ("pat, pat, clap, clap"). Watch for new ostinatos with 3 or 5 beats in a measure: count the boxes together. Some children cannot snap yet; a soft tap of two fingers on the palm works the same way.
notes: Symbols: P pat (both hands on the knees), C clap, S snap, a dash (-) a rest (a quiet beat). The model ostinato is measure 1 P P C C, measure 2 P C S -. Teach it by echo: you perform one measure, they copy. Then loop it 4 times while the class sings a song it knows well with a steady beat. The rest is the hardest part: have them open their hands silently on beat 4 of measure 2. In the Stretch, partners perform their two ostinatos at the same time, which makes a layered texture.

## Warm up: The picture key
picword:
  - pat | P = pat
  - clap | C = clap
  - snap | S = snap
text: A dash (-) is a rest: a quiet beat.

## Main: Learn the ostinato
table: Measure | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4
  - 1 | P | P | C | C
  - 2 | P | C | S | -
fill:
  - The whole ostinato has {8} beats.
  - It has {3} pats, {3} claps and {1} snap.
  - The snap is on beat {3} of measure 2.
  - The rest is on beat {4} of measure 2.
choice: (Read aloud.) Why is this pattern called an ostinato?
  - It is very loud.
  - It uses only claps.
  * It repeats again and again.
text: Perform it 4 times without stopping while the class sings a song you know.

## Main: Write a new one
text: Write a new ostinato. Use P, C, S and at least one rest. Make it different from the one above.
chart: Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4
  labels: Measure 1; Measure 2
  height: 0.5
  key: Answers vary. Check for exactly 4 symbols in each measure, only P, C, S and -, at least one rest, and a pattern different from the model. Example: measure 1 C S C S, measure 2 P P C -.
choice: Which one can NOT be a measure of your ostinato?
  - P P S S
  * C S C
  - S - S -

## Stretch: Layer it
write: Perform your ostinato while a partner performs theirs at the same time. Did the two patterns fit together? What did you hear?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A good answer tells what was heard, for example: "Yes. We both kept the beat, and my snaps happened while my partner was patting, so it sounded like a band."

# 20. Instrument Riddles
minutes: 20
materials: pencil
ican: I can name instruments and their families from clues.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each riddle. Find the instrument in the decoder and write its name. Write its letter in the box with the same number to spell a secret word. Then write each instrument's family.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: Students often put the flute in the brass family because it is made of metal. A flute is a woodwind: the family comes from how you play it, not what it is made of. Watch for the piano: it has strings, but its hammers hit them, so Orchestra Zoo puts it in the percussion family (some books call it a keyboard instrument; accept either).
notes: Answers: 5 piano, 6 trumpet, 7 flute, 8 violin, 9 timpani, 10 bassoon. Families: piano percussion (or keyboard), trumpet brass, flute woodwind, violin strings, timpani percussion, bassoon woodwind. The message is MELODY. Cello, tuba, clarinet and harp are in the decoder as decoys. A modern piano has 88 keys (52 white and 36 black). Timpani have pedals that tighten or loosen the drumhead to change the pitch. Read the riddles aloud for students who need it.

## Warm up: The four families
fill:
  - Instruments you play with a bow or by plucking strings are in the {strings} family.
  - Instruments you play by buzzing your lips are in the {brass} family.
  - Instruments you blow into or across, often with a reed, are in the {woodwind} family.
  - Instruments you hit, shake or scrape are in the {percussion} family.

## Main: Solve the riddles
code: Who am I?
  decoys: cello tuba clarinet harp
  - I have 88 keys. Hammers inside me hit my strings. | piano | M
  - Buzz your lips into me and press my 3 valves. | trumpet | E
  - I am silver and you hold me sideways. Blow across my hole. | flute | L
  - I have 4 strings. Hold me under your chin and play me with a bow. | violin | O
  - I am a big copper bowl with a drumhead on top. My pedal makes me higher or lower. | timpani | D
  - I am a long wooden tube with a double reed. I play low notes. | bassoon | Y

## Main: Name the family
table: Riddle | Family
  - 5 | {percussion}
  - 6 | {brass}
  - 7 | {woodwind}
  - 8 | {strings}
  - 9 | {percussion}
  - 10 | {woodwind}

## Stretch: Write a riddle
write: Write your own riddle for one of the decoy instruments: cello, tuba, clarinet or harp. Give two clues.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: "I am the biggest brass instrument. I play the lowest notes in the band. Who am I? (tuba)"

# 21. Sorcerer's Apprentice Instrument Hunt
minutes: 25
materials: pencil; a recording of The Sorcerer's Apprentice by Paul Dukas (about 10 to 12 minutes)
ican: I can hear and name instruments in an orchestra piece.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story first. While the music plays, put a check in the box each time you hear an instrument. Then answer the questions.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: Children often hear the bassoon's low, bouncy tune and call it a tuba. Play a few seconds of each instrument before the piece (Orchestra Zoo has the bassoon, trumpet and flute) so the sounds are fresh. Some will want to check every box at once: pause the recording two or three times to let them catch up.
notes: Read this aloud before listening (adapted from Goethe's 1797 poem, which Paul Dukas turned into music in 1897): A young helper, the apprentice, works for an old sorcerer. When the sorcerer goes out, the apprentice uses a magic spell to make a broom carry water for him. But he cannot remember how to stop it! Water floods the room. He chops the broom in two, and now two brooms carry water. At last the sorcerer comes home and stops the spell. The broom's marching tune is first played by bassoons; listen for big timpani rolls and loud brass as the water rises. The music is in the public domain; recordings vary, so use one your school may play.

## Warm up: Meet the instruments
picword:
  - bassoon | bassoon
  - trumpet | trumpet
  - timpani | timpani
  - flute | flute

## Main: Listening log
table: Instrument | Family | Check each time you hear it | Was it high or low?
  - bassoon | {woodwind} |  | {low}
  - trumpet | {brass} |  | {high}
  - timpani | {percussion} |  | {low}
  - flute | {woodwind} |  | {high}
choice: Which instrument plays the broom's marching tune first?
  - flute
  * bassoon
  - trumpet
choice: Near the end the music gets loud and fast. What is happening in the story?
  - The apprentice is sleeping.
  * The water is rising and the brooms will not stop.
  - The sorcerer is teaching a new spell.
write: The timpani are big drums. Why do you think the composer used them when the room floods?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A good answer links the loud, booming sound to the danger or the crashing water, for example: "The timpani are loud and deep, so they sound like big splashes and show things are out of control."

## Stretch: Your broom
write: If you wrote music for a broom, which instrument would you pick? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names an instrument and a reason about its sound, for example: "A xylophone, because its tapping sounds like a broom hopping."

# 33. Season Song Idea Web
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can plan ideas for a song about a season.
directions: (Read aloud.) Match each sound idea to its season. Then pick a season and fill the idea web with words, a beat, a feeling and an instrument for your song. Use your ideas to write the first two lines.
mistakes: Students often fill the web with a list of season things ("snow, sled, hat") and leave the music boxes empty. Each box should answer its own question: the beat box says fast or slow, steady or bouncy; the feeling box names a mood. Watch for a beat and a feeling that do not match, such as a slow, sleepy beat for an exciting first snow.
notes: Seasons look different in different places; let students write about the season where they live, with snow or without. The web here is a box for each branch: the season goes in the middle box, and each idea box is one branch. A good beat idea names a speed (fast, medium, slow) and a feel (steady, bouncy, march-like). Instruments can be classroom instruments (triangle for falling snow, drum for thunder) or body percussion. Warm up answers: crunchy leaves fall, snowflakes winter, baby birds spring, splashing in the pool summer.

## Warm up: Which season?
match: Match each sound idea to a season.
  - crunchy leaves under my feet = fall
  - snowflakes falling softly = winter
  - baby birds chirping in a nest = spring
  - splashing in the pool = summer

## Main: My idea web
draw: My season (write it and draw a small picture) | 1.0
chart: My ideas
  labels: Words (3 or more); A beat (how fast? how does it move?); A feeling; An instrument (and why)
  height: 0.85
  key: Answers vary. Example for winter: words: snowflakes, mittens, cocoa, quiet; beat: slow and steady, like walking in deep snow; feeling: calm and cozy; instrument: triangle, because its ring sounds like sparkly snow.

## Main: Start the song
write: Use your words to write the first two lines of your song.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. The lines use words from the web, for example: "Snowflakes falling, soft and slow, / mittens on, and off we go."
write: Clap the beat of your lines. Does your beat idea fit the words? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: "Yes. Slow claps fit 'soft and slow,' and they sound calm like my feeling."

## Stretch: A second season
write: Which beat and instrument would you change to write a song about a different season? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: "For summer I would use a fast, bouncy beat and bongos, because summer feels playful and full of energy."
