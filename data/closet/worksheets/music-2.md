# 1. Half Note: Hold for Two
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can read and clap rhythms with quarter notes, eighth notes and half notes.
directions: (Read aloud.) Ta is a quarter note: 1 beat. Ti-ti is two eighth notes: 2 sounds in 1 beat. Ta-a is a half note: 1 sound held for 2 beats. Clap each rhythm while you count. Then write the beat counts.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: The most common slip is clapping ta-a as two claps. A half note is one clap, then hold: have students clap and then slide their hands apart for the second beat. Watch for counts that restart in the middle of a measure, and for "&" written after ta (only ti-ti gets an "&").
notes: Rhythms are written in rhythm syllables so the sheet prints without music fonts; write the same rhythms in notation on the board (quarter, beamed eighths, half note) and have students point to each as they read. Counts: a quarter note gets its beat number, ti-ti gets the beat number and "&," and a half note gets both beat numbers joined by a dash (3-4). A slash ( / ) is a bar line: counting starts over at 1 after it.

## Warm up: How long?
example: ta  ta  ta-a   counts: 1  2  3-4
  ta-a starts on beat 3 and holds through beat 4, so it is counted 3-4.
fill:
  - Ta (a quarter note) lasts {1} beat.
  - Ti-ti (two eighth notes) fits in {1} beat.
  - Ta-a (a half note) lasts {2} beats.

## Main: Clap and count
table: Rhythm | Say and clap | Write the counts
  - 1 | ta  ta  ta-a | {1  2  3-4}
  - 2 | ta-a  ta  ta | {1-2  3  4}
  - 3 | ti-ti  ta  ta-a | {1 &  2  3-4}
  - 4 | ta  ti-ti  ta-a | {1  2 &  3-4}
  - 5 | ta-a  ti-ti  ta | {1-2  3 &  4}
  - 6 | ti-ti  ti-ti  ta-a | {1 &  2 &  3-4}
  - 7 | ta-a  ta-a / ta  ti-ti  ta-a | {1-2  3-4 / 1  2 &  3-4}
  - 8 | ti-ti  ta  ta-a / ta-a  ti-ti  ti-ti | {1 &  2  3-4 / 1-2  3 &  4 &}

## Stretch: Write your own
choice: Which rhythm has 4 beats?
  - ta-a  ta-a  ta
  * ta  ti-ti  ta-a
  - ta-a  ta-a  ti-ti
write: Write your own 4-beat rhythm with one ta-a in it. Write the counts under it. Clap it for a partner.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Check that the rhythm adds up to exactly 4 beats (ta = 1, ti-ti = 1, ta-a = 2), for example ti-ti ta-a ta, counted 1 & 2-3 4.

# 21. Sorcerer's Apprentice Instrument Hunt
minutes: 25
materials: pencil; a recording of The Sorcerer's Apprentice by Paul Dukas (about 10 to 12 minutes)
ican: I can hear and name instruments in an orchestra piece.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story first. While the music plays, put a check in the box each time you hear an instrument. Then answer the questions.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: Children often hear the bassoon's low, bouncy tune and call it a tuba. Play a few seconds of each instrument before the piece (Orchestra Zoo has the bassoon, trumpet and flute) so the sounds are fresh. Some will want to check every box at once: pause the recording two or three times to let them catch up.
notes: Read this aloud before listening (adapted from Goethe's 1797 poem, which Paul Dukas turned into music in 1897): A young helper, the apprentice, works for an old sorcerer. When the sorcerer goes out, the apprentice uses a magic spell to make a broom carry water for him. But he cannot remember how to stop it! Water floods the room. He chops the broom in two, and now two brooms carry water. At last the sorcerer comes home and stops the spell. The broom's marching tune is first played by bassoons; listen for big timpani rolls and loud brass as the water rises. The music is in the public domain; recordings vary, so use one your school may play.

## Warm up: Meet the instruments
picword:
  - bassoon | bassoon
  - trumpet | trumpet
  - timpani | timpani
  - flute | flute

## Main: Listening log
table: Instrument | Family | Check each time you hear it | Was it high or low?
  - bassoon | {woodwind} |  | {low}
  - trumpet | {brass} |  | {high}
  - timpani | {percussion} |  | {low}
  - flute | {woodwind} |  | {high}
choice: Which instrument plays the broom's marching tune first?
  - flute
  * bassoon
  - trumpet
choice: Near the end the music gets loud and fast. What is happening in the story?
  - The apprentice is sleeping.
  * The water is rising and the brooms will not stop.
  - The sorcerer is teaching a new spell.
write: The timpani are big drums. Why do you think the composer used them when the room floods?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A good answer links the loud, booming sound to the danger or the crashing water, for example: "The timpani are loud and deep, so they sound like big splashes and show things are out of control."

## Stretch: Your broom
write: If you wrote music for a broom, which instrument would you pick? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names an instrument and a reason about its sound, for example: "A xylophone, because its tapping sounds like a broom hopping."
