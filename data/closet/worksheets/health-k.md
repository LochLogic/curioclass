# 1. Wash, Wash, Wash! Handwashing Steps
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons
ican: I can wash my hands in the right order.
directions: (Read aloud.) Say each picture's name. Write a number under each picture to show the order: 1 is first and 5 is last. Then cut out the pictures and glue them in order on the sink strip.
pairs: habit-hero
mistakes: Children often put soap before getting hands wet, or skip rinsing and go straight to drying. Act out the steps at a pretend sink: wet, soap, scrub, rinse, dry. The faucet picture (wet) and the hands under water (rinse) look alike: point out the soap bubbles are gone in the rinse step because it comes after scrubbing.
notes: Name every picture together before anyone writes: water on, soap, scrub with bubbles, rinse, dry with a towel. The steps follow the CDC: wet, lather, scrub for at least 20 seconds (about two times through the "Happy Birthday" song), rinse, dry. If cutting is too much for your group, the numbers alone show the order; skip the strip.

## Main: Number the steps
picword:
  - soap | soap {2}
  - towel | dry {5}
  - faucet | wet {1}
  - rinse | rinse {4}
  - suds | scrub {3}

## The sink strip
draw: Glue step 1 here at the left, then 2, 3, 4, and step 5 at the right. | 1.4

## Stretch: When do we wash?
choice: (Read aloud.) When is a time to wash your hands?
  - While we look at the clouds
  * Before we eat a snack
  - While we sing a song at circle time

# 7. Five Senses Helpers
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can name my five senses and ways to keep them safe.
directions: (Read aloud.) Say each sense picture's name and number. Draw Sam, then write each number on the part of Sam that goes with it. Then draw a helper that keeps each sense safe.
mistakes: Children may put 4 (taste) on Sam's lips or teeth instead of the tongue; inside the mouth is fine. Some draw a helper for the wrong sense, such as sunglasses for the ears: ask, "Which part does this keep safe?" Accept any safe idea: ear muffs or covering ears near loud sounds, a tissue for the nose, letting hot food cool for the tongue, soap or mittens for the hands.
notes: Name the pictures together: 1 eye (see), 2 ear (hear), 3 nose (smell), 4 tongue (taste), 5 hand (touch). Sam is a story character; children number Sam, not themselves. Ideas for the helpers: eyes, sunglasses or a hat in bright sun, and never look at the sun; ears, ear muffs or hands over ears near very loud sounds; nose, a tissue to blow it, and nothing goes in the nose; tongue, wait for hot food to cool and brush teeth and tongue; hands, wash with soap, wear mittens when it is cold, use an oven mitt only with a grown-up.

## My five senses
picword:
  - eye | 1 see
  - ear | 2 hear
  - nose | 3 smell
  - tongue | 4 taste
  - hand | 5 touch
draw: Draw Sam. Write 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 on the parts of Sam that see, hear, smell, taste and touch. | 1.9
note: Sam's eyes are 1, ears 2, nose 3, tongue or mouth 4, and hands 5.

## Keep them safe
text: (Read aloud.) Sunglasses keep eyes safe. Draw a helper for each of the other senses.
table: 2 ears | 3 nose | 4 tongue | 5 hands
  height: 1.3
  - | | |
note: Model helpers: ears, ear muffs or hands over the ears; nose, a tissue; tongue, waiting for hot soup to cool; hands, soap and water or mittens.

# 20. Calm Down Corner Menu
minutes: 20
materials: scissors, glue stick, crayons
ican: I can choose a way to calm down when I have big feelings.
directions: (Read aloud.) Say each calm-down picture's name and try it with your body. Cut out the 4 pictures. Glue them onto the calm corner menu. Then circle the picture you will try first.
pairs: calm-corner
mistakes: Some children think the calm corner is a time-out. Say it is a place anyone can choose to go to feel better, and then come back. "Ask for help" is a calm-down choice too; children sometimes think it does not count because it is not something they do alone.
notes: Practice each one before cutting: a deep breath (smell the flower, blow out the candle), count to five on your fingers, hug a stuffed toy, ask a grown-up for help. The menu can hang in your real calm corner. The Calm Corner tool leads breathing and counting on the big screen. Any order on the menu is fine; the circled picture is the child's own choice.

# 21. One Swing, Two Friends
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can take turns with a friend.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture. Two friends want the same thing. Draw how they can take turns.
mistakes: Some children draw one friend playing and the other crying or walking away. Ask, "What does the other friend do while they wait? When is it their turn?" A good drawing shows both friends, one using it and one waiting, or a number or a timer for "next."
notes: Before they draw, explain taking turns: one friend goes first, the other waits and counts to 20, then they switch. Act out one problem first with two children and a real book: one reads while the other counts to 20, then they switch. Words to practice: "Can I have a turn when you're done?" and "You go first, then me." Early finishers can tell a partner what their friends are saying.

## Main: Draw the turns
picword:
  - swing | 1. swing
  - slide | 2. slide
  - book | 3. book
draw: 1. Mia and Leo both want the swing. Draw them taking turns. | 1.05
draw: 2. Sam and Ava both want the slide. Draw them taking turns. | 1.05
draw: 3. Kai and Rosa both want the book. Draw them taking turns. | 1.05

## Stretch: Kind words
choice: (Read aloud.) Which words help friends take turns?
  - "Mine! Go away."
  * "You go first, then me."
  - "I had it first, so you never get it."

# 33. Pip Picks a Goal
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can pick a health goal that fits a problem.
directions: (Read aloud.) Meet Pip the puppy. Say each goal picture's name. Listen to Pip's problem, then circle the goal that helps Pip. Draw Pip doing the goal.
mistakes: Children may circle the goal they like best instead of the one that fits Pip's problem. Read the problem again and ask, "Which picture fixes that?" All four goals are healthy; the right one is the one that matches the problem.
notes: Pip is a story character; keep the talk on Pip. The four goals: drink water, brush teeth, sleep early, play outside. Read the problem: "Pip stays up late playing with toys. Every morning Pip is so sleepy that Pip yawns all day." The answer is sleep early. For the stretch, read: "Pip's friend Dot runs and runs at the park. Now Dot is hot and thirsty."

## Pip's goals
picword:
  - cup | drink water
  - toothbrush | brush teeth
  - pillow | sleep early
  - ball | play outside

## Pip's problem
text: (Read aloud.) Pip stays up late playing with toys. Every morning Pip is so sleepy that Pip yawns all day.
choice: (Read aloud.) Which goal helps Pip? Circle it in the pictures, too.
  - Drink water
  - Brush teeth
  * Sleep early
  - Play outside
draw: Draw Pip doing the goal. | 2.2

## Stretch: Dot's problem
choice: (Read aloud.) Dot runs and runs at the park. Now Dot is hot and thirsty. Which goal helps Dot?
  * Drink water
  - Brush teeth
  - Sleep early
