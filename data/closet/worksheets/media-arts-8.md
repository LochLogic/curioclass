# 1. Exposure Triangle
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can explain how aperture, shutter speed and ISO control light and change the look of a photo.
directions: Complete the chart for the three settings of the exposure triangle. Draw and label the triangle. Solve the stop problems, then choose settings for each photo situation.
mistakes: Students mix up f-numbers: a SMALLER f-number (f/2) means a WIDER opening and more light. Shutter speeds are fractions of a second: 1/1000 is much faster than 1/30. A "stop" doubles or halves the light; to keep the same brightness, a stop more from one setting needs a stop less from another.
notes: Exposure is how bright the photo is. Each setting has a side effect: aperture changes depth of field, shutter speed changes motion blur, and ISO changes noise (grain). Standard full stops: f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11, f/16; shutter 1/30, 1/60, 1/125, 1/250, 1/500, 1/1000; ISO 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600. If cameras are available, students can test the stop problems, but the sheet works on paper alone.

## Warm up: The three settings
table: Setting | What it controls | More light when... | Side effect
  - Aperture | {the size of the lens opening} | {the opening is wider (smaller f-number)} | {depth of field: wider means shallower focus}
  - Shutter speed | {how long the sensor is exposed to light} | {the shutter stays open longer (slower speed)} | {motion blur: slower means more blur}
  - ISO | {how sensitive the sensor is to light} | {the ISO number is higher} | {noise or grain: higher means more grain}

## Main: Draw the triangle
draw: Draw a triangle. Label each corner with a setting and write its side effect next to it. | 2.6

## Main: Stops
example: f/8 to f/5.6 is one stop MORE light (wider opening). To keep the same brightness, make the shutter one stop faster: 1/125 becomes 1/250.
fill:
  - f/2.8 to f/5.6 lets in {2} stops less light.
  - ISO 100 to ISO 800 is {3} stops brighter.
  - Start at f/4, 1/60, ISO 400. You lower ISO to 200. To keep the same brightness, the shutter becomes 1/{30}.
  - Start at f/8, 1/250, ISO 100. You open to f/5.6. To keep the same brightness, the shutter becomes 1/{500}.

## Main: Choose the settings
choice: You want to freeze a soccer player in the middle of a kick on a sunny day. What matters most?
  - A slow shutter, like 1/15
  * A fast shutter, like 1/1000
  - The smallest possible aperture, like f/22
  - The highest ISO
choice: You want a waterfall to look silky and smooth, with the camera on a tripod. What do you choose?
  * A slow shutter, like 1/2 second
  - A fast shutter, like 1/2000
  - A high ISO, like 3200
  - A flash
choice: In a dark gym with no flash, your action photos are blurry. Which change helps, and what is the cost?
  * Raise the ISO; the cost is more grain
  - Slower shutter; the cost is less blur
  - Smaller aperture; the cost is more light
  - Lower the ISO; the cost is a brighter photo
choice: You want a portrait with a soft, blurred background. What do you choose?
  - f/16
  - ISO 3200
  - 1/15 second
  * f/2

## Stretch
write: A friend's night photo of a city street is too dark. Give two different ways to brighten it and the side effect of each.
  lines: 4
  key: Any two: open the aperture (shallower depth of field), use a slower shutter on a tripod (moving cars and people blur into streaks), or raise the ISO (more grain). A strong answer pairs each change with its side effect.

# 21. Where Will It Show?
minutes: 45
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare ways to share media by how many people it reaches and how much control I keep.
directions: Read the four ways to share a student film. Answer the questions. Compare the places, then recommend where to show the film in the scenario and give reasons.
mistakes: Students often think "online" simply means "the most viewers." Many posted videos get few views, and once a video is online, others can copy, share or comment on it, so the maker has less control. Festivals reach fewer people but carefully chosen ones. Check that students consider permission: anyone shown in a film should agree to how it is shared, and schools have rules for showing students.
notes: Reach means how many people, and which people, can see it. Control means how much the maker decides: who watches, when, whether people can comment or copy it, and whether it can be taken down. Policies differ by festival and platform; students should always read the rules. Many festivals ask that a film has not been shown or posted publicly before, so posting first can rule out some festivals.

## Read: Four places to show a film
example: **Film festival:** a judged event; you submit your film and a jury picks what screens. **School broadcast:** the school's morning show or in-school channel. **Library screening:** an event at a public library, open to the community. **Online platform:** a video-sharing site or app where anyone with the link or the app might see it.

## Warm up
choice: Which place gives the maker the most control over who watches and when?
  - An online platform with comments on
  * A library screening the maker helps run
  - A video shared by strangers
  - None of them
choice: Why might posting a film online first be a problem for a student filmmaker?
  - Online videos are always low quality
  - Libraries will not show films
  * Some festivals only accept films that have not been shown publicly
  - It is against the law to post films
choice: Once a video is posted publicly online, what is true?
  * Others may copy, share or comment on it even if you take it down later
  - Deleting it always removes every copy
  - Only your friends can see it
  - It cannot be shared
choice: What should you have before sharing a film that shows other people?
  - A bigger screen
  - A festival award
  * Their permission, and the school's when classmates appear
  - A longer film

## Main: Compare
example: For each place, write its reach (who can see it), who controls it, what it is good for and what to watch out for. **Model, school broadcast:** reach: students and staff at one school; control: the school and the broadcast team decide; good for: quick feedback from peers; watch out for: only one school sees it, and it must follow school rules.
write: **Film festival**
  lines: 2
  key: Reach: a smaller audience of judges, filmmakers and fans, sometimes from far away. Control: the festival decides whether and when it screens. Good for: recognition and feedback from experts. Watch out for: entry rules, deadlines, fees and premiere rules.
write: **Library screening**
  lines: 2
  key: Reach: local families and community members who attend. Control: high; the makers and library choose the time, introduce the film and answer questions. Good for: a live audience and discussion. Watch out for: small audience; needs planning and publicity.
write: **Online platform**
  lines: 2
  key: Reach: possibly very large and worldwide, but many videos get few views. Control: low; comments, copying and sharing are hard to control and deleting does not remove copies. Good for: easy access for anyone, anytime. Watch out for: permission from people shown, privacy, platform rules and festival premiere rules.

## Main: Recommend
example: **Scenario:** Your class made a 6-minute documentary about the school garden. Classmates appear in it, and you want neighbors, families and garden volunteers to see it. You might also enter it in a youth festival next spring.
write: Where should the class show it first? Give at least two reasons using reach and control.
  lines: 5
  key: A strong answer chooses the library screening (or the school broadcast) first: it reaches the community who cares about the garden, the class controls the event, classmates' families can give permission, and it keeps the film unposted so the festival is still possible. A student who picks online should address permission, comments and the festival rule.

## Stretch
write: Design a release plan in three steps for the documentary, from first showing to widest audience.
  lines: 4
  key: Example: 1) school broadcast to classmates for feedback; 2) library screening for families and volunteers; 3) submit to the youth festival, then post online with permission after the festival if its rules allow. Each step widens reach while the class keeps control early.
