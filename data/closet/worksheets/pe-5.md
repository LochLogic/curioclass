# 1. Three Game Families
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can sort games into invasion, net and wall, and striking and fielding, and name the goal of each family.
directions: Use the three questions to help you. Cut out the 12 game cards and glue each under its family. Then write the main goal of each family.
mistakes: Students sometimes sort by the ball (all the games with a bat, all the games with a big ball) instead of by how the game is played. Kickball has no bat but is striking and fielding; volleyball and basketball both use a big ball but belong to different families. Ultimate is invasion even though there is no ball.
notes: Ultimate is the team flying-disc game. Key: Invasion: soccer, basketball, ultimate, field hockey. Net and wall: volleyball, tennis, badminton, pickleball. Striking and fielding: softball, kickball, cricket, baseball. Wall games such as handball and squash also belong to net and wall: players hit the ball against a wall instead of over a net.

## Warm up: Three questions
example: Ask about each game: Do both teams share one space and try to get into the other team's end to score? (invasion) Is there a net or a wall, with each side trying to send the ball where it cannot be returned? (net and wall) Do teams take turns, one striking the ball while the other fields it? (striking and fielding)

## Main: Sort the games
sort: Glue each game card under its family.
  - Invasion: soccer, basketball, ultimate, field hockey
  - Net and wall: volleyball, tennis, badminton, pickleball
  - Striking and fielding: softball, kickball, cricket, baseball

## Main: Name the goal
write: What is the main goal in an invasion game?
  lines: 2
  key: Move the ball (or disc) into the other team's area and score in their goal, end zone or hoop, while stopping them from scoring in yours.
write: What is the main goal in a net and wall game?
  lines: 2
  key: Send the ball or shuttle over the net (or against the wall) so the other side cannot return it, and return every shot that comes to you.
write: What is the main goal in a striking and fielding game?
  lines: 2
  key: The striking team hits (or kicks) the ball and runs to score runs; the fielding team catches and throws to get outs and stop runs.

## Stretch
write: Four square has no net, but players hit the ball into each other's squares. Which family does it fit best? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: Net and wall: players try to send the ball into another square so it cannot be returned, and the lines between squares divide the space like a net.

# 21. Weekly Activity Plan
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can plan a week of activity with at least 60 minutes a day, strength on 3 days and stretching every day.
directions: Check Dani's plan against the three rules. Then plan your own week and check it the same way.
mistakes: Students often count only sports as activity. Walking, dancing, playing tag and climbing all count. Some forget that strength activities count toward the 60 minutes too: they are part of the total, not extra.
notes: The 60 minutes a day and muscle-strengthening on at least 3 days a week come from the US physical activity guidelines for ages 6 to 17; daily stretching is this sheet's added habit. Dani's totals: 60, 60, 45, 65, 60, 70, 60, for 420 minutes. Wednesday is under 60 and has no stretching; Dani has 2 strength days (Tuesday and Thursday) and needs 1 more. A student's plan is their own: no sharing or comparing plans.
