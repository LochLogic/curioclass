# 1. Three Game Families
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can sort games into invasion, net and wall, and striking and fielding, and name the goal of each family.
directions: Use the three questions to help you. Cut out the 12 game cards and glue each under its family. Then write the main goal of each family.
mistakes: Students sometimes sort by the ball (all the games with a bat, all the games with a big ball) instead of by how the game is played. Kickball has no bat but is striking and fielding; volleyball and basketball both use a big ball but belong to different families. Ultimate is invasion even though there is no ball.
notes: Ultimate is the team flying-disc game. Key: Invasion: soccer, basketball, ultimate, field hockey. Net and wall: volleyball, tennis, badminton, pickleball. Striking and fielding: softball, kickball, cricket, baseball. Wall games such as handball and squash also belong to net and wall: players hit the ball against a wall instead of over a net.

## Warm up: Three questions
example: Ask about each game: Do both teams share one space and try to get into the other team's end to score? (invasion) Is there a net or a wall, with each side trying to send the ball where it cannot be returned? (net and wall) Do teams take turns, one striking the ball while the other fields it? (striking and fielding)

## Main: Sort the games
sort: Glue each game card under its family.
  - Invasion: soccer, basketball, ultimate, field hockey
  - Net and wall: volleyball, tennis, badminton, pickleball
  - Striking and fielding: softball, kickball, cricket, baseball

## Main: Name the goal
write: What is the main goal in an invasion game?
  lines: 2
  key: Move the ball (or disc) into the other team's area and score in their goal, end zone or hoop, while stopping them from scoring in yours.
write: What is the main goal in a net and wall game?
  lines: 2
  key: Send the ball or shuttle over the net (or against the wall) so the other side cannot return it, and return every shot that comes to you.
write: What is the main goal in a striking and fielding game?
  lines: 2
  key: The striking team hits (or kicks) the ball and runs to score runs; the fielding team catches and throws to get outs and stop runs.

## Stretch
write: Four square has no net, but players hit the ball into each other's squares. Which family does it fit best? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: Net and wall: players try to send the ball into another square so it cannot be returned, and the lines between squares divide the space like a net.

# 7. Rules Across Game Families
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare the rules of invasion, net and wall, and striking and fielding games.
directions: Read the rule summaries for three made-up games. Each game comes from a different game family. Then answer the questions, using the rules to explain.
mistakes: Students answer from games they know instead of the rules on the page (for example, saying a Wallbounce player scores on every rally because volleyball works that way). Have them underline the sentence that answers each question. Some mix up "rally" and "inning"; a rally is one exchange of hits, an inning is a turn for each team to bat or kick.
notes: Key: 1 C (Wallbounce), 2 A (Crossball), 3 B (Kick and Dash), 4 D (to keep a player from running the ball alone, so the team has to pass and get open). Written answers vary; strong answers use the rules. Crossball is invasion, Wallbounce is net and wall (a wall game), Kick and Dash is striking and fielding. The games are made up for this sheet, so students can test them in class: Crossball needs foam balls and cones for end zones, Wallbounce a playground ball and a wall, Kick and Dash a playground ball and 4 cones.

## Read: Three made-up games
passage: Three Games, Three Families | numbered
  **Crossball.** Two teams of five play on a field with an end zone at each end. Players move a foam ball only by passing. The player holding the ball may take three steps, then must pass. A team scores 1 point when a player catches a pass inside the other team's end zone. Defenders may block or catch passes but may not touch the player with the ball. If a pass hits the ground, the other team takes the ball where it landed. The game has two halves of 10 minutes each.
  **Wallbounce.** Two players share a court in front of a wall. The server hits a playground ball with an open hand so it hits the wall and bounces once inside the court. Then players take turns hitting it. A player loses the rally by missing, letting the ball bounce twice, or hitting it out. Only the server can score. If the server loses a rally, the other player serves next. The first player to 11 points wins.
  **Kick and Dash.** The kicking team kicks a rolled ball and runs around three cones and back to home to score a run. The fielding team gets a runner out by catching a kicked ball before it bounces, or by throwing the ball to the home player before the runner gets there. After three outs, the teams switch. The team with more runs after four innings wins.

## Main: Compare the rules
choice: In which game can only the server score a point?
  - Crossball
  - Kick and Dash
  * Wallbounce
choice: In which game do both teams share the same space and try to get into the other team's end?
  * Crossball
  - Wallbounce
  - Kick and Dash
choice: In which game do teams take turns, one on offense and one on defense?
  - Crossball
  * Kick and Dash
  - Wallbounce
choice: Why does Crossball let the player with the ball take only three steps?
  - To make the game last longer
  - So defenders can tackle the player
  - So there are more points
  * So players must pass and teammates must get open
write: How does each game end? Explain one way this is different across the three games.
  lines: 3
  key: Crossball ends when time runs out (two 10-minute halves); Wallbounce ends when a player reaches 11 points; Kick and Dash ends after four innings. Only Crossball uses a clock, Wallbounce uses a target score, and Kick and Dash uses a set number of turns.
write: Compare how the defense stops scoring in Crossball and in Kick and Dash.
  lines: 3
  key: In Crossball, defenders block or catch passes and guard the end zone, but cannot touch the ball carrier. In Kick and Dash, fielders stop runs by getting outs: catching a kicked ball before it bounces, or throwing to home before the runner gets there.

## Stretch
write: Pick one rule from one game. Change it, and tell how the change would affect the game.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: If Wallbounce used rally scoring, a point would be scored on every rally, so games would be faster and the receiver could score too.

# 20. Components in Sports
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain which fitness components matter most in different sports.
directions: Match each fitness component to what it means. Then rate how much each sport needs each component: 1 = a little, 2 = some, 3 = a lot. Explain one of your ratings.
mistakes: Students often rate every box 3 because athletes are fit. Push them to compare: does a softball fielder breathe hard for as long as a soccer player? Students also mix up muscle strength (a few very hard efforts) and muscle endurance (many repeats or a long effort).
notes: Match key (letters are on the key copy): cardio endurance, heart and lungs during long activity; muscle strength, one very hard effort; muscle endurance, many times without tiring; flexibility, full range of motion. The ratings in the key are a model, not the only right answer; accept any rating the student can explain with what players do. Model reasoning: soccer players run for about 90 minutes (cardio 3); gymnasts hold their body in positions and need wide range of motion (flexibility 3, strength 3); distance swimmers repeat strokes for a long time (cardio and muscle endurance 3); softball has short bursts with rest between (cardio 1); climbers pull their weight up over and over (strength and muscle endurance 3).

## Warm up: The components
match: Match each component to what it means.
  - Cardio endurance = the heart and lungs keep working during long activity
  - Muscle strength = a muscle makes one very hard push, pull or lift
  - Muscle endurance = a muscle works many times without getting tired
  - Flexibility = joints and muscles move through a full range of motion

## Main: Rate the sports
table: Sport | Cardio endurance | Muscle strength | Muscle endurance | Flexibility
  height: 0.45
  - Soccer | {3} | {2} | {3} | {2}
  - Gymnastics | {2} | {3} | {3} | {3}
  - Distance swimming | {3} | {2} | {3} | {2}
  - Softball | {1} | {2} | {1} | {2}
  - Rock climbing | {2} | {3} | {3} | {2}
write: Explain one rating. Tell what players do in that sport that needs the component.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: I gave soccer a 3 for cardio endurance because players run up and down the field for most of the game with few breaks.
write: Which sport in the chart needs cardio endurance the least? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Softball (model answer): most of the game is short sprints and throws with rest between pitches, not long nonstop activity. Accept another sport with a good reason.

## Stretch
write: Pick a sport or activity you like that is not in the chart. Which component matters most for it, and what is one way to build that component?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: Basketball needs cardio endurance because players run up and down the court; I can build it by jogging or playing tag for 20 minutes three times a week.

# 21. Weekly Activity Plan
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can plan a week of activity with at least 60 minutes a day, strength on 3 days and stretching every day.
directions: Check Dani's plan against the three rules. Then plan your own week and check it the same way.
mistakes: Students often count only sports as activity. Walking, dancing, playing tag and climbing all count. Some forget that strength activities count toward the 60 minutes too: they are part of the total, not extra.
notes: The 60 minutes a day and muscle-strengthening on at least 3 days a week come from the US physical activity guidelines for ages 6 to 17; daily stretching is this sheet's added habit. Dani's totals: 60, 60, 45, 65, 60, 70, 60, for 420 minutes. Wednesday is under 60 and has no stretching; Dani has 2 strength days (Tuesday and Thursday) and needs 1 more. A student's plan is their own: no sharing or comparing plans.

# 33. Three-Week Practice Log
minutes: 15 to start, then 5 a day for 3 weeks
materials: pencil, 3 juggling scarves (or squares of light fabric)
ican: I can log my practice, my focus cue and my best streak for three weeks and use them to see my progress.
directions: Read the juggling ladder. Then practice 5 days a week for 3 weeks. Each day, log your minutes, the one focus cue you thought about, your best streak (catches in a row) and the highest rung you reached. Draw a star in the last box on any day you reached a new rung. Total your minutes each week.
mistakes: Students change their focus cue every day and cannot tell what helped. Encourage one cue for several days in a row. Students also forget the star on the day they first pass a rung, or star every day; a star means a new rung only.
notes: The ladder: 1 one scarf, same hand; 2 one scarf, across the body to the other hand; 3 two scarves, toss, toss, catch, catch, once; 4 two scarves for 10 catches; 5 three scarves, toss and catch all three once; 6 three scarves for 10 catches. Good focus cues: toss to the high point above the opposite shoulder; claw the scarf from above; say "toss, toss, catch" out loud; keep hands at waist height. Logs are each student's own record; never post or compare them. Students who also practice on weekends can add those days in the margin. In the reflection, a plateau (several days with the same streak) is normal; the best answers name a change that broke it.

## Warm up: The juggling ladder
table: Rung | What you do
  - 1 | One scarf: toss and catch with the same hand
  - 2 | One scarf: toss across your body and catch with the other hand
  - 3 | Two scarves: toss, toss, catch, catch, one time through
  - 4 | Two scarves: keep the pattern going for 10 catches
  - 5 | Three scarves: toss all three and catch all three, one time through
  - 6 | Three scarves: keep the cascade going for 10 catches
example: Day 1 | 6 minutes | Focus cue: toss to the high point | Best streak: 5 | Rung: 2 | (no star)

write: How many stars did you earn? Which focus cue was working when you reached a new rung?
  lines: 2

  lines: 3
