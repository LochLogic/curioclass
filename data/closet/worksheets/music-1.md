# 1. Ta and Ti-Ti Picture Rhythms
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can read and write ta and ti-ti rhythms.
directions: (Read aloud.) One apple is ta: one sound on a beat. Two cherries are ti-ti: two quick sounds on one beat. Say and clap each row. Then write the sticks under each picture: one stick for ta, two sticks joined at the top for ti-ti.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: Some children draw two separate sticks for ti-ti; the joining line at the top shows the two sounds share one beat, so point it out each time. Watch for children who clap ti-ti slower than ta: keep a steady pat going while they clap so they hear that ti-ti fits inside one beat.
notes: Read each row aloud together first ("ta, ta, ti-ti, ta") while patting the beat, then let students write. The rhythms in order: 1 ta ta ti-ti ta; 2 ti-ti ta ti-ti ta; 3 ta ti-ti ta ta; 4 ti-ti ti-ti ta ta; 5 ta ta ti-ti ti-ti; 6 ti-ti ta ta ti-ti; 7 ta ti-ti ti-ti ta; 8 ti-ti ti-ti ti-ti ta. On the key, a single stick stands for ta and a stick pair joined at the top for ti-ti.

# 7. Four-Beat Rhythm Builder
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue
ican: I can build and clap a four-beat rhythm with ta, ti-ti and rest.
directions: (Read aloud.) Ta is one sound on a beat. Ti-ti is two quick sounds on one beat. Rest is a quiet beat. Plan 2 measures, then cut out the 8 cards and glue 4 cards in each measure box. Clap your rhythm for a partner.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: Some children clap on the rest, or skip the rest and rush to the next card. Have them open their hands silently on each rest so the beat still takes its turn. Watch for ti-ti clapped slowly as two full beats: keep a steady pat going so they hear both sounds fit in one beat.
notes: Every card here lasts one beat, so any 4 cards make a four-beat measure. The plan rules make the choices matter: each measure needs exactly one rest, and measure 2 must be different from measure 1. The card set is 3 ta, 3 ti-ti and 2 rest, so all 8 cards get used. The key shows one possible answer (measure 1: ta ti-ti rest ta; measure 2: ti-ti ta ti-ti rest); any order that follows the rules is correct. Partners clap each other's rhythm twice and fill in the partner check.

## Warm up: How long is each card?
fill:
  - (Read aloud.) Ta lasts {1} beat.
  - Ti-ti has {2} sounds in one beat.
  - A rest is {1} quiet beat.
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one has 4 beats?
  - ta, ti-ti, ta
  * ta, ti-ti, rest, ta
  - ti-ti, ti-ti, rest, ta, ta

## Main: Plan it
text: (Read aloud.) Rules: Put 4 cards in each measure. Put 1 rest in each measure. Make measure 2 different from measure 1. Write your plan first.
chart: Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4
  labels: Measure 1; Measure 2
  height: 0.45
  key: Answers vary. One answer: measure 1 ta, ti-ti, rest, ta; measure 2 ti-ti, ta, ti-ti, rest. Check for 4 cards and 1 rest in each measure, and two different measures.

## Main: Cut and glue
sort: Glue 4 cards in each measure, in the order of your plan.
  - Measure 1: ta, ti-ti, rest, ta
  - Measure 2: ti-ti, ta, ti-ti, rest

## Stretch: Clap it for a partner
text: (Read aloud.) Clap your 2 measures for a partner. Then your partner claps them back. Put a check for your partner.
table: Partner check | Yes | Not yet
  - Kept a steady beat |  |
  - Quiet on each rest |  |
  - Two quick sounds on each ti-ti |  |

# 20. Blow, Buzz, Bow or Hit?
minutes: 20
materials: pencil; pictures or recordings of the 8 instruments
ican: I can tell how an instrument makes its sound.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each instrument. How do you play it to make a sound? Write the number under its picture: 1 blow, 2 buzz lips, 3 bow or pluck, 4 hit.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: Children often write "blow" for the trumpet and trombone. Have them try both: blow gently across their palm, then press their lips together and buzz like a bee. Brass players buzz their lips into the mouthpiece. Watch for the guitar marked "hit": a guitar's strings are plucked with the fingers, like the violin's strings can be.
notes: Answers: flute 1 (blow), drum 4 (hit), trumpet 2 (buzz lips), guitar 3 (pluck), triangle 4 (hit), bassoon 1 (blow), violin 3 (bow), trombone 2 (buzz lips). The flute and bassoon are woodwinds: the player blows air across a hole (flute) or through a double reed (bassoon). The trumpet and trombone are brass: the player buzzes lips into a cup-shaped mouthpiece. Show the bassoon and trombone first, since many children have not seen them. The Orchestra Zoo tool lets the class hear most of these instruments. Stretch: the piano has felt hammers inside that hit its strings when you press a key.

## Warm up: Four ways to play
example: (Read aloud.) 1 blow: blow air into it. 2 buzz lips: press your lips and buzz. 3 bow or pluck: pull a bow across the strings, or pluck them with a finger. 4 hit: hit it with a stick, a beater or your hand.

## Main: Write the number
text: (Read aloud.) Top row: flute, drum, trumpet, guitar. Bottom row: triangle, bassoon, violin, trombone.
picword:
  - flute | {1}
  - drum | {4}
  - trumpet | {2}
  - guitar | {3}
picword:
  - triangle-instrument | {4}
  - bassoon | {1}
  - violin | {3}
  - trombone | {2}

## Stretch: The piano puzzle
choice: (Read aloud.) Press a piano key, and a little felt hammer inside moves to the string. How does the piano make its sound?
  - blow
  - buzz lips
  * hit

# 21. Family Sound Secret Word
minutes: 20
materials: pencil; recordings of violin, trumpet, flute, tambourine, cello and tuba
ican: I can tell which instrument family I hear.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each sound. Circle the letter under the family that is playing. Write that letter in the last box of the row. The letters spell a secret word.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: The cello and the tuba are both low, so children often guess the same family for both. Ask "Is it a bow on strings, or lips buzzing into metal?" Flute is a woodwind even though most flutes are metal: it counts because the player blows across a hole, not into a cup with buzzing lips.
notes: Play the sounds in this order: clip 1 violin (strings, H), clip 2 trumpet (brass, O), clip 3 flute (woodwinds, O), clip 4 tambourine (percussion, R), clip 5 cello (strings, A), clip 6 tuba (brass, Y). The secret word is HOORAY. Every one of these is in the Orchestra Zoo tool. Read the family names and the warm-up matches aloud. The sheet uses a letter grid with a family in each column, so students circle a letter instead of a word.

## Warm up: How do they make sound?
match: Match each family to how it makes sound.
  - Strings = bow or pluck the strings
  - Woodwinds = blow air into a tube
  - Brass = buzz your lips into a metal cup
  - Percussion = hit, shake or scrape it

## Main: Circle and write
table: Clip | Strings | Woodwinds | Brass | Percussion | My letter
  - 1 | H | M | T | P | {H}
  - 2 | L | E | O | S | {O}
  - 3 | K | O | N | D | {O}
  - 4 | B | U | I | R | {R}
  - 5 | A | W | C | F | {A}
  - 6 | J | G | Y | V | {Y}
text: The secret word is {HOORAY}.

## Stretch: Draw it
draw: Draw a person playing an instrument from your favorite family. Write the family name. | 2

# 33. New Verses for Old MacDonald
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can make up new verses for a song.
directions: (Read aloud.) Old MacDonald had a farm. Who else could live there? Draw 3 new animals. Write the sound each one makes. Then sing your new verses with the class.
mistakes: Children often draw an animal but forget its sound, or pick a sound that is hard to say twice ("cock-a-doodle-doo" in "with a ___ ___ here"). Any sound is fine; help them clap it to see how it fits. Accept any spelling that captures the sound (woof, ruff, bow-wow).
notes: Old MacDonald Had a Farm is a traditional song, free to sing and share. The pattern for each verse: "Old MacDonald had a farm, E-I-E-I-O. And on that farm he had a (animal), E-I-E-I-O. With a (sound) (sound) here and a (sound) (sound) there, here a (sound), there a (sound), everywhere a (sound) (sound). Old MacDonald had a farm, E-I-E-I-O." Animals do not have to be farm animals: a lion or a whale makes a funny verse. Sing one model verse with the cow first, then sing each child's verses, pointing to their pictures.

## Warm up: Who says what?
text: (Read aloud.) Write the sound each animal makes. Word bank: moo, woof, meow, ribbit.
picword:
  - cow | {moo}
  - dog | {woof}
  - cat | {meow}
  - frog | {ribbit}

## Main: My new verses
text: (Read aloud.) Draw a new animal in each row. Write its name and its sound. Sing: "And on that farm he had a (animal). With a (sound) (sound) here..."
chart: Draw the animal | Its name | Its sound
  labels: Verse 1; Verse 2; Verse 3
  height: 1.2
  key: Answers vary. Each row has an animal picture, its name and a sound, for example pig, oink; duck, quack; sheep, baa.

## Stretch: A long sound
fill: (Read aloud.) A rooster says cock-a-doo-dle-doo. Clap it. How many claps? {5}
