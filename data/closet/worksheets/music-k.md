# 1. Heartbeat Hearts
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can keep a steady beat.
directions: (Read aloud.) Music has a heartbeat called the beat. Touch one heart for each beat while we sing. Then listen to my claps and mark the hearts.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: Many children touch one heart per word instead of per beat, so they run out of hearts early or rush through "star." Model "star" as a held note that still gets two beats, and keep a pat on your lap they can watch. Children who mark X's far ahead of the claps are guessing: slow down and clap with a big, visible motion.
notes: Each line of Twinkle Twinkle Little Star (a traditional melody; the words are Jane Taylor's 1806 poem "The Star," public domain) is 8 beats long, so one row of 8 hearts fits one line. Sing the first line slowly and touch the hearts with the class before they try alone. For the clapping part, clap 8 slow even claps, then 8 faster even claps. Keep the beat steady: the goal is even spacing, not speed.

## Warm up: Sing and touch
text: (Read aloud.) Sing the first line of Twinkle Twinkle Little Star. Touch one heart for each beat. Count the hearts. Write the number.
count: Sing line 1. Touch the hearts.
  - 8 heart

## Main: Mark the claps
text: (Read aloud.) Listen. I will clap slowly. Draw an X on one heart for each clap. How many claps? Write the number.
count: Slow claps
  - 8 heart
text: (Read aloud.) Now I will clap faster. Draw an X on one heart for each clap. Write the number.
count: Fast claps
  - 8 heart

## Stretch: Your own beat
draw: Draw 4 hearts in a row. Pat each one as you say "1, 2, 3, 4." | 1.0

# 7. Sleeping Cat Quiet Beat
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can keep a steady beat and stay quiet on a rest.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each heart is a clap. The sleeping cat is a quiet beat: we do not clap, so we do not wake the cat. Find the cat in each row and circle it. Then we will clap each row together.
pairs: beat-buddies
mistakes: Children often stop the beat on the cat instead of keeping it going: the quiet beat still takes its turn. Have them open their hands silently or touch a finger to their lips on the cat so the beat keeps moving. Watch for children who clap on the cat out of habit: say "shh" softly on that beat the first few times.
notes: The sleeping cat stands for a rest: a beat with no sound. Before the sheet, keep a slow steady pat on your lap and say "clap, clap, clap, shh." Read each row left to right, pointing to each picture on the beat. The cat is on beat 4 in row 1, beat 2 in row 2, beat 3 in row 3 and beat 1 in row 4. Clap each row 2 times without stopping, then try all 4 rows in a row while you keep the pat going.

# 20. Metal, Wood or Skin?
minutes: 15
materials: pencil; a screen or box to hide instruments behind; a triangle, finger cymbals, a wood block, rhythm sticks, a hand drum and a big drum
ican: I can tell if a sound was made by metal, wood or skin.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each hidden sound. Was it metal, wood or skin? Put an X in the box under the right picture.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: Children often guess by loudness: a loud sound is "drum" and a soft one is "metal." Point out what to listen for: metal rings on after you hit it, wood makes a short dry click, and skin makes a low thump. Watch for children who mark two boxes in one row; play the sound again.
notes: Hide the instruments behind a screen or inside a big box. Before you start, show all three families and play each one: metal rings (triangle), wood clicks (wood block), skin thumps (hand drum). Play the sounds in this order: 1 triangle (metal), 2 hand drum (skin), 3 wood block (wood), 4 finger cymbals (metal), 5 rhythm sticks (wood), 6 big drum (skin). Play each sound 3 times. If you are missing one, use another of the same material: jingle bells or a cowbell for metal, claves or a guiro for wood, bongos or a frame drum for skin. Drumheads were once made of animal skin; many today are plastic, but music teachers still call drums "skins."

# 21. Who Is Playing?
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons; recordings or real instruments (piano, violin, drum, flute, trumpet, triangle)
ican: I can name instruments by their sound.
directions: (Read aloud.) Say the name of each instrument with me. Then listen to each sound. Find the instrument that made it. Write the sound's number under its picture.
pairs: orchestra-zoo
mistakes: Flute and violin are the pair children mix up most, because both can play high, smooth notes. Before the sheet, play the two side by side and ask, "Is it blown or bowed?" A drum and a triangle are both tapped: listen for a thud or a ring.
notes: Play the sounds in this order: 1 drum, 2 flute, 3 piano, 4 trumpet, 5 triangle, 6 violin. Play each sound for about 10 seconds, then play it once more. The Orchestra Zoo tool has real recordings of the piano, violin, flute and trumpet; tap a real drum and triangle if you have them. The sheet asks students to write the number under each picture instead of drawing lines from a list of sounds, which is easier for young children to keep straight.

## Warm up: Name them
example: (Read aloud.) This is a drum. You hit it. Say "drum" with me.

  - violin | {6}
  - drum | {1}
  - flute | {2}
  - trumpet | {4}
  - triangle-instrument | {5}

# 33. New Words for Hot Cross Buns
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can change the words of a song to make a new verse.
directions: (Read aloud.) Hot cross buns has 3 claps: hot, cross, buns. Say each food and clap it. Count the claps. Then draw a new food in each box and sing your new verses with the class.
mistakes: Children often pick a favorite food with 1 or 2 claps ("pizza") and then squeeze or stretch it to fit. That is fine to sing, but help them hear that 3 claps fit best: "ba-na-nas" fits just like "hot cross buns." Watch for children who change the "one a penny" line too; keep it the same so everyone can sing along.
notes: Hot Cross Buns is a traditional English street-cry song, free to sing and share. Sing it first: "Hot cross buns! Hot cross buns! One a penny, two a penny, hot cross buns!" The new food goes in all 3 places the buns were. Clap the food names together: ap-ple (2), ba-na-nas (3), grapes (1), birth-day cake (3). Any food can make a verse; 3-clap foods fit the rhythm exactly (more: "pea-nut soup," "cin-na-mon," "or-ange juice"). Sing the new verses as a class, pointing to each child's picture.

## Warm up: Clap the food
text: (Read aloud.) Say and clap: ap-ple, ba-na-nas, grapes, birth-day cake. Write how many claps under each food.
picword:
  - apple | {2}
  - banana | {3}
  - grapes | {1}
  - cake | {3}

## Main: Draw new foods
text: (Read aloud.) Draw a new food in each box. Sing: "(food)! (food)! One a penny, two a penny, (food)!"
