# 1. Four Feeling Faces: Happy, Sad, Mad, Scared
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons, a hand mirror if you have one
ican: I can name the feelings happy, sad, mad and scared.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each face and say its feeling. Trace each feeling word. Then draw a mouth on each blank face to match the word under it.
mistakes: Mad and sad faces get mixed up because both mouths turn down. Point to the eyebrows: mad eyebrows slant down toward the nose, and sad eyebrows lift in the middle. Some children draw a smile on every face; have them make the face themselves first, then draw what their mouth did.
notes: Make each face together before drawing: "Show me happy. Now show me scared." A mirror helps children see their own mouth and eyebrows change. Any mouth that clearly fits the feeling is right: a scared mouth can be an open circle or a wobbly line. All feelings are okay to have; the sheet is about naming them.

## Warm up: Say the feeling
text: (Read aloud.) Point to each face. Say its feeling word with me.
picword:
  - face-happy | happy
  - face-sad | sad
  - face-mad | mad
  - face-scared | scared

## Main: Trace the words
trace: happy sad mad scared

## Draw: Finish the faces
write: (Read aloud.) Draw the mouth on each face. Look at the word under the face.
  lines: 0
  key: happy: a smile that curves up. sad: a frown that curves down (a tear is a bonus). mad: a tight mouth or frown, with eyebrows slanting down. scared: an open round mouth or a wobbly line, with wide eyes.
picword:
  - face-blank | happy
  - face-blank | sad
  - face-blank | mad
  - face-blank | scared

## Stretch: A feeling for a puppy
draw: (Read aloud.) A puppy lost its ball. Draw the puppy's face. | 1.6

# 21. Calm Corner Picture Hunt
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can find things that help my body feel calm.
pairs: calm-corner
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each thing in our calm corner hunt. Circle the things that help a body feel calm. Put an X on the things that are loud. Then draw one more thing a calm corner could use.
mistakes: Some children circle everything they like, including the drum. Ask "Is it quiet or loud? Would it help a friend slow down?" A child who says a soft bell could be calm is thinking well; accept the X or the circle if they can say why.
notes: Use after the Calm Corner tool. Say each picture name together first: pillow, book, teddy bear, drum, sand timer, flower, bell. The sand timer is for waiting a few minutes until you feel calm. This sheet shows the things as separate pictures, not one drawing of a whole corner; if you have a real calm corner, walk over and find each thing there too. For the stretch, good ideas are a soft blanket, a breathing star, a squishy ball, a feelings chart or crayons and paper.

## Warm up: What is a calm corner?
text: (Read aloud.) A calm corner is a quiet spot in our room. We go there to slow down when big feelings come. What do you think we find there?

## Main: Circle the calm things
write: (Read aloud.) Circle the calm things. Put an X on the loud things.
  lines: 0
  key: Circle: pillow, book, teddy bear, sand timer, flower. Put an X on: drum, bell.
picword:
  - pillow | pillow
  - book | book
  - teddy-bear | teddy bear
  - drum | drum
  - sand-timer | sand timer
  - flower | flower
  - bell | bell

## Stretch: Add one more
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw one more thing a calm corner could use. | 1.8
