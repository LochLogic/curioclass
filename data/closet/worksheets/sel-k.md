# 1. Four Feeling Faces: Happy, Sad, Mad, Scared
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons, a hand mirror if you have one
ican: I can name the feelings happy, sad, mad and scared.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each face and say its feeling. Trace each feeling word. Then draw a mouth on each blank face to match the word under it.
mistakes: Mad and sad faces get mixed up because both mouths turn down. Point to the eyebrows: mad eyebrows slant down toward the nose, and sad eyebrows lift in the middle. Some children draw a smile on every face; have them make the face themselves first, then draw what their mouth did.
notes: Make each face together before drawing: "Show me happy. Now show me scared." A mirror helps children see their own mouth and eyebrows change. Any mouth that clearly fits the feeling is right: a scared mouth can be an open circle or a wobbly line. All feelings are okay to have; the sheet is about naming them.

## Warm up: Say the feeling
text: (Read aloud.) Point to each face. Say its feeling word with me.

  - face-sad | sad

# 7. Same Rain, Two Feelings
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell that two friends can feel different ways about the same thing.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each story. Circle how each of the two friends feels.
mistakes: Children often circle the same feeling for both friends, the one they would feel. Ask "Does Duck like rain? Does a picnic need sun?" For story 4, some children say Cat is sad; a sleepy cat woken up can be sad or mad, so accept either if the child can say why.
notes: Read each story aloud, then read the two words under each friend and point to them. Act out the feeling faces first: show me happy, sad, mad, scared. The big idea: the same thing can make two friends feel different ways, and both feelings are okay.

# 20. Count to Five Hand
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can count slowly to five to help my body calm down.
directions: (Read aloud.) Put your pencil at the bottom of the thumb. Trace up and down each finger as we count slowly together: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Trace the numbers. Then draw a calm face.
mistakes: Children race through the count. Model a slow count with one breath for each finger: "Oooone... twooo..." Some children trace the numbers before the hand; the hand comes first so the counting and the tracing go together.
notes: Counting slowly is a calm-down tool children can use anywhere: in line, at the table, before a hard job. Warm up first: hold up one hand, touch each finger and count slowly together, breathing in and out on each number. Then do the paper hand. A calm face can have a soft smile, a flat mouth or closed eyes; any face that looks quiet and relaxed is right. The numbers 1 and 5 printed under the hand show where to start and stop.

# 21. Calm Corner Picture Hunt
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can find things that help my body feel calm.
pairs: calm-corner
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each thing in our calm corner hunt. Circle the things that help a body feel calm. Put an X on the things that are loud. Then draw one more thing a calm corner could use.
mistakes: Some children circle everything they like, including the drum. Ask "Is it quiet or loud? Would it help a friend slow down?" A child who says a soft bell could be calm is thinking well; accept the X or the circle if they can say why.
notes: Use after the Calm Corner tool. Say each picture name together first: pillow, book, teddy bear, drum, sand timer, flower, bell. The sand timer is for waiting a few minutes until you feel calm. This sheet shows the things as separate pictures, not one drawing of a whole corner; if you have a real calm corner, walk over and find each thing there too. For the stretch, good ideas are a soft blanket, a breathing star, a squishy ball, a feelings chart or crayons and paper.

## Warm up: What is a calm corner?
text: (Read aloud.) A calm corner is a quiet spot in our room. We go there to slow down when big feelings come. What do you think we find there?

## Main: Circle the calm things
write: (Read aloud.) Circle the calm things. Put an X on the loud things.

# 33. Kind Heart or Unkind Cloud?
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can tell kind actions from unkind actions on the playground.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen as I read each card. Cut out the cards. Glue each kind action under the kind heart. Glue each unkind action under the unkind cloud.
mistakes: Some children sort by whether an action sounds fun ("grabs the swing" means I get to swing) rather than whether it helps or hurts a friend. Ask "How would your friend feel?" Children who cannot yet read the cards can sort by remembering what you acted out; say each card again as they glue.
notes: The cards are words, not pictures, so read each card aloud and act it out before children cut: "shares the ball" (hand a ball over), "pushes in line" (a pretend push). Kind: shares the ball, helps a friend up, takes turns on the slide, asks "Want to play?" Unkind: pushes in line, grabs the swing, says "You can't play," laughs when a friend falls.

## Warm up: Heart and cloud
picword:
  - heart | kind
  - cloud | unkind
text: (Read aloud.) A kind action makes a friend feel good and safe. An unkind action makes a friend feel sad or hurt.

## Main: Cut and glue
sort:
  - Kind heart: shares the ball; helps a friend up; takes turns on the slide; asks "Want to play?"
  - Unkind cloud: pushes in line; grabs the swing; says "You can't play."; laughs when a friend falls

## Stretch
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw one kind thing you can do on the playground. | 1.0
