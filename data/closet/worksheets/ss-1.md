# 1. Big Question, Small Questions Kite
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can ask small questions that help answer a big question.
directions: (Read aloud.) Our big question is on the kite: How do people get food? A big question needs small questions to help answer it. Draw or tell 3 small questions for the kite's tail.
mistakes: Children often ask questions that are about food but do not help answer the big question ("What is your favorite food?"). Ask: "If we found the answer, would it help us know how people get food?" Some children give an answer ("From the store!") instead of a question; turn it into one together: "Where do stores get food?"
notes: Do the warm up together, reading each question aloud and letting children vote with a thumb. Children who are not writing yet can draw each small question (a farm, a truck, a store) and dictate the words to you. Keep the class kite questions on chart paper; they guide the rest of the Wonder Kite module.

## Warm up: Does it help?
picword:
  - kite | How do people get food?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which small question helps answer the big question?
  - What color is your lunch box?
  * Where do farmers grow food?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which small question helps answer the big question?
  * How does food get to the store?
  - Do you like to fly a kite?

## Main: The kite's tail
draw: Tail bow 1: draw your small question. | 1.4
write: Tell or write tail question 1.
  lines: 1
  key: Any question that helps answer the big question, such as "Who grows our food?"
draw: Tail bow 2: draw your small question. | 1.4
write: Tell or write tail question 2.
  lines: 1
  key: For example, "How do trucks bring food to the store?"
draw: Tail bow 3: draw your small question. | 1.4
write: Tell or write tail question 3.
  lines: 1
  key: For example, "How do people pay for food?" or "How did people get food long ago?"

## Stretch: Who could help?
write: (Read aloud.) Who could we ask to answer one of our small questions?
  lines: 1
  key: A farmer, a grocery store worker, a truck driver, a cook, a librarian or a book about farms.

# 22. Hammy the Hamster's First Year
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can read a timeline and tell what happened before and after.
directions: (Read aloud.) This timeline shows Hammy the class hamster's first year. Read it from left to right. Then listen to each question and circle the answer.
mistakes: Children may read the timeline as a set of separate pictures. Run a finger along it left to right and say "first... next... then..." together. "Before" and "after" get mixed up: anchor with a known pair, "We eat lunch before we go home."
notes: Hammy is a made-up class pet, so the sheet needs no family or personal history. The answer words match the words under the pictures; children who are not reading yet can find the matching picture and circle the word next to it with your help. The timeline reads left to right, starting in June.

## Warm up: Read the timeline
text: (Read aloud.) Hammy's first year, from June to the next June. Put your finger on June. Slide to the right, then go down to the next row, just like reading a book.
picword:
  - hamster | June: Hammy is born
  - school | August: Hammy comes to our class
  - wheel | October: Hammy gets a wheel
  - book | January: we read a hamster book
  - cake | June: Hammy is 1!

## Main: Before or after?
choice: (Read aloud.) What happened first?
  * Hammy is born
  - Hammy gets a wheel
choice: (Read aloud.) Did Hammy come to our class before or after he was born?
  - before
  * after
choice: (Read aloud.) What happened right after Hammy came to our class?
  * Hammy gets a wheel
  - Hammy is 1!
choice: (Read aloud.) Did we read the hamster book before or after Hammy got his wheel?
  - before
  * after
choice: (Read aloud.) What happened last?
  * Hammy is 1!
  - we read a hamster book

## Stretch: What comes next?
draw: Draw something Hammy might do after his first birthday. | 1.8
