# 1. Two-Step Directions
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons (yellow and red)
ican: I can listen to a direction with two steps and do both steps in order.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the picture page. Listen to each direction. Do the first step. Then do the second step.
mistakes: Many children do only the first step, or only the last one they heard. Hold up one finger for "first" and two fingers for "then" as you read, and have students repeat the steps back before they start. Watch for "under" and "next to" mixed up; point to the line under a word on the board.
notes: Name the eight pictures together first (cat, dog, fish, sun, tree, house, ball, bird). Read each direction twice, then wait. The bird is not used in any direction: it is a check that students are listening rather than marking every picture. For a quick check, walk the room after each direction. Early finishers can do the Stretch with a partner.

## Warm up: First, then
example: (Read aloud.) "Touch your nose. Then clap two times."
  **First** step: touch your nose. **Then** step: clap two times. Do it with your teacher!
text: **Word bank:** first, then, circle, draw, color, put an X, line, on, under, next to, from, to

## Picture page
picword:
  - cat
  - dog
  - fish
  - sun
  - tree
  - house
  - ball
  - bird

## Main: Listen and do
write: (Read aloud.) Circle the cat. Then draw a hat on it.
  lines: 0
  key: The cat is circled and has a hat drawn on its head.
write: (Read aloud.) Put an X on the fish. Then draw two bubbles next to it.
  lines: 0
  key: The fish has an X and two small circles (bubbles) beside it.
write: (Read aloud.) Color the sun yellow. Then draw a smile on it.
  lines: 0
  key: The sun is yellow with a smile drawn in its middle.
write: (Read aloud.) Draw a line from the dog to the ball. Then circle the ball.
  lines: 0
  key: A line joins the dog and the ball, and the ball is circled.
write: (Read aloud.) Draw three apples on the tree. Then color the apples red.
  lines: 0
  key: Three red apples are on the tree.
write: (Read aloud.) Draw a flower next to the house. Then draw a line under the house.
  lines: 0
  key: A flower beside the house and a line under the house picture. The bird has no marks.

## Stretch: Your turn
write: (Read aloud.) Make up a two-step direction for a partner. Say it, then write it.
  lines: 2
  starter: First, {circle} the ... Then ...
  key: Answers vary. Example: "First, circle the bird. Then draw a worm next to it." It names two actions in order.

# 21. Math Talk Check
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare numbers, tell about shapes and explain how I solved a story problem.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each question. Use the words in the word bank to fill in the frames. Say each answer in a full sentence.
mistakes: Children often read "greater than" as "bigger number first" and write it backward when the small number comes first (6 and 16). Have them point to the first number and ask, "Is this one more or less?" Some children say a circle has 1 side; talk about straight sides and corners.
notes: Read every frame aloud, then have students say the whole sentence to a partner. Item 4 (20 and 20) checks "equal to". For the story problem, act it out with 9 counters. The circle has no straight sides and no corners, so 0 is the answer for both.

## Warm up: Compare
text: **Word bank:** greater than, less than, equal to, sides, corners, add, take away, in all, more, left
example: 12 is **greater than** 9. 12 is more.
fill:
  - 15 is {greater than} 8.
  - 6 is {less than} 16.
  - 31 is {greater than} 13.
  - 20 is {equal to} 20.

## Main: Shapes
picword:
  - triangle | {3} sides
  - square | {4} sides
  - rectangle | {4} sides
  - circle | {0} sides
write: (Read aloud.) How are a square and a rectangle the same?
  lines: 1
  starter: They both have {4} sides and {4} corners.
  key: They both have 4 sides and 4 corners.
write: (Read aloud.) How is a triangle different from a square?
  lines: 1
  starter: A triangle has {3} sides, but a square has {4} sides.
  key: A triangle has 3 sides (and 3 corners), but a square has 4 sides (and 4 corners).

## Main: Story problem
count: (Read aloud.) Mia has 6 fish. She gets 3 more fish. How many fish does Mia have in all?
  - 6 fish
  - 3 fish
fill: 6 + 3 = {9}
write: (Read aloud.) Explain how you solved it.
  lines: 2
  starter: I {added} because Mia got {more} fish. Mia has {9} fish in all.
  key: I added because Mia got more fish. 6 + 3 = 9, so Mia has 9 fish in all.

## Stretch: Take away
write: (Read aloud.) Tom has 10 apples. He gives away 4. How many apples are left? Explain.
  lines: 2
  starter: I {took away} because Tom gave some away. 10 - 4 = {6}.
  key: I took away because Tom gave some away. 10 - 4 = 6, so Tom has 6 apples left.
