# 1. Alcohol's Path Through the Body
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, colored pencil
ican: I can trace how alcohol moves through the body and explain its effects.
directions: Read about alcohol's path. Put the five stops in order on the path line. Match each stop to what happens there. Then draw the path on the body outline and answer the questions.
mistakes: Students often think the stomach digests alcohol like food. Alcohol is not digested; it is absorbed straight into the blood, mostly in the small intestine. Many believe coffee, a cold shower or exercise can sober someone up. Only time lets the liver break alcohol down.
notes: Facts follow the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA) and the CDC. About 20% of alcohol is absorbed through the stomach and most of the rest through the small intestine; blood from the gut passes through the liver first, but the liver can only break down a small amount at a time, so the rest travels on to the heart and brain. The brain keeps developing into the mid-20s. The legal drinking age in the U.S. is 21. If a student shares a family situation, follow up privately and offer the school counselor.

## Warm up: The trip
text: Alcohol is a drug that slows down the brain. It is not digested like food: it soaks straight into the blood. Blood from the gut always passes through the liver before it travels on to the heart and the rest of the body, but the liver can only break down a little alcohol at a time. Think about where a drink goes after it is swallowed.

## Main: Put the stops in order
timeline: 1 5 1
  - 1 | Mouth and throat
  - 2 | Stomach
  - 3 | Small intestine and bloodstream
  - 4 | Liver
  - 5 | Brain

## Main: What happens at each stop?
match: Match each stop to what happens there.
  - Mouth and throat = Alcohol passes through and can irritate the lining
  - Stomach = About 20% is absorbed; food in the stomach slows this down
  - Small intestine and bloodstream = Most is absorbed and carried to every organ
  - Liver = Breaks alcohol down slowly, at a steady rate; heavy drinking can scar it
  - Brain = Slows reaction time, judgment, coordination and memory
draw: Draw a simple body outline. Draw alcohol's path with arrows and label the five stops. | 2.4

## Think about it
choice: What is the only thing that lowers the amount of alcohol in someone's blood?
  - Drinking coffee
  - Taking a cold shower
  * Time, while the liver breaks it down
choice: Why is alcohol especially risky for teens?
  - Teens' livers do not work at all
  * The teen brain is still developing until about age 25, so alcohol can harm learning and memory
  - Alcohol has no effect on teens
write: Alcohol slows down the brain. Name two ways that could lead to an injury.
  lines: 3
  key: Slower reaction time and poor coordination can cause falls, crashes or drowning; poor judgment can lead to risky choices like riding with an impaired driver.

## Stretch
write: Jordan's cousin says she will "sleep it off" after drinking at a party. Using what you know about the liver, why does that not make her safe to drive in the morning?
  lines: 3
  key: The liver breaks down alcohol at a steady, slow rate. If she drank a lot, there may still be alcohol in her blood in the morning, so her reactions and judgment could still be impaired. Only enough time clears it.

# 21. Kinds of Boundaries
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can name different kinds of boundaries and respect other people's boundaries.
directions: A boundary is a limit a person sets to feel safe and respected. Cut out the 12 cards. Glue each one under physical, emotional, digital or time boundaries. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Digital and physical boundaries get mixed up when a phone is involved ("Don't take my phone" is about belongings, so physical; "Don't post my photo" is digital). Some boundaries fit two columns; accept a second answer with a good reason.
notes: Every card is said by a story character, so no student needs to share their own boundaries. Stress that boundaries go both ways: we set our own and respect others'. A healthy friend or partner accepts a boundary without guilt-tripping. If someone keeps crossing a boundary, students should tell a trusted adult.

## Warm up
choice: Which is the best example of setting a boundary?
  - "You're so annoying. Go away forever."
  * "I'm not okay with tickling. Please stop."
  - Saying nothing and hoping it stops

## Main: Sort the boundaries
sort: Glue each card under its kind of boundary.
  - Physical: "I'd rather high-five than hug.", "Please knock before coming in my room.", "Ask before you borrow my things."
  - Emotional: "I'm not ready to talk about that yet.", "Please don't joke about my grades.", "I need some quiet time before we talk this out."
  - Digital: "Ask before you post a photo of me.", "Please don't read my messages.", "I don't share my passwords."
  - Time: "I can't hang out until my homework is done.", "I need to leave by 5:00.", "I can help with your project for one hour on Saturday."

## Respecting boundaries
write: Eli tells his friend Sam, "Please don't post that video of me." Write what Sam could say to respect Eli's boundary.
  lines: 2
  key: "Okay, I won't post it. Thanks for telling me." Any reply that agrees without arguing, teasing or guilt-tripping.
write: Lena keeps texting Priya late at night, and Priya is tired. Write a time boundary Priya could set, using a calm and kind voice.
  lines: 2
  key: "I really like talking to you, but I turn my phone off at 9:00 on school nights. Let's talk tomorrow."

## Stretch
write: Priya set her boundary, but Lena keeps texting late and says, "If you were a real friend, you'd answer." What should Priya do next?
  lines: 3
  key: Repeat the boundary calmly and keep it ("I'll answer in the morning"). Guilt-tripping is not respecting a boundary. If it keeps happening, she can take a break from the friendship and talk to a trusted adult.
