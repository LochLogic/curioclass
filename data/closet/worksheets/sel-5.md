# 1. Empathy or Sympathy?
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell empathy from sympathy and respond to someone with empathy.
directions: Read the scene. Label each of the ten responses empathy or sympathy. Then rewrite three sympathy responses so they show empathy.
mistakes: Students sort by how nice a response sounds. "I feel so sorry for you" sounds kind, but it looks at Jada from the outside. Watch for "at least": it tries to make the feeling smaller, which is a sign of sympathy, not empathy.
notes: Definitions used on this sheet: sympathy is feeling sorry for someone from the outside; empathy is trying to understand and share what they feel from the inside. Sympathy is not bad, but empathy helps people feel heard. Teachers and writers draw this line in slightly different places; this sheet uses the version above, so check the sort against it. The scene is about a character; students do not share their own stories.

## Warm up
example: **Empathy** is feeling **with** someone: "That sounds hard. I'm here." **Sympathy** is feeling sorry **for** someone: "Oh, poor you."
text: **The scene:** Jada practiced for weeks, but she did not get a part in the school play. She tells her friends at lunch.

## Main: Label the responses
table: # | Response | Empathy or sympathy?
  - 1 | "That's hard. Do you want to tell me about it?" | {empathy }
  - 2 | "Oh you poor thing." | {sympathy}
  - 3 | "I can tell this mattered to you a lot." | {empathy }
  - 4 | "At least you get more free time now." | {sympathy}
  - 5 | "You practiced so hard. I'd feel let down too." | {empathy }
  - 6 | "I feel so sorry for you." | {sympathy}
  - 7 | "It makes sense that you feel upset." | {empathy }
  - 8 | "Well, at least there's always next year." | {sympathy}
  - 9 | "That sounds really disappointing. I'm here if you want to talk." | {empathy }
  - 10 | "Aww, that's too bad for you." | {sympathy}

## Stretch: Make it empathy
write: Rewrite response 4, "At least you get more free time now," so it shows empathy.
  lines: 2
  key: You were really looking forward to being in the play. It's okay to feel let down.
write: Rewrite response 2, "Oh you poor thing," so it shows empathy.
  lines: 2
  key: That sounds really painful. Do you want to talk about it?
write: Rewrite response 8, "Well, at least there's always next year," so it shows empathy.
  lines: 2
  key: You worked so hard for this. I'd be disappointed too. I'm here for you.

# 21. Short-Term and Long-Term Goals
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell short-term goals from long-term goals and plan steps toward a big goal.
directions: Read the ten goals of Nia, a fifth grader. Label each one short-term or long-term. Then fill the chart: link the short-term steps to the long-term goal they help, and add one step of your own to each.
mistakes: Students judge size instead of time ("running is big, so it's long-term"). The test is time: can it be done in about a month or less? Watch for the leftover goal: "Clean out my backpack tonight" is short-term but does not help any of the three big goals.
notes: Definitions used here: a short-term goal can be done in about a month or less; a long-term goal takes many months or more. Goals belong to the character Nia, so no one shares their own. Steps 1 and 2 can go in either order. Step 3 answers vary; the key gives one model. Strong third steps are specific and soon: "Run 15 minutes on Saturday" beats "Run more."

## Warm up
example: Long-term goal: **Learn to swim across the pool by summer.** Short-term steps: **Practice floating on Monday.** **Kick with a board three times this week.**

## Main: Label Nia's goals
table: # | Nia's goal | Short-term or long-term?
  - 1 | Run for 10 minutes three times this week | {short-term}
  - 2 | Run a 5K race next spring | {long-term }
  - 3 | Get a library card this weekend | {short-term}
  - 4 | Practice the G chord every day this week | {short-term}
  - 5 | Read 40 books this school year | {long-term }
  - 6 | Clean out my backpack tonight | {short-term}
  - 7 | Finish my chapter book by Sunday | {short-term}
  - 8 | Play 10 songs on the guitar by next summer | {long-term }
  - 9 | Find a running buddy by Friday | {short-term}
  - 10 | Learn one new song by the end of the month | {short-term}

## Link the steps
table: Long-term goal | Step 1 (from the list) | Step 2 (from the list) | Step 3 (your own idea)
  - Run a 5K race next spring | {Run for 10 minutes three times this week} | {Find a running buddy by Friday} | {Run 15 minutes without stopping on Saturday}
  - Read 40 books this school year | {Get a library card this weekend} | {Finish my chapter book by Sunday} | {Read 20 minutes every night this week}
  - Play 10 songs on the guitar by next summer | {Practice the G chord every day this week} | {Learn one new song by the end of the month} | {Ask the music teacher for an easy song list by Friday}

## Stretch
write: Which short-term goal did not fit any long-term goal? Why is it still a goal?
  lines: 2
  key: Clean out my backpack tonight. It is a short-term goal on its own; it does not have to be a step toward a bigger goal.
