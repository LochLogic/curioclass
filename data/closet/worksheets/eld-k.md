# 1. Listen and Point: Classroom Directions
minutes: 15
materials: pencil or crayon
ican: I can listen to a classroom direction and find the picture that goes with it.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each direction. Point to the picture that goes with it. Then circle that picture.
mistakes: Children who circle every picture in a row are copying a neighbor or guessing: read the direction again and act it out (sit down, raise your hand) before they circle. Some children circle the first picture they see; have them point and say the word with you before they mark.
notes: Read each row's two directions slowly, act them out, and give students time to circle after each one. Say the picture names together first (chair, sun, hand, fish; book, tree, door, apple; cat, pencil, ball, trash can) so new English speakers know the words. Accept pointing as the answer for students who are not ready to circle. Repeat the directions with gestures all week; the frame "Please ___." works for any classroom routine.

## Warm up: Listen and do
example: (Read aloud.) "Sit down." The teacher sits on a chair. We sit on a **chair**.
  Act it out together: sit down, stand up, raise your hand.
text: **Word bank:** sit down, line up, raise your hand, clean up, open, get, chair, door, book, pencil, trash can

## Stretch: Your turn
write: (Read aloud.) Say a direction to a partner with the frame. Your partner points to the picture.
  lines: 0

  key: Any classroom direction, such as "Please line up." or "Please get your pencil." The partner points to the matching picture (door, pencil).

# 7. Classroom Language Picture Check
minutes: 15
materials: pencil or crayon
ican: I can listen to classroom directions and requests and find the picture that goes with each one.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each sentence. Circle the picture that goes with it. Then trace the words help and please.
mistakes: Some children circle every picture in a row; read the sentence again and act it out before they circle. Requests ("May I get a drink of water, please?") are harder than directions because the action word comes later; stress the picture word and point to the cup or the pencil. When tracing, watch for children who start "p" at the bottom.
notes: Say the picture names together first (soap, cup, star, kite; book, sun, backpack, dog; hand, cat, pencil, tree). Read each row's two sentences slowly, with gestures, and give time to circle after each one. Four sentences are directions (wash your hands, open your book, get your backpack, raise your hand) and two are requests students can use themselves (May I get a drink of water, please? May I have a pencil, please?). Accept pointing for students who are not ready to circle.

picword:

# 20. Above, Below, Next To
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can draw shapes above, below and next to a picture when I hear the words.
directions: (Read aloud.) Name the shapes. Draw a house in the middle of the big box. Then listen and draw each shape in the right place.
mistakes: "Below" and "next to" get mixed up most. Before each direction, show the place with your hand on the board: hand over the house for above, under for below, beside for next to. Some children draw the shape on top of the house instead of above it; show a little space between.
notes: Adaptation: the house is drawn by the children (a square with a triangle on top) so there is room to draw around it; the house picture in the warm up is the model. Building the house is itself position talk: the triangle goes above the square. Read each direction twice, model with your hand, and have children say the frame back ("The sun is above the house."). Shapes and colors can be changed for more practice.

## Warm up: Name the shapes
picword:
  - circle | circle
  - square | square
  - triangle | triangle
  - house | house
write: (Read aloud.) Point to the house. A house can be a square with a triangle above it.
  lines: 0
  starter: The triangle is {above} the square.
  key: The triangle is above the square.

## Main: Listen and draw
draw: My house | 2.9
write: (Read aloud.) Draw a square in the middle of the box. Draw a triangle above the square. Now it is a house!
  lines: 0
  key: A square in the middle of the box with a triangle on top of it.
write: (Read aloud.) Draw a circle above the house. Color it yellow. It is the sun.
  lines: 0
  key: A yellow circle above the house.
write: (Read aloud.) Draw a long rectangle below the house. It is a path.
  lines: 0
  key: A rectangle under the house.
write: (Read aloud.) Draw a triangle next to the house. It is a tree.
  lines: 0
  key: A triangle beside the house, on either side.

# 21. Math Talk Picture Check
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can count, name shapes and say which group has more or fewer.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each question. Count, circle or write the number. Then say the answer with the sentence frame.
mistakes: Watch for children who count a picture twice or skip one: have them touch each picture and cross it off lightly. "Fewer" is a new word for many children; they often mark the bigger group. Line the two groups up side by side with counters to show which group has fewer.
notes: Read every frame aloud and have students say it back in a full sentence, pointing to the picture ("I see 5 apples."). The numbers in the frames are blanks on the student copy; students can write the number or just say it. Name the four shapes together before item 3. Students who answer by pointing or holding up fingers are showing the math; keep coaching the words.

## Warm up: Count and say
text: **Word bank:** count, more, fewer, circle, square, triangle, rectangle, sides
count: (Read aloud.) Count. Write how many.
  - 5 apple
  - 9 fish
write: (Read aloud.) Say it: how many apples?
  lines: 0
  starter: I see {5} apples.
  key: I see 5 apples.
write: (Read aloud.) Say it: how many fish?
  lines: 0
  starter: I see {9} fish.
  key: I see 9 fish.

## Main: Shapes

## Main: More or fewer
write: (Read aloud.) Look back at the apples and the fish. Circle the box with **more**.
  lines: 0

  key: Circle the box with 9 fish. "9 is more than 5."

  lines: 0
  starter: {5} apples are fewer than {9} fish.
  key: X on the box with 5 apples. "5 apples are fewer than 9 fish."

# 33. Helper Hats Cut and Match
minutes: 20
materials: scissors, glue, crayons
ican: I can name community helpers and say what they do with "He is a ___" or "She is a ___."
directions: (Read aloud.) Name each helper. Cut out the 4 hats. Glue each hat in the box under the helper who wears it. Say "He is a ___" or "She is a ___."
mistakes: Children often match the firefighter and the builder hats the wrong way because both are round. Point to the shield on the firefighter's helmet and the brim all the way around the hard hat. For the frames, children may say "He is chef" without "a"; model "a chef" and have them repeat it.
notes: Name the helpers together first and act out each job: the firefighter sprays water from a hose, the chef cooks with a pan, the builder uses a hammer and the police officer wears a star badge. The firefighter and the builder are drawn as women and the chef and the police officer as men; talk about how anyone can do any of these jobs. Each child can choose He or She for each frame to match the picture. Glue only the hat; color after gluing.

## Warm up: Meet the helpers
picword:
  - helper-firefighter | firefighter
  - helper-chef | chef
  - helper-builder | builder
  - helper-police | police officer

## Main: Cut and glue the hats
sort: Glue each hat under its helper.
  - firefighter: icon:fire-helmet
  - chef: icon:chef-hat
  - builder: icon:hard-hat
  - police officer: icon:police-cap

## Stretch: Say it
write: (Read aloud.) Point to each helper. Say who it is.
  lines: 0
  starter: She is a {firefighter}. He is a {chef}. She is a {builder}. He is a {police officer}.
  key: She is a firefighter. He is a chef. She is a builder. He is a police officer.
