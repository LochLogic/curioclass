# 1. Tint, Hue, Shade Scale
minutes: 35
materials: paint (one color plus white and black), brush, water, paper towel; or colored pencils with a white and a black pencil
pairs: paint-mixer
ican: I can make tints and shades of a hue by adding white or black.
directions: Read the example. Choose one hue. Paint or color a 7-step scale: three tints on the left, the pure hue in the middle, three shades on the right. Label every step.
mistakes: Black is very strong: students who add a big spoonful get black, not a shade. Add black a tiny dot at a time. For tints, start with white and add the color to it, not the other way around, or you will use up a lot of white. Steps should change evenly; if two neighbors look the same, add a little more.
notes: Bright hues like red, blue and green show the scale best; yellow turns greenish when black is added, which is a fun thing to notice but can frustrate. A **tone** (adding gray) is the stretch idea. The Paint Mixer tool makes tints and shades on screen if you want to demonstrate.

## Warm up: Words to know
example: **Hue** is the pure color, like red. A **tint** is a hue plus white (pink is a tint of red). A **shade** is a hue plus black (maroon is a shade of red).
fill:
  - hue + white = a {tint}
  - hue + black = a {shade}
  - Pink is a {tint} of red.
  - Navy is a {shade} of blue.

## Main: My scale
text: My hue: ____________________
chart: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7
  rows: 1
table: Step | What I added | Label
  - 1 | {the most white} | {tint}
  - 2 | {some white} | {tint}
  - 3 | {a little white} | {tint}
  - 4 | {nothing} | {pure hue}
  - 5 | {a little black} | {shade}
  - 6 | {some black} | {shade}
  - 7 | {the most black} | {shade}

## Check
choice: Which step is the lightest?
  * step 1
  - step 4
  - step 7
choice: To make a shade, how should you add black?
  - all at once
  * a tiny bit at a time
  - only after adding white
choice: Value means how light or dark a color is. What changes across your scale?
  - the hue
  * the value
  - the texture

## Stretch
write: Adding gray to a hue makes a **tone**. Where would you use tints, and where would you use shades, in a painting of a sunny tree?
  lines: 3
  key: Tints where light hits: the sunny side of the leaves and trunk. Shades in the shadows: under the leaves, the shady side of the trunk, the shadow on the ground.

# 21. Monet or Van Gogh?
minutes: 40
materials: pencil; the teacher shows two paintings on a screen or as printouts
ican: I can compare Impressionism and Post-Impressionism by looking at brushwork, color, subject and feeling.
directions: Look closely at the two paintings your teacher shows. Read the facts. Fill in the T-chart with what you see in each painting, then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students often write "pretty" or "messy." Push for what they see: short dabs or long swirls, soft or bold color. Both artists painted outdoors and both used visible brushstrokes; the difference is that Monet painted what light looked like in a moment, and Van Gogh painted how he felt.
notes: Show Claude Monet's Impression, Sunrise (1872) and Vincent van Gogh's The Starry Night (1889), both public domain, with good images from museum open-access sites. Monet's painting gave Impressionism its name: a critic, Louis Leroy, mocked the title in a review of the 1874 exhibition. "Post" means after; Post-Impressionists learned from the Impressionists and then went their own ways. The T-chart key gives model answers; accept any accurate observation.

## Warm up: The facts
text: **Claude Monet** (1840-1926) was a French painter and a leader of **Impressionism**. He tried to catch the light of one moment outdoors. **Vincent van Gogh** (1853-1890) was a Dutch painter who worked in France. He is called a **Post-Impressionist** because he came after the Impressionists and used color and brushstrokes to show feelings.
fill:
  - The word Impressionism came from Monet's painting {Impression}, Sunrise.
  - Post means {after}.

## Main: T-chart
table: Look at | Monet, Impression, Sunrise (Impressionism) | Van Gogh, The Starry Night (Post-Impressionism)
  - Brushwork | {short, quick dabs; loose and sketchy} | {thick paint in long swirls and curving strokes}
  - Color | {soft blues and grays with an orange sun} | {bold blues and bright yellows side by side}
  - Subject | {a harbor at sunrise, boats in the mist} | {a night sky over a village, a tall cypress tree}
  - Feeling | {calm, quiet, a misty moment} | {energetic, swirling, full of emotion}

## Think about it
choice: Which painter was trying to show how light looked at one moment?
  * Monet
  - Van Gogh
choice: Which painting has brushstrokes that swirl and curl across the sky?
  - Impression, Sunrise
  * The Starry Night
write: How are the two painters alike? Give two ways.
  lines: 2
  key: Both show their brushstrokes instead of hiding them; both painted landscapes and skies; both used color in a fresh way; Van Gogh learned from Impressionists like Monet.

## Stretch
write: Which painting would you rather step into? Use two details from your chart to explain.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the painting and uses chart details, such as "The Starry Night, because the swirling strokes and bright yellow stars make the sky feel alive."
