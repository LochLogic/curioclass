# 1. Repeat It! Spot the Pattern
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayon
ican: I can find a pattern in a program and write it with a repeat block.
directions: Read each arrow program. Circle the part that repeats. Then write the program again with a repeat block: the number of times, and the arrows inside.
pairs: loop-garden
mistakes: Students often count every arrow instead of the times the pattern repeats (writing repeat 6 for → ↓ → ↓ → ↓). Have them circle each copy of the pattern first, then count the circles. Others pick a pattern that is too big (→ ↓ → ↓ repeated 1 and a half times): the pattern must fit evenly.
notes: Accept a correct but longer pattern (repeat 2 times: ↑ ↑ ↑ for six ups) only if the student can say why the smaller one is better: fewer blocks. The key shows the smallest repeating part. Read the arrows aloud as "right, down" for students who need it.

## Warm up
example: → → → → is the same as **repeat 4 times: →**. One block instead of four!
fill:
  - ↓ ↓ ↓ is repeat {3} times: ↓
  - ← ← ← ← ← ← is repeat {6} times: ←

## Main: Circle it, then repeat it
fill:
  - ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ is repeat {5} times: {↑               }
  - → ↓ → ↓ → ↓ is repeat {3} times: {→ ↓             }
  - → → ↑ → → ↑ is repeat {2} times: {→ → ↑           }
  - ← ↑ ← ↑ ← ↑ ← ↑ is repeat {4} times: {← ↑             }
  - ↓ ↓ → ↓ ↓ → ↓ ↓ → is repeat {3} times: {↓ ↓ →           }
  - → ↑ → ↓ → ↑ → ↓ is repeat {2} times: {→ ↑ → ↓         }

## Stretch: Find the bug
write: Mia wants → ↑ → ↑ → ↑ → ↑. She wrote **repeat 3 times: → ↑**. What is wrong? Write the fix.
  lines: 2
  key: The pattern → ↑ happens 4 times, not 3. The fix is repeat 4 times: → ↑.
write: Make up your own arrow program with a pattern. Then write it with a repeat block.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Check that the repeat number times the arrows inside makes exactly the long program.

# 5. Broken Loops Fix-Up
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can find a loop that repeats the wrong number of times and fix it.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each program has a goal and a bug: its loop repeats the wrong number of times. Work out what the program does now. Then write if it repeats too many or too few times, and the right number.
mistakes: When the loop has two or more moves inside, students change the number to the goal (repeat 12 times) instead of dividing (repeat 6 times: step, step makes 12 steps). Have them count by the moves inside: 2, 4, 6. Some only check whether the number looks wrong; ask them to act the program out or tally each repeat.
notes: Key: Program 1 does 4 hops, too few, repeat 5. Program 2 draws 3 sides, too few, repeat 4 (a square has 4 sides and 4 turns). Program 3 takes 6 steps (3 x 2), too few, repeat 6 (6 x 2 = 12). Program 4 flaps 9 times, too many, repeat 7. Program 5 plants 4 seeds (2 x 2), too few, repeat 3 (3 x 2 = 6). Grade 2 students can solve 3 and 5 by skip counting.

## Warm up
example: repeat 3 times: jump means jump, jump, jump: **3 jumps**.
fill:
  - repeat 4 times: clap makes {4} claps.
  - repeat 2 times: hop, hop makes {4} hops.

## Main: Find and fix the bug
program: Program 1. Goal: the frog hops 5 times to reach the lily pad.
  | when the flag is tapped
  | repeat 4 times:
  |     hop
program: Program 2. Goal: the pen draws a square.
  | when the flag is tapped
  | repeat 3 times:
  |     move 5 steps
  |     turn right
program: Program 3. Goal: the robot walks 12 steps to the door.
  | when the space key is pressed
  | repeat 3 times:
  |     step
  |     step
program: Program 4. Goal: the bird flaps 7 times to reach its nest.
  | when the bird is tapped
  | repeat 9 times:
  |     flap
program: Program 5. Goal: the gardener plants 6 seeds.
  | when the flag is tapped
  | repeat 2 times:
  |     step
  |     plant a seed
  |     plant a seed
table: Program | What it does now | Too many or too few? | Right repeat number
  - 1 | {4 hops} | {too few} | {5}
  - 2 | {3 sides} | {too few} | {4}
  - 3 | {6 steps} | {too few} | {6}
  - 4 | {9 flaps} | {too many} | {7}
  - 5 | {4 seeds} | {too few} | {3}

## Stretch
write: Write a loop program with a bug for a partner to fix. Write the goal, the code, and the fix.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: Goal: the cat jumps 8 times. Code: repeat 4 times: jump. Fix: repeat 8 times (or repeat 4 times: jump, jump).

# 16. Hardware Team Review
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can sort parts into input and output, tell how a key press reaches the screen, and choose a safe online action.
directions: (Read aloud.) Cut out the 6 part cards and glue each one under input or output. Fill in the path from a key press to the screen. Then choose the safe action for each online problem.
mistakes: Students often call the screen input because they look at it while typing; input goes into the computer, output comes out to you. A touch screen is both, which is why it is not on this sheet. In the path, some skip the computer itself: the keyboard does not talk to the screen directly.
notes: Key: input, keyboard, mouse, microphone; output, screen, speaker, printer. Path: finger, keyboard, computer, screen. Safe actions: close the prize pop-up and tell a grown-up; never share a password, even with someone who says they are a helper, and tell a trusted adult. The first review question: a microphone is input because it sends your voice into the computer.

## Warm up
choice: A microphone is an input part because ...
  - It plays sounds out loud.
  * It sends your voice into the computer.
  - It is plugged into the wall.

## Main: Input or output?
sort: Glue each part card in its column.
  - Input (into the computer): keyboard, mouse, microphone
  - Output (out to you): screen, speaker, printer

## Main: From key to screen
text: Word bank: **computer, finger, keyboard, screen**
fill:
  - Your {finger} presses the A key.
  - The {keyboard} sends a signal down the wire.
  - The {computer} works out that the A key was pressed.
  - The {screen} shows the letter A.

## Main: Safe online choices
choice: A pop-up says, "You won a prize! Type your home address." What should you do?
  - Type your address quickly before the prize is gone.
  - Click the pop-up to see what the prize is.
  * Close it and tell a grown-up.
choice: In a game chat, someone you do not know says they are a helper and asks for your password. What should you do?
  * Do not share it, and tell a trusted grown-up.
  - Share it, because helpers need passwords.
  - Share only half of the password.

## Stretch
write: Name one part that is both input and output, and tell why.
  lines: 2
  key: A touch screen: you touch it to send information in (input), and it shows you pictures and words (output). Also accept a headset with a microphone.

# 22. Comment Your Code
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can write a comment that tells what a program does.
directions: Read each program. Write a comment that tells a reader what the program does. Use the sentence starter.
mistakes: Students often copy the code word for word ("repeat 4 move 1 say Done") instead of telling what it does. Ask "If your friend could not read code, what would you tell them happens?" Some leave out the event: a good comment says when the code runs.
notes: A comment is a note for people, not for the computer; the computer skips it. A strong grade 2 comment names the event (when it starts), what happens, and how many times. Accept any comment that is true to the code. Pairs can trade sheets and check: "Does the comment match the code?"

## Warm up
example: Code: when the flag is tapped, say "Hello," wave. Comment: When the game starts, the robot says hello and waves.
choice: Which comment is best for this code? When the flag is tapped: repeat 3 times: jump.
  - This is code.
  * When the game starts, the frog jumps 3 times.
  - The frog likes to jump.

## Main: Comment each program
table: Program 1: Cat
  - when the flag is tapped
  - repeat 4 times: move 1 step
  - say "Done!"
write: Comment for Program 1.
  lines: 2
  starter: When the flag is tapped, the cat ...
  key: When the flag is tapped, the cat walks 4 steps and then says "Done!"
table: Program 2: Car
  - when the green light turns on
  - move 5 steps
  - honk 2 times
write: Comment for Program 2.
  lines: 2
  starter: When the green light turns on, ...
  key: When the green light turns on, the car drives 5 steps and honks two times.
table: Program 3: Bird
  - when the bird is tapped
  - repeat 3 times: flap wings, fly up 1
write: Comment for Program 3.
  lines: 2
  starter: When you tap the bird, ...
  key: When you tap the bird, it flaps its wings and flies up, 3 times, so it ends 3 steps higher.
table: Program 4: Garden robot
  - when the space key is pressed
  - repeat 2 times: step, plant a seed
  - say "All done!"
write: Comment for Program 4.
  lines: 2
  starter: When you press the space key, ...
  key: When you press the space key, the robot steps and plants a seed 2 times, so it plants 2 seeds, then says "All done!"

## Stretch: Does the comment tell the truth?
write: A comment on Program 1 says "The cat walks 2 steps." Is that true? Write a better comment.
  lines: 2
  key: No. The code repeats move 1 step 4 times, so the cat walks 4 steps. Better: When the flag is tapped, the cat walks 4 steps and says "Done!"

# 26. Rewrite the Unfair Rule
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain who a game rule leaves out and write a fairer rule.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each unfair rule. Write who loses out and why. Then write a fairer version of the rule that still lets the game work.
mistakes: Students write fairer rules that are not rules ("be nice," "share") instead of a step people can follow. Ask "What exactly happens, and when?" Others fix the rule by banning the game; a fair rule keeps the game and gives everyone a chance.
notes: Model answers are in the key. A strong fairer rule is a clear step (a timer, a line, a rotation list, a shared ball), treats everyone the same, and could be checked: you can see whether it was followed. Connect it to computing: people write the rules in games and apps, and a rule that leaves people out is a bug in the design.

## Warm up
example: **Unfair rule:** Only kids in the first row can use the class computer. **Who loses out:** kids in the other rows never get a turn. **Fairer rule:** Kids take turns on the computer by a name list, 10 minutes each.
choice: Which rule for the class tablet is the fairest?
  - The kid who gets there first keeps it all day.
  - Only the best readers can use it.
  * Each kid gets 10 minutes, in name-list order.

## Main: Rule 1
text: **Unfair rule:** Only kids who bring a ball from home can play four square.
write: Who loses out, and why?
  lines: 2
  starter: This rule is unfair to ...
  key: This rule is unfair to kids who do not have a ball at home or forgot it; they never get to play even if they want to.
write: Write a fairer rule.
  lines: 2
  starter: Fairer rule: ...
  key: Fairer rule: The class four square ball is for everyone. Kids wait in one line and the player who is out goes to the back.

## Main: Rule 2
text: **Unfair rule:** Whoever gets to the swings first can stay on all recess.
write: Who loses out, and why?
  lines: 2
  starter: This rule is unfair to ...
  key: This rule is unfair to kids who are slower to get outside or come from a far classroom; they wait all recess and never swing.
write: Write a fairer rule.
  lines: 2
  starter: Fairer rule: ...
  key: Fairer rule: Each swinger counts 30 swings, then gets off for the next kid in line.

## Main: Rule 3
text: **Unfair rule:** The two tallest kids are always the team captains.
write: Who loses out, and why?
  lines: 2
  starter: This rule is unfair to ...
  key: This rule is unfair to everyone who is not one of the tallest; they never get a turn to be captain, and height has nothing to do with being a good leader.
write: Write a fairer rule.
  lines: 2
  starter: Fairer rule: ...
  key: Fairer rule: Captains go in name-list order, two new captains each day, until everyone has had a turn.

## Stretch
write: Pick one of your fairer rules. How could you test that it really is fair?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Keep a tally of who swings each day for a week; if everyone who wants a turn gets one, the rule is fair.

# 37. School Helper Device Questions
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can plan good questions to learn how a school worker uses devices.
directions: (Read aloud.) Sort the question cards into open questions and yes or no questions. Then pick a school helper and plan 4 open questions to ask about the devices they use at work.
mistakes: Many first questions are yes or no ("Do you use a computer?"), which gets a one-word answer. Help students turn them into open questions with how, what or why. Some questions ask about the person's private life instead of their work; keep questions about the job and the devices.
notes: Sort key: open, "How do you use the scanner?", "What do you do if the computer stops working?", "Why do you use a laptop?"; yes or no, "Do you use a computer?", "Is your job fun?", "Do you have a printer?". Arrange the visits ahead with the workers and send students in small groups with an adult. Model questions are for the librarian; accept any open, job-focused questions. Devices a librarian might use: a barcode scanner, a computer with the library catalog, a printer, a laminator.

## Warm up: Open or yes or no?
sort: Glue each question card in its column.
  - Open questions (how, what, why): How do you use the scanner?; What do you do if the computer stops working?; Why do you use a laptop?
  - Yes or no questions: Do you use a computer?; Is your job fun?; Do you have a printer?

## Main: Plan my questions
text: Helper I will ask (circle one): **nurse, librarian, office manager, custodian, cafeteria manager**
write: Name 2 devices you think this helper uses.
  lines: 1
  key: Librarian: a barcode scanner and a computer.
write: Question 1 (what)
  lines: 2
  starter: What devices ...
  key: What devices do you use every day in the library?
write: Question 2 (how)
  lines: 2
  starter: How does ...
  key: How does the scanner help you check out books?
write: Question 3 (why)
  lines: 2
  starter: Why do you ...
  key: Why do you use a computer to find books instead of a card list?
write: Question 4 (what if)
  lines: 2
  starter: What would happen if ...
  key: What would happen if the computer stopped working on a busy day?

## Stretch
write: Write how you will start the interview politely.
  lines: 2
  starter: Hello, ...
  key: Hello, my name is Sam and I am in second grade. Thank you for meeting with us. Can we ask you 4 questions about the devices you use at work?
