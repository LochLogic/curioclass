# 1. Given Circumstances Detective: Rip Van Winkle
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can find a character's given circumstances in a script: who, where, when, what happened before, and what the character wants.
directions: Read the scene. Highlight clues about who, where and when. Then answer the questions like a detective, using clues from the script.
mistakes: Students often answer "when" with "in the morning" and miss the bigger clue: twenty years have passed, and the American Revolution has happened. Watch for "what happened before" answered with what happens in the scene; it means the events before the scene starts.
notes: The scene is adapted by Curio Class from Washington Irving's story "Rip Van Winkle" (1819, public domain), not quoted. In the story, Rip sleeps for twenty years in the Catskill Mountains of New York; he went to sleep before the American Revolution and wakes after it, so the inn's portrait of King George now says General Washington. About 230 words. Given circumstances is Stanislavski's term for everything the script tells an actor about the character's situation.

## Read
passage: Rip Comes Home (a scene adapted from "Rip Van Winkle" by Washington Irving)
  RIP: (rubbing his eyes) What a strange night! My knees are stiff, and my gun is covered in rust. Wolf! Here, Wolf! Where has that dog gone?
  (RIP walks down the mountain path into the village. The houses look different. Strangers stare at his long gray beard.)
  RIP: This is my village, I am sure of it. There are the Catskill Mountains, and there is the river. But where is the old inn? This sign says "Union Hotel." And the picture of King George has a new name painted under it: General Washington.
  VILLAGER: Old man, which side are you on? Who did you vote for?
  RIP: Vote? I am a quiet man who lives here, and a loyal subject of the King!
  VILLAGER: A loyal subject of the King? Here? Has nobody told you about the war? We have not had a king for years!
  RIP: Years? Only yesterday I went up the mountain with my dog and my gun to hunt squirrels. I met some odd little men playing ninepins. I drank from their jug, and I fell asleep.
  (A young woman steps forward, holding a baby.)
  JUDITH: Sir, my father went up that mountain twenty years ago with his gun and his dog. He never came home. His name was Rip Van Winkle.
  RIP: (staring at her) Judith? Little Judith? I am your father! Does nobody here know me?

## Warm up
choice: Which clue first tells the audience that a lot of time has passed?
  - Rip calls for his dog.
  * Rip's gun is covered in rust, and he has a long gray beard.
  - Rip sees the Catskill Mountains.
  - A villager asks a question.

## Main: The detective file
write: Who is Rip? Give two facts about him from the script.
  lines: 2
  key: An old man with a long gray beard who lives in this village; he is Judith's father and owns a dog named Wolf and a gun.
write: Where does the scene take place?
  lines: 2
  key: In Rip's village near the Catskill Mountains and a river, on the mountain path and outside the inn, now called the Union Hotel.
write: When does the scene take place? Use two clues.
  lines: 2
  key: Twenty years after Rip went up the mountain, after the war (the American Revolution): the inn has a new name, the picture now says General Washington, and there is no king.
write: What happened before the scene began?
  lines: 3
  key: Rip went up the mountain to hunt squirrels with his dog and gun, met odd little men playing ninepins, drank from their jug and fell asleep for twenty years.
write: What does Rip want in this scene?
  lines: 2
  key: He wants to find his home and family and to have someone recognize him; he also wants to understand what has changed.
choice: Why does the villager get upset when Rip speaks?
  - Rip will not tell his name.
  * Rip says he is loyal to the King, but the country no longer has a king.
  - Rip is hunting in the village.
  - Rip is holding a baby.

## Stretch
write: You are playing Rip. Choose one given circumstance and explain how it changes the way you walk or speak.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary: he has slept twenty years and is old and stiff, so he walks slowly and rubs his knees; he is confused, so he looks around and speaks with puzzled pauses; he is overjoyed at the end, so his voice rises when he sees Judith.

# 21. Costume Plate With Swatches
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, colored pencils, glue stick; optional fabric or paper scraps for swatches
ican: I can draw costume plates with fabric swatches and explain what each costume reveals.
directions: Our class play, The Bus Route, follows a neighborhood bus for one day. Draw a front view costume plate for 2 characters. Fill in the swatch boxes with a scrap or a colored-in sample, label each fabric, and write notes on what the costume reveals.
mistakes: Students often draw the character from the side or in a busy pose; a plate shows the front view, standing straight, so the costume is easy to read and build. Watch for swatch labels with only a color ("blue"); a swatch label names the fabric and the color ("navy wool").
notes: The play premise is original. Real costume designers attach small fabric samples (swatches) to each plate so the costume shop knows exactly what to buy. Scraps from old clothes, felt or wrapping paper work well; colored-in boxes are fine. A strong plate: front view, head to toe, every piece labeled, swatches that match the drawing, and notes that connect choices to the character.

## Warm up
choice: Why does a costume designer glue fabric swatches to a costume plate?
  - To make the drawing heavier
  * To show the costume shop the exact color and texture of each fabric
  - To hide mistakes in the drawing
choice: Why is a costume plate usually drawn as a front view?
  * So every piece of the costume is easy to see and build
  - Because actors only face the front
  - Because side views are not allowed in theater

## Plate 1: Mr. Abebe, the bus driver. Has driven the route for 25 years. Steady and kind.

  - | {knit, any warm color} | {scarf or sweater}
write: Notes: What does this costume reveal about Mr. Abebe?
  lines: 2
  key: Model: a neat, slightly worn navy uniform shows his job and his years on the route; a hand-knit scarf in a warm color shows he is kind and someone cares about him.

## Plate 2: Tasha, a teen who rides the bus to her first job. Nervous but trying to look grown up.
draw: Front view, head to toe | 3
table: Swatch (glue or color) | Fabric and color | Used for
  - | {gray wool blend} | {blazer, a little too big}
  - | {white cotton} | {shirt}
  - | {denim} | {jeans}
write: Notes: What does this costume reveal about Tasha?
  lines: 2
  key: Model: a blazer that is a little too big shows she is trying to look grown up; everyday denim underneath shows she is still a teen; a name tag pinned crooked shows she is nervous.

## Stretch
write: Your two characters stand side by side on stage. How do your colors help the audience tell them apart from the back row?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer contrasts value or color: Mr. Abebe's dark navy against Tasha's lighter gray and white, so each reads clearly from far away.
