# 1. Ethos, Pathos, Logos Sort
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can identify appeals to ethos, pathos and logos and explain how each one tries to persuade.
directions: All 12 lines come from speeches at a city council meeting about building a skate park. Cut out the cards and sort each line by its main appeal: ethos (trust in the speaker or source), pathos (feelings) or logos (facts, numbers and logic). Then explain three of your choices.
mistakes: Students often sort any line with a number as logos and any line with a person as ethos. Ask what the line mainly asks the listener to trust: the speaker, a feeling, or a reason. "Dr. Lee studies youth recreation" names an expert, so it is ethos; a survey percentage is logos even if it mentions students.
notes: The lines and speakers are written for this sheet; the town and figures are fictional. Some lines use more than one appeal; accept a different sort if the student's explanation names the stronger appeal clearly. Follow up: which appeal would persuade this council most, and why? The glue boxes are short for sentence strips: students can glue the cards in overlapping rows, or glue them on the back under the same three headings.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** ethos, pathos, logos, appeal, credibility, expert, emotion, evidence, statistic, logic, persuade, the speaker appeals to
example: **Ethos:** "As a doctor, I have seen..." (trust the speaker)
  **Pathos:** "Imagine how lonely..." (feel something)
  **Logos:** "The park costs 2 percent of the budget..." (think it through)
choice: Which appeal relies mainly on the speaker's experience or expertise?
  * Ethos
  - Pathos
  - Logos

## Main: Sort the lines
sort: Glue each line under its main appeal.
  - Ethos: I am a city engineer who has designed three parks and I can tell you this site is safe for skating., Our plan has the support of the parks department and the county safety board., I have coached youth sports in this town for twenty years., Dr. Lee studies youth recreation at the state university and recommends parks like this one.
  - Pathos: Picture a twelve-year-old skating alone in an empty parking lot because there is nowhere else to go., This park would give our kids a place where they finally belong., Do we want our children to remember a town that told them no?, The joy on their faces when the first ramp opened would say everything.
  - Logos: The park would cost about 2 percent of the yearly parks budget., The site is two blocks from three schools and most riders could walk there., A survey of 400 local students found that 62 percent would use the park every week., Skaters who use a park are not skating on busy sidewalks downtown.

## Main: Explain three choices
write: Choose an **ethos** card. Explain how it persuades.
  lines: 3
  starter: The line "___" appeals to ethos because the speaker ___.
  key: Example: The line "I am a city engineer who has designed three parks and I can tell you this site is safe for skating" appeals to ethos because the speaker uses professional experience to make listeners trust the safety claim.
write: Choose a **pathos** card. Explain how it persuades.
  lines: 3
  starter: The line "___" appeals to pathos because it makes the audience feel ___.
  key: Example: The line "Picture a twelve-year-old skating alone in an empty parking lot because there is nowhere else to go" appeals to pathos because it makes the audience feel sympathy and worry for a lonely child.
write: Choose a **logos** card. Explain how it persuades.
  lines: 3
  starter: The line "___" appeals to logos because it uses ___ to show ___.
  key: Example: The line "A survey of 400 local students found that 62 percent would use the park every week" appeals to logos because it uses survey data to show many students would actually use the park.

# 7. Academic Argument Check
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can identify rhetorical appeals, write a concession and plan a synthesis argument from several sources.
directions: Name the appeal in each of three lines from a city council debate about bike lanes. Write a concession to an opposing view. Then chart three short sources and write a claim that uses all three.
mistakes: Students often call any line with a number logos and any line with a person in it ethos. Ask what the line asks the listener to trust: the speaker (ethos), their feelings (pathos) or facts and reasoning (logos). A concession must admit something true about the other side before turning; "While it is true that they are wrong" is not a concession. A synthesis claim uses the sources together, not one after another.
notes: Riverton, Elm Avenue and the report numbers are made up for this sheet. Source C states a well-established finding (regular physical activity lowers the risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers). Accept any synthesis claim that takes a position and draws on safety or use data (A), the business concern (B) and health (C).

## Warm up: Name the appeal
text: **Word bank:** ethos, pathos, logos, appeal, credibility, emotion, evidence, concede, while it is true that, synthesis, source
choice: "As a nurse who has treated injured cyclists for twenty years, I know what an unprotected lane can lead to."
  * Ethos
  - Pathos
  - Logos
choice: "Reported bike crashes on Elm Avenue fell from 14 to 5 in the year after the protected lanes opened."
  - Ethos
  - Pathos
  * Logos
choice: "Picture a ten-year-old pedaling to school while trucks roar past, inches from her handlebars."
  - Ethos
  * Pathos
  - Logos

## Main: Concede
text: **Opposing view:** Shop owners say that removing parking spaces for bike lanes will keep customers away.
write: Write a concession and then turn back to the claim that Riverton should add protected bike lanes.
  lines: 4
  starter: While it is true that ___, ___.
  key: While it is true that some customers drive and need parking nearby, the city can add short-term parking on side streets, and the bike lanes would bring new customers who arrive by bike.

## Main: Plan a synthesis
text: **Source A** (Riverton Transportation Office report): In the year after protected bike lanes opened on Elm Avenue, bike trips on that street rose by 40 percent, and reported bike crashes fell from 14 to 5.
text: **Source B** (letter from Elm Avenue shop owners): "We support safe streets, but removing 30 parking spaces could keep customers away from our small businesses."
text: **Source C** (public health article): Adults who get regular physical activity, such as biking to work, have a lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers.
chart: Main point | How I will use it in my argument
  labels: Source A; Source B; Source C
  height: 0.7
  key: A: lanes increased biking and cut crashes; use as main evidence for safety. B: shop owners fear losing parking and customers; use as the counterclaim to concede and answer. C: biking improves health; use as a second benefit beyond safety.
write: Write one synthesis claim that draws on all three sources.
  lines: 3
  starter: Although ___, Riverton should ___ because ___ and ___.
  key: Although some shop owners worry about lost parking, Riverton should add protected bike lanes on more streets because they made Elm Avenue safer and more popular for cyclists, and more biking leads to healthier residents.

## Stretch: Which appeal is strongest?
write: Which of the three warm-up lines would persuade the city council most? Explain why, using the word "audience."
  lines: 3
  starter: The ___ line would persuade the council most because the audience ___.
  key: The logos line would persuade the council most because the audience has to make a budget decision and will want local data showing that the lanes reduced crashes.

# 20. Data Explanation Paragraph
minutes: 40
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain a graph in a paragraph with a claim, data and scientific reasoning.
directions: Read the graph of ocean temperature at different depths. Fill in the data, then write a claim, data and reasoning paragraph that explains the pattern. Finish by naming a limitation of the data.
mistakes: Students often describe every point instead of the pattern. Ask for the trend in one sentence and the two or three numbers that prove it. In reasoning, watch for "because it is deeper" with no cause. The cause is sunlight warming only the top layer and cold polar water sinking to fill the deep ocean.
notes: The profile is a typical one for a warm, open-ocean region, rounded to whole degrees; real profiles vary by place and season. Oceanographers usually draw depth going down the side of the graph; here depth runs along the bottom so the grid reads like other graphs. The steep drop below the warm surface layer is the thermocline. Deep water is cold because it formed at the surface near the poles, became cold and dense, and sank.

## Warm up: Read the graph
graph: Depth (meters) | 0 1000 100 | Water temperature (°C) | 0 30 5
  line: 0,26 100,24 200,18 300,13 400,10 500,8 600,7 700,6 800,5 900,5 1000,4
  minor: off
text: **Word bank:** claim, data, reasoning, depth, decreases, sharply, gradually, levels off, thermocline, sunlight, dense, sinks, limitation
fill:
  - At the surface (0 m), the water is {26} °C.
  - At 500 m, the water is {8} °C.
  - At 1,000 m, the water is {4} °C.
  - The biggest drop in 100 m happens between {100} m and {200} m.

## Main: Write the paragraph
write: **Claim:** What happens to ocean temperature as depth increases?
  lines: 2
  starter: The graph shows that as depth increases, ___.
  key: The graph shows that as depth increases, ocean temperature decreases, falling quickly near the surface and then more slowly.
write: **Data:** Use at least three numbers from the graph.
  lines: 3
  starter: At the surface, ___. Between ___ and ___ m, ___. By 1,000 m, ___.
  key: At the surface, the water is about 26 °C. Between 100 and 300 m, it drops sharply from 24 to 13 °C. By 1,000 m, it is only about 4 °C, 22 degrees colder than the surface.
write: **Reasoning:** Explain why the pattern happens.
  lines: 4
  starter: This happens because ___. In addition, ___.
  key: This happens because sunlight warms only the top layer of the ocean, and waves and wind mix that heat just through the upper part. In addition, the deep ocean is filled with cold, dense water that sank near the poles, so below the thermocline the water stays cold.
write: **Limitation:** What can this one graph not tell us?
  lines: 3
  starter: One limitation of these data is that ___, so a next step would be ___.
  key: One limitation of these data is that they come from one warm region at one time, so a next step would be to compare profiles from polar oceans and from different seasons.

## Stretch: Predict
write: Predict how the graph would look for an ocean near the North Pole. Explain your prediction.
  lines: 3
  starter: Near the North Pole, the line would ___ because ___.
  key: Near the North Pole, the line would start much colder at the surface, close to 0 °C, and stay fairly flat because the sun does not warm the surface much, so there is little difference between surface and deep water.

# 21. Explaining Models and Data Check
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, calculator if available
ican: I can compare two models with data, hedge a result and describe a data table.
directions: Two students made models to predict a town's population. Finish the table, compare each model with the actual data, then describe the data and write a hedged conclusion.
mistakes: Students compute Model B by adding 5 percent of the first year every time (10,500 each year), which makes it the same as Model A. Each year's 5 percent is of the year before. Watch for conclusions like "Model B is right" or "Model B proves..." instead of hedged language.
notes: The town and its numbers are fictional. Model B values are rounded to the nearest whole person: 10,000 x 1.05 = 10,500; x 1.05 = 11,025; x 1.05 = 11,576.25, about 11,576; x 1.05 = 12,155.06, about 12,155. Differences from actual in year 4: Model A 160, Model B 5.

## Warm up: Two models
text: **Word bank:** model, predict, linear, exponential, percent growth, actual, difference, fits, closer, suggests, appears, likely, may, the table shows
text: **Riverton** had 10,000 people in Year 0. **Model A** (linear): add 500 people every year. **Model B** (exponential): grow by 5 percent every year.
choice: Which model adds the same number of people every year?
  * Model A
  - Model B
  - Both models
  - Neither model

## Main: Finish the table
table: Year | Model A | Model B | Actual population
  - 0 | 10,000 | 10,000 | 10,000
  - 1 | 10,500 | 10,500 | 10,510
  - 2 | {11,000} | {11,025} | 11,030
  - 3 | {11,500} | {11,576} | 11,570
  - 4 | {12,000} | {12,155} | 12,160
fill:
  - In Year 4, Model A is off by {160} people.
  - In Year 4, Model B is off by {5} people.

## Main: Compare the models
write: Which model fits the actual data better? Use numbers.
  lines: 3
  starter: Model ___ fits the data better because ___, while Model ___ ___.
  key: Model B fits the data better because it stays within about 10 people of the actual population every year (off by only 5 in Year 4), while Model A falls further behind each year (off by 160 in Year 4).
write: Describe the actual data in the table.
  lines: 3
  starter: The table shows that the population ___ from ___ to ___. Each year the increase ___.
  key: The table shows that the population grew from 10,000 to 12,160 in four years. Each year the increase got a little larger (510, 520, 540, 590), which is what percent growth does.

## Stretch: Hedge the result
write: A student writes: "Model B proves Riverton will have 26,500 people in 20 years." Rewrite it as a hedged conclusion, and give one reason the prediction could be wrong.
  lines: 4
  starter: Based on four years of data, Model B suggests ___. However, ___.
  key: Based on four years of data, Model B suggests Riverton may grow to roughly 26,500 people in 20 years if growth stays near 5 percent a year. However, growth could slow if housing, jobs or water run short, and four years is a short record to predict 20 years from.

# 33. Mock Interview Feedback
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can score an interview for formality, clarity and examples and suggest specific changes.
directions: Read the mock interview for a junior counselor job. Score Alex on each trait from 1 to 4 and give a reason. Then suggest two changes, and rewrite one of Alex's answers.
mistakes: Students often score the whole interview from one answer. Look at every answer: Alex is strong in two and weak in three. Suggestions must be specific ("give an example from a group project"), not "be more professional." Watch for rewrites that become stiff and long; formal does not mean wordy.
notes: Alex, Ms. Grant and Lakeside Day Camp are made up. Strong answers: 2 (a clear reason) and 4 (a clear, step-by-step plan). Weak: 1 (fillers "uh", "like", "and stuff"), 3 (no example; "chill"), 5 (no question for the interviewer). Accept scores within one point of the key with a sound reason. The STAR frame (Situation, Task, Action, Result) helps with question 3.

## Read the transcript
text: **Ms. Grant:** Thank you for coming in. Tell me a little about yourself.
text: **Alex:** Uh, yeah, so I'm Alex, I'm in tenth grade, and I like, really like kids and stuff.
text: **Ms. Grant:** Why do you want to be a junior counselor at Lakeside Day Camp?
text: **Alex:** I went to a day camp when I was younger and loved it. I'd like to help younger kids have that same experience.
text: **Ms. Grant:** Tell me about a time you solved a problem with a group.
text: **Alex:** Um, I don't know. I'm pretty good at that. People say I'm chill.
text: **Ms. Grant:** What would you do if two campers were arguing over a ball?
text: **Alex:** I would calmly separate them, ask each one what happened, and help them agree on a way to take turns, maybe with a timer.
text: **Ms. Grant:** Do you have any questions for us?
text: **Alex:** Nah, I'm good.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** formality, clarity, example, filler words, specific, register, STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result), suggestion, I recommend
choice: Which of Alex's answers is the strongest?
  - "Nah, I'm good."
  - "People say I'm chill."
  * "I would calmly separate them, ask each one what happened, and help them agree on a way to take turns."
  - "I like, really like kids and stuff."

## Main: Score the interview
table: Trait | Score (1 to 4) | Reason, with a quote from the transcript
  height: 0.7
  - Formality | {2} | {Fillers and slang: "uh," "like," "and stuff," "chill," "Nah."}
  - Clarity | {3} | {Answers 2 and 4 are clear and direct; answer 1 wanders.}
  - Examples | {2} | {Answer 4 gives a plan, but answer 3 has no real example.}

## Main: Two changes
write: Change 1
  lines: 3
  starter: I recommend that Alex ___ because ___.
  key: I recommend that Alex answer question 3 with a real example, such as a group project where he helped the team divide the work, because the interviewer asked for a time, not a description of himself.
write: Change 2
  lines: 3
  starter: I also recommend ___ because ___.
  key: I also recommend asking a question at the end, such as "What does a typical day look like for a junior counselor?", because it shows interest in the job.

## Stretch: Rewrite an answer
write: Rewrite Alex's answer to "Tell me about a time you solved a problem with a group." Use STAR.
  lines: 5
  starter: In ___ (Situation), our group needed to ___ (Task). I ___ (Action). As a result, ___ (Result).
  key: In my science class last spring, our group needed to finish a poster, but two members wanted different topics. I suggested we list the pros of each topic and vote, and I offered to research the parts no one wanted. As a result, we chose a topic in ten minutes and finished the poster a day early.
