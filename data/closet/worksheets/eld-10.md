# 1. Ethos, Pathos, Logos Sort
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can identify appeals to ethos, pathos and logos and explain how each one tries to persuade.
directions: All 12 lines come from speeches at a city council meeting about building a skate park. Cut out the cards and sort each line by its main appeal: ethos (trust in the speaker or source), pathos (feelings) or logos (facts, numbers and logic). Then explain three of your choices.
mistakes: Students often sort any line with a number as logos and any line with a person as ethos. Ask what the line mainly asks the listener to trust: the speaker, a feeling, or a reason. "Dr. Lee studies youth recreation" names an expert, so it is ethos; a survey percentage is logos even if it mentions students.
notes: The lines and speakers are written for this sheet; the town and figures are fictional. Some lines use more than one appeal; accept a different sort if the student's explanation names the stronger appeal clearly. Follow up: which appeal would persuade this council most, and why? The glue boxes are short for sentence strips: students can glue the cards in overlapping rows, or glue them on the back under the same three headings.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** ethos, pathos, logos, appeal, credibility, expert, emotion, evidence, statistic, logic, persuade, the speaker appeals to
example: **Ethos:** "As a doctor, I have seen..." (trust the speaker)
  **Pathos:** "Imagine how lonely..." (feel something)
  **Logos:** "The park costs 2 percent of the budget..." (think it through)
choice: Which appeal relies mainly on the speaker's experience or expertise?
  * Ethos
  - Pathos
  - Logos

## Main: Sort the lines
sort: Glue each line under its main appeal.
  - Ethos: I am a city engineer who has designed three parks and I can tell you this site is safe for skating., Our plan has the support of the parks department and the county safety board., I have coached youth sports in this town for twenty years., Dr. Lee studies youth recreation at the state university and recommends parks like this one.
  - Pathos: Picture a twelve-year-old skating alone in an empty parking lot because there is nowhere else to go., This park would give our kids a place where they finally belong., Do we want our children to remember a town that told them no?, The joy on their faces when the first ramp opened would say everything.
  - Logos: The park would cost about 2 percent of the yearly parks budget., The site is two blocks from three schools and most riders could walk there., A survey of 400 local students found that 62 percent would use the park every week., Skaters who use a park are not skating on busy sidewalks downtown.

## Main: Explain three choices
write: Choose an **ethos** card. Explain how it persuades.
  lines: 3
  starter: The line "___" appeals to ethos because the speaker ___.
  key: Example: The line "I am a city engineer who has designed three parks and I can tell you this site is safe for skating" appeals to ethos because the speaker uses professional experience to make listeners trust the safety claim.
write: Choose a **pathos** card. Explain how it persuades.
  lines: 3
  starter: The line "___" appeals to pathos because it makes the audience feel ___.
  key: Example: The line "Picture a twelve-year-old skating alone in an empty parking lot because there is nowhere else to go" appeals to pathos because it makes the audience feel sympathy and worry for a lonely child.
write: Choose a **logos** card. Explain how it persuades.
  lines: 3
  starter: The line "___" appeals to logos because it uses ___ to show ___.
  key: Example: The line "A survey of 400 local students found that 62 percent would use the park every week" appeals to logos because it uses survey data to show many students would actually use the park.

## Stretch: Balance the appeals
write: A speaker used only pathos lines. Why might that be weak with a city council, and what should the speaker add?
  lines: 3
  starter: Using only pathos may be weak because ___. The speaker should add ___.
  key: Using only pathos may be weak because council members must justify spending and safety with facts. The speaker should add logos, like the cost and survey data, and ethos, like support from the parks department.

# 21. Explaining Models and Data Check
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, calculator if available
ican: I can compare two models with data, hedge a result and describe a data table.
directions: Two students made models to predict a town's population. Finish the table, compare each model with the actual data, then describe the data and write a hedged conclusion.
mistakes: Students compute Model B by adding 5 percent of the first year every time (10,500 each year), which makes it the same as Model A. Each year's 5 percent is of the year before. Watch for conclusions like "Model B is right" or "Model B proves..." instead of hedged language.
notes: The town and its numbers are fictional. Model B values are rounded to the nearest whole person: 10,000 x 1.05 = 10,500; x 1.05 = 11,025; x 1.05 = 11,576.25, about 11,576; x 1.05 = 12,155.06, about 12,155. Differences from actual in year 4: Model A 160, Model B 5.

## Warm up: Two models
text: **Word bank:** model, predict, linear, exponential, percent growth, actual, difference, fits, closer, suggests, appears, likely, may, the table shows
text: **Riverton** had 10,000 people in Year 0. **Model A** (linear): add 500 people every year. **Model B** (exponential): grow by 5 percent every year.
choice: Which model adds the same number of people every year?
  * Model A
  - Model B
  - Both models
  - Neither model

## Main: Finish the table
table: Year | Model A | Model B | Actual population
  - 0 | 10,000 | 10,000 | 10,000
  - 1 | 10,500 | 10,500 | 10,510
  - 2 | {11,000} | {11,025} | 11,030
  - 3 | {11,500} | {11,576} | 11,570
  - 4 | {12,000} | {12,155} | 12,160
fill:
  - In Year 4, Model A is off by {160} people.
  - In Year 4, Model B is off by {5} people.

## Main: Compare the models
write: Which model fits the actual data better? Use numbers.
  lines: 3
  starter: Model ___ fits the data better because ___, while Model ___ ___.
  key: Model B fits the data better because it stays within about 10 people of the actual population every year (off by only 5 in Year 4), while Model A falls further behind each year (off by 160 in Year 4).
write: Describe the actual data in the table.
  lines: 3
  starter: The table shows that the population ___ from ___ to ___. Each year the increase ___.
  key: The table shows that the population grew from 10,000 to 12,160 in four years. Each year the increase got a little larger (510, 520, 540, 590), which is what percent growth does.

## Stretch: Hedge the result
write: A student writes: "Model B proves Riverton will have 26,500 people in 20 years." Rewrite it as a hedged conclusion, and give one reason the prediction could be wrong.
  lines: 4
  starter: Based on four years of data, Model B suggests ___. However, ___.
  key: Based on four years of data, Model B suggests Riverton may grow to roughly 26,500 people in 20 years if growth stays near 5 percent a year. However, growth could slow if housing, jobs or water run short, and four years is a short record to predict 20 years from.
