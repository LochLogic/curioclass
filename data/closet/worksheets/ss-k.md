# 1. Safe or Not Safe? Playground Pictures
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell a safe choice from an unsafe choice on the playground.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture while I read the words under it. Is it a safe choice? Circle the picture if it is safe. Cross it out with a big X if it is not safe. You can also draw O or X on the line. Then draw one more safe choice.
mistakes: Some children circle every picture they like (the slide, the swing) rather than judging the choice I read. Point to the words and reread: "Is standing on the swing safe?" Watch for children who think waiting is "not fun" and so "not safe": fun and safe are different questions.
notes: Key: O (circle) means safe, X means not safe. The pictures show the place or thing; the choice is in the words you read. Read each one slowly, two times. Talk about why before children mark: "What could happen if...?" Keep it about choices, never about a child who got hurt. A quick class rule chart afterward ("We go down the slide feet first") ties the sheet to your class rules.

## Warm up: Try one together
text: (Read aloud.) "Go down the slide feet first." Is that safe? Yes! We circle it.

## Main: Circle safe, cross out not safe
picword:
  - slide | feet first {O}
  - swing | stand on the swing {X}
  - people | push a friend {X}
  - ball | play catch on the grass {O}
  - sand-timer | wait your turn {O}
  - trash-can | climb on the trash can {X}
  - stairs | push on the steps {X}
  - sun | wear a hat in the sun {O}

## Stretch: Draw one more
draw: Draw one more safe choice on the playground. | 1.9
write: (Read aloud.) Tell a grown-up about your picture. They can write your words here.
  lines: 1
  key: Any safe choice, such as holding the rail on the climber, sitting on the swing, sharing the ball or staying inside the fence.

# 5. What Should Pip Do?
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can choose a fair and kind way to act.
directions: (Read aloud.) Pip is a puppy in our class. Listen to each story about Pip. Circle the picture that shows the fair or kind choice. Put an X on the other picture.
mistakes: Some children circle the picture they like best (the ball, the pillow) instead of the choice in the story. Point to the words under the picture and reread both choices: "Should Pip wait, or push?" Ask "How would Kit feel?" before children mark.
notes: Key: O (circle) is the fair or kind choice, X is the other one. The kind choice is first in stories 1 and 4 and second in stories 2 and 3, so children must listen, not guess a pattern. Read each story two times. Pip is a made-up storybook puppy, so the talk stays about Pip's choices, never about a child in the room. Tie each story to a class rule afterward ("We take turns," "We help fix it").

## Main: Help Pip choose
text: (Read aloud.) **Story 1.** Pip and Kit the kitten both want to go down the slide. Should Pip wait for a turn, or push Kit out of the way? **Story 2.** Pip bumps into Bo's block tower, and it falls down. Should Pip run away, or say sorry and help build it again?
picword:
  - sand-timer | 1. wait {O}
  - people | 1. push {X}
  - run | 2. run away {X}
  - abc-blocks | 2. help fix it {O}
text: (Read aloud.) **Story 3.** Dot sits all alone at recess. She looks sad. Should Pip keep playing ball by himself, or ask Dot to play? **Story 4.** It is clean-up time. Should Pip help put the toys away, or take a nap on a pillow?
picword:
  - ball | 3. play alone {X}
  - heart | 3. ask Dot to play {O}
  - broom | 4. clean up {O}
  - pillow | 4. take a nap {X}

## Stretch: Draw Pip
draw: Draw Pip doing one more kind thing. | 1.5
note: Model answers: Pip shares a toy, helps a friend who fell, says thank you, holds the door, gives a hug when a friend is sad.

# 15. Path to School Picture Map
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can follow a path on a picture map.
directions: (Read aloud.) This is a picture map of a little town. Put your finger on the house. Draw a path from the house to the school. Your path must go past the park and the store. Then listen to the questions and circle the answers.
mistakes: Some children draw a straight line from the house to the school and skip the park and the store. Have them touch each stop with a finger first: house, park, store, school. Others start at the school; point out that a path has a start (the house) and an end (the school).
notes: Key: one good path goes house, right to the park, down to the flower, right to the dog, right to the store, then down to the school. Any path that moves square to square, starts at the house, passes the park and then the store, and ends at the school is correct. The park comes first on every good path. Read the map like a book: top row first. Extension: children draw one more place on the back (a library, a fire station, a bus stop) and tell where it goes on the map.

## Main: Draw the path
picword:
  - house | house
  - tree | park
  - flower |
  - grass |
  - grass |
  - flower |
  - dog |
  - store | store
  - cat |
  - grass |
  - flower |
  - school | school
note: Model path: house, park, flower, dog, store, school. Squares touch side to side; children may also draw one long curvy line through the park and the store.

## Questions: Circle the answer
choice: (Read aloud.) On the way to school, what do you pass first?
  * the park
  - the store
choice: (Read aloud.) Which place is right next to the school?
  - the park
  * the store

# 21. Baby, Kid, Grown-Up
minutes: 20
materials: scissors, glue stick, crayons
ican: I can put pictures of someone growing up in order from first to last.
directions: (Read aloud.) This is Lulu from our story. She grows up! Cut out the 4 pictures of Lulu. Glue them on the strip in order: baby first, grown-up last.
mistakes: Key: the order is baby (1), toddler (2), kid (3), grown-up (4). Children may order by how much they like a picture or line them up by width. Ask "Who is the littlest? Who came first?" and have them hold two pictures side by side to compare height. Some think a toddler and a kid are the same: point to the bigger body and longer legs.
notes: Lulu is a made-up storybook character, so no child shares their own baby pictures or family history. Before cutting, tell a short Lulu story aloud ("First Lulu was a tiny baby who slept all day..."). Children who cannot cut yet can show the order with the numbers 1 to 4 alone.

## Warm up: Who is littlest?
text: (Read aloud.) Point to the littlest Lulu. Point to the biggest Lulu.

## Main: Cut out Lulu
text: (Read aloud.) Write 1, 2, 3, 4 under the pictures to show the order. Then cut them out.
picword:
  - kid | {3}
  - baby | {1}
  - grown-up | {4}
  - toddler | {2}

## Glue: Lulu grows up
draw: Glue strip: baby first on the left, then toddler, then kid, then grown-up last on the right. | 1.8

## Stretch: What can Lulu do now?
draw: Draw something kid Lulu can do that baby Lulu could not do yet. | 2

# 25. Past, Present, Future Check
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell if something is from the past, the present or the future.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture while I read about it. Did it happen long ago (past), does it happen now (present), or might it happen some day (future)? Listen for the time words: long ago is the past, today is the present, some day is the future. Make an X in the right box. Then draw a classroom of the future.
mistakes: Children may judge by whether they have seen the thing: "I have seen candles, so candles are now." Reread the words: "Long ago, people used candles to see at night." Listen for the time words: long ago means past, today means present, some day means future. The future is not "pretend"; it is what might happen later.
notes: Read each sentence two times, stressing the time words: (1) "Long ago, people used candles to see at night." (2) "Today, we use computers at school." (3) "Some day, people might live on another planet." (4) "Today, we flip a switch to turn on the lights." (5) "Long ago, many people rode horses to town." (6) "Some day, robots might help in classrooms." Point to each column as you say past, present, future.

  - horse | 5
  - robot | 6

## Stretch: A future classroom
draw: Draw one thing a classroom might have in the future. | 1.2
note: Any idea that does not exist in classrooms yet, such as a robot helper, flying chairs, a window that shows any place in the world, or desks that clean themselves.

# 35. Signs and Symbols All Around
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell what a sign or symbol tells us.
directions: (Read aloud.) A symbol is a picture that tells us something. Listen to what each one tells us. Draw a line from each number to its meaning. Then draw a new symbol for our classroom.
mistakes: Children mix up the stop sign and the hand on a walk light, since both mean "do not go." Name the place: the stop sign is for cars at a corner, and the hand light is for people waiting to cross. Some read the recycling arrows as "go around"; show a real recycling bin.
notes: Read the right column aloud, two times, while children point. Children who are not ready to draw lines can say the number to you. The walk light hand is the orange hand on a crossing signal; a quick walk around the school to spot the exit sign, the first aid kit and the recycling bin makes the sheet real. For the stretch, good classroom symbols are simple: a book for the reading corner, a hand for "raise your hand," a sink for "wash hands."

## Main: What does it tell us?
picword:
  - stop-sign | 1 stop sign
  - recycle | 2 recycle
  - exit-sign | 3 exit
  - crosswalk | 4 crosswalk
  - first-aid | 5 first aid
  - trash-can | 6 trash
  - hand | 7 walk light
  - flag | 8 flag
match: (Read aloud.) Draw a line from the number to what it tells us.
  - stop sign = Cars stop here and look.
  - recycle = Put cans and paper here to use again.
  - exit = This is the way out.
  - crosswalk = Walk across the street here.
  - first aid = Help for a cut or a bump.
  - trash = Throw away garbage here.
  - walk light = Wait. Do not cross yet.
  - flag = Our country, the United States.

## Stretch: A new symbol
draw: Draw a new symbol for our classroom. What does it tell us? | 1.0
note: Any simple picture with a clear message: a book for the reading corner, a raised hand for "raise your hand," a sink for "wash your hands," a finger to the lips for "quiet."
