# 1. Ratio Language at the Animal Shelter
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can use ratio language and write a ratio three ways, comparing part to part and part to whole.
directions: Count the animals at the shelter. For each ratio, say whether the second quantity is a part or the whole (part to part, or part to whole), then write it three ways: with "to", with a colon, and as a fraction. Last, write it in simplest form.
pairs: smoothie-lab
mistakes: Order matters: "cats to dogs" is 6:8, not 8:6. Students also forget that the whole is all 18 animals, not the number of kinds (3). In the last row, "animals that are not dogs" is 10 (6 cats and 4 birds), which many students miss.
notes: A part-to-part ratio written as a fraction (8/6) is fine as notation, but point out that it is not a fraction of a whole: 8/6 of what? Simplest forms: 4:3, 3:4, 1:2, 3:2, 4:9, 2:9, 1:3 and 5:4. Equivalent answers in other forms are correct.

## Warm up: Count the shelter
count: How many of each animal are at the shelter today?
  - 8 dog
  - 6 cat
  - 4 bird
fill: There are {18} animals in all.

## Main: Write the ratios
example: Birds to cats: part to part. 4 to 6, 4:6, 4/6. Simplest form 2:3. For every 2 birds there are 3 cats.
table: Ratio | Compares to a part or the whole? | With "to" | With a colon | As a fraction | Simplest form
  - dogs to cats | {part} | {8 to 6} | {8:6} | {8/6} | {4:3}
  - cats to dogs | {part} | {6 to 8} | {6:8} | {6/8} | {3:4}
  - birds to dogs | {part} | {4 to 8} | {4:8} | {4/8} | {1:2}
  - cats to birds | {part} | {6 to 4} | {6:4} | {6/4} | {3:2}
  - dogs to all animals | {whole} | {8 to 18} | {8:18} | {8/18} | {4:9}
  - birds to all animals | {whole} | {4 to 18} | {4:18} | {4/18} | {2:9}
  - cats to all animals | {whole} | {6 to 18} | {6:18} | {6/18} | {1:3}
  - animals that are not dogs to dogs | {part} | {10 to 8} | {10:8} | {10/8} | {5:4}
fill: Finish the sentence: For every {4} dogs at the shelter, there are 3 cats.

## Stretch
write: Two more cats arrive. Is the ratio of dogs to cats still 4:3? Explain.
  lines: 2
  key: No. Now there are 8 dogs and 8 cats, so the ratio is 8:8, or 1:1: for every dog there is one cat.
write: What fraction of the animals are cats now (after the 2 new cats)? Write it in simplest form.
  lines: 2
  key: 8 cats out of 20 animals: 8/20 = 2/5.

# 22. Formula Plug-In
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, calculator (optional)
ican: I can evaluate a formula by substituting values, including formulas with exponents.
directions: Write the formula, substitute the values, and evaluate. Include units in every answer. Use the order of operations: exponents before multiplication.
mistakes: The most common error is reading s² as s × 2 (7² = 14) or s³ as s × 3. Students also drop or mix units: area is in square units, volume in cubic units. In the temperature formula, students sometimes add 32 before multiplying.
notes: Have students write each step on its own line: formula, substitution, value. In item 6, (9/5)(25) = 45, then 45 + 32 = 77 degrees Fahrenheit. The stretch shows that doubling the side multiplies the volume by 8 (2³).

## Warm up: Squares and cubes
table: s | s² | s³
  - 2 | {4} | {8}
  - 3 | {9} | {27}
  - 5 | {25} | {125}
  - 10 | {100} | {1,000}
  - 1/2 | {1/4} | {1/8}

## Main: Plug in
example: A square tile has side s = 9 in. A = s² = 9² = 81. The area is 81 square inches.
fill: A square garden has sides of 7 feet. Use A = s². A = {49 square feet}
fill: A cube-shaped storage box has edges of 4 inches. Use V = s³. V = {64 cubic inches}
fill: A cyclist rides at r = 12 miles per hour for t = 2.5 hours. Use d = rt. d = {30 miles}
fill: A rectangular field is l = 15 m long and w = 8 m wide. Use P = 2l + 2w. P = {46 m}
fill: A triangular sail has base b = 10 cm and height h = 6 cm. Use A = (1/2)bh. A = {30 square cm}
fill: The temperature is C = 25 degrees Celsius. Use F = (9/5)C + 32. F = {77 degrees Fahrenheit}
draw: Show your work for items 1 to 6 | 2.6

## Stretch: Double the side
write: A cube has edges of 4 inches. Another cube has edges of 8 inches. Use V = s³ to find both volumes. How many times greater is the bigger volume?
  lines: 3
  key: 4³ = 64 cubic inches and 8³ = 512 cubic inches. 512 ÷ 64 = 8, so the bigger cube holds 8 times as much, even though its edge is only 2 times as long (2³ = 8).
