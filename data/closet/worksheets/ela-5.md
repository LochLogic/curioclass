# 1. Quote It Exactly
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can quote a text accurately to answer a question and to explain what I infer.
directions: Read the story. Answer each question by copying the exact words from the story inside quotation marks. After each quotation, write the paragraph number in parentheses, like this: (para. 2).
mistakes: Watch for "almost quotes": a word changed, dropped or added (the boat turned to the channel). Have students point to each word in the story as they copy it. Students also forget the closing quotation mark or put the paragraph number inside the quotation marks. A quotation that answers a different question, even if copied perfectly, does not count.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class (about 215 words); paragraph numbers are printed in brackets. The key gives the best quotation for each question; accept another exact quotation from the story that answers the question just as well.

## Read
passage: The Keeper of Gull Point
  **[1]** For thirty years, Ada Harlow had climbed the one hundred twelve steps of Gull Point Lighthouse every evening at sunset. She counted each step out loud, the way her father had taught her.
  **[2]** Tonight a storm was rolling in from the west. "The sea is restless," Ada told her cat, Biscuit, as rain rattled the windows. "That means the ships need us more than ever."
  **[3]** At the top, she wiped the great glass lens until it shone. She lit the lamp, and a beam of gold swept across the black water. Every ten seconds, the light turned and flashed.
  **[4]** Near midnight, Ada spotted a small fishing boat tossing between the waves. It was heading straight for the rocks below the point. Her heart pounded. She grabbed her brass horn and blew three long blasts into the wind.
  **[5]** For a moment, nothing changed. Then, slowly, the boat turned toward the safe channel, following the sweep of her light.
  **[6]** In the morning, a young fisherman knocked on her door, holding a basket of fresh fish. "I couldn't see a thing last night," he said. "Your light was the only thing I could trust."
  **[7]** Ada smiled and set the basket on the table. "That is what a light is for," she said.

## Warm up: Which is exact?
choice: Which one quotes paragraph 3 exactly and correctly?
  - "A beam of gold went across the black water" (para. 3).
  - a beam of gold swept across the black water (para. 3)
  * "a beam of gold swept across the black water" (para. 3).
  - "a beam of gold swept across the black water (para. 3)."

## Main: Answer with a quotation
write: How long has Ada been the lighthouse keeper?
  lines: 2
  key: "For thirty years, Ada Harlow had climbed the one hundred twelve steps of Gull Point Lighthouse every evening at sunset" (para. 1).
write: Why does Ada think the storm makes her job more important?
  lines: 2
  key: "That means the ships need us more than ever" (para. 2).
write: What does the light look like when Ada lights the lamp?
  lines: 2
  key: "a beam of gold swept across the black water" (para. 3); also accept "Every ten seconds, the light turned and flashed" (para. 3).
write: Which sentence shows that Ada is frightened when she sees the boat?
  lines: 2
  key: "Her heart pounded" (para. 4).
write: What does Ada do to warn the boat?
  lines: 2
  key: "She grabbed her brass horn and blew three long blasts into the wind" (para. 4).
write: How does the fisherman feel about Ada's light?
  lines: 2
  key: "Your light was the only thing I could trust" (para. 6).

## Stretch: Quote and infer
write: What does Ada believe about her job? Quote the story, then explain what you can infer that the story does not say outright.
  lines: 4
  starter: Ada says, "..." (para. __). This shows ...
  key: Ada says, "That is what a light is for" (para. 7). She believes keeping ships safe is simply her duty, so she does not need thanks or praise; she has done it every night for thirty years.
# 25. Two Main Ideas, One Summary
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can find two main ideas in an article and write a summary that quotes the text accurately.
directions: Read the article. Find two main ideas and the details that support each one. Then write a summary that states both main ideas and includes at least one exact quotation.
mistakes: Students often pick a detail (the tires were filled with air) as a main idea. Ask, "Is this what a whole part of the article is about, or one fact that supports it?" In summaries, watch for opinions ("bicycles are awesome") and for quotations that are not copied word for word.
notes: The article is original to Curio Class (about 235 words). Dates: Karl Drais's running machine, 1817; pedal bicycles in France, 1860s; high-wheelers, 1870s; John Kemp Starley's Rover safety bicycle, 1885; John Boyd Dunlop's air-filled tire, 1888. Paragraphs 1 to 3 tell how the design changed; paragraphs 4 and 5 tell how bicycles changed people's lives.

## Read
passage: From Running Machine to Bicycle
  **[1]** The first bicycle had no pedals at all. In 1817, a German inventor named Karl Drais built a wooden "running machine." Riders sat on it and pushed along the road with their feet, like a child on a balance bike.
  **[2]** In the 1860s, makers in France added pedals to the front wheel. These bicycles were nicknamed "boneshakers" because their iron-rimmed wheels made every bump hurt. Next came the high-wheeler, with a huge front wheel that could go fast. But riders sat so high that a stone in the road could throw them over the handlebars.
  **[3]** In 1885, John Kemp Starley sold the Rover, a "safety bicycle." Its two wheels were nearly the same size, and a chain turned the back wheel. A few years later, tires filled with air made the ride smooth. The bicycle finally looked much like the ones we ride today.
  **[4]** Safety bicycles changed daily life. In the 1890s, millions of people bought one. For the first time, many workers could live farther from their jobs and ride to work. Young people could visit friends in the next town without a horse.
  **[5]** Bicycles gave many women new freedom, too. Women rode on their own, and many traded long, heavy skirts for clothes that were easier to move in. Riders of every kind also demanded smoother roads, and their campaigns helped pave many roads that cars would later use.

## Warm up: Main idea or detail?
choice: Which is a main idea of paragraphs 1 to 3?
  - The high-wheeler had a huge front wheel.
  * The bicycle's design changed many times until it became safe and easy to ride.
  - Karl Drais was German.
  - Boneshakers had iron-rimmed wheels.
choice: Which is a main idea of paragraphs 4 and 5?
  - Millions of people bought bicycles.
  - Riders wanted smoother roads.
  * Bicycles changed the way people lived and traveled.
  - Women wore long skirts.

## Main: Two main ideas
write: Main idea 1 and its two details
  lines: 3
  key: The design changed many times until bicycles were safe and easy to ride. Details: the 1817 running machine had no pedals (para. 1); the safety bicycle had equal wheels and a chain (para. 3). Also accept the boneshaker or high-wheeler details (para. 2).
write: Main idea 2 and its two details
  lines: 3
  key: Bicycles changed how people lived and traveled. Details: many workers "could live farther from their jobs and ride to work" (para. 4); bicycles "gave many women new freedom" (para. 5). Also accept smoother roads (para. 5).
write: Copy one exact sentence from the article that best supports main idea 2. Use quotation marks and the paragraph number.
  lines: 2
  key: "Safety bicycles changed daily life" (para. 4); also accept "Bicycles gave many women new freedom, too" (para. 5).

## Write: One summary
write: Write a summary of the article in 4 to 6 sentences. State both main ideas, use the most important details, and include at least one exact quotation. Leave out opinions.
  lines: 8
  key: Bicycles changed a great deal over time. The first one, built by Karl Drais in 1817, had no pedals, and later boneshakers and high-wheelers were bumpy or dangerous. In 1885 the Rover "safety bicycle" with nearly equal wheels and a chain made riding safe. Bicycles also changed how people lived: "Safety bicycles changed daily life" (para. 4), letting workers ride to jobs and giving many women new freedom. Riders even pushed for smoother roads.
