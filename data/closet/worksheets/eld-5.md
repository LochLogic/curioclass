# 1. Putting Ideas Together
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can combine two classmates' ideas and add an idea of my own.
directions: Read each pair of comment cards. Find what the two classmates agree on. Write one sentence that puts their ideas together, then add your own idea.
mistakes: Students often repeat both comments word for word instead of naming the shared idea. Ask, "What do both of them think?" and have them say it in a few words first. Watch for an "I would add" that only repeats the cards; it should be a new reason, example or detail.
notes: The comment cards are written for this sheet. Read each pair aloud and have partners say the combined sentence out loud before they write. In the Stretch the two classmates disagree, so students use a contrast frame with "while" instead of "both".

## Warm up: See the pattern
example: **Rosa:** "Our class should read more poems." **Tomás:** "Poems are short, so we could read one every morning."
  **Combined:** Both Rosa and Tomás think we should read more poems, and I would add that we could take turns reading them aloud.
text: **Word bank:** both, also, agree, think, add, build on, in addition, similar, idea, while, however
choice: Which sentence puts Rosa's and Tomás's ideas together best?
  - Rosa likes poems and Tomás likes mornings.
  * Both Rosa and Tomás think reading poems often would be good for our class.
  - Tomás is right and Rosa is wrong.
  - I think poems are too hard.

## Main: Comment cards
cards: Read each pair of cards. Cards with the same topic go together.
  - Ana (school garden) says: "A garden would let us learn science outside, like how seeds grow."
  - Ben (school garden) says: "We could grow vegetables and learn about healthy food."
  - Chloe (library time) says: "More library time means more time to find books we really like."
  - Dev (library time) says: "Kids who choose their own books usually read more."
  - Eli (field trips) says: "Field trips help us see things we only read about in class."
  - Fatima (field trips) says: "At the museum, I understood fossils better when I saw real ones."
  - Gus (recycling) says: "Our class throws away a lot of paper that could be recycled."
  - Hana (recycling) says: "If every room had a recycling bin, people would use it."
write: Put Ana's and Ben's ideas together.
  lines: 2
  starter: Both ___ and ___ think ___, and I would add ___.
  key: Both Ana and Ben think a school garden would help us learn, and I would add that students could take turns caring for it, which teaches responsibility.
write: Put Chloe's and Dev's ideas together.
  lines: 2
  starter: Both ___ and ___ think ___, and I would add ___.
  key: Both Chloe and Dev think more time to choose books would help kids read more, and I would add that classmates could share book recommendations.
write: Put Eli's and Fatima's ideas together.
  lines: 2
  starter: Both ___ and ___ think ___, and I would add ___.
  key: Both Eli and Fatima think seeing real things on field trips helps us learn, and I would add that we remember trips because we use more of our senses.
write: Put Gus's and Hana's ideas together.
  lines: 2
  starter: Both ___ and ___ think ___, and I would add ___.
  key: Both Gus and Hana think our school should recycle more paper, and I would add that students could take turns emptying the bins.

## Stretch: When classmates disagree
text: **Ivan:** "Homework helps us practice what we learned." **Jada:** "Too much homework leaves no time for sports and rest."
write: Combine Ivan's and Jada's ideas. Then say which you agree with more.
  lines: 4
  starter: ___ thinks ___, while ___ thinks ___. I agree more with ___ because ___.
  key: Ivan thinks homework helps us practice, while Jada thinks too much homework leaves no time for sports and rest. I agree more with Jada because short homework can give practice without taking the whole evening. (Either side, with a reason.)

# 7. Seminar Moves Self-Check
minutes: 20
materials: pencil
ican: I can rate how I used discussion moves in a seminar and set a goal for next time.
directions: Fill this in right after a seminar. First, match each discussion move to an example. Then rate how often you used each move and write an example of what you said. Finally, choose one move to use more next time.
mistakes: Students often rate themselves high on every move. Ask for the words they said in the example column; a move with no example is probably "never." Watch for goals that are too general ("talk more"). A good goal names a move and the exact frame.
notes: Use this sheet right after a Socratic seminar or any whole-class discussion. The self-ratings are the student's own and have no key; only the warm-up match does. Collect the sheets and use the goals to set up the next seminar: before it starts, have each student read their goal frame to a partner.

## Warm up: Know the moves
match: Match each move to an example.
  - Ask an open question = Why do you think the author ended the story that way?
  - Build on an idea = I would like to build on what Ken said. Also, ...
  - Disagree with evidence = I see it differently because the text says ...
  - Cite the text = In paragraph 3, the author writes ...
  - Paraphrase = So what you are saying is ...
  - Invite someone in = Rosa, we have not heard from you yet. What do you think?

## Main: Rate yourself
chart: Never | Once | 2 or more times | What I said (an example)
  labels: Ask an open question; Build on an idea; Disagree with evidence; Cite the text; Paraphrase; Invite someone in
  height: 0.55
  key: Self-ratings vary. Check that every move marked "once" or "2 or more" has an example in the last column.
write: Which move was your strongest today? How do you know?
  lines: 3
  starter: My strongest move was ___. I know because ___.
  key: Example: My strongest move was citing the text. I know because twice I told the group the paragraph number and read the sentence that proved my point.

## Main: My goal
write: Choose one move to use more next time. Why did you choose it?
  lines: 3
  starter: Next time I will use ___ more because ___.
  key: Example: Next time I will use invite someone in more because three people did not talk today, and the seminar is better when we hear more ideas.
write: Write the exact sentence you will say to use that move.
  lines: 2
  starter: I will say, "___."
  key: Example: I will say, "Marco, what do you think about the question? I would like to hear your idea."

## Stretch: Help the group
write: What is one thing the whole group could do better in the next seminar? Use a polite frame.
  lines: 3
  starter: Our group could ___. One way to do this is ___.
  key: Example: Our group could let more people speak. One way to do this is for each person to wait until two others have spoken before talking again.

# 20. Word Problem Task Strips
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, scissors (optional), scratch paper
ican: I can solve word problems and explain my operation in one clear sentence.
directions: Solve each word problem on the task strips. Write the answer with its unit on the strip. Then write one sentence that explains which operation you used and why.
mistakes: Students often explain with "because the problem said so" or name only the operation. A good explanation says what the numbers mean: "I multiplied because there are 24 equal groups of 12." Watch for missing units (288, not 288 markers) and for card 5, where students divide 1/2 by 4 instead of 4 by 1/2.
notes: The strips can be cut apart and spread around the room for a scoot, or solved in place. Accept any correct operation explained clearly: card 3 can be 3/4 + 3/4 + 3/4 + 3/4 or 4 x 3/4. Keys: 24 x 12 = 288; 156 ÷ 4 = 39; 4 x 3/4 = 3; 6 x 2.5 = 15; 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8; 8.5 x 4 = 34.

## Warm up: Model
example: **Strip:** A pack has 6 juice boxes. How many juice boxes are in 9 packs? **54 juice boxes**
  **Explanation:** I multiplied 9 x 6 because there are 9 equal groups with 6 juice boxes in each.
text: **Word bank:** multiply, divide, add, subtract, equal groups, each, share equally, total, how many in each, how many groups, area, because

## Main: Task strips
cards: Solve each strip. Write the answer with its unit.
  - The art room orders 24 boxes of markers. Each box holds 12 markers. How many markers? {288 markers}
  - 156 students ride 4 buses. Each bus has the same number. How many students are on each bus? {39 students}
  - A muffin recipe uses 3/4 cup of oats. How many cups of oats are needed for 4 batches? {3 cups}
  - Mia runs 2.5 km every day for 6 days. How far does she run in all? {15 km}
  - Ty has 4 cups of juice. He pours 1/2-cup servings. How many servings can he pour? {8 servings}
  - A garden is a rectangle 8.5 m long and 4 m wide. What is its area? {34 square meters}
write: Explain strip 1.
  lines: 1
  starter: I ___ because ___.
  key: I multiplied 24 x 12 because there are 24 equal groups of 12 markers.
write: Explain strip 2.
  lines: 1
  starter: I ___ because ___.
  key: I divided 156 ÷ 4 because 156 students are shared equally among 4 buses.
write: Explain strip 3.
  lines: 1
  starter: I ___ because ___.
  key: I multiplied 4 x 3/4 because there are 4 batches with 3/4 cup in each.
write: Explain strip 4.
  lines: 1
  starter: I ___ because ___.
  key: I multiplied 6 x 2.5 because she runs the same distance, 2.5 km, on each of 6 days.
write: Explain strip 5.
  lines: 1
  starter: I ___ because ___.
  key: I divided 4 ÷ 1/2 because I need to know how many halves fit in 4 cups, and each cup has 2 halves.
write: Explain strip 6.
  lines: 1
  starter: I ___ because ___.
  key: I multiplied 8.5 x 4 because the area of a rectangle is length times width.

## Stretch: Write a strip
write: Write your own word problem for 3 x 1/4. Solve it and explain it in one sentence.
  lines: 3
  starter: Problem: ___. Answer: ___. I ___ because ___.
  key: Example: Problem: Each sandwich uses 1/4 of a cup of cheese. How much cheese for 3 sandwiches? Answer: 3/4 cup. I multiplied 3 x 1/4 because there are 3 equal groups of 1/4 cup.

# 21. Of, Times, Per
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell which operation a math phrase means and use it in a problem sentence.
directions: Read each math phrase and example. Write the operation it means (multiply or divide) and solve. Then use the phrases in your own problem sentences.
mistakes: "Per" trips students up: it can mean divide (120 miles in 3 hours: how many miles per hour?) or multiply ($2 per pound for 5 pounds). Have them ask, "Do I know the amount for one, or am I finding it?" Students also read "of" as add; show 1/3 of 12 with 12 counters in 3 groups.
notes: Read each phrase aloud and have students say it in a full sentence ("One third of twelve is four."). Fractions of a number use multiplying: 1/3 of 12 = 1/3 x 12. For the problem sentences, accept any correct problem that uses the phrase with its meaning.

## Warm up: Model
example: **of** with a fraction means multiply: 1/2 of 8 = 1/2 x 8 = 4.
  **split into** equal parts means divide: 8 split into 2 equal parts = 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
text: **Word bank:** of, times, split into, per, each, equal groups, multiply, divide, product, quotient, rate

## Main: What does it mean?
table: Phrase | Example | Operation | Answer
  - of | 1/3 of 12 | {multiply} | {4}
  - of | 2/5 of 20 | {multiply} | {8}
  - times | 4 times as many as 7 | {multiply} | {28}
  - times | 6 times 9 | {multiply} | {54}
  - split into | 24 split into 4 equal groups | {divide} | {6}
  - split into | 45 split into 9 equal parts | {divide} | {5}
  - per | 120 miles in 3 hours: how many miles per hour? | {divide} | {40}
  - per | $2 per pound: cost of 5 pounds | {multiply} | {$10}

## Main: Use the phrases
write: Write a problem with **of**. Then solve it.
  lines: 2
  starter: ___ of ___ is ___.
  key: Example: Lin ate 1/4 of a pizza with 8 slices. 1/4 of 8 is 2 slices.
write: Write a problem with **times**. Then solve it.
  lines: 2
  starter: ___ has ___ times as many ___ as ___, so ___.
  key: Example: Max has 3 times as many stickers as Pia. Pia has 5, so Max has 3 x 5 = 15 stickers.
write: Write a problem with **split into**. Then solve it.
  lines: 2
  starter: ___ split into ___ equal groups is ___ in each group.
  key: Example: 30 students split into 5 equal teams is 6 students in each team, because 30 ÷ 5 = 6.
write: Write a problem with **per**. Then solve it.
  lines: 2
  starter: ___ costs ___ per ___, so ___.
  key: Example: Apples cost $3 per bag, so 4 bags cost 4 x $3 = $12.

## Stretch: Two kinds of per
write: Look at the two "per" rows in the table. Why is one divide and the other multiply?
  lines: 3
  starter: In the miles row, we ___ because ___. In the pounds row, we ___ because ___.
  key: In the miles row, we divide because we know the total (120 miles) and need the amount for one hour. In the pounds row, we multiply because we know the amount for one pound ($2) and need the total for 5 pounds.

# 33. The Colony Was Founded
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can rewrite a history sentence in the passive voice with was and a past participle.
directions: Each sentence starts with who did the action. Rewrite it so it starts with the place or thing that the action happened to. Use "was" and the past participle, then add "by" and who did it.
mistakes: Students often keep the past tense instead of the past participle ("was gave" instead of "was given"). Point to the participle chart. Watch for sentences that drop the date or the "by" phrase, and for "were" with a singular subject (Georgia was, not Georgia were).
notes: Facts: English colonists founded Jamestown in 1607; the Pilgrims founded Plymouth Colony in 1620; Roger Williams founded Providence in 1636; the Massachusetts Bay Colony founded Harvard College in 1636; Benjamin Franklin and friends started the Library Company of Philadelphia, a lending library, in 1731; King Charles II granted William Penn the charter for Pennsylvania in 1681; James Oglethorpe founded the Georgia colony at Savannah in 1733. The Stretch shows why history books often drop the "by" phrase: when the doer is unknown or not the point.

## Warm up
example: **Active:** English colonists **founded** Jamestown in 1607.
  **Passive:** Jamestown **was founded** in 1607 **by** English colonists.
text: **Word bank:** passive voice, active voice, was, were, past participle, by, founded, built, given, started, written, colony
table: Verb | Past tense | Past participle (was ___)
  - found | founded | {founded}
  - build | built | {built}
  - give | gave | {given}
  - write | wrote | {written}
choice: Which sentence is in the passive voice?
  - The Pilgrims sailed on the Mayflower.
  - Settlers built houses near the river.
  * Plymouth Colony was founded by the Pilgrims.
  - Roger Williams left Massachusetts.

## Main: Rewrite in the passive voice
write: The Pilgrims founded Plymouth Colony in 1620.
  lines: 1
  starter: Plymouth Colony was ...
  key: Plymouth Colony was founded in 1620 by the Pilgrims.
write: Roger Williams founded Providence in 1636.
  lines: 1
  starter: Providence was ...
  key: Providence was founded in 1636 by Roger Williams.
write: The Massachusetts Bay Colony founded Harvard College in 1636.
  lines: 1
  starter: Harvard College was ...
  key: Harvard College was founded in 1636 by the Massachusetts Bay Colony.
write: Benjamin Franklin started a lending library in Philadelphia in 1731.
  lines: 1
  starter: A lending library was ...
  key: A lending library was started in Philadelphia in 1731 by Benjamin Franklin.
write: King Charles II gave the land for Pennsylvania to William Penn in 1681.
  lines: 2
  starter: The land for Pennsylvania was ...
  key: The land for Pennsylvania was given to William Penn in 1681 by King Charles II.
write: James Oglethorpe founded the Georgia colony in 1733.
  lines: 1
  starter: The Georgia colony was ...
  key: The Georgia colony was founded in 1733 by James Oglethorpe.

## Stretch: No "by" needed
text: History books often write: "Many houses in the colonies **were built** of wood." There is no "by ___" phrase.
choice: Why does this sentence leave out "by ___"?
  * The builders are unknown or not important; the houses are the point.
  - Houses build themselves.
  - Passive sentences can never use "by."
  - The sentence is in the active voice.
write: Write your own passive sentence about a colony with no "by" phrase.
  lines: 2
  starter: In the colony, ___ was (were) ___.
  key: Example: In the colony, corn was grown in small fields, and candles were made from animal fat.
