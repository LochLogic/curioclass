# 1. Fact, Value or Policy Claim?
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can tell fact, value and policy claims apart and write each kind on a school topic.
directions: Cut out the 12 claim cards. Sort each one: a fact claim (can be checked as true or false), a value claim (judges something as good, bad, better or more important) or a policy claim (says what should be done). Then write one claim of each kind about a school topic.
mistakes: Students often call any claim they agree with a fact. A fact claim can be checked with evidence even if it turns out to be false. Watch for "should" hidden in value claims and "best" hidden in policy claims; the key words are clues, not rules, so ask what the claim is really asking the listener to accept.
notes: Signal words: fact claims use is, are, was, more than, causes; value claims use best, worse, important, fair, moving; policy claims use should, ought to, must, needs to. A fact claim does not have to be true to be a fact claim: "Our school day starts at 7:45 a.m." is checkable either way. Teenagers needing more sleep than most adults matches CDC and AASM guidance (8 to 10 hours for teens, 7 or more for adults). The glue boxes are short for sentence strips: students can glue the cards in overlapping rows, or glue them on the back under the same three headings.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** fact claim, value claim, policy claim, evidence, judge, should, ought to, must, best, important, can be checked, I claim that
example: **Fact:** The school library has 9,000 books. (Count them to check.)
  **Value:** Reading for fun is more important than reading for a grade. (A judgment.)
  **Policy:** The library should stay open until 5:00 p.m. (An action.)

## Main: Sort the claims
sort: Glue each claim card under its type.
  - Fact claim: Our school day starts at 7:45 a.m., The new library has more computers than the old one., More students rode bikes to school this year than last year., Teenagers need more sleep each night than most adults.
  - Value claim: Group projects are the best way to learn., The fall play was the most moving performance in years., Honesty matters more than winning a game., Art class is just as important as math class.
  - Policy claim: The school should start the day at 8:30 a.m., Students ought to be allowed to use phones at lunch., The city must add a bike lane on the road to school., Every student should take a public speaking class before graduating.

## Main: Explain a choice
write: Pick one card that was hard to sort. Explain where you put it and why.
  lines: 3
  starter: I sorted "___" as a ___ claim because ___.
  key: Example: I sorted "Teenagers need more sleep each night than most adults" as a fact claim because it can be checked with sleep research, even though it sounds like advice.

## Main: Write your own
write: Write a **fact claim** about your school or a school topic.
  lines: 2
  starter: It is a fact that ...
  key: Example: It is a fact that our cafeteria serves about 600 lunches a day. (Checkable with a number or record.)
write: Write a **value claim** on the same topic.
  lines: 2
  starter: I believe ___ is (better, more important, fairer) than ___ because ...
  key: Example: I believe a longer lunch is more important than a longer class period because students need time to eat and rest.
write: Write a **policy claim** on the same topic.
  lines: 2
  starter: The school should ___ so that ___.
  key: Example: The school should add a second lunch line so that every student has at least 20 minutes to eat.

## Stretch: Claims work together
write: How could your fact claim support your policy claim in a debate?
  lines: 3
  starter: My fact claim supports my policy because ...
  key: Example: My fact claim supports my policy because if the cafeteria serves 600 lunches with one line, the numbers show why the wait is long, which gives a reason to add a second line.

# 21. Explaining Models and Data Check
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can describe a graph, label the parts of a model and name a limitation of a model.
directions: Plot the data and describe what the graph shows. Then match the parts of a classroom model to what they stand for, and explain what the model cannot show.
mistakes: Students describe a graph by listing every point. Push for the trend and two numbers that show it. For limitations, "it's not real" is too vague; name a specific thing the model leaves out or gets wrong (size, distance, tilt).
notes: The oxygen values are standard figures for fresh water at sea level (about 14.6 mg/L at 0 °C, 11.3 at 10 °C, 9.1 at 20 °C and 7.6 at 30 °C), rounded to one decimal. The drop is steeper at low temperatures. The lamp and globe model is a common day and night demonstration.

## Warm up: Language for data and models
text: **Word bank:** increase, decrease, trend, steady, steep, level off, model, represent, stands for, scale, limitation, does not show, dissolved oxygen
fill:
  - When a line goes down from left to right, the values {decrease}.
  - A model {represents} a real thing in a simpler way.

## Main: Describe a graph
text: Dissolved oxygen is the oxygen gas mixed into water. Fish and other water animals breathe it. The table shows the most oxygen that fresh water can hold at different temperatures.
table: Water temperature (°C) | Dissolved oxygen (mg/L)
  - 0 | 14.6
  - 5 | 12.8
  - 10 | 11.3
  - 15 | 10.1
  - 20 | 9.1
  - 25 | 8.3
  - 30 | 7.6
graph: Water temperature (°C) | 0 30 5 | Dissolved oxygen (mg/L) | 0 16 2
  key: 0,14.6 5,12.8 10,11.3 15,10.1 20,9.1 25,8.3 30,7.6
write: Describe the trend in the graph. Use two numbers.
  lines: 3
  starter: As water temperature increases from ___ to ___, dissolved oxygen ___ from ___ to ___. The line is steeper ___.
  key: As water temperature increases from 0 °C to 30 °C, dissolved oxygen decreases from 14.6 to 7.6 mg/L, almost half. The line is steeper at low temperatures and levels off a little as the water warms.
write: What could this mean for fish in a pond on a hot summer day?
  lines: 2
  starter: The graph suggests that ...
  key: The graph suggests that warm water holds less oxygen, so fish may have less oxygen to breathe on hot days.

## Main: Label a model
text: **Model:** In a dark room, a student shines a lamp at a globe and slowly spins the globe.
match: What does each part of the model represent?
  - The lamp = The Sun
  - The globe = Earth
  - Spinning the globe = Earth's rotation on its axis
  - The side of the globe facing the lamp = Places having daytime
  - The side of the globe away from the lamp = Places having night

## Stretch: Limitation
write: Name one limitation of the lamp and globe model.
  lines: 3
  starter: One limitation of this model is that it does not show ___, because in reality ___.
  key: One limitation is that it does not show true size and distance, because in reality the Sun is about 109 times wider than Earth and about 150 million km away, not a lamp a few feet from a globe.
