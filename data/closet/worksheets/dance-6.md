# 1. BEST Elements Chart
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, open floor space
ican: I can analyze a dance phrase using Body, Energy, Space and Time.
directions: Sort the warm up observations by element. Then watch the phrase (your teacher will perform it, or read it below and try it yourself) and list 2 specific observations for each BEST element.
mistakes: Students write vague observations ("it was cool," "lots of moving"). Push for specifics: which body part, what kind of energy, which level or direction, how fast. Energy and Time get confused: "quick" is Time; "sharp" or "strong" is Energy.
notes: BEST is a common classroom frame for the elements of dance: Body (what moves, shapes), Energy (how it moves: force, flow, weight), Space (where: levels, directions, pathways, size) and Time (when: tempo, rhythm, pauses). Perform the sample phrase twice, or let students try it in small groups and observe each other. Students may also analyze a phrase they made earlier in the unit.

## Warm up: Which element?
example: "The dancer froze for 2 counts" is about **Time**: it describes when and how long.
table: Observation | Element (Body, Energy, Space or Time)
  - The arms made a wide, round shape. | {Body}
  - The punch was sharp and strong. | {Energy}
  - She traveled on a diagonal toward the audience. | {Space}
  - The turns sped up, then slowed down. | {Time}
  - He stayed in a crouch at a low level. | {Space}
  - The walk was heavy, like trudging through mud. | {Energy}

## Main: The phrase
passage: Sample Phrase: Storm Arrives
  Counts 1-4: Stand upstage center with the feet together. Slowly raise both arms out to the sides until they reach high above the head, palms facing each other.
  Counts 5-6: Bring both fists down fast and hard in front of the body while bending the knees into a deep squat.
  Counts 7-8: Hold the squat, perfectly still, with the head bowed.
  Counts 1-4: Spring up and travel forward on a zigzag path with four quick, light runs on the balls of the feet.
  Counts 5-8: Spin once to the right with the arms wrapped around the body, then unfold one arm slowly toward the audience and hold the reach until the music ends.
  Count the phrase as two sets of 8, 16 counts in all. As you watch or try it, notice what the body does, how the energy changes, where it travels and how the timing shifts from slow to sudden to still.
  The phrase is short, but almost every element changes at least once. The body grows tall, then folds small, then spirals. The energy swings from calm to forceful to light. The dancer starts far from the audience and ends close to it. The timing moves from slow to sudden, then stops completely before rushing forward again. A choreographer uses contrasts like these on purpose, because a change is what makes an audience notice.

## Main: BEST chart
text: Rows in order: **B**ody (what moves, shapes), **E**nergy (force, flow, weight), **S**pace (levels, directions, pathways), **T**ime (tempo, rhythm, stillness).
chart: Element | Observation 1 (what I saw, and on which counts) | Observation 2 (what I saw, and on which counts)
  rows: 4
  key: Body: arms rise to a high V; fists strike down; arms wrap the body in the spin. Energy: sustained rise; strong, sudden strike; light runs. Space: starts upstage center; drops to a low level; zigzag path forward toward the audience. Time: slow 4-count rise; 2-count hold of stillness; quick runs; slow final reach.
write: Which element changes the most in this phrase? Give evidence from two different counts.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Model: Energy changes the most: counts 1-4 are slow and sustained, counts 5-6 are sudden and strong, and the runs are light and quick.

## Stretch
write: Change one element of the phrase on purpose (for example, do the strike slowly). How does that change what the phrase means or how it feels?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the element, the change and its effect, for example "A slow strike feels heavy and sad instead of angry, so the storm seems tired instead of fierce."

# 21. Notes into Actions
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, open floor space, a run-through of a class dance
ican: I can turn a director's notes into specific changes and check that I made them.
directions: Turn each director's note into a specific action you can do on stage: say what, where or when. Then do two run-throughs and check off each action you remembered.
mistakes: Students restate the note instead of turning it into an action ("Be bigger" becomes "be big"). A specific action names a body part, a count, a place or a direction. Students also stop checking after Run 1: the second run is where notes stick.
notes: These six notes are typical when a dance moves from the classroom to a new space (a gym, a stage, outdoors). Read each note aloud and act out the vague version, then a specific fix. Run 1 and Run 2 can be the same day or two different rehearsals. In Run 2 students can ask a partner to watch for their actions.

## Warm up: Vague or specific?
choice: Which is the most specific action for the note "Use the whole stage"?
  - Move around more.
  * On counts 1-8, travel all the way to the downstage right corner.
  - Remember the stage is big.
choice: Which is the most specific action for the note "I can't see your face"?
  - Smile.
  - Face the front.
  * On the first 8 counts, lift my chin and look over the audience's heads, not at the floor.

## Main: Translate the notes
write: Note 1: "Bigger!"
  lines: 1
  key: Model: Stretch my arms all the way to the fingertips on the reach (counts 3-4) and take wider steps on the travel.
write: Note 2: "You're late on the turn."
  lines: 1
  key: Model: Start my turn exactly on count 5 by preparing on count 4 and listening for the drum hit.
write: Note 3: "The lines are crooked."
  lines: 1
  key: Model: Before we start, check the dancer on my left with my eyes and stand on my tape mark.
write: Note 4: "The ending is fuzzy."
  lines: 1
  key: Model: Hit my final pose on count 8 and hold it, without moving, until the music fully stops.
write: Note 5: "Too quiet: we can't feel the energy."
  lines: 1
  key: Model: Make the stomps on counts 1 and 3 strong and sharp, and breathe out on each one.
write: Note 6: "This space is wider than our classroom."
  lines: 1
  key: Model: Spread the formation so there are two big steps between me and the next dancer, and travel farther on the zigzag.

## Stretch
write: Which note was hardest to fix? What will you do in the next rehearsal to make it stick?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names one note, explains why it was hard (for example, the count goes by fast), and gives a plan such as marking it slowly first or asking a partner to cue it.
