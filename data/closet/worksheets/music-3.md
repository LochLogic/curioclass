# 1. Five Lines, Four Spaces
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can name the lines and spaces of the treble staff.
directions: Number the lines and spaces of the staff from the bottom up. Trace the treble clef. Then name notes and draw your own staff with notes in the right places.
pairs: note-ninja
mistakes: Students often number from the top down, because we read pages from the top. Hold a ladder picture: you climb a staff from the bottom. Watch for notes drawn so a line cuts the edge of a space note, or a line note floating between lines: a line note has the line through its middle; a space note sits between two lines and touches both.
notes: Treble staff from the bottom: lines E G B D F ("Every Good Boy Does Fine") and spaces F A C E. The treble clef is also called the G clef because its curl wraps around line 2, the G line. Placing notes needs a blank staff, so in Part C students rule their own five lines; staff paper works too.

## Warm up: Count them
fill:
  - A staff has {5} lines and {4} spaces.
  - We number lines and spaces from the {bottom} up.
  - The treble clef curls around line {2}, so it is also called the {G} clef.

## Trace the clef
text: Trace each treble clef. Start in the middle of the curl, circle out and up to the top loop, then come straight down to the hook at the bottom.

text: Write the letter name under each note. Then fill in the table.

text: Use a ruler to draw a five-line staff in the box. Draw a whole note (an open oval) on each place below, left to right, and write its letter name under it.

# 21. Melody Partner Critique
minutes: 30
materials: pencil; a xylophone, glockenspiel or keyboard with C D E G A (or a recorder)
ican: I can give helpful feedback on a pentatonic melody.
directions: Practice on the sample melody first. Then play your partner's melody two times. Rate it, give evidence, and suggest one change.
pairs: melody-maker
mistakes: Feedback like "it's good" or "I don't like it" gives the composer nothing to change. Ask for a note name or a measure number in every answer. Watch for students who suggest adding F or B: those notes are not in the C pentatonic scale.
notes: The C pentatonic scale is C D E G A (do re mi so la): no F and no B, so almost any order sounds pleasant. On barred instruments, take off the F and B bars before class. A melody that ends on C (do) sounds finished; one that ends on D or A sounds like a question. Pair students who have each written a 4-measure melody of letter names. Rubric scores are 3 (strong), 2 (getting there), 1 (not yet).

## Warm up: The pentatonic scale
fill:
  - A pentatonic scale has {5} different notes.
  - The C pentatonic scale is C, D, E, {G}, {A}.
choice: Which note is NOT in the C pentatonic scale?
  - E
  * F
  - G
text: Sample melody (a bar line separates the measures): | C D E G | A G E D | C D E G | E D C C |
choice: What is the shape of the sample melody in measures 1 and 2?
  - It stays on one note.
  * It goes up, then comes back down, like a hill.
  - It only goes down.
choice: Does the sample melody sound finished at the end? Why?
  * Yes, because it ends on C, the home note.
  - No, because it ends on a high note.
  - No, because it has no A.

## Main: Rate your partner's melody
text: My partner's melody (write the letters): ________ | ________ | ________ | ________
table: Criterion | 3 Strong | 2 Getting there | 1 Not yet
  - Shape | Clear ups and downs, with a high point | Some movement, but wanders | Stays on one or two notes
  - Ending | Ends on C and sounds finished | Ends on C, but the last step is a jump | Ends on another note
  - Singable | Easy to sing; steps and small skips | One hard jump | Many big jumps
write: Circle a score for each row. Then give evidence for your Shape score: name the notes or measure that show it.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names notes or a measure, for example: "Shape 3: measure 1 climbs C D E G, then measure 2 peaks on A and comes back down."
write: Give evidence for your Ending and Singable scores.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Look for a note name, for example: "Ending 1: it stops on D, so it sounds like a question. Singable 2: the jump from C up to A in measure 3 is hard to sing."
write: Suggest one change. Say exactly which note or measure to change and why.
  lines: 3
  starter: In measure ___, try ...
  key: Answers vary. A useful suggestion is specific and uses only C D E G A, for example: "In measure 4, change the last note from D to C so the melody sounds finished."

## Stretch: Try it
write: Play your partner's melody again with your change. Did it sound better? Explain.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A good answer says what changed in the sound, for example: "Yes, ending on C made it sound done instead of stopping in the middle."
