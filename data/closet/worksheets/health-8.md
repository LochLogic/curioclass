# 1. Drug Categories Chart
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can classify drugs by how they affect the body and brain.
directions: Read the category key. Write the category for each of the 10 substances. Then write one effect for each category and answer the questions.
mistakes: Students often call nicotine and caffeine "not real drugs." Both are stimulants that change how the brain works, and nicotine is highly addictive. Opioids also slow the body like depressants; they get their own category because of how they act on pain and how easily they cause overdose. Some students think prescription drugs are always safe: oxycodone and benzodiazepines are prescription drugs that are dangerous when misused.
notes: Categories follow the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) and the DEA's drug fact sheets. Keep the sheet factual; no street names or details of use are needed. Naloxone is a medicine that can reverse an opioid overdose; many states let anyone carry it. Fentanyl is now found in many fake pills that look like prescription drugs, which is why a pill not from a pharmacy is never safe. Follow up privately with any student who shares personal or family use.

## Warm up: The category key
text: **Stimulants** speed up the brain and body. **Depressants** slow down the brain and body. **Opioids** block pain signals and slow breathing. **Cannabis** products contain THC, which changes thinking and memory. **Hallucinogens** change how a person sees, hears and feels reality.

## Main: Classify the substances
table: Substance | Category
  - Caffeine | {stimulant}
  - Alcohol | {depressant}
  - Fentanyl | {opioid}
  - Marijuana | {cannabis}
  - Nicotine | {stimulant}
  - LSD | {hallucinogen}
  - Benzodiazepines (prescription sedatives) | {depressant}
  - Oxycodone (prescription painkiller) | {opioid}
  - Methamphetamine | {stimulant}
  - Psilocybin mushrooms | {hallucinogen}

## Main: One effect of each category
table: Category | One effect on the body or brain
  - Stimulants | {Faster heart rate and higher blood pressure; feeling alert, then a crash}
  - Depressants | {Slowed reactions, drowsiness, poor coordination; can slow breathing}
  - Opioids | {Slowed breathing that can stop in an overdose; highly addictive}
  - Cannabis | {Trouble with memory, attention and coordination; can harm the developing brain}
  - Hallucinogens | {Seeing or hearing things that are not there; changed sense of time; unpredictable reactions}

## Think about it
choice: Mixing alcohol with a benzodiazepine is very dangerous. Why?
  - They cancel each other out
  * Both are depressants, so together they can slow breathing to a dangerous level
  - Stimulants and depressants always explode together
choice: What medicine can reverse an opioid overdose?
  - Caffeine
  * Naloxone
  - Ibuprofen

## Stretch
write: Fentanyl is often hidden in fake pills made to look like real prescription medicine. What is one safe rule this fact suggests? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: Only take medicine prescribed to you and given by a pharmacy or a trusted adult. A pill from a friend or online could contain fentanyl, and even a tiny amount can cause a deadly overdose.

# 21. Healthy, Unhealthy, Abusive
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can tell the difference between healthy, unhealthy and abusive relationship behaviors.
directions: Relationships fall on a spectrum. Cut out the 12 behavior cards and glue each one under healthy, unhealthy or abusive. Then explain the line between two categories.
mistakes: Students often put "checks your phone" or "tells you who you can see" under unhealthy because it can look like caring. When one person controls the other, it is abusive. Watch for students who think abuse must be physical: threats, isolation and control are abuse too.
notes: The spectrum follows the healthy, unhealthy and abusive framework used by loveisrespect, the national youth dating abuse helpline (call 1-866-331-9474 or text LOVEIS to 22522; check the numbers before printing). Behaviors apply to friendships and dating relationships. Use story framing only; never ask students to describe their own relationships. Have counselor contacts ready; a disclosure means following your school's reporting policy.

## Warm up
choice: Which is the best description of a healthy relationship?
  - One person makes all the decisions
  * Both people feel safe, respected and free to be themselves
  - The two people never disagree

## Main: Sort the behaviors
sort: Glue each card under its column.
  - Healthy: Talks through disagreements calmly, Respects when you say no, Supports time with your other friends, Is happy for you when you succeed
  - Unhealthy: Gives the silent treatment after an argument, Teases in ways that hurt then says "just kidding", Often cancels plans without telling you, Pressures you to spend all your free time together
  - Abusive: Demands your passwords and checks your phone, Threatens to hurt you or themselves if you leave, Pushes or grabs you when angry, Tells you which friends you are allowed to see

## Explain the line
write: What is the difference between an unhealthy behavior and an abusive one? Use one card from each column.
  lines: 4
  starter: An unhealthy behavior like ... but an abusive behavior like ...
  key: Unhealthy behaviors (like the silent treatment) hurt the relationship but can change if both people talk and work on it. Abusive behaviors (like demanding passwords or threats) are used to gain power and control over the other person, and they can make that person feel afraid. Any physical violence is abuse.
write: What is the difference between a healthy behavior and an unhealthy one? Use one card from each column.
  lines: 3
  key: Healthy behaviors show respect and trust (like talking through disagreements calmly). Unhealthy ones show poor communication or pressure (like giving the silent treatment) even if no one means harm.

## Stretch
write: Maya notices her friend Kira's partner checks Kira's phone and tells her who she can hang out with. Write two things Maya could do to help.
  lines: 3
  key: Tell Kira she is worried and that Kira does not deserve to be controlled; listen without judging; encourage Kira to talk to a trusted adult or counselor; share the loveisrespect helpline; tell a trusted adult herself if Kira is in danger.
