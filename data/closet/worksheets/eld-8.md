# 1. Summarizing an Argument
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can summarize an argument by naming its claim, its reasons and the evidence for each reason.
directions: Read the opinion article. Find the author's claim, three reasons and the evidence for each reason. Fill in the organizer, then write an objective summary of the argument.
mistakes: Students often list evidence as a reason. A reason answers "why?"; evidence is the fact, number or example that proves the reason. Watch for summaries that add the student's own opinion ("which is a great idea"). A summary of an argument reports what the author argues.
notes: The article is original to Curio Class (about 390 words). The district, schools, people and numbers are fictional. The author answers one counterclaim in the fifth paragraph (students need a break); that is the Stretch.

## Read
passage: Keep the Library Doors Open This Summer
  Every June, the libraries in our district's three middle schools lock their doors until late August. For ten weeks, hundreds of books sit on shelves that no student can reach. I believe the Riverside School District should keep each middle school library open two days a week during the summer.
  First, summer library hours would help students keep their reading skills strong. Teachers often see students lose some reading progress over a long break, especially students who read very little in the summer. Last year, Lincoln Middle School tried a small pilot and opened its library on Tuesdays in July. One hundred forty students borrowed more than 1,100 books. In the fall, the librarian found that pilot students had kept their reading levels, while many students who did not attend had slipped.
  Second, not every student can easily get to the public library. The nearest public branch is four miles from Lincoln Middle School, and the bus that goes there runs only once an hour. A school library, by contrast, is within walking distance for most students. When I asked my neighbors with younger kids, several told me their children would read more if the library were close by.
  Third, the plan would cost very little. According to the district's own budget report, staffing three libraries for two days a week would cost about $9,000 for the whole summer. That is less than one percent of what the district spends each year on new textbooks. Volunteers from the high school could also help shelve books and run reading games, which would keep costs down.
  Some people argue that students need a break from school in the summer. I agree that rest matters. But an open library is not a classroom. No one would be required to come, and there would be no grades or homework. Students could simply pick books they enjoy, which is exactly the kind of reading that builds a lifelong habit.
  Our district already owns the books, the shelves and the buildings. Locking them away for ten weeks wastes all three. By opening school libraries two days a week, Riverside can help students stay strong readers, reach families who cannot easily travel, and do it for a small cost. The school board should approve this plan at its May meeting.
  Written by Amira Haddad, eighth grade

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** claim, reason, evidence, counterclaim, response, the author argues, according to, first, second, third, objective, summarize
choice: Which sentence states the author's claim?
  - Every June, the libraries in our district's three middle schools lock their doors.
  * I believe the Riverside School District should keep each middle school library open two days a week during the summer.
  - The nearest public branch is four miles from Lincoln Middle School.
  - Some people argue that students need a break from school in the summer.

## Main: Chart the argument
write: State the claim in your own words.
  lines: 2
  starter: The author argues that ...
  key: The author argues that the Riverside district should open its middle school libraries two days a week in the summer.
chart: Reason (why?) | Evidence from the article (what proves it?)
  rows: 3
  key: 1. Summer hours help students keep reading skills: the Lincoln pilot had 140 students borrow more than 1,100 books, and pilot students kept their reading levels while many others slipped. 2. Not every student can get to the public library: the branch is four miles away and the bus runs once an hour, but the school library is walkable. 3. The plan costs little: about $9,000 for the summer, less than one percent of the yearly textbook budget, and volunteers could help.
choice: Which piece of evidence is the weakest, because it is based on a few conversations rather than data?
  - 140 students borrowed more than 1,100 books.
  - The bus runs only once an hour.
  * Several neighbors said their children would read more if the library were close by.
  - The plan would cost about $9,000.

## Main: Write the summary
write: Write an objective summary of the argument (3 to 5 sentences). Do not add your own opinion.
  lines: 6
  starter: In "Keep the Library Doors Open This Summer," Amira Haddad argues that ... First, ... Second, ... Third, ...
  key: In "Keep the Library Doors Open This Summer," Amira Haddad argues that Riverside's middle school libraries should open two days a week in summer. First, she says it would help students keep their reading skills, pointing to a pilot where 140 students borrowed over 1,100 books and kept their reading levels. Second, she notes many students cannot easily reach the public library, which is four miles away by an hourly bus. Third, she says the plan would cost only about $9,000. She ends by asking the school board to approve it in May.

## Stretch: The other side
write: What counterclaim does the author answer, and how does she respond?
  lines: 3
  starter: The author admits that some people think ___. She responds that ___.
  key: The author admits that some people think students need a break from school in the summer. She responds that an open library is not a classroom: coming is optional and there are no grades or homework, so students can read for fun.

# 7. Summarizing and Citing Check
minutes: 40
materials: pencil
ican: I can summarize an argument, cite sources correctly and tell a paraphrase from plagiarism.
directions: Read the argument and the two source cards. Write an objective summary of the argument. Then cite each source in a sentence, and find the plagiarized sentence.
mistakes: Students often summarize the topic (composting) instead of the argument (the school should compost, and why). In citations, watch for the title in place of the author, a missing page number, or the period inside the parentheses. Plagiarism is not only copying a whole paragraph: one copied sentence without quotation marks is plagiarism even with a citation.
notes: The article, its author, the two sources and their authors are made up for this sheet; the facts in the sources are accurate (fruit and vegetable scraps and paper napkins can be composted, and compost adds nutrients and helps soil hold water). Citations follow MLA style: (Author page), with the period after the parentheses. Sentence B is plagiarism because it copies the source's words without quotation marks, even though it has a citation.

## Read
passage: Turn Lunch Scraps into Soil
  Every day after lunch, students at Hillview Middle School scrape apple cores, banana peels and leftover bread into the trash. All of it rides a truck to the landfill. I believe Hillview should start a composting program so our food scraps become soil instead of garbage.
  First, much of what we throw away does not belong in a landfill. In her guide to school waste, Dana Rivera explains that most of what is left on lunch trays, from peels to used napkins, could be turned into compost (Rivera 4). If we separated these items, our trash bins would be much lighter.
  Second, compost would help our school garden. According to Marcus Chen, finished compost "adds nutrients to soil and helps it hold water" (Chen 12). Our garden club spends money every spring on bags of fertilizer and waters the beds almost every day in June. Compost from our own scraps could cut both costs.
  Third, a composting program would teach students to take responsibility. Students could take turns sorting scraps and checking the bins, and science classes could measure how fast the piles break down.
  Some people worry that compost bins will smell or attract animals. That can happen when bins are not managed well. However, closed bins that are turned each week and kept away from doors rarely cause problems. Starting small, with one lunch period and two bins, would let us test the plan before it grows.
  Hillview already has a garden, eager students and plenty of scraps. The principal should approve a composting pilot this spring.
  Written by Tariq Mansour, eighth grade
text: **Source 1:** Dana Rivera, *Less Waste at School*, page 4: "Most food left on school lunch trays, including fruit peels, vegetable scraps and paper napkins, can be composted rather than sent to a landfill."
text: **Source 2:** Marcus Chen, *Composting Basics*, page 12: "Finished compost adds nutrients to soil and helps it hold water, so gardens need less fertilizer and less watering."

## Warm up
choice: Which in-text citation is written correctly?
  - Compost helps soil hold water. (Composting Basics, 12)
  - Compost helps soil hold water (Chen).
  * Compost helps soil hold water (Chen 12).
  - Compost helps soil hold water. (page 12)

## Main: Summarize the argument
write: Write an objective summary of Tariq's argument in 3 or 4 sentences.
  lines: 5
  starter: In "Turn Lunch Scraps into Soil," Tariq Mansour argues that ___. He gives three reasons: ___. He also answers the concern that ___.
  key: In "Turn Lunch Scraps into Soil," Tariq Mansour argues that Hillview Middle School should start a composting program. He gives three reasons: most lunch scraps can be composted instead of landfilled, compost would feed and water-proof the school garden for less money, and the program would teach responsibility. He also answers the concern that bins smell, saying closed, well-managed bins rarely cause problems and a small pilot could test the plan.

## Main: Cite the sources
write: Write a sentence that paraphrases Source 1 with a signal phrase and a citation.
  lines: 2
  starter: According to ___, ___ (___ ___).
  key: According to Dana Rivera, most of the food students leave on lunch trays could go into compost instead of a landfill (Rivera 4).
write: Write a sentence that quotes part of Source 2 with a signal phrase and a citation.
  lines: 2
  starter: ___ explains that compost "___" (___ ___).
  key: Marcus Chen explains that compost "adds nutrients to soil and helps it hold water" (Chen 12).

## Main: Spot the plagiarism
text: **A.** Chen writes that compost makes soil richer and helps it stay moist, which means less fertilizer and watering (Chen 12).
text: **B.** Finished compost adds nutrients to soil and helps it hold water, so gardens need less fertilizer and less watering (Chen 12).
text: **C.** Chen notes that compost "helps it hold water," so gardens need watering less often (Chen 12).
choice: Which sentence is plagiarized?
  - Sentence A
  * Sentence B
  - Sentence C
  - None of them
write: Explain why, and fix the plagiarized sentence.
  lines: 2
  starter: Sentence ___ is plagiarized because ___. A fixed version: ___.
  key: Sentence B is plagiarized because it copies Chen's exact words without quotation marks, even though it has a citation. A fixed version: According to Chen, compost "adds nutrients to soil and helps it hold water," so gardens need less fertilizer and water (Chen 12).

# 20. Peer Review a CER
minutes: 40
materials: pencil
ican: I can use a rubric to review a science argument for claim, evidence, reasoning and counterclaim.
directions: Read Sam's science argument. Score each part with the rubric (3 = strong, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning) and give a reason for each score. Then write one "glow" and one "grow" comment.
mistakes: Students often give every part a 3 to be kind. Remind them that a clear score with a reason is the kind thing to do, because it shows the writer how to improve. Many students miss the science error in Sam's reasoning: metal feels cold because it conducts heat away from your hand, and the same property moves heat into the ice.
notes: Sam is a made-up classmate. Ice melts faster on a metal tray than on a plastic one at room temperature because metal is a good conductor of heat: it moves heat from the room and the table into the ice quickly. Plastic is an insulator. The times are sample class data. Model scores: claim 3, evidence 2 (numbers but one trial and no starting conditions), reasoning 1 (wrong science), counterclaim 1 (named but not answered with evidence).

## Read Sam's argument
example: **Question:** Will an ice cube melt faster on a metal tray or on a plastic tray?
  **Claim:** An ice cube melts faster on a metal tray than on a plastic tray.
  **Evidence:** In our test, the ice cube on the aluminum tray melted in 6 minutes. The ice cube on the plastic tray took 19 minutes.
  **Reasoning:** The metal tray melted the ice faster because metal is cold, and cold things melt ice.
  **Counterclaim:** Some people might say the plastic tray would melt it faster. They are wrong.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, rebuttal, conductor, insulator, transfer, trial, rubric, glow, grow
choice: Which part of a CER explains WHY the evidence supports the claim?
  - The claim
  - The evidence
  * The reasoning
  - The question

## Main: Score with the rubric
table: Part | 3 Strong | 2 Developing | 1 Beginning | Score | Reason for my score
  - Claim | answers the question clearly | answers it, but vaguely | does not answer it | {3} | {Clear and answers the question.}
  - Evidence | specific data, enough trials | some data, gaps | no data | {2} | {Two times given, but only one trial each.}
  - Reasoning | correct science links data to claim | some science, gaps | wrong or missing science | {1} | {Metal is not melting ice by being cold; wrong science.}
  - Counterclaim | named and answered with evidence | named, weak answer | missing or just "wrong" | {1} | {Named, but "they are wrong" gives no evidence.}

## Main: Glow and grow
write: Glow: What did Sam do well?
  lines: 2
  starter: One strength is ___ because ___.
  key: One strength is the claim, because it answers the question directly and matches the data (6 minutes vs. 19 minutes).
write: Grow: What is the most important thing Sam should fix?
  lines: 3
  starter: I suggest ___ because ___.
  key: I suggest fixing the reasoning, because metal does not melt ice by being cold. Metal is a good heat conductor, so it moves heat from the room into the ice faster than plastic, which is an insulator.

## Stretch: Rewrite two parts
write: Rewrite Sam's reasoning with correct science.
  lines: 3
  key: Metal is a good conductor of heat, so the aluminum tray moved heat from the room and the table into the ice quickly. Plastic is an insulator, so heat reached that ice cube more slowly, and it took longer to melt.
write: Rewrite Sam's counterclaim and add a rebuttal with evidence.
  lines: 3
  starter: Some classmates think ___. However, ___.
  key: Some classmates think the metal tray would keep ice frozen longer because metal feels colder to touch. However, metal feels cold because it pulls heat from our hands quickly, and in our test that same property melted the ice in 6 minutes instead of 19.

# 21. Lab Reports and Claims Check
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can write a claim with evidence, reasoning and a counterclaim, name a source of error and describe a scatter plot.
directions: Read about the cricket study and its data. Plot the data as a scatter plot (dots only, do not connect them). Then describe the pattern and write a CER that answers a counterclaim.
mistakes: Students often connect the dots in a scatter plot; it shows separate measurements, so dots only. In the CER, watch for reasoning that just repeats the evidence. A source of error is something in how the data were collected, not "we did it wrong."
notes: The data are sample data for this sheet, close to Dolbear's law (Amos Dolbear, "The Cricket as a Thermometer," 1897), which links the chirp rate of snowy tree crickets to air temperature. Crickets cannot control their body temperature the way mammals do, so their muscles work faster when the air is warmer. Students plot the dots; check them against the table (the graph key is left blank on purpose, since a scatter plot is not connected).

## The study
text: A science club recorded the chirps of snowy tree crickets in a park on 6 different evenings. Each evening they measured the air temperature and counted how many times one cricket chirped in one minute.
table: Air temperature (°C) | Chirps per minute
  - 15 | 72
  - 19 | 105
  - 21 | 118
  - 23 | 134
  - 27 | 162
  - 29 | 175
text: **Word bank:** claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, rebuttal, source of error, scatter plot, positive, negative, strong, weak, linear, trend, as ___ increases

## Warm up: Scatter plot
graph: Air temperature (°C) | 10 30 5 | Chirps per minute | 50 200 25
choice: What kind of pattern does the scatter plot show?
  - A negative pattern: as temperature goes up, chirps go down
  * A strong positive pattern that is close to a straight line
  - No pattern at all
  - A pattern that goes up and then down
write: Describe the scatter plot. Use numbers.
  lines: 2
  starter: As the air temperature increases from ___ to ___, the chirps per minute ___. The points ___.
  key: As the air temperature increases from 15 °C to 29 °C, the chirps per minute increase from 72 to 175. The points form a strong positive, nearly straight-line pattern: about 7 more chirps for each degree.

## Main: CER with a counterclaim
write: **Claim:** Answer the question "Does air temperature affect how fast crickets chirp?"
  lines: 1
  starter: The data show that ...
  key: The data show that snowy tree crickets chirp faster when the air is warmer.
write: **Evidence:** Give two data points.
  lines: 2
  starter: At ___ °C, the cricket chirped ___ times per minute, but at ___ °C it chirped ___ times.
  key: At 15 °C, the cricket chirped 72 times per minute, but at 29 °C it chirped 175 times.
write: **Reasoning:** Explain why, using a science idea.
  lines: 2
  starter: This happens because ...
  key: This happens because crickets cannot keep their body temperature steady, so in warmer air their bodies are warmer and their muscles move faster, which makes them chirp more often.
write: **Counterclaim and rebuttal:** Someone says, "The crickets chirped more because it was later in the summer, not because it was warmer." Answer them.
  lines: 2
  starter: Some might argue that ___. However, ___.
  key: Some might argue that the time of year caused the change. However, the chirp rate rose steadily with every rise in temperature, which matches the link scientists such as Dolbear have reported. To test the time-of-year idea, the club could compare warm and cool evenings in the same week.

## Stretch: Source of error
write: Name one source of error in this study and how to reduce it.
  lines: 2
  starter: One source of error is ___. To reduce it, the club could ___.
  key: One source of error is miscounting chirps by ear or counting two crickets at once. To reduce it, the club could record the sound and count twice, or have two people count and average.

# 33. Idiom Riddle Code
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can figure out what an idiom means from a description and use idioms correctly.
directions: Read each riddle. Find the idiom it describes in the decoder and write the idiom's letter in the numbered box. The letters spell a hidden expression.
mistakes: Students may pick an idiom because one word matches the riddle (a riddle about animals is not always "let the cat out of the bag"). Have them say the meaning in their own words first, then look for the idiom with that meaning. Two riddles have answers that begin with E and two with L, so read carefully.
notes: Each idiom's letter in the decoder is its first letter, and the message is WELL DONE. The riddles describe meanings, not the literal words. After the code, have students use one idiom in a sentence about a school situation.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** idiom, figurative, literal, meaning, context, riddle, decoder, expression
example: **Riddle:** I mean that something is very easy, as easy as eating a dessert. Who am I?
  **Idiom:** a piece of cake
choice: "Hit the books" means ...
  - punch a book
  - drop your books
  - put books away
  * study hard

## Main: Solve the riddles
code: Solve each riddle. Write the idiom, then its letter in the box.
  - I promise something will never happen, because farm animals cannot take off into the sky. | when pigs fly | W
  - I say that even a bad day has something good hiding in it, like light at the edge of a storm. | every cloud has a silver lining | E
  - You did this when you told a secret by accident. A pet escaping from a sack is involved. | let the cat out of the bag | L
  - You do this in your first weeks at a new job, when you learn how everything works. | learn the ropes | L
  - I tell you not to be upset about a mistake that already happened and cannot be changed. | don't cry over spilled milk | D
  - I describe something that happens very, very rarely. | once in a blue moon | O
  - I remind you that you have to work hard to get better. | no pain, no gain | N
  - I mean that something is very simple to do, like a dessert. | easy as pie | E

## Stretch: Use one
write: Choose one idiom from the decoder. Use it in a sentence about a school situation.
  lines: 2
  key: Example: I was nervous about the science quiz, but it turned out to be easy as pie.
write: What is the hidden expression? When might a teacher or coach say it?
  lines: 2
  starter: The hidden expression is "___." A coach might say it when ___.
  key: The hidden expression is "Well done." A coach might say it when a player finishes a hard practice or plays a great game.
