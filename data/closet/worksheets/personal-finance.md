# 1. Gross to Net
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, calculator
ican: I can compute net pay from gross pay after federal, state, Social Security and Medicare withholding.
directions: Each invented paycheck lists the hours (h), the pay rate and the federal income tax withheld. Overtime is paid at time and a half; Dev's yearly salary is split into 26 equal paychecks. Compute gross pay, then withhold state tax at 4%, Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. Round each amount to the cent. Subtract to find net pay.
mistakes: Students often compute the percentages from net pay or from each other. Every percentage is taken from gross pay. Watch for overtime paid at the regular rate; overtime is time and a half (1.5 x the hourly rate). Rounding each withholding to the cent before subtracting matters: compute, round, then subtract.
notes: Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) are the actual employee FICA rates; together they are 7.65%. The 4% state rate is invented (states differ, and some have no income tax). Federal amounts are invented; real ones come from the IRS withholding tables in Publication 15-T, based on pay and the worker's Form W-4. Workers and wages are made up.

## Example
example: Ava works 40 hours at $15.00 an hour. Gross = 40 x $15.00 = $600.00. Federal = $28.00 (given). State = 4% x $600.00 = $24.00. Social Security = 6.2% x $600.00 = $37.20. Medicare = 1.45% x $600.00 = $8.70.
  Net = $600.00 - $28.00 - $24.00 - $37.20 - $8.70 = **$502.10**

## Questions
fill: Fay's overtime rate is 1.5 x $22.00 = {$33.00} an hour.
fill: Social Security and Medicare together take {7.65}% of every paycheck.
write: Cora's paycheck says $1,600.00 at the top, but she takes home $1,293.60. What percent of her gross pay does she keep? Show your work.
  lines: 2
  key: $1,293.60 / $1,600.00 = 0.8085, so she keeps 80.85% (about 81%).

## Stretch
write: Eli says, "If I get a raise, I'll take home less because I'll pay more tax." Is he right? Explain using percentages.
  lines: 3
  key: No. State, Social Security and Medicare are percentages of gross pay, so each extra dollar earned has only a part of it withheld (here 11.65 cents plus any extra federal tax); he still keeps most of every new dollar, so his net pay rises.

# 22. Credit Score Factors
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, protractor, colored pencils
ican: I can explain what makes up a credit score and how everyday actions raise or lower it.
directions: Read the table of the five factors in a FICO credit score. Turn each percent into degrees and sketch the circle graph. Then decide how each of 8 actions affects a score and rank them.
mistakes: Students often think checking your own credit report lowers your score; it does not (it is a soft inquiry). Watch for the belief that carrying a balance builds credit; paying the full balance on time does that. Students may rank "opening several cards" as helpful because it adds credit; several new accounts in a short time usually lowers a score.
notes: The five factors and weights are FICO's published general guide for the general population (myFICO, "What's in my FICO Scores"): payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. FICO notes the weights vary from person to person. The actions are about invented people. The ranking key is a model based on the weights; accept a defended order in which on-time payment ranks as the most helpful and a 60-day late payment as the most harmful. Circle graph degrees: percent x 3.6.

## Read: What goes into a FICO score

## Warm up

## Main: How does each action affect a score?
table: Action | Factor | Raises, lowers or no effect?
  - A. Paying every bill on time for a year | {payment history} | {raises}
  - B. Paying a credit card 60 days late | {payment history} | {lowers}
  - C. Paying a card balance down from 80% of the limit to 10% | {amounts owed} | {raises}
  - D. Running a card up to its full limit | {amounts owed} | {lowers}
  - E. Opening four new credit cards in two months | {new credit} | {lowers}
  - F. Keeping your first card open and in good standing for years | {length of credit history} | {raises}
  - G. Adding a car loan you pay on time when you only had cards | {credit mix} | {raises, a little}
  - H. Checking your own credit report | {none} | {no effect}
write: Rank the 8 actions from most helpful to most harmful. Write the letters in order.
  lines: 1
  key: Model: A, C, F, G, H, E, D, B. On-time payments (35%) help most and a 60-day late payment hurts most; H has no effect, so it sits in the middle.

## Stretch
write: Nico, an invented 19-year-old, has one credit card with a $1,000 limit and a $900 balance. He pays on time. What two changes would most raise his score, and why?
  lines: 3
  key: Pay the balance down well below the limit (for example under $300, under 30% use), since amounts owed are 30% of the score; keep paying on time every month, since payment history is 35%. Avoid opening several new cards to get more credit at once.
