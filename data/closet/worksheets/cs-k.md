# 1. Brush, Brush, Brush: Steps in Order
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons
ican: I can put the steps of a task in order.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each card. Cut out the cards and glue them in order: first, next, then, last. Then write 1, 2, 3 or 4 under each picture.
pairs: robot-dance
mistakes: Children who glue "smile" first are thinking about the happy ending, not the order of doing. Act it out together with pretend brushes: "Can we brush before the toothpaste is on?" Watch for cards glued upside down; read each card aloud again before gluing.
notes: The cards are words, so read every card aloud and act each step out before anyone cuts. Children can add a tiny drawing to each card before cutting. Link it to computers: a computer, like a robot, follows steps in exactly the order we give them, so order matters. The picture part checks the same order with pictures: cup is 3, toothpaste is 1, smile is 4, toothbrush is 2.

## Warm up
example: (Read aloud.) Getting dressed: **first** socks, **then** shoes. Shoes first will not work!
choice: (Read aloud.) Which do you do first?
  - Put on your shoes.
  * Put on your socks.

## Main: Cut and glue the steps
sort: (Read aloud.) Glue each card in its box.
  - First: put toothpaste on the brush
  - Next: brush all your teeth
  - Then: spit and rinse
  - Last: smile!

## Stretch: Number the pictures
text: (Read aloud.) Write the step number under each picture: 1, 2, 3 or 4.
picword:
  - cup | {3}
  - toothpaste | {1}
  - face-happy | {4}
  - toothbrush | {2}

# 5. Spot the Silly Step
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayon
ican: I can find the step that is in the wrong place.
directions: (Read aloud.) Socks, then shoes. Shoes, then socks? Silly! Each row shows steps. One step is in a silly place. Listen, then circle the silly step.
mistakes: Children may circle the first picture in every row. Read each row aloud in the order shown and ask, "Can we do this yet?" at each picture. Some children circle a step that is fine but just looks different; act the row out together.
notes: The rows are: 1 washing hands, 2 growing up, 3 bedtime, 4 a snowy day, 5 growing a tree. Name the row, read it aloud left to right using the word under each picture, then ask which step is silly. Key: row 1, the towel (drying before washing); row 2, the grown-up (a baby grows into a toddler and a kid before a grown-up); row 3, the pillow (going to sleep before brushing teeth and reading); row 4, the melting snowman (it melts before it snows and before we build it); row 5, the seed (the seed is planted first, before it sprouts and grows). Ask children where the silly step should go, and act out the fixed order. The idea for computers: a program's steps must be in the right order, or the computer does silly things too.

# 15. Code Detective Check
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, a counter or eraser
ican: I can put steps in order, follow arrows, and find the stop sign.
directions: (Read aloud.) Write 1, 2, 3 under the pictures to put them in order. Follow the arrows with your finger and circle where the bunny stops. Then circle the sign that means stop.
mistakes: In the arrow part, children often move the counter several squares for one arrow; say "one arrow, one hop" and tap each arrow as they hop. Some circle the exit sign because it is also a sign on a wall; ask "Which one tells us to stop?" and trace the eight sides of the stop sign together.
notes: Key: seed 1, sprout 2, flower 3. Arrow path: → → ↓ ↓ takes the bunny right to the cat's square, then down past the empty square to the duck, so the bunny stops on the duck. The stop sign is the second sign (the eight-sided shape on a pole). Use this as a quick check of the module: watch who can tap one square per arrow.

## Warm up: Put them in order
text: (Read aloud.) Write 1, 2 or 3 under each picture.
picword:
  - sprout | {2}
  - flower | {3}
  - seed | {1}

## Main: Follow the arrows
text: (Read aloud.) Put your counter on the bunny. Hop one square for each arrow: **→ → ↓ ↓**
table: | column 1 | column 2 | column 3
  - row 1 | bunny | | cat
  - row 2 | | dog |
  - row 3 | frog | | duck
choice: (Read aloud.) Where does the bunny stop? Circle it.
  - frog
  * duck
  - dog

## Stretch: Find the stop sign
text: (Read aloud.) Circle the sign that means stop.
picword:
  - exit-sign |
  - stop-sign |
  - recycle |
  - crosswalk |
note: Circle the stop sign, the second picture.

# 21. Class Vote: Favorite Fruit
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons, 4 fruit pictures for the class vote
ican: I can count votes and tell which has the most.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each picture is one vote. Count Room 5's votes and write the number. Then our class votes: color one box for each vote, and circle the fruit with the most votes.
mistakes: Children often color from the wrong end or skip a box, which makes a bar look longer or shorter than it is. Have them start at the box marked 1 and color one box for each vote, touching each child's name or tally as they go. Some circle their own favorite fruit as "the most": point back to the boxes.
notes: The warm up uses Room 5's votes (apple 5, banana 3, grapes 7, cherries 4), so it has a fixed key. For the main part, hold a real class vote: put the four fruit pictures in the corners, each child stands by one, and count each group aloud together while children color one box per vote. Check the graphs against your own count; the fruit with the most votes is whichever row is longest, and a tie is a fine thing to talk about.

## Warm up: Room 5 voted
count: (Read aloud.) Count the votes for each fruit. Write the number.
  - 5 apple
  - 3 banana
  - 7 grapes
  - 4 cherries
choice: (Read aloud.) Which fruit got the most votes in Room 5?
  - apple
  - banana
  * grapes
  - cherries

# 26. Number or Word?
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayon
ican: I can tell if an answer is a number or a word.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the question for each picture. Look at the answer under it. If the answer is a number, circle it. If the answer is a word, draw a box around it.
mistakes: Children may circle every answer they can read. Hold up a number card and a word card: "Numbers count things. Words name things." Some box "10" because it has two parts; it is still one number. If a child asks, the clock shows 4 o'clock and the cake has 1 candle.
notes: Read each question aloud, then point to the answer. Questions: apple, "How many apples did our class pick?" (6); dog, "What is the dog's name?" (Spot); sun, "What is the weather today?" (sunny); clock, "What time does the clock show?" (4); cake, "How many candles are on the cake?" (1); ball, "What game do we play with this ball?" (soccer); banana, "What fruit is this?" (banana); book, "How many books did our class read?" (10). Key: circle 6, 4, 1 and 10; box Spot, sunny, soccer and banana. The idea for computers: data can be numbers we can count and add, or words that name things.

## Warm up
example: (Read aloud.) 3 is a number: circle it. cat is a word: box it.
choice: (Read aloud.) "How many legs does a dog have?" Is the answer a number or a word?
  * a number
  - a word

## Main: Circle or box
picword:
  - apple | 6
  - dog | Spot
  - sun | sunny
  - clock | 4
  - cake | 1
  - ball | soccer
  - banana | banana
  - book | 10
note: Circle 6, 4, 1, 10. Box Spot, sunny, soccer, banana.

## Stretch
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw something you can count. Write the number. | 1.4

# 36. Who Uses This Device?
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons
ican: I can match a device to the helper who uses it at work.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each helper's job. Cut out the device cards at the bottom. Glue each device under the helper who uses it at work.
mistakes: Children may glue the computer under the cashier because stores have computers. Many helpers use several devices; ask "Which one does this helper use the most for the job we heard?" Some cut through the pictures: show them to cut on the dashed lines.
notes: Read each helper's job aloud before cutting: the cashier scans what people buy; the photographer takes pictures; the nurse checks if you have a fever; the singer sings so a big crowd can hear; the librarian looks up and checks out books; the pilot talks to the airport while flying. Key: cashier, barcode scanner; photographer, camera; nurse, thermometer; singer, microphone; librarian, computer; pilot, headset (headphones with a microphone). Name each device as you hold up its card. Accept another match if a child can explain how the helper uses it.

## Main: Glue the device under the helper
text: (Read aloud.) Point to each card as I name it: barcode scanner, camera, thermometer, microphone, computer, headset.
