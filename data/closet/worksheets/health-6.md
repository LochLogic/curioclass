# 1. Inside a Vape
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain how a vape works and what is in the aerosol it makes.
directions: Match each vape part to its job. Draw and label a simple vape diagram. Then complete the aerosol chart with one health effect for each ingredient.
mistakes: Many students believe vapes make "just water vapor." They make an aerosol: tiny particles and chemicals that are breathed deep into the lungs. Students may also think nicotine-free labels can be trusted; CDC reports that some products labeled nicotine-free have been found to contain nicotine.
notes: Facts follow the CDC and the U.S. Surgeon General's report on e-cigarette use among youth. Show a plain parts diagram of an e-cigarette on the board (or sketch one: a long body with the battery at the bottom, the coil in the middle and the pod and mouthpiece on top) for students to copy. Use no brand names and no product photos. Keep the focus on facts and on staying vape-free; if a student says they or someone they know vapes, follow up privately and share school supports.

## Warm up: How it works
text: A vape (also called an e-cigarette) uses a **battery** to heat a metal **coil**. The coil heats a liquid from the **pod** or tank and turns it into an **aerosol**: a cloud of tiny particles that the user breathes in through the **mouthpiece**.

## Main: Parts and jobs
match: Match each part to its job.
  - Battery = Powers the device and sends electricity to the coil
  - Heating coil = Gets hot and turns the liquid into aerosol
  - Pod or tank = Holds the e-liquid
  - Mouthpiece = Where the aerosol is breathed in
draw: Draw a simple vape from the board. Label the battery, heating coil, pod and mouthpiece. | 2.2

## Main: What is in the aerosol?
table: Ingredient | What it is | One health effect
  - Nicotine | The addictive drug found in tobacco | {Highly addictive; harms the developing teen brain (attention, learning, mood and impulse control)}
  - Flavorings | Chemicals that make it taste like candy or fruit | {Some, like diacetyl, are linked to serious lung disease}
  - Metals | Tiny bits of nickel, tin and lead from the coil | {Breathed deep into the lungs, where they can do harm}
  - Solvents | Liquids (propylene glycol, glycerin) that carry the nicotine and flavor | {When heated they can form harmful chemicals that irritate the lungs and airways}
choice: Which statement about vape aerosol is true?
  - It is just harmless water vapor
  * It contains nicotine, ultrafine particles and harmful chemicals
  - It is safe because it has a fruit flavor

## Stretch
write: A friend says, "Vaping is fine because it's not smoking." Use two facts from this sheet to answer them.
  lines: 4
  key: Vapes usually contain nicotine, which is highly addictive and harms the developing brain. The aerosol also carries metals and chemicals like diacetyl into the lungs. Not burning tobacco does not make it safe.

# 21. Qualities of a Good Friend
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can describe what makes a healthy friendship and support my ideas with examples.
directions: Read the story. Rank the ten friendship qualities from most important (1) to least important (10) for you. Then explain your top three with examples from the story.
mistakes: Students sometimes rank "fun" or "popular" highly and then cannot find story evidence. Every top-three choice needs an example of a character's action. Watch for students who equate "keeps every secret" with loyalty: a good friend gets help from a trusted adult when a secret means someone could get hurt.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class (about 300 words). The ranking is personal, so there is no single right order; grade the evidence. Story examples: honest (Jonah tells Ruby the truth about the poster), good listener (Jonah asks and waits after the lost game), includes others (Ruby invites Theo), loyal (Ruby defends Jonah), forgiving (Jonah accepts her apology), keeps safe secrets and gets help (Ruby tells the counselor).

## Read
passage: The Poster Project
  Ruby and Jonah had been friends since second grade, but sixth grade was testing them. On Monday, their teacher assigned partner posters about the water cycle. Ruby asked Jonah to be her partner before anyone else could.
  Ruby spent Tuesday night drawing a giant raincloud. On Wednesday, she showed Jonah. "Be honest," she said. "Is it good?" Jonah looked for a while. "The colors are great," he said, "but the arrows go the wrong way. Evaporation goes up." Ruby frowned, then laughed and fixed it.
  On Thursday, a new student named Theo stood alone at lunch holding his tray. Ruby waved him over. "Sit with us," she said. Jonah slid his backpack off the seat.
  Friday was the soccer game. Jonah missed an easy goal, and the team lost. On the bus, a boy said Jonah had cost them the game. Ruby turned around. "He played the whole game. Leave him alone." Later, Jonah was quiet. Ruby asked, "Do you want to talk about it?" and then she just listened.
  The next week, Ruby forgot to bring her half of the poster on the day it was due. Jonah was upset. Ruby said, "I'm sorry. I let you down. I'll stay in at recess to finish it." Jonah thought about it. "Okay," he said. "Let's finish it together."
  That Friday, Theo told Ruby something that worried her. An older kid had been threatening him online, and he made her promise not to tell. Ruby thought about it all night. The next morning, she went with Theo to see the school counselor. "Some secrets aren't safe to keep," she told him. Theo was nervous, but he was also relieved.
