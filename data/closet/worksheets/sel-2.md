# 1. Annoyed, Mad, Furious: Feelings Ladders
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell how strong a feeling is and pick a word that fits.
directions: (Read aloud.) Feelings can be small or big. Put each set of words on a ladder from a little at the bottom to a lot at the top. Then pick the word that fits each story and use one word in your own sentence about a character.
mistakes: Some children put the word they know best on top. Ask "Which word is the biggest, loudest version?" Thrilled and terrified are new for many children; act them out. For the stories, a small problem gets a small word: a lost crayon is annoying, not furious.
notes: Draw three ladders on the board and climb them with your hand as you read the words. The ladder is shown as a table: each row is one ladder, top rung on the left. There is no wrong feeling to have; the point is finding a word that fits how big it is. Characters only: students write about Max, Zoe or a character they make up.

## Warm up: How cold?
example: A ladder for cold. A little: **chilly**. Middle: **cold**. A lot: **freezing**.

## Main: Climb the ladders
text: (Read aloud.) Write each set of words on its ladder.
table: Words | Top rung: a lot | Middle rung | Bottom rung: a little
  - 1. mad, furious, annoyed | {furious} | {mad} | {annoyed}
  - 2. scared, nervous, terrified | {terrified} | {scared} | {nervous}
  - 3. thrilled, pleased, happy | {thrilled} | {happy} | {pleased}

## Use the ladders
fill:
  - (Read aloud.) Zoe's friend tapped her pencil again and again. Zoe felt a little bit {annoyed}.
  - (Read aloud.) Max's tower got knocked down for the fifth time. He was so angry he could not talk. Max felt {furious}.
  - (Read aloud.) Zoe won the art prize she had dreamed about all year. She jumped and cheered. Zoe felt {thrilled}.
  - (Read aloud.) Max had a spelling test after lunch. His tummy felt a little fluttery. Max felt {nervous}.

## Stretch: Your sentence
write: (Read aloud.) Pick one word from a ladder. Write a sentence about a character who feels that way.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. The word should fit how big the problem is, for example: Zoe felt annoyed when her shoelace came untied again.

# 21. Rumble the Volcano Cools Down
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can name steps that help me calm down.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story about Rumble the volcano. Then answer the questions about his steps.
mistakes: Children often skip the first step (noticing his body) and go straight to breathing. Reread the line about his hot top. For question 5, some children write "he felt better"; ask what he did next.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class (about 110 words). Read it twice: once for the story, once while children put a finger on each step. Rumble's steps are notice, stop, breathe, say the feeling, then fix the problem. Practice the breathing together: in through the nose, out through the mouth, three times.

## Read
passage: Rumble Cools Down
  Rumble was a little volcano on a sunny island. When things went wrong, his top got hot and he puffed out smoke.
  One day, a bird built a nest on Rumble's side. A strong wind blew the nest away. Rumble began to shake.
  "Wait, Rumble," said Old Turtle. "First, notice your body. Then stop and take three slow breaths."
  Rumble noticed his hot top. He stopped. He breathed in through his nose and out through his mouth. One, two, three. His shaking slowed down.
  "Now say what you feel," said Turtle.
  "I feel mad that the wind broke the nest," said Rumble.
  Then he helped the bird find new sticks.

## Questions
choice: (Read aloud.) What did Rumble notice first?
  - The bird singing
  * His hot top
  - The sunny sky
fill: (Read aloud.) Rumble took {three} slow breaths.
choice: (Read aloud.) How did Rumble breathe?
  - Fast, with his mouth open
  * In through his nose and out through his mouth
  - He held his breath
fill: (Read aloud.) Rumble said, "I feel {mad} that the wind broke the nest."
write: (Read aloud.) What did Rumble do after he calmed down?
  lines: 2
  key: He helped the bird find new sticks for a new nest.

## Stretch
write: (Read aloud.) Which of Rumble's steps do you think helped him most? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: Breathing helped most because his shaking slowed down.
