# 1. Annoyed, Mad, Furious: Feelings Ladders
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell how strong a feeling is and pick a word that fits.
directions: (Read aloud.) Feelings can be small or big. Put each set of words on a ladder from a little at the bottom to a lot at the top. Then pick the word that fits each story and use one word in your own sentence about a character.
mistakes: Some children put the word they know best on top. Ask "Which word is the biggest, loudest version?" Thrilled and terrified are new for many children; act them out. For the stories, a small problem gets a small word: a lost crayon is annoying, not furious.
notes: Draw three ladders on the board and climb them with your hand as you read the words. The ladder is shown as a table: each row is one ladder, top rung on the left. There is no wrong feeling to have; the point is finding a word that fits how big it is. Characters only: students write about Max, Zoe or a character they make up.

## Warm up: How cold?
example: A ladder for cold. A little: **chilly**. Middle: **cold**. A lot: **freezing**.

## Main: Climb the ladders
text: (Read aloud.) Write each set of words on its ladder.
table: Words | Top rung: a lot | Middle rung | Bottom rung: a little
  - 1. mad, furious, annoyed | {furious} | {mad} | {annoyed}
  - 2. scared, nervous, terrified | {terrified} | {scared} | {nervous}
  - 3. thrilled, pleased, happy | {thrilled} | {happy} | {pleased}

## Use the ladders
fill:
  - (Read aloud.) Zoe's friend tapped her pencil again and again. Zoe felt a little bit {annoyed}.
  - (Read aloud.) Max's tower got knocked down for the fifth time. He was so angry he could not talk. Max felt {furious}.
  - (Read aloud.) Zoe won the art prize she had dreamed about all year. She jumped and cheered. Zoe felt {thrilled}.
  - (Read aloud.) Max had a spelling test after lunch. His tummy felt a little fluttery. Max felt {nervous}.

## Stretch: Your sentence
write: (Read aloud.) Pick one word from a ladder. Write a sentence about a character who feels that way.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. The word should fit how big the problem is, for example: Zoe felt annoyed when her shoelace came untied again.

# 7. Hidden Feelings
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, highlighter or crayon
ican: I can find clues that show a feeling someone is hiding behind a smile.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each scene. Each character smiles, but feels something else inside. Underline the clues. Then write the hidden feeling and two clues in the chart.
mistakes: Children often write "happy" because of the smile. Ask "What does the character do after the smile?" The clues come after the smile: an action, a body sign or worried words, like Ana's question. In scene 4, annoyed, frustrated and mad all fit; accept any of them with a clue.
notes: The scenes are original to Curio Class. Talk about why people hide feelings: to be polite (scene 1), to look tough (scene 2), or to be kind to a friend (scene 4). Hiding a feeling is not wrong, but noticing the clues helps us be a good friend. Clues are body signs (shaking hands, shaky voice, crossed arms) and actions (looked at the floor, kicked the wood chips).

## Warm up
example: Jay smiled and said, "I'm fine." But he sat by himself and stared out the window. **Hidden feeling:** lonely. **Clues:** sat by himself; stared out the window.

## Main: Find the clues
passage: Scenes 1 and 2
  **1.** Maya opened a gift from her aunt. It was a scratchy sweater. "Thank you!" Maya said with a big smile. But she looked down at the floor. Later, she pushed the sweater to the back of her closet.
  **2.** Leo's team lost the big game. He smiled and said, "It's fine. I don't even care." But his voice was shaky. On the way to the bus, he wiped his eyes with his sleeve.
passage: Scenes 3 and 4
  **3.** Ana was picked to read her poem at the school show. She smiled at her teacher. But her hands shook, and she kept asking, "What if I forget the words?"
  **4.** Sam's friend got the last swing. Sam smiled and said, "Go ahead." But he crossed his arms. While he waited, he kicked hard at the wood chips.
text: Word bank: **annoyed**, **disappointed**, **nervous**, **sad**
table: Scene | Hidden feeling | Clue 1 | Clue 2
  height: 0.5
  - 1. Maya | {disappointed} | {looked down at the floor} | {pushed the sweater to the back}
  - 2. Leo | {sad} | {his voice was shaky} | {wiped his eyes}
  - 3. Ana | {nervous} | {her hands shook} | {"What if I forget the words?"}
  - 4. Sam | {annoyed} | {crossed his arms} | {kicked hard at the wood chips}

## Stretch
write: Pick Leo or Sam. What could a friend say to show they noticed the hidden feeling?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Leo: "It's okay to be sad about losing. You played hard." Sam: "Want to take turns? You can have the swing after five more pushes."

# 20. Three Breathing Patterns
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, a clock or timer for the teacher
ican: I can use three breathing patterns to help my body slow down.
directions: (Read aloud.) Learn each breathing pattern. Try each one with the class for one minute while your teacher keeps time. Count your breaths and write the number. Then circle the slowest pattern.
mistakes: Children often count fast to "win." Remind them this is not a race: the goal is slow, gentle breaths, and a smaller number means slower breathing. Some children hold their breath too long on square breathing; the holds are short and easy, never a strain.
notes: Lead each pattern together before the timed minute. Counts will vary from child to child. Typical results: square breathing is slowest (each breath takes 16 counts, so about 4 in a minute), balloon breathing next, and five finger breathing fastest. Accept any honest counts; the slowest pattern is the one with the smallest number. If anyone feels dizzy, they go back to normal breathing. Breathing patterns are a calm tool for anyone; nobody has to share why they might need one.

## Learn: Three patterns
example: **Square breathing:** Trace a square in the air. Breathe in for 4, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. One square is one breath.
  **Five finger breathing:** Hold up one hand. Trace up a finger as you breathe in, and down the finger as you breathe out. One finger is one breath.
  **Balloon breathing:** Put your hands on your belly. Breathe in slowly and fill your belly like a balloon. Let the air out slowly, like a balloon going flat.

## Warm up: Count it
fill:
  - One square breath: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. How many counts in one square breath? {16}
  - Five finger breathing: how many breaths to trace one whole hand? {5}
  - How many breaths to trace two hands? {10}

## Main: Try each pattern for one minute
table: Breathing pattern | Breaths in one minute | Circle the slowest
  height: 0.55
  - Square breathing | | slowest
  - Five finger breathing | | slowest
  - Balloon breathing | | slowest
note: Breath counts vary. Square breathing is usually slowest (about 4 breaths in a minute at one count per second, since 60 / 16 is a little less than 4). Check that the circled pattern is the one with the smallest number the child wrote.
choice: (Read aloud.) Which pattern has holds in it?
  - Five finger breathing
  * Square breathing
  - Balloon breathing

## Stretch
write: (Read aloud.) Which pattern felt the calmest to you? When could a character in a story use it?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: Balloon breathing felt calmest. A character could use it before a spelling test or when waiting in a long line.

# 21. Rumble the Volcano Cools Down
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can name steps that help me calm down.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story about Rumble the volcano. Then answer the questions about his steps.
mistakes: Children often skip the first step (noticing his body) and go straight to breathing. Reread the line about his hot top. For question 5, some children write "he felt better"; ask what he did next.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class (about 110 words). Read it twice: once for the story, once while children put a finger on each step. Rumble's steps are notice, stop, breathe, say the feeling, then fix the problem. Practice the breathing together: in through the nose, out through the mouth, three times.

## Read
passage: Rumble Cools Down
  Rumble was a little volcano on a sunny island. When things went wrong, his top got hot and he puffed out smoke.
  One day, a bird built a nest on Rumble's side. A strong wind blew the nest away. Rumble began to shake.
  "Wait, Rumble," said Old Turtle. "First, notice your body. Then stop and take three slow breaths."
  Rumble noticed his hot top. He stopped. He breathed in through his nose and out through his mouth. One, two, three. His shaking slowed down.
  "Now say what you feel," said Turtle.
  "I feel mad that the wind broke the nest," said Rumble.
  Then he helped the bird find new sticks.

## Questions
choice: (Read aloud.) What did Rumble notice first?
  - The bird singing
  * His hot top
  - The sunny sky
fill: (Read aloud.) Rumble took {three} slow breaths.
choice: (Read aloud.) How did Rumble breathe?
  - Fast, with his mouth open
  * In through his nose and out through his mouth
  - He held his breath
fill: (Read aloud.) Rumble said, "I feel {mad} that the wind broke the nest."
write: (Read aloud.) What did Rumble do after he calmed down?
  lines: 2
  key: He helped the bird find new sticks for a new nest.

## Stretch
write: (Read aloud.) Which of Rumble's steps do you think helped him most? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: Breathing helped most because his shaking slowed down.

# 33. Group Poster Team Plan
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can plan how a team will share jobs, share supplies and make decisions.
directions: (Read aloud.) Your team will make a poster called "Ways to Be a Good Friend." Before you start, plan together. Give out the four jobs, plan how to share supplies, and choose how you will decide.
mistakes: Teams often give every job to the strongest drawer or the fastest writer. Each person gets one job, and every job matters. Watch for "we will share" with no plan; push for something they can check, like "one marker color each, trade when done."
notes: Use before any group poster or project. Jobs: the Materials Manager gets and returns supplies; the Artist leads the pictures; the Writer leads the words; the Timekeeper watches the clock and makes sure everyone gets a turn to talk. In a team of three, one person takes two jobs; in a team of five, two people share the Artist job. Plans will vary; look for a fair way to share and a fair way to decide.

## Warm up: Fair or not?
choice: (Read aloud.) Two teammates both want the red marker. What is a fair plan?
  - The one who grabs it first keeps it.
  - Nobody uses red.
  * They take turns, or each uses it for one part.
choice: (Read aloud.) The team cannot agree on a title color. What is a fair way to decide?
  * Vote, or take turns choosing.
  - The loudest person picks.
  - Stop working on the poster.

## Main: Our team plan
table: Job | What this job does | Team member
  height: 0.5
  - Materials Manager | Gets the supplies and puts them back |
  - Artist | Leads the drawing and coloring |
  - Writer | Leads the words and the title |
  - Timekeeper | Watches the time and makes sure everyone gets a turn to talk |
write: How will we share the supplies?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Each person gets one marker color and trades when done. The glue stick goes around the circle.
write: How will we decide when we do not agree?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Everyone shares an idea, then we vote. If it is a tie, we take turns choosing.
write: What is one thing each person can do to be a good teammate?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Listen when someone talks, say "good idea," and ask quiet teammates what they think.

## Stretch
draw: Sketch a plan for the poster: where the title, pictures and words will go. | 2.0
