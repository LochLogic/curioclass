# 1. What Makes an Invasion Game
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare invasion games by their goal, scoring, playing area and key skills.
directions: Fill in the blanks in the comparison chart. Then use the chart to answer the questions.
mistakes: Students mix up scoring rules between games: a soccer or handball goal is worth 1, a basketball field goal 2 or 3, a flag football touchdown 6. In team handball, players may take up to 3 steps while holding the ball; students who play basketball expect a traveling call after 2.
notes: Rules are the common official ones (FIFA for soccer, the international handball rules for team handball, high school and college basketball). Flag football rules vary by league: player counts of 5 to 7 a side and the extra-point values are the most common differences, so the sheet asks only for the touchdown value. Team handball is played 7 a side, counting the goalkeeper, on a 40 by 20 meter court.

## Warm up
choice: What do all invasion games have in common?
  - Teams take turns batting and fielding.
  * Both teams share one area and try to get the ball into the other team's scoring area.
  - A net divides the court and players never cross it.
choice: In which game do defenders pull a flag instead of tackling?
  - Team handball
  - Soccer
  * Flag football
  - Basketball

## Main: Comparison chart
table: Game | Goal | Scoring | Field and players | Key skills
  - Soccer | Kick or head the ball into the other team's goal | {1} point per goal | Grass field with a goal at each end; {11} players a side | Dribbling, passing and shooting with the feet; only the {goalkeeper} may use hands, inside the penalty area
  - Basketball | Shoot the ball through the other team's hoop | 2 points for a field goal, {3} from beyond the arc, 1 for a free throw | Hard court with a hoop at each end; {5} players a side | Dribbling, passing, shooting, rebounding
  - Team handball | Throw the ball into the other team's goal | 1 point per goal | Court with a goal and a goal area at each end; {7} players a side, counting the goalkeeper | Passing, dribbling with the hand, jump shots; up to {3} steps while holding the ball
  - Flag football | Run or pass the ball into the other team's end zone | {6} points for a touchdown, then a try for extra points | Field with an end zone at each end; usually 5 to 7 players a side | Passing, catching, running routes, pulling flags instead of {tackling}

## Main: Use the chart
write: Name one way soccer and team handball are alike and one way they are different.
  lines: 3
  key: Alike: both score by getting the ball into a goal guarded by a goalkeeper, 1 point per goal. Different: soccer players move the ball with their feet; handball players move it with their hands (passing, dribbling, throwing).
write: Basketball gives more points for a shot from far away. Why might a game reward a harder shot with more points?
  lines: 3
  key: A long shot is harder to make, so extra points reward the skill and the risk; it also spreads the defense out, which opens space near the basket.
write: Which skill shows up in all four games? Give an example from two of them.
  lines: 2
  key: Passing: a soccer pass with the inside of the foot, a chest pass in basketball, a shoulder pass in handball, a forward pass in flag football.

## Stretch
write: Invent one rule change for any of the four games that would help a team use open space. Explain how it would change the game.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: in basketball, allow only 3 dribbles per player, so teams must pass and move into open space to advance the ball.

# 7. Defending the Goal Side
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can position defenders goal-side and show the lane each one blocks.
directions: Each grid is a section of a field. The goal you defend is the thick line on the bottom edge, from (4, 0) to (6, 0). Dots show the attackers: B is the ball carrier and T is a teammate. Draw two defenders as X: defender 1 goal-side of B, and defender 2 goal-side of T on the ball side. Then use a ruler to draw the lane each defender blocks.
mistakes: Students often place a defender right on top of the attacker or behind the attacker (on the side away from the goal), which leaves a straight path to the goal. Goal-side means between your attacker and the goal, about a step or two away. Others place defender 2 next to T but away from the ball, which leaves the passing lane from B to T open.
notes: Key positions (accept any spot on or near the line, 1 to 2 squares from the attacker): Diagram 1: B (1, 4), T (5, 8); defender 1 at about (2, 3) on the line from B to the goal center (5, 0); defender 2 at about (4, 7) on the line from T to B. Diagram 2: B (9, 4), T (1, 8); defender 1 at about (8, 3); defender 2 at about (3, 7). Diagram 3: B (5, 5), T (9, 9); defender 1 at about (5, 4); defender 2 at about (8, 8). Defender 1 blocks the shooting lane from B to the goal; defender 2 blocks the passing lane from B to T and is still closer to the goal than T. On the key, the solid line is the shooting lane and the dashed line is the passing lane; the dot on each line is the defender. Walk it out on a real field with cones afterward.

## Warm up
choice: What does it mean to be goal-side of an attacker?
  - Standing behind the attacker, farther from your goal
  * Standing between the attacker and the goal you defend
  - Standing next to the goal post
choice: Why does a defender stay a step or two away instead of touching the attacker?
  - So the defender can rest
  - So the attacker has more room to shoot
  * So the defender has time to react to a pass or a dribble

# 20. Four-Week Activity Log
minutes: 15 to start, then 5 a day for 4 weeks
materials: pencil
ican: I can log my activity type, minutes and intensity and check my days against the 60-minute guideline.
directions: Read the intensity key and practice with Dev's sample week. Then log 4 weeks. In each day's box write the activity, the minutes and the intensity (L, M or V). Star each day with 60 or more minutes of moderate or vigorous activity, and count your starred days each week.
mistakes: Students count light activity (a slow stroll, easy stretching) toward the 60 minutes. The guideline counts moderate and vigorous activity only. Others write one activity a day and forget recess, walking to school or practice; add every bout of 10 minutes or so.
notes: The guideline is from the US Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2nd edition, 2018): ages 6 to 17 should do 60 minutes or more of moderate to vigorous activity every day, including vigorous activity on at least 3 days a week. Talk test: light, you can sing; moderate, you can talk but not sing; vigorous, you can say only a few words without a breath. Dev's key: Mon 60, Tue 20, Wed 60, Thu 50, Fri 60, Sat 90, Sun 0 moderate or vigorous minutes; starred days Mon, Wed, Fri, Sat (4); vigorous days Mon, Wed, Thu, Fri (4); total 340 moderate or vigorous minutes. Logs are each student's own private record; never post or compare them.

## Warm up: Dev's sample week
example: **L** = light (you can sing). **M** = moderate (you can talk but not sing). **V** = vigorous (you can say only a few words).
table: Day | Dev's activities | Moderate or vigorous minutes | Star? (60+)
  - Mon | Soccer practice 60 V | {60} | {yes}
  - Tue | Walk to school 20 M; stretching 15 L | {20} | {no}
  - Wed | Bike ride 45 M; jump rope 15 V | {60} | {yes}
  - Thu | Dance class 50 V | {50} | {no}
  - Fri | P.E. class 30 M; tag at recess 30 V | {60} | {yes}
  - Sat | Hike with family 90 M | {90} | {yes}
  - Sun | Slow walk 40 L | {0} | {no}
fill:
  - Dev's starred days this week: {4}
  - Days with vigorous activity (the guideline asks for 3 or more): {4}
  - Dev's total moderate or vigorous minutes: {340}

  - 1 | | | | | | | |

write: Describe your four weeks. Did your starred days go up, go down or stay about the same? Use numbers.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer uses the student's own star counts, for example: "I had 3, 4, 4 and 5 starred days, so I went up a little each week."

## Stretch
write: Pick the week with the fewest stars. What got in the way, and what is one realistic change for next month?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: Week 2 had the fewest because of rain. Next month I will plan an indoor option, like a 20-minute dance or jump rope workout.

# 21. Activity Log Graph
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler, your activity log from the last 6 weeks
ican: I can graph my weekly active minutes and describe the trend in my own data.
directions: Practice reading Jo's log first. Then copy your own weekly totals into the table, graph them, and describe what your graph shows.
mistakes: Students often connect the points in the order they wrote them rather than week by week, or start the y-axis at a number other than 0 without noticing. Remind them a trend is the overall direction across all the weeks, not the change between the last two.
notes: This sheet works with each student's own activity log; each graph shows one student's effort over time and is never compared with anyone else's. A student without 6 weeks of data can use the weeks they have, or Jo's numbers. Jo's key: highest week 5 (330), lowest week 1 (240), change from week 1 to week 6 is 315 - 240 = 75 minutes, total 1,710, average 285. A week of 420 minutes is 60 minutes on each of 7 days.

# 33. Dribble Observation
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, a basketball and 6 cones for each pair
ican: I can observe a partner's dribble against cues and find where control breaks down.
directions: Read the cues and answer the warm-up questions. Then watch your partner dribble through a line of 6 cones three times. After each run, check each cue your partner showed, and mark the cone where they lost control, if any. Then give feedback and switch.
mistakes: Observers watch the ball instead of the dribbler, so they miss the eyes and the body cues. Assign one or two cues to watch per run if needed. Feedback is often "good job" or "you messed up"; push for a specific cue and a specific cone.
notes: Warm-up key: 1 B, 2 A, 3 C. Checklists vary. The usual breakdown points are the cones where the dribbler changes direction: the ball gets too high or too far in front, or the dribbler looks down and stops using the outside hand. A good tip names one cue and where it broke down, for example "Switch hands as you pass cone 3 so the ball is on the side away from the cone." Remind pairs that the goal is help, not a grade; observers start with something the dribbler did well.

## Warm up: The 5 cues
example: 1. **Finger pads:** push the ball with the pads of your fingers, not a flat palm. 2. **Waist high or lower:** keep the ball at your waist or below. 3. **Eyes up:** look ahead, not at the ball. 4. **Protect it:** keep the ball on the side away from the cone (or defender) and use your other arm as a shield. 5. **Low and balanced:** bend your knees and keep your body low.
choice: Why should the dribbler keep their eyes up?
  - To keep the ball from bouncing too high
  * To see teammates, defenders and open space
  - To dribble faster with the palm
choice: Your partner dribbles past a cone on their right. Which hand should they use?
  * The left hand, so the ball is away from the cone
  - The right hand, so the ball is close to the cone
  - Either hand, because cones do not matter
choice: Your partner's ball keeps bouncing up to their chest. Which cue should you give?
  - Eyes up
  - Protect it
  * Waist high or lower

write: Where did control break down most often? What was happening there?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: At cones 3 and 4, where the path turned. My partner looked down and the ball got ahead of her, so she had to reach for it.

## Main: Feedback
write: Write one thing your partner did well and one specific tip. Name the cue.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: You kept your eyes up the whole first run. Tip: switch to your left hand before cone 3 so the ball stays away from the cone.

## Stretch
write: Your partner improves on run 3. How could you make the course harder to keep them learning?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Move the cones closer together, add a defender who walks beside them, or have them use only their weaker hand.
