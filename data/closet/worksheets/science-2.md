# 1. Material Detectives
minutes: 30
materials: for each group: a metal spoon, a kitchen sponge, a rock, a paper clip, a pipe cleaner, a glass marble, a satin ribbon, a wooden block; pencil
ican: I can test and describe materials by their properties.
directions: Do three tests on each object. Squeeze it: hard or soft? Try to bend it gently: bendy or stiff? Rub it with a finger: rough or smooth? Put an X in one box of each pair.
mistakes: Students may call the paper clip stiff because it is hard. Hard and bendy can go together: a paper clip does not squish, but it bends. Remind them to bend gently and never force an object that does not bend.
notes: The key shows typical results for common versions of these objects. A rough rock or a smooth river stone, or a sponge with a smooth side, can change the answer: accept any result that matches the object the group tested. Collect the marbles at the end.

  - Something that feels bumpy or scratchy is {rough}.

## Stretch: Pick a material
write: You need to build a little bridge for toy cars. Which object from the chart would you use? Tell which properties make it a good choice.
  lines: 2
  key: The wooden block (or the rock): it is hard and stiff, so it will hold up a toy car without bending.

# 22. Plant Needs Check-In
minutes: 20
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell what plants need to grow and how seeds and bees help plants.
directions: Circle what a plant needs. Match each seed to how it travels. Then explain a bee's job.
mistakes: Some students say plants need food from us, like people do. Plants need water and light to grow, and they make their own food. Students also think bees visit flowers to help them: bees visit to get food, and moving pollen happens as they go.
notes: Read the picture words aloud for students who need it. Plants also need air: accept it if a student adds it. The four seeds are real: dandelion seeds have fluffy parachutes, burdock burrs have tiny hooks, coconuts float, and squirrels bury acorns and forget some.

## Warm up: What does a plant need?
picword:
  - sun | sunlight
  - faucet | water
  - ball | a ball
  - teddy-bear | a teddy bear
  - sock | a sock
  - book | a book
write: Circle the two pictures above that show what a plant needs to grow.
  lines: 0
  key: Circle sunlight and water.

## Main: Seeds on the move
match: Match each seed to how it travels.
  - dandelion seed with a fluffy top = The wind blows it far away.
  - burr with tiny hooks = It sticks to an animal's fur.
  - coconut = It floats across the water.
  - acorn = A squirrel buries it and forgets it.
choice: Why is it good for a seed to travel away from its parent plant?
  - It gets to see new places.
  * It gets more space, light and water to grow.
  - It can grow bigger seeds.

## Stretch: A bee's job
write: Explain a bee's job for a flower in one sentence. Use the word pollen.
  lines: 2
  starter: A bee helps a flower by ...
  key: A bee carries pollen from one flower to another, which helps the plant make seeds.
