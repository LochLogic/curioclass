# 1. Bass Clef Basics
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can name the lines and spaces of the bass staff.
directions: Label the lines and spaces of the bass staff. Then name 12 notes from a low instrument part and decode a word.
pairs: note-ninja
mistakes: The biggest trap is reading bass clef notes as if they were treble clef: a note on the bottom line is G in bass clef, not E. Have students say "bass clef" before every line of notes. Watch for space names in the wrong order (A C E G, not F A C E).
notes: Bass staff lines from the bottom: G B D F A ("Good Boys Do Fine Always"); spaces A C E G ("All Cows Eat Grass"). The bass clef is also called the F clef: its two dots sit on either side of line 4, the F line. Middle C sits on one ledger line above the bass staff. Tuba, trombone, bassoon, cello and string bass usually read bass clef (the string bass sounds an octave lower than written). Notes in Part B: G B D F A A, C E G E F B.

## Warm up: Lines and spaces
fill:
  - Bass staff lines, bottom to top: {G}, {B}, {D}, {F}, {A}
  - Bass staff spaces, bottom to top: {A}, {C}, {E}, {G}
  - The bass clef's two dots sit around line 4, the {F} line, so it is also called the F clef.
  - Middle C sits on one ledger line {above} the bass staff.

text: These notes come from a part for a cello or tuba. Write the letter name under each note.
staff: bass

## Stretch: Bass clef word
text: Name each note to find the word.
staff: bass
  - B2
  - A2
  - D3
  - G2
  - E3
fill: The word is {BADGE}.
write: Why can a note on the bottom line be G in one clef and E in another?
  lines: 2
  key: The clef tells which pitch each line stands for. The bass clef marks line 4 as F, which makes the bottom line G; the treble clef marks line 2 as G, which makes the bottom line E.

# 7. Markings Crossword
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can name and use dynamic and articulation markings.
directions: Solve the crossword: each clue describes a marking that tells you how loud or how to play a note. Write one letter in each box. Then mark 4 markings on the melody.
pairs: note-ninja
mistakes: Students mix up pianissimo and fortissimo because both end in "-issimo": the start of the word tells soft (piano) or loud (forte), and "-issimo" means "very." Watch for a slur confused with a tie: a slur connects different notes, a tie connects two notes of the same pitch. In Part C, check that staccato dots go above or below the note head, not beside it.
notes: The crossword uses the Italian words; MEZZOFORTE is two words (mezzo forte) written as one in the grid. Across: 1 SLUR, 7 PIANISSIMO, 8 FERMATA, 10 PIANO, 11 FORTE, 12 MEZZOFORTE. Down: 2 LEGATO, 3 FORTISSIMO, 4 DIMINUENDO, 5 CRESCENDO, 6 STACCATO, 9 ACCENT. Dynamics (how loud): pp, p, mf, f, ff, crescendo, diminuendo. Articulations (how each note starts, ends and connects): staccato, accent, legato, slur, fermata (strictly a hold sign, often taught with articulations).

## Warm up: Loud and soft
fill:
  - From softest to loudest: pp, {p}, mp, {mf}, f, {ff}.
  - "Mezzo" means {medium}.

write: How would the melody sound different if the markings were f, a diminuendo, legato and no accent?
  lines: 2

# 20. Minor Melody Composer
minutes: 40
materials: pencil; a barred instrument, keyboard or recorder
ican: I can compose a minor melody with a sequence and a strong ending on the tonic.
directions: Name the notes of the A natural minor scale and study the model. Then plan an 8-measure melody in A minor with at least one sequence and an ending on A, and write it on the staff.
pairs: melody-maker
mistakes: Students often write a sequence that is really a plain repeat (the same notes again). A sequence moves the whole pattern up or down: A B C becomes B C D. Watch for G sharp sneaking in: it is used in harmonic minor, but this sheet uses natural minor (no sharps or flats). And check the very last note: ending on C makes the melody sound like C major.
notes: A natural minor: A B C D E F G A, the same notes as C major with A as home (the tonic). The model melody, one measure per box: | A B C B | B C D C | C D E D | E - - - | F E D C | E D C B | D C B G | A - - - |. Measures 2 and 3 are a sequence of measure 1, each one step higher; measure 6 is a sequence of measure 5, one step lower. Measure 4 ends on E, which sounds unfinished (a question); measure 8 ends on A, the tonic (the answer). Students will bring their finished melody to the next sheet, Minor Melody Peer Critique.

## Warm up: A natural minor
text: Write the letter name under each note.
staff: treble
  - A4
  - B4
  - C5
  - D5
  - E5
  - F5
  - G5
  - A5
fill:
  - The tonic (home note) of A minor is {A}.
  - A minor has {0} sharps and flats. It shares its notes with {C} major.
choice: Which pair of measures shows a sequence?
  - | A B C | A B C |
  * | A B C | B C D |
  - | A B C | E D A |
choice: Which last measure gives the strongest ending in A minor?
  - | C - - - |
  - | E - - - |
  * | A - - - |

## Main: Plan your melody
text: **Model:** | A B C B | B C D C | C D E D | E - - - | F E D C | E D C B | D C B G | A - - - | ("-" holds the note one more beat)
chart: My notes (4 beats) | What this measure does
  labels: Measure 1; Measure 2; Measure 3; Measure 4 (question: not A); Measure 5; Measure 6; Measure 7; Measure 8 (ends on A)
  height: 0.33
  key: Answers vary. Check for 4 beats in each measure, only A B C D E F G, at least one sequence labeled (for example measures 1 and 2: A C B, then B D C), measure 4 ending on a note other than A, and measure 8 ending on A.

## Main: Write it on the staff
text: Write measures 1 to 4 on the first staff and 5 to 8 on the second. Draw bar lines. Circle your sequence.
staff: treble | blank
staff: treble | blank

## Stretch: Test the ending
write: Play your melody, then play it again ending on C instead of A. Describe the difference.
  lines: 2
  key: Ending on A sounds finished and a little serious, like coming home. Ending on C sounds brighter, like the melody switched to C major or stopped too soon.

# 21. Minor Melody Peer Critique
minutes: 35
materials: pencil; a barred instrument, keyboard or recorder
ican: I can evaluate a minor melody with a rubric and evidence.
directions: Warm up with the A minor scale and a sample melody. Then play your partner's minor melody twice, score it with the rubric, and write evidence for each score.
pairs: melody-maker
mistakes: Students often call any slow melody "minor." Mood comes partly from tempo, but minor is about the notes: the home note is A and the third note above it is C, not C#. Watch for "sequence" used for any repeat: a sequence repeats a pattern starting on a different note, while a plain repeat starts on the same note.
notes: A natural minor: A B C D E F G A, no sharps or flats (the same notes as C major, with A as home). A sequence repeats a short pattern a step or more higher or lower, for example A B C, then B C D. Students should arrive with an 8-measure melody in A minor written in letter names. Rubric: 3 strong, 2 getting there, 1 not yet. In the sample melody, measure 2 repeats the pattern of measure 1 one step lower.

## Warm up: A minor
fill:
  - The A natural minor scale: A B {C} D {E} F {G} A
  - A minor has {0} sharps or flats.
text: Sample melody: | E D C B | D C B A | C B A G | A - - - |
choice: Which describes measures 1 and 2 of the sample?
  - a plain repeat: the same notes again
  * a sequence: the same pattern one step lower
  - a leap
choice: Why does the sample sound finished?
  - It ends on a high note.
  * It ends on A, the home note of A minor.
  - It uses only steps.
choice: Which mood word fits the sample best?
  - bright and bouncy
  * calm and a little sad
  - silly

## Main: Score your partner's melody
table: Criterion | 3 Strong | 2 Getting there | 1 Not yet
  - Mood | Clearly minor; tempo and dynamics fit the mood | Minor, but the mood is unclear | Sounds major or random
  - Shape | A clear high point and a mix of steps and leaps | Some shape, but wanders | Flat or jumpy throughout
  - Sequence | At least one clear sequence | A repeat, but not a sequence | No pattern
  - Ending | Ends on A and sounds finished | Ends on A after an awkward leap | Ends on another note
write: Circle a score for each row. Give evidence for Mood and Shape: name notes or measures.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Evidence should name notes or measures, for example: "Mood 3: it stays around A, C and E, and played slowly it sounds thoughtful. Shape 2: measures 3 to 6 all stay between C and E."
write: Give evidence for Sequence and Ending.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. For example: "Sequence 3: measure 3 is A C B and measure 4 is B D C, the same pattern one step higher. Ending 1: it stops on G, so it does not sound finished."
write: Suggest one specific change the composer could make, and explain how it would improve the melody.
  lines: 3
  starter: In measure ___, change ...
  key: Answers vary. The change is specific and tied to a criterion, for example: "In measure 8, end on A instead of G so the melody sounds finished."

## Stretch: Reflect
write: Your partner reviewed your melody too. Which piece of feedback will you use, and why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the feedback and the change it will cause, for example: "I will add a sequence in measures 5 and 6, because my middle section repeats the same notes and gets boring."

# 33. In Tune or Out?
minutes: 30
materials: pencil; instruments or voices; a reference tone (keyboard, tuning app on the teacher's device, or tuning bars)
ican: I can tell if a pitch is sharp, flat or in tune, and try a fix.
directions: With a partner, play or sing 5 pitches against a reference tone. Record whether each one is sharp, flat or in tune, try a fix, and listen again. Then answer the conclusion questions.
mistakes: Students often call any note that sounds "wrong" flat. Sharp means too high and flat means too low, so have partners point up or down while they listen. Watch for fixes that change the note entirely (playing a different note) instead of nudging the same note up or down. Loud playing hides tuning problems: test at a comfortable medium volume.
notes: When two pitches are close but not the same, you hear a wobble called beats. The beats slow down as the pitches get closer and disappear when they match. Common fixes: on recorder, blowing harder raises the pitch and blowing more gently lowers it; on brass and many woodwinds, pushing the tuning slide or barrel in shortens the tube and raises the pitch, pulling it out lowers it; singers fix flat notes with more breath support and by thinking the pitch higher before singing. Choose pitches your ensemble uses, such as recorder G, A, B, C and D, or concert B flat, F, C, E flat and A flat for band. Each partner takes a turn as player and listener.

## Warm up: Sharp or flat?
choice: Your note sounds a little higher than the reference tone. It is:
  * sharp
  - flat
  - in tune
choice: As two pitches get closer together, the wobble (beats) you hear:
  - gets faster
  * gets slower, then disappears
  - gets louder
choice: On a recorder, your note is sharp. What should you try?
  - Blow harder.
  - Cover one more hole.
  * Blow more gently.
choice: Every note on a trumpet is flat. Pushing the main tuning slide in makes the tube shorter. What does that do?
  - lowers the pitch
  * raises the pitch
  - makes it louder

## Main: Test 5 pitches
text: **Question:** Can we hear when a pitch is out of tune, and fix it?
write: **Prediction:** How many of your 5 pitches do you think will be in tune on the first try? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: "3 of 5, because my high notes are usually sharp when I blow too hard."
chart: Pitch | First try (sharp, flat or in tune) | Fix we tried | After the fix
  labels: 1; 2; 3; 4; 5
  height: 0.45
  key: Answers vary. Sample row: G | sharp | blew more gently | in tune. A fix should move a sharp pitch down and a flat pitch up.

## Main: Conclusion
write: Which pitches were hardest to play in tune? What pattern do you notice?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: "The highest two notes were sharp on the first try. When I blow harder for high notes, the pitch goes up."
write: Which fix worked best, and why does it change the pitch?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. For example: "Blowing more gently worked best. Slower air makes the recorder's pitch lower, so the sharp notes came down to match."

## Stretch: Tune the ensemble
write: Why do ensembles tune to one reference pitch before they play?
  lines: 2
  key: If every player starts from the same reference, the same notes match across the ensemble. Without it, even players who are in tune with themselves can clash, and the group hears beats and a sour sound.
