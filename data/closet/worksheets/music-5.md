# 1. Bass Clef Basics
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can name the lines and spaces of the bass staff.
directions: Label the lines and spaces of the bass staff. Then name 12 notes from a low instrument part and decode a word.
pairs: note-ninja
mistakes: The biggest trap is reading bass clef notes as if they were treble clef: a note on the bottom line is G in bass clef, not E. Have students say "bass clef" before every line of notes. Watch for space names in the wrong order (A C E G, not F A C E).
notes: Bass staff lines from the bottom: G B D F A ("Good Boys Do Fine Always"); spaces A C E G ("All Cows Eat Grass"). The bass clef is also called the F clef: its two dots sit on either side of line 4, the F line. Middle C sits on one ledger line above the bass staff. Tuba, trombone, bassoon, cello and string bass usually read bass clef (the string bass sounds an octave lower than written). Notes in Part B: G B D F A A, C E G E F B.

## Warm up: Lines and spaces
fill:
  - Bass staff lines, bottom to top: {G}, {B}, {D}, {F}, {A}
  - Bass staff spaces, bottom to top: {A}, {C}, {E}, {G}
  - The bass clef's two dots sit around line 4, the {F} line, so it is also called the F clef.
  - Middle C sits on one ledger line {above} the bass staff.

## Main: Notes for low instruments
text: These notes come from a part for a cello or tuba. Write the letter name under each note.
staff: bass
  - G2
  - B2
  - D3
  - F3
  - A3
  - A2
staff: bass
  - C3
  - E3
  - G3
  - E2
  - F2
  - B3
choice: Which instrument usually reads music in bass clef?
  - flute
  - violin
  * tuba
  - trumpet
choice: The 10th note sits on a ledger line below the staff. What is it?
  - G
  * E
  - C
  - A

## Stretch: Bass clef word
text: Name each note to find the word.
staff: bass
  - B2
  - A2
  - D3
  - G2
  - E3
fill: The word is {BADGE}.
write: Why can a note on the bottom line be G in one clef and E in another?
  lines: 2
  key: The clef tells which pitch each line stands for. The bass clef marks line 4 as F, which makes the bottom line G; the treble clef marks line 2 as G, which makes the bottom line E.

# 21. Minor Melody Peer Critique
minutes: 35
materials: pencil; a barred instrument, keyboard or recorder
ican: I can evaluate a minor melody with a rubric and evidence.
directions: Warm up with the A minor scale and a sample melody. Then play your partner's minor melody twice, score it with the rubric, and write evidence for each score.
pairs: melody-maker
mistakes: Students often call any slow melody "minor." Mood comes partly from tempo, but minor is about the notes: the home note is A and the third note above it is C, not C#. Watch for "sequence" used for any repeat: a sequence repeats a pattern starting on a different note, while a plain repeat starts on the same note.
notes: A natural minor: A B C D E F G A, no sharps or flats (the same notes as C major, with A as home). A sequence repeats a short pattern a step or more higher or lower, for example A B C, then B C D. Students should arrive with an 8-measure melody in A minor written in letter names. Rubric: 3 strong, 2 getting there, 1 not yet. In the sample melody, measure 2 repeats the pattern of measure 1 one step lower.

## Warm up: A minor
fill:
  - The A natural minor scale: A B {C} D {E} F {G} A
  - A minor has {0} sharps or flats.
text: Sample melody: | E D C B | D C B A | C B A G | A - - - |
choice: Which describes measures 1 and 2 of the sample?
  - a plain repeat: the same notes again
  * a sequence: the same pattern one step lower
  - a leap
choice: Why does the sample sound finished?
  - It ends on a high note.
  * It ends on A, the home note of A minor.
  - It uses only steps.
choice: Which mood word fits the sample best?
  - bright and bouncy
  * calm and a little sad
  - silly

## Main: Score your partner's melody
table: Criterion | 3 Strong | 2 Getting there | 1 Not yet
  - Mood | Clearly minor; tempo and dynamics fit the mood | Minor, but the mood is unclear | Sounds major or random
  - Shape | A clear high point and a mix of steps and leaps | Some shape, but wanders | Flat or jumpy throughout
  - Sequence | At least one clear sequence | A repeat, but not a sequence | No pattern
  - Ending | Ends on A and sounds finished | Ends on A after an awkward leap | Ends on another note
write: Circle a score for each row. Give evidence for Mood and Shape: name notes or measures.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Evidence should name notes or measures, for example: "Mood 3: it stays around A, C and E, and played slowly it sounds thoughtful. Shape 2: measures 3 to 6 all stay between C and E."
write: Give evidence for Sequence and Ending.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. For example: "Sequence 3: measure 3 is A C B and measure 4 is B D C, the same pattern one step higher. Ending 1: it stops on G, so it does not sound finished."
write: Suggest one specific change the composer could make, and explain how it would improve the melody.
  lines: 3
  starter: In measure ___, change ...
  key: Answers vary. The change is specific and tied to a criterion, for example: "In measure 8, end on A instead of G so the melody sounds finished."

## Stretch: Reflect
write: Your partner reviewed your melody too. Which piece of feedback will you use, and why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the feedback and the change it will cause, for example: "I will add a sequence in measures 5 and 6, because my middle section repeats the same notes and gets boring."
