# 1. Main Idea or Detail?
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can tell the main idea of a text from the key details that support it and the extra details that do not.
directions: Read the passage. Cut out the 12 sentence cards. Sort each one: the main idea, a key detail that supports it, or an extra detail that is interesting but does not support the main idea. Glue them down, then explain one choice.
mistakes: Students often call the most interesting sentence a key detail (the wing beats, the Nobel Prize). Ask, "Does this help prove that the bees work together to keep the hive alive?" Watch for a key detail chosen as the main idea because it comes first in a paragraph.
notes: The passage is original to Curio Class (about 300 words). Facts checked: a colony has one queen that can lay well over a thousand eggs a day; workers are female and their jobs change with age; drones are male; the waggle dance shows direction and distance (Karl von Frisch shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine); wing beats are about 200 to 230 a second; European colonists brought honeybees to North America in the 1600s; jars of honey have been found in ancient Egyptian tombs. The glue boxes are short for sentence strips: students can glue the cards in overlapping rows, or glue them on the back under the same three headings.

## Read
passage: The Hive Team
  A honeybee hive can hold tens of thousands of bees, but it works almost like one living thing. A honeybee colony survives because thousands of bees work together and each one does a job.
  At the center of the colony is the queen. The queen's main job is to lay eggs. She can lay more than a thousand in one day. She does not rule the hive or tell other bees what to do. Worker bees are all female and do almost every other job in the hive.
  A worker's job changes as she gets older. Young workers clean the wax cells and feed the larvae. Larvae are the young bees that hatch from eggs. Middle-aged workers build new comb, store food and fan their wings to dry nectar until it becomes honey. Guard bees stand at the entrance and keep out bees from other hives. A honeybee beats its wings more than 200 times a second.
  Older workers fly out to collect nectar and pollen from flowers. A forager that finds food does a waggle dance that shows other bees where to fly. The dance tells them which way to go and how far. A scientist named Karl von Frisch won a Nobel Prize in 1973 partly for figuring out what the dance means.
  Drones are male bees whose one job is to mate with a queen from another colony. They do not gather food or guard the hive, and most of them are pushed out of the hive when winter comes.
  People have kept honeybees for thousands of years. Archaeologists have found jars of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs. Honeybees were brought to North America by European colonists in the 1600s.
  Each bee alone could not survive for long. A single worker cannot lay eggs, and the queen cannot feed herself. Together, by sharing the work, the colony stays strong through the seasons.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** main idea, key detail, extra detail, support, colony, queen, worker, drone, forager, larvae, comb, nectar
example: A **main idea** is what the whole text is mostly about. A **key detail** helps prove the main idea. An **extra detail** is true and interesting, but the main idea would still be clear without it.

## Main: Sort the sentences
sort: Glue each sentence card under the right heading.
  - Main idea: A honeybee colony survives because thousands of bees work together and each one does a job.
  - Key detail: The queen's main job is to lay eggs., Worker bees are all female and do almost every other job in the hive., Young workers clean the wax cells and feed the larvae., Guard bees stand at the entrance and keep out bees from other hives., Older workers fly out to collect nectar and pollen from flowers., A forager that finds food does a waggle dance that shows other bees where to fly., Drones are male bees whose one job is to mate with a queen from another colony.
  - Extra detail: A honeybee beats its wings more than 200 times a second., A scientist named Karl von Frisch won a Nobel Prize in 1973 partly for figuring out what the dance means., Archaeologists have found jars of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs., Honeybees were brought to North America by European colonists in the 1600s.

## Explain
write: Choose one key detail. Explain how it supports the main idea.
  lines: 3
  starter: The detail "___" is a key detail because it shows ___.
  key: Example: The detail "Guard bees stand at the entrance and keep out bees from other hives" is a key detail because it shows one job a bee does to keep the whole colony safe.
write: Choose one extra detail. Explain why it does not support the main idea.
  lines: 3
  starter: The detail "___" is an extra detail because ___.
  key: Example: The detail "A honeybee beats its wings more than 200 times a second" is an extra detail because it is about one bee's body, not about how the bees share work to keep the colony alive.

## Stretch: Say it your way
write: Write the main idea in your own words, using two key details.
  lines: 3
  starter: The passage is mainly about ___. For example, ___ and ___.
  key: The passage is mainly about how honeybees share jobs so the colony can survive. For example, the queen lays the eggs, and older workers collect nectar and pollen for food.

# 21. Lab Reports and Claims Check
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can name the parts of a lab report, write a hypothesis and write a claim with evidence and reasoning.
directions: Match each lab report part to an example from one experiment. Write a hypothesis. Then use the data table to finish the averages and write a short claim, evidence and reasoning (CER).
mistakes: Students mix up the hypothesis (a prediction before testing) and the conclusion (what the data showed). Ask, "Was this written before or after the test?" In the CER, students often give evidence with no numbers; push for exact data. Reasoning is the science idea that explains why, not a repeat of the evidence.
notes: The data are sample data for this sheet, close to what a class gets with one sugar cube in 200 mL of water, stirred the same way. Averages: (130 + 126) ÷ 2 = 128, (78 + 74) ÷ 2 = 76, (41 + 45) ÷ 2 = 43. Use warm tap water only; no boiling water or heat sources.

## Warm up: Parts of a lab report
text: **Word bank:** question, hypothesis, materials, procedure, data, conclusion, variable, claim, evidence, reasoning, average, dissolve, particles
match: Match each part to its example from a sugar cube experiment.
  - Question = Does water temperature change how fast sugar dissolves?
  - Hypothesis = If the water is warmer, then the sugar will dissolve faster.
  - Materials = 3 cups, sugar cubes, a thermometer, a timer, a spoon
  - Procedure = Put one cube in each cup, stir 10 times, and time it.
  - Data = Cold water took 128 seconds on average.
  - Conclusion = Sugar dissolved fastest in the warmest water.

## Main: Hypothesis
write: Another class asks, "Does stirring change how fast sugar dissolves?" Write a hypothesis.
  lines: 2
  starter: If ___, then ___ because ___.
  key: If we stir the water, then the sugar will dissolve faster because stirring moves fresh water past the sugar.

## Main: Data and CER
table: Water temperature | Trial 1 (seconds) | Trial 2 (seconds) | Average (seconds)
  - 10 °C (cold) | 130 | 126 | {128}
  - 22 °C (room) | 78 | 74 | {76}
  - 40 °C (warm) | 41 | 45 | {43}
write: **Claim:** What does the data show?
  lines: 2
  starter: The data show that ___.
  key: The data show that sugar dissolves faster in warmer water.
write: **Evidence:** Use numbers from the table.
  lines: 2
  starter: For example, in cold water it took ___ seconds, but in warm water it took only ___ seconds.
  key: For example, in cold water it took 128 seconds on average, but in warm water it took only 43 seconds.
write: **Reasoning:** Explain why, using a science idea.
  lines: 3
  starter: This happens because ___.
  key: This happens because water particles move faster when the water is warmer, so they hit the sugar more often and pull sugar particles away more quickly.

## Stretch: Fair test
write: Why did the class stir every cup 10 times and use the same size of sugar cube?
  lines: 2
  starter: They kept these the same so that ___.
  key: They kept these the same so that only the water temperature changed, and they could tell the temperature caused the difference (a fair test with controlled variables).
