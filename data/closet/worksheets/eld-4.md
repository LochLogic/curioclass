# 1. Point to the Page
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can support an idea with evidence by telling where the text says it.
directions: Read the passage about bats. It has four short pages. For each idea, find the evidence. Write which page it is on, what it says, and what that shows.
mistakes: Students often copy a whole page as evidence. Ask them to underline only the words that prove the idea. Watch for answers that skip the "so" part: the evidence has to be connected back to the idea.
notes: The passage is original to Curio Class (about 200 words). The four paragraphs are marked as pages so students can practice the frame "On page ___, it says ___, so ___." Facts checked: bats are the only mammals capable of true flight; the order name Chiroptera comes from Greek words for "hand" and "wing"; no bat species is blind; many bats use echolocation; some bats pollinate flowers and many fruit bats spread seeds.

## Read
passage: Bats: Night Fliers
  **Page 1.** When the sun goes down, bats wake up. Bats are mammals, like dogs and people. They have fur, and mother bats feed their babies milk. But bats can do something no other mammal can do. They can truly fly.
  **Page 2.** A bat's wing is made of thin skin stretched over long finger bones. The scientific name for the bat group, Chiroptera, means "hand wing." Bats can bend their fingers to change the shape of their wings, so they can turn and dive quickly in the air.
  **Page 3.** Many bats hunt insects in the dark. They do not need light to find food. A bat makes high sounds, and the sounds bounce off a moth or a beetle. The bat listens to the echoes to learn where the insect is. This is called echolocation. Bats are not blind, though. All bats can see.
  **Page 4.** Bats help people and plants. One bat can eat hundreds of insects in a single night, so farmers have fewer bugs eating their crops. Some bats drink nectar from flowers. As they move from flower to flower, they carry pollen, the way bees do. Other bats eat fruit and drop the seeds far away, where new plants can grow.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** mammal, wing, finger bones, echolocation, echo, insect, nectar, pollen, seed, evidence, it says, this shows, so
example: **Idea:** Bats are active at night.
  On page **1**, it says "when the sun goes down, bats wake up," so bats are awake at night.
choice: Which page explains how bats find insects in the dark?
  - Page 1
  - Page 2
  * Page 3
  - Page 4

## Stretch: Two pages
write: **Idea:** Bats are important to plants. Use evidence from page 4 and tell what it shows.
  lines: 4
  starter: On page ___, it says ___, so ___. Also, it says ___, so ___.
  key: On page 4, it says bats "carry pollen, the way bees do," so they help flowers make seeds. Also, it says fruit bats "drop the seeds far away, where new plants can grow," so they help new plants spread.

# 21. Area or Perimeter?
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can explain whether a question asks for area or perimeter and solve it.
directions: Read each question. Draw the rectangle and label its sides. Decide if the question asks for area or perimeter, explain why with the frame, and solve.
mistakes: The most common mistake is multiplying for every question. Ask, "Is it going around the edge, or covering the inside?" Watch for missing units: perimeter is in feet or inches, area is in square units. Some students add only two sides for perimeter.
notes: Clue words: around, edge, border, fence and frame point to perimeter; cover, fill, inside, carpet and tiles point to area. For the Stretch, the only rectangle with whole-number sides, area 24 and perimeter 20 is 6 by 4 (or 4 by 6).

## Warm up: Clue words
text: **Word bank:** area, perimeter, around, edge, cover, inside, length, width, square units, feet, meters, inches
fill:
  - Perimeter is the distance {around} the edge of a shape.
  - Area is the space {inside} a shape. We measure it in {square} units.
  - A fence goes around a yard, so it is about {perimeter}.
  - Tiles cover a floor, so they are about {area}.

## Main: Three rectangles
text: **A.** Ms. Diaz wants to put a fence around her garden. The garden is 6 feet long and 4 feet wide. How much fence does she need?
draw: Draw and label the garden. | 1.3
write: Does the question ask for area or perimeter? Why?
  lines: 1
  starter: This question asks for the {perimeter} because ...
  key: This question asks for the perimeter because a fence goes around the edge of the garden.
fill: 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = {20} feet of fence
text: **B.** A new rug will cover the whole floor of a room. The room is 5 meters long and 3 meters wide. How big must the rug be?
draw: Draw and label the floor. | 1.3
write: Does the question ask for area or perimeter? Why?
  lines: 1
  starter: This question asks for the {area} because ...
  key: This question asks for the area because the rug covers the inside of the floor.
fill: 5 x 3 = {15} square meters
text: **C.** Jae is gluing ribbon around the edge of a poster. The poster is 9 inches long and 2 inches wide. How much ribbon does Jae need?
draw: Draw and label the poster. | 1.3
write: Does the question ask for area or perimeter? Why?
  lines: 1
  starter: This question asks for the {perimeter} because ...
  key: This question asks for the perimeter because the ribbon goes around the edge of the poster.
fill: 9 + 2 + 9 + 2 = {22} inches of ribbon

## Stretch: Both at once
write: A rectangle has an area of 24 square units and a perimeter of 20 units. What are its length and width? Explain how you know.
  lines: 3
  starter: The rectangle is ___ by ___ because the area is ___ x ___ = 24 and the perimeter is ___.
  key: The rectangle is 6 by 4 because the area is 6 x 4 = 24 and the perimeter is 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20.
