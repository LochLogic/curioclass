# 1. Germ Hiding Spots
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can find places where germs hide and tell how to stop them.
directions: (Read aloud.) Germs like places that many hands touch. Look at each thing in our pretend classroom. Write yes if many hands touch it. Write no if hands almost never touch it. Then draw soap bubbles around every yes.
mistakes: Children may say "no" to the pencil cup or the ball because they look clean. Germs are too small to see: ask, "How many hands touch this every day?" The clock, flag and flower are up high or left alone, so few hands touch them; a child who argues "someone waters the flower" is thinking well, so ask how many hands touch it in a day compared with the doorknob.
notes: Name each picture together first: doorknob, clock, sink handle, pencil cup, flag, light switch, ball, flower, chair. The six yes answers are the classroom's high-touch spots. Follow up with a real walk around the room to find your own high-touch spots, and remind children that washing hands with soap and water is how we stop germs we pick up from them.

## Warm up: Germs are tiny
example: (Read aloud.) Germs are too small to see. They ride on hands from one thing to the next. Washing with soap and water washes them away.

## Main: Yes or no?
picword:
  - door | doorknob {yes}
  - clock | wall clock {no}
  - faucet | sink handle {yes}
  - pencil-cup | pencil cup {yes}
  - flag | flag {no}
  - light-switch | light switch {yes}
  - ball | recess ball {yes}
  - flower | flower {no}
  - chair | chair {yes}

## Stretch: Stop the germs
choice: (Read aloud.) Ben played with the recess ball. Now it is snack time. What should Ben do first?
  - Wipe his hands on his shirt
  * Wash his hands with soap and water
  - Blow on his hands
draw: Draw a friend washing germs away. Put lots of soap bubbles in your picture! | 1.4

# 21. Asking for Help Steps
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can ask a grown-up for help.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the help cards with your teacher. Write each card's letter on the number line to show the order of the steps. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Children often start with "find a helper" before they can say what is wrong. Act it out: first notice the problem ("My shoe is untied and I keep tripping"), then find the grown-up. Some put "say thank you" in the middle; ask, "Do we say thank you before or after we get help?"
notes: The four steps: notice the problem, find a helper, say what happened, say thank you. Brainstorm school helpers first (teacher, school nurse, lunch helper, bus driver, principal). For anything scary or unsafe, children should go to a grown-up right away and keep telling until someone helps; say this out loud as you finish.

## Warm up: Who can help?
choice: (Read aloud.) Lily feels sick at school. Who is a good grown-up to help her?
  - A big kid on the slide
  * Her teacher or the school nurse
  - Nobody; she should keep it to herself
choice: (Read aloud.) Owen is lost at the park. Who is a good grown-up to help him?
  - A dog walking by
  - Nobody; he should hide
  * A park worker or a police officer

## Main: Put the steps in order
timeline: 1 4 1
  - 1 | Notice the problem: "My lunch box is missing."
  - 2 | Find a helper: walk to your teacher.
  - 3 | Say what happened: "I can't find my lunch box."
  - 4 | Say thank you: "Thank you for helping me!"

## Stretch: Show it
draw: Draw Mia asking a grown-up for help. Mia's kite is stuck in a tree. | 1.8
