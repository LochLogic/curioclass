# 1. Germ Hiding Spots
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can find places where germs hide and tell how to stop them.
directions: (Read aloud.) Germs like places that many hands touch. Look at each thing in our pretend classroom. Write yes if many hands touch it. Write no if hands almost never touch it. Then draw soap bubbles around every yes.
mistakes: Children may say "no" to the pencil cup or the ball because they look clean. Germs are too small to see: ask, "How many hands touch this every day?" The clock, flag and flower are up high or left alone, so few hands touch them; a child who argues "someone waters the flower" is thinking well, so ask how many hands touch it in a day compared with the doorknob.
notes: Name each picture together first: doorknob, clock, sink handle, pencil cup, flag, light switch, ball, flower, chair. The six yes answers are the classroom's high-touch spots. Follow up with a real walk around the room to find your own high-touch spots, and remind children that washing hands with soap and water is how we stop germs we pick up from them.

## Warm up: Germs are tiny
example: (Read aloud.) Germs are too small to see. They ride on hands from one thing to the next. Washing with soap and water washes them away.

## Main: Yes or no?
picword:
  - door | doorknob {yes}
  - clock | wall clock {no}
  - faucet | sink handle {yes}
  - pencil-cup | pencil cup {yes}
  - flag | flag {no}
  - light-switch | light switch {yes}
  - ball | recess ball {yes}
  - flower | flower {no}
  - chair | chair {yes}

## Stretch: Stop the germs
choice: (Read aloud.) Ben played with the recess ball. Now it is snack time. What should Ben do first?
  - Wipe his hands on his shirt
  * Wash his hands with soap and water
  - Blow on his hands
draw: Draw a friend washing germs away. Put lots of soap bubbles in your picture! | 1.4

# 7. Healthy Habits Picture Words
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can use healthy habit words and show what they mean.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each word with your teacher. Draw a picture that shows what the word means. Then finish each sentence with a word from the word bank.
mistakes: Children often draw a germ as a big bug they can see. Remind them germs are too tiny to see without a microscope; a drawing can show a germ "zoomed in" or tiny dots on hands. "Rest" and "sleep" are close: rest can also mean sitting quietly or lying down when you are sick.
notes: Talk about each word before drawing. Germ: a tiny living thing, too small to see, that can make people sick. Soap: it lifts germs and dirt off your hands so water can wash them away. Rest: quiet time or sleep that lets the body get better and grow. Exercise: moving your body hard enough to breathe faster, like running, jumping or dancing. Children in grade 1 need about an hour of active play every day (CDC).

## Draw the words
draw: germ | 1.6
draw: soap | 1.6
draw: rest | 1.6
draw: exercise | 1.6

## Finish the sentences
text: Word bank: **germ**, **soap**, **rest**, **exercise**
fill:
  - A {germ} is too tiny to see, and it can make you sick.
  - Wash your hands with {soap} and water.
  - When you are sick, your body needs lots of {rest}.
  - Running, jumping and dancing are kinds of {exercise}.

## Stretch: Use two words
fill:
  - Mia scrubs with {soap} so a {germ} will not make her sick.
write: (Read aloud.) Tell what your favorite kind of exercise is. Write one sentence.
  lines: 2
  starter: My favorite exercise is ...
  key: Answers vary. Model: My favorite exercise is jumping rope.

# 20. Good Listener Pictures
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can show I am listening with my eyes, mouth and body.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each pair of pictures, A and B. Circle the good listener. Write its letter. Then draw one more good listener.
mistakes: Pair 4 is the hardest: children may pick the sleepy face because it is quiet and still. A good listener is awake and thinking; nodding shows "I hear you." If a child says the wiggling child might be listening, agree that some people listen better when they move a little, then ask which picture shows a still body.
notes: Name the pictures together. Pair 1: eyes looking at the speaker, or eyes looking away. Pair 2: talking, or a quiet mouth. Pair 3: a wiggly body, or a still body. Pair 4: nodding, or falling asleep. Good listeners: eyes looking, quiet mouth, still body, nodding. Some children, including many autistic children, listen best without eye contact or while moving; teach these as signals that help the speaker, not as the only way to listen.

  - listen-nod | 4A
  - listen-sleepy | 4B
fill:
  - Pair 1: {A}
  - Pair 2: {B}
  - Pair 3: {B}

## Listening words
choice: (Read aloud.) Your friend is telling you about her new puppy. What can you say to show you listened?
  - "Can we play tag now?"
  * "What is your puppy's name?"
  - "My cat is better."

## Stretch: Draw a good listener
draw: Draw a good listener at story time. Show eyes looking, a quiet mouth and a still body. | 2.2

# 21. Asking for Help Steps
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can ask a grown-up for help.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the help cards with your teacher. Write each card's letter on the number line to show the order of the steps. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Children often start with "find a helper" before they can say what is wrong. Act it out: first notice the problem ("My shoe is untied and I keep tripping"), then find the grown-up. Some put "say thank you" in the middle; ask, "Do we say thank you before or after we get help?"
notes: The four steps: notice the problem, find a helper, say what happened, say thank you. Brainstorm school helpers first (teacher, school nurse, lunch helper, bus driver, principal). For anything scary or unsafe, children should go to a grown-up right away and keep telling until someone helps; say this out loud as you finish.

## Warm up: Who can help?
choice: (Read aloud.) Lily feels sick at school. Who is a good grown-up to help her?
  - A big kid on the slide
  * Her teacher or the school nurse
  - Nobody; she should keep it to herself
choice: (Read aloud.) Owen is lost at the park. Who is a good grown-up to help him?
  - A dog walking by
  - Nobody; he should hide
  * A park worker or a police officer

## Main: Put the steps in order
timeline: 1 4 1
  - 1 | Notice the problem: "My lunch box is missing."
  - 2 | Find a helper: walk to your teacher.
  - 3 | Say what happened: "I can't find my lunch box."
  - 4 | Say thank you: "Thank you for helping me!"

## Stretch: Show it
draw: Draw Mia asking a grown-up for help. Mia's kite is stuck in a tree. | 1.8

# 33. Ray Needs a Goal
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can match a health problem to a goal that helps.
directions: (Read aloud.) Ray the raccoon has 3 problems. Read each problem with your teacher. Draw a line from each problem to the goal that fixes it, and write the goal's letter. Then draw Ray doing one goal.
mistakes: Children may match "thirsty" to eating a snack. Ask, "What does a thirsty body need?" Some match sticky hands to a bath; a bath gets hands clean too, so praise the thinking and point to the goal that fits best for right now: washing hands.
notes: Ray is a story character; keep the talk on Ray. Act out each problem with Ray's voice before matching: "Yawn! I'm always tired." "I played tag and now I'm so thirsty!" "My hands are sticky from honey!" Goals start with "I will," which makes them something Ray can do.

## Ray's goals
picword:
  - pillow | go to bed on time
  - cup | drink water
  - soap | wash hands

## Match the problem to the goal
match: Draw a line, then write the letter.
  - Ray is always tired. = I will go to bed on time.
  - Ray is thirsty after tag. = I will drink water.
  - Ray's hands are sticky from honey. = I will wash my hands with soap.

## When will Ray do it?
choice: (Read aloud.) Ray's goal is "I will wash my hands with soap." When is the best time?
  - Only on his birthday
  - After he eats his snack, and never before
  * Before he eats and after he uses the bathroom

## Stretch: Draw Ray
draw: Draw Ray doing one of his goals. | 2.0
write: (Read aloud.) Finish Ray's goal sentence.
  lines: 1
  starter: I will ...
  key: Answers vary. Models: I will go to bed on time. I will drink water. I will wash my hands with soap.
