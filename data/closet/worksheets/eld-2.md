# 1. Three-Step Direction Page
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons (yellow and green)
ican: I can read and follow directions with three steps, and write a direction for a partner.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each direction. Do the three steps in order: first, next, last. Then write your own direction for a partner.
mistakes: Students often skip the middle step or do the steps out of order. Have them touch each step word (first, next, last) and check it off with a small mark as they finish it. Left and right get mixed up: have students hold up their left hand and make an L.
notes: Read the directions aloud with students who are still building reading, but let them point to each step word as you read. Item 5 and item 6 build one picture in the box, so check item 5 before students start item 6. The partner box in the Stretch is for a classmate to follow the student's direction; trade papers.

## Warm up: First, next, last
example: **First**, find the star. **Next**, circle it. **Last**, write the number 1 under it.
  Three steps, in order. Each step starts with a step word.
text: **Word bank:** first, next, last, above, below, under, left, right, middle, corner, circle, write, draw, color
choice: Which word tells you the step that comes at the end?
  - First
  - Next
  * Last
  - Then

## Main: Draw a picture
draw: My picture | 2.6
write: First, draw a big house in the middle of the box. Next, draw a door on the house. Last, draw a sun in the top right corner.
  lines: 0
  key: A house in the middle with a door; a sun in the top right corner of the box.

## Stretch: Write a direction
write: Write a three-step direction for a partner. Use first, next and last. Your partner follows it in the box below.
  lines: 3
  starter: First, ... Next, ... Last, ...
  key: Answers vary. Example: "First, draw a circle in the middle of the box. Next, draw two eyes in the circle. Last, draw a hat above the circle." Each step has an action word and a place word.
draw: Partner's box | 1.8

# 21. Math Talk Review
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can describe place value, compare numbers and explain my strategy.
directions: (Read aloud.) Use the word bank and the sentence frames to answer. Write numbers and words on the lines.
mistakes: Students often name the digit instead of its value ("the 7 means 7" in 472). Ask, "7 what? 7 tens is how much?" When comparing, some students look at the ones first; have them start at the biggest place. In 38 + 25, watch for 513 written by putting 50 and 13 side by side.
notes: Have students say each frame aloud to a partner before they write it. The rods are tens and the cubes are ones; let students build the warm-up number with real base-ten blocks if you have them. The stretch has a wrong answer on purpose: students practice "I disagree because".

## Warm up: Tens and ones
text: **Word bank:** hundreds, tens, ones, digit, value, place, greater than, less than, equal to, add, strategy, agree, disagree
count: (Read aloud.) How many tens? How many ones?
  - 3 ten-rod
  - 4 one-cube
fill: 3 tens and 4 ones make {34}.

## Main: Place value
table: Number | Hundreds | Tens | Ones
  - 245 | {2} | {4} | {5}
  - 608 | {6} | {0} | {8}
  - 370 | {3} | {7} | {0}
fill:
  - In 472, the 7 is in the {tens} place. Its value is {70}.
  - In 815, the 8 is in the {hundreds} place. Its value is {800}.
  - In 539, the digit in the ones place is {9}.

## Main: Compare
fill:
  - 462 {>} 426
  - 319 {<} 391
  - 705 {>} 570
write: Explain why 462 is greater than 426.
  lines: 2
  starter: Both numbers have {4} hundreds. 462 has {6} tens, but 426 has only {2} tens.
  key: Both numbers have 4 hundreds. 462 has 6 tens, but 426 has only 2 tens, so 462 is greater than 426.

## Main: My strategy
write: Explain how you can solve 38 + 25.
  lines: 2
  starter: First, I added the tens: 30 + 20 = {50}. Next, I added the ones: 8 + 5 = {13}. Last, I put the two parts together: {63}.
  key: First, I added the tens: 30 + 20 = 50. Next, I added the ones: 8 + 5 = 13. Last, I added 50 + 13 = 63.

## Stretch: Agree or disagree?
write: Lena says 52 + 19 = 61. Do you agree? Explain.
  lines: 3
  starter: I {disagree} because ...
  key: I disagree because 50 + 10 = 60 and 2 + 9 = 11, and 60 + 11 = 71. Lena forgot the ten she made from the ones.
