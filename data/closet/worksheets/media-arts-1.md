# 1. Snowman Four-Panel Storyboard
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can plan a story in order with a storyboard.
directions: (Read aloud.) A storyboard is a plan for a story, drawn in boxes. Answer the two questions. Then draw a snowman story in 4 boxes, one part in each box. Write one word under each box.
mistakes: Children often draw the whole story in the first box, or the same picture four times. Point to each box and ask, "What happens now?" Watch for a last box that is not an ending: the story should finish, not just stop.
notes: Talk through a snowman story together before drawing: rolling the snow, stacking the balls, adding a hat and scarf, and an ending (the sun melts it, or the snowman waves goodbye). Children may tell a different snowman story; any 4 parts in order are fine. Keep drawings quick: stick figures and simple shapes are what storyboard artists use too.

## Warm up: What comes first?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which picture comes first in a snowman story?
  * Kids roll a big ball of snow.
  - The snowman melts in the sun.
  - The snowman wears a hat.
choice: (Read aloud.) Which picture could be the end?
  - Kids put on their mittens.
  - Kids roll a small ball of snow.
  * The sun comes out and the snowman melts.

## Main: My storyboard
text: Word bank: roll, stack, hat, scarf, sun, melt, wave, snow
draw: Box 1: Start | 1.7
write: Word for box 1
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary; one word that names the action, such as roll or snow.
draw: Box 2 | 1.7
write: Word for box 2
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary, such as stack.
draw: Box 3 | 1.7
write: Word for box 3
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary, such as hat or scarf.
draw: Box 4: End | 1.7
write: Word for box 4
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary, such as melt, sun or wave. The word should match an ending.

## Stretch: Add a sound
write: (Read aloud.) What sound could go with box 4? Write it or say it.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary: drip drip for melting, whoosh for wind, or "bye!" for a waving snowman.

# 7. Picture Plus Sound
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can choose a sound that goes with a picture.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story. Look at the 4 pictures. Cut out the sound cards. Glue the right sound under each picture. Two cards are left over.
mistakes: Children sometimes glue the sounds in the order the cards were printed. Read each picture's sentence again and ask, "What would we hear?" The two extra cards (moo and buzz) are there on purpose: there is no cow or bee in this story.
notes: Read the story aloud twice: "1. Tick-tock! Sam's clock wakes him up. 2. Rain falls on the window. 3. A frog hops into a puddle. 4. Sam's dog runs in and barks hello." Answers: 1 tick-tock, 2 pitter-patter, 3 ribbit, 4 woof; moo and buzz are left over. After gluing, read the whole story with the class making each sound.

## Warm up: What do you hear?
choice: (Read aloud.) What sound does rain make?
  - moo
  * pitter-patter
  - ribbit

# 20. Practice My Lines
minutes: 20
materials: pencil; your line for our class show
ican: I can practice my lines so my audience can hear and understand me.
directions: (Read aloud.) Write your line. Say it 3 times. After each time, check a box for each thing you did: loud voice, eyes up, slow speaking.
mistakes: Children often check every box every time. Have a partner watch and say "yes" or "not yet" for each box. Slow speaking is the hardest: most children speed up when they are nervous. A good test is to pause after every few words, like walking on stepping stones.
notes: Pair children so one practices while the other watches, then switch. The checks will vary; the goal is that the third practice has more checks than the first. Help children copy their line from the board or a script strip. A child who is not writing yet can say the line to you and you can scribe it.

## Warm up: Speak up
example: Loud voice: the back of the room can hear you. Eyes up: look at your audience, not your paper. Slow: say each word clearly.
choice: (Read aloud.) Where should your eyes be when you say your line?
  - on your shoes
  * on your audience
  - on the ceiling

## Main: My line
write: (Read aloud.) Write your line here.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary: the child's line from the class show.
table: Practice | Loud voice | Eyes up | Slow speaking
  height: 0.55
  - 1 | | |
  - 2 | | |
  - 3 | | |
note: Checks vary. Look for more checks in practice 3 than in practice 1.

## Stretch
write: (Read aloud.) What got better from practice 1 to practice 3?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary, such as "I looked up more" or "I talked slower the last time."

# 21. Invitation for Another Class
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can make an invitation that tells who, when and where.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the sample invitation with your teacher. Answer the questions. Then plan and make your own invitation to our slideshow for another class.
mistakes: Children often leave out the time or the place, or write only the day. Have them check the invitation against the plan: To, What, When, Where, From. Pictures sometimes crowd out the words: the words must be easy to read from across a desk.
notes: Fill in the real day, time and place on the board before children start so they can copy them. Deliver the finished invitations to the other class, or choose one to send and post the rest. A good invitation has big words, all five parts and a picture that shows what the event is.

## Warm up: Read an invitation
example: To: Room 12
  Come see our slideshow about bugs!
  When: Friday at 10:00
  Where: the library
  From: Room 8
choice: (Read aloud.) Which part tells WHEN?
  - the library
  * Friday at 10:00
  - Room 8
choice: (Read aloud.) Which part tells WHERE?
  * the library
  - Friday at 10:00
  - Room 12
choice: (Read aloud.) Who is the invitation FROM?
  - Room 12
  - the bugs
  * Room 8

# 33. Media in Maya's Day
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can find the media in a day.
directions: (Read aloud.) Media is anything that sends us words, pictures or sounds, like a song, a sign, a video or a book. Listen to the story about Maya. Circle each time she meets media. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Children may circle every thing Maya does, like brushing her teeth. Ask, "Does it send her words, pictures or sounds that someone made?" Some will miss the cereal box: the words and pictures on a box were made by someone to send a message, so it counts.
notes: Read the story twice, the second time pausing after each sentence so children can circle. The five media are the song on the radio, the words on the cereal box, the movie poster, the frog video and the picture book. Brushing her teeth and playing tag are not media. The story is original to Curio Class (65 words).

## Read
passage: Maya's Day
  Maya wakes up and hears a song on the radio. She brushes her teeth. At breakfast, she reads the words on her cereal box. On the way to school, she sees a big poster for a new movie.
  In class, Maya and her friends watch a short video about frogs. After school, she plays tag in the park. At bedtime, she reads a picture book.

## Questions
fill: (Read aloud.) Maya met media {5} times.
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is NOT media?
  - the poster
  * brushing her teeth
  - the video
choice: (Read aloud.) Which media did Maya hear?
  * a song on the radio
  - a picture book
  - a cereal box

## Stretch: My media
draw: Draw one kind of media you see or hear in a day. | 2
note: Answers vary: a show, a sign, a book, a song, a game screen, a poster.
