# 1. Roots as Exponents
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can rewrite expressions between radical form and rational exponent form and simplify them with the properties of exponents.
directions: In this sheet x^(m/n) means x raised to the power m/n. Rewrite each expression in the other form, then simplify the numerical ones. In Part C, use the properties of exponents and write each answer with one exponent.
mistakes: The numerator and denominator trade places easily: x^(2/3) is the cube root of x², not the square root of x³. Remember: the denominator is the root (the "index"), the numerator is the power. For 16^(3/4), taking the root first (⁴√16 = 2, then 2³ = 8) keeps the numbers small. A negative exponent means a reciprocal, not a negative number: 25^(-1/2) = 1/5, not -5.
notes: Accept either radical form when rewriting (∛x)⁴ and x^(3/4): ∛(x⁴) or (∛x)⁴, and ⁴√(x³) or (⁴√x)³. Variables are positive throughout. In Part C, 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 needs a common denominator, which is where most errors happen.

## Warm up: Match the forms
match: Match each radical to its exponent form.
  - √x = x^(1/2)
  - ∛x = x^(1/3)
  - ∛(x²) = x^(2/3)
  - (√x)³ = x^(3/2)

## Main: Rewrite
example: The cube root of x is x^(1/3), because (x^(1/3))³ = x^(3/3) = x. In the same way, ⁵√(a²) = a^(2/5): the root is the denominator and the power is the numerator.
fill: y^(1/4) = {⁴√y}
fill: ⁵√(a²) = {a^(2/5)}
fill: (∛x)⁴ = {x^(4/3)}
fill: x^(3/4) = {⁴√(x³)}
fill: 7^(1/2) = {√7}
fill: 27^(1/3) = {∛27} = {3}
fill: 16^(3/4) = {(⁴√16)³} = {8}
fill: 8^(2/3) = {(∛8)²} = {4}

## Part C: Simplify with exponent rules
fill: x^(1/2) · x^(1/3) = {x^(5/6)}
fill: (x^(2/3))^(3/2) = {x}
fill: 25^(-1/2) = {1/5}
fill: x^(5/4) ÷ x^(1/4) = {x}

## Stretch: Why it works
write: Explain why 8^(1/3) must equal 2. Use the power of a power rule, (a^m)^n = a^(mn).
  lines: 3
  key: (8^(1/3))³ = 8^((1/3)(3)) = 8¹ = 8. So 8^(1/3) is a number whose cube is 8, which is the cube root of 8, and 2³ = 8, so 8^(1/3) = 2.

# 19. Spot the Solving Mistake
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can find and fix mistakes in solving linear equations and inequalities.
directions: Each problem was solved by a student who made one mistake. Find the first wrong step, write the correct answer, and explain what went wrong. Check your answer by substituting it.
mistakes: Students often mark the last line as wrong instead of the first wrong step. The inequality problem is the one most students miss: dividing by a negative flips the sign.
notes: Encourage substitution: putting the correct answer back into the original equation proves it. Pairs can swap explanations and grade each other's "what went wrong" sentences.

## Warm up
example: Solve 4x - 3 = 9. Add 3 to both sides: 4x = 12. Divide by 4: x = 3. Check: 4(3) - 3 = 9. It works.

## Main: Find and fix
fixit: 3(x - 2) = 12
  * 3x - 2 = 12
  - 3x = 14
  - x = 14/3
  answer: x = 6
  why: The 3 must multiply both terms: 3x - 6 = 12, so 3x = 18 and x = 6.
fixit: 2x + 5 = 17
  * 2x = 22
  - x = 11
  answer: x = 6
  why: To undo + 5, subtract 5 from both sides: 2x = 12.
fixit: x/4 - 3 = 5
  - x/4 = 8
  * x = 2
  answer: x = 32
  why: To undo dividing by 4, multiply by 4: x = 32.
fixit: 5x - 7 = 3x + 9
  - 2x - 7 = 9
  * 2x = 2
  - x = 1
  answer: x = 8
  why: To undo - 7, add 7 to both sides: 2x = 16.
fixit: -2x + 4 > 10
  - -2x > 6
  * x > -3
  answer: x < -3
  why: Dividing both sides by a negative number flips the inequality sign.
fixit: 4(x + 1) - 2x = 14
  - 4x + 4 - 2x = 14
  * 6x + 4 = 14
  - 6x = 10
  - x = 10/6
  answer: x = 5
  why: 4x - 2x is 2x, not 6x: 2x + 4 = 14, so x = 5.

## Stretch
write: Write your own problem with a hidden mistake for a partner to find. Show the correct answer on the back.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Check that the problem has exactly one first wrong step and a correct answer.
