# 1. Roots as Exponents
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can rewrite expressions between radical form and rational exponent form and simplify them with the properties of exponents.
directions: In this sheet x^(m/n) means x raised to the power m/n. Rewrite each expression in the other form, then simplify the numerical ones. In Part C, use the properties of exponents and write each answer with one exponent.
mistakes: The numerator and denominator trade places easily: x^(2/3) is the cube root of x², not the square root of x³. Remember: the denominator is the root (the "index"), the numerator is the power. For 16^(3/4), taking the root first (⁴√16 = 2, then 2³ = 8) keeps the numbers small. A negative exponent means a reciprocal, not a negative number: 25^(-1/2) = 1/5, not -5.
notes: Accept either radical form when rewriting (∛x)⁴ and x^(3/4): ∛(x⁴) or (∛x)⁴, and ⁴√(x³) or (⁴√x)³. Variables are positive throughout. In Part C, 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 needs a common denominator, which is where most errors happen.

## Warm up: Match the forms
match: Match each radical to its exponent form.
  - √x = x^(1/2)
  - ∛x = x^(1/3)
  - ∛(x²) = x^(2/3)
  - (√x)³ = x^(3/2)

## Main: Rewrite
example: The cube root of x is x^(1/3), because (x^(1/3))³ = x^(3/3) = x. In the same way, ⁵√(a²) = a^(2/5): the root is the denominator and the power is the numerator.
fill: y^(1/4) = {⁴√y}
fill: ⁵√(a²) = {a^(2/5)}
fill: (∛x)⁴ = {x^(4/3)}
fill: x^(3/4) = {⁴√(x³)}
fill: 7^(1/2) = {√7}
fill: 27^(1/3) = {∛27} = {3}
fill: 16^(3/4) = {(⁴√16)³} = {8}
fill: 8^(2/3) = {(∛8)²} = {4}

## Part C: Simplify with exponent rules
fill: x^(1/2) · x^(1/3) = {x^(5/6)}
fill: (x^(2/3))^(3/2) = {x}
fill: 25^(-1/2) = {1/5}
fill: x^(5/4) ÷ x^(1/4) = {x}

## Stretch: Why it works
write: Explain why 8^(1/3) must equal 2. Use the power of a power rule, (a^m)^n = a^(mn).
  lines: 3
  key: (8^(1/3))³ = 8^((1/3)(3)) = 8¹ = 8. So 8^(1/3) is a number whose cube is 8, which is the cube root of 8, and 2³ = 8, so 8^(1/3) = 2.

# 5. How Precise Should It Be?
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can choose a sensible unit and level of accuracy for a measurement and report a result with sensible precision.
directions: For each measurement, choose the unit and the level of accuracy that make sense for the job. Think about who uses the number and what goes wrong if it is too rough or too precise. Then explain two of your choices.
mistakes: Students often think "more decimal places is always better". A city's population to the nearest person is out of date in an hour, and a highway length to the millimeter is impossible to measure. The opposite mistake matters too: a medicine dose rounded to the nearest 100 mL could be dangerous. In the stretch, students copy every digit from the calculator; an answer cannot be more precise than the measurements it came from.
notes: Accept reasonable alternatives with a good reason (for example, a highway length to the tenth of a mile). Race times in track are reported to the hundredth of a second. Stretch work: 3.6 × 4.3 = 15.48, so about 15.5 square meters; 236 ÷ 62 ≈ 3.806 hours, about 3.8 hours or 3 hours and 48 minutes.

## Warm up
fill:
  - A ruler is marked in millimeters. The most precise length it can give for a pencil is to the nearest {millimeter}.
  - Which is more precise, 12.5 cm or 12.50 cm? {12.50 cm}
  - 1 kilometer = {1,000} meters, and 1 meter = {100} centimeters.

## Main: Choose the unit and the accuracy
choice: The dose of a liquid medicine for a child
  - Liters, to the nearest liter
  * Milliliters, to the nearest tenth of a milliliter
  - Gallons, to the nearest tenth of a gallon
  - Milliliters, to the nearest 100 milliliters
choice: The length of a highway between two cities, for a road map
  - Inches, to the nearest inch
  - Feet, to the nearest tenth of a foot
  - Centimeters, to the nearest centimeter
  * Miles, to the nearest mile
choice: A runner's time in a 100-meter race
  * Seconds, to the nearest hundredth of a second
  - Minutes, to the nearest minute
  - Hours, to the nearest tenth of an hour
  - Seconds, to the nearest 10 seconds
choice: The width of a window, to order a blind that fits inside the frame
  - Feet, to the nearest foot
  - Meters, to the nearest meter
  * Inches, to the nearest eighth of an inch
  - Yards, to the nearest yard
choice: The population of a large city, for a news story
  * People, to the nearest thousand
  - People, to the nearest person
  - People, to the nearest ten million
  - People, to the nearest billion
choice: The high temperature for a weather report
  - Degrees, to the nearest thousandth of a degree
  - Degrees, to the nearest 50 degrees
  - Degrees, to the nearest 10 degrees
  * Degrees, to the nearest degree
write: Explain your choice for the medicine dose. What could go wrong with a rougher measurement?
  lines: 2
  key: A child's dose is small, often a few milliliters, so it must be measured in milliliters and closely. Rounding to the nearest 100 mL, or using liters, could give far too much or none at all.
write: Explain your choice for the city's population. Why not to the nearest person?
  lines: 2
  key: The exact count changes every day as people are born, die and move, and no one can count it exactly. The nearest thousand is accurate enough for a news story and easy to read.

## Stretch: Sensible answers
write: A room measures 3.6 m by 4.3 m. A calculator says the area is 15.48 square meters. What should you report, and why?
  lines: 2
  key: About 15.5 square meters. The lengths were measured to the nearest tenth of a meter, so the area cannot honestly be known to the hundredth.
write: A 236-mile trip at 62 miles per hour takes 3.806451613 hours, says the calculator. Give a sensible answer.
  lines: 2
  key: About 3.8 hours, or about 3 hours and 48 minutes. Speeds change on a real trip, so more digits mean nothing.

# 16. Rearrange the Formula
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can solve a formula for one of its variables, using the same steps I use to solve an equation.
directions: Solve each formula for the variable named. Undo the operations in reverse order, just as you would with numbers. Box your answer.
mistakes: Students often divide only part of a side: from P = 2l + 2w, writing w = P - 2l/2 instead of w = (P - 2l)/2. In the trapezoid formula, (b₁ + b₂) is one group: divide by the whole sum. In F = (9/5)C + 32, subtract 32 before multiplying by 5/9.
notes: Equivalent forms are correct, such as w = P/2 - l, m = y/x - b/x, or C = (F - 32) ÷ 1.8. In the warm up, the same steps solve 3x + 5 = 20 and ax + b = c. Stretch: h = 2A/(b₁ + b₂) = 120/20 = 6 units, and h = 3V/(πr²).

## Warm up: Numbers, then letters
fill:
  - Solve 3x + 5 = 20: x = {5}
  - Solve ax + b = c for x: x = {(c - b)/a}

## Main
example: Solve A = (1/2)bh for h. Multiply both sides by 2: 2A = bh. Divide both sides by b: h = 2A/b.
fill:
  - A = (1/2)(b₁ + b₂)h, the area of a trapezoid, for h: h = {2A/(b₁ + b₂)}
  - d = rt for t: t = {d/r}
  - P = 2l + 2w for w: w = {(P - 2l)/2}
  - A = (1/2)bh for b: b = {2A/h}
  - V = lwh for w: w = {V/(lh)}
  - F = (9/5)C + 32 for C: C = {(5/9)(F - 32)}
  - y = mx + b for m: m = {(y - b)/x}
  - I = Prt for r: r = {I/(Pt)}
  - C = 2πr for r: r = {C/(2π)}
  - ax + by = c for y: y = {(c - ax)/b}
draw: Show your steps | 3.4

## Stretch
write: A trapezoid has an area of 60 square units and bases of 7 and 13. Use your trapezoid formula to find its height.
  lines: 2
  key: h = 2A/(b₁ + b₂) = 2(60)/(7 + 13) = 120/20 = 6 units.
write: Solve V = (1/3)πr²h, the volume of a cone, for h.
  lines: 2
  key: Multiply by 3: 3V = πr²h. Divide by πr²: h = 3V/(πr²).

# 19. Spot the Solving Mistake
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can find and fix mistakes in solving linear equations and inequalities.
directions: Each problem was solved by a student who made one mistake. Find the first wrong step, write the correct answer, and explain what went wrong. Check your answer by substituting it.
mistakes: Students often mark the last line as wrong instead of the first wrong step. The inequality problem is the one most students miss: dividing by a negative flips the sign.
notes: Encourage substitution: putting the correct answer back into the original equation proves it. Pairs can swap explanations and grade each other's "what went wrong" sentences.

## Warm up
example: Solve 4x - 3 = 9. Add 3 to both sides: 4x = 12. Divide by 4: x = 3. Check: 4(3) - 3 = 9. It works.

## Main: Find and fix
fixit: 3(x - 2) = 12
  * 3x - 2 = 12
  - 3x = 14
  - x = 14/3
  answer: x = 6
  why: The 3 must multiply both terms: 3x - 6 = 12, so 3x = 18 and x = 6.
fixit: 2x + 5 = 17
  * 2x = 22
  - x = 11
  answer: x = 6
  why: To undo + 5, subtract 5 from both sides: 2x = 12.
fixit: x/4 - 3 = 5
  - x/4 = 8
  * x = 2
  answer: x = 32
  why: To undo dividing by 4, multiply by 4: x = 32.
fixit: 5x - 7 = 3x + 9
  - 2x - 7 = 9
  * 2x = 2
  - x = 1
  answer: x = 8
  why: To undo - 7, add 7 to both sides: 2x = 16.
fixit: -2x + 4 > 10
  - -2x > 6
  * x > -3
  answer: x < -3
  why: Dividing both sides by a negative number flips the inequality sign.
fixit: 4(x + 1) - 2x = 14
  - 4x + 4 - 2x = 14
  * 6x + 4 = 14
  - 6x = 10
  - x = 10/6
  answer: x = 5
  why: 4x - 2x is 2x, not 6x: 2x + 4 = 14, so x = 5.

## Stretch
write: Write your own problem with a hidden mistake for a partner to find. Show the correct answer on the back.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Check that the problem has exactly one first wrong step and a correct answer.

# 27. Sensible Domains
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can state a reasonable domain and range for a function that models a real situation.
directions: For each situation, write the domain (the inputs that make sense) and the range (the outputs that make sense). Then say whether the inputs are discrete (only whole numbers or separate values) or continuous (every value in an interval).
mistakes: Students often give "all real numbers" because the equation works for any x. Negative hours and half a ticket make no sense. Others mix up domain and range. For the ball, the domain ends when the ball lands (h = 0 at t = 4), and the range ends at the highest point, 64 feet at t = 2.
notes: 1) 0 ≤ h ≤ 8, 0 ≤ p ≤ 120, continuous. 2) Whole numbers 0 to 250, multiples of 8 from 0 to 2,000, discrete. 3) 0 ≤ t ≤ 8, 0 ≤ V ≤ 40, continuous. 4) 0 ≤ t ≤ 4, 0 ≤ h ≤ 64, continuous. 5) Whole numbers 1 to 200, the values 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, discrete. 6) 0 ≤ t ≤ 12.5, 0 ≤ B ≤ 100, continuous. Accept a domain for situation 1 that allows a longer shift if the student explains it; pay can be counted for parts of an hour.

## Warm up
choice: A function gives the cost C(n) of n concert tickets. Which input makes sense?
  - n = -3
  - n = 2.5
  * n = 4
  - n = 0.75
fill: In f(x), the set of x-values that make sense is the {domain}, and the set of f(x) values is the {range}.

## Main
text: 1) Pay: p = 15h for a shift of up to 8 hours. 2) Tickets: a theater with 250 seats sells tickets for $8 each, T = 8n. 3) Tank: a 40-gallon tank fills at 5 gallons per minute, V = 5t. 4) Ball: a ball is thrown up from the ground, h = -16t² + 64t feet after t seconds. 5) Buses: each bus holds 40 students, and a trip takes 1 to 200 students, b(n) = the fewest buses needed. 6) Battery: a phone battery starts at 100% and drops 8% per hour, B = 100 - 8t.
table: Situation | Domain | Range | Discrete or continuous?
  - 1. Pay | {0 ≤ h ≤ 8} | {0 ≤ p ≤ 120} | {continuous}
  - 2. Tickets | {whole numbers 0 to 250} | {multiples of 8 from 0 to 2,000} | {discrete}
  - 3. Tank | {0 ≤ t ≤ 8} | {0 ≤ V ≤ 40} | {continuous}
  - 4. Ball | {0 ≤ t ≤ 4} | {0 ≤ h ≤ 64} | {continuous}
  - 5. Buses | {whole numbers 1 to 200} | {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} | {discrete}
  - 6. Battery | {0 ≤ t ≤ 12.5} | {0 ≤ B ≤ 100} | {continuous}
  height: 0.6
draw: Work space for the ball and the battery | 1.4
choice: Why does the ball's domain stop at t = 4?
  - The ball reaches its highest point at t = 4.
  - The equation cannot be used past 4.
  - Time stops at 4 seconds.
  * The ball hits the ground at t = 4, when h = 0.

## Stretch
write: Describe a situation whose domain is the whole numbers from 0 to 12. Give a function and its range.
  lines: 3
  key: Possible answer: eggs in a carton. A carton holds 12 eggs that cost $0.25 each; C(e) = 0.25e for e = 0, 1, 2, ..., 12. The range is 0, 0.25, 0.50, ..., 3.00 dollars.

# 38. Quadratic Code Breaker
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, calculator (optional)
ican: I can solve quadratic equations by factoring, by square roots or with the quadratic formula.
directions: Solve each equation by any method. Write both solutions, smaller first, with a comma and no space (like -3,3). Find your answer in the decoder and write its letter in the numbered box. The letters spell a message about parabolas.
mistakes: Watch for sign slips when factoring: x² + 2x - 15 = (x + 5)(x - 3), so the roots are -5 and 3, not 5 and -3 (a decoy). In 2x² - 8x = 0, students often divide by x and lose the root 0. In (x - 7)² = 4, both +2 and -2 count. x² - 6x + 9 = 0 has one repeated root, 3. x² - 2x - 1 = 0 does not factor: use the quadratic formula.
notes: The message is MIRROR AXIS: the axis of symmetry is a mirror line for every parabola. Methods for items 3 to 12: 3) (x - 2)(x - 3). 4) x² = 9. 5) (x + 5)(x - 3). 6) 2x(x - 4). 7) (x - 3)². 8) (x - 4)(x - 6). 9) (x - 5)(x + 1). 10) x - 7 = ±2. 11) (2x - 1)(x + 2). 12) x = (2 ± √8)/2 = 1 ± √2. The decoys are common wrong answers.

## Warm up
fill:
  - If (x - 4)(x + 1) = 0, then x = {4} or x = {-1}.
  - The quadratic formula: x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / {2a}

## Main: Solve and decode
code: Solve and decode.
  - x² - 5x + 6 = 0, so x | 2,3 | M
  - x² - 9 = 0, so x | -3,3 | I
  - x² + 2x - 15 = 0, so x | -5,3 | R
  - 2x² - 8x = 0, so x | 0,4 | R
  - x² - 6x + 9 = 0, so x | 3 | O
  - x² - 10x + 24 = 0, so x | 4,6 | R
  - x² - 4x - 5 = 0, so x | -1,5 | A
  - (x - 7)² = 4, so x | 5,9 | X
  - 2x² + 3x - 2 = 0, so x | -2,1/2 | I
  - x² - 2x - 1 = 0, so x | 1-√2,1+√2 | S
  decoys: -3,-2 -3,5 4 3,11 -4,-6 -1/2,2 9
draw: Work space | 3

## Stretch
write: What do the solutions of x² - 5x + 6 = 0 tell you about the graph of y = x² - 5x + 6? Where is its axis of symmetry?
  lines: 3
  key: The parabola crosses the x-axis at x = 2 and x = 3. The axis of symmetry is halfway between the roots, at x = 2.5.
write: The equation x² - 6x + 9 = 0 has only one solution. What does that look like on the graph of y = x² - 6x + 9?
  lines: 2
  key: The parabola touches the x-axis at one point, its vertex (3, 0), and does not cross it.
