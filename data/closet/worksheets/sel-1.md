# 1. New Feeling Words: Proud, Worried, Excited, Frustrated
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can name the feelings proud, worried, excited and frustrated.
directions: (Read aloud.) Trace the four new feeling words. Then write the word that matches each face. Last, draw a character who feels one of the words.
mistakes: Frustrated and worried get mixed up. Frustrated is "I keep trying and it is not working" (tight teeth, eyebrows down); worried is "something might go wrong" (eyebrows up in the middle, a wobbly mouth). Proud and happy are close: proud is a good feeling about something you did, so look for the medal.
notes: Act out each word before the sheet: a proud stand (chin up), a worried face, excited jumping, a frustrated growl. Read the word bank aloud and point to each word. The scene is about a character, so nobody needs to share a time they felt this way. Accept any scene that fits the word circled.

## Warm up: Trace the words
trace: proud worried excited frustrated

## Main: Match the word to the face
text: (Read aloud.) Word bank: **proud**, **worried**, **excited**, **frustrated**. Write the word that matches each face.
picword:
  - face-worried | {worried}
  - face-proud | {proud}
  - face-frustrated | {frustrated}
  - face-excited | {excited}

## Stretch: Draw a scene
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw a character who feels proud, worried, excited or frustrated. Show what is happening. | 2.4
write: (Read aloud.) Write the feeling word your character feels.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary. The word should fit the scene: for example, a child holding a finished block tower who feels proud, or a child whose kite keeps falling who feels frustrated.

# 7. Two Feelings at Once
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can name two feelings a character has at the same time.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each story with me. The character feels two feelings at the same time. Circle the two faces that match.
mistakes: Children often circle one feeling and stop, or circle the feeling that came last. Reread the story and ask "What else does Pip feel?" Some children circle happy for Fox and also proud; point back to the words "She will miss them" to find the second feeling.
notes: The idea of the sheet: one moment can hold two feelings, like excited and scared before something new. Read each story twice and point to the clue words. All the stories are about animal characters, so nobody needs to share their own feelings. Answers: 1 excited and scared; 2 happy and sad; 3 proud and worried.

## Warm up
example: Kit gets a new puppy. She laughs and pets it. But it is very jumpy, and she is not sure what to do. Kit feels **happy** and **nervous** at the same time.

## Main: Circle two faces
text: **1.** Pip the penguin has his first swim lesson. He jumps up and down. He cannot wait to swim! But the pool looks so deep. His legs shake.
picword:
  - face-mad | mad
  - face-excited | excited
  - face-proud | proud
  - face-scared | scared
text: **2.** It is Fox's last day of camp. She laughs at the fun camp show. Then she hugs her camp friends goodbye. She will miss them.
picword:
  - face-sad | sad
  - face-scared | scared
  - face-happy | happy
  - face-frustrated | frustrated
text: **3.** Bear is the line leader today! He stands up tall and smiles. But he is not sure he knows the way to the library. His tummy feels funny.
picword:
  - face-worried | worried
  - face-mad | mad
  - face-sad | sad
  - face-proud | proud
note: 1. excited and scared. 2. happy and sad. 3. proud and worried.

## Stretch
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw Pip at the pool. Show both of his feelings on his face and body. | 1.8
write: (Read aloud.) Write the two feelings Pip has.
  lines: 1
  key: excited and scared (also accept excited and nervous or worried)

# 20. Rocket the Frustrated Puppy
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can name calm tools that help when I feel frustrated.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the story with me. Circle the 3 calm tools Rocket tries. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Children may circle "jump around" or "bang a drum" because they are fun, or circle the ball because it is in the story. Ask "Did Rocket do that to feel calm?" Some children think getting Mia is a calm tool; Rocket asked for help after he was calm, which is a good next step, but it is not one of the three things he did to calm down.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class. Read it twice: once for fun, once to find the tools, with children holding up a finger for each one. Calm tools in the story: slow breaths, counting to five, hugging a soft toy. Talk about why Rocket calmed down first and then asked for help: a calm body can think of a plan.

## Read: Rocket and the red ball
passage: Rocket and the Red Ball
  Rocket the puppy had a red ball. The ball rolled under the bed. Rocket pushed his nose under. He could not reach it! Rocket felt frustrated. He wanted to bark and bark.
  Instead, Rocket took three slow breaths. He counted to five. He hugged his soft bear. Now Rocket felt calm. He ran to get Mia. Mia got the ball out for him.

## Main: Circle 3 calm tools
picword:
  - flower | slow breaths
  - drum | bang a drum
  - hand | count to five
  - microphone | yell loud
  - teddy-bear | hug a toy
  - jump | jump around
note: Circle: slow breaths (flower), count to five (hand), hug a toy (teddy bear).

## Questions
choice: (Read aloud.) How did Rocket feel when he could not reach the ball?
  - sleepy
  * frustrated
  - proud
choice: (Read aloud.) What did Rocket want to do at first?
  * bark and bark
  - take a nap
  - eat his food
choice: (Read aloud.) How did Rocket feel after he used his calm tools?
  - mad
  - scared
  * calm

## Stretch
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw Rocket using one of his calm tools. | 1.6

# 21. Plan a Class Calm Corner
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can plan a calm corner with things that help us calm down.
pairs: calm-corner
directions: (Read aloud.) Draw four things a class calm corner needs, one in each box. Then choose one rule from the word bank and copy it on the lines.
mistakes: Some children draw toys for playing (a race car, a ball to throw). Ask "Would this help a friend slow down and feel calm, or would it make them wind up?" Children may copy a rule with missing words; have them point to each word as they copy.
notes: Use after the Calm Corner tool. Before drawing, list ideas together: a pillow or beanbag, a soft toy, books, a sand timer, a feelings chart, paper and crayons, a breathing star or a squeeze ball. Rule choice is open; check that the rule is copied fully and makes sense.

## Warm up: Calm or not?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one belongs in a calm corner?
  - a loud drum
  * a soft pillow
  - a bouncy ball to throw
choice: (Read aloud.) What can you do in a calm corner?
  - Run and jump
  - Shout to a friend
  * Take slow breaths

## Main: Draw four things
draw: (Read aloud.) 1. Something soft | 1.5
draw: (Read aloud.) 2. Something to look at or read | 1.5
draw: (Read aloud.) 3. Something to help you breathe or wait | 1.5
draw: (Read aloud.) 4. Your own idea | 1.5

## Rule: Copy one rule
text: (Read aloud.) Rule bank: **One friend at a time.** **Use a quiet voice.** **Put things back.** **Go back when you feel calm.**
write: (Read aloud.) Copy one rule for the calm corner.
  lines: 2
  key: Any one rule copied fully, for example: Use a quiet voice.

# 33. Owl's Sorry Steps
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can say sorry in three steps: tell what happened, say sorry, and offer a fix.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story about Owl. Fill the three boxes for Owl. Draw a picture and write a few words in each box. Use the word bank.
mistakes: Children often skip the first step and just say "Sorry!" Point out that saying what happened shows Owl knows what went wrong. In the fix box, some children draw Owl giving a toy; a fix should match the problem, so Owl helps pick up the nuts.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class. Read it twice. Model the steps with a puppet or a quick role play: "I knocked over your blocks. I am sorry. Can I help build it again?" Spelling does not matter here; a picture with one or two words is a full answer.

## Read: Owl in a hurry
passage: Owl in a Hurry
  Owl was late for Moon Class. She flew fast through the trees. Swoosh! Her wing hit Squirrel's big pile of nuts. The nuts rolled all over the ground. Squirrel looked at the mess. "I worked all day on that pile," he said.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is a sorry?
  - "It was not my fault."
  * "I am sorry I knocked over your nuts."
  - "Nuts are silly."
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is a good fix?
  - Owl flies away fast.
  - Owl gives Squirrel a feather.
  * Owl helps pick up the nuts.

## Main: Owl's three steps
text: Word bank: **knocked**, **nuts**, **sorry**, **help**, **pick up**
chart: 1. What happened | 2. Say sorry | 3. Offer a fix
  rows: 1
  height: 2.6
  key: 1. A picture of Owl's wing hitting the nuts, with words like "I knocked over your nuts." 2. Owl saying "I am sorry." 3. Owl and Squirrel picking up nuts, with words like "Can I help pick up the nuts?"

## Stretch
write: (Read aloud.) What could Owl do next time so this does not happen again?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary: fly slower near the nuts, leave home earlier so she is not in a hurry, or look where she is flying.
