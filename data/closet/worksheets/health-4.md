# 1. The 5-20 Label Rule
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can use the 5-20 rule to tell if a food is low or high in a nutrient.
directions: Read the rule. Look at the percent Daily Value for each nutrient on the two pretend labels. Write low or high. Then answer the questions.
pairs: label-detective
mistakes: Students often read the grams instead of the percent. The 5-20 rule only works with the % Daily Value column. Watch for students who think "high" always means bad: high fiber and high calcium are good things to get more of.
notes: The 5-20 rule is from the FDA: 5% Daily Value or less of a nutrient per serving is low, 20% or more is high. The FDA suggests getting more of dietary fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron and potassium, and less of saturated fat, sodium and added sugars. The pretend labels use real Daily Values (sodium 2,300 mg, saturated fat 20 g, fiber 28 g, added sugars 50 g, calcium 1,300 mg). Keep the talk about the foods, not about any child's own eating.

## Warm up: The rule
example: The 5-20 rule: **5% DV or less is low. 20% DV or more is high.** A label says Iron 25%. 25 is more than 20, so this food is **high** in iron.

## Main: Pretend Bean Soup (1 cup)
table: Nutrient | % Daily Value | Low or high?
  - Saturated fat | 5% | {low}
  - Sodium | 30% | {high}
  - Dietary fiber | 21% | {high}
  - Added sugars | 2% | {low}

## Main: Pretend Fruit Yogurt (1 cup)
table: Nutrient | % Daily Value | Low or high?
  - Calcium | 20% | {high}
  - Added sugars | 24% | {high}
  - Sodium | 3% | {low}
  - Dietary fiber | 0% | {low}

## Use the rule
fill:
  - Which food is high in calcium? {the yogurt}
  - Which food is high in fiber? {the bean soup}
  - Name a nutrient that is high in the soup that most people should get less of: {sodium}
  - Name a nutrient that is high in the yogurt that most people should get less of: {added sugars}
choice: A granola bar has 12% DV of fiber. What does the 5-20 rule say?
  - It is low in fiber
  - It is high in fiber
  * It is in between, not low and not high

## Stretch
write: Maya wants a snack with less added sugar than the fruit yogurt. What should she look for on the label of another yogurt? Use the 5-20 rule.
  lines: 3
  key: She should look at the Added Sugars line and pick a yogurt with a lower % DV, ideally 5% or less (low), such as plain yogurt, while still checking that calcium is high.

# 5. Cereal Comparison Chart
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can compare two food labels and recommend a choice with reasons.
directions: Read the two pretend cereal labels. Fill in the comparison chart with each cereal's numbers and decide which is the better choice for each feature. Then write a recommendation with two reasons.
pairs: label-detective
mistakes: Students decide one cereal "wins" every row. Choco Puffs has less sodium than Bran Flakes, so a fair comparison notes that, even if Bran Flakes is the better choice overall. Watch for "more is better" on every line: more fiber and iron are better, but less added sugar and sodium are better.
notes: Both labels are pretend, written for Curio Class, and use real Daily Values: added sugars 50 g, dietary fiber 28 g, sodium 2,300 mg, iron 18 mg. Bran Flakes: 6 g added sugars (12%), 7 g fiber (25%), 210 mg sodium (9%), 8.1 mg iron (45%). Choco Puffs: 15 g added sugars (30%), 1 g fiber (4%), 160 mg sodium (7%), 4.5 mg iron (25%). The FDA suggests getting more fiber and iron and less added sugar and sodium. Ingredients are listed from most to least by weight. Keep the talk on the cereals, not on what any child eats at home.

## The pretend labels (1 cup)
table: Label line | Pretend Bran Flakes | Pretend Choco Puffs
  - Calories | 150 | 150
  - Added sugars | 6 g, 12% DV | 15 g, 30% DV
  - Dietary fiber | 7 g, 25% DV | 1 g, 4% DV
  - Sodium | 210 mg, 9% DV | 160 mg, 7% DV
  - Iron | 8.1 mg, 45% DV | 4.5 mg, 25% DV
  - Ingredients | whole grain wheat, wheat bran, sugar, salt | sugar, corn flour, cocoa, salt

## Warm up
fill:
  - Both cereals have {150} calories, so calories do not help you choose.
  - Choco Puffs has {9} more grams of added sugars than Bran Flakes.
  - The first ingredient in Choco Puffs is {sugar}, which means there is more sugar than any other ingredient.
choice: By the 5-20 rule, Bran Flakes' fiber (25% DV) is
  - low
  - in between
  * high

## Main: Comparison chart
table: Feature | Bran Flakes | Choco Puffs | Better choice
  - Added sugars (% DV) | {12%} | {30%} | {Bran Flakes}
  - Dietary fiber (% DV) | {25%} | {4%} | {Bran Flakes}
  - Sodium (% DV) | {9%} | {7%} | {Choco Puffs}
  - Iron (% DV) | {45%} | {25%} | {Bran Flakes}
  - First ingredient | {whole grain wheat} | {sugar} | {Bran Flakes}
write: Write a recommendation for a classmate choosing between the two cereals. Give two reasons from the chart.
  lines: 5
  starter: I recommend ... because ...
  key: I recommend Pretend Bran Flakes because it has much less added sugar (12% DV compared with 30%) and much more fiber (25% DV, which is high, compared with 4%, which is low). It also has more iron, and its first ingredient is whole grain wheat instead of sugar.

## Stretch
write: Choco Puffs wins one row. Is that enough to change your recommendation? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: No. Choco Puffs has a little less sodium (7% compared with 9%, a difference of only 2%, and both are in between, close to low), but Bran Flakes is much better on added sugar, fiber, iron and its first ingredient, so it is still the better choice overall.

# 15. How Vaccines Train the Body
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can explain how vaccines help the immune system fight germs.
directions: Read the passage. Highlight the sentence that tells what antibodies do. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students often think a vaccine is a medicine that kills germs already in the body. A vaccine works before the germ arrives: it trains the immune system ahead of time. Some think a sore arm means the vaccine made them sick; mild soreness is a sign the immune system is practicing.
notes: The passage follows CDC explanations of how vaccines work. Kinds of vaccines include weakened or inactivated germs, pieces of a germ (such as one protein), and mRNA vaccines, which give cells instructions to make a harmless protein. Edward Jenner tested the first vaccine, against smallpox, in 1796; the World Health Organization declared smallpox eradicated in 1980. Keep the lesson on how the body works: do not ask about any student's or family's vaccination records or choices.

## Read
passage: How Vaccines Train the Body
  Your body has a defense team called the immune system. It is made of special cells and organs that fight germs, such as viruses and bacteria. When a new germ gets inside, the immune system has to figure out how to beat it. That can take days, and while it works, the germ can make a person sick.
  The immune system has a memory, though. After it beats a germ, some of its cells remember what that germ looks like. If the same germ comes back, the body recognizes it right away and quickly makes antibodies. Antibodies are proteins that stick to a germ and help the body destroy it.
  A vaccine uses this memory. It shows the immune system a harmless copy of a germ, a weakened or killed germ, or just a piece of one. The immune system practices on it and builds memory cells, without the person getting the serious illness. Later, if the real germ shows up, the body is ready. Some vaccines need more than one dose to make the memory strong.
  Vaccines also protect people nearby. When most people in a community are protected, a germ has a hard time spreading. That helps babies and people who are too sick to get some vaccines.

## Questions
choice: What is the main job of a vaccine?
  - To kill germs that are already making someone sick
  * To train the immune system to recognize a germ before it causes illness
  - To replace the immune system
choice: What do antibodies do?
  - They carry oxygen through the blood.
  - They make a germ stronger.
  * They stick to a germ and help the body destroy it.
choice: Why does a body fight a germ faster the second time?
  * Memory cells recognize the germ right away.
  - The germ gets weaker every time.
  - The body grows new organs.
write: Explain how a vaccine is like a fire drill. Use two details from the passage.
  lines: 3
  key: In a fire drill, people practice without a real fire so they know what to do if one happens. A vaccine lets the immune system practice on a harmless copy or piece of a germ and build memory cells, so it is ready to make antibodies fast if the real germ shows up.
write: How do vaccines help people who cannot get some vaccines themselves?
  lines: 3
  key: When most people in a community are protected, the germ has a hard time spreading, so it is less likely to reach babies or people who are too sick to get some vaccines.

## Stretch
write: A friend says, "My arm was sore after a shot, so the vaccine made me sick." What would you tell your friend?
  lines: 3
  key: A sore arm is a common, mild side effect that usually goes away in a day or two. It is a sign the immune system is practicing, not the serious illness the vaccine protects against.

# 21. Changes Inside and Out
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can name ways people change as they grow up.
directions: Read about Jada. Cut out the change cards and glue each one under body changes, feeling changes or friendship changes. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: "Wanting more privacy" and "feelings changing quickly" are feeling changes, even though they show up at home. "Caring more what friends think" is a friendship change. If a student makes a good case for a different column, accept it with the reason.
notes: Jada is a story character; keep the talk on her, never on any student's own body. Puberty usually starts somewhere between about ages 8 and 14, and every body follows its own schedule. Sweating more and oilier skin come from hormones; daily washing and deodorant help. If your district teaches puberty in separate groups or with a family notice, follow that plan. Leave room for anonymous questions in a question box.

## Warm up: Meet Jada
text: Jada is in fifth grade. This year she grew so fast that her sneakers got tight twice! She also noticed that her feelings change quickly, and her friend group is changing too.

## Main: Sort the changes
sort: Glue each card under its kind of change.
  - Body changes: Growing taller fast, Sweating more, Skin getting oilier
  - Feeling changes: Feelings changing quickly, Wanting more privacy, Feeling embarrassed more easily
  - Friendship changes: Friend groups changing, Caring more what friends think

## Think about it
choice: Why did Jada's sneakers get tight twice this year?
  - She wore them every day
  * Her body is growing, and her feet grew too
  - Sneakers always shrink
choice: Jada's friend started growing two years before Jada. What does this show?
  - Something is wrong with Jada
  * Every body changes on its own schedule
  - Jada will not grow
write: Jada feels embarrassed and her feelings change quickly. Name one trusted adult she could talk to and one healthy thing she could do.
  lines: 3
  key: A parent or caregiver, school nurse, counselor or teacher. Healthy things: talk about it, write in a journal, get enough sleep, take deep breaths, be active.

## Stretch
write: Her friend group is changing. Write one kind thing Jada could say to a new friend or an old one.
  lines: 2
  key: "Want to sit with us at lunch?" or "I miss hanging out. Want to play after school?" Any kind, inviting words.

# 26. Report It Right
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can report bullying to a trusted adult with clear facts: who, what, where, when and how often.
directions: Read each story. Fill in a report form for it with the facts an adult needs. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students write opinions ("He is a bully and he is mean") instead of facts. A report says what happened, not what kind of person someone is. Watch the "how often" box: it is the fact that shows a pattern, and students often leave it out. In story 2, the reporter is a witness, not the target; both can report.
notes: Bullying is unwanted, aggressive behavior that is repeated or likely to be repeated, often with an imbalance of power (StopBullying.gov). Reporting is telling an adult to get help or keep someone safe; it is not tattling. Characters are invented. Make sure students know your school's real reporting options (a teacher, counselor, principal, or an anonymous report box or form). If a student writes about something real, follow your school's procedure.

## Warm up
choice: Which of these is a fact for a report, not an opinion?
  - Rocco is the meanest kid in the school.
  * Rocco took Lena's jump rope at morning recess on Monday.
  - Everyone hates Rocco.
choice: Why does "how often" matter in a bullying report?
  - It tells the adult which student is more popular.
  - It makes the report longer.
  * It shows whether the behavior is a pattern that keeps happening.

## Story 1
text: Lena plays jump rope by the swings at morning recess. On Monday, Wednesday and Friday this week, a fifth grader named Rocco grabbed her jump rope, threw it on the roof of the shed, and said, "Little kids can't play here." Lena told her friend she does not want to go outside anymore.
table: Report form | Facts
  - Who was involved? | {Rocco, a fifth grader, took the rope; Lena was the target.}
  - What happened? | {He grabbed her jump rope, threw it on the shed roof and told her little kids can't play there.}
  - Where? | {On the playground, by the swings}
  - When? | {Morning recess on Monday, Wednesday and Friday this week}
  - How often? | {3 times in one week}

## Story 2
text: Sam rides bus 12 home every afternoon. For the past two weeks, Malik has watched a student named Drew kick the back of Sam's seat and call him "baby" during the ride, every day. Sam sits very still and does not say anything. Malik decides to report it.
table: Report form | Facts
  - Who was involved? | {Drew did the kicking and name-calling; Sam was the target; Malik saw it.}
  - What happened? | {Drew kicked the back of Sam's seat and called him "baby."}
  - Where? | {On bus 12}
  - When? | {On the ride home in the afternoon, for the past two weeks}
  - How often? | {Every day for two weeks}

## Think about it
write: Name two adults Malik could report to.
  lines: 2
  key: The bus driver, his teacher, the school counselor, the principal or assistant principal, or a parent or caregiver who can contact the school. Any two trusted adults.
write: Malik is worried Drew will find out he told. What could he do?
  lines: 3
  key: He can report privately or ask the adult to keep his name private, use an anonymous report box or form if the school has one, and tell an adult right away if Drew bothers him for reporting.

# 36. Signal Word Labels
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can read signal words on product labels and tell which products are most dangerous.
directions: Learn the three signal words. Read the 5 pretend product labels. Circle the signal word on each label, write it in the chart, and give each product its risk level. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students think "caution" means safe. Every product with a signal word can hurt someone; caution is the lowest level, not "no risk." Watch for students who rank by how scary the product name sounds instead of by the signal word printed on the label.
notes: Signal words come from US labeling rules. On pesticides (such as bug sprays and many disinfectants), the EPA requires CAUTION for the lowest hazard, WARNING for moderate, and DANGER for the highest; DANGER with POISON and a skull and crossbones marks products that are highly toxic if swallowed, breathed or touched. Household chemicals fall under the Federal Hazardous Substances Act, which uses DANGER for the most serious hazards (such as corrosive or extremely flammable products) and CAUTION or WARNING for others. All five labels are pretend, written for Curio Class. The Poison Help line in the United States is 1-800-222-1222.

## Warm up: The three signal words
example: **CAUTION**: can cause mild harm. Level 3 (lowest).
  **WARNING**: can cause more serious harm. Level 2.
  **DANGER**: can cause severe harm, such as burns, blindness or poisoning. Level 1 (highest).
  Every product with a signal word must be kept out of reach of children.

## Main: Read the labels
table: Pretend product and label | Signal word | Risk level (1 highest, 3 lowest)
  - A. Pretend Glass Spray: "CAUTION. May irritate eyes. Keep out of reach of children." | {CAUTION} | {3}
  - B. Pretend Drain Opener: "Keep out of reach of children. DANGER. Corrosive. Causes severe burns to skin and eyes." | {DANGER} | {1}
  - C. Pretend Bug Spray: "WARNING. Causes moderate eye irritation. Harmful if swallowed. Keep out of reach of children." | {WARNING} | {2}
  - D. Pretend Hand Dish Soap: "Keep out of reach of children. CAUTION. Eye irritant." | {CAUTION} | {3}
  - E. Pretend Oven Cleaner: "DANGER. Contains lye. Causes severe burns. Keep out of reach of children." | {DANGER} | {1}
fill:
  - The two most dangerous products are {B} and {E}.
  - The two least dangerous products are {A} and {D}.

## Use it
choice: You find product B open under the sink. What should you do?
  - Put the cap back on yourself and keep playing
  - Smell it to find out what it is
  * Leave it alone, keep younger kids away, and tell a grown-up right away
choice: Why should products always stay in their original containers?
  - Original containers keep products from expiring.
  * The label with the signal word and first aid steps stays with the product.
  - Products work better in their own bottles.

## Stretch
write: A friend says, "The glass spray only says caution, so it is safe to play with." What would you say?
  lines: 3
  key: Caution is the lowest signal word, but it still means the product can hurt you, for example by irritating your eyes. Any product with a signal word should be kept away from children and used only by an adult as the label says.
