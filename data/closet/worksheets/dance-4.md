# 1. ABA Dance Form
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, open floor space
ican: I can plan a dance in ABA form with contrasting sections.
directions: Answer the warm up questions. Then plan your own ABA dance: describe the moves and movement qualities of A and B, and decide what returns at the end and what you might change.
mistakes: Students often make B just "different moves" with the same feel as A. B should contrast in quality (smooth instead of sharp, heavy instead of light, slow instead of fast). Watch for a final A that is a new phrase: the point of ABA is that the audience recognizes A coming back.
notes: ABA is the same form as a sandwich: the same bread on both sides. Music in ABA (ternary) form works well for a demonstration. The final A may repeat exactly or return with a small change (a new ending pose, a different facing); either is still ABA as long as A is clearly recognizable.

## Warm up: What is ABA?
example: **A** is a phrase. **B** is a new phrase that feels different. Then **A** comes back. A sharp, fast A, a smooth, slow B, then the sharp, fast A again.
choice: In ABA form, what happens at the end?
  - A new section C begins
  * The A section returns
  - The B section repeats
choice: Which B would contrast most with an A of sharp, quick punches and jumps?
  - faster punches and jumps
  * slow, smooth arm waves at a low level
  - the same punches facing a new way
fill:
  - The opposite of a sharp quality is {smooth}.
  - The opposite of a heavy quality is {light}.
  - If A lasts 16 counts and B lasts 16 counts, the whole ABA dance lasts {48} counts.

## Main: Plan your ABA dance
table: Section | Counts | Moves | Movement quality (2 words)
  - A | | |
  - B | | |
  - A again | | |
write: How does your B section contrast with your A section?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names a quality for each, for example "A is sharp and strong with quick kicks; B is smooth and light with slow turns and reaches."
write: What returns at the end? Will the last A be exactly the same, or will you change one small thing?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. The A phrase must come back recognizably; a small change (a new ending pose, a different facing, a slower final count) is allowed and should be named.

## Stretch
write: A friend says, "ABA is boring because A happens twice." How would you answer?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer explains that the return feels satisfying because the audience recognizes it, and that B makes A look fresh when it comes back; songs and stories often use the same pattern.

# 7. Hula Kahiko and Hula Auana
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare two styles of hula by their instruments, costumes and movement.
directions: Read the passage. Answer the questions. Then fill in the T-chart to compare hula kahiko and hula ʻauana.
mistakes: Students mix up the instruments: the ipu and pahu are drums for kahiko; the ʻukulele and guitar are for ʻauana. Some think kahiko is no longer danced; both styles are danced today. Students may also say one style is "better"; the chart compares, it does not rank.
notes: The passage is written for Curio Class. The ʻokina (ʻ) in Hawaiʻi and ʻauana is a letter in Hawaiian; say it as a short break, like the middle of "uh-oh." Hula is a living tradition with deep cultural and spiritual meaning; treat it as an art form, not a costume or a joke, and do not have students dress up in grass skirts. If you can, show a video of a hula hālau (school) dancing both styles, such as from the Merrie Monarch Festival.

## Warm up: Read
passage: Two Kinds of Hula
  Hula is the dance of Hawaiʻi. Dancers use their hands, hips and feet to tell stories, and many hand movements stand for something, like rain, a flower or the ocean. There are two main kinds of hula.
  Hula kahiko is the older style. It was danced long before Europeans came to Hawaiʻi. Dancers move to chanting and to traditional instruments, such as the ipu, a drum made from a gourd, and the pahu, a drum covered with sharkskin. Costumes are made from plants, like skirts of ti leaves, with lei of flowers and leaves. The movements are often strong and grounded, with bent knees.
  Hula ʻauana is the modern style. It grew in the late 1800s and the 1900s, after new instruments came to the islands. Dancers move to songs played on the ʻukulele, guitar and steel guitar. Costumes are often long flowing dresses or aloha shirts. The movements are softer and smoother. The word ʻauana means "to wander" or "to drift."
  In the 1800s, missionaries discouraged hula. King Kalākaua brought it back to his court, and today both styles are danced at festivals all over Hawaiʻi.
choice: Which instrument goes with hula kahiko?
  - the guitar
  * the ipu, a gourd drum
  - the steel guitar
choice: Which word best describes the movement of hula ʻauana?
  - stiff
  - grounded
  * flowing
choice: Which sentence is true?
  * Both styles of hula are danced today.
  - Only hula ʻauana is danced today.
  - Hula kahiko was invented in the 1900s.

## Main: T-chart
chart: Hula kahiko (ancient) | Hula ʻauana (modern)
  labels: Instruments; Costume; Movement
  height: 0.9
  key: Instruments: kahiko uses chanting with the ipu (gourd drum) and pahu (sharkskin drum); ʻauana uses songs with the ʻukulele, guitar and steel guitar. Costume: kahiko uses plant materials like ti-leaf skirts and lei of flowers and leaves; ʻauana uses flowing dresses or aloha shirts. Movement: kahiko is strong and grounded with bent knees; ʻauana is softer, smoother and flowing.
write: What is one thing both styles of hula share?
  lines: 2
  key: Both use hands, hips and feet to tell stories, and hand movements stand for things like rain, flowers or the ocean. Both are danced today.

## Stretch
write: Why do you think dancers today still learn hula kahiko, even though newer styles exist?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer uses the passage: kahiko keeps the old chants, instruments and stories alive, so dancers learn their history and culture by dancing it.

# 20. Pathway and Level Map
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, colored pencil, open floor space
ican: I can map my phrase's floor pathway and the level of each move.
directions: Label each move with its level: H for high, M for middle, L for low. Then map your own phrase on the stage grid: put a numbered dot at each move, connect the dots with arrows, and write H, M or L next to each dot. List the moves in the table.
mistakes: Students mark the level of the whole phrase instead of each point. A pathway can stay the same while the level changes, so look at each dot on its own. Students also forget that the audience sits at the bottom of the grid.
notes: Levels: high is on tiptoes, jumping or reaching up; middle is standing or walking; low is kneeling, sitting, crawling or rolling. Have students walk their pathway first, calling out each level, while a partner checks. Grid squares help students place dots, but exact placement is not graded.

## Warm up: Name the level
fill:
  - Rolling on the floor: {L}
  - Walking with arms at your sides: {M}
  - Jumping with arms up: {H}
  - Kneeling and reaching forward: {L}
  - Rising onto your toes and stretching up: {H}
  - Stepping side to side: {M}

## Main: My stage map

  minor: off

  - 1 | | |
  - 2 | | |
  - 3 | | |
  - 4 | | |
  - 5 | | |
  - 6 | | |
write: Where does your phrase change level the most? Why did you put the change there?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the dots, for example "From dot 3 to dot 4 I go from a high jump to a low roll, because the falling leaf in my idea drops to the ground there."

## Stretch
write: Look at your map. Does your phrase use all three levels? If not, which move could you change to add the missing level?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary, for example "I have no middle level, so I could change the crawl at dot 5 to a walk."

# 21. Five-Minute Notation
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, timer, a partner, open floor space
ican: I can write down a dance phrase with symbols so someone else can learn it.
directions: Read the sample notation and answer the questions. Then make a symbol key and notate your own idea phrase count by count. Trade with a partner: can they learn it from your paper in 5 minutes?
mistakes: Students often draw a symbol without saying which count it starts on, or invent a new symbol halfway through without adding it to the key. Every symbol on the page must be in the key, and every count needs a box, even if it says "hold."
notes: This is a classroom notation, not Labanotation; the goal is clarity, not a formal system. During the partner test the writer must stay silent and only point to the paper, so the notation has to do the teaching. Time it: 5 minutes, then the partner performs and the writer marks what matched.

## Warm up: Read some notation
example: Key: **→** step right, **←** step left, **↑** jump, **○** turn around, **▼** sink to a low level, **–** hold.

  - On counts 3 and 4 the dancer {steps left}.

  - The dancer turns around on count {7}.
  - The phrase ends at a {low} level.

# 33. Program Note Frame
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can write a program note that tells an audience a dance's title, idea and what to watch for.
directions: Read the model program note and answer the questions about it. Then write a 3-sentence program note for a class dance: its title, its idea and what to watch for.
mistakes: Students retell every move in order. A program note is short: it names the idea and points to one or two things to notice, not the whole dance. Others write "watch for the dancing"; push for something specific, like a canon or a level change.
notes: A program note is the short paragraph printed in a program that helps the audience understand a dance before it starts. Use a dance your class made or is making. Read the best notes aloud before a class performance.

## Warm up: A model note
example: **Leaf Fall.** This dance shows a leaf's journey from a tall tree to the ground in autumn. Watch for the moment the dancers drop from high to low level, one after another, like leaves in the wind.
choice: Which part of the model gives the title?
  * Leaf Fall
  - This dance shows a leaf's journey
  - Watch for the moment the dancers drop
choice: Which sentence tells what to watch for?
  - the first sentence
  - the second sentence
  * the third sentence
choice: What is the idea of Leaf Fall?
  - how to rake leaves
  * a leaf's journey from a tree to the ground
  - a race between dancers

## Main: My program note
write: Sentence 1: the title.
  lines: 1
  starter: Our dance is called ...
  key: Answers vary, for example "Our dance is called City Morning."
write: Sentence 2: the idea.
  lines: 2
  starter: This dance shows ...
  key: Answers vary, for example "This dance shows a busy city waking up, from quiet streets to rush hour."
write: Sentence 3: what to watch for.
  lines: 2
  starter: Watch for ...
  key: Answers vary; it should name something specific, for example "Watch for the dancers crossing in zigzag pathways like people rushing to work."

## Stretch
write: Rewrite your note as one smooth paragraph, with a word or two that makes the audience curious.
  lines: 4
  key: Answers vary, for example "City Morning. Before the sun is up, the streets are still. This dance shows a city waking, from one sleepy walker to a rush of zigzag pathways. Watch for the moment everyone freezes at a pretend red light."
