# 1. Policy Debate Brief
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can build a policy debate brief with a plan, harms, solvency and rebuttals, using evidence from a case file.
directions: Read the case file on a fare-free bus proposal. Then build a brief for the affirmative side: state the plan, name the harms of the current system, explain solvency (how the plan fixes them), and prepare rebuttals to the arguments the negative side is likely to make.
mistakes: Students often list harms that the plan cannot fix, or claim solvency with no numbers. Each harm should match a solvency point. Watch for rebuttals that only repeat the affirmative case; a good rebuttal answers the specific objection, ideally with evidence. Students who spot the $1.5 million funding gap are reading closely: the strongest briefs admit it and answer it.
notes: Lakeport and all of its numbers are fictional, built for this sheet. The background line is real: Kansas City, Missouri, voted in 2019 to make its city buses fare-free, and Tallinn, Estonia, has offered free public transit to registered residents since 2013. Funding math: lost fares $6 million a year; savings from no fare collection $1.5 million; new parking revenue $3 million; gap $1.5 million. Debate vocabulary: harms (problems now), inherency (why the problem continues), solvency (the plan works), disadvantage (a new problem the plan causes).

## Case file
passage: Case File: Fare-Free Buses in Lakeport
  **Resolution:** The City of Lakeport should eliminate fares on its public buses.
  **Current system:** A bus ride in Lakeport costs $2.00. Fares bring in about $6 million a year, which covers about 15 percent of the bus system's $40 million operating cost. Collecting fares (fareboxes, fare cards, cash handling and staff) costs about $1.5 million a year.
  **Ridership:** Rides fell from 9 million a year to 7 million over the last five years. In a city survey of 1,200 riders, 38 percent said cost keeps them from riding as often as they would like, and 44 percent said buses come too rarely.
  **Proposal:** A two-year fare-free pilot. To replace lost fares, the city would raise downtown parking meter rates by $0.50 an hour, which city staff estimate would bring in $3 million a year. Without fareboxes, boarding through all doors could speed up each trip.
  **Concerns raised at a public hearing:** Riders worry that buses will become crowded. A council member asked how the city will pay for the rest of the lost fare money. A downtown shop owners' group says higher parking rates will keep customers away.
  **Background:** Kansas City, Missouri, voted in 2019 to make its city buses fare-free. Tallinn, Estonia, has offered free public transit to registered residents since 2013.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** resolution, affirmative, negative, plan, harms, solvency, disadvantage, rebuttal, evidence, fiscal, ridership, fund, offset, the evidence indicates
match: Match each debate term to its meaning.
  - Plan = The specific action the affirmative side proposes
  - Harms = The problems caused by the current system
  - Solvency = Proof that the plan would fix the harms
  - Disadvantage = A new problem the plan would cause
  - Rebuttal = A direct answer to the other side's argument

## Main: Plan
write: State the affirmative plan in one or two sentences, including how it is funded.
  lines: 3
  starter: We propose that Lakeport ___, funded by ___.
  key: We propose that Lakeport eliminate bus fares for a two-year pilot, funded by raising downtown parking meters by $0.50 an hour ($3 million a year) plus the $1.5 million saved by ending fare collection.

## Main: Harms and solvency
chart: Harm (problem now) | Evidence from the case file | Solvency (how the plan fixes it)
  rows: 3
  key: 1. Cost keeps people off buses: 38 percent of surveyed riders say so; free fares remove the cost barrier. 2. Ridership is falling: down from 9 million to 7 million rides in five years; removing fares can draw riders back, as cities trying fare-free service hope. 3. Fare collection wastes money and time: $1.5 million a year to collect $6 million, plus slow boarding; no fares means no collection cost and all-door boarding speeds trips.

## Main: Rebuttals
write: **Negative:** "The plan leaves a funding gap." Write your rebuttal.
  lines: 4
  starter: The negative says ___. However, ___.
  key: The negative says the plan cannot pay for itself. However, the gap is $1.5 million ($6 million lost minus $3 million parking minus $1.5 million saved), under 4 percent of the $40 million budget, and the two-year pilot lets the city measure results before committing; the gap could be covered by a small budget transfer or a higher parking increase.
write: **Negative:** "Free buses will be overcrowded." Write your rebuttal.
  lines: 3
  starter: The negative claims ___, but ___.
  key: The negative claims buses will be overcrowded, but full buses would show the plan is working, and the city could use the pilot data to add buses on busy routes; 44 percent of riders already want more frequent service.
write: **Negative:** "Higher parking rates will hurt downtown shops." Write your rebuttal.
  lines: 3
  starter: Although ___, the evidence suggests ___.
  key: Although some drivers may park less downtown, the plan would bring more shoppers by bus at no cost to them, and a $0.50 increase is small; the pilot could track downtown sales to test the concern.

# 7. Academic Argument Check
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can plan a rebuttal, nominalize sentences for an academic tone and synthesize two sources in one argument.
directions: Read two short sources about later start times for high schools. Match the argument terms, turn verbs into nouns, plan a rebuttal, and then write one paragraph that synthesizes both sources.
mistakes: In synthesis, students often summarize Source A, then summarize Source B, with no link between them. A synthesis sentence uses both at once ("While A shows..., B warns..."). In nominalization, watch for students who change the noun but leave the sentence's meaning or tense wrong, or who stack so many nouns that the sentence becomes unclear. A concession is not giving up: it admits a true point, then the rebuttal answers it.
notes: Both sources are fictional, written for Curio Class, and the district and its numbers are invented. The pattern matches real research and policy: the American Academy of Pediatrics recommended in 2014 that middle and high schools start at 8:30 a.m. or later, and California law has required most public high schools to start no earlier than 8:30 a.m. since the 2022-23 school year. Source math: 12 buses x $85,000 = $1,020,000 a year. Accept any rebuttal plan that concedes a real cost and answers it with evidence.

## Read two sources
source: Source A: from a fictional school district report, "One Year of Later Start Times" (written for Curio Class)
  Last fall, Riverbend High School moved its first bell from 7:30 a.m. to 8:30 a.m. Students wore sleep trackers for two weeks before and after the change. On school nights, average sleep rose from 6 hours 50 minutes to 7 hours 24 minutes. First-period tardies dropped by 22 percent, and the share of students who said they felt "very tired" in morning classes fell from 61 percent to 38 percent. Teachers reported that more students took part in first-period discussions.
source: Source B: from a fictional memo by Riverbend's transportation director (written for Curio Class)
  Moving the high school bell later is not free. Our buses run three routes each morning: high school first, then middle school, then elementary. If the high school starts later and the other schools stay the same, we cannot reuse the same buses. We would need 12 more buses and drivers, at about $85,000 each per year. Afternoon practices and games would also end later, and some students who work after school have told us they would lose hours at their jobs.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** claim, counterclaim, concession, rebuttal, synthesis, nominalization, evidence, whereas, although, consequently, the evidence suggests
match: Match each term to its meaning.
  - Counterclaim = An argument against your position
  - Concession = Admitting that part of the other side is true
  - Rebuttal = An answer that shows why the other side is weaker
  - Synthesis = Combining ideas from several sources into one point
  - Nominalization = Turning a verb or adjective into a noun

## Main: Nominalize
example: **Verb sentence:** The district changed the start time, and students slept more.
  **Nominalized:** The **change** in start time led to an **increase** in student sleep.
fill:
  - decide: {decision}
  - reduce: {reduction}
  - attend: {attendance}
  - require: {requirement}
write: Nominalize: "Because the school started later, fewer students were late."
  lines: 2
  key: The later start time led to a reduction in tardiness (or: The later start resulted in fewer tardies).
write: Nominalize: "If schools start later, buses will cost more."
  lines: 2
  key: Later start times would result in an increase in transportation costs.

## Main: Plan a rebuttal
text: Your claim: **Riverbend should keep the 8:30 a.m. start time.** Plan how you will answer Source B.
chart: My plan
  labels: Counterclaim (from Source B); Concession (what is true); Rebuttal (my answer); Evidence (cite a source)
  height: 0.45
  key: Model. Counterclaim: the later start costs about $1,020,000 a year for 12 more buses and pushes practices and jobs later. Concession: Although the cost is real and some students may lose work hours, ... Rebuttal: the gains in sleep, punctuality and alertness are worth the cost, and the district could reduce it, for example by changing the order of bus routes. Evidence: Source A: sleep rose 34 minutes a night, tardies fell 22 percent, and "very tired" students fell from 61 to 38 percent.

## Main: Synthesize
choice: Which sentence synthesizes both sources instead of summarizing one?
  - Source A says students slept from 6 hours 50 minutes to 7 hours 24 minutes.
  - Source B says the district would need 12 more buses.
  * Although Source A shows that a later start gave students about 34 more minutes of sleep, Source B warns that this benefit would cost about $1 million a year in buses.
  - Many people have opinions about when school should start.
write: Write one paragraph (4 to 6 sentences) that takes a position and synthesizes both sources. Use at least two nominalizations and your rebuttal plan.
  lines: 6
  starter: The evidence suggests that ___. Whereas Source A ___, Source B ___. Admittedly, ___. However, ___.
  key: Model: The evidence suggests that Riverbend should keep its 8:30 a.m. start. Whereas Source A reports an increase in sleep of 34 minutes a night and a 22 percent reduction in tardies, Source B points to the cost of 12 more buses, about $1,020,000 a year. Admittedly, this expense is significant, and later practices may mean fewer work hours for some students. However, the improvement in student alertness, with "very tired" students falling from 61 to 38 percent, is a direct benefit to learning, and the district could lower the cost by changing the order of its bus routes. Rubric: position stated; both sources used and linked; concession plus rebuttal; at least two nominalizations; formal tone.

# 20. Lab Data Conclusion
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, calculator, ruler
ican: I can analyze lab data and write a formal conclusion with evidence and error analysis.
directions: A class timed a pendulum to answer the question: **Does the length or the mass of a pendulum change its period** (the time for one full swing)? Complete the data tables, graph the results, and write a formal conclusion with an error analysis.
mistakes: Students often divide the time for 10 swings by 3 (the number of trials) instead of finding the average first and then dividing by 10. Watch for conclusions that say "period is proportional to length"; the period grows with length, but not in a straight line (four times the length gives about twice the period). In the error analysis, "human error" alone is too vague: name the error (reaction time with the stopwatch, measuring length to the wrong point) and say how it affects the data.
notes: The data are invented for practice but follow the physics: for small swings, the period T = 2π√(L/g) depends on length, not mass. With g = 9.8 m/s², theory gives 0.90, 1.27, 1.55, 1.80 and 2.01 s for lengths 0.20 to 1.00 m. Averages: 9.0, 12.7, 15.6, 18.0 and 20.0 s; periods 0.90, 1.27, 1.56, 1.80, 2.00 s. Percent error at 1.00 m: |2.00 - 2.01| / 2.01 x 100 = 0.50 percent. Timing 10 swings divides a reaction-time error of about 0.2 s by 10.

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** period, trial, average, variable, trend, the data indicate, remained nearly constant, source of error, percent error
example: **Informal:** The pendulum went slower when it was longer, and mass didn't really do anything.
  **Formal:** The data indicate that the period increased as the length increased, while mass had little effect.

## Main: Data table 1 (length changes, mass 100 g)
text: Average = (trial 1 + trial 2 + trial 3) / 3. Period = average / 10. Round to the hundredth.
table: Length (m) | Trial 1 (s) | Trial 2 (s) | Trial 3 (s) | Average for 10 swings (s) | Period (s)
  - 0.20 | 9.0 | 8.9 | 9.1 | {9.0} | {0.90}
  - 0.40 | 12.6 | 12.8 | 12.7 | {12.7} | {1.27}
  - 0.60 | 15.5 | 15.7 | 15.6 | {15.6} | {1.56}
  - 0.80 | 18.0 | 17.9 | 18.1 | {18.0} | {1.80}
  - 1.00 | 20.1 | 19.9 | 20.0 | {20.0} | {2.00}

## Main: Data table 2 (mass changes, length 0.60 m)
table: Mass (g) | Average for 10 swings (s) | Period (s)
  - 50 | 15.6 | {1.56}
  - 100 | 15.5 | {1.55}
  - 200 | 15.7 | {1.57}

## Main: Graph
text: Plot period against length from table 1. Draw a smooth curve through the points.
graph: Length (m) | 0 1 0.1 | Period (s) | 0 2.2 0.2
  key: 0.2,0.9 0.4,1.27 0.6,1.56 0.8,1.8 1,2
fill:
  - When the length went from 0.20 m to 0.80 m (4 times as long), the period went from 0.90 s to {1.80} s, about {2} times as long.
  - When the mass doubled from 100 g to 200 g, the period changed by only {0.02} s.

## Main: Error analysis
fill: Theory predicts a period of 2.01 s at 1.00 m. Percent error = |measured - theory| / theory x 100 = {0.5}%.
write: Name one source of error in this lab, explain how it could affect the data, and suggest one improvement.
  lines: 2
  starter: One source of error was ___, which may have caused ___. To reduce this error, ___.
  key: Model: One source of error was reaction time in starting and stopping the stopwatch, which may have caused each time to be off by about 0.2 s. Timing 10 swings already reduces this, since the error is divided by 10; to reduce it further, the class could time 20 swings or use a motion sensor. (Also accept: measuring length to the top of the bob instead of its center; swinging from a large angle; air resistance.)

## Main: Formal conclusion
write: Write a formal conclusion: answer the question, cite data, describe the trend, and state limits.
  lines: 5
  starter: The purpose of this investigation was to ___. The data indicate that ___. For example, ___. In contrast, ___. These results are limited by ___.
  key: Model: The purpose of this investigation was to determine whether the length or the mass of a pendulum affects its period. The data indicate that the period increased as the length increased, but not in direct proportion. For example, when the length quadrupled from 0.20 m to 0.80 m, the period only doubled, from 0.90 s to 1.80 s. In contrast, doubling the mass from 100 g to 200 g changed the period by just 0.02 s, which is within the variation between trials. These results agree with theory (0.5 percent error at 1.00 m) but are limited by reaction time in timing and by measuring only five lengths and three masses.

# 21. Explaining Models and Data Check
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, calculator if available
ican: I can summarize a research abstract, describe a projection and translate a technical sentence into plain language.
directions: Read the practice abstract. Summarize it, finish and describe the projection table, and rewrite technical sentences in plain English.
mistakes: Summaries often copy the abstract's first sentence (background) instead of its findings. Point students to the results and the limitation. In the projection, watch for "will" where the model only estimates; projections depend on assumptions. "Associated with" does not mean "caused."
notes: The abstract and study are fictional, written for this sheet; the pattern (more tree shade, cooler pavement) matches real urban heat research, but the numbers are invented for practice. Projection math: the model's 2.1 °C per 10 percentage points is 0.21 °C per point. 2032: 15 - 8 = 7 points, 7 x 0.21 = 1.47, about 1.5 °C. 2037: 16 points, 3.36, about 3.4 °C. 2042: 22 points, 4.62, about 4.6 °C.

## Read
source: Practice abstract written for Curio Class (fictional study)
  Urban schoolyards often contain large areas of asphalt that absorb solar radiation and raise surface temperatures. This study measured midafternoon surface temperatures at 24 elementary schoolyards in a mid-sized city during July and August. Sites were stratified by tree canopy cover: low (under 10 percent), moderate (10 to 30 percent) and high (over 30 percent). Mean surface temperature in direct sun was 8 °C higher at low-canopy sites than at high-canopy sites. A regression model estimated that each 10-percentage-point increase in canopy cover was associated with a 2.1 °C decrease in mean surface temperature, after controlling for pavement type. [1] These findings suggest that tree planting may reduce heat exposure for students, although the study did not measure air temperature or student health outcomes. [2]

## Warm up
text: **Word bank:** abstract, findings, method, limitation, canopy, mean, associated with, controlling for, projection, estimate, assumption, in plain terms, the study found
choice: Which part of the abstract is a limitation the authors admit?
  - The study measured 24 schoolyards.
  - Low-canopy sites were 8 °C hotter.
  * The study did not measure air temperature or student health outcomes.
  - Asphalt absorbs solar radiation.

## Main: Summarize the abstract
write: Summarize the abstract in 2 or 3 sentences: method, main finding, limitation.
  lines: 5
  starter: The study measured ___. It found that ___. However, ___.
  key: The study measured summer afternoon surface temperatures at 24 schoolyards with different amounts of tree cover. It found that schoolyards with more tree canopy were cooler: about 2.1 °C cooler for every 10 extra percentage points of canopy, and 8 °C cooler at high-canopy sites than low ones. However, it did not measure air temperature or student health.

## Main: Describe a projection
text: A school district plans to plant trees at one schoolyard that now has 8 percent canopy. Use the study's estimate (2.1 °C cooler for every 10 percentage points of canopy) to project the cooling. Round to the nearest tenth.
table: Year | Projected canopy cover | Increase since 2027 (points) | Projected cooling (°C)
  - 2027 | 8 percent | 0 | 0
  - 2032 | 15 percent | {7} | {1.5}
  - 2037 | 24 percent | {16} | {3.4}
  - 2042 | 30 percent | {22} | {4.6}
write: Describe the projection, and name one assumption it depends on.
  lines: 4
  starter: The projection estimates that ___ by 2042. This assumes ___.
  key: The projection estimates that the schoolyard's surface may be about 4.6 °C cooler by 2042 as canopy grows from 8 to 30 percent. This assumes the trees survive and grow as planned and that the study's 2.1 °C estimate applies to this schoolyard.

## Main: Translate it
write: Rewrite the sentence marked 1 in plain language.
  lines: 3
  starter: In plain terms, ...
  key: In plain terms, every time tree shade covered 10 percent more of a schoolyard, the ground in the sun was about 2 degrees Celsius cooler, even when comparing schoolyards with the same kind of pavement.
write: Rewrite the sentence marked 2 in plain language.
  lines: 3
  starter: In other words, ...
  key: In other words, planting trees might help keep students cooler, but the researchers did not check how hot the air felt or whether students were healthier.

## Stretch: Associated with
write: Why does the abstract say canopy was "associated with" cooler surfaces instead of "caused" them?
  lines: 3
  starter: The authors say "associated with" because ___.
  key: The authors say "associated with" because they compared existing schoolyards rather than running an experiment, so other differences between sites (like nearby buildings or shade from walls) could also play a part.

# 33. Workplace Scenario Responses
minutes: 40
materials: pencil
ican: I can write formal, polite responses to common workplace situations.
directions: Read the phrase bank and the example. Change informal phrases into formal ones. Then write a short, formal message for each of the five workplace scenarios.
mistakes: Students often over-apologize or blame others when responding to a mistake; a professional response owns the mistake once, states the fix and moves on. Watch for messages that state a problem but never make a request or offer a solution, and for casual openings ("Hey," "So,") and closings ("Thx"). Very long sentences can sound less clear, not more formal.
notes: All workplaces and people are invented. The model responses in the key are one good version; accept any response that is polite, clear, complete (problem, action or request, thanks) and in a formal register. Phrase bank functions: apologizing, requesting, responding to feedback, giving feedback and asking for clarification.

## Phrase bank
text: **Apologize:** I apologize for... / I take responsibility for... **Request:** Would it be possible to...? / I would appreciate it if... **Respond to feedback:** Thank you for pointing this out. / I will make sure to... **Give feedback:** One strength was... / One thing you might consider is... **Ask:** Could you please clarify...? / I want to make sure I understand...
example: **Scenario:** Your bus broke down, and you will be about 15 minutes late to your shift.
  **Response:** Hello Ms. Park, I am writing to let you know that my bus has broken down and I expect to arrive about 15 minutes late. I apologize for the inconvenience. I would be glad to stay 15 minutes after my shift to make up the time. Thank you for understanding. Dana

## Warm up: Informal to formal
table: Informal | Formal
  - Sorry, my bad. | {I apologize for the error.}
  - Can I switch days? | {Would it be possible to change my shift?}
  - What do you mean? | {Could you please clarify what you mean?}
  - Got it, thx. | {Thank you. I understand.}

## Main: Five scenarios
write: **A mistake.** You work at a garden store. You entered the wrong delivery date, so 40 bags of soil arrived a day early and no one was there to sign for them. Write to your supervisor, Mr. Ortiz.
  lines: 4
  starter: Dear Mr. Ortiz, I am writing to let you know that ___.
  key: Model: Dear Mr. Ortiz, I am writing to let you know that I entered the wrong delivery date for the soil order, so it arrived a day early. I take responsibility for the error. I have called the supplier to confirm the bags are safe, and from now on I will double-check delivery dates before submitting orders. Thank you, (name).
write: **A schedule conflict.** You are scheduled to work on Saturday, March 14, but you are registered for a college entrance exam that morning. Write to your manager, Ms. Chen.
  lines: 4
  starter: Dear Ms. Chen, I am writing to request ___.
  key: Model: Dear Ms. Chen, I am writing to request a change to my schedule on Saturday, March 14. I am registered for a college entrance exam that morning. A coworker, Sam, has offered to switch shifts with me, and I could work his Sunday shift instead. Would that be possible? Thank you for considering my request. (name)
write: **Feedback you receive.** Your supervisor tells you that your weekly inventory reports have been late three times. Write your response.
  lines: 4
  starter: Thank you for pointing this out. ___
  key: Model: Thank you for pointing this out. I understand that late reports make it harder to order supplies on time. Starting this week, I will set aside time on Thursday afternoons to finish the report before the Friday deadline. Please let me know if there is anything else I should change.
write: **Feedback you give.** A coworker asks what you thought of her training talk for new staff. It was well organized, but she spoke very fast. Write your feedback.
  lines: 4
  starter: One strength of your talk was ___. One thing you might consider is ___.
  key: Model: One strength of your talk was how clearly it was organized; the three steps were easy to follow. One thing you might consider is slowing down a little, especially on the safety rules, so new staff have time to take notes. Overall, it was very helpful.
write: **A question.** Your trainer showed you a new procedure for closing the cash register, but you are not sure what to do with the receipts at the end. Write to your trainer, Mr. Lee.
  lines: 4
  starter: Dear Mr. Lee, I want to make sure I understand ___.
  key: Model: Dear Mr. Lee, I want to make sure I understand the new closing procedure. Could you please clarify what I should do with the receipts after I count the register? Should they go in the safe or to the manager's office? Thank you for your help. (name)

## Stretch
choice: A coworker's message to a customer says: "We messed up your order. It happens. It'll be there whenever." Which revision is the most professional?
  - We messed up your order, so sorry!!! It will be there soon, probably.
  * We apologize for the error with your order. A replacement will arrive by Friday, March 6.
  - Your order is wrong because the warehouse made a mistake, not us.
  - It happens. Your order will be there whenever it gets there.
