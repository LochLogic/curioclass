# 1. Keyword Highlighter
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, highlighter (optional)
ican: I can pick out the keywords in a research question.
directions: Underline the keywords in each question: the words that tell what you want to know about. Cross out the small words a search does not need. Then write your search words in the box.
mistakes: Students often keep question words (how, what, why) and small words (the, do, a) in their search. Others drop a word that matters, like "fall" in question C or "children" in question G, which changes the topic. Ask: "If you took this word out, would you still find what you need?"
notes: Search tools match the important words, so a short search of 2 to 4 keywords usually works better than a whole question. The key shows one good search for each question; accept any search that keeps the topic and the specific thing asked (for example "cheetah top speed" or "cheetah running speed"). Word order does not matter.

## Warm up: Watch me do one
example: Question: What do **koalas** like to **eat**?
  Cross out: What, do, like, to
  Keywords (underline): **koalas**, **eat**
  Search words: **koalas eat** (or **koala diet**)

## Main: Find the keywords
table: Research question | My search words
  - A. How fast can a cheetah run? | {cheetah speed}
  - B. What do giant pandas eat? | {giant pandas eat}
  - C. Why do leaves change color in the fall? | {leaves change color fall}
  - D. Who invented the telephone? | {telephone inventor}
  - E. How many moons does Jupiter have? | {Jupiter moons}
  - F. Where do sea turtles lay their eggs? | {sea turtles lay eggs}
  - G. What was life like for children in ancient Rome? | {children ancient Rome}
  - H. How do bees make honey? | {bees make honey}
  - I. When did people first walk on the Moon? | {first Moon walk}
  - J. What are the rules of chess? | {chess rules}

## Check: Which search is best?
choice: You want to know how tall giraffes grow. Which search is best?
  - how tall do giraffes grow when they are all grown up
  - giraffes
  - tall animals
  * giraffe height

## Stretch: Your question
write: Write a question you wonder about. Underline its keywords, then write your search words.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Example: Why do cats purr? Search words: cats purr why (or cat purring).

# 21. Picture Credit Lines
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can give credit to the person who made a picture and tell where I found it.
directions: Each picture card tells who made the picture and where it came from. Write a credit line for each picture using the pattern in the example.
mistakes: Students often write only where they found the picture ("from the internet") and leave out the person who made it. "The internet" is not a place: name the website, book or museum. Others copy the picture's title as the creator.
notes: All four pictures, people and sources on this sheet are made up for practice; the small line drawings stand in for the real photo, drawing and painting. A credit line is the short line printed under a picture; it is not yet a full citation (that comes in grade 5). The pattern on this sheet is kind of picture, "by" the creator, then "from" the source. Some sources also ask for the license, such as "CC BY," at the end: if a source gives one, it is good to add it.

## Warm up: The pattern
example: Picture card: a photo of a dog, taken by Lee Chen, found on the website Happy Paws Photos.
  Credit line: **Photo by Lee Chen, from Happy Paws Photos.**
choice: Which credit line is complete?
  - Picture from the internet.
  - Photo by Lee Chen.
  * Photo by Lee Chen, from Happy Paws Photos.

## Think about it
choice: Why do we write a credit line?
  * To thank the creator and show readers where the picture came from
  - To make the page longer
  - So nobody else can use the picture
write: Mia used a photo in her report but did not write a credit line. Why might the photographer be unhappy?
  lines: 2
  key: The photographer worked to make the photo, and nobody reading Mia's report will know who made it; it looks like Mia's own work.
