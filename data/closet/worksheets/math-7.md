# 1. Complex Unit Rates
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can compute unit rates from ratios of fractions.
directions: For each situation, write the rate as a complex fraction (a fraction divided by a fraction), then divide to find the unit rate: the amount for 1 hour, 1 pound, 1 batch, and so on. Write mixed numbers in simplest form and include units.
mistakes: Students often divide in the wrong order (1/4 ÷ 1/2 instead of 1/2 ÷ 1/4) and get hours per mile instead of miles per hour. Ask, "per what?": the "per" quantity goes on the bottom. Others multiply the two fractions; a quick estimate (half a mile in a quarter hour must be more than half a mile per hour) catches it.
notes: Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal: (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = (1/2) × 4 = 2. Improper fractions (3/2) and mixed numbers (1 1/2) are both correct; the key shows mixed numbers. For the mower, the reciprocal rate is 2 1/4 hours per acre, which is also a correct unit rate if labeled.

## Warm up: Divide fractions
table: Problem | Answer
  - 1/2 ÷ 1/4 | {2}
  - 3/4 ÷ 3 | {1/4}
  - 2 ÷ 2/3 | {3}

## Main: Find the unit rate
example: Maya walks 1/2 mile in 1/4 hour. Rate: (1/2 mile) / (1/4 hour). 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4 = 2. Maya walks 2 miles per hour.
table: Situation | Complex fraction | Unit rate
  - A recipe uses 3/4 cup of sugar for 1/2 batch. Cups per batch? | {(3/4) / (1/2)} | {1 1/2 cups per batch}
  - Leo paints 2/3 of a wall in 1/2 hour. Walls per hour? | {(2/3) / (1/2)} | {1 1/3 walls per hour}
  - 5/6 pound of cheese costs $5. Dollars per pound? | {5 / (5/6)} | {$6 per pound}
  - A snail crawls 1/8 meter in 1/3 minute. Meters per minute? | {(1/8) / (1/3)} | {3/8 meter per minute}
  - A hiker covers 2 1/2 miles in 3/4 hour. Miles per hour? | {(5/2) / (3/4)} | {3 1/3 miles per hour}
  - A faucet fills 3/5 gallon in 1/4 minute. Gallons per minute? | {(3/5) / (1/4)} | {2 2/5 gallons per minute}
  - A mower cuts 1/3 acre in 3/4 hour. Acres per hour? | {(1/3) / (3/4)} | {4/9 acre per hour}
  - A printer prints 3/4 of a poster in 1/6 minute. Posters per minute? | {(3/4) / (1/6)} | {4 1/2 posters per minute}
draw: Show your work | 2.2

## Stretch: Who is faster?
write: Ana walks 2/3 mile in 1/4 hour. Ben walks 3/4 mile in 1/3 hour. Who walks faster? Show both unit rates.
  lines: 3
  key: Ana: (2/3) ÷ (1/4) = 8/3 = 2 2/3 miles per hour. Ben: (3/4) ÷ (1/3) = 9/4 = 2 1/4 miles per hour. Ana is faster.

# 5. Sales, Tips and Interest
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, calculator
ican: I can solve multi-step percent problems about sales, tax, tips, markups, simple interest and percent error.
directions: Solve each problem. Show each step: find the percent of the amount, then add or subtract it, or use a multiplier like 0.75 or 1.06. Round money to the nearest cent.
mistakes: The most common error is taking both percents of the original price (25% off and 6% tax of $64) instead of taxing the sale price. In the markdown problem, 20% off the marked-up price is not the same as 20% off the cost. For percent error, students divide by the estimate instead of the actual value. In the last problem, the $54 already includes the tip: dividing by 1.20 gives the bill, but subtracting 20% of $54 does not.
notes: Work, in order: 1) 64 × 0.75 = 48, 48 × 1.06 = 50.88. 2) 38.50 × 0.18 = 6.93, total 45.43. 3) I = Prt = 1,200 × 0.035 × 4 = 168, balance 1,368. 4) |250 - 320| ÷ 320 = 0.21875, about 21.9%. 5) 30 × 1.60 = 48, 48 × 0.80 = 38.40, profit 8.40, which is 28% of the $30 cost. 6) 1.20b = 54, b = 45. Stretch: 500 × 0.06 × t = 90, t = 3 years.

# 16. Two-Step Tape Diagrams
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can solve equations like px + q = r and p(x + q) = r with a tape diagram and with algebra.
directions: Each story matches an equation. Fill in the tape diagram: the whole tape is the total, and each box labeled x is the same amount. Then solve the equation with algebra and check that both ways agree.
mistakes: In px + q = r, students sometimes divide first (29 ÷ 3) instead of taking away the q. In p(x + q) = r, the tape has p equal pieces that each hold x + q, so divide first, then subtract. The fix-it shows the common slip of multiplying only the x: 2(x + 3) is 2x + 6, not 2x + 3.
notes: Answers: 1) 3x + 5 = 29, x = 8. 2) 4x + 7 = 31, x = 6. 3) 2x + 11 = 25, x = 7. 4) 5x + 4 = 49, x = 9. 5) 2(x + 3) = 22, x + 3 = 11, x = 8. 6) 4(x + 2.5) = 30, x + 2.5 = 7.5, x = 5. For 5 and 6, students may also distribute first (2x + 6 = 22); both ways are correct.

## Warm up
example: 3x + 5 = 29. The tape is 29 long: three x boxes and a 5. Take away the 5: the three x boxes are 24. Each box is 24 ÷ 3 = 8. Algebra: 3x = 24, x = 8.

## Main: Tape, then algebra
text: Maya buys 3 notebooks that cost the same and a $5 pen. She spends $29. The whole tape is 29.

  - {8} | {8} | {8} | 5
fill: Algebra: 3x + 5 = 29, so 3x = {24} and x = {8}

table: x | x | x | x | 7
  - {6} | {6} | {6} | {6} | 7
fill: Algebra: 4x + 7 = 31, so 4x = {24} and x = {6}
text: A plant was 11 cm tall and grew the same amount each week for 2 weeks. Now it is 25 cm. The whole tape is 25.
table: x | x | 11
  - {7} | {7} | 11
fill: Algebra: 2x + 11 = 25, so 2x = {14} and x = {7}
text: A gym charges a $4 sign-up fee and the same price for each of 5 classes. The total is $49. The whole tape is 49.

  - {9} | {9} | {9} | {9} | {9} | 4

## Stretch
fixit: 2(x + 3) = 22
  - The tape shows two pieces of x + 3.
  * 2x + 3 = 22
  - 2x = 19
  - x = 9.5
  answer: x = 8
  why: 2(x + 3) means 2 times x and 2 times 3, so it is 2x + 6 = 22. Then 2x = 16 and x = 8.
write: Write your own story for 3x + 4 = 19. Draw its tape and solve it.
  lines: 2
  key: Possible story: I buy 3 books that cost the same and a $4 bookmark for $19 in all. The tape is three x boxes and a 4, total 19. 3x = 15, so x = 5: each book costs $5.

# 22. Redraw the Park Map
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can redraw a scale drawing at a different scale and compare the areas of the two drawings.
directions: The original map of Maple Park was drawn on grid paper at 1 square = 5 meters. Its features are listed in the table. Redraw the map on the grid at the new scale, 1 square = 10 meters, then compare the two drawings.
mistakes: The big one: students expect the new drawing to have half the area because the lengths are halved. Halving both length and width makes the area 1/4 as large (384 squares to 96). Students also confuse map area with real area; the real park does not shrink, so its area in square meters is the same at both scales.
notes: The original map is given as a table of rectangles rather than a picture, so every student works from the same exact sizes. If you want, draw the original on a 24 by 16 grid on the board first. Every new measurement is half the original: park 12 by 8, path 12 by 1, pond 4 by 3, playground 3 by 2, garden 2 by 2. The answer key grid shows the park outline and the path.

# 27. Circumference and Area
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, calculator
ican: I can find the circumference and area of a circle from its radius or diameter, in terms of π and rounded.
directions: For each circle, find the radius and the diameter. Then find the circumference (C = 2πr or C = πd) and the area (A = πr²), first in terms of π and then rounded to the nearest tenth using the π key.
mistakes: When the diameter is given, students often put it into A = πr² without halving it first (d = 10 gives 100π instead of 25π). Some square 2πr instead of r, or write r² as 2r. In terms of π, the number goes in front: 6π, not π6.
notes: Rounded answers use the calculator's π key. With 3.14, a few areas come out slightly lower (circle 4: 254.3; circle 7: 452.2; circle 8: 706.5; circle 9: 50.2); accept either if students say which value they used. Stretch: C = 31.4 gives d = 10, r = 5, A = 25π ≈ 78.5 square units. One 16-inch pizza is 64π ≈ 201.1 square inches; two 10-inch pizzas are 2 × 25π = 50π ≈ 157.1 square inches.

## Warm up
fill:
  - A circle has a radius of 6 cm. Its diameter is {12} cm.
  - A circle has a diameter of 9 m. Its radius is {4.5} m.
  - In A = πr², r² means r × {r}.

## Main
example: Circle with diameter 10: r = 5. C = π × 10 = 10π ≈ 31.4. A = π × 5² = 25π ≈ 78.5.

# 38. Compare Two Teams
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, calculator (optional)
ican: I can compare two groups using their means, their mean absolute deviations and how much their dot plots overlap.
directions: The dot plots show the heights, in inches, of 10 players on a volleyball team and 10 players on a basketball team. Find each team's mean and mean absolute deviation (MAD), then compare the teams.
mistakes: Students often forget to take the absolute value, so the deviations add to 0. Others divide by the number of different heights instead of the number of players (10). When comparing, students may say "every basketball player is taller", but the plots overlap from 66 to 68 inches.
notes: Volleyball: sum 640, mean 64; deviations 4, 3, 2, 1, 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 sum to 20, MAD 2. Basketball: sum 700, mean 70; the same deviations, MAD 2. The means differ by 6 inches, which is 3 MADs. A common rule of thumb in grade 7: when the difference in means is 2 or more MADs, the groups are clearly different. The heights are made up for practice.
