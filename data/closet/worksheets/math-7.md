# 1. Complex Unit Rates
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can compute unit rates from ratios of fractions.
directions: For each situation, write the rate as a complex fraction (a fraction divided by a fraction), then divide to find the unit rate: the amount for 1 hour, 1 pound, 1 batch, and so on. Write mixed numbers in simplest form and include units.
mistakes: Students often divide in the wrong order (1/4 ÷ 1/2 instead of 1/2 ÷ 1/4) and get hours per mile instead of miles per hour. Ask, "per what?": the "per" quantity goes on the bottom. Others multiply the two fractions; a quick estimate (half a mile in a quarter hour must be more than half a mile per hour) catches it.
notes: Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal: (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = (1/2) × 4 = 2. Improper fractions (3/2) and mixed numbers (1 1/2) are both correct; the key shows mixed numbers. For the mower, the reciprocal rate is 2 1/4 hours per acre, which is also a correct unit rate if labeled.

## Warm up: Divide fractions
table: Problem | Answer
  - 1/2 ÷ 1/4 | {2}
  - 3/4 ÷ 3 | {1/4}
  - 2 ÷ 2/3 | {3}

## Main: Find the unit rate
example: Maya walks 1/2 mile in 1/4 hour. Rate: (1/2 mile) / (1/4 hour). 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4 = 2. Maya walks 2 miles per hour.
table: Situation | Complex fraction | Unit rate
  - A recipe uses 3/4 cup of sugar for 1/2 batch. Cups per batch? | {(3/4) / (1/2)} | {1 1/2 cups per batch}
  - Leo paints 2/3 of a wall in 1/2 hour. Walls per hour? | {(2/3) / (1/2)} | {1 1/3 walls per hour}
  - 5/6 pound of cheese costs $5. Dollars per pound? | {5 / (5/6)} | {$6 per pound}
  - A snail crawls 1/8 meter in 1/3 minute. Meters per minute? | {(1/8) / (1/3)} | {3/8 meter per minute}
  - A hiker covers 2 1/2 miles in 3/4 hour. Miles per hour? | {(5/2) / (3/4)} | {3 1/3 miles per hour}
  - A faucet fills 3/5 gallon in 1/4 minute. Gallons per minute? | {(3/5) / (1/4)} | {2 2/5 gallons per minute}
  - A mower cuts 1/3 acre in 3/4 hour. Acres per hour? | {(1/3) / (3/4)} | {4/9 acre per hour}
  - A printer prints 3/4 of a poster in 1/6 minute. Posters per minute? | {(3/4) / (1/6)} | {4 1/2 posters per minute}

## Stretch: Who is faster?
write: Ana walks 2/3 mile in 1/4 hour. Ben walks 3/4 mile in 1/3 hour. Who walks faster? Show both unit rates.
  lines: 3
  key: Ana: (2/3) ÷ (1/4) = 8/3 = 2 2/3 miles per hour. Ben: (3/4) ÷ (1/3) = 9/4 = 2 1/4 miles per hour. Ana is faster.

# 22. Redraw the Park Map
minutes: 40
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can redraw a scale drawing at a different scale and compare the areas of the two drawings.
directions: The original map of Maple Park was drawn on grid paper at 1 square = 5 meters. Its features are listed in the table. Redraw the map on the grid at the new scale, 1 square = 10 meters, then compare the two drawings.
mistakes: The big one: students expect the new drawing to have half the area because the lengths are halved. Halving both length and width makes the area 1/4 as large (384 squares to 96). Students also confuse map area with real area; the real park does not shrink, so its area in square meters is the same at both scales.
notes: The original map is given as a table of rectangles rather than a picture, so every student works from the same exact sizes. If you want, draw the original on a 24 by 16 grid on the board first. Every new measurement is half the original: park 12 by 8, path 12 by 1, pond 4 by 3, playground 3 by 2, garden 2 by 2. The answer key grid shows the park outline and the path.

## Stretch
write: The lengths on the new map are 1/2 of the original lengths. Explain why the area is not 1/2 of the original area. What would happen to the area if the new scale were 1 square = 15 m?
  lines: 4
  key: Area uses two lengths, and both are halved: 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4. At 1 square = 15 m the lengths would be 1/3 of the original (park 8 by about 5.3 squares), so the area would be 1/3 × 1/3 = 1/9 of the original: 384 ÷ 9 = 42 2/3 squares.
