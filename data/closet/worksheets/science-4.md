# 1. Ramp Height Cup Crash
minutes: 40
materials: for each group: a ramp (a grooved ruler, a cardboard tube cut in half, or a stiff folder), books to stack, a marble or small rubber ball, a paper cup with a doorway cut in its rim, a meter stick or ruler, tape, pencil
ican: I can use evidence to explain how the speed of an object relates to its energy.
directions: Set the cup upside down at the bottom of the ramp with its doorway facing the ramp. Release the ball from the top without pushing. Measure how far the cup slides. Do 3 trials at each of 3 ramp heights, then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students often push the ball to "help" it, which changes the energy. They also measure from different places each time: always measure from the bottom of the ramp to the front edge of the cup. Watch for changing two things at once, like the height and the ball.
notes: Heights of 5, 10 and 15 cm work well. Tape a start line on the ramp and a mark for the cup. Typical results: the cup slides farther each time the ramp goes up, roughly 2 times as far for twice the height. Typical class data (middle values on the key graph): 5 cm: 8, 9, 7 cm (middle 8); 10 cm: 16, 15, 17 (middle 16); 15 cm: 23, 25, 24 (middle 24). Real numbers depend on the cup, ball and floor. Marbles roll: sweep the floor area clear and collect marbles at the end.

## Plan
fill:
  - We change: {the ramp height}  We measure: {how far the cup slides}
write: List two things you must keep the same so the test is fair.
  lines: 1
  key: The same ball, the same cup, the same starting spot on the ramp, the same floor, and letting go without pushing.
write: Predict: What will happen to the cup's slide as the ramp gets higher? Why?
  lines: 1
  key: Accept any prediction with a reason. Many predict the cup slides farther because the ball goes faster.

## Data
table: Ramp height (cm) | Trial 1 (cm) | Trial 2 (cm) | Trial 3 (cm) | Middle value (cm)
  - 5 | | | |
  - 10 | | | |
  - 15 | | | |
graph: Ramp height (cm) | 0 20 5 | Cup slide (cm) | 0 40 5
  key: 5,8 10,16 15,24

## Explain
write: What pattern do you see in your data?
  lines: 2
  key: Typical: the higher the ramp, the farther the cup slides; doubling the height about doubles the slide.
write: Use the words speed and energy to explain your results.
  lines: 3
  key: A ball from a higher ramp is going faster at the bottom, so it has more energy. When it hits the cup, it passes more energy to the cup, so the cup slides farther.
choice: Why do we do 3 trials at each height?
  - To make the ball roll faster
  * To check that our results are not a fluke
  - So everyone gets a turn to measure
write: When the ball hits the cup, where does the ball's energy go?
  lines: 2
  key: Into the cup (making it move), and into sound and heat as the cup rubs on the floor until it stops.

## Stretch
write: Predict how far the cup would slide from a 20 cm ramp. Use your data to explain.
  lines: 2
  key: Typical: about 32 cm, since the slide grew about 8 cm for each 5 cm of height. Accept a prediction that follows the student's own pattern.

# 21. Waves and Senses Check-In
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can describe waves, use a pattern to send information, and explain how we see objects.
directions: Plot and label a wave, decode and encode a signal, and explain how light helps you see.
mistakes: The most common mistake on the wave is measuring amplitude from the bottom of a trough to the top of a crest; amplitude is from the rest line to a crest. On the light question, many students draw light going out of the eye to the object; light travels from the object into the eye.
notes: Morse code is a real pattern code: short and long pulses of sound, light or electricity. The decoder shows only the letters used here. The key graph joins the points with straight lines; students should draw a smooth wave. A light path answer should include a light source, reflection off the object, and the light entering the eye.

## Warm up: Plot a wave
text: Plot these points and connect them with a smooth curve: (0, 0) (1, 3) (2, 0) (3, -3) (4, 0) (5, 3) (6, 0) (7, -3) (8, 0). The line y = 0 is the rest position.
graph: Distance (m) | 0 8 1 | Height (m) | -4 4 1
  key: 0,0 1,3 2,0 3,-3 4,0 5,3 6,0 7,-3 8,0
fill:
  - Label a **crest** and a **trough** on your wave.
  - The amplitude of this wave is {3} m.
  - The wavelength (crest to crest) is {4} m.
  - If the amplitude grew, the wave would carry {more} energy.

## Main: Decode the signal
table: Letter | A | E | G | H | I | L | O | S | T | V | W
  - Code | • – | • | – – • | • • • • | • • | • – • • | – – – | • • • | – | • • • – | • – –
text: In Morse code, a dot is a short beep or flash and a dash is a long one. A slash (/) separates the letters.
fill:
  - • • • • / • • = {HI}
  - • – – / • – / • • • – / • / • • • = {WAVES}
  - • – • • / • • / – – • / • • • • / – = {LIGHT}
  - Write SOS in Morse code: {• • • / – – – / • • •}
write: Why is a code like this useful for sending a message far away?
  lines: 2
  key: A simple on and off pattern of sound, light or electricity can travel a long way, and anyone with the code can turn it back into words.

## Explain: How do you see?
draw: Draw a lamp, an apple and an eye. Use arrows to show the path of light. | 1.8
write: Explain how light lets you see the apple, step by step.
  lines: 3
  key: Light from the lamp travels to the apple. It reflects (bounces) off the apple. The reflected light travels in a straight line into my eye, and my eye sends a signal to my brain, which makes sense of it.
choice: In a room with no light at all, can you see the apple?
  - Yes, if you look hard
  * No, because no light reflects off it into your eyes
  - Yes, because eyes give off light

## Stretch
write: An owl has very large eyes for its head. How might this help it hunt at night?
  lines: 2
  key: Large eyes let in more light, so the owl can see better in dim light at night to find prey.
