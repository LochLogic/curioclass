# 1. Ten Times Greater
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, place-value chart (optional)
ican: I can explain that a digit in one place is worth ten times what it is worth in the place to its right.
directions: Each number has the same digit in two places next to each other. Write the value of each one, then tell how many times greater the left one is. Finish with the fill-ins and the stretch.
mistakes: Students often write the digit instead of its value (7 instead of 7,000). Others count zeros wrong in the larger numbers; a place-value chart with the number written in it fixes this quickly. In the stretch, the two 5s are not next to each other, so the answer is 100, not 10.
notes: Every answer in the "times greater" column is 10, and that is the point: let students notice it and say why (moving one place left multiplies by ten). Ask early finishers to find a number in the classroom or a book with a repeated digit and compare the values.

## Warm up: Value of a digit
example: In 7,700 the left 7 is in the thousands place, so it is worth 7,000. The right 7 is worth 700. 7,000 ÷ 700 = 10, so the left 7 is worth 10 times as much.
fill:
  - The value of the 5 in 352 is {50}
  - The value of the 8 in 8,416 is {8,000}
  - The value of the 6 in 260,931 is {60,000}

## Main: Compare the twins
table: Number | Digit | Value of the left one | Value of the right one | How many times greater?
  - 3,384 | 3 | {3,000} | {300} | {10}
  - 55,210 | 5 | {50,000} | {5,000} | {10}
  - 2,992 | 9 | {900} | {90} | {10}
  - 448,906 | 4 | {400,000} | {40,000} | {10}
  - 6,605 | 6 | {6,000} | {600} | {10}
  - 924,401 | 4 | {4,000} | {400} | {10}
  - 177,215 | 7 | {70,000} | {7,000} | {10}
  - 811 | 1 | {10} | {1} | {10}
fill:
  - 700 is {10} times as much as 70.
  - {4,000} is 10 times as much as 400.
  - 60 is 10 times as much as {6}.
  - The 3 in 30,000 is worth {10} times the 3 in 3,000.
choice: Ravi says the 2 in 2,000 is worth 2 times the 2 in 200. Is he right?
  - Yes, because there are two 2s.
  * No, it is worth 10 times as much: 2,000 is 10 x 200.
  - No, it is worth 100 times as much.

## Stretch: Not next door
write: In 5,050 the two 5s are not next to each other. How many times greater is the left 5 than the right 5? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: 100 times. The left 5 is worth 5,000 and the right 5 is worth 50. Moving one place left is 10 times, and moving two places is 10 x 10 = 100 times: 50 x 100 = 5,000.

# 48. Lines of Symmetry: Find and Finish
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can find lines of symmetry and finish a symmetric design.
directions: Draw every line of symmetry on each figure with a ruler and write how many there are. Then finish each dot-paper design so both halves match across the dashed line.
mistakes: Many students draw the diagonals of a rectangle or parallelogram as lines of symmetry. Have them imagine folding along the line: the halves must land exactly on each other. Students often find only 2 or 3 of the hexagon's 6 lines.
notes: Tracing paper or a small mirror makes a good check for students who are stuck. The parallelogram has no lines of symmetry (it has turn symmetry, which comes later).

## Warm up
example: A line of symmetry folds a figure into two halves that match exactly. A square has 4.

## Main: Find every line
symmetry: Draw the lines of symmetry. Write how many.
  - square
  - rectangle
  - equilateral triangle
  - isosceles triangle
  - regular hexagon
  - parallelogram
  - kite
  - arrow

## Finish: Make it symmetric
halves: Draw the other half of each design so it matches across the dashed line.
  - 6,1 2,4 2,8 6,8
  - 6,0 4,3 4,6 2,8 6,7
  - 6,8 1,8 1,2 3,5 5,2 6,4
  - 6,7 1,3 2,1 4,1 6,3

## Stretch
write: Draw a capital letter that has exactly 2 lines of symmetry. Which letter did you choose?
  lines: 2
  key: H, I or X (an oval O also works). A, M, T, U, V, W and Y have only 1; F, G, J, L, P, R have none.
