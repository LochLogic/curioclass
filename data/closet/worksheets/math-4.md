# 48. Lines of Symmetry: Find and Finish
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can find lines of symmetry and finish a symmetric design.
directions: Draw every line of symmetry on each figure with a ruler and write how many there are. Then finish each dot-paper design so both halves match across the dashed line.
mistakes: Many students draw the diagonals of a rectangle or parallelogram as lines of symmetry. Have them imagine folding along the line: the halves must land exactly on each other. Students often find only 2 or 3 of the hexagon's 6 lines.
notes: Tracing paper or a small mirror makes a good check for students who are stuck. The parallelogram has no lines of symmetry (it has turn symmetry, which comes later).

## Warm up
example: A line of symmetry folds a figure into two halves that match exactly. A square has 4.

## Main: Find every line
symmetry: Draw the lines of symmetry. Write how many.
  - square
  - rectangle
  - equilateral triangle
  - isosceles triangle
  - regular hexagon
  - parallelogram
  - kite
  - arrow

## Finish: Make it symmetric
halves: Draw the other half of each design so it matches across the dashed line.
  - 6,1 2,4 2,8 6,8
  - 6,0 4,3 4,6 2,8 6,7
  - 6,8 1,8 1,2 3,5 5,2 6,4
  - 6,7 1,3 2,1 4,1 6,3

## Stretch
write: Draw a capital letter that has exactly 2 lines of symmetry. Which letter did you choose?
  lines: 2
  key: H, I or X (an oval O also works). A, M, T, U, V, W and Y have only 1; F, G, J, L, P, R have none.
