# 1. Assembly Dance Brief
minutes: 45
materials: pencil, highlighter
ican: I can respond to a choreography brief with a clear intent, audience, cast and structure.
directions: Read the brief and highlight every requirement. Answer the questions. Then write your response: your intent, how you will serve the audience, your cast and a timed structure that fits 3 minutes.
mistakes: Students design for themselves instead of the audience in the brief (families and younger students). Watch the timing: a structure that adds up to 4 or 5 minutes does not meet the brief. Students also skip the practical limits (stage size, no lifts) until it is too late.
notes: A brief is how real commissions start: a client names the purpose, audience, length and limits, and the choreographer answers with a plan. The school in the brief is fictional. Students can work alone or in groups of up to 4; groups still submit one response each. The count math assumes music at 120 beats per minute, a common dance tempo.

## Warm up: The brief
passage: Brief: Spring Unity Assembly, Ridgeview Middle School
  Ridgeview Middle School is looking for a student choreographer to create a short dance for the Spring Unity Assembly. The theme this year is "Many Voices, One School." The dance will open the assembly, so it needs to grab attention and set a welcoming mood.
  The audience will be about 600 people: every student in grades 6, 7 and 8, the teachers, and families who are invited to attend. Some guests will be watching from the back of the gym, about 30 meters from the stage, so movements must read clearly from far away.
  The dance must last 3 minutes, plus or minus 10 seconds. Music must be instrumental or have school-appropriate lyrics, and it must be approved by the music teacher two weeks before the assembly.
  The cast should be between 8 and 16 dancers. Dancers do not need any training, so every dancer must be able to learn the material in four after-school rehearsals of 45 minutes each. At least one section should include every cast member moving in unison.
  The stage is 10 meters wide and 6 meters deep, with a wooden floor. There is no curtain, so dancers will enter and exit from the sides of the stage in view of the audience. For safety, the dance may not include lifts, flips or jumps from objects.
  Please submit a written response that states the intent of the dance, describes how it will connect with this audience, names the cast size, and outlines the structure with timings.

write: The back row sits about 30 meters away. Name two choices that would help movement read clearly from there.
  lines: 2
  key: Large, full-body movements; clear unison; strong level changes; formations that spread across the width; held shapes at key moments; costumes that contrast with the background.

## Main: My response to the brief
write: Intent: What is the dance about, and how does it show "Many Voices, One School"?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Model: Small groups enter with different movement styles that represent different voices, then gradually share moves until the whole cast dances one phrase together, showing a school made of many different people.
write: Audience: How will you welcome and connect with this audience?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Strong answers mention the mixed audience of students and families, an energetic opening, clear big moves, and perhaps a moment that faces or invites the audience (a wave, a group clap pattern).

## Stretch
write: The music teacher rejects your song one week before the assembly. What is your backup plan?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Strong plans: pick a backup instrumental track with the same tempo now, or choreograph to counts so any approved song at about 120 beats per minute will fit.

# 21. Cool Down and Recovery Plan
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can plan a cool down and recovery routine and explain how each step helps the body.
directions: Read about the dance team's rehearsal. Match the stretches to the muscles they target. Then plan a cool down for the team and explain how each step helps recovery.
mistakes: Students often plan bouncing stretches or start stretching the instant the dancing stops. A cool down begins with easy movement, then slow stretches held without bouncing. Students may also claim stretching prevents all soreness; the evidence for that is weak, so focus on gradual slowing down, flexibility, water and rest.
notes: This sheet uses a fictional team so no one shares personal health information. Facts used in the key: a cool down of easy movement for about 3 to 5 minutes lets heart rate and breathing come down gradually; static stretches are done when muscles are warm and held about 15 to 30 seconds without bouncing; water replaces fluid lost in sweat; sleep and rest days let muscles repair. Any student with a medical plan follows it.

## Warm up: The rehearsal
example: The Westbrook dance team just finished a 60-minute rehearsal with lots of jumps, runs and floor work. Everyone is sweaty and breathing hard. Their teacher asks you to plan a 10-minute cool down.
match: Match each stretch to the main muscle group it targets.
  - lunge with the back heel pressed down = calf (back of the lower leg)
  - standing, hold one foot behind you = quadriceps (front of the thigh)
  - sit with legs straight and reach toward the toes = hamstrings (back of the thigh)
  - sit with the soles of the feet together, knees out = inner thigh
  - clasp hands behind the back and open the chest = chest and front of the shoulders
choice: Which is the safest way to stretch during a cool down?
  - quick bounces to go deeper
  * hold a gentle stretch still for about 15 to 30 seconds
  - push into a stretch until it hurts

## Explain
write: Why should the team start with easy movement instead of stopping all at once?
  lines: 2
  key: Easy movement lets heart rate and breathing come down gradually, which can keep dancers from feeling dizzy or light-headed after hard work.
write: Why does the team need water and rest after rehearsal?
  lines: 3
  key: Water replaces the fluid lost through sweat. Rest and sleep give muscles time to repair and get stronger, so the team is ready for the next rehearsal.

## Stretch
write: The team has a show tomorrow. What one thing would you add to the plan for tonight, and why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Strong answers: a full night of sleep, a healthy meal and water, or a light walk-through instead of more hard dancing, so muscles recover before the show.
