# 1. Parts of a Book Cut and Glue
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons, a real picture book to hold up
ican: I can name the cover, the spine and the pages of a book.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each book picture. The arrow points to one part. Listen as I read each label. Cut out the labels and glue each one under the picture it names.
mistakes: Children often call the spine the "side" or the "back." Hold up a real book, run a finger down the spine, and say "the spine holds the book together, like your backbone holds you up." Some children glue "pages" under the cover because the cover is a page to them: open the real book to show the difference.
notes: Model with a real picture book before handing out the sheet: touch the cover, the spine and the pages as the class says each word. Each picture shows the same closed book, with an arrow pointing to the cover, the spine or the edges of the pages. Read the three labels aloud while children point to them. The stretch can be done the next day.

## Warm up: Touch the parts
text: (Read aloud.) Touch the cover of a real book. Run your finger down its spine. Flip its pages.

# 21. Where Can We Find Out?
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can pick a good place to find the answer to a question.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the three helpers: A is a book, B is a grown-up, C is looking outside. I will read a question for each picture. Find the best place to find out, and write its letter, A, B or C, on the line.
mistakes: Many children choose B for everything, because a grown-up seems to know it all. Ask, "Could a grown-up know that for sure, or would they look it up?" For questions about right now (clouds, rain), looking outside is fastest and best.
notes: The five questions are printed under the pictures for you to read. Read each one twice while children point to its picture. Children who cannot yet write a letter can point to the helper and you can write it for them. Questions 1 and 5 are best answered by looking outside, 2 and 4 by a book, and 3 by a grown-up at school. Accept a good reason for another answer during discussion (a grown-up might know about seeds), but the key shows the best place.

## Warm up: Our three helpers
picword:
  - book | A: book
  - grown-up | B: grown-up
  - window | C: look outside

## Main: Where can we find out?
picword:
  - cloud | 1. {C}
  - star | 2. {A}
  - clock | 3. {B}
  - flower | 4. {A}
  - sun | 5. {C}
text: (Read aloud.) 1. Are there clouds in the sky right now? 2. How far away are the stars? 3. What time do we go to lunch today? 4. How does a seed grow into a flower? 5. Is the sun shining right now?

## Stretch: My question
draw: Draw something you wonder about. Write A, B or C to show where you could find out. | 1.2
