# 1. Parts of a Book Cut and Glue
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons, a real picture book to hold up
ican: I can name the cover, the spine and the pages of a book.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each book picture. The arrow points to one part. Listen as I read each label. Cut out the labels and glue each one under the picture it names.
mistakes: Children often call the spine the "side" or the "back." Hold up a real book, run a finger down the spine, and say "the spine holds the book together, like your backbone holds you up." Some children glue "pages" under the cover because the cover is a page to them: open the real book to show the difference.
notes: Model with a real picture book before handing out the sheet: touch the cover, the spine and the pages as the class says each word. Each picture shows the same closed book, with an arrow pointing to the cover, the spine or the edges of the pages. Read the three labels aloud while children point to them. The stretch can be done the next day.

## Warm up: Touch the parts
text: (Read aloud.) Touch the cover of a real book. Run your finger down its spine. Flip its pages.

# 7. Story Book or Fact Book? Listen and Circle
minutes: 15
materials: pencil or crayon, one story book and one fact book to show
ican: I can tell if a sentence comes from a story book or a fact book.
directions: (Read aloud.) The castle book is a story book. Stories can be make-believe. The magnifying glass book is a fact book. Fact books teach true things. Listen to each sentence. Circle the story book or the fact book for that number.
mistakes: Children may call any sentence about an animal a fact. "The pig put on a cape and flew over the moon" is about an animal, but pigs cannot fly: ask, "Could that really happen?" Talking toys and animals are a strong clue for a story. Some children circle both books in a pair; remind them to pick one.
notes: Hold up a real story book and a real fact book first, and name the picture on each sheet book: the castle for stories, the magnifying glass for facts. Read each sentence twice and pause while children circle in that number's pair. Key: 1 story (once upon a time, a magic hat), 2 fact (bees make honey from the nectar of flowers), 3 fact (the sun is a star), 4 story (pigs cannot fly), 5 fact (a frog begins life as a tadpole), 6 story (a teddy bear cannot talk). Accept a child's good reason in discussion, but the key shows the best answer.

## Main: Listen and circle
text: (Read aloud.) 1. Once upon a time, a little fox found a magic hat. 2. Bees make honey from flowers. 3. Our sun is a star. 4. The pig put on a cape and flew over the moon. 5. A frog starts life as a tadpole. 6. The teddy bear said, "Let's have a tea party!"

# 20. Ask About the Farm Picture
minutes: 15
materials: pencil or crayon
ican: I can find the part of a picture that answers a question.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the farm pictures. I will read four questions. Each question has a number in a thinking cloud. Draw a line from the number to the picture that answers it.
mistakes: Children may draw a line to the duck for "Where can the ducks swim?" because they hear "duck." Ask, "Is that a who or a where?" Point out that a where question needs a place. For the egg question, some pick the barn because hens live there; read the question again: what did the hen lay?
notes: Say the farm picture names together first: cow, horse, pond, egg, barn, tree, wagon, sun. Read each question twice and pause while children draw their line. Key: 1 (milk) to the cow; 2 (where ducks swim) to the pond; 3 (what the hen laid) to the egg; 4 (where the animals sleep at night) to the barn. The extra pictures (horse, tree, wagon, sun) are there so children must listen. Talk about the question words: who and what ask for a thing, where asks for a place.

## Main: Draw the lines
text: (Read aloud.) 1. Which animal gives us milk? 2. Where can the ducks swim? 3. What did the hen lay? 4. Where do the animals sleep at night?

# 21. Where Can We Find Out?
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can pick a good place to find the answer to a question.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the three helpers: A is a book, B is a grown-up, C is looking outside. I will read a question for each picture. Find the best place to find out, and write its letter, A, B or C, on the line.
mistakes: Many children choose B for everything, because a grown-up seems to know it all. Ask, "Could a grown-up know that for sure, or would they look it up?" For questions about right now (clouds, rain), looking outside is fastest and best.
notes: The five questions are printed under the pictures for you to read. Read each one twice while children point to its picture. Children who cannot yet write a letter can point to the helper and you can write it for them. Questions 1 and 5 are best answered by looking outside, 2 and 4 by a book, and 3 by a grown-up at school. Accept a good reason for another answer during discussion (a grown-up might know about seeds), but the key shows the best place.

  - sun | 5. {C}
text: (Read aloud.) 1. Are there clouds in the sky right now? 2. How far away are the stars? 3. What time do we go to lunch today? 4. How does a seed grow into a flower? 5. Is the sun shining right now?

## Stretch: My question

# 33. Title Page Maker
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can make a title page for our class book.
directions: (Read aloud.) This is the title page for our class book. Trace the title, Many Voices. Then listen: circle the place where the authors' names go. Last, draw an idea for our cover in the frame.
mistakes: Children may circle the title because it is the biggest. Ask, "Who wrote our book?" and point to the word "by." The authors go after "by." Some children trace only part of a letter; model slow tracing on the board first.
notes: The class book in this module is "Many Voices," a book every child adds a page to. Show a real title page first and point to the title, the authors after "by," and the publisher at the bottom. Key: children circle the line that says "by the children of Room ___" (the second row of the title page). Write your room number on the board for children to copy. The cover ideas are open; collect them and let the class vote, or use several on the front and back covers.
