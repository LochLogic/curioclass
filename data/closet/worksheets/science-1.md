# 1. Feel the Buzz
minutes: 25
materials: a 30 cm (12 inch) ruler for each pair (wood or plastic), a desk or table, pencil, crayons
ican: I can show that sound happens when something vibrates.
directions: (Read aloud.) Do each test with a partner. Feel and listen. Then draw what moves and circle your answers.
mistakes: Children often say the sound "comes out of" the ruler or the mouth without noticing anything moving. Ask them to touch and watch: what is shaking? A fast shake is hard to see but easy to feel.
notes: Show the ruler test first: press one end flat on the desk with one hand and gently flick the free end with the other. Keep faces back from the ruler. Typical result: a longer free end shakes slower and sounds lower; a shorter free end shakes faster and sounds higher. Children hum quietly with their fingertips resting lightly on the front of their own throat; nobody touches anyone else's throat.

## Warm up: Hum test
text: (Read aloud.) Rest your fingers on your throat. Hum a long "mmmm." Now stop.
choice: (Read aloud.) While you hum, your throat feels:
  - still
  * shaky, like a buzz
  - cold
choice: (Read aloud.) When you stop humming, the buzz:
  - gets bigger
  * stops
draw: Draw your hand on your throat while you hum. Draw lines to show the buzz. | 1.8

## Main: Ruler test
text: (Read aloud.) Hold one end of the ruler flat on the desk. Flick the other end. Watch and listen.
draw: Draw the ruler on the desk. Draw lines to show the part that moves. | 1.8
choice: (Read aloud.) Which part of the ruler moves?
  - the part pressed on the desk
  * the part sticking out past the desk
choice: (Read aloud.) Put your hand on the ruler to stop it. What happens to the sound?
  * The sound stops.
  - The sound gets louder.
choice: (Read aloud.) Slide more ruler past the edge and flick it again. The sound is:
  * lower
  - higher

## Stretch: Your turn
write: (Read aloud.) Name or draw something else that shakes when it makes a sound.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary: a drum, a guitar string, a rubber band, a bell, a speaker.

# 5. Loud or Soft, High or Low
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick; a drum and a small bell to demonstrate if you have them
ican: I can tell if a sound is loud or soft and high or low.
directions: (Read aloud.) Loud or soft tells how big a sound is. High or low tells its pitch: a mouse squeak is high, a lion's roar is low. Listen to each sound picture. Cut out the cards and glue each one in the box that fits both ways.
mistakes: Children often mix up loud with high and soft with low. The drum cards show the difference: the same drum is low whether you bang it (loud) or tap it (soft). Make each sound with your voice or an instrument: loud and soft first, then ask, "Is it high like a mouse or low like a lion?"
notes: Read every picture and card aloud before cutting. Make or play each sound if you can: siren and whistle (loud, high), tuba, thunder and a banged bass drum (loud, low), a chirping bird, a mewing kitten and a tiny bell (soft, high), a dog's quiet growl and a gently tapped bass drum (soft, low). A whisper is soft but has no clear high or low pitch, so it is in the warm up only.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) A whisper is
  * soft
  - loud
choice: (Read aloud.) A mouse squeak is
  - low
  * high

## Main: Sound pictures
picword:
  - siren | siren
  - whistle | whistle
  - tuba | tuba
  - thunder | thunder
  - drum | drum, banged hard
  - bird | bird chirping
  - cat | kitten mewing
  - bell | tiny bell
  - dog | dog's quiet growl
  - drum | drum, tapped gently

## Sort: Glue each sound in its box
sort:
  - Loud and high: siren; whistle
  - Loud and low: tuba; thunder; drum, banged hard
  - Soft and high: bird chirping; kitten mewing; tiny bell
  - Soft and low: dog's quiet growl; drum, tapped gently

# 16. Shadow Puppet Stage
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, crayons, scissors, tape, a craft stick or pencil for each puppet, a flashlight, a blank wall or a sheet of white paper taped to the wall
ican: I can show how a shadow changes when an object moves closer to the light.
directions: (Read aloud.) Draw an animal puppet in the box and cut it out. Tape it to a stick. Shine the flashlight on the wall and hold your puppet between the light and the wall. Move it closer to the light and watch the shadow. Then draw what you see.
mistakes: Children often expect the shadow to shrink as the puppet gets closer to the light, because the puppet gets closer to them. Keep the light still and move only the puppet. Some hold the puppet behind the light; it must be between the light and the wall.
notes: Draw the puppet as a solid outline (no holes) with a fat body so it is easy to cut. Darken the room if you can. Typical result: near the wall the shadow is about puppet-sized with sharp edges; close to the flashlight it is much bigger and fuzzier. A shadow forms because the puppet blocks the light. Remind children never to shine a flashlight into anyone's eyes.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) A shadow happens when something
  * blocks the light
  - makes its own light
choice: (Read aloud.) Where do you hold the puppet to make a shadow on the wall?
  - behind the flashlight
  * between the flashlight and the wall

## Main: Make your puppet
draw: Draw a big animal here. Cut it out. Tape it to a stick. | 2.6

## Main: Watch the shadow
draw: Puppet near the wall: draw its shadow. | 1.8
draw: Puppet close to the light: draw its shadow. | 1.8
choice: (Read aloud.) When the puppet moves closer to the light, the shadow gets
  * bigger
  - smaller
  - the same

## Stretch: Your turn
write: (Read aloud.) How could you make a tiny shadow of your puppet?
  lines: 1
  key: Move the puppet far from the light, close to the wall.

# 22. Bird Beak Buffet
minutes: 30
materials: for each group: tweezers, a clothespin, a plastic spoon, a small bowl each of dried beans, uncooked rice and water, three empty cups, a timer, paper towels, pencil
ican: I can tell how a bird's beak shape helps it get food.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each tool is a pretend bird beak. Pick up food with it for 20 seconds and drop what you get into a cup. Make a tally mark for each bean, each pinch of rice, and each spoonful of water you move. Then circle the tally of the best tool in each food column.
mistakes: Children may count every rice grain from a big spoonful and get tired: count one tally for each scoop or pinch of rice. Some groups let the tool touch the cup to drag food: food must be lifted.
notes: Beans and rice are uncooked and not to be eaten. Keep water low in a small bowl and have paper towels ready. Typical results: the clothespin and spoon do well with beans; the spoon wins for rice and is the only tool that carries water; tweezers move one grain or bean at a time. Any honest data is fine. The key's answers are the typical results. Tie it back to birds: a duck's flat bill scoops, a thin pointed beak picks small things one at a time, a thick strong beak grips and cracks seeds.

## Warm up: Meet the beaks
choice: (Read aloud.) Which tool is wide and can scoop?
  - tweezers
  * spoon
  - clothespin
choice: (Read aloud.) Which tool is thin and pointed?
  * tweezers
  - spoon
  - clothespin

## Main: Tally your food
table: Tool | Beans | Rice | Water
  - tweezers | | |
  - clothespin | | |
  - spoon | | |
fill:
  - (Read aloud.) Which tool was best for water? {spoon}
  - (Read aloud.) Which tool was best for rice? {spoon}
  - (Read aloud.) Which tool could not pick up water at all? {tweezers and clothespin}

## Stretch: Birds and beaks
choice: (Read aloud.) A duck has a wide, flat bill. Which tool is it most like?
  - tweezers
  * spoon
  - clothespin
write: (Read aloud.) A bird has a thin, pointed beak. What food might it pick up? Tell why.
  lines: 1
  key: Small things one at a time, like insects or seeds from small spaces, because a thin beak can pinch tiny food like the tweezers did.

# 27. Parts Check-In
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can name plant and animal parts and tell how they help.
directions: (Read aloud.) Write the name of each plant part the arrow points to. Then write the letter of each animal part's job. Last, draw an invention that copies a plant or animal part.
mistakes: Children often call the stem a "stick" or the roots "the bottom." Point to each part on a real plant if you can. For the matches, ask, "What does this part help the animal do?"
notes: Read the word bank, the parts and the jobs aloud. Invention ideas children may know: a hook-and-loop strap copies burrs that stick to fur; swim fins copy duck feet; a helmet copies a turtle shell; a grabber tool copies a bird's beak. Accept any invention that names the part it copies and what it helps people do.

## Part A: Label the plant
text: Word bank: roots, stem, leaf, flower
picword:
  - plant-arrow-roots | {roots}
  - plant-arrow-stem | {stem}
  - plant-arrow-leaf | {leaf}
  - plant-arrow-flower | {flower}

## Part B: Match the animal part to its job
match: Write the letter of each part's job.
  - a bird's beak = picks up food
  - a fish's fins = helps it swim
  - a turtle's shell = keeps it safe
  - a bird's wings = helps it fly

## Part C: Copy nature
draw: Draw an invention that copies a plant or animal part. | 2.4
write: (Read aloud.) What part does it copy? How does it help people?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Swim fins copy a duck's webbed feet so I can swim fast. Or: a helmet copies a turtle's shell to keep my head safe.

# 38. Moon Shapes in Order
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can put the moon's shapes in the order they happen.
directions: (Read aloud.) The moon seems to change shape over about a month. Cut out the four moon cards. Glue them in order, starting with the full moon: full moon, then half moon, then new moon, then the other half moon. Then the full moon comes back.
mistakes: Children often put the two half moons side by side. Ask, "Which way is the moon getting darker? Which way is it getting brighter?" After the full moon, the dark part grows from the right until the moon is all dark (new moon); then the bright part comes back from the right.
notes: The order shown is the one seen from the Northern Hemisphere: full moon, third quarter (left half lit), new moon (we cannot see it; the dark circle stands for it), first quarter (right half lit), then full again. On the key, moon-half-left is the moon with its left half bright and moon-half-right the moon with its right half bright. It takes about 29 and a half days to go all the way around. Children in the Southern Hemisphere see the half moons the other way around.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) A full moon looks like
  * a whole bright circle
  - a dark circle
choice: (Read aloud.) At new moon, we see
  - a whole bright circle
  * almost nothing: the moon looks dark

## Main: Cut and glue in order
sort:
  - 1. Full moon: icon:moon-full
  - 2. Half moon: icon:moon-half-left
  - 3. New moon: icon:moon-new
  - 4. Half moon: icon:moon-half-right

## Stretch: What comes next?
choice: (Read aloud.) After picture 4, which moon comes next?
  * full moon
  - new moon
draw: Draw the moon you saw last night, or the one in a picture your teacher shows. | 1.6
