# 1. Line Zoo
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons or markers, a thick marker if you have one
pairs: line-safari
ican: I can draw with different kinds of lines and name them.
directions: (Read aloud.) First, name each line and write its name. Then draw 6 animals for the Line Zoo. Use one kind of line for each animal. Write the name of the line under each animal.
mistakes: Children often switch line types halfway through an animal. Remind them each animal gets one kind of line all over: a zigzag lion has a zigzag mane, zigzag legs and a zigzag tail. Thick and thin are about the width of the line, not its length: press hard or use a marker for thick, a sharp pencil and a light touch for thin.
notes: Brainstorm matches before drawing: a wavy snake or fish, a zigzag crocodile or porcupine, a spiral snail, a dotted ladybug or leopard, a thick elephant, a thin spider. Any animal is fine as long as the line choice is clear. Write the word bank on the board for spelling.

text: Word bank: **wavy, zigzag, spiral, dotted, thick, thin**

## Stretch: Tell about it
write: (Read aloud.) Which line made the best animal? Why does it fit that animal?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer links the line to the animal: "Wavy lines fit my snake because snakes wiggle." "Zigzag lines make my crocodile look sharp."

# 5. Secondary Color Recipes
minutes: 30
materials: red, yellow and blue crayons, pencil
ican: I can name the two primary colors that mix to make orange, green and violet.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each recipe card makes a new color. Write the two primary colors that go in it. Then test each recipe: color one crayon lightly in the box, then color the other crayon on top. Did you make the new color?
mistakes: Children often write "orange" as an ingredient of orange or guess green comes from blue and white. Keep the word bank to red, yellow and blue: every recipe uses two of those three. For the test, color lightly with the lighter crayon (yellow) first, then layer the darker one; pressing hard with the first crayon makes a waxy layer the second cannot cover.
notes: Key: orange = red + yellow; green = yellow + blue; violet = red + blue (either order is right; accept "purple" for violet). Crayon mixes look duller than paint mixes, and red plus blue crayon often comes out a dark brownish violet; that is a real result worth talking about, not a mistake. Primary colors cannot be mixed from other colors; secondary colors are mixed from two primaries.

## Warm up: Primary colors
text: (Read aloud.) Word bank: **red, yellow, blue**
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is a primary color?
  - orange
  * blue
  - green
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is **not** a primary color?
  - red
  - yellow
  * violet

## Main: Recipe cards
text: (Read aloud.) Write the two primary colors in each recipe.
fill:
  - {red} + {yellow} = orange
  - {yellow} + {blue} = green
  - {red} + {blue} = violet

## Test: Try each recipe
chart: My crayon test
  labels: Orange; Green; Violet
  height: 1.1
text: (Read aloud.) Look at your tests. Did each one make the new color?
chart: Yes, it worked! | Sort of | No
  labels: Orange; Green; Violet
  height: 0.45
note: Answers vary with the crayons. Orange and green usually work well; violet often comes out dark or brownish.

## Stretch: A new recipe
write: (Read aloud.) What do you think red + yellow + blue makes? Try it in the corner of a box.
  lines: 1
  key: All three primaries together make a dark, muddy brown or gray-brown.

# 15. For Using or For Looking?
minutes: 30
materials: scissors, glue stick, pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell if a work of art was made mostly for using or mostly for looking.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each card. Cut out the cards. Glue each one under "Made for using" or "Made for looking." Then draw a pot you could use, and give it a pattern to look at.
mistakes: Some children think only paintings are art. Hold up a real basket or mug: someone designed and decorated it with care, and that makes it art too. Others put the quilt under "looking" because it is pretty; ask "What is it for? Do people sleep under it?"
notes: Key: made for using, a clay water pot, a woven basket, a quilt for a bed; made for looking, a painting of flowers, a statue in a park, a portrait of a person. Many cultures make useful things beautiful, and some useful things (old quilts, pots and baskets) now hang in museums just to be looked at, so the line is not always sharp. Accept a thoughtful "both" in discussion. Show a few photos first: a Pueblo pottery jar, a woven basket, a painting.

## Warm up: What is it for?
choice: (Read aloud.) A cup is made for...
  - looking only
  * using: you drink from it
  - nothing
choice: (Read aloud.) A painting on the wall is made for...
  - drinking from
  - sitting on
  * looking at

## Main: Cut and sort
sort: (Read aloud.) Glue each card in its column.
  - Made for using: a clay water pot, a woven basket, a quilt for a bed
  - Made for looking: a painting of flowers, a statue in a park, a portrait of a person

## Make: A pot for using and looking
draw: My pot with a pattern | 2.6
write: (Read aloud.) What would you keep in your pot?
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary: water, flowers, beans, pencils. Any use that fits a pot is right.

# 21. First, Next, Last Story Plan
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can plan a picture story with a beginning, a middle and an end.
directions: (Read aloud.) Number the three pictures in order. Then plan your own story about a fun day. Draw one sketch and write one word in each box.
mistakes: Children often draw three pictures of the same moment. Ask "What changed between First and Next?" Something should happen: arrive, play, go home. Keep sketches quick: this is a plan, not the final art.
notes: The fun day can be real or made up: the park, a snow day, a game, anything. Nobody needs to share family details. Model one plan on the board first (First: sled, Next: crash, Last: cocoa). The one word can be a noun or an action word.

## Warm up: What comes first?
text: (Read aloud.) Write 1, 2 or 3 to show the order.
fill:
  - {2} Build a snowman.
  - {3} Go inside and warm up.
  - {1} Put on boots and a coat.

## Main: My story plan
draw: First | 2
text: One word: ____________________
draw: Next | 2
text: One word: ____________________
draw: Last | 2
text: One word: ____________________

## Stretch: Give it a title
write: (Read aloud.) What is a good title for your story?
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary. A good title tells what the story is about: "The Big Snow Day" or "Our Game at the Park."

# 25. Happy Day, Gloomy Day
minutes: 35
materials: crayons (bright and dark colors), pencil
ican: I can use bright colors and dark colors to show a happy mood and a gloomy mood.
directions: (Read aloud.) Draw the same simple scene in both boxes: a house, a tree, the ground and the sky. Then color the first box with bright colors for a happy day. Color the second box with dark colors for a gloomy day.
mistakes: Children often draw two different pictures. Draw the scene in both boxes with the same few shapes first, and only then pick up the crayons. Others make the gloomy box just "black everywhere": show dark blue, gray, dark green and purple, and rain or clouds instead of the sun.
notes: Bright, happy colors: yellow, orange, light green, sky blue, pink. Dark, gloomy colors: gray, dark blue, dark purple, brown, black. Weather helps: sun for happy, clouds or rain for gloomy. Model on the board with the same house drawn twice. The sheet asks for drawing the scene because each child draws it; a printed scene would be too small to color.

## Warm up: Bright or dark?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which colors look bright and happy?
  * yellow and orange
  - gray and black
  - dark blue and brown
choice: (Read aloud.) Which weather feels gloomy?
  - a sunny sky
  * dark rain clouds
  - a rainbow

## Main: The same place, two moods
draw: Happy day: bright colors | 3.2
draw: Gloomy day: dark colors | 3.2

## Stretch: Which feels which?
write: (Read aloud.) Point to your gloomy day. Which colors did you use to make it feel gloomy?
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary. A good answer names dark colors: "I used gray and dark blue and made rain."

# 35. Handle with Care
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell safe ways and unsafe ways to carry and store art.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each picture. Circle the picture if it is a safe way to take care of art. Put an X on it if it is unsafe.
mistakes: Children may circle the cup because juice is nice to have. Ask "What could happen to the art?" Drips, crumples and rain all hurt art. Some will X the drawer: a flat drawer keeps art safe from bending.
notes: Read each card: wash hands first (safe, circle); walk slowly holding art flat (safe, circle); run with art (unsafe, X); store art flat in a drawer (safe, circle); a juice cup next to a painting (unsafe, X); stuff art in a backpack (unsafe, X); carry art out in the rain (unsafe, X); hold art by the edges (safe, circle). Four safe and four unsafe.

## Main: Circle or X
picword:
  - soap | wash hands first
  - walk | walk with art flat
  - run | run with art
  - drawer | store it flat
  - cup | juice next to art
  - backpack | stuff it in a bag
  - rain | carry it in rain
  - hand | hold the edges
note: Circle: wash hands first, walk with art flat, store it flat, hold the edges. X: run with art, juice next to art, stuff it in a bag, carry it in rain.

## Stretch: Your rule
write: (Read aloud.) Tell one rule to keep our art safe. Your teacher can write it.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary: "Wash your hands." "Carry it with two hands." "Keep drinks away from art."
