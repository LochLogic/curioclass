# 1. Traveling Moves Picture Dictionary
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can name and do 8 ways to travel across the floor.
directions: (Read aloud.) Trace each move word. In each box, draw a stick figure doing that move. Then match each move to the clue about how feet do it.
mistakes: Skip, gallop and slide are easy to mix up. Skip is step-hop with feet taking turns; gallop goes forward with the same foot always in front; slide goes sideways with the same foot leading. Hop and jump get mixed up too: a hop is one foot, a jump is two feet.
notes: Do each move together before children draw it, across the room in small groups so no one runs into anyone. Run only in a large clear space, or switch to "jog in place" in a small room. Leaps can be tiny: over a pretend puddle, not over real objects. Stick figures can show a move with motion lines, a bent knee or one foot up; a child who draws feet off the ground for jump has the idea.

## Stretch: How do the feet move?
match: (Read aloud.) Match each move to its clue.
  - hop = one foot up, land on the same foot
  - jump = two feet up, land on two feet
  - leap = push off one foot, fly, land on the other foot
  - skip = step, hop, step, hop
  - slide = go sideways, step together

# 7. Mirror Me Partner Shapes
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space, a partner
ican: I can copy my partner's shape like a mirror.
directions: (Read aloud.) Face your partner. One of you makes a shape. The other copies it like a mirror. Draw both shapes. Then circle yes or no: did they match exactly?
mistakes: Children often copy a shape after it is finished instead of moving with the leader. Have leaders move very slowly so the mirror can follow at the same time. A child who draws two stick figures facing each other with arms up on the same side of the page has the mirror idea.
notes: Model it first with a child: you lead with slow arm moves while the class mirrors you. Pairs stand an arm's length apart with no touching. Switch leaders after each shape. "Matched exactly" means same level, same arms, same legs; a pair that almost matched can still circle no and say what was different. There are no wrong drawings in Part B.

## Warm up: What is a mirror shape?
example: You lift one arm up high. Your mirror partner lifts an arm up high too, at the same time.
choice: (Read aloud.) You make a small, low shape. Your mirror partner makes
  - a big, tall shape
  * a small, low shape
  - no shape at all
choice: (Read aloud.) How should the leader move so the mirror can follow?
  * slowly
  - very fast
  - by running away
choice: (Read aloud.) Mirror partners stand
  - back to back
  - far across the room
  * facing each other

## Main: Draw 3 pairs of mirror shapes
text: (Read aloud.) Draw stick figures. Draw your shape on the left and your partner's mirror shape on the right. Circle yes or no.

## Stretch
write: (Read aloud.) What helped you and your partner match?

# 20. Strong or Light Energy
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue, open floor space
ican: I can show a feeling with strong or light energy.
directions: (Read aloud.) Cut out the move words and glue each one under strong or light. Then look at each feeling picture. Circle strong or light, and show the move with your arms.
mistakes: Children may think strong means fast and light means slow. Strong energy can be slow, like pushing a heavy wall; light energy can be quick, like fluttering fingers. Some children make every arm move the same: ask "Show me your mad arms. Now your calm arms."
notes: Practice before the sheet: push a pretend heavy wall (strong), then float a pretend feather (light). After each feeling in Part B, the class stands and shows the arm move together. Feelings do not always have one energy; if a child circles the other answer and shows a matching arm move with a good reason, accept it.

# 21. Story Time Improvise
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can make up moves that show the beginning, middle and end of a story.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story. Dance it with me as I read. Then answer the story questions and circle the moves you made.
mistakes: Children often do the same big move for the whole story. Read it a second time slowly and pause at each part: "Show me curled up small. Now show me pushing up." Some children circle every word: ask "Which one did your body really do?"
notes: Read the story once while children listen, then again while they improvise in their own space. There are no wrong moves in Part B; the circles are each child's own. Children who cannot get down to the floor can curl up small while sitting or standing.

## Warm up: The story
passage: The Little Seed
  A little seed sleeps under the ground. It is curled up small and still.
  Then the rain comes, drip, drop. The sun comes out, warm and bright. The seed pushes up, up, up. It grows a stem and stretches its leaves wide.
  The wind blows, and the tall flower sways from side to side. At last the flower opens wide to the sun.

## Story questions
choice: (Read aloud.) At the beginning, the seed is
  * curled up small
  - tall and wide
  - blowing in the wind
choice: (Read aloud.) In the middle, the seed
  - falls asleep
  * pushes up and grows
  - rolls away
choice: (Read aloud.) At the end, the flower
  - curls up small
  - melts
  * opens wide

## Main: Circle the moves you made
text: (Read aloud.) Circle one move you made in each row.
table: Part of the story | Move | Move | Move | Move
  - Beginning | curl up | shrink | hide | be still
  - Middle | push up | grow | stretch | reach
  - End | sway | spin | open wide | float

# 33. Dance Trip Map
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons, scissors, glue
ican: I can find where dances come from on a world map.
directions: (Read aloud.) We will take a pretend trip to 4 dances. Write the stop number by each dot on the map. Draw a line from stop 1 to 2 to 3 to 4. Then cut out the pictures and glue each one under its stop.
mistakes: Children may connect the dots in any order; the trip goes 1, 2, 3, 4 in the order the stops are listed. The Philippines and Ireland are islands, so their dots sit off the big land shapes; that is right.
notes: The map is simplified: land shapes are rough, and small islands (like the Philippines, Ireland and Japan) are left off, so those dots sit on their own. Read each stop aloud and point to its dot: stop 1, the Philippines, is the dot in the far right sea near Asia; stop 2, Mexico, is in North America; stop 3, Ireland, is the dot near the top of Europe; stop 4, Ghana, is in Africa. Pictures: two bamboo poles for tinikling from the Philippines, where dancers hop between poles that clap together; a ruffled skirt for the jarabe tapatío from Mexico, where dancers swirl wide skirts; a hard dance shoe for Irish step dance, with fast tapping feet and still arms; a drum for Ghana, where dances such as Adowa move to drumming. Show a short video or photo of each dance if you can.

## Warm up: The 4 stops
example: Stop 1: the Philippines. Stop 2: Mexico. Stop 3: Ireland. Stop 4: Ghana.
choice: (Read aloud.) In the Philippines, tinikling dancers hop between two
  - drums
  * bamboo poles
  - hats
choice: (Read aloud.) In Irish step dance, the feet tap fast and the arms
  - wave over the head
  - spin in circles
  * stay still at the sides
