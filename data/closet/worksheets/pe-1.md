# 1. Skip: Step and Hop
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell that a skip is a step and a hop on the same foot.
directions: (Read aloud.) Trace the words. Finish the footprint strip with step or hop. Then circle the 2 pictures that show a real skip.
mistakes: The most common mix-up is stepping on one foot and hopping on the other, which turns the skip into a run or a gallop. Say "step-hop, same foot" and have children whisper it while they practice. Arms help too: the arm opposite the lifted knee swings up.
notes: Practice in a line before the sheet: step on the left foot and hop on the left foot, then step on the right foot and hop on the right foot. In the strip the left foot is drawn with its big toe on the right side; point out that the foot changes after every hop. Key for the strip: step, hop, step, hop. The jump picture uses two feet and the walk picture never leaves the ground, so neither is a skip.

## Warm up: Trace
trace: step hop

## Main: Footprint strip
text: (Read aloud.) A skip is a step and a hop on the same foot. Then a step and a hop on the other foot. Say it as you point: step, hop, step, hop.
picword:
  - footprint-left | step
  - footprint-left | hop
  - footprint-right | step
  - footprint-right | hop
  - footprint-left | {step}
  - footprint-left | {hop}
  - footprint-right | {step}
  - footprint-right | {hop}

## Main: Find the skip
text: (Read aloud.) Look for one knee up and the other arm swinging up. Circle the 2 real skips.
picword:
  - skip | A
  - jump | B
  - skip-left | C
  - walk | D
fill: (Read aloud.) The real skips are pictures {A} and {C}.

## Stretch
choice: (Read aloud.) You step on your left foot. Which foot do you hop on next?
  * Left foot
  - Right foot

# 7. Movement Sentence Builder
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can put a movement, a pathway and a speed together and do it.
directions: (Read aloud.) Name the 3 pathways. Cut out the cards. Pick one move card, one path card and one speed card, and glue them in a row to build a movement sentence. Build 3 sentences. Then do them for the class.
mistakes: Children often glue two move cards in one row, or skip the speed card. Have them check each row: one move, one path, one speed. When they perform, the pathway gets lost first; tape the three pathways on the floor so they can follow a line.
notes: Warm-up key: straight, curvy, zigzag. Any mix of one move, one path and one speed makes a correct sentence; read each one aloud with the frame "I will hop on a zigzag path, slow." Medium speed means in between fast and slow. Perform in small groups so everyone moves at once, and have the watchers guess each sentence. Keep a few spare cards for children who lose one.

# 20. Partner Throw Check
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, one ball or beanbag for each pair
ican: I can watch a partner throw and check the cues for an overhand throw.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the 4 cue pictures and say each cue. Watch your partner throw 3 times. After each throw, make a check for every cue your partner did. Then switch jobs.
mistakes: Watchers try to check all 4 cues during one fast throw and guess. Have the thrower freeze at the end of each throw, and let watchers look for only 1 or 2 cues at first. The step is the cue most often missed: many children step with the foot on the same side as the throwing hand.
notes: The four cues: 1 point at the target with your other hand (side to the target); 2 arm way back, ball up high; 3 step with the foot on the other side from your throwing hand; 4 follow through, throwing arm comes down across your body. Checks will vary with each pair; there is no right number. Warm-up key: a right-handed thrower steps with the left foot (a left-handed thrower steps with the right). Remind watchers that a check sheet is to help, not to judge: say what your partner did well first.

## Warm up: The 4 cues
picword:
  - hand | 1 point
  - throw-overhand | 2 arm back
  - footprint-left | 3 step
  - throw-follow | 4 follow
choice: (Read aloud.) You throw with your right hand. Which foot steps forward?
  * Left foot
  - Right foot

## Main: Check my partner
table: Cue | Throw 1 | Throw 2 | Throw 3
  height: 0.5
  - 1 Point at the target | | |
  - 2 Arm way back | | |
  - 3 Step with the other foot | | |
  - 4 Follow through | | |
write: (Read aloud.) Which cue did your partner do every time?
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary. The student names a cue with 3 checks, for example: "Arm way back."
write: (Read aloud.) Pick one cue for your partner to practice. Tell them in a kind way.
  lines: 2
  starter: Next time, try to ...
  key: Answers vary. Example: Next time, try to step with your other foot.

  - Up over your head, and it stops
  * Down and across your body

# 21. Underhand or Overhand?
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can tell an underhand throw from an overhand throw.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture card and say its name. Cut out the word cards at the bottom. Glue each one under underhand or overhand.
mistakes: Children sort by the ball instead of the arm: softball pitch and baseball throw both use a small ball, but the pitch swings low (underhand) and the baseball throw starts high (overhand). Have them stand and act out each one before they glue it.
notes: Name every picture together first; the cap stands for baseball, and the egg toss is the field day game where partners toss an egg back and forth. Cues to say aloud: underhand means the arm swings back and then forward low, below the waist, and the ball leaves the hand in front; overhand means the arm goes back up high, above the shoulder, and comes over the top. In both throws you step with the foot opposite your throwing hand.

## Warm up: Watch the arm
example: (Read aloud.) **Underhand:** your arm swings low, below your waist, like rolling a bowling ball.
  **Overhand:** your arm starts up high, above your shoulder, like throwing a baseball far.

## Main: Picture cards
picword:
  - bowling | bowling
  - softball | softball pitch
  - horseshoe | horseshoes
  - beanbag | beanbag toss
  - egg | egg toss
  - cap | baseball throw
  - football | football pass
  - javelin | javelin throw

## Sort: Underhand or overhand
sort: Glue each word card under its throw.
  - Underhand: bowling, softball pitch, horseshoes, beanbag toss, egg toss
  - Overhand: baseball throw, football pass, javelin throw

## Stretch
choice: (Read aloud.) You want to throw a ball far, all the way across the field. Which throw is best?
  - Underhand
  * Overhand

# 33. Fair Play Choices
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can make a fair choice when I play games with others.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture and listen to its story. Circle the fair choice.
mistakes: Some children circle the choice that helps them win. Before each item, ask "Would this be fair to everyone in the game?" Others think telling the truth about a ball that went out means their team loses; praise the honesty out loud when you play.
notes: Key: 1 A (warm up), then 2 B, 3 A, 4 B, 5 A. Read each story slowly and both choices twice. Picture 1 is a line of children, picture 2 a ball, picture 3 a child running in tag, and picture 4 a sad face for losing. Act out one story with a volunteer, then ask the class to show the fair choice. Link to the class rules for games.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) What does fair play mean?
  * Following the rules and being kind
  - Winning every time

## Main: Circle the fair choice
picword:
  - people | 1
  - ball | 2
  - run | 3
  - face-sad | 4
choice: (Read aloud.) You come late to the line for the game. Your friend is at the front. What is fair?
  - Cut in next to your friend.
  * Go to the back of the line.
choice: (Read aloud.) You kick the ball and it rolls out of the lines. No one else saw it. What is fair?
  * Say "It went out" and give the ball to the other team.
  - Keep playing like it stayed in.
choice: (Read aloud.) In tag, a friend says she tagged you. You are not sure. What is fair?
  - Shout "No, you did not!" and keep running.
  * Say "Okay" and take your turn being it.
choice: (Read aloud.) Your team loses the game. What is fair?
  * Say "Good game" to the other team.
  - Say "That was not fair!" and stomp away.

## Stretch
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw yourself being a good sport in a game. | 1.8
