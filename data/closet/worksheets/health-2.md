# 1. How Germs Travel
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can show how germs travel and where washing stops them.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the story. Number the pictures to show the germs' trip. Draw arrows from 1 to 2 to 3 to 4. Then draw a soap bubble on the 2 arrows where washing hands would stop the germs.
mistakes: Students may put the bubble on the sneeze or the mouth itself. Washing happens on the trip between pictures: after Jay's hand catches the sneeze, and before Nina's hand goes to her mouth. Some say "wash the doorknob"; cleaning the doorknob helps too, so praise the idea, but it is cleaning, not handwashing.
notes: Germs can also travel straight through the air from a sneeze, which is why covering with an elbow or a tissue matters (CDC). Act out the story with a pretend doorknob and a glitter-on-hands demo if you have one: the glitter spreads just like germs. Keep the tone light; germs are a fact of life, and washing is the superpower.

## Warm up: The story
text: (Read aloud.) Jay sneezes into his hand. Then he opens the door. Later, Nina opens the same door. She bites her nails, and the germs go into her mouth.

## Main: The germs' trip
picword:
  - door | doorknob {3}
  - sneeze | sneeze {1}
  - mouth | Nina's mouth {4}
  - hand | Jay's hand {2}
fill:
  - Washing hands stops germs between picture {2} and picture {3}.
  - Washing hands also stops germs between picture {3} and picture {4}.

## Stretch: Cover it
choice: (Read aloud.) What should Jay do the next time he has to sneeze?
  - Sneeze into his hand and keep playing
  * Sneeze into his elbow or a tissue
  - Sneeze toward a friend
write: (Read aloud.) Why is the elbow a better place for a sneeze than the hand?
  lines: 2
  key: Your elbow does not touch doorknobs, toys or food, so the germs do not get passed along. Hands touch everything.

# 21. Parts of a Real Apology
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can make a real apology with all three parts.
directions: (Read aloud.) A real apology has 3 parts. Read each apology. Write yes or no for each part. Then fix 2 weak apologies so they have all three parts.
mistakes: Students often count "sorry" alone as a full apology. Point to part 2: does the person show they understand how the other one felt? Watch for apologies that add a "but" ("I'm sorry, but you started it"); a but takes the apology back.
notes: The three parts used here: 1. say what you did, 2. say how it made the other person feel, 3. say how you will make it right or what you will do next time. Practice saying them in a kind voice; a real apology also needs a real face and tone. The other person does not have to say "it's okay" right away, and that is fine.

## Warm up: The 3 parts
example: "I'm sorry I knocked over your tower. (1. what I did) That made you sad because you worked hard. (2. how you felt) Let me help you build it again. (3. make it right)"

## Main: Find the parts
table: Apology | 1. Says what I did? | 2. Says how you felt? | 3. Makes it right?
  - A. "I'm sorry I took your seat. You were upset. I'll move back." | {yes} | {yes} | {yes}
  - B. "Sorry." | {no} | {no} | {no}
  - C. "I'm sorry I called you a name. Next time I will use kind words." | {yes} | {no} | {yes}
  - D. "I'm sorry I grabbed the ball. That was frustrating for you." | {yes} | {yes} | {no}

## Fix it: Make it real
write: Theo bumped into Sara and she dropped her paint. Theo said, "Sorry, but you were in my way." Write a real apology for Theo.
  lines: 3
  starter: I'm sorry I ...
  key: I'm sorry I bumped into you and made you drop your paint. That was frustrating for you. I'll help you clean it up and get new paint.
write: Ana forgot to save a seat for Max like she promised. She said, "Sorry." Write a real apology for Ana.
  lines: 3
  starter: I'm sorry I ...
  key: I'm sorry I forgot to save you a seat when I promised. You must have felt left out. Tomorrow I will save you a seat first thing.
