# 1. How Germs Travel
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can show how germs travel and where washing stops them.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the story. Number the pictures to show the germs' trip. Draw arrows from 1 to 2 to 3 to 4. Then draw a soap bubble on the 2 arrows where washing hands would stop the germs.
mistakes: Students may put the bubble on the sneeze or the mouth itself. Washing happens on the trip between pictures: after Jay's hand catches the sneeze, and before Nina's hand goes to her mouth. Some say "wash the doorknob"; cleaning the doorknob helps too, so praise the idea, but it is cleaning, not handwashing.
notes: Germs can also travel straight through the air from a sneeze, which is why covering with an elbow or a tissue matters (CDC). Act out the story with a pretend doorknob and a glitter-on-hands demo if you have one: the glitter spreads just like germs. Keep the tone light; germs are a fact of life, and washing is the superpower.

## Warm up: The story
text: (Read aloud.) Jay sneezes into his hand. Then he opens the door. Later, Nina opens the same door. She bites her nails, and the germs go into her mouth.

## Main: The germs' trip
picword:
  - door | doorknob {3}
  - sneeze | sneeze {1}
  - mouth | Nina's mouth {4}
  - hand | Jay's hand {2}
fill:
  - Washing hands stops germs between picture {2} and picture {3}.
  - Washing hands also stops germs between picture {3} and picture {4}.

## Stretch: Cover it
choice: (Read aloud.) What should Jay do the next time he has to sneeze?
  - Sneeze into his hand and keep playing
  * Sneeze into his elbow or a tissue
  - Sneeze toward a friend
write: (Read aloud.) Why is the elbow a better place for a sneeze than the hand?
  lines: 2
  key: Your elbow does not touch doorknobs, toys or food, so the germs do not get passed along. Hands touch everything.

# 7. Why Habits Matter Sentences
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain why a healthy habit matters.
directions: (Read aloud.) Match each habit to the reason it matters. Read the model sentence. Then finish 3 sentence frames to explain why habits matter. Use the word "because" and tell what the habit does for the body.
mistakes: Students often repeat the habit as the reason ("People wash hands because they need to wash hands"). The part after "because" should tell what the habit does: removes germs, makes muscles strong, stops cavities. Watch for "because it's good" with no detail; ask, "Good for what?"
notes: Facts used: washing with soap removes germs that can make people sick (CDC); brushing removes plaque, a sticky film of germs that can cause cavities (American Dental Association); sleep helps the body grow and the brain remember what it learned; exercise makes the heart, lungs and muscles stronger. Keep examples about "people" and story characters, not about any child's own habits at home.

## Warm up: Habit and reason
match: Match each habit to why it matters.
  - Washing hands with soap = It removes germs that can make people sick.
  - Brushing teeth = It cleans off plaque that can cause cavities.
  - Getting enough sleep = It helps the body grow and the brain remember.
  - Running and playing = It makes the heart and muscles stronger.

## Model
example: **Habit:** brushing teeth. **Frame:** People brush their teeth because ___.
  **Sentence:** People brush their teeth because brushing cleans off plaque that can cause cavities.

## Main: Finish the frames
write: Handwashing
  lines: 2
  starter: People wash their hands because
  key: People wash their hands because soap and water wash away germs that can make them sick.
write: Sleep
  lines: 2
  starter: Kids need sleep every night because
  key: Kids need sleep every night because sleep helps their bodies grow and helps their brains remember what they learned.
write: Exercise
  lines: 2
  starter: People exercise because
  key: People exercise because moving makes their hearts, lungs and muscles stronger.

## Stretch: Your own frame
write: Choose another healthy habit, such as drinking water or eating vegetables. Write a sentence that tells why it matters.
  lines: 3
  starter: People ... because
  key: Answers vary. Models: People drink water because the body needs water to work and to stay cool. People eat vegetables because vegetables have vitamins that help the body grow and stay healthy.

# 20. Conflict Fix-It Steps
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can use five steps to solve a conflict with a friend.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read the five step cards. Write each card's letter on the number line to show the order. Then read the recess story and write what Mateo and Ivy could do at each step.
mistakes: Students jump straight to "choose" ("Just take turns!") and skip naming feelings and listening. Ask, "How does Ivy feel? Did Mateo hear her side?" Brainstorm means more than one idea; push for at least two before choosing.
notes: The steps: 1 stop and calm down, 2 name your feelings, 3 listen to each other, 4 brainstorm ideas, 5 choose a fair idea and try it. Practice with puppets or two volunteers before writing. If a conflict involves hurting, threats or the same child being picked on again and again, it is not a fix-it conflict: children should get a grown-up right away.

## Main: Put the steps in order
timeline: 1 5 1
  - 1 | Stop and calm down. Take a deep breath.
  - 2 | Name your feelings: "I feel ___ because ___."
  - 3 | Listen to each other without interrupting.
  - 4 | Brainstorm ideas that could work for both.
  - 5 | Choose a fair idea and try it.

## Use the steps
text: (Read aloud.) At recess, Mateo and Ivy both grab the red ball at the same time. "I had it first!" says Mateo. "No, I did!" says Ivy. They both pull.
write: Step 1: How can they stop and calm down?
  lines: 1
  key: They both let go of the ball (or set it down) and take a deep breath.
write: Step 2: What could Ivy say to name her feeling?
  lines: 1
  key: "I feel frustrated because I wanted to play four square with the red ball."
write: Step 3: What does listening look like here?
  lines: 1
  key: Mateo looks at Ivy and lets her finish talking, then Ivy lets Mateo finish.
write: Step 4: Brainstorm two ideas.
  lines: 2
  key: They could play with the ball together; they could take turns, 5 minutes each; they could ask a friend to join and play a game for three.
write: Step 5: Which idea is the most fair? Why?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Model: Play four square together, because then both of them get to use the ball the whole recess.

## Stretch
choice: (Read aloud.) Mateo and Ivy chose to take turns, but Mateo keeps the ball the whole recess. What is the best next step?
  - Ivy grabs the ball and runs away.
  - Ivy never plays with Mateo again.
  * Ivy reminds Mateo of their plan, and if that does not work, she tells a recess helper.

# 21. Parts of a Real Apology
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can make a real apology with all three parts.
directions: (Read aloud.) A real apology has 3 parts. Read each apology. Write yes or no for each part. Then fix 2 weak apologies so they have all three parts.
mistakes: Students often count "sorry" alone as a full apology. Point to part 2: does the person show they understand how the other one felt? Watch for apologies that add a "but" ("I'm sorry, but you started it"); a but takes the apology back.
notes: The three parts used here: 1. say what you did, 2. say how it made the other person feel, 3. say how you will make it right or what you will do next time. Practice saying them in a kind voice; a real apology also needs a real face and tone. The other person does not have to say "it's okay" right away, and that is fine.

## Warm up: The 3 parts
example: "I'm sorry I knocked over your tower. (1. what I did) That made you sad because you worked hard. (2. how you felt) Let me help you build it again. (3. make it right)"

## Main: Find the parts
table: Apology | 1. Says what I did? | 2. Says how you felt? | 3. Makes it right?
  - A. "I'm sorry I took your seat. You were upset. I'll move back." | {yes} | {yes} | {yes}
  - B. "Sorry." | {no} | {no} | {no}
  - C. "I'm sorry I called you a name. Next time I will use kind words." | {yes} | {no} | {yes}
  - D. "I'm sorry I grabbed the ball. That was frustrating for you." | {yes} | {yes} | {no}

## Fix it: Make it real
write: Theo bumped into Sara and she dropped her paint. Theo said, "Sorry, but you were in my way." Write a real apology for Theo.
  lines: 3
  starter: I'm sorry I ...
  key: I'm sorry I bumped into you and made you drop your paint. That was frustrating for you. I'll help you clean it up and get new paint.
write: Ana forgot to save a seat for Max like she promised. She said, "Sorry." Write a real apology for Ana.
  lines: 3
  starter: I'm sorry I ...
  key: I'm sorry I forgot to save you a seat when I promised. You must have felt left out. Tomorrow I will save you a seat first thing.

# 33. Goal or Wish?
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can tell a goal from a wish and turn a wish into a goal.
directions: (Read aloud.) A goal is something you can do, with a plan. A wish is something you hope for but cannot make happen by yourself. Cut out the 8 cards and glue each one under Goal or Wish. Then turn 2 wishes into goals.
mistakes: Students sort by how much they like the idea, not by whether a person can do it. Ask, "Can you do this yourself? Do you know when?" "I want to never get sick" sounds healthy but no one can control it; the goal version is something like washing hands before eating.
notes: A goal names an action you can take and when, usually starting with "I will." A wish is a hope that depends on luck or things outside your control. The goal cards are written as story characters' goals in the first person; keep the talk on the cards, not on any child's own home.

## Main: Sort the cards
sort: Glue each card under Goal or Wish.
  - Goal: I will drink water at lunch; I will brush my teeth before bed; I will play outside after school; I will eat a fruit with breakfast
  - Wish: I want to never get sick; I wish I could run as fast as a cheetah; I want to win every game; I wish I never had to go to bed

## Turn a wish into a goal
example: **Wish:** I want to never get sick. **Goal:** I will wash my hands with soap before I eat.
write: Wish: I want to win every game.
  lines: 2
  starter: Goal: I will ...
  key: Answers vary. Model: I will practice kicking the ball for 10 minutes after school.
write: Wish: I wish I could run as fast as a cheetah.
  lines: 2
  starter: Goal: I will ...
  key: Answers vary. Model: I will run and play tag at recess every day so I get faster.

## Stretch
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is a goal?
  - I wish it was always summer.
  * I will ride my bike at the park on Saturday.
  - I hope I get a new bike.
