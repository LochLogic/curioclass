# 1. Net and Wall Games Compared
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare net and wall games by their court, scoring, serve rules and key shots.
directions: Fill in the blanks in the comparison chart. Then use the chart to answer the questions.
mistakes: Tennis scoring trips students up: the points go love, 15, 30, 40, then game, and at 40 to 40 (deuce) a player must win two points in a row. Students also assume every game uses rally scoring; in handball only the serving side can score a point.
notes: Rules are the common official ones: indoor volleyball (rally scoring, sets to 25 won by 2, a deciding set to 15), badminton (games to 21 won by 2, capped at 30), tennis, and American handball (one-wall or four-wall, games to 21, only the server scores). In the US, "handball" in a PE class can also mean team handball, which is an invasion game; this sheet means the wall game.

## Warm up
choice: What is the main goal in every net and wall game?
  - Carry the ball into the other team's end zone.
  - Hit the ball and run around the bases.
  * Send the ball or shuttle where the opponent cannot return it.
choice: Which game is played against a wall instead of over a net?
  - Badminton
  * Handball
  - Volleyball
  - Tennis

## Main: Comparison chart
table: Game | Court | Scoring | Serve rules | Key shots
  - Volleyball | 18 by 9 meters, net across the middle, 6 players a side | Rally scoring: a point on every rally; sets to {25}, win by 2 | Serve from behind the end line, one try; a team may touch the ball up to {3} times to return it | Forearm pass (bump), set, spike, block
  - Badminton | Long narrow court, net about 1.5 meters high; singles or doubles | Rally scoring; games to {21}, win by 2 | Serve underhand and {diagonally} into the opposite service court | Clear, drop shot, smash, net shot
  - Tennis | Rectangle court with a net; wider alleys for doubles | Points go love, 15, 30, {40}, game; 6 games win a set (win by 2 or a tiebreak) | Serve diagonally into the service box; two tries, and missing both is a {double fault} | Forehand, backhand, volley, lob, overhead smash
  - Handball | A front wall (one-wall) or a closed room (four-wall) | Games to {21}; only the {serving} side can score | Bounce the ball and hit it with the hand to the front wall so it lands back past the short line | Kill shot, pass shot, ceiling shot (four-wall)

## Main: Use the chart
write: Volleyball and badminton both use rally scoring. Explain what rally scoring means and how handball scoring is different.
  lines: 3
  key: In rally scoring a point is scored on every rally, by whichever side wins it, no matter who served. In handball only the serving side can score; if the receiver wins the rally, they just win the serve.
write: Volleyball is the only game on the chart where teammates can hit the ball more than once on their side. How does that change the way a team plays?
  lines: 3
  key: With up to three touches, a team can pass, set and then spike, building an attack together instead of returning the ball right away.
write: Pick one key shot from two different games that does the same job. Explain the job.
  lines: 2
  key: Example: the badminton drop shot and the tennis drop shot (or the handball kill shot) land short and low so the opponent cannot reach them in time; or the smash in badminton and tennis and the volleyball spike all hit down hard to end the rally.

## Stretch
write: Why do badminton and tennis both make the server hit diagonally? What would happen if the server could aim anywhere?
  lines: 3
  key: A diagonal serve travels farther and lands in a set box, which gives the receiver a fair chance to return it; if the server could aim anywhere, the serve would be too easy to win points with, and rallies would be short.

# 21. Plan vs Actual
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler, two colored pencils
ican: I can compare my planned and actual active minutes on a double line graph and explain the differences.
directions: Graph Avery's planned and actual minutes on the same grid, using a different color for each line and a key that shows which is planned and which is actual. Use the notes column to explain the differences. Then look at your own plan and log the same way.
mistakes: Students often plot one line and forget the key (legend), so a reader cannot tell which line is which. Others read the difference from the graph by eye instead of subtracting from the table. A week below the plan is not a failure; the notes column shows the reasons.
notes: The key graph shows Avery's actual line; the planned line rises 20 minutes a week from 300 to 420 and then stays at 420 for week 8. Differences (actual - planned): -10, +10, -30, +5, -60, +10, -25, +10. Avery met or beat the plan in weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8; the biggest gap was week 5 (60 minutes); the totals are 2,940 planned and 2,850 actual, 90 minutes short. The stretch uses each student's own plan and log, which stay private and are never compared.

## Stretch: My plan and log
write: Look at your own plan and your own log for the same weeks. Name one week your actual minutes were below your plan, the reason, and one change you will make.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. The student names a week from their own records, a specific reason, and a realistic change.
