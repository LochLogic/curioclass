# 1. Net and Wall Games Compared
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can compare net and wall games by their court, scoring, serve rules and key shots.
directions: Fill in the blanks in the comparison chart. Then use the chart to answer the questions.
mistakes: Tennis scoring trips students up: the points go love, 15, 30, 40, then game, and at 40 to 40 (deuce) a player must win two points in a row. Students also assume every game uses rally scoring; in handball only the serving side can score a point.
notes: Rules are the common official ones: indoor volleyball (rally scoring, sets to 25 won by 2, a deciding set to 15), badminton (games to 21 won by 2, capped at 30), tennis, and American handball (one-wall or four-wall, games to 21, only the server scores). In the US, "handball" in a PE class can also mean team handball, which is an invasion game; this sheet means the wall game.

## Warm up
choice: What is the main goal in every net and wall game?
  - Carry the ball into the other team's end zone.
  - Hit the ball and run around the bases.
  * Send the ball or shuttle where the opponent cannot return it.
choice: Which game is played against a wall instead of over a net?
  - Badminton
  * Handball
  - Volleyball
  - Tennis

## Main: Comparison chart
table: Game | Court | Scoring | Serve rules | Key shots
  - Volleyball | 18 by 9 meters, net across the middle, 6 players a side | Rally scoring: a point on every rally; sets to {25}, win by 2 | Serve from behind the end line, one try; a team may touch the ball up to {3} times to return it | Forearm pass (bump), set, spike, block
  - Badminton | Long narrow court, net about 1.5 meters high; singles or doubles | Rally scoring; games to {21}, win by 2 | Serve underhand and {diagonally} into the opposite service court | Clear, drop shot, smash, net shot
  - Tennis | Rectangle court with a net; wider alleys for doubles | Points go love, 15, 30, {40}, game; 6 games win a set (win by 2 or a tiebreak) | Serve diagonally into the service box; two tries, and missing both is a {double fault} | Forehand, backhand, volley, lob, overhead smash
  - Handball | A front wall (one-wall) or a closed room (four-wall) | Games to {21}; only the {serving} side can score | Bounce the ball and hit it with the hand to the front wall so it lands back past the short line | Kill shot, pass shot, ceiling shot (four-wall)

## Main: Use the chart
write: Volleyball and badminton both use rally scoring. Explain what rally scoring means and how handball scoring is different.
  lines: 3
  key: In rally scoring a point is scored on every rally, by whichever side wins it, no matter who served. In handball only the serving side can score; if the receiver wins the rally, they just win the serve.
write: Volleyball is the only game on the chart where teammates can hit the ball more than once on their side. How does that change the way a team plays?
  lines: 3
  key: With up to three touches, a team can pass, set and then spike, building an attack together instead of returning the ball right away.
write: Pick one key shot from two different games that does the same job. Explain the job.
  lines: 2
  key: Example: the badminton drop shot and the tennis drop shot (or the handball kill shot) land short and low so the opponent cannot reach them in time; or the smash in badminton and tennis and the volleyball spike all hit down hard to end the rally.

## Stretch
write: Why do badminton and tennis both make the server hit diagonally? What would happen if the server could aim anywhere?
  lines: 3
  key: A diagonal serve travels farther and lands in a set box, which gives the receiver a fair chance to return it; if the server could aim anywhere, the serve would be too easy to win points with, and rallies would be short.

# 7. Badminton Scoring Practice
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can keep score in badminton singles with rally scoring and tell who serves from which side.
directions: Read the scoring rules for singles. For each rally sequence, write the score after each rally, who serves next, and which service court (right or left) they serve from. Write the score with player A first.
mistakes: Students switch the serve after every rally, as in some backyard games; in badminton the winner of the rally serves next. Students also choose the side from the receiver's score, or from the total; the side comes from the server's own score: even (including 0) is right, odd is left. At 20-all, students end the game at 21; a player must lead by 2.
notes: Rules follow the Badminton World Federation Laws for singles: rally scoring (every rally scores a point for its winner), the rally winner serves next, the server serves from the right service court when their own score is even (0, 2, 4 ...) and from the left when it is odd, games to 21 with a 2-point lead needed from 20-all, and at 29-all the next point (the 30th) wins. Sequence 1 key: 1-0 A left, 2-0 A right, 2-1 B left, 2-2 B right, 3-2 A left, 3-3 B left. Sequence 2 key: 20-20 A right, 21-20 A left, 21-21 B left, 22-21 A right, 23-21 A wins. Stretch key: 28-28 A right, 28-29 B left, 29-29 A left, 30-29 A wins.

## Warm up: The rules
example: **Rally scoring:** the winner of every rally gets a point, whoever served. **Serve:** the winner of the rally serves next. **Side:** the server serves from the right service court when the server's own score is even (0, 2, 4 ...) and from the left when it is odd. **Winning:** first to 21, but at 20-all a player needs a 2-point lead. At 29-all, the next point wins (30 is the cap).
choice: The score is 7-4 and player B just won the rally. Who serves next?
  - Player A, because serves take turns
  * Player B, because the rally winner serves
  - Whoever has more points
choice: Player A is serving with a score of 12-9. Which court does A serve from?
  * Right, because A's score (12) is even
  - Left, because B's score (9) is odd
  - Left, because the total (21) is odd

# 20. Six-Week Fitness Log
minutes: 15 to start, then 5 after each session for 6 weeks
materials: pencil
ican: I can log my sessions with FITT details and an effort rating, and total each week.
directions: Read the FITT key and practice with Rae's sample week. Then log 3 or more sessions a week for 6 weeks. In each session box write the type, intensity (L, M or V), time in minutes and effort (1 to 10). At the end of each week, write the frequency (number of sessions) and the total minutes.
mistakes: Students mix up intensity and effort. Intensity is how hard the body works during the activity (use the talk test); effort is a 1 to 10 rating of how hard the whole session felt. Students also leave weekly totals blank and then cannot see a trend; total each week on the day it ends.
notes: Rae's key: frequency 3 sessions; total time 25 + 30 + 40 = 95 minutes; average effort (7 + 6 + 5) / 3 = 6; one vigorous session. FITT: frequency is how often, intensity how hard, time how long, type what kind. Talk test: light, you can sing; moderate, you can talk but not sing; vigorous, you can say only a few words. The effort scale is a version of the rating of perceived exertion (RPE). This is each student's own log; never post, rank or compare logs. Missed sessions are written as missed, not judged.

## Warm up: Rae's sample week
example: **F**requency: how many sessions. **I**ntensity: L light (can sing), M moderate (can talk, not sing), V vigorous (only a few words). **T**ime: minutes. **T**ype: the activity. **Effort:** how hard the whole session felt, 1 (very easy) to 10 (as hard as possible).

  - 2 | Bodyweight circuit | M | 30 | 6

write: Look at your weekly totals. Describe the trend in your minutes and in your effort ratings over the 6 weeks.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer uses the student's own numbers, for example: "My minutes went from 80 to 120, and my effort for the same jog dropped from 7 to 5, so the jog got easier."

## Stretch
write: Which FITT letter will you change for the next 6 weeks, and how? Keep the change small and safe.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: Time: I will add 5 minutes to each jog every 2 weeks, from 25 to 35 minutes.

# 21. Plan vs Actual
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, ruler, two colored pencils
ican: I can compare my planned and actual active minutes on a double line graph and explain the differences.
directions: Graph Avery's planned and actual minutes on the same grid, using a different color for each line and a key that shows which is planned and which is actual. Use the notes column to explain the differences. Then look at your own plan and log the same way.
mistakes: Students often plot one line and forget the key (legend), so a reader cannot tell which line is which. Others read the difference from the graph by eye instead of subtracting from the table. A week below the plan is not a failure; the notes column shows the reasons.
notes: The key graph shows Avery's actual line; the planned line rises 20 minutes a week from 300 to 420 and then stays at 420 for week 8. Differences (actual - planned): -10, +10, -30, +5, -60, +10, -25, +10. Avery met or beat the plan in weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8; the biggest gap was week 5 (60 minutes); the totals are 2,940 planned and 2,850 actual, 90 minutes short. The stretch uses each student's own plan and log, which stay private and are never compared.

# 33. Feedback Tone Check
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can judge coaching feedback for respect, specificity and encouragement, and improve it.
directions: Read the rubric. Then read each short coaching conversation between classmates practicing a volleyball serve. Rate each conversation from 1 to 3 on all three parts of the rubric. Then rewrite the weakest one.
mistakes: Students rate feedback as respectful just because it is positive; praise that is not true or not useful is still weak on specificity. Others call any blunt feedback disrespectful; direct is fine if it is about the skill and not the person. Rate each column on its own.
notes: Key ratings (respect, specificity, encouragement): 1: 1, 1, 1 (puts the person down, names no cue); 2: 3, 3, 3; 3: 3, 1, 3 (friendly but says nothing about the serve, which went into the net); 4: 2, 3, 1 (exact cue but no encouragement and a curt order). Accept a rating one point off with a sound reason. Best overall: 2. The rewrite should keep conversation 4's specific cue and add respect and encouragement, or turn conversation 1 into a cue-based tip.

## Warm up: The rubric
table: Score | Respect | Specificity | Encouragement
  - 3 | About the skill, never the person; calm and kind | Names an exact cue and what to change | Notes what went well and believes the player can improve
  - 2 | Not rude, but curt or impatient | Hints at the problem, but the cue is unclear | Neutral; no praise and no put-down
  - 1 | Insults, laughs at or blames the player | Vague: "do better," "that was wrong," or praise with no detail | Discouraging, or no reason to keep trying

## Main: Four conversations
text: **Conversation 1.** Theo serves into the net. Mia says, "Ugh, that was bad. Just do it better."
text: **Conversation 2.** Theo's serve lands short. Mia says, "Nice job watching the ball. Next time, follow through toward your target. You are really close!"
text: **Conversation 3.** Theo serves into the net. Mia says, "Great job! Awesome! You are the best!"
text: **Conversation 4.** Theo serves three low balls in a row. Mia says, "Your toss is too low every time, so you hit the ball late and it goes low. Fix it."
table: Conversation | Respect | Specificity | Encouragement
  - 1 | {1} | {1} | {1}
  - 2 | {3} | {3} | {3}
  - 3 | {3} | {1} | {3}
  - 4 | {2} | {3} | {1}
write: Which conversation gives Theo the most useful help? Use the rubric to explain.
  lines: 3
  key: Conversation 2: it is kind, it names one exact cue (follow through toward the target), and it tells Theo what went well and that he is close, so he knows what to change and wants to keep trying.

## Stretch: Rewrite it
write: Rewrite conversation 4 so it scores 3 in every column.
  lines: 3
  key: Example: "Your arm swing looks strong. Your toss is a little low, so you hit late and the ball stays low. Try tossing higher, just above your hitting shoulder, and see what happens. You will get it."
