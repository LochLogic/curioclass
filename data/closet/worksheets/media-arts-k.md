# 1. Seed to Flower Story Order
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, crayons
ican: I can put pictures in order to tell a story.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at the three pictures. Write 1 under what happens first, 2 under what happens next and 3 under what happens last. Cut out the pictures and glue them in the boxes in order. Then trace 1, 2, 3.
mistakes: Some children number the pictures left to right as they appear on the page instead of by what happens first. Ask, "What did the plant look like at the very start?" A child who puts the flower first may be thinking of picking a flower, not growing one: ask them to tell the story out loud.
notes: Name the pictures together first: a seed under the ground, a sprout poking up, a flower. Tell the finished story aloud as a class ("First the seed is in the ground. Next a sprout pops up. Last a flower blooms."). For a sound story, add a sound to each box: a soft tap for the seed going in, a "pop" for the sprout, a "ta-da" for the flower.

## Warm up: First, next, last
example: (Read aloud.) A story has a beginning, a middle and an end. First, next, last.

## Main: Number, cut and glue
text: (Read aloud.) Write 1, 2 or 3 under each picture.
picword:
  - flower | {3}
  - seed | {1}
  - sprout | {2}
draw: 1 First | 1.0
draw: 2 Next | 1.0
draw: 3 Last | 1.0

## Stretch: Trace
trace: 1 2 3

# 5. Make That Sound
minutes: 20
materials: pencil or crayon; a few real objects to try (a cup, a book, wooden blocks)
ican: I can choose things to make the sound in a story.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each sound. Look at the things in its row. Circle the 2 things you could use to make that sound. Then try one!
mistakes: Children may circle a thing that goes with the sound (a towel for a rainy day) instead of a thing that can make it. Say the row's sound again and ask, "Which of these could we use to make it?" Soft things (a pillow, a sock, a towel) make almost no sound; let children test one to hear why.
notes: The warm-up pictures show the three sounds to make; each row in Main is the things to choose from. Answers: rain, circle the hand (tap fingertips on the table) and the pencil (tap the eraser end lightly). Horse, circle the cup (two cups tapped upside down on the table go clip-clop) and the blocks (tap two wooden blocks). Door, circle the book (close a big book with a bang) and the drawer (slide it shut). Do not circle the pillow, sock, towel, teddy bear or leaf. Accept another choice if the child can show you how it makes the sound. Sound people in movies use tricks like these; they are called Foley artists. To finish, let pairs pick one row, make the sound and have the class guess it.

## Warm up: Three sounds
text: (Read aloud.) Say each sound with me. Tap your fingers for rain!
picword:
  - rain | pitter-patter
  - horse | clip-clop
  - door | bang!

## Main: Circle 2 in each row
text: (Read aloud.) 1. Rain: pitter-patter.
picword:
  - pillow | pillow
  - hand | hand
  - sock | sock
  - pencil | pencil
text: (Read aloud.) 2. A horse: clip-clop.
picword:
  - cup | cup
  - towel | towel
  - abc-blocks | blocks
  - teddy-bear | teddy bear
text: (Read aloud.) 3. A door: bang!
picword:
  - leaf | leaf
  - book | book
  - drawer | drawer
  - sock | sock
note: Circle: hand and pencil (rain); cup and blocks (horse); book and drawer (door).

# 15. Crew Jobs Match
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell what each helper on a media crew does.
directions: (Read aloud.) Here are 4 crew helpers. Each helper has a number. Look at each tool. Write the number of the helper who uses it.
mistakes: The microphone and the headphones are the ones children mix up. The presenter talks into the microphone so everyone can hear; the sound helper wears headphones to listen and check the sound. Let children put on pretend headphones (hands over ears) and talk into a pretend microphone (a fist) to feel the difference.
notes: Act out each job before the sheet. Camera person: holds the camera and says, "I see you!" Director: snaps the clapperboard and says, "Action!" Sound helper: puts on headphones and listens. Presenter: holds the microphone and talks to the audience. Answers: camera 1, clapperboard 2, headphones 3, microphone 4. In class projects, the same child may do more than one job.

## Warm up: Meet the crew
text: (Read aloud.) **1** camera person. **2** director. **3** sound helper. **4** presenter.
trace: 1 2 3 4

## Main: Who uses it?
text: (Read aloud.) Write the helper's number under each tool.
picword:
  - microphone | {4}
  - camera | {1}
  - headphones | {3}
  - clapperboard | {2}

## Stretch: My crew job
draw: Draw you doing one crew job. | 2.6
note: Answers vary. Ask the child to name the job and point to the tool in the picture.

# 21. Point and Tell Card
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can show my picture and tell about it.
directions: (Read aloud.) Draw a picture for your slide. Trace the words "This is." Then write or tell your teacher what your picture shows. Last, circle how to stand when you share.
mistakes: Children often turn to face their picture while they talk. Practice pointing to the picture with one hand while looking at the class. Some children will want to say a whole story: the frame "This is ___" is enough for now.
notes: Scribe the last word for children who are not writing yet, or let them say it to you. Use the finished cards for a "point and tell" circle: each child holds up the card, points and reads it. The cards can be photographed for the class slideshow.

## Warm up: Listen
example: (Read aloud.) Point to your picture. Look at your friends. Say, "This is my cat."

## Main: Draw and tell
draw: My slide picture | 3.4
trace: This is
write: (Read aloud.) Write or tell what your picture shows.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary: the name of what the child drew, such as "my cat" or "a big tree." The teacher may write the words the child says.

## Stretch: How do you stand?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which way should you stand when you share?
  * Face your friends and point to your picture.
  - Turn your back to your friends.
  - Hide your picture behind you.

# 25. How Did Our Crew Do?
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell how my crew worked together.
directions: (Read aloud.) Think about our crew. Circle one face in each row. Then draw your favorite slide from our show.
mistakes: Children sometimes circle the happy face every time, or circle how they feel today instead of how the crew worked. Read each row slowly and give an example: "Did we take turns with the camera?" A child who circles "not yet" is being honest; ask what would help next time.
notes: Read each row aloud and give children a moment to think before they circle. Talk about the "not yet" answers as a group without naming anyone: "Some of us said taking turns was hard. What could help?" Answers vary for every item; there is no right face. A strong drawing shows one slide clearly enough that a friend could name it.

## Warm up: Faces
example: (Read aloud.) Great! Okay. Not yet. Point to each face as I say it.

## Main: Circle one face
text: (Read aloud.) 1. We took turns.
picword:
  - face-happy | great
  - face-blank | okay
  - face-sad | not yet
text: (Read aloud.) 2. We helped each other.
picword:
  - face-happy | great
  - face-blank | okay
  - face-sad | not yet

## Draw
draw: My favorite slide | 2.4
note: Answers vary. Ask, "Why is this slide your favorite?"

# 35. Who Made It?
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell that people make the media I see.
directions: (Read aloud.) Every book, cartoon, sign and ad was made by someone. Look at the 4 makers. Write the maker's number under each thing they made.
mistakes: Children may say "the store" made the ad or "the TV" made the cartoon. Ask, "Who drew it? Who paid for it?" A company is the business that wants you to buy something; the town is the people who keep the roads safe.
notes: Answers: book 1 (an author wrote it), cartoon 2 (an artist drew it), street sign 3 (the town put it up), ad 4 (a company made it to sell something). Many people help make media: an illustrator draws the pictures in a book and a designer makes its cover, so accept other helpers if a child explains. The big idea is that someone made it, for a reason.

## Warm up: The makers
picword:
  - pencil | 1 author
  - paintbrush | 2 artist
  - town-hall | 3 town
  - factory | 4 company

## Main: Who made it?
text: (Read aloud.) Write the maker's number under each one.
picword:
  - tv | cartoon {2}
  - billboard | ad {4}
  - book | book {1}
  - stop-sign | street sign {3}

## Stretch: Why?
choice: (Read aloud.) Who made the ad? What do they want?
  - The town wants you to stop.
  * The company wants you to buy something.
  - The author wants you to read.
