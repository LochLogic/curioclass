# 1. Push or Pull? Picture Sort
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick, a ball and a small toy wagon or box with a string to demonstrate
ican: I can tell a push from a pull.
directions: (Read aloud.) A push moves something away from you. A pull moves it toward you. Listen to each picture's words. Cut out the cards and glue each one under Push or Pull.
mistakes: Children often sort by the object instead of the action (both drawer cards go together). Act out each card with them: a push moves something away from you, a pull moves it toward you. A wagon can be pushed from behind; if a child says push and shows how, accept it.
notes: Read every card aloud and point to its picture before cutting, since the cards carry words, not pictures. Hold up each finished card and have the class act it out with their hands before gluing. A quick demo first: push a ball away across the floor, then pull a toy back by its string.

## Main: Look and listen
picword:
  - ball | kick a ball
  - light-switch | press a switch
  - drawer | shut a drawer
  - cart | roll a cart
  - drawer | open a drawer
  - sock | put on a sock
  - flower | pick a flower
  - wagon | tow a wagon

## Sort: Push or pull?
sort:
  - Push: kick a ball, press a switch, shut a drawer, roll a cart
  - Pull: open a drawer, put on a sock, pick a flower, tow a wagon

# 5. Bump! What Happens Next?
minutes: 20
materials: pencil; to demonstrate: a playground ball, a wall, a light wooden block or small box, a pillow, a few blocks stacked into a tower
ican: I can tell what happens when a moving thing bumps into something.
directions: (Read aloud.) Each ball is rolling toward something. Look at the picture. Listen to the two choices. Circle the letter of what happens next.
mistakes: Children may guess the ball always bounces back. Roll a real ball into each object first and ask, "What moved? Which way?" A hard wall that cannot move sends the ball back; a light block gets pushed and moves; a soft pillow squishes and stops the ball; a tower gets knocked over.
notes: Name each picture together before starting: a wall, a block, a pillow, a tower of blocks. Read each question and both choices aloud twice. After the sheet, talk about the big idea: when a moving ball hits something, it can push it, stop, or change direction.

## Main: Look and circle
picword:
  - bump-wall | 1. wall
  - bump-block | 2. block
  - bump-pillow | 3. pillow
  - bump-tower | 4. tower
choice: (Read aloud.) Picture 1: The ball hits the wall. What happens next?
  * The ball bounces back.
  - The wall falls down.
choice: (Read aloud.) Picture 2: The ball hits a light block. What happens next?
  - Nothing moves.
  * The block slides away.
choice: (Read aloud.) Picture 3: The ball hits a soft pillow. What happens next?
  * The pillow squishes and the ball stops.
  - The ball bounces far away.
choice: (Read aloud.) Picture 4: The ball hits a tower of blocks. What happens next?
  - The tower gets taller.
  * The tower falls down.

# 16. Make My Shade Better
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons; the shade structures students built and tested earlier (or a photo of them)
ican: I can tell how to make a shade block more sunlight.
directions: (Read aloud.) Think about the shade you made. Draw one change that would make it keep the sun off better. Then tell a partner why. Your teacher will write your words.
mistakes: Children often add decorations (a flag, a color) that do not change how much sunlight is blocked. Ask, "Will this stop more sun from getting through?" Good changes make the shade bigger, wider or thicker, close the holes, or turn it to face the sun.
notes: Scribe each child's reason in their own words on the lines. Accept any change that blocks more sunlight: a bigger roof, thicker or darker cloth, a cover with no gaps, adding a side wall, moving or tilting it so its shadow covers the spot. Back in the sun, children can test the change by feeling the ground under the old and new shade.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) On a sunny day, where does the ground feel cooler?
  - in the sun
  * in the shade
choice: (Read aloud.) A shade works by
  * blocking the sunlight
  - making more sunlight

## Main: My change
draw: Draw your shade with your change. | 3.2
write: (Read aloud.) Tell a partner why your change helps. Your teacher writes your words here.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: "I made the roof bigger so more sun can't get in, and the shadow is bigger."

## Stretch: Did it work?
choice: (Read aloud.) Does a bigger shade make a bigger shadow?
  * yes
  - no

# 22. Feed the Animals Match
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell what animals eat.
directions: (Read aloud.) Write the letter of each animal's food. Circle the plant eaters.
mistakes: Children may match by where an animal lives (frog with fish, since both live near water). Ask "What does it put in its mouth?" Some think the bee eats the whole flower: it drinks nectar and eats pollen from the flower.
notes: Name every picture together first: grass, flower, leaf, fly, fish, worm; then cow, bee, caterpillar, frog, cat, bird. Plants: grass, flower, leaf. Animals: fly, fish, worm. Children who cannot write the letter yet can draw a line from the animal to its food instead. Talk about it after: a cow, a bee and a caterpillar eat only plants; a frog, a cat and this bird eat animals.

  lines: 0
  key: Circle the cow (grass), the bee (flower) and the caterpillar (leaf).

# 26. Living Things Check-In
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can tell what living things need.
directions: (Read aloud.) Circle the living things. Write the letter of each animal's food. Then draw one thing every plant needs.
mistakes: Children may call a ball or a chair living because it moves or helps us. Ask, "Does it eat or drink? Does it grow?" For plants, some draw soil; plants need water and sunlight above all, so ask what a plant on a windowsill needs every day.
notes: Name every picture together first. Living: dog, flower. Not living: ball, chair. Foods: A seed, B leaf, C fly, D grass. Bird eats seeds, caterpillar eats leaves, frog eats flies, cow eats grass. For the drawing, accept water, sunlight (the sun) or air.

## Part A: Circle the living things
picword:
  - ball | ball
  - dog | dog
  - chair | chair
  - flower | flower
note: Circle the dog and the flower.

## Part B: Who eats what?
picword:
  - seed | A seed
  - leaf | B leaf
  - fly | C fly
  - grass | D grass
picword:
  - bird | bird {A}
  - caterpillar | caterpillar {B}
  - frog | frog {C}
  - cow | cow {D}

## Part C: Plants need
draw: Draw one thing every plant needs. | 1.4
note: Water, sunlight or air.

# 37. Nest Builders and Diggers
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons; if possible, an old bird's nest (with gloves, or photos) and a few acorns or nuts
ican: I can show how animals change the place where they live.
directions: (Read aloud.) Animals change the places they live. Draw a bird's nest in the tree. Then draw the hole a squirrel digs to hide a nut, and the dirt it pushes out.
mistakes: Children may draw the bird carrying the whole tree or the squirrel eating the nut right away. Ask, "What does the bird bring to the tree? What does the squirrel do to the ground?" The change is the new thing (the nest, the hole and the pile of dirt).
notes: Talk about it first: a bird carries twigs, grass and mud to a tree and weaves a nest; a squirrel digs small holes to bury nuts and seeds to eat later, and some it forgets grow into new trees. Never touch a nest that might be in use.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) What does a bird use to build a nest?
  - rocks and metal
  * twigs and grass
choice: (Read aloud.) Why does a squirrel dig a hole?
  * to hide a nut for later
  - to take a bath

## Main: Draw the change
draw: A bird builds a nest in a tree. Draw the tree with the nest. | 2.4
draw: A squirrel digs in the ground to hide a nut. Draw the hole and the dirt. | 2.4
note: The tree drawing should show a nest of twigs on a branch; the ground drawing should show a hole, a pile of loose dirt and a nut.
