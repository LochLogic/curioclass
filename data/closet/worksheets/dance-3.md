# 1. Eight-Count Phrase Builder
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, open floor space
ican: I can build a dance phrase that fits into counts of 8.
directions: Pick 4 moves. Decide how many counts each one lasts. Write one move in each count box so each phrase fills all 8 counts. Then dance both phrases without stopping.
mistakes: Students often write a move only on the count where it starts and leave the next boxes empty. A move that lasts 2 counts fills 2 boxes (write it, then an arrow). Watch for phrases that add up to 7 or 9 counts: have students clap and count aloud while pointing at each box.
notes: Count out loud together, "5, 6, 7, 8" before starting, the way dancers do. A steady drumbeat or music at about 100 to 120 beats per minute helps. Keep all moves in the student's own space for this sheet; traveling comes later.

## Warm up: Count it out
example: Phrase: march (counts 1 2), clap (3 4), turn (5 6 7 8). That is 2 + 2 + 4 = 8 counts.
fill:
  - If every move lasts 2 counts, {4} moves fit in one 8-count phrase.
  - If every move lasts 1 count, {8} moves fit in one 8-count phrase.
  - A move lasts 4 counts and the next lasts 2. There are {2} counts left in the phrase.
  - Two 8-count phrases are {16} counts in all.

## Main: Fill the count grid
text: Write the letter of a move (A, B, C or D) in every box. Use each move at least once across the two phrases.

write: Check: Does each phrase fill exactly 8 counts? Show the counts for Phrase 1 as an addition sentence.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary. The numbers must add to 8, for example 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8 or 4 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 8.

## Stretch
write: Change one thing in Phrase 2 so it feels different from Phrase 1 (faster, slower, a new level or a new direction). What did you change?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names one clear change, for example "In Phrase 2, I do move B at a low level instead of standing tall."

# 21. Pathway Map of My Phrase
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, colored pencil, open floor space
ican: I can map the floor pathway of my dance phrase on a stage.
directions: Learn the stage areas. Then draw the pathway your phrase travels on the stage map. Put a numbered dot where each move happens and list the moves below.
mistakes: Stage right and stage left are named from the dancer's view while facing the audience, so on a map with the audience at the bottom, stage left is on the right side of the page. Students also lose track when a pathway crosses itself: number the dots in order so the path can be followed.
notes: Tape a large X at center stage and mark the audience side of the room before students map. Have each student walk their phrase slowly while a partner traces the path in the air, then draw it. Straight, curved and zigzag pathways from earlier grades all count.

## Warm up: Know the stage
example: A dancer stands at center stage facing the audience. Walking toward the audience goes **downstage**. Walking away goes **upstage**.
choice: Upstage is
  - the part of the stage closest to the audience
  * the part of the stage farthest from the audience
  - the middle of the stage
choice: You face the audience and walk toward your right hand. You are moving toward
  * stage right
  - stage left
  - upstage
choice: On a map with the audience at the bottom of the page, stage left is on
  - the left side of the page
  * the right side of the page
  - the top of the page

text: **UPSTAGE (back)**

## Stretch
write: Where does your phrase end on the stage? Why is that a strong place to finish?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names a stage area (for example downstage center) and gives a reason, such as the audience can see the ending pose clearly.
