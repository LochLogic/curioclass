# 1. Eight-Count Phrase Builder
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, open floor space
ican: I can build a dance phrase that fits into counts of 8.
directions: Pick 4 moves. Decide how many counts each one lasts. Write one move in each count box so each phrase fills all 8 counts. Then dance both phrases without stopping.
mistakes: Students often write a move only on the count where it starts and leave the next boxes empty. A move that lasts 2 counts fills 2 boxes (write it, then an arrow). Watch for phrases that add up to 7 or 9 counts: have students clap and count aloud while pointing at each box.
notes: Count out loud together, "5, 6, 7, 8" before starting, the way dancers do. A steady drumbeat or music at about 100 to 120 beats per minute helps. Keep all moves in the student's own space for this sheet; traveling comes later.

## Warm up: Count it out
example: Phrase: march (counts 1 2), clap (3 4), turn (5 6 7 8). That is 2 + 2 + 4 = 8 counts.
fill:
  - If every move lasts 2 counts, {4} moves fit in one 8-count phrase.
  - If every move lasts 1 count, {8} moves fit in one 8-count phrase.
  - A move lasts 4 counts and the next lasts 2. There are {2} counts left in the phrase.
  - Two 8-count phrases are {16} counts in all.

## Main: Choose your 4 moves
table: Move | What it looks like | How many counts?
  - A | |
  - B | |
  - C | |
  - D | |

## Main: Fill the count grid
text: Write the letter of a move (A, B, C or D) in every box. Use each move at least once across the two phrases.
table: Phrase | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8
  - Phrase 1 | | | | | | | |
  - Phrase 2 | | | | | | | |
write: Check: Does each phrase fill exactly 8 counts? Show the counts for Phrase 1 as an addition sentence.
  lines: 1
  key: Answers vary. The numbers must add to 8, for example 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8 or 4 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 8.

## Stretch
write: Change one thing in Phrase 2 so it feels different from Phrase 1 (faster, slower, a new level or a new direction). What did you change?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names one clear change, for example "In Phrase 2, I do move B at a low level instead of standing tall."

# 7. Hora Step Counts
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, open floor space, hora music (optional)
ican: I can write a folk dance step as a pattern in counts.
directions: Learn the hora step with your class. Write which foot does what on each count. Then answer the pattern questions and draw the circle with an arrow showing which way it travels.
mistakes: Students often write only the steps and forget the hops on counts 4 and 6. Others think the circle never moves, because count 5 steps back; the step goes two steps left and only one back right, so the circle travels left. Seen from above, dancers facing the center and moving to their left go clockwise.
notes: The hora is a circle dance done at weddings and celebrations in Israel and in Jewish communities around the world, and in related forms in Romania, Moldova and the Balkans. This sheet uses one common version: facing the center, hands joined, step left, cross right behind, step left, hop on the left, step right, hop on the right. Some groups use other versions or travel the other way; if you teach a different one, change the counts with the class and accept answers that match it. Learn it slowly without music first, then with music.

## Warm up: Circle dance facts
choice: In a hora, the dancers stand
  - in two lines facing each other
  * in a circle, often holding hands
  - alone in their own space
choice: A hora is most often danced at
  - a funeral
  - a sports game
  * a wedding or celebration

## Main: The step in counts
example: Count 1: step the **left** foot to the left side.
table: Count | Which foot? | What does it do?
  - 1 | left | step to the left side
  - 2 | {right} | {cross behind the left foot}
  - 3 | {left} | {step to the left side}
  - 4 | {left} | {hop, while the right leg swings forward}
  - 5 | {right} | {step to the right side}
  - 6 | {right} | {hop, while the left leg swings forward}
fill:
  - One hora step takes {6} counts.
  - The step has {2} hops.
  - Doing the step 4 times in a row takes {24} counts.
  - In one step, the dancer steps to the left {2} times and to the right {1} time, so the circle travels to the {left}.
choice: Dancers face the center and travel to their left. Seen from above, the circle turns
  * clockwise
  - counterclockwise
  - not at all

## Main: Draw the circle
draw: Draw the circle from above with 8 dancers as dots. Add an arrow to show which way it travels. | 2.4
note: The key drawing is a circle of 8 dots with an arrow going clockwise (like a clock's hands).

## Stretch
write: The hora step is 6 counts, but the music is counted in 8s. After how many counts do the step and the 8-count phrase start together again? Show how you know.
  lines: 3
  key: 24 counts. Steps start on counts 1, 7, 13, 19, 25; 8-count phrases start on 1, 9, 17, 25. Both start again on count 25, after 24 counts (4 hora steps = 3 eight-counts).

# 20. Idea to Phrase Planner
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, colored pencil, open floor space
ican: I can plan a dance phrase from an idea in a poem, a picture or a sound.
directions: Choose one idea starter. Plan a 16-count phrase with 4 moves of 4 counts each, a pathway and an ending shape. Then dance it and check your plan.
mistakes: Students pick moves that do not connect to the idea ("a jump because I like jumping"). Each move should show a word, a part of the picture or a quality of the sound. Watch that the counts add up to 16 with no gaps.
notes: Read the poem lines aloud, show a picture (a storm, a city street, a garden) or play a short sound (rain, a train, a bell) so every student has an idea to choose. The poem lines are written for Curio Class. Give time to try moves on the floor before writing. A phrase can stay in place for some moves; the pathway is how it travels when it does move.

## Warm up: Find the moves in an idea
source: Poem lines written for Curio Class
  The kite pulls up, the kite dips low,
  it spins and dives where breezes blow.
fill:
  - Two action words from the poem: {pulls, dips, spins, dives or blow (any two)}
  - A move for "dips low" would be at a {low} level.
choice: Which pathway best fits "spins and dives where breezes blow"?
  - a straight line
  * a curving, swooping pathway
  - no pathway at all

## Main: My plan
text: My idea starter (circle one): poem   picture   sound
text: My idea: ____________________________________________
table: Move | Counts | My move | What it shows about my idea
  - 1 | {1 to 4} | |
  - 2 | {5 to 8} | |
  - 3 | {9 to 12} | |
  - 4 | {13 to 16} | |
draw: My pathway on the floor: start with a dot, show the path with arrows, end with an X. | 2
draw: My ending shape (stick figure) | 1.6
write: Why does your ending shape fit your idea?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer connects the shape to the idea, for example "My kite ends tangled in a tree, so I end in a twisted, high shape."

## Stretch
write: Dance your phrase. What would you change to show your idea more clearly?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary, for example "I would make move 3 bigger and slower so the kite looks like it is floating."

# 21. Pathway Map of My Phrase
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, colored pencil, open floor space
ican: I can map the floor pathway of my dance phrase on a stage.
directions: Learn the stage areas. Then draw the pathway your phrase travels on the stage map. Put a numbered dot where each move happens and list the moves below.
mistakes: Stage right and stage left are named from the dancer's view while facing the audience, so on a map with the audience at the bottom, stage left is on the right side of the page. Students also lose track when a pathway crosses itself: number the dots in order so the path can be followed.
notes: Tape a large X at center stage and mark the audience side of the room before students map. Have each student walk their phrase slowly while a partner traces the path in the air, then draw it. Straight, curved and zigzag pathways from earlier grades all count.

## Warm up: Know the stage
example: A dancer stands at center stage facing the audience. Walking toward the audience goes **downstage**. Walking away goes **upstage**.
choice: Upstage is
  - the part of the stage closest to the audience
  * the part of the stage farthest from the audience
  - the middle of the stage
choice: You face the audience and walk toward your right hand. You are moving toward
  * stage right
  - stage left
  - upstage
choice: On a map with the audience at the bottom of the page, stage left is on
  - the left side of the page
  * the right side of the page
  - the top of the page

## Main: My stage map
text: **UPSTAGE (back)**
draw: Stage map. Start with dot 1. Draw your pathway with arrows and number each move. | 3.4
text: **DOWNSTAGE (front)**: the audience sits here.
table: Dot | Move | Pathway to the next dot (straight, curved or zigzag)
  - 1 | |
  - 2 | |
  - 3 | |
  - 4 | |
  - 5 | |

## Stretch
write: Where does your phrase end on the stage? Why is that a strong place to finish?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names a stage area (for example downstage center) and gives a reason, such as the audience can see the ending pose clearly.

# 33. Costume Sketch
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, crayons or colored pencils
ican: I can design a costume that helps show the main idea of a dance.
directions: Answer the warm up questions. Then sketch a costume for your idea dance, label its colors and shapes, and explain how they help show the main idea.
mistakes: Students design a costume they like rather than one that fits the dance. Ask "How does this color or shape help the audience understand the idea?" Watch for costumes that would be unsafe to dance in: long hems, loose pieces near the feet, or big hats that block sight.
notes: Use the idea dance students planned earlier, or let them pick one: a storm, a garden growing, a busy city, a kite. Colors carry common meanings in many places (blue and white for water or calm, red and orange for fire or energy), but meanings vary across cultures; accept any choice the student explains. A costume should let a dancer move freely and safely.

## Warm up: Costume choices
choice: A dance is about a calm ocean. Which costume colors fit best?
  * blues, greens and white
  - black and bright orange
  - brown and gray
choice: Which costume shape would make spinning look like a flower opening?
  - a stiff box shape
  - tight clothes with no extra fabric
  * a wide skirt or long sleeves that flare out when you turn
choice: Which costume is safest for a dance with lots of floor rolls and turns?
  - a long skirt that drags on the floor
  * fitted clothes that let you bend and stretch
  - a big hat and loose scarves

## Main: My costume
text: My dance idea: ____________________________________________
draw: Sketch your costume on a stick figure. Label the colors, shapes and fabrics. | 3.4
table: Costume choice | What I chose | How it shows my idea
  - Main color | |
  - Shape or cut | |
  - One special detail | |
write: How do the color and shape of your costume help the audience understand your main idea?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names a color and a shape and ties each to the idea, for example "My storm costume is dark gray and silver like clouds and lightning, and the ragged strips on the sleeves whip around when I spin, like wind."

## Stretch
write: Is your costume safe to dance in? Name one thing you checked.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary, for example "Yes, the hem is above my ankles so I will not trip, and nothing covers my eyes."
