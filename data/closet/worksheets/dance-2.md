# 1. Movement Combo Spinner
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, a paper clip, open floor space
ican: I can mix a travel move, a level and a pathway into one dance combo.
directions: (Read aloud.) Make the spinner in the box. Spin 3 times: once for a travel move, once for a level, once for a pathway. Write your combo in the chart, then dance it and check the box.
mistakes: Children often forget the level once they start moving: a low-level gallop means bent knees the whole way, not just at the start. Zigzag pathways turn into curves when children rush; have them stop at each corner. The paper clip spins best when the pencil point holds the clip's end on the center dot.
notes: To spin: draw a circle with a plus sign in it to make 4 parts, number them 1 to 4, put a pencil point through a paper clip at the center, and flick the clip. The number it stops on picks from the menu. Students who struggle with the spinner can roll a die and re-roll 5 and 6. Dance each combo across the room in small groups, everyone in the same direction, so no one crosses paths. A low level is bent knees or a crouch-walk, never crawling fast.

## Warm up: Levels and pathways
match: (Read aloud.) Match each word to what it means.
  - low level = close to the floor, knees bent
  - middle level = standing up, regular height
  - high level = tall, on tiptoes, arms up
  - curved pathway = bends like the letter S
  - zigzag pathway = sharp corners like the letter Z

## Stretch

  lines: 2
  starter: Combo ... was hardest because ...
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the combo and a reason tied to a level or pathway, for example "Combo 2 was hardest because hopping at a low level made my legs tired."

# 7. Slow, Medium, Fast Phrase
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, open floor space, a drum or clapping for the beat
ican: I can dance the same phrase at a slow, medium and fast tempo.
directions: (Read aloud.) Learn the 8-count phrase. Dance it slow, then medium, then fast while your teacher keeps the beat. After each tempo, check how clear your moves were. Circle the tempo that made the moves hardest to keep clear.
mistakes: At a slow tempo children rush through the moves and then wait; each move should stretch to fill its counts. At a fast tempo moves get small and blurry: the turn becomes a wobble and the freeze is skipped. Ask "Can I still see every move?"
notes: Keep a steady beat on a drum or by clapping: slow about one beat each second and a half, medium one beat each second, fast two beats each second. Dance in personal space, everyone facing the same way. The turn is a small walking turn in place, never a fast spin. Most children find fast the hardest to keep clear, but some find slow hardest because balance is harder; accept either with a reason.

## Warm up: Tempo words
example: **Tempo** means how fast or slow the beat goes. The same 8 counts can be slow, medium or fast.
fill:
  - The phrase has {8} counts.
  - At a fast tempo, the 8 counts take {less} time.
  - At a slow tempo, the 8 counts take {more} time.

# 20. Three Ways to Show Nervous
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can make up different dance moves to show one feeling.
directions: (Read aloud.) A dancer is playing a nervous character. Make up 3 different ways to show nervous with your body. Draw each one. Circle the clearest one and tell why.
mistakes: Children may make three moves that are almost the same, such as three kinds of shaking. Push for variety: one with the hands, one with the feet, one with the whole body or face. Some children draw a sad face only; ask what the body is doing too.
notes: This is about showing a feeling in a character, not about the children's own worries. Warm up with "Show me a nervous cat. A nervous mouse. A nervous tree in the wind." Then give quiet improvising time in personal space. Good nervous ideas: shaking hands, tiny quick steps, looking side to side, hugging yourself, freezing and then darting, biting a pretend fingernail, curling small.

## Warm up: What does nervous look like?

  - jumping high with arms open
choice: (Read aloud.) Which move does NOT show nervous?
  - shaking hands
  - hugging yourself small
  * stretching wide and smiling
choice: (Read aloud.) A dancer shows nervous with

  - only the face
  - only a costume

# 21. The Wind Poem Moves
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can turn words from a poem into dance moves.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the poem. Answer the questions. Underline 5 action words you could dance. Write them in the chart with a move for each. Then draw one move.
mistakes: Children may underline nouns (kites, birds, grass) instead of action words. Ask "Can your body do that word?" Toss, blow, pass, push, call, sings and hid all work; so do describing words like strong, cold and loud if the child shows how the move feels.
notes: The poem is "The Wind" by Robert Louis Stevenson, from A Child's Garden of Verses (1885), in the public domain. The printed text replaces two dashes in the original (after "grass" and "all") with commas, by house style; check the wording against a scan of the 1885 book before printing. Read it twice: once to listen, once while children move their arms like the wind in their own space.

## Warm up: The poem

  I saw you toss the kites on high

  O wind, that sings so loud a song!
  I saw the different things you did,
  But always you yourself you hid.
  I felt you push, I heard you call,
  I could not see yourself at all,

  Or just a stronger child than me?

  O wind, that sings so loud a song!

# 33. Korean Fan Dance Flower
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space, paper plates or paper fans (optional)
ican: I can design a group shape inspired by a dance from Korea.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at pictures of the Korean fan dance with your teacher. Answer the questions. Then design a flower shape for 4 dancers, each holding 2 fans, and draw it from above.
mistakes: Children may draw 4 dancers in a line. A flower needs a center with petals all around it: dancers can kneel in a small circle and open their fans outward like petals. Some draw one fan per dancer; in this dance each dancer holds a fan in each hand.
notes: The Korean fan dance, buchaechum, is danced by groups of dancers in hanbok (traditional Korean dress) holding a large folding fan in each hand. The fans are often painted with flowers and edged with feathers. Dancers open, close and ripple their fans together to make shapes like flowers, waves and butterflies. The modern stage version is usually credited to choreographer Kim Baek-bong in the 1950s. Show photos or a short video before the sheet. If you have them, paper plates make safe pretend fans for trying the flower shape.
