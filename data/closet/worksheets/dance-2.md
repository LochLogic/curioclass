# 1. Movement Combo Spinner
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, a paper clip, open floor space
ican: I can mix a travel move, a level and a pathway into one dance combo.
directions: (Read aloud.) Make the spinner in the box. Spin 3 times: once for a travel move, once for a level, once for a pathway. Write your combo in the chart, then dance it and check the box.
mistakes: Children often forget the level once they start moving: a low-level gallop means bent knees the whole way, not just at the start. Zigzag pathways turn into curves when children rush; have them stop at each corner. The paper clip spins best when the pencil point holds the clip's end on the center dot.
notes: To spin: draw a circle with a plus sign in it to make 4 parts, number them 1 to 4, put a pencil point through a paper clip at the center, and flick the clip. The number it stops on picks from the menu. Students who struggle with the spinner can roll a die and re-roll 5 and 6. Dance each combo across the room in small groups, everyone in the same direction, so no one crosses paths. A low level is bent knees or a crouch-walk, never crawling fast.

## Warm up: Levels and pathways
match: (Read aloud.) Match each word to what it means.
  - low level = close to the floor, knees bent
  - middle level = standing up, regular height
  - high level = tall, on tiptoes, arms up
  - curved pathway = bends like the letter S
  - zigzag pathway = sharp corners like the letter Z

## Stretch
write: Which combo was the hardest to dance? Why?
  lines: 2
  starter: Combo ... was hardest because ...
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the combo and a reason tied to a level or pathway, for example "Combo 2 was hardest because hopping at a low level made my legs tired."

# 21. The Wind Poem Moves
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons, open floor space
ican: I can turn words from a poem into dance moves.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the poem. Answer the questions. Underline 5 action words you could dance. Write them in the chart with a move for each. Then draw one move.
mistakes: Children may underline nouns (kites, birds, grass) instead of action words. Ask "Can your body do that word?" Toss, blow, pass, push, call, sings and hid all work; so do describing words like strong, cold and loud if the child shows how the move feels.
notes: The poem is "The Wind" by Robert Louis Stevenson, from A Child's Garden of Verses (1885), in the public domain. The printed text replaces two dashes in the original (after "grass" and "all") with commas, by house style; check the wording against a scan of the 1885 book before printing. Read it twice: once to listen, once while children move their arms like the wind in their own space.

## Warm up: The poem
source: Robert Louis Stevenson, "The Wind," A Child's Garden of Verses, 1885 (public domain)
  I saw you toss the kites on high
  And blow the birds about the sky;
  And all around I heard you pass,
  Like ladies' skirts across the grass,
  O wind, a-blowing all day long,
  O wind, that sings so loud a song!
  I saw the different things you did,
  But always you yourself you hid.
  I felt you push, I heard you call,
  I could not see yourself at all,
  O wind, a-blowing all day long,
  O wind, that sings so loud a song!
  O you that are so strong and cold,
  O blower, are you young or old?
  Are you a beast of field and tree,
  Or just a stronger child than me?
  O wind, a-blowing all day long,
  O wind, that sings so loud a song!

## Questions
choice: (Read aloud.) Who is the poem talking to?
  - a bird
  * the wind
  - a kite
choice: (Read aloud.) What could the poet NOT do?
  - hear the wind
  - feel the wind
  * see the wind
choice: (Read aloud.) What does the wind do to the kites?
  * tosses them up high
  - hides them
  - sings to them
