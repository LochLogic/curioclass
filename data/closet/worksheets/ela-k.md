# 1. Which Way Do We Read? Arrow Tracing
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayon
ican: I can show that we read from left to right and top to bottom.
directions: (Read aloud.) Trace the arrows. They show the way we read. Then look at each picture sentence. Circle the first word. Draw a dot on the last word. Draw an arrow under the words from left to right.
mistakes: Some children circle the picture or the word under the picture's middle instead of the first word: have them put a finger on the far left word before they circle. A child who dots the first word instead of the last may be reading right to left; sweep a finger under the words together and say "start here, stop here."
notes: Key for the picture sentences: circle I, I, A, We, Look; dot on star, sun, up, flower, cloud. Read each sentence aloud while pointing to every word, then let children "read" it back while they point. Sentence 4 may wrap onto a second line: that is a chance to show the return sweep (back to the left, down one line).

## Warm up: Trace the arrows
text: (Read aloud.) Start at the left end of each arrow. Trace it to the right. Then draw your own arrow on the line next to it.
trace: → → → →

## Main: Picture sentences
picword:
  - star | I see a star.
  - sun | I see the sun.
  - flag | A flag is up.
  - flower | We like the flower.
  - cloud | Look at the cloud.

## Stretch: Show the way
choice: (Read aloud.) Where do we start reading a line of words?
  - On the right side
  * On the left side
  - In the middle
choice: (Read aloud.) When we get to the end of a line, where do we go next?
  * Back to the left, on the next line down
  - Up to the line on top
  - Back to the right

# 6. Beginning, Middle, End Sound Boxes
minutes: 20
materials: pencil or crayon, one counter (a bean, button or small cube) for each child
ican: I can hear if a sound is at the beginning, middle or end of a word.
directions: (Read aloud.) Say the picture word. Push your counter into a sound box for each sound you hear. Then listen to the sound I say. Circle the box where you hear it: first, middle or last.
mistakes: Children often push the counter once per letter they imagine, not per sound: fish has three sounds (f-i-sh) and sock has three (s-o-ck). Say each word slowly and stretch it. Middle sounds are the hardest; if a child circles the first box for the vowel, stretch the word again ("d-o-o-o-g") and ask, "Is /o/ at the start?"
notes: Say each picture name together first: sun, cat, dog, cup, fish, bell, sock, cap. Then use the big sound boxes to model: say the word, push the counter one box for each sound. Read each item: "1. sun. Where is /s/?" Key, with the sound to say: 1. sun /s/ first; 2. cat /t/ last; 3. dog /o/ middle; 4. cup /p/ last; 5. fish /f/ first; 6. bell /e/ middle; 7. sock /s/ first; 8. cap /a/ middle. Say sounds, not letter names.

# 19. One Cat, Two Cats: Add the s
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayon
ican: I can add s to a word to mean more than one.
directions: (Read aloud.) Count the animals in each box and write how many. Then circle the word that tells about the box: cat or cats, dog or dogs. Last, trace the s on the words that mean more than one.
mistakes: Children may circle the word with s for every box. Ask, "Is there one cat or more than one?" For a box with one animal, the word has no s. Some children read 1 as "one cats"; say it together: one cat, three cats.
notes: Read the first line together, holding up one pencil and then two pencils. The boxes go in reading order: number them 1 to 6 with the class before starting (left to right, then down), so box 1 is the one cat and box 6 is the two dogs. Key: box 1 has 1 cat (cat), box 2 has 3 cats (cats), box 3 has 1 dog (dog), box 4 has 5 dogs (dogs), box 5 has 4 cats (cats), box 6 has 2 dogs (dogs). In the tracing row, children can trace the s again on the plural words they circled.

## Main: Count and circle
text: (Read aloud.) One **cat**. Two **cats**. The **s** means more than one.
count: (Read aloud.) Count the animals in each box. Write how many.
  - 1 cat
  - 3 cat
  - 1 dog
  - 5 dog
  - 4 cat
  - 2 dog
write: (Read aloud.) Look at box 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Circle the word that tells about each box.
  lines: 0
  key: 1. cat 2. cats 3. dog 4. dogs 5. cats 6. dogs
table: Box | One | More than one
  - 1 | cat | cats
  - 2 | cat | cats
  - 3 | dog | dogs
  - 4 | dog | dogs
  - 5 | cat | cats
  - 6 | dog | dogs

## Trace: Trace the s
text: (Read aloud.) These words mean more than one. Trace each word. Make the s extra dark.
trace: cats dogs

# 26. Three-Box Story: The Lost Puppy
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can draw and tell a story with a beginning, a middle and an end.
directions: (Read aloud.) Make up a story about a lost puppy. Draw what happens first, next and last. Under each box, write a word or a sentence, or tell your words to a grown-up who writes them.
mistakes: Children often draw three pictures of the same moment (the puppy, the puppy, the puppy). Ask: "What happens first? Then what? How does it end?" Accept dictated words: the goal is a story in order, not spelling. Watch for stories that stop before the puppy is found or safe; ask how the story ends.
notes: Tell the class a quick sample first (A puppy runs out the gate. A girl looks in the park. She finds it under a slide!). Let children tell their story to a partner before they draw, using three questions: Who loses the puppy? Where does the puppy go? Who finds it, and how? Accept invented spelling and labels (puppy, park, home). The model answers in the key are only one possible story.

## Main: Draw your story
draw: 1. First: the puppy gets lost | 1.3
write: (Read aloud.) Write or tell: what happens first?
  starter: First, ...
  lines: 1
  key: First, the puppy runs out the gate.
draw: 2. Next: someone looks for the puppy | 1.3
write: (Read aloud.) Write or tell: what happens next?
  starter: Next, ...
  lines: 1
  key: Next, Mia looks in the park.
draw: 3. Last: the puppy is found | 1.3
write: (Read aloud.) Write or tell: how does it end?
  starter: Last, ...
  lines: 1
  key: Last, she finds the puppy under the slide and takes it home.

# 31. Duck at the Pond: Who, What, Where
minutes: 15
materials: pencil or crayon
ican: I can answer who, what and where questions about a story.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to the story. Then listen to each question. Circle the picture that answers it.
mistakes: Children may circle the boy for "Who is the story about?" because he talks at the end. Ask, "Who does the most in the story?" For "where," some circle the grass, where the ball ends up; ask where Dot lives.
notes: Read the story aloud twice, pointing to each word. Teach the question words with a hand signal: who (point to a person), what (hold out open hands), where (look around). The story is original to Curio Class. Say the picture names before each row: frog, duck, cat, dog; fish, leaf, kite, ball; house, school, pond, tree.

## Listen: The story
passage: Dot and the Ball

  Push, push, push! Dot pushes the ball with her bill. The ball rolls up on the grass. A boy runs to get it. "Thank you, Dot!" he says. Dot quacks and swims away.

# 44. Speak Up Checklist
minutes: 10
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can check how I spoke when I shared, and pick one thing to try next time.
directions: (Read aloud.) Think about the time you just shared. Listen to each question. Circle the smiling face for yes. Circle the other face for not yet. Then draw one thing you will try next time.
mistakes: Some children circle "yes" for everything. Make it safe to say "not yet": tell them every speaker has a next step, even grown-ups. Others pick a next step that is not about speaking (a new toy to bring); steer them back: "What will you do with your voice, your eyes or your words?"
notes: Use right after Show and Tell, while the share is fresh. Read each question and wait for everyone to circle. Model it first with your own share: "Did I use a loud voice? Yes. Did I look at my listeners? Not yet, I looked at the floor." Good next steps: speak louder, look at friends, tell two things about my object, stand still, hold my object up. Collect the sheets to see who wants help with which skill.

## Main: How did I do?
fill:
  - (Read aloud.) Did I use a loud, clear voice?
  - (Read aloud.) Did I look at my listeners?
  - (Read aloud.) Did I tell 2 things about what I shared?
text: (Read aloud.) For each question, circle yes or not yet under its number.
note: Any honest choice for each question is right. Yes and not yet are both fine answers.
picword:
  - face-happy | 1. yes
  - face-okay | 1. not yet
  - face-happy | 2. yes
  - face-okay | 2. not yet
  - face-happy | 3. yes
  - face-okay | 3. not yet

## Next time: My goal
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw one thing you will try next time you share. | 1.6
write: (Read aloud.) Tell your teacher about your picture. Your teacher can write your words.
  lines: 1
  key: One speaking goal, such as "I will talk loud," "I will look at my friends," or "I will tell two things about my rock."
