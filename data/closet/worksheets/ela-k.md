# 1. Which Way Do We Read? Arrow Tracing
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayon
ican: I can show that we read from left to right and top to bottom.
directions: (Read aloud.) Trace the arrows. They show the way we read. Then look at each picture sentence. Circle the first word. Draw a dot on the last word. Draw an arrow under the words from left to right.
mistakes: Some children circle the picture or the word under the picture's middle instead of the first word: have them put a finger on the far left word before they circle. A child who dots the first word instead of the last may be reading right to left; sweep a finger under the words together and say "start here, stop here."
notes: Key for the picture sentences: circle I, I, A, We, Look; dot on star, sun, up, flower, cloud. Read each sentence aloud while pointing to every word, then let children "read" it back while they point. Sentence 4 may wrap onto a second line: that is a chance to show the return sweep (back to the left, down one line).

## Warm up: Trace the arrows
text: (Read aloud.) Start at the left end of each arrow. Trace it to the right. Then draw your own arrow on the line next to it.
trace: → → → →

## Main: Picture sentences
picword:
  - star | I see a star.
  - sun | I see the sun.
  - flag | A flag is up.
  - flower | We like the flower.
  - cloud | Look at the cloud.

## Stretch: Show the way
choice: (Read aloud.) Where do we start reading a line of words?
  - On the right side
  * On the left side
  - In the middle
choice: (Read aloud.) When we get to the end of a line, where do we go next?
  * Back to the left, on the next line down
  - Up to the line on top
  - Back to the right
# 26. Three-Box Story: The Lost Puppy
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can draw and tell a story with a beginning, a middle and an end.
directions: (Read aloud.) Make up a story about a lost puppy. Draw what happens first, next and last. Under each box, write a word or a sentence, or tell your words to a grown-up who writes them.
mistakes: Children often draw three pictures of the same moment (the puppy, the puppy, the puppy). Ask: "What happens first? Then what? How does it end?" Accept dictated words: the goal is a story in order, not spelling. Watch for stories that stop before the puppy is found or safe; ask how the story ends.
notes: Tell the class a quick sample first (A puppy runs out the gate. A girl looks in the park. She finds it under a slide!). Let children tell their story to a partner before they draw, using three questions: Who loses the puppy? Where does the puppy go? Who finds it, and how? Accept invented spelling and labels (puppy, park, home). The model answers in the key are only one possible story.

## Main: Draw your story

  lines: 1
  key: First, the puppy runs out the gate.

write: (Read aloud.) Write or tell: what happens next?
  starter: Next, ...
  lines: 1
  key: Next, Mia looks in the park.
draw: 3. Last: the puppy is found | 1.3
write: (Read aloud.) Write or tell: how does it end?
  starter: Last, ...
