# 1. Walk, Run, Jump Picture Match
minutes: 15
materials: pencil, crayons
pairs: animal-moves
ican: I can name the ways I move: walk, run, jump, hop and gallop.
directions: (Read aloud.) Listen to each move word and its number. Find the picture of that move. Write the number under the picture.
mistakes: Hop and jump get mixed up most: a hop takes off and lands on the same one foot, a jump takes off and lands on two feet. Gallop and run are next: in a gallop the same foot always stays in front and the back foot catches up; in a run both feet take turns in front.
notes: Act out all five moves together before the sheet, saying the cue for each: walk, one foot always touches the ground; run, both feet leave the ground for a moment; jump, two feet push off and two feet land; hop, one foot pushes off and the same foot lands; gallop, one foot leads and the back foot catches up. Then read the list slowly: "Number 1 is walk. Find the walk picture and write 1 under it." Children who are not yet writing numerals can draw that many dots instead. Key: hop 4, walk 1, gallop 5, jump 3, run 2.

## Main: Match the move
text: (Read aloud.) **1** walk. **2** run. **3** jump. **4** hop. **5** gallop.
picword:
  - hop | {4}
  - walk | {1}
  - gallop | {5}
  - jump | {3}
  - run | {2}

## Stretch: My move
draw: Draw yourself doing your favorite move. | 2

# 7. Fast or Slow?
minutes: 20
materials: scissors, glue stick
ican: I can move at a fast speed and at a slow speed.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture card. Does it go fast or slow? Cut out the 8 cards. Glue each card under Fast or under Slow.
mistakes: Children sometimes sort by size (big animals fast, small animals slow) or by what they like. A rabbit is small but fast; a turtle is big next to a snail but still slow. Ask: "Show me with your body. Does it zoom or creep?"
notes: Name each card together before cutting: cheetah running, rabbit hopping away, a child racing to the finish line, a child running, snail crawling, turtle walking, a child tiptoeing, caterpillar crawling. Key: Fast: cheetah, rabbit, race, run. Slow: snail, turtle, tiptoe, caterpillar. Play "fast and slow" afterward: call a card name and children move like it in their own space, then freeze on "stop".

  - Picture B

  - Fast: icon:cheetah, icon:rabbit, icon:race, icon:run
  - Slow: icon:snail, icon:turtle, icon:tiptoe, icon:caterpillar

# 20. Step and Toss Footprints
minutes: 15
materials: crayons
ican: I can step forward with one foot when I throw.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each thrower. Trace the dotted foot that steps forward. Then circle the arm that holds the ball.
mistakes: Children often circle the arm that is pointing instead of the arm with the ball. Have them put a finger on the ball first, then slide it to the arm. Some trace both feet; remind them the dotted foot is the one that steps toward the target.
notes: Key: in each picture, the dotted foot is the front foot, on the side the thrower faces; trace only that one. Circle the arm with the ball: in picture 1 it is up high and back; in picture 2 it is down low and back (an underhand toss); in picture 3 it is up high and back. The other arm points toward the target. When children are ready, add the next cue aloud: step with the foot on the other side from your throwing hand. Practice with beanbags afterward: "Point, step, toss."

## Warm up: Trace the words
trace: step toss

## Main: Look at the throwers
picword:
  - throw-overhand | 1
  - throw-underhand | 2
  - throw-overhand-left | 3
choice: (Read aloud.) Picture 1: is the ball up high or down low?
  * Up high
  - Down low
choice: (Read aloud.) Picture 2: is the ball up high or down low?
  - Up high
  * Down low

## Stretch
draw: (Read aloud.) Draw yourself throwing a ball. Show the foot that steps forward. | 1.6

# 21. Beanbag Target Graph
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can make a picture graph and tell which group has the most.
directions: (Read aloud.) Our class tossed beanbags at three hoops. Count the beanbags that landed in each hoop. Color one box for each beanbag. Then circle the hoop with the most.
mistakes: Children often color from the middle of a row or skip a box. Show them to start at the box next to the hoop name and color every box in order, one box for each beanbag, touching each beanbag as they count.
notes: Graph key: color 7 boxes for the big hoop, 4 for the medium hoop and 2 for the small hoop. To use your own class's throws instead, tally the hits during the game and have children color those numbers; the counting part then changes to match. Talk about why the big hoop got the most: a bigger target is easier to hit. Stretch question to ask aloud: which hoop has the fewest? (the small hoop)

# 33. Heart After Moving
minutes: 10 before the game and 10 after
materials: crayons
ican: I can tell how my heart and my breathing change when I move.
directions: (Read aloud.) Before the parachute game, sit still and draw a small, slow heart. After the game, draw a big, fast heart. Then circle how breathing felt.
mistakes: Children may draw both hearts the same size. Have them place a hand on the chest before and right after the game, and tap the beat on the desk together: slow taps, then fast taps. Some think a fast heart means something is wrong; say that a fast heart after play is normal and healthy, and it slows down again with rest.
notes: Do the first box at the start of class, play the parachute game (for example, waves, popcorn and running under the parachute), then do the rest right away while hearts are still fast. Breathing answers will vary a little; most children circle slow and calm before and fast and puffing after. Accept any honest answer and talk about it. No one shares their drawing unless they want to, and a child who sat out can draw how a friend's heart might look.

## Warm up: Feel it
text: (Read aloud.) Put your hand flat on your chest. Sit very still. Can you feel your heart beat?

## Main: Before and after
draw: Before the game: a small, slow heart | 1.6
draw: After the game: a big, fast heart | 1.6
choice: (Read aloud.) Before the game, my breathing was:
  * Slow and calm
  - Fast and puffing
choice: (Read aloud.) After the game, my breathing was:
  - Slow and calm
  * Fast and puffing

## Stretch
choice: (Read aloud.) What helps a fast heart slow down again?
  - Running some more
  * Resting and breathing slowly
