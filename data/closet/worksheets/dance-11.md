# 1. Global Concert Dance Survey
minutes: 50
materials: pencil, video clips or demonstrations of each form chosen by the teacher
ican: I can compare concert dance forms from around the world by technique and cultural context.
directions: Match each form to its home and a key feature. Then complete the survey chart on technique and context, and answer the questions comparing two forms.
mistakes: Students describe forms as "exotic" or "traditional" without specifics. Ask for the trained technique (stance, footwork, hands, use of the face) and the context (where it began, who performed it, what music goes with it). Bharatanatyam and Kathak are often confused: Bharatanatyam is from South India, with a deep bent-knee stance; Kathak is from North India, with fast spins and footwork in an upright stance.
notes: Key facts: Bharatanatyam comes from Tamil Nadu in South India, grew from temple and court dance, and was revived for the concert stage in the 1930s; it uses the araimandi half-sitting stance, precise footwork, hand gestures (mudras) and expressive storytelling (abhinaya), with Carnatic music. Kathak comes from North India, from storytellers (kathakars), and developed further in Hindu and Mughal courts; it is known for fast spins (chakkars) and footwork sounded with ankle bells (ghungroo), with Hindustani music and tabla. Butoh began in Japan in the late 1950s with Tatsumi Hijikata and Kazuo Ohno, in the years after World War II; it uses very slow, controlled movement, often white body paint and intense, transformed images of the body. Flamenco comes from Andalusia in southern Spain, shaped above all by Romani (Gitano) communities along with other influences; dance (baile) joins song (cante), guitar (toque) and handclaps (palmas) with strong footwork (zapateado). Kabuki began in Kyoto around 1603, credited to Izumo no Okuni; after women were banned from the stage in 1629, men played all roles, including women's roles (onnagata); it features stylized poses (mie), elaborate costumes and makeup, and shamisen music. Chinese classical dance was shaped as a concert form in the 20th century from Chinese opera movement and martial arts, with round, flowing paths, jumps and turns, and sleeve dances. Present every form through its own dancers where possible.

## Warm up: Match the form
match: Match each form to its home and one feature.
  - Bharatanatyam = South India; half-sitting stance and hand gestures called mudras
  - Kathak = North India; fast spins and ankle bells
  - butoh = Japan, late 1950s; very slow, controlled movement
  - flamenco = southern Spain; footwork with guitar, song and handclaps
  - Kabuki dance = Japan, early 1600s; stylized poses called mie
  - Chinese classical dance = China; movement drawn from opera and martial arts, with flowing sleeves

## Main: Survey chart
text: Write one form in each row, in this order: Bharatanatyam, Kathak, butoh, flamenco, Kabuki dance, Chinese classical dance.
chart: Form | Technique (stance, footwork, hands, face) | Music | Context (origin, who performed it, purpose)
  rows: 6
  key: See the teacher notes for each form's technique, music and context; accept other accurate details.
write: Which two forms rely most on rhythmic footwork? What makes the sound in each?
  lines: 2
  key: Kathak and flamenco (Bharatanatyam footwork also counts). Kathak dancers sound rhythms with bare feet and the ankle bells (ghungroo); flamenco dancers strike the floor with the heels, toes and soles of their shoes (zapateado).

## Compare
write: Butoh began after World War II. How might its historical context explain why it looks so different from the other forms on this chart?
  lines: 4
  key: Butoh artists rejected both Western dance and older Japanese forms, searching for a new way to show the body after the war's destruction and rapid change in Japan; that is why it uses slow, transformed, sometimes unsettling images instead of codified steps or graceful lines.
write: Choose two forms. Compare how each uses the hands or arms to communicate.
  lines: 4
  key: Answers vary. Model: Bharatanatyam uses mudras, codified hand gestures that can show words, objects or feelings while telling a story; flamenco uses curving arm and wrist movements (braceo) and handclaps that add to the rhythm and mood rather than telling a story word by word.

## Stretch
write: Many of these forms changed when they moved from temples, courts or communities onto concert stages. What can be gained and what can be lost in that change?
  lines: 4
  key: Answers vary. Gains: wider audiences, preservation, new training and new works. Losses: the original setting and purpose, community participation, or meaning that depends on a ritual or occasion. Strong answers give an example such as Bharatanatyam's revival in the 1930s.

# 21. Running a Rehearsal
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, timer, your issue piece and cast
ican: I can run an efficient rehearsal segment that warms up, teaches, cleans and gives notes.
directions: Plan a 20-minute rehearsal segment for your issue piece. Answer the timing questions, fill in the checklist, then run the segment with your cast and record what happened.
mistakes: Student directors spend 15 minutes teaching and skip cleaning and notes. Watch the clock and move on at each time marker. Notes should be specific ("arms at shoulder height on count 3") and include something that went well, not only corrections.
notes: The segment shape: warm up (prepare bodies and focus), teaching (new material), cleaning (make the same material sharp and together: counts, spacing, unison), notes (specific feedback and the plan for next time). Rotate student directors so everyone runs one segment. The issue piece is the dance each group is creating about an issue in this module.

## Warm up: Plan the clock
fill:
  - Warm up 5 minutes, teaching 7 minutes and cleaning 5 minutes leaves {3} minutes for notes in a 20-minute segment.
  - If teaching runs 4 minutes long, and notes must stay at 3 minutes, cleaning shrinks to {1} minute.
  - A 32-count section taught 8 counts at a time takes {4} chunks to teach.
choice: Which is the most useful cleaning note?
  - That looked kind of messy.
  * Everyone, the arm on count 5 goes straight up, not forward. Let's run counts 1 to 8 again.
  - Try harder next time.

## After the rehearsal
write: Write the two most important notes you gave your cast.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Strong notes are specific and name counts, body parts or spacing, for example "Counts 5-8: the diagonal line drifts; stay on your marks" and "The ending pose was strong and together: keep that."
write: Did you stay on time? What would you change in the next segment?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. A strong reflection compares planned and actual minutes and names a fix, for example "Teaching ran 3 minutes over because I taught 16 counts at once; next time I will teach 8 counts, then run them before adding more."

## Stretch
write: A cast member keeps doing a move that hurts their knee. What do you say and do as the rehearsal director?
  lines: 3
  key: Stop that move for that dancer right away, let them rest and tell the teacher; offer a safe alternative (change the level or take out the deep bend) and adjust the choreography so no one has to dance through pain.
