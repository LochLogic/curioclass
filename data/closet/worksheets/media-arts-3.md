# 1. Thaumatrope Spinner
minutes: 40
materials: thin cardboard or cardstock, a cup to trace (about 3 inches across), scissors, hole punch, two pieces of string or two rubber bands, pencil, markers
ican: I can make an optical toy that blends two pictures into one.
directions: Read how a thaumatrope works. Put the steps in order. Plan your two pictures in the boxes, then build your spinner and test it. Answer the questions after you spin it.
mistakes: The most common problem is a cage that comes out upside down. Students must flip the disk top to bottom (like turning a page on a clipboard) before drawing side 2, because the strings go on the left and right and the disk spins top over bottom. A bird drawn too small or off center will not line up with the cage: draw both in the middle and make the cage bars bigger than the bird. Thin, light lines disappear when spinning: use dark marker.
notes: A thaumatrope is a spinning optical toy that became popular in London in the 1820s; the bird and cage is the classic design. When it spins fast, the eye and brain cannot keep the two pictures apart, so they blend into one. If the string tangles, twisting the strings between thumb and finger and then pulling them tight works best. Rubber bands looped through the holes also work.

## Warm up: How it works
example: A thaumatrope is a paper disk with a picture on each side. When you spin it fast, the two pictures switch faster than your eyes and brain can follow, so you see them as one picture.
choice: What will you see when the bird and cage disk spins fast?
  - Only the bird
  - Only the cage
  * The bird inside the cage
  - Nothing at all
choice: Why do you flip the disk top to bottom before drawing the cage?
  - So the cage is smaller than the bird
  * So the cage is right side up when the disk spins
  - So the marker does not smudge
  - So the strings are easier to tie

## Main: Put the steps in order
table: Step number | What to do
  - {4} | Punch one hole on the left edge and one on the right edge.
  - {1} | Trace a circle about 3 inches across on cardstock and cut it out.
  - {6} | Twist the strings, then pull them tight so the disk spins.
  - {3} | Flip the disk top to bottom and draw a cage on the other side.
  - {2} | Draw a bird in the middle of one side.
  - {5} | Tie a string through each hole.

## Main: Plan your pictures
draw: Side 1: the bird (in the middle, dark lines) | 2.3
draw: Side 2: the cage (bars wider than the bird) | 2.3

## After you spin it
write: What did you see when it spun? Was the bird inside the cage?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Most see the bird inside the cage. If the bird looked outside the cage or upside down, the student should name the fix: center both pictures or flip the disk top to bottom before drawing.
write: Name another pair of pictures that would make one picture when they spin. Tell what you would draw on each side.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary: a fish and a fishbowl, a flower and a vase, a face and a hat, a tree with no leaves and leaves, a horse and a rider.

## Stretch
write: Cartoons and movies are many pictures shown quickly. How is a thaumatrope like a cartoon?
  lines: 3
  key: Both show pictures so fast that the brain blends them. A thaumatrope blends two pictures into one; a cartoon shows many pictures in a row, each a little different, so the brain sees movement.

# 7. Small Moves, Smooth Motion
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, eraser
ican: I can plan small changes between frames so an animation moves smoothly.
directions: Read the example. Answer the warm-up questions. Then draw the same character in all 5 frames, moving only the arm a little each time, to show one wave hello.
mistakes: The most common mistake is big jumps: the arm is down in frame 1 and straight up in frame 2. Each frame should change only a little. Students also redraw the whole character differently each time; trace or copy the body so only the arm moves. Keep the character the same size and in the same spot.
notes: In stop motion, a camera takes one picture (frame) at a time and the model is moved a tiny bit between pictures. Played quickly, the frames blend into motion. Many stop-motion films use 12 or more frames for every second. A good wave: frame 1 arm down by the side; frame 2 arm out to the side, partly raised; frame 3 arm up with the hand near the head; frame 4 hand tipped one way; frame 5 hand tipped the other way. Students can trace the body from frame 1 with the paper held up to a window.

## Warm up
example: Frame 1: the arm hangs down. Frame 2: the arm is lifted a little. Frame 3: the arm is lifted a little more. Small changes look smooth. Big jumps look jerky.
choice: Which plan will make the smoothest wave?
  - 2 frames: arm down, then arm up
  * 6 frames, each moving the arm a little
  - 1 frame with the arm up
choice: What should stay the same in every frame?
  - the position of the arm
  * the size and place of the body
  - nothing; change everything
fill:
  - A crew shoots 10 frames for each second. A 3-second wave needs {30} frames.
  - A crew shoots 12 frames for each second. A 2-second wave needs {24} frames.

## Main: One wave in 5 frames
draw: Frame 1: arm down by the side | 1.5
draw: Frame 2: arm moves out a little | 1.5
draw: Frame 3: arm up, hand by the head | 1.5
draw: Frame 4: hand tips one way | 1.5
draw: Frame 5: hand tips the other way | 1.5
note: Drawings vary. Check that the body stays the same in all 5 frames and that the arm moves a similar small amount each time, with no big jump.

## Stretch
write: Your wave looks jerky between frames 2 and 3. What would you add or change?
  lines: 2
  key: Add an in-between frame where the arm is halfway between its place in frame 2 and frame 3, so the jump is smaller.

# 20. Thunder and Footsteps Lab
minutes: 40
materials: pencil; for thunder: a sheet of poster board, an empty plastic bin, a large paper bag; for footsteps: two old shoes, a sealed zip bag of cornstarch, a tray of dry leaves or crumpled paper
ican: I can test different ways to make a sound effect and choose the best one with reasons.
directions: Choose one sound to make: thunder or footsteps. Predict which way will work best. Test all 3 ways, rate each one from 1 (not like it) to 5 (just like it), then choose the best and explain why.
mistakes: Students often rate the loudest try the best. Ask, "Does it sound like the real thing, or is it just loud?" Another mistake is changing two things at once (a new object and a new way of moving it); test one change at a time. Partners should rate with eyes closed so they judge only the sound.
notes: People who make sound effects for movies are called Foley artists, named after Jack Foley, who made sounds for films at Universal Studios. Thunder ideas: shake or wobble a sheet of poster board, drum softly with fists on an upside-down bin, puff and crumple a paper bag. Footstep ideas: put shoes on your hands and walk them on a desk; squeeze a sealed bag of cornstarch to sound like footsteps in snow; press your hands into a tray of dry leaves for steps on a path. Keep the cornstarch bag sealed and taped. Ratings and choices vary; strong answers give a reason tied to what the sound is like.

## Warm up
choice: What does a Foley artist do?
  - draws the pictures for a cartoon
  * makes sound effects for movies with everyday objects
  - writes the words the characters say
choice: Why should your partner close their eyes when they rate a sound?
  * So they judge only the sound, not what they see
  - So they do not get sleepy
  - So the sound gets louder

## Main: Test 3 ways
text: My sound (circle one): thunder or footsteps
write: Prediction: I think way number ___ will sound the most real because ...
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong prediction gives a reason, such as "the poster board, because it rumbles and booms like real thunder."
table: Way | What I used and how I moved it | Rating (1 to 5) | What it sounded like
  height: 0.75
  - 1 | | |
  - 2 | | |
  - 3 | | |
note: Answers in the table vary. Each row should name the object and the action, give a rating and describe the sound with a word like rumble, crunch or tap.

## Conclusion
write: The best way was ___ because ...
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Model: "The poster board was best because when I shook it slowly it rumbled, then a fast shake made a crack like close thunder. The bin only sounded like drumming."
write: Was your prediction right? What surprised you?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer compares the prediction to the result and names one surprise.

## Stretch
write: How could you make your best sound even better? Name one change to try next.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary, such as shaking the poster board faster for a close crack, or adding a slow bin rumble after it for faraway thunder.

# 21. Timed Cue Sheet
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can plan when music and sound effects play in a cartoon.
directions: Read the cartoon plan. Do the time warm up. Fill in the missing times on the cue sheet, then choose music and a sound effect for scenes 2, 3 and 4. Use seconds for every time.
mistakes: Students often write the end time where the length goes. Remind them: length = end time minus start time. The end of one scene is the start of the next, and the last scene must end at 60 seconds for a 1-minute cartoon.
notes: A cue sheet tells the sound team exactly when each sound starts. Students can test their plan by reading the scenes aloud while a partner watches a clock with a second hand and makes the sounds. Accept any music and sound effects that fit the action and mood of each scene.

## Warm up: Time in seconds
fill:
  - 1 minute = {60} seconds
  - Half a minute = {30} seconds
  - A scene from 20 to 35 seconds lasts {15} seconds.
  - A 10-second scene that starts at 40 seconds ends at {50} seconds.

## Main: The Runaway Pancake (1 minute)
text: **Scene 1:** A pancake flips out of the pan. **Scene 2:** It rolls out the kitchen door. **Scene 3:** A dog chases it down the street. **Scene 4:** It rolls onto a plate at a picnic, and everyone cheers.
table: Scene | Start (sec) | End (sec) | Length (sec) | Music | Sound effect
  - 1 | 0 | 12 | {12} | bouncy and happy | sizzle, then boing
  - 2 | 12 | {25} | 13 | |
  - 3 | {25} | 45 | {20} | |
  - 4 | 45 | {60} | {15} | |
choice: Which music fits Scene 3 best?
  - Slow, quiet bells
  * Fast drums and quick, jumpy notes
  - A sleepy lullaby
  - No music at all
choice: Which sound effect fits Scene 4 best?
  - A door creaking
  - Thunder
  * A soft plop and people cheering
  - A dog growling
write: Explain the music and sound effect you chose for Scene 2.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. A good answer fits the action and mood, such as rolling music that speeds up, with a door creak or a "whoosh" as the pancake rolls out.

## Stretch
write: The director wants Scene 3 to be 5 seconds shorter, but the cartoon must still last exactly 1 minute. Give the new start and end times for Scenes 3 and 4.
  lines: 3
  key: Scene 3: 25 to 40 seconds (15 seconds). Scene 4: 40 to 60 seconds (20 seconds). Another fair answer gives the 5 seconds to Scene 1 or 2 and shifts the later start and end times so everything still ends at 60.

# 33. Feel It or Believe It?
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can explain how music, color and words in a commercial try to make me feel or believe something.
directions: Read about the four pretend commercials. For each one, tell how the music and colors try to make you feel and what the words want you to believe. Then answer the questions.
mistakes: Students often write what is in the commercial ("drums") instead of what it does ("the loud drums make it feel exciting"). Push for "makes me feel ..." and "wants me to believe ...". Some decide every commercial is lying; the point is to notice the tools, not to call every ad bad.
notes: All products are made up. Feelings are what a commercial tries to make you feel (excited, calm, safe); beliefs are ideas about the product it wants you to accept (it is the best, it will make you a winner). Accept reasonable answers that match the details. In the key, the music and color answers are examples.

## Warm up
choice: A commercial plays soft, slow music. How does it probably want you to feel?
  - angry
  - in a hurry
  * calm
choice: The words say, "The number one snack for champions!" What do they want you to believe?
  * that the snack will make you a winner
  - that the snack is calm
  - that the snack is cheap

## Main: Four pretend commercials
text: **1. Product A cereal:** fast, bouncy music; bright yellow and orange colors; the words "The most fun breakfast ever!"
text: **2. Product B bandages:** soft piano music; light blue and white colors; the words "Gentle on every scrape."
text: **3. Product C video game:** loud drums; dark purple and red colors; the words "Are you brave enough?"
text: **4. Product D sneakers:** a cheering crowd; shiny gold and team colors; the words "Be a winner."
table: Commercial | The music makes me feel | The colors make me feel | The words want me to believe
  height: 0.75
  - 1. Cereal | {excited, happy} | {cheerful, full of energy} | {this cereal makes breakfast fun}
  - 2. Bandages | {calm, safe} | {clean, calm, cared for} | {these bandages will not hurt}
  - 3. Video game | {thrilled, a little nervous} | {mysterious, a bit scary} | {only brave kids play it, and you should be brave}
  - 4. Sneakers | {proud, like a star} | {special, like a champion} | {these sneakers will make you a winner}

## Think about it
write: Which commercial do you think works best? Tell how its music, color and words work together.
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Model: "The sneaker one works best because the cheering crowd makes you feel like you won, the gold color looks like a medal, and the words say 'Be a winner,' so everything says the same thing."
write: Pick one commercial. What would you want to know before you believe its words?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: For the bandages, I would want to know if they really do not hurt when you pull them off, maybe from someone who has used them.

## Stretch
write: Plan a commercial for a pretend lunchbox that makes people feel calm. What music, colors and words would you use?
  lines: 3
  key: Answers vary. Model: soft guitar music, light green and blue colors, and the words "Lunch made easy." Every choice should point toward calm.
