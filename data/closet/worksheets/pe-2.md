# 1. Movement Combo Cards
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can put three moves together in an order and do them smoothly.
directions: (Read aloud.) Match each move to its cue. Then cut out the move cards and glue three into each combo box, in the order you will do them. Write each order in the chart. Try each combo in the gym.
mistakes: Slide and gallop get confused: both have a lead foot and a catch-up foot, but a slide goes sideways and a gallop goes forward. A leap is not a big jump: it takes off from one foot and lands on the other.
notes: Any order is right as long as each combo uses all three moves and the three combos are different from each other; the key shows one set. Let partners perform a combo for each other and say which move came first, next and last. A gallop, slide and leap can be done in 6 different orders.

## Warm up: Match the cue
match: Write the letter of the cue next to each move.
  - gallop = One foot leads forward. The back foot catches up but never passes.
  - slide = Move sideways: step, together. Your feet never cross.
  - leap = Push off one foot, stretch long in the air, land on the other foot.
  - hop = Take off and land on the same one foot.
  - jump = Take off from two feet and land on two feet.

## Main: Build your combos
sort: Glue 3 move cards in each box, first move on top.
  - Combo 1: gallop, slide, leap
  - Combo 2: slide, leap, gallop
  - Combo 3: leap, gallop, slide
table: Combo | First | Next | Last
  - 1 | | |
  - 2 | | |
  - 3 | | |

## Stretch: Make it new
write: Pick one combo. Add a change: fast or slow, or high or low. Write your new combo.
  lines: 2
  starter: I will ...
  key: Answers vary. Example: I will gallop fast, slide slow, then leap high.

# 7. Locomotor Fill in the Blank
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can use cue words to tell how to do each locomotor skill.
directions: (Read aloud.) Name each move in the warm up. Read each cue sentence with your teacher and fill in the blank with a word from the word bank. Use each word once. Then draw one cue.
mistakes: Hop and jump get swapped (one foot or two feet), and so do gallop and slide (a gallop moves forward with the same foot in front; a slide moves sideways and the feet never cross). Have children stand and try each cue before they write the word.
notes: Key: 1 cross, 2 front, 3 one, 4 two, 5 hop, 6 bent, 7 other, 8 ground. Warm-up key: hop, jump, gallop, skip. Read each sentence aloud twice with the blank as "hmm", then let children act it out to choose the word. For the drawing, accept any picture that shows the cue: feet apart and not crossing for a slide, one foot down for a hop, and so on.

## Warm up: Name the move
text: (Read aloud.) Word bank: hop, jump, gallop, skip
picword:
  - hop | {hop}
  - jump | {jump}
  - gallop | {gallop}
  - skip | {skip}

## Main: Cue sentences
text: **Word bank:** cross, front, one, two, hop, bent, other, ground
fill:
  - In a slide, my feet never {cross}.
  - In a gallop, the same foot always stays in {front}.
  - In a hop, I take off and land on {one} foot.
  - In a jump, I take off and land on {two} feet.
  - In a skip, I do a step and a {hop} on the same foot.
  - When I run, my arms swing with my elbows {bent}.
  - In a leap, I take off on one foot and land on the {other} foot.
  - When I walk, one foot always touches the {ground}.

## Stretch: Draw a cue
draw: Pick one cue sentence. Draw a child doing it. Write the sentence number in the corner. | 2

# 20. Target Game Plan
minutes: 30 (15 to plan, 15 to play)
materials: pencil; to play: beanbags, hoops, buckets or cones, and tape for a throw line
ican: I can plan a target game that is fair and safe, and try it with a partner.
directions: (Read aloud.) Answer the warm-up questions. Then plan a target game for you and one partner: pick your targets, how far away to stand, the points and one safety rule. Draw your game. Then play it and keep score.
mistakes: Children often make every target worth the same points or make the hardest target the closest one. Ask: "Which target is hardest to hit? Should it be worth more?" Safety rules are often vague ("be safe"); push for an action, such as "Wait until both players finish throwing before anyone picks up a beanbag."
notes: Warm-up key: 1 A (big and close is easiest), 2 B (small and far is hardest, so it earns the most). Plans will vary. A good plan names real targets, gives a throw line in steps or a tape mark, gives more points for harder targets, sets the same number of throws for each player, and has a safety rule that is an action. Give each pair a space along a wall so no one throws toward another pair. Check the plan before they collect equipment.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) Which target is easiest to hit?
  * A big hoop close to you
  - A small cup far away
choice: (Read aloud.) Which target should be worth the most points?
  - A big hoop close to you
  * A small bucket far away

## Main: My plan
example: Sample plan: Targets: a hoop (1 point) and a bucket (3 points). Throw line: 4 big steps from the hoop. Each player throws 5 beanbags a turn. Safety rule: wait until both players finish throwing before anyone picks up beanbags.

## Stretch
write: Was your game too easy, too hard or just right? What is one change that would make it better?
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: It was too easy because we hit the hoop every time. We will move the throw line 2 steps back.

# 21. Manipulative Skills Sort
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can sort ball skills into throwing, catching, dribbling and kicking.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at each picture card and read its name. Cut out the word cards at the bottom. Glue each one under the skill it uses.
mistakes: Soccer dribble is the tricky card: dribbling is tapping the ball again and again to move it along, and in soccer you do it with your feet, so it goes under dribbling, not kicking. A football can be thrown or kicked: read the action word, not just the ball.
notes: Read every card name together first. The cap stands for baseball and the mitt for catching. Skill cues to review: throwing, step with the opposite foot; catching, reach out with hands ready and pull the ball in; dribbling, push the ball with finger pads (hand) or the inside of the foot (feet) and keep it close; kicking, step beside the ball and swing the kicking leg through.

## Warm up
example: (Read aloud.) A **manipulative skill** moves an object with your hands, your feet or a tool. **Dribbling** means tapping a ball again and again to move it with you.

## Main: Picture cards
picword:
  - cap | baseball throw
  - football | football pass
  - beanbag | beanbag toss
  - mitt | fly ball catch
  - beanbag | beanbag catch
  - basketball | basketball dribble
  - soccer-ball | soccer dribble
  - playground-ball | kickball kick
  - football | football punt
  - soccer-ball | soccer pass

## Sort: Glue each card under its skill
sort: Throwing, catching, dribbling or kicking?
  - Throwing: baseball throw, football pass, beanbag toss
  - Catching: fly ball catch, beanbag catch
  - Dribbling: basketball dribble, soccer dribble
  - Kicking: kickball kick, football punt, soccer pass

## Stretch
choice: (Read aloud.) A soccer player dribbles the ball with her ...
  - hands
  * feet
  - head
write: (Read aloud.) Name two skills from the sort that you use in a basketball game.
  lines: 2
  key: Any two of dribbling, throwing (passing) and catching.

# 33. Teamwork Code
minutes: 20
materials: pencil
ican: I can name ways to be a good teammate in a game.
directions: (Read aloud.) Read each teamwork sentence and find the word that fills the ___ in the decoder. Write the word on the line. Then write the letter that goes with it in the box with the same number. The letters spell a hidden team motto.
mistakes: Children may write the first word that fits the sentence instead of a word from the decoder. Some of the decoder words do not fit any sentence: grab, quit and push are not teamwork. Ask, "Would a good teammate do this?"
notes: Key: 1 turn (T), 2 try (E), 3 share (A), 4 rules (M), 5 game (U), 6 listen (P). The motto is TEAM UP. Grab, quit and push are decoys. After the sheet, say the motto together before a cooperative game, and ask children to catch a teammate doing one of the six things.

## Warm up
choice: (Read aloud.) Your team has one ball and four players. What is a teamwork choice?
  - Keep the ball so you can score.
  * Pass the ball so everyone gets a turn.

## Main: Crack the code
code: Find each missing word in the decoder.
  decoys: grab quit push
  - In a game, we wait for our ___ | turn | T
  - A teammate misses. We say, "Nice ___!" | try | E
  - We ___ the ball so everyone can play | share | A
  - We follow the ___ of the game | rules | M
  - Win or lose, we say, "Good ___." | game | U
  - We ___ to each other's ideas | listen | P

## Stretch
write: The motto says TEAM UP. Tell one thing you will do to team up in our next game.
  lines: 2
  key: Answers vary. Example: I will pass the ball to a friend who has not had a turn.
