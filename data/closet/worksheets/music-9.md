# 1. Circle of Fifths Builder
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, ruler, compass or a round object to trace
ican: I can build the circle of fifths with every major key, its relative minor and its key signature.
directions: Learn the order of sharps and flats and two shortcuts. Complete the key chart for all 15 major keys. Then draw the circle from your chart.
mistakes: Students mix up the order of sharps (F C G D A E B) and flats (B E A D G C F); they are the same letters in reverse. Watch for relative minors a minor 3rd up instead of down (the relative minor of C is A, not Eb). F major has one flat (Bb), so F is not on the sharp side even though it begins the order of sharps.
notes: The circle clockwise from 12 o'clock: C, G, D, A, E, B/Cb, F#/Gb, C#/Db, Ab, Eb, Bb, F. Three positions hold two names for the same sounding key (enharmonic keys): B (5 sharps) and Cb (7 flats), F# (6 sharps) and Gb (6 flats), C# (7 sharps) and Db (5 flats). That is how 12 positions hold 15 major keys. A relative minor starts on the 6th note of its major scale, 3 half steps below the tonic, and shares the key signature. Shortcuts: in a sharp key, the last sharp is a half step below the tonic; in a flat key, the next-to-last flat names the key (F major, with one flat, is the one to memorize).

## Warm up: Orders and shortcuts
fill:
  - Order of sharps: F C {G} D {A} E {B}
  - Order of flats: B {E} A {D} G C {F}
  - Each step clockwise around the circle goes up a perfect {5th} and adds one {sharp} (or takes away one flat).
  - A major key's last sharp is D#. Go up a half step: the key is {E} major.
  - A major key's flats are Bb Eb Ab Db. The next-to-last flat names the key: {Ab} major.
  - The relative minor of a major key starts {3} half steps below its tonic.

## Stretch: Neighbors
write: Why are keys next to each other on the circle called closely related? Use G major and D major as your example.
  lines: 3
  key: Neighbors differ by only one sharp or flat, so they share six of their seven notes. G major (F#) and D major (F# C#) differ only in C versus C#, which makes it easy to modulate between them.
write: Which three positions on the circle hold two keys, and why do both names work?
  lines: 3
  key: Positions 5 (B and Cb), 6 (F# and Gb) and 7 (C# and Db). The two keys in each pair sound the same on a keyboard (they are enharmonic) but are spelled with sharps on one side of the circle and flats on the other.

# 21. Music History Checkpoint 9
minutes: 40
materials: pencil
ican: I can place works in their eras, recognize each era's style traits, and explain how music changed between eras.
directions: Write each work's letter on the timeline where you think it belongs, match each to its era, identify eras from short style descriptions, and then explain one change between eras with evidence.
mistakes: Students often memorize dates but cannot hear or describe the difference between eras; push for a trait (texture, instruments, form) in every answer. Watch for Baroque and Classical traits swapped: the basso continuo and terraced dynamics are Baroque; balanced phrases, crescendos and sonata form are Classical.
notes: Era dates are approximate and overlap: Medieval about 500 to 1400, Renaissance about 1400 to 1600, Baroque about 1600 to 1750, Classical about 1750 to 1820, Romantic about 1820 to 1900, then the 20th century. Timeline dates: Palestrina's Pope Marcellus Mass was published in 1567; Handel's Messiah was first performed in Dublin in 1742; Mozart finished Eine kleine Nachtmusik in 1787; Beethoven's Fifth Symphony premiered in 1808; Tchaikovsky's The Nutcracker premiered in 1892; Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring premiered in Paris in 1913. Students place the works from what they know of the eras, so accept any letter that lands in the right era rather than requiring the exact year. Beethoven's Fifth is left out of the era match on purpose: it is often taught as a bridge between Classical and Romantic, a good discussion point.

## Stretch: Explain a change
write: Choose two neighboring eras. Explain one important change in the music from the first to the second. Give evidence from a work or a trait on this sheet.
  lines: 6
  starter: From the ... era to the ... era, music changed because ...
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names a specific change and evidence, for example: "From the Baroque to the Classical era, dynamics changed from sudden terraced contrasts to gradual crescendos. Baroque pieces like Messiah were accompanied by harpsichord and organ, which cannot easily swell, while Classical composers like Mozart wrote for the new piano, which can grow louder and softer as the player presses harder."
