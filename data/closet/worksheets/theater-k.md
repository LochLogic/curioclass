# 1. Magic Scarf: What Can It Be?
minutes: 20
materials: crayons, pencil, a plain lightweight scarf (one per child, or a few to share)
ican: I can pretend an object is something new.
directions: (Read aloud.) Look at your scarf. What else could it be in a pretend story? Draw 4 things your scarf could become. Then circle the one you will act out.
mistakes: Many children draw the scarf itself or copy the examples (a cape, a river). Ask "What is your scarf pretending to be?" and offer a nudge: "Could it be an animal? Weather? Something you eat with?" Any idea counts if the child can show how the scarf becomes it.
notes: Play first, draw second: pass out the scarves and let children try ideas for a few minutes. Ideas children often find: a cape, a river, a snake, a flag, a blanket for a doll, a tail, wings, a sail, a wave, a mop, a bandage. Safety: scarves drape over shoulders or arms; never tie one around a neck. Finish by letting each child show the circled idea while the class guesses.

## Warm up: Listen and circle
choice: (Read aloud.) Sam holds a scarf on his shoulders. It flaps behind him as he runs and flies like a hero. What is his scarf pretending to be?
  * A cape
  - A sandwich
  - A shoe
choice: (Read aloud.) Lena lays a long blue scarf on the floor. She jumps over it so she does not get wet. What is her scarf pretending to be?
  - A hat
  * A river
  - A cup

# 7. Sound Effect Story: The Rainy Day
minutes: 20
materials: crayon or pencil
ican: I can use my body to make sounds for a story.
directions: (Read aloud.) Learn the 4 body sounds. Listen to the rain story. At each star, color the box under the sound that fits. Then we will tell the story together and make the sounds.
mistakes: Children often pick their favorite sound (stomp!) every time. Read each star part twice and ask, "Is the rain soft or loud now?" Soft rain gets small sounds (tap, pat); loud rain and thunder get big sounds (clap, stomp).
notes: Teach the sounds first: tap (one finger taps the palm or the table), pat (hands pat the knees), clap, stomp (one foot stomps). Read the story slowly and stop at each star. Point to each row and its star as you read. The key gives the sounds the story hints at; accept a different choice if the child can say why it fits (for example, clap for thunder). Finish by performing the whole story: you read, the class makes the sounds, getting louder to the thunder and softer at the end.

# 20. What Does Fox Like?
minutes: 20
materials: crayons, pencil; for the show: a fox puppet (a sock puppet works) and a few small props (berries, a ball, a spray bottle or a blue scarf for rain)
ican: I can tell what a puppet character likes and does not like.
directions: (Read aloud.) Watch the fox puppet show. Draw 3 things Fox likes in the heart boxes. Draw 1 thing Fox does not like in the last box.
mistakes: Children may draw what they like themselves. Ask, "What did Fox say? How did Fox's face and body look?" Some mix up likes and does not like; replay the rain part and ask how Fox moved (hiding, shaking).
notes: The show (about 2 minutes). Fox wakes up and stretches: "Mmm, sunshine! I love the warm sun!" Fox finds berries: "Yum, yum, red berries. My favorite!" (munching). Fox finds a ball and bounces it: "Boing! I love to play ball!" Then the rain comes (sprinkle a little water or wave a blue scarf). Fox hides and shakes: "Oh no! I do NOT like rain. My fur gets all wet!" The sun comes back and Fox does a happy spin. Likes: the sun, berries, playing ball. Does not like: rain. Do the show twice; the second time, children can call out "Likes!" or "Does not like!"

# 21. The Lost Mitten Puppet Show Order
minutes: 25
materials: scissors, glue stick, crayons; for the show: two puppets (any animal puppets or sock puppets) and one mitten
ican: I can tell the beginning, middle and end of a puppet show.
directions: (Read aloud.) Watch the puppet show. Cut out the 4 scene cards. Glue each card in order: first, next, then, last. Draw a tiny picture on each card to help you remember it.
mistakes: Children who glue the happy ending first are often remembering the strongest feeling; retell the show using "first, next, then, last" while they point to each box. The cards have words a kindergartner cannot read yet: read every card aloud twice, and let children draw a small picture on each card before they cut.
notes: The show (about 2 minutes, two puppets, Bear and Bunny). First: Bear plays in the snow, clapping two warm mittens together. Next: Bear's paw feels cold; one mitten is gone! Bear is sad. Then: Bear and Bunny look for it: under the snow, behind a tree, in a bush. Last: Bunny finds the mitten on a branch, and Bear says "Thank you!" with warm paws again. Do the show twice. After gluing, children can retell it to a partner with their hands as puppets.

## Warm up: Listen
choice: (Read aloud.) How did Bear feel when the mitten was gone?
  - Happy
  * Sad
  - Sleepy

## Main: Cut and glue the scenes in order
sort: Glue each scene card in its box.
  - 1 First: Bear plays in the snow.
  - 2 Next: A mitten is gone!
  - 3 Then: Bear and Bunny look.
  - 4 Last: Bunny finds it!

# 33. Ask the Commander
minutes: 20
materials: pencil, crayons
ican: I can ask a question in role to help my crew.
directions: (Read aloud.) We are the moon crew. Our commander can help us save the plants in the moon greenhouse. Trace "Can we" two times. Then draw what you will ask the commander.
mistakes: Children often draw a moon picture with no question in it. Ask, "What do you want the commander to say yes to?" Some say a statement ("I like rockets"); help them start with "Can we...?"
notes: The teacher plays the commander, in role (a jacket or a badge marks the role). Use this after the Moon Facts sheet: the plants need water, light and warmth because the moon has no air, no rain and long cold nights. Children ask their questions in role: "Can we turn on the big lamps?" "Can we use the water tank?" "Can we make the greenhouse warm?" The commander answers each one as a real choice ("Yes, but which tank?"). Any question that helps the crew and starts with "Can we" counts.

## Warm up: Which one is a question?
choice: (Read aloud.) Which one is a question for the commander?
  - I like the moon.
  * Can we water the plants?
  - The rocket is big.
