# 1. Clue Finder: How Do Others Feel?
minutes: 25
materials: pencil, highlighter or colored pencil
ican: I can use words and actions as clues to tell how someone feels.
directions: Read each scene. Underline the words and actions that show how the character feels. Then write the feeling from the word bank and copy one clue you underlined.
mistakes: Students underline what happened (the rain, the trip) instead of the clue (shoulders sank, sighed). Ask "What did the character do or say that shows the feeling?" In scene 4, sad or left out are fair answers too if the student can point to a clue.
notes: The scenes are original to Curio Class. Model scene 1 together, thinking aloud: "Her hands shook. That is a body clue." Clues come in three kinds: body (shaking hands, red cheeks), actions (kicked the dirt), and words ("Can I go last?"). A follow-up: students act out a scene and the class names the clues.

## Warm up
example: Jo **slammed the door** and **stomped** down the hall. Clues: slammed the door, stomped. Feeling: angry.

## Main: Find the clues
passage: Six Scenes
  1. Lena stood at the front of the room with her poem. Her hands shook, and she whispered, "Can I go last?"
  2. Omar held up the bridge he had built from craft sticks. He grinned and showed it to everyone who walked by.
  3. Rosa saw rain pouring down outside. "No field trip today," said Mr. Diaz. Rosa's shoulders sank, and she let out a long sigh.
  4. At recess, Ben sat alone on the bench. He watched the others play tag and kicked at the dirt with his shoe.
  5. Kai bounced on his toes in line. "Is it time yet? Is it time yet?" he asked, again and again, before the puppet show.
  6. Mia tripped on the stage steps, and a few kids giggled. Her cheeks turned red, and she covered her face with her hands.
text: Word bank: nervous, proud, disappointed, lonely, excited, embarrassed
table: Scene | Feeling | One clue you underlined
  - 1. Lena | {nervous} | {hands shook; whispered "Can I go last?"}
  - 2. Omar | {proud} | {grinned; showed it to everyone}
  - 3. Rosa | {disappointed} | {shoulders sank; long sigh}
  - 4. Ben | {lonely} | {sat alone; watched others; kicked the dirt}
  - 5. Kai | {excited} | {bounced on his toes; "Is it time yet?"}
  - 6. Mia | {embarrassed} | {cheeks turned red; covered her face}

## Stretch
write: Pick one scene. What could a friend say or do to help that character?
  lines: 3
  starter: In scene ... a friend could ...
  key: Answers vary. For example: In scene 4, a friend could say "Want to play tag with us?" In scene 1, a friend could say "You practiced. You can do it," or offer to stand nearby.

# 6. Perspective Words at Work
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, crayons or colored pencils
ican: I can use the words empathy, perspective, point of view and clue to talk about how others see and feel things.
directions: Match each word to its meaning. Then read each of the four scenes and write one sentence about it, using the word in bold. Last, sketch one scene and label it with the word.
mistakes: Students mix up perspective and point of view. In this sheet, point of view is where someone is standing or whose eyes we see through; perspective is how someone thinks about a thing because of what matters to them. Others use empathy to mean "feeling sorry for"; empathy is understanding the feeling from the inside, as if you were in the person's place.
notes: The scenes are original to Curio Class. Kid-friendly meanings used here: empathy, understanding and sharing how someone else feels; perspective, the way a person thinks about something, shaped by what they know and care about; point of view, what someone can see or know from where they are; clue, a small detail that helps you figure something out. Accept any sentence that uses the word correctly about the scene.

## Warm up: Match the words
match: Draw a line, or write the letter
  - empathy = understanding and sharing how someone else feels
  - perspective = the way a person thinks about something, shaped by what they know and care about
  - point of view = what someone can see or know from where they are
  - clue = a small detail that helps you figure something out

## Main: Use the word
example: Scene: Gus forgot his lines in the class play and ran off the stage. Word: **empathy**. Sentence: Mia showed **empathy** when she said, "That happened to me once. It feels awful."
write: Nia's dog is sick at the vet. At recess, Nia sits on the bench and does not want to play. Write a sentence about this scene using **empathy**.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: Jake showed empathy when he sat next to Nia and said, "I'd be worried too if my dog were sick."
write: It rains all morning. Carlos cheers because his bean plants need water. Jen groans because the class picnic is canceled. Write a sentence about this scene using **perspective**.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: From Carlos's perspective the rain is good news for his garden, but from Jen's perspective it ruined the picnic.
write: In a soccer game, the goalie dives and the ball rolls past her. From behind the goal, Coach Ray sees it stop just before the line. From the far end of the field, the fans think it went in. Write a sentence about this scene using **point of view**.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: From Coach Ray's point of view behind the goal, the ball never crossed the line, but the fans' point of view was too far away to see it.
write: During reading time, Mr. Lee sees Tasha yawn three times. Her head keeps dropping onto her hand. Write a sentence about this scene using **clue**.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: Tasha's yawning was a clue that she was very tired.

## Stretch: Sketch it
draw: Sketch one scene. Label it with the word you used. | 2.2

# 7. Comic Strip Choices: Listening to Understand
minutes: 30
materials: pencil, scissors, glue stick
ican: I can choose a reply that shows I understand how someone feels.
directions: Read each comic. Cut out the replies at the bottom of the page. Glue the reply that shows the listener really understood into the last box of each comic. Then explain one choice.
mistakes: Students often pick the "fix it" reply ("Just draw a new one") because it sounds helpful. Talk about how fixing too fast can feel like not listening. A reply about yourself ("I lost a hat once") turns the talk away from the speaker.
notes: There is no wrong feeling here, only replies that do or do not show listening. Read the comics aloud with two voices for students who need it. A good follow-up: pairs act out one strip using the reply they chose.

## Warm up
example: Listening to understand means saying back what the person feels, like "That sounds frustrating," before you give advice.

## Main: Pick the reply
comic: The Lost Drawing
  - A: I worked on my drawing all week.
  - A: Then I left it on the bus, and now it's gone.
  * You worked so hard on it. That must feel awful.
  - I lost a hat once. Want to play tag?
  - Just draw a new one. It's no big deal.
comic: The Big Move
  - A: My family is moving after spring break.
  - A: I'm excited, but I'll miss everyone here.
  * So you feel two ways at once: excited and sad?
  - Cool! I want to move too.
  - Don't be sad. You'll make new friends.
comic: The Science Fair
  - A: My volcano didn't erupt at the science fair.
  - A: Everyone was watching. I wanted to hide.
  * That sounds embarrassing. Do you want to tell me what happened?
  - Mine worked great!
  - Volcanoes are boring anyway.
comic: Left Out
  - A: Everyone played soccer at recess today.
  - A: Nobody asked me to join.
  * You felt left out. Want to play with me tomorrow?
  - Soccer is fun. You should play.
  - Why do you care so much?
replies: Cut out the replies. Glue one into each comic.

## Explain
write: Pick one comic. Why does the reply you chose show the listener understood?
  lines: 3
  starter: In the comic about ... the reply shows understanding because ...
  key: Answers vary. A strong answer names the feeling the reply says back (awful, excited and sad, embarrassed, left out) and notes it keeps the focus on the speaker.

# 20. Perspective Taking Review
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can use clues to figure out what others think and feel, judge what is fair, and choose caring responses.
directions: Read the three scenes. Then answer the questions. Use clues from the scenes in your answers.
mistakes: Students answer the "thinking" questions with what they themselves would think. Push for a clue: "What in the scene makes you think so?" On fairness, some students say the kickball vote is fair because most people voted. A vote can follow the rules and still leave someone out; the question is whether everyone can take part.
notes: The scenes are original to Curio Class and review the module: clues to feelings (scene 1), fairness (scene 2), and thoughts behind actions (scene 3), with caring responses throughout. Wren's cast is temporary; the point is planning so everyone can join, not feeling sorry for her. Multiple choice answers: 1 C, 3 A, 4 D, 7 B.

## Read: Three scenes
passage: Three Scenes
  **Scene 1.** At lunch, Dante tripped and dropped his tray. Milk splashed across the floor, and a few kids laughed. Dante's face turned red. He stared at the floor and did not move. Priya grabbed a stack of napkins and knelt down beside him.
  **Scene 2.** The class voted on a game for Fun Friday. Most hands went up for kickball. Wren, who had a cast on her leg, looked down at her crutches. "I guess I'll keep score again," she said quietly.
  **Scene 3.** Theo finished his math quiz first. "Great job, Theo! So fast!" said Ms. Park in front of the class. Ellie was still on question 4. She sighed, erased hard, and pulled her paper closer.

## Questions
choice: Which clues show that Dante feels embarrassed?
  - Milk splashed across the floor.
  - Priya grabbed napkins.
  * His face turned red and he stared at the floor.
  - He was eating lunch.
write: What might Dante be thinking? Use a clue.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: "Everyone is looking at me. I wish I could disappear." His red face and staring at the floor are clues.
choice: Which reply would also show caring to Dante?
  * "That happens to everyone. Want me to help you get a new tray?"
  - "Ha! That was so loud!"
  - "You should watch where you walk."
  - "Why are you just standing there?"
choice: Was the kickball vote fair to Wren?
  - Yes, because most people voted for kickball.
  - Yes, because Wren likes to keep score.
  - No, because votes are never fair.
  * Not fully, because Wren cannot play, so she is left out again.
write: Give one caring way the class could make Fun Friday fair for everyone.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: Pick a game Wren can play too, like a board game tournament, or change kickball so Wren can be the pitcher sitting down, or take turns choosing games each week.
write: What might Ellie be thinking after Ms. Park's praise? Use a clue.
  lines: 2
  key: Sample: "I'm so slow. Everyone will think I'm bad at math." Her sigh and hard erasing are clues that she feels frustrated or worried.
choice: Which change would be most caring to the whole class in scene 3?
  - Ms. Park praises only the fastest students.
  * Ms. Park praises careful work and effort, not just speed.
  - Ms. Park tells Ellie to hurry up.
  - Ms. Park stops giving quizzes.

## Stretch
write: Retell scene 3 in two or three sentences from Ellie's perspective, using "I."
  lines: 4
  key: Sample: I was still stuck on question 4 when Ms. Park said "So fast!" to Theo. My face got hot, and I erased so hard I almost ripped my paper. I wanted to do it right, not just fast.

# 33. Self-Talk Switch
minutes: 25
materials: pencil
ican: I can switch discouraged self-talk into encouraging self-talk that helps me keep trying.
directions: Mateo is learning to ride a bike. Read each discouraged thought he has. Switch it into encouraging self-talk that is true and helpful. Use the switch tips.
mistakes: Students often switch a thought into something untrue ("I'm the best bike rider ever!"). Encouraging self-talk is honest: it admits the hard part and points to a next step. Others just add "not" ("I can not do this"); the switch should change the meaning, not just the grammar.
notes: Mateo is a made-up character. Switch tips come from growth mindset language used across this module: add "yet," name a strategy, remember past progress, and treat mistakes as information. Sample switches are in the answer key; any honest, encouraging switch that fits the thought earns credit.

## Warm up: Switch tips
example: **Add "yet":** "I can't balance" becomes "I can't balance **yet**."
  **Name a strategy:** "Next time I'll look ahead, not down at my feet."
  **Remember progress:** "Yesterday I could not even start. Today I rode three pedals."
  **Learn from the mistake:** "I fell when I turned fast. Now I know to turn slowly."
choice: Which thought is encouraging self-talk?
  - "I'll never get this."
  * "This is hard, but I'm getting closer."
  - "Everyone is better than me."

## Main: Switch the thought
table: Mateo's discouraged thought | Mateo's encouraging switch
  height: 0.6
  - 1. "I'll never learn to ride this bike." |
  - 2. "I fell again. I'm so bad at this." |
  - 3. "My little cousin can ride, so something is wrong with me." |
  - 4. "I keep wobbling. I should just quit." |
  - 5. "Everyone at the park is watching me mess up." |
  - 6. "I'll never be able to ride without training wheels." |
note: Sample switches. 1. "I can't ride yet, but every try teaches my body something." 2. "I fell, but I rode farther than last time. I'll pedal faster so I can balance." 3. "Everyone learns at a different time. I'm learning too." 4. "Wobbling means I'm learning to balance. I'll keep my eyes up and try again." 5. "Most people are busy with their own things. And everyone fell when they learned." 6. "I can't do it yet. I'll practice a little every day."

## Stretch
write: Mateo finally rides to the end of the block! Write what he could tell himself to remember the next time something new feels hard.
  lines: 3
  key: Sample: "Remember the bike? I fell a lot and kept trying, and I got there. This new thing will take practice too."
