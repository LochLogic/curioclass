# 11. Melting Ice Temperature Log
minutes: 45
materials: crushed ice, a clear cup, a larger bowl of warm tap water, a thermometer (digital or alcohol), a timer, safety goggles, pencil
ican: I can collect temperature data and explain what happens to energy while ice melts.
directions: Work with a partner. Set the cup of crushed ice in the warm water bath. Read the temperature of the ice every minute until it has all melted, then keep going for 3 more minutes. Graph your data and explain the flat part.
mistakes: Students often expect the temperature to rise steadily. The flat stretch at about 0 °C is the point: the energy is going into breaking the ice's structure (melting), not into raising the temperature. Thermometers resting on the cup wall read the warm bath, not the ice: keep the bulb in the middle of the ice and stir gently.
notes: Use warm tap water only, never boiling. A digital probe thermometer works best; no mercury thermometers. Expect a start around -2 to 0 °C, a plateau near 0 °C for 6 to 10 minutes while ice remains, then a rise toward the bath temperature. The key graph shows typical data, not the only right answer.

## Before you start
write: Predict: What will the temperature do while the ice is melting? Sketch or describe it.
  lines: 2
  key: Many students predict a steady rise. Accept any prediction with a reason; the data will test it.

## Explain
write: Find the flat part of your graph. What temperature was it, and how long did it last?
  lines: 2
  key: About 0 °C, for several minutes (typically 6 to 10) while ice was still in the cup.
write: The warm water kept adding energy the whole time. Where did that energy go during the flat part?
  lines: 3
  key: Into melting the ice: breaking the bonds that hold water molecules in the solid structure, so the temperature stayed at 0 °C until all the ice was liquid.
write: What would the graph look like if you started with twice as much ice? Explain.
  lines: 2
  key: The flat part would last longer, because more ice needs more energy to melt; the plateau would still be at 0 °C.
