# 1. Passive, Aggressive or Assertive?
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell passive, aggressive and assertive responses apart and speak up assertively.
directions: Read each situation and the response. Label the response passive, aggressive or assertive. Then rewrite three responses so they are assertive.
mistakes: Students label sarcasm (row 9) as assertive because it is not shouting. Sarcasm and put-downs attack the person, so they are aggressive. Another mix-up: polite but silent giving in (row 8) feels nice, yet it is passive because the speaker's own need disappears.
notes: Definitions used here. **Passive:** gives up your own needs or stays silent to avoid trouble. **Aggressive:** gets your needs met by attacking, blaming, threatening or putting down the other person. **Assertive:** states your needs clearly and calmly while respecting the other person, often with an "I" statement and a request. The situations are about characters, so nobody has to share their own.

## Warm up
example: Someone keeps kicking your chair. Passive: say nothing and get upset. Aggressive: "Stop it, you weirdo!" Assertive: "**Please stop kicking my chair. It's distracting me.**"

## Main: Label each response
table: # | Situation | Response | Label
  - 1 | Someone cuts in front of Jae in the lunch line. | "Hey, the line starts back there. Please wait your turn." | {assertive }
  - 2 | Someone cuts in front of Rosa in the lunch line. | She sighs, says nothing and stews about it all afternoon. | {passive   }
  - 3 | Someone cuts in front of Tom in the lunch line. | "Move, or you'll be sorry." | {aggressive}
  - 4 | A group member has not started their slides. | "I need your slides by Thursday so we can practice. Can you do that?" | {assertive }
  - 5 | A friend borrows Mina's hoodie without asking. | Mina says nothing, even though she is upset. | {passive   }
  - 6 | A friend borrows Leo's hoodie without asking. | "You're so selfish. You always take my stuff." | {aggressive}
  - 7 | A teammate never passes to Kofi. | "I'm open a lot. Can you pass to me when I'm free?" | {assertive }
  - 8 | A classmate asks Ivy to do his share of the poster, and she doesn't want to. | "Um, sure, I guess I can do it." | {passive   }
  - 9 | A classmate shares an idea Ben doesn't like. | He rolls his eyes and says, "Great, another genius idea." | {aggressive}
  - 10 | A sibling reads Zara's journal. | "I don't like it when you read my journal. Please ask first." | {assertive }

## Stretch: Make it assertive
write: Rewrite response 3 so it is assertive.
  lines: 2
  key: Excuse me, I was in line. The end of the line is back there.
write: Rewrite response 5 so it is assertive.
  lines: 2
  key: Mina says, "I'd like you to ask before you borrow my hoodie. Can you give it back after school?"
write: Rewrite response 6 so it is assertive.
  lines: 2
  key: I felt upset when you took my hoodie without asking. Please ask me first next time.

# 21. Decision Tree Deep Dive
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, ruler
ican: I can map options and possible outcomes in a decision tree and judge how likely each outcome is.
pairs: decision-tree
directions: Read about Sofia's choice. Build a three-level decision tree: the decision, the three options, and two possible outcomes for each option. Rate how likely each outcome is, using Sofia's facts.
mistakes: Students rate outcomes by what they hope will happen. Ask "Which fact supports that rating?" Also watch for outcomes that are really options ("take band") instead of results ("plays in the spring concert").
notes: Use after the Decision Tree tool. Level 1 is the decision, level 2 the options, level 3 the outcomes. The tree is drawn by hand in the box, then the chart holds the ratings and reasons. Likelihood ratings: likely, possible or unlikely. The key gives model outcomes and ratings; accept others that cite the facts.

## Read
text: **Sofia's choice.** Sofia is in seventh grade and must pick one elective for next semester: **Art**, **Band** or **Journalism**.
text: **Fact 1.** Sofia has never played an instrument. Beginning band students rent an instrument and are asked to practice 20 minutes a day at home.
text: **Fact 2.** Sofia draws in a sketchbook almost every day. The art class ends with a show in the front hall.
text: **Fact 3.** Journalism writes the school newspaper. Sofia's friend Ana is an editor, and every student must write one article each month.
text: **Fact 4.** Sofia says she gets bored when a class is "too easy" and loves learning new skills.

## Main: Build the tree
draw: Draw your tree. Level 1 (left): the decision. Level 2: three branches, one per elective. Level 3: two outcomes on each branch. | 3.4
chart: Elective | Possible outcome (what might happen) | Likely, possible or unlikely? | Fact that supports your rating
  rows: 6
  key: Model answers. Art: her work is in the art show (likely, she draws every day); she gets bored because she already draws a lot (possible). Band: she learns to read music and play a new instrument (likely, she loves new skills and practice is built in); she plays a solo at the first concert (unlikely, she has never played). Journalism: she publishes an article each month (likely, it is required); she works with Ana on stories (likely or possible, Ana is an editor).

## Check the facts
choice: Which outcome is the least likely, based on the facts?
  - Sofia's drawing is in the art show.
  * Sofia plays a solo at the first band concert.
  - Sofia writes an article for the newspaper.
choice: Which fact best supports choosing Band?
  - Sofia's friend Ana is an editor.
  - Sofia draws almost every day.
  * Sofia loves learning new skills.

## Stretch
write: Which elective would you advise Sofia to take? Use your tree's ratings to explain.
  lines: 4
  key: Answers vary. Example: Band, because her most likely outcome is learning a brand new skill, which she loves, and the daily practice keeps it from being too easy. A strong answer cites at least one likely outcome and one risk.
