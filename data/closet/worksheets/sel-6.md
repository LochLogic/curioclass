# 1. Conflict or Not?
minutes: 30
materials: pencil
ican: I can tell when a situation is a conflict and name what kind of conflict it is.
directions: Read each scenario. Write yes if it is a conflict and no if it is not. For each conflict, write its type: resources, values or misunderstanding. If it is not a conflict, write none.
mistakes: Students call any problem a conflict. A flat tire or a nervous feeling is a problem, but a conflict needs people whose needs, wants or ideas clash. Watch scenario 4: two people wanting the same thing is not a conflict once they have agreed.
notes: Definitions used here. **Conflict:** a clash between people over needs, wants or ideas. **Resources:** both sides want something limited (space, time, money, things). **Values:** the sides disagree about what is right or most important. **Misunderstanding:** one side got the wrong idea about what the other meant or did. Some real conflicts mix types; if a student argues a second type with evidence, give credit.

## Warm up
example: Two friends both want the last seat on the bus by the window. Conflict? **yes**. Type: **resources** (one seat, two people).

## Main: Sort the scenarios
table: # | Scenario | Conflict? | Type
  - 1 | Ava and Jun both want the one free library computer for the whole period. | {yes} | {resources       }
  - 2 | Leo's bike gets a flat tire on the way to school. | {no } | {none            }
  - 3 | Sam thinks Tia ignored him in the hall on purpose, but Tia never saw him. | {yes} | {misunderstanding}
  - 4 | Rina and Cole both want the last slice of pizza, so they happily agree to split it. | {no } | {none            }
  - 5 | The chess club and the drama club both want the gym on Thursday afternoon. | {yes} | {resources       }
  - 6 | Maya thinks every player should get equal time in games; Dev thinks the best players should play the most. | {yes} | {values          }
  - 7 | Eli forgets his lunch at home. | {no } | {none            }
  - 8 | Nora texts "fine." Jada reads it as angry, but Nora was just busy. | {yes} | {misunderstanding}
  - 9 | Kim feels nervous before her piano recital. | {no } | {none            }
  - 10 | Lucas thinks the class should tell the teacher who broke a window; Ana thinks they should stay quiet to protect a friend. | {yes} | {values          }

## Stretch
write: Choose one conflict from the table. What is one good first step to resolve it?
  lines: 3
  starter: In scenario ... a good first step would be ...
  key: Answers vary. For example: In scenario 8, Jada could ask Nora, "Are you upset with me?" before reacting. In scenario 5, the clubs could ask the office to split the time or take turns by week.

# 21. Five Steps to a Decision
minutes: 35
materials: pencil
ican: I can use five steps to make a responsible decision.
directions: Read about Jordan's choice. Then use the five steps to help Jordan decide: define the decision, list the options, weigh the consequences, choose, and reflect.
mistakes: Students skip straight to "choose" and pick what they would want. Push them to use Jordan's facts. In the consequences chart, watch for only pros or only cons; each option should have both.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class. There is no single right choice: either option can be a responsible decision if the reasons come from the facts and Jordan's goals. Grade the steps, not the pick. Step 5 asks how Jordan could check the choice later, so it is written as a plan.

## Read
passage: Club or Team?
  Jordan is in sixth grade at Lakeview Middle School. This fall, two after-school activities meet at the same time: the robotics club and the cross-country team. Both meet on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 3:15 to 4:45. Jordan can only pick one.
  Jordan has wanted to join robotics since fourth grade, after watching a robot competition at the library. The club builds a robot for a regional contest in February. Jordan's best friend, Sam, already signed up. The club costs 20 dollars for parts, and Jordan has saved 15 dollars from helping a neighbor rake leaves.
  Cross-country is free. The coach, Ms. Patel, told Jordan, "You have good endurance. I'd love to have you." Running helps Jordan feel calm when school gets stressful, and Jordan likes being outside after sitting in class all day. The team has meets on some Saturdays, which is when Jordan usually visits a grandparent.
  Jordan also has a math grade that slipped to a C last quarter and wants to bring it up. Both activities end early enough to leave time for homework, but cross-country meets can run long.
  Sign-ups close on Friday. Jordan has made a list in a notebook twice, crossed it out both times, and still feels stuck.

## Step 1: Define
write: What decision does Jordan need to make, and by when?
  lines: 2
  key: Jordan must choose either the robotics club or the cross-country team, because they meet at the same time, and must decide by Friday.

## Step 2: Options
fill:
  - Option A: {the robotics club}
  - Option B: {the cross-country team}

## Step 3: Consequences
table: Option | Possible good results | Possible problems
  - Robotics club | {follows a long-time interest; time with Sam; build for a February contest} | {costs 20 dollars and Jordan has 15; less time outside and moving}
  - Cross-country | {free; coach wants Jordan; running helps Jordan feel calm; time outside} | {Saturday meets clash with grandparent visits; meets can run long}

## Step 4: Choose
write: Which option should Jordan choose? Give two reasons from the facts.
  lines: 4
  key: Answers vary. Example: Robotics, because Jordan has wanted it for years and only needs 5 more dollars, which could come from one more job; Jordan could still run on weekends for calm. Or: Cross-country, because it is free, helps Jordan handle stress and the coach values Jordan; Saturday visits could move to Sunday.

## Step 5: Reflect
write: In December, how could Jordan check whether this was a good decision? Name two things to look at.
  lines: 3
  key: Jordan could check whether they enjoy the activity and look forward to it, whether the math grade is improving, whether stress is lower, and whether the money or Saturday plans worked out.

## Stretch
write: Jordan's friend says, "Just pick robotics because I'm in it." Is that a responsible reason on its own? Explain.
  lines: 3
  key: Not on its own. A friend is a fair reason to weigh, but a responsible decision looks at all the consequences: cost, Jordan's interests, stress, schoolwork and time.
