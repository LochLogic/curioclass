# 1. Theme Hunt: What Is the Story Really About?
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can state the theme of a story and use details to show it.
directions: Read the story. Answer the questions. Use details from the story to prove your answers.
mistakes: Students often give the topic ("a spelling bee") or a summary instead of a theme. A theme is a lesson that could fit many stories: ask "What did Marcus learn that anyone could learn?"
notes: The story is original to Curio Class (about 230 words). A strong theme statement: being too sure of yourself can keep you from preparing, or practice matters more than talent alone.

## Read
passage: The Word Marcus Knew
  Marcus could spell anything. At least, that is what he told everyone the week before the spelling bee.
  "I don't need to study," he said at lunch, waving a carrot stick. "I've never missed a word in my life." His friend Priya shrugged and went back to her flash cards. She had been practicing every night, even the long words with silent letters.
  On the day of the bee, Marcus strolled onto the stage. He spelled "garden" and "planet" without even thinking. He winked at the audience. Priya spelled her words slowly, tapping each letter on her leg.
  Then the judge said, "Marcus, your word is rhythm."
  Marcus opened his mouth. He knew this word. He had heard it a hundred times in music class. "R-H-Y-T-H-U-M," he said.
  The bell rang. Marcus felt his face turn hot as he walked back to his seat. Priya spelled "rhythm" correctly two rounds later and went on to win.
  After the bee, Marcus found Priya by the water fountain. "How did you know that one?" he asked.
  "I didn't, at first," she said. "I missed it at home three times."
  The next week, Marcus borrowed a stack of her flash cards.

## Questions
choice: What is the story mostly about?
  - A spelling bee at school
  * Learning that you have to prepare, even when you feel sure of yourself
  - Why Priya is good at music
  - How to spell the word rhythm
write: State the theme of the story in one sentence.
  lines: 2
  key: Being too sure of yourself can keep you from preparing; practice matters more than bragging.
write: Find two details that show the theme. Write them, then explain how each one shows it.
  lines: 5
  starter: One detail is ... This shows ...
  key: "I don't need to study," and he misspells rhythm (bragging leads to a mistake); Priya practiced every night and missed it at home three times (practice pays off); Marcus borrows flash cards (he learned the lesson).
choice: Why does the author tell us Priya "missed it at home three times"?
  - To show that Priya is not a good speller
  * To show that her success came from practice, not from already knowing
  - To make the reader feel sorry for Marcus
  - To explain why the bee lasted so long
write: What does the last sentence tell you about how Marcus changed?
  lines: 2
  key: He now understands he needs to practice; borrowing the cards shows he will study instead of brag.

# 25. Character Evidence Web
minutes: 35
materials: pencil, highlighter if you have one
ican: I can describe a character in depth using her thoughts, words and actions and what others say.
directions: Read the story. Sort four clues in the warm up. Then fill the web with evidence about Lena, name her trait in the center, and explain it.
mistakes: Students often list what happens (she gives him a seat) without saying what it shows about her. Push for the "so what": what kind of person does that? Watch for traits that are feelings (happy) instead of character traits (kind, observant, brave). "What others say" means what other characters say about Lena, not what Lena says to others.
notes: The story is original to Curio Class (about 195 words). Strong center traits: kind, caring, observant, brave (it takes courage to call out across the lunchroom). Accept any trait supported by at least three kinds of evidence.

## Read
passage: The Empty Chair
  Lena noticed the new boy, Omar, standing alone with his lunch tray. Every table looked full. He must feel like everyone is staring at him, Lena thought. She remembered her own first day last year, when her hands had shaken so hard that she spilled her milk.
  Lena's friends were busy laughing about a video game. She took a deep breath and pulled her backpack off the empty chair beside her. "Omar! There's a seat here," she called, waving her arm.
  Omar walked over slowly. "Thanks," he said quietly. "I didn't know where to go."
  "Everybody gets lost the first week," Lena told him. "I sat by the windows by myself for three days." Omar laughed for the first time all morning.
  After lunch, Lena walked Omar to the art room using the shortcut past the library. She drew him a map of the school on the back of her spelling list.
  Later, Jada caught up with her at the lockers. "That was really nice, Lena. I didn't even notice he was alone."
  Mr. Ruiz, the lunch helper, smiled at her as he passed. "You always see the kid nobody else sees," he said.

## Warm up: What kind of clue?
match: Match each clue about Lena to the kind of clue it is.
  - "He must feel like everyone is staring at him." = A thought
  - "Everybody gets lost the first week." = Something Lena says
  - She drew him a map of the school. = Something Lena does
  - "You always see the kid nobody else sees." = What another character says

## Main: Evidence web
text: Write two pieces of evidence in each part of the web. Then write Lena's trait in the center box.
chart: Lena's thoughts | Lena's words | Lena's actions | What others say about her
  rows: 2
  key: Thoughts: he must feel like everyone is staring; she remembers spilling her milk on her own first day. Words: "Omar! There's a seat here"; "Everybody gets lost the first week"; "I sat by the windows by myself for three days." Actions: notices Omar alone; takes a deep breath and moves her backpack; walks him to art; draws him a map. Others: Jada, "That was really nice"; Mr. Ruiz, "You always see the kid nobody else sees."
draw: Center of the web: Lena is ... | 0.8
write: Name Lena's trait in one word for the center of the web.
  lines: 1
  key: Kind (also observant, caring, brave, thoughtful).

## Explain
write: Use evidence from at least three parts of your web to explain why your trait fits Lena.
  lines: 6
  starter: Lena is ... One clue is ... This shows ... Another clue is ...
  key: Lena is kind and observant. She thinks Omar "must feel like everyone is staring at him," so she notices how others feel. She calls "Omar! There's a seat here" and later draws him a map, which shows she helps without being asked. Mr. Ruiz says she "always see[s] the kid nobody else sees," so others notice her kindness too.

## Stretch
write: Why does the author tell us about Lena's own first day? What does it add to her character?
  lines: 3
  key: It shows why she understands how Omar feels: she has been new and nervous herself. Her kindness comes from remembering her own experience, which makes her a deeper character.
