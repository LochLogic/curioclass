title: Make Your Own Path Game
grade: 1
order: 26
kind: Game
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a large sheet of paper (or 2 taped together, or the inside of a cereal box), crayons or markers, a die (or paper slips numbered 1 to 3 in a cup), small toys or bottle caps as game pieces
nice: sidewalk chalk for a giant outdoor version, stickers

## The big idea
Read aloud: "You get to invent a board game! You choose the path, the traps, the shortcuts and the prize at the end. Will players have to cross a lava river? Escape a dragon? Let's make a game that no one has ever played before."

## Goals
- Your child designs and draws a path board game with at least 25 spaces.
- Your child plays the game with someone and changes a rule to make it better.

## Objectives
- Your child can count spaces and move the right number.
- Your child can create game rules and explain them.
- Your child can test a game and change it to make it fairer or more fun.

## Get ready
Lay the big paper on the floor or table. Pick a game piece for each player. If you don't have a die, write 1, 2 and 3 on paper slips (twice each) and put them in a cup.

## Steps
1. **Pick a theme.** Ask: "Where does your game happen?" A jungle, outer space, a candy land, under the sea. Ask: "What's the prize at the end?" (Treasure, a rocket home, a giant cake.)
2. **Draw the path** (15 minutes). Your child draws a winding path from START to FINISH with at least 25 squares. Number them if they like.
3. **Special squares** (15 minutes). Your child colors some squares and decides what they do: a green square means move ahead 2; a red square means go back 3; a "dragon" square means skip a turn.
4. **Draw the world.** Your child adds pictures around the path: mountains, rivers, monsters, a castle at the finish.
5. **Write the rules.** Your child tells you the rules and you write them on a card: how to move, what the special squares do, how to win.
6. **Play** (15 minutes). Roll and move. Your child counts the spaces out loud.
7. **Fix it.** After one game ask: "Was it too easy? Too long? Should we add a shortcut?" Your child adds one new rule or square and you play again.

## Make it yours
- Draw it giant with sidewalk chalk outside and use yourselves as the game pieces.
- Add "challenge cards": land on a star and do 5 jumping jacks or sing a song.
- Make it a cooperative game where everyone works together to beat the dragon before it reaches the castle.

## Stuck?
- The path is too short: add loops or a long winding road around the edge.
- Your child gets upset losing: add a rule where everyone who finishes gets a prize, or play together as a team.

## Show it off
Teach the family the game after dinner. Your child is the game master who explains the rules.

## Clean up
Fold or roll the board and keep the pieces and rules in a zip bag.

## For the grown-up
This builds counting and adding, rule making, fairness and handling winning and losing. Changing the rules after testing is real game design thinking. Keep tiny game pieces away from younger siblings. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the trickiest square in your game?"
