title: Magic Milk Colors
grade: 2
order: 15
kind: Science
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a shallow dish or plate with a rim, milk (whole milk works best), food coloring (at least 3 colors), dish soap, cotton swabs, a small cup, paper towels, paper and crayons
nice: a pipette or eyedropper, a magnifying glass, a second dish to compare skim milk

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Today you are a color wizard. You will pour milk, drop in colors, and with one magic touch, the colors will swirl and dance like fireworks. Can you figure out the secret behind the magic?"

## Goals
- At least three color shows made with milk, food coloring and soap.
- A drawing of your child's favorite swirl pattern.

## Objectives
- Your child can follow steps in order to repeat an experiment.
- Your child can describe what they see using words like swirl, spread and mix.
- Your child can compare two tests and say which one moved more.

## Get ready
Set the dish on a tray or a towel. Pour milk until it just covers the bottom (about half an inch). Pour a little dish soap into a small cup.

## Steps
1. **Add colors.** Your child puts 4 drops of food coloring in the middle of the milk, each a different color, close together but not touching.
2. **Test a plain swab.** Touch a clean, dry cotton swab to the middle. Ask: "What happened?" (Not much.)
3. **The magic touch.** Dip a new swab in soap and touch it to the middle of the colors. Hold it still for 10 seconds. Watch them burst away!
4. **Try again.** Touch the soapy swab to different spots: near the edge, between colors. Your child decides where.
5. **Explain the trick.** Milk has tiny bits of fat. Soap pulls on the fat and makes it move, and the colors ride along.
6. **Test a new milk** (10 minutes). If you have skim milk, try it in a second dish. Ask: "Which one moves more? Why do you think?"
7. **Draw it.** Your child draws their favorite swirl and writes one sentence: "The colors ran away from the soap."

## Make it yours
- Drop colors in a pattern, like a rainbow ring or a smiley face, and see what the soap does to it.
- Try warm milk and cold milk side by side.
- Lay a paper towel on top for a second to make a colorful print to keep.

## Stuck?
- Colors don't move: use more soap on the swab, or switch to whole milk.
- The show stops: add a new drop of soap in a different spot.

## Show it off
Save a fresh dish of milk for when the family gets home, and let your child perform the "magic" and explain the secret.

## Clean up
Pour the milk down the sink, rinse the dish, and throw away the swabs. Your child wipes up drips.

## For the grown-up
This shows how soap breaks up fats, the same reason it cleans greasy dishes. It builds observation, describing words and repeated testing. Food coloring can stain, so use old clothes and a towel. The milk is for science, not drinking. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did the colors run away from the soap?"
