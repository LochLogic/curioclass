title: Paper Stained Glass Window
grade: 3
order: 17
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1
materials: black paper or a cereal box painted dark, wax paper or parchment, tissue paper or colored paper or markers, scissors, glue stick, tape, a pencil
nice: cooking oil and a cotton ball (to make plain paper see-through), a ruler

## The big idea
Old castles have windows that glow with color. Sunlight shines through and paints the floor! Make your own glowing window art and hang it where the sun hits.

## Goals
- You make a window picture that glows in the light.
- You plan a design with bold lines and color shapes.

## Objectives
- I can plan a picture with simple, bold shapes.
- I can cut along lines carefully.
- I can see how light passes through some things and not others.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Cut 2 sheets of wax paper the same size as your black paper. Get the colors ready in piles.

## Steps
1. Pick a design. A fish, a sun, a dragon, a flower or your initial. Keep shapes big.
2. Draw it on the black paper with a pencil. Draw a thick border, 1 finger wide, around the edge.
3. Draw thick lines through the design to split it into sections. Keep each line 1 finger wide.
4. Cut out the sections, not the lines (20 minutes). Fold the paper to start each cut. Ask for help if it's tricky.
5. Lay the black frame on wax paper.
6. Fill each hole with tissue paper. Glue it to the frame. Pick colors that look good next to each other.
7. Put the second wax paper on top. Glue or tape the edges.
8. Tape it to a sunny window. Look at the color on the floor!

## Make it yours
- Make a window that tells a story in 3 panels.
- Use only warm colors (red, orange, yellow) or only cool colors (blue, green, purple).
- No tissue paper? Color plain paper with markers, then rub a little oil on it to make it glow.

## Stuck?
- Lines break: make them thicker.
- Tissue tears: use less glue, just on the black parts.
- Not enough light: hang it on a window in the morning or near a lamp.

## Show it off
Hang it on a window. Show the family the colored light it makes.

## Clean up
Sweep up the scraps. Close glue sticks so they don't dry out.

## For the grown-up
This builds planning, fine motor control with scissors, and early ideas about light (see-through, see-through-some, and blocking). If your child uses oil, do it on a tray. Stay nearby for scissors. **Ask at dinner:** "What colors did you put next to each other, and why?"
