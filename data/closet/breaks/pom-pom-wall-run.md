title: Pom-Pom Wall Run
grade: 1
order: 2
kind: Build
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 6 to 10 paper towel and toilet paper tubes, painter's tape or masking tape, scissors (grown-up cuts tubes), small soft things to roll: pom-poms, foil balls, crumpled paper balls, a cup or bowl to catch them
nice: a cereal box, paper cups, a timer

## The big idea
Read aloud: "There's a fluffy ball that wants to go on the wildest ride ever! It needs a twisty, turny run down the wall from way up high to a cup at the bottom. Can you build a run so good that the ball never falls off?"

## Goals
- Your child builds a run on a wall or door that carries a ball from top to bottom.
- Your child changes the run to make the ball go faster, slower or farther.

## Objectives
- Your child can test, see a problem, and change the design.
- Your child can explain that steeper slopes make the ball go faster.
- Your child can compare two runs and say which works better.

## Get ready
Pick a wall, door or fridge where tape is okay (test a corner first). Cut some tubes in half the long way to make open chutes (grown-up). Make 6 small foil or paper balls about the size of a grape.

## Steps
1. Hold a tube against the wall, tilted. Drop a ball in. Ask: "What happens if we tilt it more? Less?"
2. **Start at the top** (10 minutes). Your child picks where the first tube goes, as high as they can reach. Tape it on a slant.
3. **Add the next piece.** Where does the ball come out? Put the next tube there, slanted the other way, so the ball zig-zags.
4. **Test after every piece.** Drop the ball each time. Did it fall off? Your child moves the tube until it works.
5. **Keep building** (20 minutes). Use whole tubes as tunnels and half tubes as chutes. Your child decides where the turns go. End with a cup taped at the bottom.
6. **Run tests.** Time the ball by counting. Make one part steeper. Is it faster now? Make it flatter. Does it get stuck?
7. **Add a surprise.** Your child adds one special part: a drop into a cup with a hole, a split where the ball goes left or right, a "waterfall" jump.

## Make it yours
- Make it a race: build two runs side by side and race two balls.
- Give the ball a story: it's a snowball rolling down a mountain, or a lost marble finding its way home.
- Build a run with a rule: it must have 5 turns, or it must be as slow as possible.

## Stuck?
- Tape won't hold: use more tape, or tape to a big flattened box leaned against the wall.
- The ball shoots off at the turns: tape a paper "wall" or a cup at each corner to catch it.

## Show it off
Give each family member a ball to drop when they get home. Your child explains how the run works.

## Clean up
Peel off tape slowly at a low angle so it doesn't take paint. Save tubes in a bag for next time.

## For the grown-up
This is a first engineering project and a look at slopes, speed and gravity. The real learning is the test-and-fix loop. Use painter's tape and test it on a hidden spot first. Use balls bigger than a grape if a younger sibling is around. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the hardest part of the run to get right?"
