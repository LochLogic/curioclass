title: Paper Tube Rain Stick
grade: 1
order: 18
kind: Music
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a long cardboard tube (wrapping paper or paper towel tube), aluminum foil, dry rice or small dry beans (about 1/2 cup), paper, strong tape, crayons or markers, scissors (grown-up cuts circles)
nice: a second tube to compare, ribbon or yarn, a funnel

## The big idea
Read aloud: "In a dry land far away, the plants are thirsty and waiting for rain. You can make the sound of rain with just a tube and some rice! Build a rain stick, then lead a rainstorm concert."

## Goals
- Your child builds a rain stick that sounds like falling rain.
- Your child leads a "rainstorm" piece that goes from soft to loud to soft.

## Objectives
- Your child can test materials and choose the one that makes the sound they want.
- Your child can play soft and loud, fast and slow on purpose.
- Your child can explain that the rice bumps into the foil to make the sound.

## Get ready
Cut two paper circles a bit bigger than the end of the tube (grown-up). Tape one over one end of the tube tightly. Tear off two sheets of foil about 2 feet long each.

## Steps
1. **Listen to real rain** (5 minutes). Ask your child: "What does a little rain sound like? A big storm?" Make the sounds with fingers on the table, then hands.
2. **Make the inside.** Your child scrunches each foil sheet into a long, loose snake and twists it into a spring. Slide them into the tube. These are the bumps that slow the rice down.
3. **Test the filling** (10 minutes). Pour a spoonful of rice in (grown-up), cover the open end with your hand, and tip it. Now try beans. Which sounds more like rain? Your child picks.
4. Pour in about 1/2 cup of the winner (grown-up). Tape the second circle on tightly, then wrap tape all around both ends.
5. **Decorate** (15 minutes). Your child wraps the tube in paper and draws rain clouds, lightning, raindrops, plants. Name it ("The Thunder Tube").
6. **Play it.** Tip slowly for gentle rain, quickly for a downpour, and shake it for hail.
7. **Rainstorm concert** (10 minutes). Your child leads a storm: start with fingers tapping (drizzle), add the rain stick, then clapping (thunder), then slowly back to quiet. Everyone follows the leader's hands.

## Make it yours
- Make a "storm story": the rain stick plays the rain while your child tells about a frog waiting for the pond to fill.
- Build two rain sticks with different fillings and play them as a duet.
- Add a drum (an upside-down pot) for thunder.

## Stuck?
- The rice runs out too fast: add more foil inside so it's slower and bumpier.
- The ends pop open: wrap tape around the end and down the side of the tube in a star shape.

## Show it off
Perform the rainstorm concert for the family with the lights dimmed, and have them be the thunder.

## Clean up
Sweep up spilled rice. Store the rain stick upright.

## For the grown-up
This builds listening, loud and soft (dynamics), and simple sound science, plus following a conductor. Rice and beans are small, so do the pouring yourself, tape the ends very securely, and keep the rain stick away from babies and toddlers. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you make the rain sound like a big storm?"
