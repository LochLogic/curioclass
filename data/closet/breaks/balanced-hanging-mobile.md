where: Indoors
mess: Low

players: 1
materials: 3 or 4 sticks, straws, wooden skewers or a wire coat hanger cut into pieces (with a grown-up's OK), string or thread, scissors, cardstock or cardboard, markers or colored pencils, tape, a ruler, paper clips
nice: a hole punch, colored paper, natural objects like leaves, feathers or shells

## The big idea
A mobile is a sculpture that moves, floats and balances. It was invented by an artist who loved both art and engineering. The secret is **torque**: a light shape far from the center can balance a heavy shape close to it. You'll design a theme, cut your shapes and balance a whole floating world.

## Goals
- A hanging mobile with at least 3 levels and 6 shapes that balances on its own.
- A theme that ties the shapes together.

## Objectives
- I can find the balance point of an arm by moving the string.
- I can explain torque: weight times distance from the pivot.
- I can design shapes that look good from every angle.

## Get ready
Pick a theme: the solar system, birds in flight, sea creatures, an imaginary weather system, or a set of symbols for your family. Clear a table and find a hook or a chair back to hang from while you build.

## Steps
1. **Design shapes** (15 minutes). Draw 6 to 8 shapes in different sizes on cardstock. Color both sides, since mobiles turn. Cut them out and punch or poke a hole at the top.
2. **Build from the bottom up.** Take your smallest arm (a short straw or stick). Tie a shape on each end with strings of different lengths.
3. **Find the balance point.** Tie a string loosely around the middle of the arm and slide it until the arm hangs level. Tape it there.
4. **Check the math.** A shape twice as heavy needs to be half as far from the balance point. Weight times distance should match on both sides.
5. Hang that whole arm from one end of a longer arm. Hang a bigger shape from the other end. Find the new balance point.
6. Repeat for a third level. Hang the whole thing from the top string (30 minutes).
7. Adjust. Make sure no shapes hit each other when it turns. Change string lengths if they do.

## Make it yours
- Make a mobile that tells a story from top to bottom, like a bird's life from egg to flight.
- Use only found natural objects, no cut paper.
- Hang a sound element, like two spoons or keys that touch gently, to make a wind chime mobile.

## Stuck?
- It keeps tilting: slide the balance string a few millimeters toward the heavier side.
- Shapes tangle: use longer arms or stagger string lengths.

## Show it off
Hang it near a window or a doorway and give a short talk on the theme.

## Clean up
Pick up string bits and paper scraps. Put sharp wire pieces in a safe spot or the trash.

## For the grown-up
This combines art with physics: balance, torque and center of mass. If they cut a wire hanger, it needs pliers or wire cutters and care with sharp ends. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you balance the big shape with the small one?"
