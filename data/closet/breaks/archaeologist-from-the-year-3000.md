title: Archaeologist from the Year 3000
grade: 5
order: 25
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 6 to 8 everyday objects (a whisk, a sock, a remote control, a key, a toothbrush, a comb), paper, a pencil, index cards or paper squares, a table or shelf for the display
nice: a magnifying glass, a soft paintbrush, a hat and a notebook for your costume

## The big idea
It is the year 3000. You are an archaeologist (a scientist who studies old objects to learn about the past). You just dug up a home from the year 2026! But nobody remembers what anything was for. What was a "whisk"? A hair tool? A tiny cage for crickets? Make your best guesses and set up a museum.

## Goals
- You set up a museum display of everyday objects with "future" labels.
- You give a tour as the archaeologist from the year 3000.

## Objectives
- I can describe an object closely: its shape, size, and material.
- I can make a guess and back it up with clues.
- I can write a museum label that is short and clear.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up which objects you can borrow. Lay them on a table with space between each one. Put on your hat if you have one.

## Steps
1. "Discover" each object. Brush off pretend dirt with a paintbrush. Look at it closely. Use the magnifying glass.
2. Write field notes for each (15 minutes). Shape? Size? What is it made of? Any marks or buttons?
3. Guess its use, but guess wrong on purpose, with clues. Example: "The sock. Probably a hat for a long-headed person. Note the stretchy material."
4. Name each object something new: "The Sacred Scrubbing Stick" for a toothbrush.
5. Write a museum label for each. Use three lines: the new name, the year it was found, your theory.
6. Arrange the display. Put the "most important" object in the center with a big sign.
7. Practice a tour. Speak like a serious scientist, even when it's silly.

## Make it yours
- Write an "ancient legend" about how one object was used in a ceremony.
- Have a rival archaeologist (a family member) who disagrees with every theory.
- Make a pretend broken object: put two objects together and claim they were one strange machine.

## Stuck?
- Every guess is correct: try harder to be wrong. Look at the object upside down.
- Ideas run out: ask "Would a person wear it, eat with it, or worship it?"
- The display is cluttered: use four objects instead of eight.

## Show it off
Invite your family on a museum tour. Charge one pretend coin for entry. Answer their questions in character.

## Clean up
Return every object to where it lives. Keep your labels in an envelope.

## For the grown-up
This is pretend play with real thinking skills: close observation, making claims from evidence, and writing short, clear labels. It also shows how archaeologists can get things wrong when they only have objects to go on. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the silliest theory in your museum? What clues led you there?"
