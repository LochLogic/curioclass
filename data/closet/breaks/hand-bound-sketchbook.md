where: Indoors

players: 1
materials: 20 to 30 sheets of plain paper, two pieces of cardboard (from a cereal box or shipping box), a needle, strong thread or dental floss, a thumbtack or pushpin, scissors, a ruler, a pencil, glue, scrap fabric or paper for covers
nice: a bone folder or butter knife for crisp folds, waxed thread, binder clips, an awl

## The big idea
Before machines, every book was sewn by hand. Bookbinders folded pages into bundles called **signatures** and sewed
them together with a stitch that leaves the spine open and flexible. You will bind a real sketchbook with an exposed
stitch spine that lies flat, then give it a cover nobody else has.

## Goals
- A finished hand-bound sketchbook with at least four signatures.
- A custom cover that you designed.

## Objectives
- I can fold and arrange paper into signatures.
- I can sew a binding that holds pages together firmly.
- I can measure and cut covers to fit.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Fold each sheet in half. Nest five or six folded sheets inside each other to make one signature. Make
four or five signatures.

## Steps
1. Make a punching guide (10 minutes): a folded strip of paper the height of your pages, with four or six evenly
   spaced marks, starting 2 cm from each end.
2. Punch holes along the fold of every signature using the guide and the thumbtack. Open the signature on a folded
   towel so the tack goes through cleanly.
3. Cut two covers from cardboard, 5 mm larger than the pages on three sides. Punch matching holes along one edge.
4. Sew (35 minutes). Use about 1.5 m of thread. Start at the back cover and the first signature. Go in and out
   through the holes. At each hole, loop around the thread of the signature below before going into the next
   signature. This **kettle stitch** locks them together.
5. Add the front cover last and tie off with a double knot inside.
6. Cover the boards with fabric, paper or collage, wrapping and gluing edges inside.
7. Press it under a stack of heavy books for 15 minutes.

## Make it yours
- Make a tiny pocket-sized notebook as a gift.
- Use mixed paper: lined, colored, and grid paper in different signatures.
- Design the cover around a theme: a field journal, a spell book, a ship's logbook.

## Stuck?
- Thread tangles: use shorter thread and rub it over a bar of soap or a crayon to wax it.
- Signatures are loose: pull each stitch snug before moving to the next hole.

## Show it off
Show the family how the spine works, and draw the first page in front of them.

## Clean up
Put the needle and tack away safely and recycle paper scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds fine motor skills, measuring, patience and an understanding of how books were made for centuries. **Ask
at dinner:** "What will you put on the first page?"
