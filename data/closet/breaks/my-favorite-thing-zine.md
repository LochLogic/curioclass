kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 1 sheet of paper, scissors, markers or colored pencils, a pencil, a glue stick
nice: old magazines or junk mail to cut up, stickers

## The big idea
A zine (say "zeen") is a tiny homemade magazine. It's all about one thing you love: sharks, soccer, slime, your dog, or space. Fold one sheet of paper into an 8-page zine and fill it with facts, drawings and jokes.

## Goals
- You fold and fill an 8-page zine about one topic.
- You include facts, pictures and at least one fun extra.

## Objectives
- I can choose a topic and write facts about it.
- I can plan pages so a reader moves through them.
- I can mix words and pictures to share ideas.

## Get ready
Fold the paper in half the long way. Unfold. Fold it in half the short way, then in half again, then once more. Unfold. You have 8 boxes.

## Steps
1. Fold in half the short way. Cut along the middle crease, from the fold to the center. Stop at the center!
2. Open it. Fold it the long way. Push the ends together so the middle pops out. Flatten into a little book.
3. Pick your topic. What could you talk about for an hour?
4. Plan the 8 pages (10 minutes). Cover, 4 fact pages, a quiz, a comic, and a back cover.
5. Fill the fact pages. Write one big fact on each with a drawing.
6. Make the quiz page. Ask 3 questions about your topic.
7. Draw a 3-box comic starring your topic.
8. Make an eye-catching cover with a title and your name.

## Make it yours
- Make a zine about your family, with an "interview" page.
- Make a zine with only pictures, no words.
- Make 3 copies and hand them out.

## Stuck?
- The fold won't work: unfold it and try the cut again slowly.
- You run out of facts: add "Top 5" lists or "Did you know?" bubbles.
- Your writing is too big: write first in pencil, then trace over.

## Show it off
Give the zine to someone at home to read. Ask them to take your quiz.

## Clean up
Put scraps in recycling. Store your zine in a folder.

## For the grown-up
This builds informational writing, planning and design. Zines are a real art form, and kids love to make a whole series. **Ask at dinner:** "What's one fact from your zine that surprised you?"
