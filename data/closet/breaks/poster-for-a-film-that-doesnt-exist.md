title: Poster for a Film That Doesn't Exist
grade: 12
order: 20
kind: Art
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a large sheet of paper or poster board, pencils, markers or paint, a ruler, scissors, old magazines, glue
nice: colored paper, stencils, a craft knife and cutting mat

## The big idea
A great movie poster sells a film in three seconds: one image, a title, a tagline. You will invent a film that does
not exist, from its genre and plot to its stars, and design the poster that makes everyone want to see it.
Bonus points if your family asks when it comes out.

## Goals
- A finished poster with a strong central image, title, tagline and credit block.
- A one-paragraph pitch for the film.

## Objectives
- I can design a layout with a clear focal point and visual hierarchy.
- I can write a tagline and title that capture a story.
- I can use type and image together to communicate genre.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Study the way posters work: a big image, a title, a tagline above or below, and small credits at the
bottom.

## Steps
1. Invent the film (10 minutes): genre, setting, main character, conflict. Example: "A quiet librarian discovers the
   town's books are rewriting themselves every night."
2. Write a title and a tagline. Taglines are short and punchy: "Every story has an ending. This one has a choice."
3. Thumbnail three layouts (10 minutes): a close-up face, a tiny figure in a huge space, a symbolic object.
4. Pick the strongest and sketch it full size.
5. Make the title type match the genre: sharp for thriller, soft for romance, blocky for action.
6. Finish the image (40 minutes) with paint, markers or collage.
7. Add the credit block at the bottom in small, tight capitals: invented actors, director and studio.

## Make it yours
- Make a poster series: three posters for the same film, each from a different character's view.
- Make the poster for a remake of the film set 100 years later.
- Design a poster for a film about your family, in a genre that does not fit (a horror poster about grocery shopping).

## Stuck?
- The poster looks busy: remove everything except the image and title, then add back only what is needed.
- The title does not stand out: make it bigger, bolder, or in a high-contrast color.

## Show it off
Hang the poster and pitch the film to the family. Take ticket orders.

## Clean up
Put art supplies away and recycle scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds graphic design, storytelling and visual communication. **Ask at dinner:** "Who would you cast in your
film?"
