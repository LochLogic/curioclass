title: Balloon Powered Car
grade: 5
order: 2
kind: Build
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a piece of cardboard about 10 cm by 20 cm, 4 bottle caps for wheels, 2 straws, 2 wooden skewers or thin straight sticks, a balloon, tape, scissors, a ruler or tape measure
nice: a sharp pencil to poke holes, a third straw, a hallway or smooth floor for racing

## The big idea
A balloon is a tiny tank of pushed-in air. When the air rushes out the back, it shoves the balloon forward. Put that on wheels and you have a jet car. Can you build one that zooms across the whole kitchen?

## Goals
- You build a car that rolls forward on balloon power alone.
- You beat your own distance record at least once.

## Objectives
- I can explain that air rushing back pushes the car forward.
- I can find and fix what slows my car down, like friction (rubbing that slows things).
- I can measure and record each test run.

## Get ready
Find a smooth floor with about 4 meters of clear space. Ask a grown-up to poke a small hole in the center of each bottle cap. Lay out your parts on a table.

## Steps
1. Tape the two straws across the bottom of the cardboard, one near each end. These hold the axles (the sticks the wheels spin on).
2. Slide a skewer through each straw. Push a bottle cap onto each end of each skewer. Check that the wheels spin freely.
3. Tape the neck of the balloon around the end of a straw. Wrap it tight so air only comes out the straw.
4. Tape that straw on top of the car. The open end should stick off the back about 3 cm.
5. Blow up the balloon through the straw. Pinch the straw, set the car down, and let go!
6. Measure how far it went. Write it down. Now pick one thing to change: a bigger balloon breath, lighter body, or wheels that wobble less (20 minutes).
7. Run at least five tests. Circle your record.

## Make it yours
- Add a second balloon. Does twice the air mean twice the distance?
- Build a heavy "truck" and a light "racer." Which goes farther? Why?
- Make a finish line ramp and see if your car can climb a slight hill.

## Stuck?
- The car won't move: check the wheels. If they rub the cardboard, move the straws out a little.
- Air leaks out the neck: add more tape, or use a rubber band to seal it.
- No balloon at home: wind a rubber band around the back axle instead, and roll it back to power it up.

## Show it off
Mark a start line with tape. Challenge a grown-up to guess how far your car will go, then prove it.

## Clean up
Pick up caps and scraps. Keep your car on a shelf for the next race.

## For the grown-up
This builds early physics ideas (action and reaction, friction) and the habit of testing one change at a time. Balloons are fine at this age, but pop pieces are a choking risk for children under 8, so pick up any broken balloon right away if little ones are around. Do the hole poking in the caps yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "What slowed your car down the most, and how did you fix it?"
