title: Make a Star Wheel
grade: 12
order: 26
kind: Science
time: 75-120 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: two sheets of heavy paper or thin cardboard, a pencil, a ruler, a protractor or a folded-paper angle guide, scissors, a brass fastener or a pin and eraser, a black pen
nice: a star chart from a library book, glow-in-the-dark paint, a red flashlight for night use

## The big idea
Sailors and astronomers used a **planisphere**, a star wheel, to know which stars would be up on any night of the
year. It is two discs: a star map that rotates under a window showing your sky. You will build one and take it
outside to find real constellations.

## Goals
- A working star wheel with a rotating star map and a viewing window.
- At least three constellations found in the real sky using your wheel.

## Objectives
- I can explain why the stars we see change with time and season.
- I can measure and construct a rotating model accurately.
- I can use a tool to find constellations in the night sky.

## Get ready
Cut a circle 20 cm across from one sheet. This is the star disc. Mark its center. Around its edge, write the 12 months
spaced 30 degrees apart.

## Steps
1. Draw the star map (30 minutes). Put Polaris (the North Star) at the center. Draw the Big Dipper, Cassiopeia, Orion
   and other constellations you know or can find in a book, at roughly the right distances from Polaris.
2. Make the holder. On the second sheet, cut a slightly larger circle and add a 2 cm tab at the bottom.
3. Cut an oval window in the holder. For mid-northern latitudes, make it about 60 percent of the circle,
   off-center toward the bottom (the horizon).
4. Write 24 hours around the window's edge, spaced 15 degrees apart.
5. Pin the star disc behind the holder at its center with a brass fastener so it spins.
6. Set it: turn the disc until today's date lines up with the current time.
7. Go outside (or to a dark window), hold the wheel overhead with north at the top, and find three constellations.

## Make it yours
- Add your own invented constellations from another night's sketching.
- Make a southern-hemisphere version with the South Celestial Pole at the center.
- Add glow-in-the-dark dots so you can read it in the dark.

## Stuck?
- Stars do not match: double-check the date and time alignment and make sure you face north.
- The disc does not spin: loosen the fastener or make the hole a little bigger.

## Show it off
Take a family member outside and use the wheel to show them one constellation.

## Clean up
Put away scissors and store the star wheel flat.

## For the grown-up
This builds astronomy, geometry and measurement. Going outside at night means telling you where they will be. **Ask at
dinner:** "What constellation is up right now?"
