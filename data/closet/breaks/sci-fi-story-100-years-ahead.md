title: Sci-Fi Story 100 Years Ahead
grade: 10
order: 2
kind: Story
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, a pen, a quiet spot
nice: index cards for worldbuilding notes, a timer

## The big idea
The best science fiction isn't about lasers. It's about one change and everything it touches. What if sleep became optional? What if every city floated? What if no one could lie? You'll pick one change, follow it 100 years into the future, and write a story about one ordinary person living with the extraordinary result.

## Goals
- A finished short story of 1,000 to 2,000 words set in the year 2126.
- A "consequence web" page that shows how your one change reshaped the world.

## Objectives
- I can extrapolate (follow a trend forward) and imagine first, second and third-order effects.
- I can reveal a world through small details instead of long explanations.
- I can write a protagonist whose personal problem connects to the world's big change.

## Get ready
Get paper and a pen. Write "2126" at the top of a page.

## Steps
1. **One change** (10 minutes). Write 10 "what ifs." Pick the one that makes you most curious, not the coolest-sounding.
2. **Consequence web.** Write the change in the center. Around it, write first-order effects (direct results). Off those, write second-order ones (results of results). If sleep is optional: people work 20-hour days, then beds become antiques, then "sleepers" become a rebel subculture.
3. Pick a character who lives at the edge of that world, not at the center. A kid whose family still sleeps. A museum guard in charge of the last bed.
4. Give them a want and a deadline. "She has to stay awake for a 48-hour exam, but she's never not slept."
5. **Detail bank** (10 minutes): list 10 small, specific things in your world. What do people say instead of "good night"? What's on the breakfast table?
6. **Draft** (45 minutes). Start in the middle of a scene. Never stop to explain the world. Let readers figure it out, the way you'd figure out a foreign city.
7. Revise: cut any paragraph that only explains. Make sure the ending shows your character changed or chose something.

## Make it yours
- Write it as a series of messages, signs and news headlines from 2126.
- Write a companion story set in the same world, 50 years earlier, when the change first happened.
- Make the world better, not worse: write a hopeful future with a real problem in it.

## Stuck?
- The story becomes a tour of the world: give your character a problem in the first paragraph.
- The ending fizzles: decide what your character gives up or gains, and end on that.

## Show it off
Read the first page aloud at dinner, stop at a cliffhanger and see who wants the rest.

## Clean up
Keep your consequence web. You've built a world, and it can hold more stories.

## For the grown-up
Speculative fiction builds the English skills of narrative structure, character and theme, plus critical thinking about technology and society. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the one change in your future, and what surprised you about where it led?"
