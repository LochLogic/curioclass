title: Water Clock
grade: 10
order: 21
kind: Build
time: 60-120 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: two clear plastic bottles or cups (one larger), a pushpin or thin nail, scissors, tape, a permanent marker or strip of tape for markings, water, a clock or timer with a second hand, a tray or towel
nice: food coloring, a third bottle, a ruler

## The big idea
Before mechanical clocks, people told time with water. The **clepsydra**, a water clock, was used in ancient Egypt, Greece and China to time speeches, court cases and the night watch. You'll build one, calibrate it minute by minute, and then fight its biggest flaw: water flows slower as the container empties.

## Goals
- A water clock that measures at least 10 minutes with marked lines.
- A test of its accuracy, and an improved version.

## Objectives
- I can **calibrate** an instrument against a known standard.
- I can explain why flow rate drops as water pressure drops.
- I can design an improvement and measure whether it works.

## Get ready
Work over a tray or outside. Cut the top off the bigger bottle to make a collecting jar. Cut the bottom off the smaller bottle to make a funnel-shaped top reservoir, cap still on.

## Steps
1. Poke a small hole in the smaller bottle's cap with a pushpin. Start small: you can make it bigger later.
2. Set the smaller bottle upside down in the mouth of the collecting jar, cap down, and tape it so it's steady.
3. Add a few drops of food coloring to a jug of water. Fill the top reservoir to a line you mark as "full."
4. **Calibrate** (15 minutes). Start the timer as you remove your finger from the hole. Every minute, mark the water level on the collecting jar. Label 1, 2, 3 and so on.
5. **Look at the marks.** Are they evenly spaced? Probably not. As the top empties, there's less pressure pushing water out, so flow slows down.
6. **Test it.** Empty and refill. Have someone time a task (like 50 jumping jacks) with a real clock while you time it with your water clock. How far off is it?
7. **Improve it** (25 minutes). Try a fix: a **constant-head** design where you keep the top reservoir always full (top it up with a cup while it runs), or a third bottle that feeds the reservoir. Re-calibrate and compare.

## Make it yours
- Build an alarm: a light object floats in the collecting jar and tips over a domino when it reaches the 10-minute mark.
- Use your water clock to time a board game turn or a speech for the whole evening.
- Design an "ancient" version with a story: the timer for a court in a fictional kingdom.

## Stuck?
- Water runs out too fast: tape over the hole and poke a smaller one.
- The water stops: there's an air lock. Poke a tiny hole near the top of the reservoir to let air in.

## Show it off
Challenge the family: time a 5-minute challenge with your water clock and a real clock side by side.

## Clean up
Pour the water on a plant (coloring is fine in small amounts), and dry the bottles.

## For the grown-up
This builds physics (pressure, flow rate) and the science practice of calibration and testing an instrument, as well as history. The pushpin is the only sharp object. **Ask at dinner:** "Why do the marks get closer together near the bottom?"
