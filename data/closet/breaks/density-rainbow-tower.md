title: Density Rainbow Tower
grade: 6
order: 18
kind: Science
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a tall clear glass or jar, 5 cups or small glasses, sugar, warm water, measuring spoons and a 1/4 cup measure, a spoon, food coloring (or juice and drink mix for color), a tray, paper towels
nice: a dropper or turkey baster, honey, dish soap, cooking oil, small objects to float (a grape, a cherry tomato, a cork, a bead)

## The big idea
Density is how much stuff is packed into a space. A denser liquid sinks, and a lighter one floats on top. With sugar, water, and patience, you can stack five colors of liquid in one glass without them mixing. It looks like magic, and it's pure science.

## Goals
- You build a rainbow tower with at least four layers that stay separate.
- You test which objects float at which layer.

## Objectives
- I can explain that a liquid with more dissolved sugar is denser.
- I can predict which layer will sink or float.
- I can pour carefully and control my results.

## Get ready
Set everything on a tray. Line up the 5 cups and label them 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 (the number of tablespoons of sugar each will get).

## Steps
1. Put 1/4 cup warm water in each cup.
2. Add sugar: 0 tablespoons in cup 0, 1 in cup 1, 2 in cup 2, 3 in cup 3, and 4 in cup 4. Stir each until it dissolves completely (a few minutes for the heavy ones).
3. Color each cup differently: cup 4 red, cup 3 orange, cup 2 yellow, cup 1 green, cup 0 blue. Two drops each.
4. Pour the red (heaviest) into the tall glass first. About 2 cm deep.
5. Add the next layer very slowly. Hold a spoon upside down just above the red layer and pour the orange over the back of the spoon so it trickles down the side. Repeat for each color, heaviest to lightest.
6. Look at the tower from the side. How many layers can you see? Write it down and draw it.
7. Float test (10 minutes): gently drop in a grape, a bead, or a small piece of fruit. Where does each object stop? Predict before each drop.

## Make it yours
- Build a "liquids tower" instead: honey on the bottom, then dish soap, then water, then oil on top. Which is densest?
- Make a tower with 7 layers by using half-tablespoon steps.
- Let the tower sit overnight. What happens to the layers? Why?

## Stuck?
- Colors mix right away: pour slower, and make sure the sugar is fully dissolved.
- Two layers look the same: the sugar amounts were too close. Add more sugar to the heavier one.
- Objects all sink: try lighter things, like a piece of foam or a bottle cap.

## Show it off
Show your family the tower and ask them to guess what's making the layers before you explain.

## Clean up
Pour everything down the drain with lots of water, wash the glasses, and wipe sticky spots off the counter.

## For the grown-up
This is physical science: density, solutions, and making predictions. Everything here is food safe. If your child wants to try the liquids version, keep it to kitchen liquids (honey, soap, oil, water); no other household chemicals. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did the red layer stay on the bottom?"
