title: Thought Experiment Night
grade: 11
order: 19
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 2+
materials: index cards or paper, pens, a bowl or hat, a timer, a partner or two (a sibling, a friend over, or grown-ups after dinner)
nice: a whiteboard or big sheet of paper, snacks, costumes for "philosopher" roles

## The big idea
Philosophers use **thought experiments** to test big ideas with impossible situations. If a ship has every plank
replaced, is it still the same ship? If you could live in a perfect dream forever, would you? You are hosting a
night of these puzzles as a game show of ideas, where people argue, switch sides and change their minds.

## Goals
- A deck of six thought experiments, written as short scenarios.
- A hosted evening where everyone argues at least two sides.

## Objectives
- I can explain a thought experiment and the question it tests.
- I can argue a position I do not agree with.
- I can ask follow-up questions that push an argument further.

## Get ready
Set up a "stage": two chairs facing the audience. Write each thought experiment on a card in 3 or 4 sentences.

## Steps
1. Write your deck (20 minutes). Classics: **The Ship of Theseus**, **The Experience Machine** (a perfect
   simulation), **The Trolley Problem** (steer a runaway trolley), **The Teleporter** (if a machine copies you and
   destroys the original, did you travel?). Invent at least two of your own.
2. Set rules: each round, draw a card, read it, then two players argue opposite sides for two minutes each.
3. Add a twist card after each round: "The ship's old planks were used to build a second ship." Players must respond.
4. Vote. The audience moves to one side of the room or the other. Then swap sides and argue again.
5. Host it (40 minutes). Play the part of a dramatic host. Keep time strictly.
6. End with the "mind changer" award: who switched their opinion the most?

## Make it yours
- Make it a courtroom: one person is the judge, and evidence is anything said earlier in the night.
- Run a "future dinner party" where each person plays a famous thinker from history.
- Write thought experiments about your own house: "If you swapped every item in your room, is it still your room?"

## Stuck?
- Nobody wants to argue: give points for the most creative argument, not the right one.
- The debate gets heated: call a 30-second "agree on one thing" pause.

## Show it off
Invite a grown-up to judge one round and give a ruling.

## Clean up
Stack the cards, put the chairs back, and keep the deck for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds philosophy, logic and debate skills: building and testing arguments, listening and changing your mind.
**Ask at dinner:** "Which thought experiment do you still disagree with yourself about?"
