title: Backyard Olympics in Feet
grade: 2
order: 24
kind: Move
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a ruler (12 inches long), string or masking tape, a pencil, paper, a soft ball or rolled socks, a paper plate, chalk for outdoors
nice: a tape measure, a stopwatch or timer, a homemade medal (paper and yarn)

## The big idea
Read aloud: "The Olympic Games have come to your home, and you are the athlete AND the judge! You will jump, throw and run. Then you will measure every result in feet. Can you beat your own record?"

## Goals
- Five events played, with every result measured and written down.
- A new personal record in at least one event.

## Objectives
- Your child can measure distances using a ruler or a foot-long string.
- Your child can compare numbers to find the biggest and smallest.
- Your child can try again and improve on their own score.

## Get ready
Go to a yard, a park, a sidewalk, or a long hallway indoors. Cut a piece of string exactly 12 inches long using the ruler. That's one foot! Make a score sheet with the 5 events.

## Steps
1. **Learn the foot.** Your child measures their own shoe and compares it to the string. Is their foot a "foot" long?
2. **Event 1: Long Jump.** Stand at a start line, both feet together, and jump. Mark where heels land. Measure with the string: how many feet?
3. **Event 2: Sock Throw.** Throw a rolled sock or soft ball as far as possible. Measure in feet.
4. **Event 3: Frog Hop.** Three frog hops in a row. Measure the total.
5. **Event 4: Paper Plate Toss.** Throw a plate like a flying disk. Measure.
6. **Event 5: Your child's own event** (10 minutes). Your child invents one: backward walk, one-foot hop, crab crawl. They set the rules.
7. **Round 2** (20 minutes). Do all five again. Can your child beat their score? Circle each new record.
8. **Medal ceremony.** Your child makes a paper medal and writes their best record on it.

## Make it yours
- Make it a team event with stuffed animal "teammates" who each get a turn (your child throws for them).
- Measure in a silly unit too, like "spoons" or "noodles," and compare.
- Add an obstacle course event and measure it in seconds instead of feet.

## Stuck?
- Measuring with a short string takes a long time: tie 5 strings together into a 5-foot string.
- Indoors with little room: do the events in a hallway and use smaller ones (paper ball throw, standing jump).

## Show it off
Hold a medal ceremony for the family, with your child reading out the records.

## Clean up
Coil up the strings, bring in any balls or plates, and keep the score sheet to beat next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds measuring in feet (a second-grade math skill), comparing numbers and staying active. Trying to beat a personal best teaches goal setting. Outdoors, choose a safe spot away from streets. **Ask at dinner:** "Which event got your best record, and how did you beat it?"
