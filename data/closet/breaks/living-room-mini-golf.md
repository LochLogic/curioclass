title: Living Room Mini Golf
grade: 2
order: 14
kind: Game
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a ball (a ping-pong ball or small soft ball), a "club" (a wrapping paper tube, a yardstick, or a broom handle turned upside down), paper cups or small boxes, books, pillows, cardboard, tape, paper, a pencil
nice: paper towel tubes for tunnels, a small toy to be an obstacle, a timer

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Welcome to Couch Valley Mini Golf! You are the course designer, and every hole is your idea. Build tunnels, ramps and tricky turns. Then grab your club and see who can finish with the fewest hits."

## Goals
- A 5-hole golf course your child built from things around the house.
- A scorecard and a game played to the end.

## Objectives
- Your child can design an obstacle and test whether it works.
- Your child can count hits and add up a score.
- Your child can take turns and follow the rules of a game.

## Get ready
Clear a room, rolling up any rugs that bunch. Gather cups, books, pillows and cardboard. Lay a paper cup on its side as the first "hole," taped so it doesn't roll.

## Steps
1. **Plan the course** (10 minutes). Your child draws a map of 5 holes around the room. Number them 1 to 5.
2. **Build hole 1.** Easy start: a straight path with the cup at the end.
3. **Build holes 2 to 5** (30 minutes). Your child invents each hole. Ideas: a book ramp, a tunnel from a box with a door cut in each end (you cut), a zig-zag of books, a pillow "hill." Each hole needs a start line made of tape.
4. **Set "par."** Par means the number of hits a good player needs. Your child plays each hole once and writes the par next to it.
5. **Make a scorecard.** A paper with hole numbers down the side and each player's name across the top.
6. **Play the course** (20 minutes). Count every hit. Write it down after each hole.
7. **Add it up.** In golf, the lowest score wins!

## Make it yours
- Name every hole, like "The Dragon's Cave" or "Sock Mountain."
- Make one hole that changes every round: move one obstacle after each player goes.
- Make a "trick shot" hole where the ball must bounce off a wall to get in.

## Stuck?
- The ball rolls too far: use a softer ball, or tap more gently.
- A hole is impossible: make the cup bigger by using a box or a bowl on its side.

## Show it off
Invite the family to play a round. Your child is the course guide and keeps score.

## Clean up
Return books, pillows and cups. Fold cardboard pieces to save for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds engineering thinking, adding scores, and good sportsmanship. Use a light ball and short gentle swings indoors, and keep fragile things out of the room. You do any cutting of the tunnel doors. **Ask at dinner:** "Which hole was the hardest to build, and how did you make it work?"
