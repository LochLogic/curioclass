title: Sustainable Home Model
grade: 11
order: 15
kind: Build
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: High
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: cardboard boxes, scissors, tape, glue, a ruler, a pencil, aluminum foil, plastic wrap, paper, scraps of fabric or felt, a desk lamp, a thermometer if you have one
nice: a craft knife and cutting mat, paint, twigs and moss, a small fan

## The big idea
It is the year 2080. Summers are brutal, winters are wild, and energy is precious. Your client wants a home that
stays comfortable with almost no power. Design and build a scale model that uses sun, shade, wind and insulation the
way real green architects do, then test it under a "sun" lamp.

## Goals
- A cardboard model home at about 1:50 scale with at least four sustainable features.
- A test that shows one feature actually works.

## Objectives
- I can apply passive design: orientation, overhangs, insulation and cross ventilation.
- I can build to scale with measured walls and windows.
- I can test a design claim with a fair comparison.

## Get ready
Clear a big table and cover it with newspaper. Pick your site and climate: desert, coast, snowy mountain, city
rooftop. Write one sentence about the biggest weather challenge.

## Steps
1. Sketch a floor plan (15 minutes). At 1:50, 2 cm on paper is 1 m in real life. A small house might be 20 cm by 15 cm.
2. Decide where the sun comes from. In the northern hemisphere, big windows face south for winter warmth.
3. Build the walls and roof from cardboard. Cut window openings and cover them with plastic wrap.
4. Add four features (30 minutes). Ideas: **overhangs** that shade summer sun, **insulation** (felt or fabric
   between wall layers), **reflective roof** (foil), **cross ventilation** (windows on opposite walls),
   **green roof** (paper moss or real moss), **rain barrel** from a bottle cap.
5. Test one claim. Build a plain box the same size. Put both under the lamp for 15 minutes, then compare how warm
   the inside feels or read the thermometer.
6. Label every feature with a tiny sign explaining what it does.

## Make it yours
- Design for a disaster: a home that survives floods on stilts or wildfire with a no-burn zone around it.
- Make it a tiny house on wheels that fits on a 2.5 m trailer.
- Add a cutaway wall so viewers can see inside.

## Stuck?
- Walls keep falling over: tape an inside corner strip or add a cardboard floor to glue them to.
- The test shows no difference: run it longer and keep the lamp the same distance from each box.

## Show it off
Give the family a sales tour as the architect, pointing out each feature and showing the test result.

## Clean up
Sweep cardboard scraps, recycle what you can, and store tools.

## For the grown-up
This combines environmental science, geometry and scale, and design thinking. If a craft knife is used, a cutting
mat and slow cuts keep it safe. The lamp gets warm, so keep it away from paper. **Ask at dinner:** "What would make
our home use less energy?"
