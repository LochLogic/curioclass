title: Pollinator Patch
grade: 11
order: 30
kind: Outside
time: 75-120 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a notebook, a pencil, a trowel or big spoon, a few pots or containers with drainage holes (or a patch of ground), soil, seeds from the spice cabinet (coriander, dill, mustard or fennel seeds often sprout), a shallow dish, pebbles, water
nice: native wildflower seeds, a magnifying glass, a field guide to insects

## The big idea
Bees, butterflies and hoverflies keep a third of our food growing, and many of them are in trouble. You are going
to scout your area for pollinators, find out what they visit, and plant a small patch built just for them. Even a pot
on a balcony can become a pollinator rest stop.

## Goals
- A 20-minute pollinator survey with notes on every visitor you see.
- A planted patch or pot and a "puddling station" where insects can drink.

## Objectives
- I can observe and record insects and the flowers they visit.
- I can choose plants based on what pollinators need: food, water and shelter.
- I can design a habitat for a specific purpose.

## Get ready
Tell a grown-up where you will be. Pick a sunny spot: a corner of a yard, a balcony, or a sunny windowsill box. Check
the spice cabinet for whole seeds and ask if you can use a few.

## Steps
1. Do the survey (20 minutes). Sit near flowers or a weedy area. For each visitor, note type (bee, wasp, fly,
   butterfly), color and what flower it chose. Keep your distance; most are calm if left alone.
2. Make a quick tally. Which flowers got the most visitors? What colors?
3. Plan the patch on paper: sun, soil and what to plant. Mix flower shapes and colors for different insects.
4. Plant (25 minutes). Fill pots or loosen soil. Sow seeds about twice as deep as they are wide. Water gently.
5. Build a puddling station: a shallow dish of pebbles and water, so insects can land and drink without drowning.
6. Add shelter: a small pile of sticks or a bundle of hollow stems in a sunny corner.
7. Make a care plan: water every day or two and check for sprouts in a week.

## Make it yours
- Make a pollinator field guide for your own block with drawings of each visitor.
- Design a "pollinator highway" map showing where patches could connect across your neighborhood.
- Paint a sign for the patch with the names of the visitors you hope to attract.

## Stuck?
- No pollinators around: it may be too cold or cloudy. Plant first and survey on a warm sunny day.
- Seeds do not sprout: spice seeds can be old. Try a different spice or ask a neighbor for a few cuttings.

## Show it off
Give the family a tour of the patch and your survey results, and set a date to check on it together.

## Clean up
Rinse the trowel, sweep spilled soil, and wash your hands.

## For the grown-up
This builds ecology, observation and planning skills. If anyone has a bee or wasp allergy, keep them away during the
survey. Without a yard, the windowsill version works. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the most common pollinator you
saw?"
