title: Living Room Campout
grade: 1
order: 24
kind: Pretend
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: blankets, chairs or a table for a tent, pillows, a flashlight, a towel for a "campfire" (red, orange and yellow paper or tissue), a snack, a book, paper, crayons
nice: a sleeping bag, binoculars from cardboard tubes, a small speaker for nature sounds

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Pack your backpack, camper! We're going on a camping trip without even leaving home. We'll set up a tent, build a pretend campfire, tell stories, spot the stars and listen for owls."

## Goals
- Your child sets up an indoor campsite with a tent and a pretend campfire.
- Your child leads at least two camp activities.

## Objectives
- Your child can make a packing list of things a camper needs.
- Your child can tell a short story with a beginning, middle and end.
- Your child can name things found in nature at night (owls, stars, the moon).

## Get ready
Pick a room you can make dark. Move a table or chairs to make the tent frame. Gather blankets and pillows.

## Steps
1. **Packing list** (10 minutes). Ask: "What do campers need?" Your child draws a list: flashlight, pillow, snack, book, hat. They gather each thing and pack a backpack or bag.
2. **Build the tent** (15 minutes). Drape blankets over the table or chairs. Your child decides where the door goes. Put pillows inside.
3. **Build the campfire.** Your child crumples red, orange and yellow paper and piles it in the middle of a towel ring of "rocks" (balled socks). Put the flashlight under it, pointing up, so it glows.
4. **Campfire snack.** Eat a snack by the "fire." Talk about real camping safety: fires are only for grown-ups.
5. **Stars and owls** (15 minutes). Turn off the lights. Poke holes in a piece of paper (grown-up) and shine the flashlight through it onto the ceiling to make stars. Make up constellations. Hoot like owls back and forth.
6. **Campfire stories** (15 minutes). Your child tells a story about animals in the forest, and you tell one too. Use the flashlight under your chin for drama!
7. **Sleepy time.** Read a book in the tent with the flashlight, then lie quietly and "listen to the forest."

## Make it yours
- Make it a jungle camp, an arctic camp or a moon camp.
- Go on a night hike through the dark house with a flashlight, looking for hidden stuffed animals.
- Make up a camp song and sing it around the fire.

## Stuck?
- The tent keeps falling: tuck blanket corners under heavy books on the chair seats.
- Your child gets scared in the dark: keep a small light on in the next room, and give them the flashlight.

## Show it off
Invite the family into the tent after dinner for campfire stories.

## Clean up
Take down the tent together and fold the blankets. Your child puts the paper campfire in a bag for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds storytelling, planning (packing lists), nature knowledge and lots of imagination. Use flashlights only, never real candles or fire. If you poke star holes, do it yourself with a pencil. **Ask at dinner:** "What was your favorite part of our camping trip?"
