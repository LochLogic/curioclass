title: Fix Something Broken
grade: 12
order: 1
kind: Build
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: one broken, non-electric thing (a wobbly chair, a drawer that sticks, ripped jeans, a cracked mug, a toy with a snapped part, a backpack with a dead zipper), a screwdriver set, glue suitable for the material, tape, needle and thread, sandpaper, a pencil and paper
nice: wood glue and clamps, a bar of soap for sticky drawers, pliers, a sewing patch

## The big idea
Every house has a pile of things waiting for "someday." Today is someday. You are the repair shop: you will
diagnose what actually failed, plan a fix, and bring one thing back to life. Repair is a quiet superpower, and the
first fix is always the hardest.

## Goals
- One broken object, working again or better than before.
- A short repair log: what failed, why, and what you did.

## Objectives
- I can diagnose a failure by looking for the root cause, not just the symptom.
- I can choose the right fix and the right material for the job.
- I can test a repair and judge whether it will last.

## Get ready
Ask the household which broken thing they would most like back. Clear a work surface and lay down newspaper or an
old towel. Lay out tools so you can see them all.

## Steps
1. Inspect it (10 minutes). Take photos or sketch it if it comes apart. Ask: what moves, what should not, and where
   is the wear?
2. Find the root cause. A wobbly chair is often loose screws or a joint where old glue failed. A sticky drawer is
   usually swollen wood or a bent runner. A zipper that splits is usually a worn slider, not the teeth.
3. Plan the fix and write it down in three steps before touching anything.
4. Repair it (30 to 60 minutes). Chairs: remove the loose joint, scrape off old glue, apply wood glue, clamp or tie
   tight with string overnight. Drawers: sand the rubbing edge and rub soap on the runners. Jeans: patch from the inside
   and stitch around with a tight running stitch. Zippers: squeeze the slider gently with pliers.
5. Test it. Sit in the chair, slide the drawer 20 times, tug the seam.
6. Fill in your repair log: problem, cause, fix, test result, what you would do differently.

## Make it yours
- Add an upgrade: a visible mend in a bright color (the Japanese idea of making repairs beautiful), or a new handle.
- Open a family "repair desk" for the week and take two more jobs.
- Turn something unfixable into something new: a broken mug into a pen holder or a planter.

## Stuck?
- You cannot figure out the cause: move it slowly and watch where it gives.
- Glue will not hold: the surfaces were dirty or smooth. Scrape and sand them, then glue again.

## Show it off
Hand it back to its owner at dinner with your repair log, like a shop ticket.

## Clean up
Put every tool back where it lives, sweep sanding dust, and cap the glue.

## For the grown-up
This builds problem solving, practical engineering and independence. Keep it non-electrical; nothing that plugs in.
Tools are fine with your permission. **Ask at dinner:** "What was really wrong with it, under the obvious problem?"
