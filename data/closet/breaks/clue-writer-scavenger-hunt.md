title: Clue Writer Scavenger Hunt
grade: 2
order: 9
kind: Game
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 2+
materials: index cards or paper cut into 8 pieces, a pencil, a small "treasure" (a snack, a sticker, a note, a small toy), a bag or box for the treasure, tape
nice: an envelope for the first clue, a paper crown for the winner, a flashlight for a nighttime hunt

## The big idea
Read aloud: "This time YOU are the treasure master. You will hide a prize and write the clues that lead to it. Will your hunter figure them out? Make the clues tricky, but not too tricky!"

## Goals
- A treasure hunt with 6 clues your child wrote and hid.
- A hunter (you, a sibling or a friend over) follows every clue and finds the prize.

## Objectives
- Your child can write a short clue that describes a place without naming it.
- Your child can put clues in order so each one leads to the next.
- Your child can test a clue by reading it out loud and checking that it makes sense.

## Get ready
Decide on the hunt area: two or three rooms, or a yard if you have one. Pick the treasure. Number the cards 1 to 6 in the corner.

## Steps
1. **Learn the trick.** Show a sample clue: "I'm cold inside and keep the milk. Look on my door." (The fridge.) Ask your child to guess. A clue tells what a place does or looks like.
2. **Pick 6 hiding spots** (5 minutes). Walk around together. Your child chooses six spots: the bathtub, a shoe, under a pillow, the mailbox, a plant pot, the bookshelf.
3. **Write the clues** (25 minutes). For each spot, your child writes a clue. Help with spelling, but let them come up with the ideas. Examples: "I have lots of stories but I can't talk." "You put your feet in me every day." Rhymes are a bonus: "Look where you get clean, in the tub so white and green."
4. **Check the order.** Clue 1 leads to spot 1, where clue 2 waits. The last spot holds the treasure.
5. **Hide them.** Your child tapes each clue in its spot, out of sight but reachable. You hold clue 1.
6. **Run the hunt.** The hunter reads clue 1 and goes. Your child follows along and may give one hint per clue.
7. **Treasure!** The hunter finds the prize. Then swap: the hunter writes a hunt for your child.

## Make it yours
- Make a theme: a pirate hunt with "ye" and "arr" in every clue, or a space hunt.
- Use picture clues for a younger sibling and word clues for a grown-up.
- Add one secret-code clue, where every letter is written backward.

## Stuck?
- A clue is too hard: add one more detail, like the color or the room.
- Your child gets stuck writing: ask, "What does this thing do? What does it look like?"
- No partner right now: hide the clues and save the hunt for when someone gets home.

## Show it off
Run the hunt for each person who comes home. Your child times each hunter and announces the fastest.

## Clean up
Collect every card, even the ones nobody found. Keep the clues for a future hunt.

## For the grown-up
Writing clues builds describing words, reading for meaning, and sequence (first, next, last). It also teaches kids to think about the reader. Keep hiding spots safe: nothing high, near the stove, or inside appliances. **Ask at dinner:** "Which clue were you proudest of, and why?"
