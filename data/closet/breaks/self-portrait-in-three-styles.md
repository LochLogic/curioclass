grade: 8
order: 20
kind: Art
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a mirror, paper, a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, colored pencils or markers, a black pen
nice: paints and brushes, old magazines for collage, a desk lamp to light one side of your face

## The big idea
Artists have drawn themselves for hundreds of years, and no two self-portraits look the same. Today you'll draw yourself three times, in three totally different styles, and see how much each one says about you.

## Goals
- Three self-portraits in three different styles.
- A short artist's note about which one feels most like you.

## Objectives
- I can use face proportions to draw a realistic portrait.
- I can change my style on purpose: realistic, cartoon and abstract.
- I can explain how style changes the mood of a picture.

## Get ready
Set the mirror up on a table so you can see your face while you draw. Light your face from one side if you can, so you can see shadows.

## Steps
1. **Learn the proportions.** Draw an oval for your head. Your eyes are halfway down, not near the top. The bottom of your nose is halfway between your eyes and chin. The mouth is a third of the way down from the nose to the chin. Ears go from eye level to nose level.
2. **Portrait 1: Realistic (25 minutes).** Look in the mirror more than at the paper. Draw lightly first, then add details and shading. Darker where your face turns away from the light.
3. **Portrait 2: Cartoon (15 minutes).** Exaggerate your most noticeable feature, like big curly hair or a wide smile. Simplify everything else to clean shapes and lines.
4. **Portrait 3: Abstract (20 minutes).** Pick a style: geometric (your face as triangles, squares, and circles, maybe showing the front and side at once), pattern (fill each part with a pattern), or color (use colors for feelings, not real skin tones). This one doesn't have to look like you. It has to feel like you.
5. **Compare them.** Line them up. What does each one say about you?
6. **Write an artist's note.** Two or three sentences: which one is most "you," and why?
7. **Frame them.** Glue each onto a slightly bigger piece of colored or cardboard paper for a frame.

## Make it yours
- Draw yourself as you might be in 30 years, or as you were at age five.
- Make a collage portrait from magazine pieces: eyes from one page, hair from another.
- Draw yourself as an animal that matches your personality.

## Stuck?
- Realistic one looks weird: check the proportions. Most people draw the eyes too high.
- You don't like your face: art isn't a photograph. Pick out one thing to make the hero of the drawing.
- Abstract feels random: decide on one rule (only triangles, only blue) and stick to it.

## Show it off
Hang all three side by side and let your family vote on which style captures you best.

## Clean up
Wash brushes if you painted, cap markers, and stack your art flat.

## For the grown-up
This builds observational drawing, proportion and knowledge of art styles (realism, cartooning, and abstraction like cubism), plus self-reflection. **Ask at dinner:** "Which of your three portraits feels most like you, and why?"
