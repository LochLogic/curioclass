title: Card Magic Show
grade: 6
order: 9
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a deck of playing cards, a table, a mirror, paper and pencil
nice: a cape or jacket, a "magic wand" (a pencil wrapped in tape), a cloth to cover the table

## The big idea
A magician is half trickster, half actor. The secret move is only part of it; the rest is the patter (what you say), the character you play, and keeping eyes where you want them. Learn three tricks, invent a stage persona, and put on a real show.

## Goals
- You learn and practice at least three card tricks.
- You perform a full show with a magician character and a finale.

## Objectives
- I can do a card trick smoothly without giving away the secret.
- I can use misdirection, getting the audience to look away from the secret move.
- I can perform with confidence and stay in character.

## Get ready
Sit at a table with the mirror where you can see your hands. Shuffle the deck and lay out paper for notes.

## Steps
1. **Trick 1: the key card.** Secretly look at the bottom card of the deck before the trick (this is your key card). Have someone pick any card, look at it, and put it on top. Cut the deck and complete the cut. Their card is now right after your key card. Deal through the cards face up and "sense" it.
2. **Trick 2: the 21-card trick.** Deal 21 cards into three columns of 7, face up, row by row. The spectator silently picks a card and tells you only which column it's in. Gather the columns, putting that column in the middle. Deal and repeat two more times. Their card is now the 11th card.
3. **Trick 3: the four aces.** Before the show, secretly put the four aces on top of the deck. Have someone cut off about a quarter of the deck and set it down (pile A), then cut three more piles beside it (B, C, D). Only you know the aces are on top of A. They pick up D, move its top 3 cards to the bottom, then deal one card onto each of the other piles. Repeat with C, then B, then A last. Flip the top card of each pile: four aces.
4. Practice each trick five times in front of the mirror (20 minutes). Watch for moments that look suspicious.
5. Write your patter. What do you say during each trick? Add a story: "My grandmother taught me to read cards from the way people blink..."
6. Create your magician persona. Name? Costume? Signature phrase? "The Great Mysterio has spoken."
7. Order your show: open with the quickest trick, finish with the four aces. Run the whole show once start to finish.

## Make it yours
- Invent your own variation on one trick, like finding the card inside a book or a shoe.
- Add a comedy bit where you "fail" a trick on purpose, then save it at the last second.
- Make a mind-reading act: a partner helps by secretly signaling you (agree on the code in advance).

## Stuck?
- The 21-card trick doesn't work: make sure you put the chosen column in the middle every single time.
- Your hands are nervous: slow down. Smooth and slow beats fast and fumbly.
- People keep asking how you did it: a magician never tells. Say "Magic," and smile.

## Show it off
Set up a "stage" in the living room and perform for your family. Dim the lights, introduce yourself, and bow at the end.

## Clean up
Count the cards (52) and put the deck away. Keep your patter notes for your next show.

## For the grown-up
Magic builds public speaking, practice habits, memory, and a little math (the 21-card trick is a sorting pattern). It's also a great confidence builder for shy kids. Be an enthusiastic audience, and resist asking for the secret. **Ask at dinner:** "Which trick fooled people best, and why?"
