title: Box Stage Puppet Show
grade: 1
order: 9
kind: Pretend
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a cardboard box (cereal box or larger), a box cutter or sharp scissors (grown-up only), paper, crayons or markers, tape, straws or craft sticks, a dish towel or fabric scrap for a curtain, safety scissors
nice: string to make the curtain open, a flashlight for a spotlight

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Ladies and gentlemen, the show is about to begin! You are the puppet maker, the stage builder and the star of the show. Build a tiny theater, make your actors, and put on a play."

## Goals
- Your child builds a box stage and makes at least 3 stick puppets.
- Your child performs a short puppet play with a beginning, middle and end.

## Objectives
- Your child can plan a story with characters, a setting and a problem.
- Your child can use different voices and actions for different characters.
- Your child can perform for an audience and speak clearly.

## Get ready
Cut a large rectangle out of the front of the box to make the stage window, and cut the back off so hands can reach in (grown-up). Leave a frame about 1 inch wide around the window.

## Steps
1. **Decorate the theater** (15 minutes). Your child names the theater and draws on the frame: lights, stars, curtains. Write the name across the top.
2. **Plan the play** (10 minutes). Ask: "Who is in your play? Where does it happen? What goes wrong?" Example: a frog and a cat want the same lily pad, and a storm comes.
3. **Make the scenery.** Your child draws the background (a forest, the ocean, a kitchen) on paper. Tape it inside the back of the box so it shows through.
4. **Make the puppets** (20 minutes). Your child draws each character about the size of a hand and cuts them out with safety scissors (you help with tricky parts). Tape each to a straw or stick.
5. **Add a curtain.** Tape the dish towel over the top of the window so it can flip up and down.
6. **Rehearse** (10 minutes). Your child practices the play. One voice for each puppet. You can be the helper who moves a second puppet.
7. **Showtime!** Lift the curtain and perform. Take a bow at the end.

## Make it yours
- Do a play with a surprise ending the audience doesn't expect.
- Let the audience pick what happens next at the middle of the show.
- Add sound effects: knock on the table for a door, crinkle paper for fire, blow for wind.

## Stuck?
- The box tips over: put a heavy book inside the back corners.
- Your child forgets the story: draw the 3 parts on a card and tape it inside the box where only they can see.

## Show it off
Put on the show for the family when they get home. Your child makes tickets and sets out chairs.

## Clean up
Store puppets inside the theater box. Your child picks up scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds story structure, oral language, confidence speaking to an audience and fine motor skills. Do all box cutting yourself. Shy children often find it easier to speak through a puppet. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the problem in your play, and how did the characters fix it?"
