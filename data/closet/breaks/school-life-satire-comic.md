grade: 10
order: 20
kind: Story
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, a black pen or marker
nice: colored pencils, a fine-tip pen, tracing paper

## The big idea
Satire uses humor to point at something that's absurd and needs fixing. School is full of material: the bell that rings at the wrong time, a dress code no one understands, homework that needs three websites. You'll write and draw a one-page satirical comic that exaggerates something real until everyone sees how silly it is.

## Goals
- A one-page satirical comic with 6 to 9 panels.
- A clear target: a real thing about school life you're commenting on.

## Objectives
- I can use satire tools: exaggeration, irony and parody.
- I can aim satire at systems and situations, not at real people.
- I can pace a comic so the joke lands in the last panel.

## Get ready
List 10 things about school life that are weird or frustrating. Circle one.

## Steps
1. **Find the truth** (5 minutes). Write one sentence about what's actually wrong. "The cafeteria lunch period is 22 minutes, but the line is 20 minutes long."
2. **Pick a tool**:
   - **Exaggeration**: the lunch line is now 4 days long and has its own weather.
   - **Irony**: a "Wellness Week" so packed with activities that everyone is exhausted.
   - **Parody**: present the problem in another format, like a nature documentary about students waiting for lunch.
3. **Script it.** Write 6 to 9 panels with a short description and dialogue for each. The last panel is the punchline.
4. **Thumbnail** the page tiny to plan panel sizes. The punchline panel is usually a bit bigger.
5. **Draw** (40 minutes). Pencil, then ink. Keep characters simple and consistent.
6. **Letter** it. Write the words first, then draw balloons around them. Keep balloons small.
7. Check your aim: are you poking fun at a situation or system, not a real teacher or classmate? Change names and details if needed.

## Make it yours
- Do a series: three one-page comics, each a different tool.
- Make it a "news report" comic from a fake school newspaper.
- Write a solution comic next, showing the fix in an equally ridiculous way.

## Stuck?
- It's just a rant: turn up the exaggeration until it's absurd.
- It's mean: switch the target from a person to a rule or system.

## Show it off
Let your family read it. Ask a grown-up if school was the same in their day.

## Clean up
Cap pens and keep your comic flat in a folder.

## For the grown-up
Satire builds English skills: tone, purpose, audience and rhetorical devices, plus critical thinking. Encourage aiming at systems, not real people. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the real problem behind your comic?"
