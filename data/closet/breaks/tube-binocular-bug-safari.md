title: Tube Binocular Bug Safari
grade: k
order: 11
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: two toilet paper tubes, tape, yarn or string, crayons or markers, paper, a pencil, a clear plastic cup or jar with holes poked in the lid (grown-up pokes), a hat
nice: a magnifying glass, a clipboard, a spoon for gently scooping

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Welcome to the Bug Safari! Tiny animals are hiding under leaves, on flowers and in the cracks of the sidewalk. Explorers need binoculars to find them. Let's make yours and go looking!"

## Goals
- Your child makes a pair of safari binoculars.
- Your child finds and draws at least 5 different small creatures (or their signs).

## Objectives
- Your child can look closely and describe a bug: how many legs, what color, how it moves.
- Your child can count legs and wings.
- Your child can treat small animals gently and put them back where they found them.

## Get ready
Tape the two tubes side by side. Punch or poke a hole on the outside of each tube and tie on yarn for a neck strap. Fold a sheet of paper into 6 boxes for the "Safari Log." Pick a spot: a yard, a park, a sidewalk with plants, or a balcony with pots.

## Steps
1. **Decorate the binoculars** (10 minutes). Your child colors them like a jungle explorer would want. Put on the hat.
2. Talk about safari rules: look with your eyes first, touch only if a grown-up says yes, and always put creatures back home.
3. **Where do bugs hide?** Ask your child to guess. Then check: under a rock, under a leaf, on a flower, in the grass, along a wall.
4. **The safari** (30 minutes). Your child leads. When they spot something, they look through the binoculars, then up close. Count its legs together. Does it have wings? Is it fast or slow?
5. **Safari Log.** Your child draws each find in one box. You write what they say: "Ant. Black. Very fast. 6 legs."
6. If your child wants a closer look, gently scoop a bug into the cup with a leaf. Look for one minute, then let it go right where you found it.
7. Look for bug clues too: a spider web, a chewed leaf, a snail trail.

## Make it yours
- Your child becomes a bug: crawl, buzz or wiggle like each animal you found.
- Give each bug a name and a job. ("This ant is the Mail Ant. She is delivering a crumb.")
- Go on a night safari with a flashlight in the yard or by a window, and see who comes out after dark.

## Stuck?
- No bugs anywhere: lay a damp piece of cardboard or a board on the ground, wait 15 minutes, and lift it. Or look for birds, worms after rain, or bug clues instead.
- No yard: do a window safari, a balcony plant safari, or walk to a nearby patch of grass with a grown-up.

## Show it off
Tape the Safari Log to the fridge. Your child gives the family a "safari report" about the best find.

## Clean up
Rinse the cup, wash hands well, and hang the binoculars on a hook for the next safari.

## For the grown-up
This builds observation, counting, animal knowledge and care for living things, all early life science. Keep your child away from bees, wasps, spiders you don't know, and stinging plants, and teach "look, don't grab." Everyone washes hands after. **Ask at dinner:** "What is the coolest little animal you found, and where was it hiding?"
