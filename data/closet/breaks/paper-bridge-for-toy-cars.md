title: Paper Bridge for Toy Cars
grade: 2
order: 2
kind: Build
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: printer paper (about 15 sheets), tape, two stacks of books the same height, a toy car, coins or small blocks for weight, a ruler, a pencil
nice: a paper cup to hold the weights, index cards, string

## The big idea
Read aloud: "A river has cut Toy Town in half, and the cars can't get across! The town needs an engineer. You have only paper and tape. Can you build a bridge strong enough to hold the cars?"

## Goals
- A paper bridge that spans a gap of 8 inches and holds a toy car.
- A record of how many coins each bridge design held before it sagged.

## Objectives
- Your child can try a design, see where it fails, and change it.
- Your child can count and compare weights to tell which design is strongest.
- Your child can use the words fold, strong, weak and span.

## Get ready
Make two book stacks about 4 inches tall and set them 8 inches apart (measure with the ruler). That gap is the river. Put the paper, tape, car and coins nearby. Write "Bridge Test" at the top of a sheet of paper with three lines under it: Flat, Folded, My Idea.

## Steps
1. **Test a flat bridge.** Lay one plain sheet across the gap. Set the car on it. Splash! Ask: "Why do you think it fell?"
2. **Fold it.** Show your child how to fold a sheet like a fan: a fold about the width of a finger, flip, fold again, all the way down. Lay it across the gap. Now add coins one at a time and count until it sags. Your child writes the number on the Bridge Test sheet.
3. **Try other shapes** (10 minutes). Roll a sheet into a tube and tape it. Fold one into a U-shaped channel. Test each with coins.
4. **Design your own** (20 minutes). Your child picks the best ideas and combines them: two tubes side by side with a flat deck on top, a fan folded under a flat sheet, sides folded up like walls. Use no more than 5 sheets.
5. **The big test.** Drive the car across slowly. Then load coins in the middle until it bends. Write down the number.
6. **Make it wider.** Move the books to 12 inches apart. Does the winning bridge still work? What has to change?
7. **Name it.** Every real bridge has a name. Your child writes one on a paper flag and tapes it to the end.

## Make it yours
- Build a drawbridge: tape a string to one end so it can lift for paper boats.
- Make it a two-lane bridge so two cars can pass at once.
- Add a toll booth made from a small box, and your child writes the price sign.

## Stuck?
- Folds keep flattening out: tape the ends of the fan folds so they hold their shape.
- The bridge slides off the books: tape each end to the top book.
- Your child gets frustrated: remind them that real engineers test and break lots of bridges. A broken bridge is information.

## Show it off
Hold a ribbon-cutting. Cut a strip of paper across the bridge and let the family drive the first car over while your child tells how many coins it held.

## Clean up
Collect the coins in a cup, recycle the scrap paper, and put the books back on the shelf.

## For the grown-up
This builds early engineering and measurement: same paper, different shapes, very different strength. Counting and comparing coins is real math. Keep coins away from younger siblings. **Ask at dinner:** "Why do you think a folded sheet is so much stronger than a flat one?"
