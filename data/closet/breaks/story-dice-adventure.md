title: Story Dice Adventure
grade: 3
order: 16
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pencil, scissors, tape, markers, a ruler
nice: a small box, real dice

## The big idea
Roll the dice. A dragon! A bathtub! A broken key! Now make a story that uses all three. Build your own story dice and see what wild tale they give you.

## Goals
- You make 3 paper dice with pictures on every side.
- You write or tell a story using what you rolled.

## Objectives
- I can make up a story with a beginning, middle and end.
- I can connect things that don't seem to go together.
- I can make a 3D shape from a flat paper.

## Get ready
Draw a cross shape on paper. Draw 4 squares in a column. Add 1 square on each side of the second one. Make each square 2 inches. Add small flaps on the edges for tape.

## Steps
1. Draw and cut out 3 cross shapes. Each will become a die.
2. Die 1 is WHO. Draw 6 characters: a dragon, a grandma, a robot, a dog, a pirate, a baby.
3. Die 2 is WHERE. Draw 6 places: a castle, a bathtub, the moon, a school, a jungle, a pizza shop.
4. Die 3 is WHAT. Draw 6 problems or things: a lost key, a storm, a giant egg, a magic hat, a hole, a sneeze.
5. Fold each cross into a cube. Tape the edges.
6. Roll all 3. Write down what you got.
7. Write the story (20 minutes). Beginning: who and where. Middle: the problem. End: how it got fixed.
8. Roll again. Tell this story out loud, without writing.

## Make it yours
- Make a fourth die for feelings: happy, angry, scared, silly, sleepy, brave.
- Make a "twist" die. One side says "It was all a dream!"
- Draw your story as a 4-box comic.

## Stuck?
- Cubes won't close: make the tape flaps bigger.
- The roll makes no sense: that is the fun! Ask "how could this happen?"
- The story stops: add "Suddenly..." and something new happens.

## Show it off
Roll for a family member at dinner. They must tell a 1-minute story. Then you do!

## Clean up
Keep the dice in a box. Recycle scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds story structure and creative thinking, plus spatial skills when folding a net into a cube. It also works as a quick game in a waiting room later. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the strangest roll you got, and how did the story end?"
