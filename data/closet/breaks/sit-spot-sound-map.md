materials: paper, a pencil, colored pencils, a clipboard or hard book, a blanket, cushion or chair
nice: a watch or timer, binoculars, a warm drink in a travel mug

## The big idea
Naturalists have a trick: sit in the same spot, totally still, and the world starts happening around you. Birds come back, bugs crawl out, and you hear sounds you never noticed. Find your "sit spot" and map everything you hear.

## Goals
- Two or three sound maps from the same spot.
- A short piece of writing about the most interesting sound you heard.

## Objectives
- I can sit still and focus my attention for 10 to 15 minutes.
- I can map sounds by direction and distance.
- I can sort sounds as natural, animal or human-made.

## Get ready
Pick your spot: the yard, a balcony, a front step, a park with permission, or an open window if that's all you've got. Dress for the weather. Tell someone where you'll be.

## Steps
1. **Make the map.** Put an X in the middle of the page: that's you. Draw an arrow pointing to the front of where you sit.
2. **Settle in.** Sit, breathe slowly, and close your eyes for one minute. Let your body get still.
3. **Listen and map (15 minutes).** Every time you hear a sound, draw a small symbol where it seems to come from. Close sounds go near the X, far ones near the edge. Use symbols: a wavy line for wind, a little bird for a call, a box for a car.
4. **Try "deer ears."** Cup your hands behind your ears and turn your head slowly. Then cup them in front of your ears to hear what's behind you.
5. **Color code.** Use one color for animal sounds, one for weather or plants, one for human-made sounds. Count each kind.
6. **Do it again.** Take a break inside, then return in 20 minutes, or at a different time of day, for a second map. What changed?
7. **Write it.** Pick one sound and write about it: a three-line poem, a mini-story of what made it, or a description so clear someone else could imagine it.

## Make it yours
- Do a "sound safari" walk instead: stop every 50 steps and map 60 seconds of sound.
- Invent a creature for your most mysterious sound, and draw it.
- Compare maps: do a map at night (from a window or doorway) and one in the morning.

## Stuck?
- It's boring at first: keep going past 5 minutes. That's when birds and bugs come back.
- It's too noisy to hear anything natural: map the human sounds instead. Cities have their own wildlife.
- Can't sit still: lie down on your back and look up.

## Show it off
Show your family your maps and play "guess the sound": you describe it, they guess what it was.

## Clean up
Shake out the blanket, bring everything inside, and keep your maps together.

## For the grown-up
This builds focus, careful observation, and environmental awareness, the habits behind life science and nature writing. Know where your child is sitting, and if it's outside the yard, make sure they have your permission. **Ask at dinner:** "What sound did you hear today that you'd never noticed before?"
