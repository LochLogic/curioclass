## The big idea
You don't need drums to make a beat. You already carry an instrument everywhere: your body! Claps, snaps, stomps, and pats make a whole band. Today you compose (write) your own body percussion piece and perform it.

## Goals
- You compose a body percussion piece with three sections.
- You write it down in your own symbols and perform it.

## Objectives
- I can keep a steady beat while playing different sounds.
- I can write a rhythm with symbols so someone else can read it.
- I can build a piece with a pattern, like A, B, A.

## Get ready
Clear a space to stand. Rule a sheet of paper into 4 rows of 8 boxes. Each box is one beat.

## Steps
1. Find your sounds (5 minutes). Try at least six: clap, snap, stomp, pat legs, pat chest, rub hands, click your tongue.
2. Make a symbol for each one. Example: C = clap, S = stomp, P = pat legs, and a dash means rest.
3. Write Section A in the first row. Fill 8 boxes. Example: S - C - S S C -. Play it four times in a row.
4. Write Section B in the second row (10 minutes). Use different sounds. Make it busier or quieter than A.
5. Build the piece. Play A, then B, then A again. That shape is called ABA.
6. Add a big ending: three loud stomps, then silence, then one clap.
7. Practice three times. Count "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8" in your head to stay steady.

## Make it yours
- Add a section C that speeds up, like a train going faster.
- Write a part for a second player. Play both parts at once.
- Make it a story: soft pats for rain, stomps for thunder, claps for lightning.

## Stuck?
- You lose the beat: slow down and tap your foot on every beat.
- It sounds boring: add one rest (a silent box) in a surprising spot.
- Your score is confusing: add a key at the top showing every symbol.

## Show it off
Perform your piece for your family. Then teach them Section A so they can join in.

## Clean up
Fold your score and keep it. Get a drink of water after all that stomping.

## For the grown-up
This builds rhythm, steady beat, and music writing (notation), plus the musical idea of form (ABA). It is quiet enough for an apartment if your child swaps stomps for leg pats. **Ask at dinner:** "Can you teach me your Section A?"
