title: Family Coupon Book
grade: 2
order: 27
kind: Kindness
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: 3 or 4 sheets of paper, scissors, a stapler, crayons or markers, a pencil, a ruler
nice: colored paper, stickers, an envelope for each book, ribbon

## The big idea
Read aloud: "A coupon is a little paper ticket you can trade for something. Today you are making a coupon book full of kind things YOU will do for someone you love. Who will get your gift, and what will you give them?"

## Goals
- A coupon book with at least 6 coupons for someone at home.
- One coupon "cashed in" today so your child sees it in action.

## Objectives
- Your child can think about what someone else would enjoy.
- Your child can write a short, clear coupon with a promise.
- Your child can keep a promise by doing a coupon task.

## Get ready
Fold each sheet in half, then in half again, and cut along the folds to make 4 small rectangles per sheet. You'll have 12 to 16 coupons.

## Steps
1. **Pick a person.** Ask: "Who would you like to surprise? What do they like?"
2. **Brainstorm** (10 minutes). Your child lists kind things they can really do: one free hug, help set the table, read you a story, a backrub, a quiet hour, help with laundry, make you a drawing, choose the movie.
3. **Write the coupons** (20 minutes). Each coupon says what it is and who it's for: "GOOD FOR: One back rub. To: Dad. From: Ava." Help spell, but let your child try first.
4. **Decorate.** Your child adds a drawing on each coupon, like a heart or a plate.
5. **Make a cover.** Title: "Coupons for ___." Add a picture of the person.
6. **Staple it.** Stack the coupons behind the cover and staple on the left side.
7. **Cash one in.** Ask your child to give you one coupon and do it right now.

## Make it yours
- Make a "mystery coupon" that says only "Surprise!" and decide later what it is.
- Make coupons for a neighbor or grandparent and mail them.
- Add a "silly coupon": "Good for one dance party in the kitchen."

## Stuck?
- Your child only thinks of big things: help them pick things they can do by themselves.
- Writing feels long: make fewer, bigger coupons, or let them dictate half.

## Show it off
Give the book as a surprise gift when the person gets home, with a hug.

## Clean up
Recycle scraps, cap the markers, and put the stapler back.

## For the grown-up
This builds empathy, thinking about others' feelings, practical writing and following through on promises. Help your child keep coupons realistic and kind. **Ask at dinner:** "Which coupon do you think they'll use first?"
