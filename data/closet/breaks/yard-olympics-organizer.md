time: 60-120 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper and a pencil, a timer or clock with a second hand, a tape measure or a measured piece of string, household items for events (a sock ball, a frisbee or paper plate, a bucket, cones made from cups or shoes), water bottles
nice: sidewalk chalk, a whistle, paper medals and a marker, a hula hoop

## The big idea
The Olympics don't just happen; someone plans every event, every rule, and every medal. You're the organizer and the athlete. Invent five events, run an opening ceremony, and compete for gold against your siblings, your family, or your own personal records.

## Goals
- You design and run a five-event competition with clear rules and scoring.
- You award medals (or personal records) at a closing ceremony.

## Objectives
- I can design events that are fair and safe.
- I can measure and record times and distances accurately.
- I can keep my body moving for an hour, with breaks.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up which outdoor space you can use: a yard, a courtyard, or a park nearby. Check the area for holes, rocks, or broken glass. Write an event list and a score sheet with columns for each event.

## Steps
1. Design five events (15 minutes). Mix speed, accuracy, and silliness. Ideas: 20-meter dash, sock-ball toss into a bucket from 3 meters, standing long jump, cup-and-spoon balance relay, 60-second jumping jacks.
2. Write one rule for each. Example: "Long jump: feet together at the line, measure to the back heel."
3. Set up the "stadium." Mark start and finish lines with chalk, string, or shoes. Measure distances so they're the same for everyone.
4. Hold an opening ceremony. Make up a country name, march in, and carry a paper torch.
5. Compete (40 minutes). Run each event. Write every result. Rest and drink water between events.
6. Score it: 3 points for first, 2 for second, 1 for third. Playing alone? Do each event twice and try to beat your first score.
7. Closing ceremony: hand out paper medals, play an anthem (hum it), and take a bow.

## Make it yours
- Invent a totally new sport and include it as the final event.
- Add a handicap system so younger siblings get a head start and everyone has a fair shot.
- Be a sports announcer for every event, with play-by-play commentary.

## Stuck?
- Events are too easy: make the target farther, the time shorter, or add a hop-on-one-foot rule.
- Nobody wants to play: compete against a "rival" stuffed animal and record its pretend scores.
- No outdoor space: do an indoor version in a hallway: paper-plate discus, sock-ball toss, wall sit, and balance on one foot.

## Show it off
Post your results board on the fridge, with a gold medal winner and any new records.

## Clean up
Pick up every cone, ball, and marker. Wash chalk off hands and bring the bottles inside.

## For the grown-up
This builds fitness and also planning, measuring, fair rules, and leadership. Please agree on where your child can go, and make sure they ask before leaving the yard. Water breaks matter, especially in warm weather. **Ask at dinner:** "Which event did you invent, and who won it?"
