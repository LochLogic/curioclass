title: Family Recipe and Its Story
grade: 11
order: 3
kind: Kitchen
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a dish that someone in your life makes (or made), its ingredients, normal cooking tools, paper and a pen, an index card or a sheet of heavy paper
nice: a phone to call a relative, an old photo, colored pencils

## The big idea
Every family has a dish that is never written down quite right: "a handful of this, cook it till it smells done."
Your mission is to capture one before it disappears. You will cook it, turn the vague bits into a real recipe, and
write the story that goes with it, so someone fifty years from now can make it too.

## Goals
- The dish, cooked by you, on the table.
- A finished recipe card with exact amounts plus a short story of where the dish came from.

## Objectives
- I can turn vague directions into measured amounts by testing and noting.
- I can interview someone with open questions and pull out a good story.
- I can write a recipe clear enough for a stranger to follow.

## Get ready
Choose a dish someone in your life knows by heart: a sauce, a bread, a soup, a holiday plate, or a weeknight
standby. Check the kitchen has the ingredients. Ask a grown-up which pans and burners are fine to use.

## Steps
1. Interview the keeper of the recipe (15 minutes), in person or by phone. Ask: "Who taught you this? When did you
   first make it? What goes wrong if you rush it?" Write down their exact words.
2. Ask them to walk you through it while you write. Every time they say "some" or "a little," ask them to show you or
   guess in spoons or cups.
3. Measure as you cook. Before each ingredient goes in, measure it and write it down. If you add more later, write that too.
4. Taste at three points and note what changes. These notes become the "how you know it's ready" lines.
5. Write the card. Ingredients in order of use, numbered steps, oven or burner settings, and a "you'll know it's
   done when..." line.
6. Write the story (15 minutes): 150 to 250 words on the back. Lead with a moment, not a date. Example: "My
   grandmother never used a timer. She listened to the onions."

## Make it yours
- Make a "remix" version: same soul, one new twist, and write a card for that too.
- Draw the dish or the person who taught it on the card's front.
- Start a family cookbook: a folder with this card as page one and blank pages for the next.

## Stuck?
- Nobody is around to interview: pick the dish you make most often and write your own story with it.
- The amounts still feel like guesses: make a half batch first, then adjust.

## Show it off
Serve the dish at dinner and read the story aloud before anyone eats.

## Clean up
Wash every pan, wipe the stove and counters, and put the card somewhere it will survive (a page protector helps).

## For the grown-up
This builds practical cooking, measurement and fractions, interviewing and personal writing, and it often starts
family conversations that would not otherwise happen. Agree beforehand on which appliances are okay, and check
allergies for anyone eating. **Ask at dinner:** "What surprised you about how this recipe is really made?"
