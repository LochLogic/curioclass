title: Design a Theme Park
grade: 4
order: 22
kind: Art
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 2 large sheets of paper taped together, a pencil, colored pencils or markers, a ruler, scrap paper
nice: stickers, a compass for circles

## The big idea
You're the boss of a brand-new theme park! Design the rides, the food stands, the shows and the map. Make it so amazing that everyone wants a ticket.

## Goals
- You draw a theme park map with at least 6 rides or attractions.
- You make a ticket and a ride guide.

## Objectives
- I can plan a space so people can move around it.
- I can use a map key with symbols.
- I can describe a ride so someone wants to try it.

## Get ready
Tape your paper together on a big table or the floor.

## Steps
1. Pick a theme. Underwater? Dinosaurs? Candy? Space? Your favorite animal?
2. Brainstorm 10 rides and attractions (10 minutes). Example: "The Shark Slide," a water slide through a fake shark's mouth.
3. Pick the best 6. Draw them on the map. Use the whole paper.
4. Draw paths that connect every ride. Add a front gate.
5. Add 3 food stands. Name each one.
6. Add a bathroom, a first-aid spot, and benches. Real parks need them!
7. Make a key. Use symbols for rides, food, bathrooms and paths.
8. Make a ticket and a ride guide with 1 sentence about each ride.

## Make it yours
- Add height rules for each ride: "You must be this tall to ride."
- Design a park mascot and draw it at the gate.
- Make a ride that's secretly scary but looks cute.

## Stuck?
- Too much empty space: add a lake, a garden or a show stage.
- Rides look the same: give each a different shape and color.
- Paths get messy: draw them in pencil first.

## Show it off
Give each family member a ticket and a guided tour of the map.

## Clean up
Put the markers away. Roll up the map and keep it safe.

## For the grown-up
This builds spatial planning, map skills, persuasive writing and imagination. **Ask at dinner:** "Which ride in your park would you go on first?"
