title: Nature Documentary About Your Family
grade: 12
order: 4
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper and a pen, a clipboard, comfortable shoes, a quiet voice
nice: a pair of binoculars or two paper tubes taped together, a hat with a brim, a phone to record audio

## The big idea
"Here, in the warm glow of the refrigerator, the adult male searches for the last yogurt. He has not yet noticed it
is gone." Wildlife documentaries take ordinary animal behavior and make it epic with a hushed voice and sweeping
music. You are going to observe the humans in your home like a field biologist, then narrate a live documentary about them.

## Goals
- A field notebook with observations of at least three "species" in your home (family, pets, neighbors at a distance).
- A live, narrated five-minute documentary performance.

## Objectives
- I can observe and record behavior with specific, accurate details.
- I can write narration that uses a documentary voice: calm, dramatic, scientific.
- I can perform with timing and comic restraint.

## Get ready
Make a field notebook page for each species: name, habitat, diet, behavior. Tell your family you are observing them
for a project, so they act natural (or not).

## Steps
1. Observe (20 minutes). Pick a time when people are home: after work, cooking, in the evening. Note small,
   specific behaviors: the way someone hunts for their keys, the evening migration to the couch.
2. Name the species with a Latin-style name: "Homo sapiens snackus."
3. Write your narration (20 minutes). Use documentary language: "remarkable," "territory," "the season of homework."
   Example: "The young one is defending her territory, the bathroom, from all challengers."
4. Plan a **story arc**: a hunt, a rivalry, a triumph. Every good documentary has drama.
5. Rehearse your voice: slow, low and quiet, like you do not want to scare the animals.
6. Perform it live, narrating from behind the couch or doorway as someone does the thing. Or narrate from your notes
   while the family watches you act it out.

## Make it yours
- Narrate the backyard, a balcony or your street: birds, dogs, the mail carrier's route.
- Add a sound track: hum dramatic music during the chase scene.
- Write it as a nature special about your pet, from the pet's point of view.

## Stuck?
- Nothing interesting is happening: zoom in. The way someone makes tea is full of ritual.
- It feels mean: aim the jokes at yourself first, and make every species a little heroic.

## Show it off
Perform it at dinner and give each family member a "species card" with their observed traits.

## Clean up
Put the notebook away and return any borrowed props.

## For the grown-up
This builds observation, scientific vocabulary, comic writing and performance. Your teen will be watching closely, so
play along. **Ask at dinner:** "What behavior did you observe that surprised you most?"
