title: Care Package for a Neighbor
grade: 3
order: 26
kind: Kindness
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a shoebox or paper bag, paper, markers, tape, things to give (a drawing, a homemade card, a bookmark, a plant cutting, a snack your family can share)
nice: string or ribbon, stickers, a small jar

## The big idea
Some neighbors live alone. Some are sick. Some just need a smile. Make a surprise care package full of homemade happy things. Small gifts can make a big day.

## Goals
- You make a care package with at least 4 homemade things.
- You give it to a neighbor with a grown-up.

## Objectives
- I can think about what someone else would like.
- I can write a kind note.
- I can plan and make gifts by hand.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up who to make it for. Maybe a neighbor, a grandparent, a building helper, or a family friend.

## Steps
1. Think about the person. What do they like? Gardens? Cats? Jokes? Write 3 things you know.
2. Make a card. Write a kind note. "I hope you have a sunny day! From your neighbor, Leo."
3. Make a drawing just for them, like their pet or their house.
4. Make a bookmark. Cut a strip of cardboard, draw on it, and add a joke.
5. Make a "smile jar" or envelope. Write 5 happy notes on scraps. Fold them up.
6. Add one more thing (15 minutes). A plant cutting in a cup. A paper flower. A family-made snack.
7. Decorate the box. Pack it nicely. Tape it closed with a ribbon.

## Make it yours
- Make it a theme box: a "sunshine" box with yellow things only.
- Make a coupon inside, like "Good for one leaf-raking day."
- Make a mini puzzle or maze for them to solve.

## Stuck?
- Don't know what they like: make a happy picture of flowers, a joke and a card.
- Not sure what to write: start with "I hope..." or "Thank you for..."
- Gifts look small: that's OK! A homemade thing means a lot.

## Show it off
Deliver it with a grown-up. Knock, smile, hand it over. Or leave it by the door with a note.

## Clean up
Put craft stuff away. Sweep scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds empathy, writing for a reader, and planning. Please go with your child to deliver it, and pick someone you know. If food goes in, check the person's allergies and keep it sealed. **Ask at dinner:** "How do you think they felt when they opened it?"
