title: Surprise for Someone at Home
grade: 6
order: 17
kind: Kindness
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, markers, tape, an envelope, whatever your surprise needs (see the steps; most use only paper and things around the house)
nice: wrapping paper or newspaper, ribbon, a small box

## The big idea
This is a top-secret mission. Your target: one person you live with. Your objective: make their day better without them figuring out it's coming. Find out what they'd love, plan it, pull it off, and keep it quiet until the big reveal.

## Goals
- You plan and carry out a secret surprise for one person you live with.
- They're surprised (and happy) when you reveal it.

## Objectives
- I can figure out what someone else would enjoy by noticing and asking.
- I can plan a project with steps and finish it on time.
- I can keep a secret and do something kind without expecting anything back.

## Get ready
Pick your target: a parent, a sibling, a grandparent, a roommate, anyone at home. Write their name at the top of a page labeled "Mission File."

## Steps
1. Gather intel (10 minutes). What do they like? What's stressing them? What do they never have time for? Sneak in a question like, "If you had a whole free afternoon, what would you do?"
2. Brainstorm five surprise ideas. Examples: clean and organize their messy space, a book of handmade coupons ("one foot rub," "I do your chore"), a handwritten "Top 10 Reasons You're Awesome" list, a mini art gallery of drawings of them, a fancy snack plate waiting when they get home.
3. Pick one, or combine two. Make a step-by-step plan with a time for each step.
4. Make it happen (40 minutes). Work while they're out or busy. Hide your materials.
5. Add a reveal. A note on their pillow with clues? A "Do Not Open Until 7 PM" sign?
6. Write a short card explaining why you did it. Be specific: "You always drive me to practice, even when you're tired."
7. Do a final check: is everything ready, hidden, and secret?

## Make it yours
- Make it a treasure hunt where three clues lead them to the surprise.
- Add a second "decoy" surprise to throw them off.
- Recruit an accomplice (someone else at home) to keep the target distracted.

## Stuck?
- You can't think of what they'd like: think about what they do for others, then do it for them.
- The surprise feels too small: small is perfect. A handwritten note can mean more than anything bought.
- They almost catch you: play it cool. "Oh, it's just a school thing."

## Show it off
Watch them find the surprise. Try not to give it away with your face.

## Clean up
Put away supplies and clean any space you used, so the surprise isn't "a mess in the living room."

## For the grown-up
This builds empathy, planning, and generosity, plus the fun of keeping a good secret. If you're asked a sneaky "intel" question this week, play along. **Ask at dinner:** "What made you pick that surprise for that person?"
