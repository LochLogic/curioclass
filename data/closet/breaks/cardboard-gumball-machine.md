title: Cardboard Gumball Machine
grade: 6
order: 14
kind: Build
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a cardboard box (a shoebox or cereal box), extra cardboard, a plastic cup or clear jar for the top, tape, glue, scissors, a ruler, a pencil, "gumballs" (balls of crumpled foil about 2 cm wide, marbles, or round candy)
nice: a hot glue gun with permission, a craft knife (grown-up use only), paint or markers

## The big idea
A gumball machine looks simple: push a slider, one gumball drops. But inside it's a clever bit of engineering that lets exactly one ball out at a time. Can you design a machine that never jams and never spits out two at once?

## Goals
- You build a working machine that releases one ball each time you push or pull a slider.
- Your machine runs at least 10 times in a row without jamming.

## Objectives
- I can design a mechanism that controls how objects move.
- I can measure carefully so parts fit.
- I can troubleshoot a jam and fix the cause.

## Get ready
Make your "gumballs" if you need them: crumple foil into balls about 2 cm across, all close to the same size. Test that one ball fits in your cup. Lay out your box and tools.

## Steps
1. Plan your design (10 minutes). Draw a side view: the cup on top, a slider plate under it, a drop hole below, and a ramp leading to the exit.
2. Cut a hole in the top of the box a little bigger than one ball. Cut the bottom off the cup and tape the cup upside down over the hole, so it works like a funnel. You pour balls in through the open top.
3. Make the slider: a strip of cardboard about 4 cm wide and 20 cm long, with one hole the size of a ball near one end. Cut a slot in the side of the box for it to slide through just under the top.
4. Cut the drop hole in a cardboard shelf below the slider, offset from the top hole by about 5 cm. When you pull the slider out, the ball in its hole lines up with the drop hole and falls.
5. Build a ramp from the drop hole down to an exit door you cut in the front of the box.
6. Test it (20 minutes). Fill the cup with balls. Push in, pull out, push in. Does one ball come out each time?
7. Fix any problems, then decorate the box so it looks like a real machine.

## Make it yours
- Add a "coin slot" lock: the slider only moves when a bottle cap is dropped in to push it.
- Make the ball take a twisty path before it exits, with a spiral ramp or a zigzag.
- Design it to give a "prize" sometimes: one special ball among the plain ones.

## Stuck?
- Two balls come out: the slider is too thick. Use thinner cardboard so the hole only fits one ball.
- Balls jam in the cup: make the top hole wider, or angle the bottom so balls roll toward the hole.
- The slider sticks: widen the slot or rub the cardboard edges with a pencil (graphite helps it slide).

## Show it off
Hold a "grand opening." Let family members try it and count how many perfect runs it does in a row.

## Clean up
Pick up scraps and put away tools. If you used real candy, keep it covered.

## For the grown-up
This builds engineering skills (mechanisms, measuring, troubleshooting) and persistence. If your child wants to use a hot glue gun, please give permission and check in; any craft knife cutting should be yours. If you use candy as the balls, be mindful of younger siblings and choking. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the hardest jam to fix?"
