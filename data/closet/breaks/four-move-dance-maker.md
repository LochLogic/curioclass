title: Four-Move Dance Maker
grade: 1
order: 16
kind: Move
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: paper, crayons, scissors (child can use safety scissors), tape, an open space, music (sing, hum or play some)
nice: a music player, scarves or ribbons, a mirror

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Every famous dance is made of moves put together. You're going to be the dance maker! Invent four moves, put them in order, and teach your dance to the whole family."

## Goals
- Your child invents a dance with 4 moves in order.
- Your child teaches the dance to someone else.

## Objectives
- Your child can make up movements and give them names.
- Your child can put moves in a sequence and repeat it.
- Your child can count to 8 in time to music.

## Get ready
Clear a space with nothing to trip on. Cut a sheet of paper into 8 cards. Pick 2 or 3 songs your child likes, or songs you can sing.

## Steps
1. **Warm up.** Stretch up high, touch toes, wiggle everything. Ask your child to show you 3 ways to move: fast, slow, big, small.
2. **Invent moves** (15 minutes). Your child makes up moves and gives each one a silly name: "The Robot Arm," "The Spaghetti Wiggle," "The Kangaroo Hop," "The Freeze Pose." Draw each one on a card as a stick figure.
3. **Pick four.** Your child picks their 4 favorite cards and puts them in a row on the floor. That's the dance!
4. **Count it.** Each move lasts for 8 counts. Practice: "Robot Arm, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. Spaghetti Wiggle, 2, 3..."
5. **Add music** (10 minutes). Put the song on or sing it. Do the dance over and over.
6. **Mix it up.** Your child switches the order of the cards. Is it more fun? Harder?
7. **Teach it** (10 minutes). Your child is the dance teacher and teaches you, card by card. They can correct you!

## Make it yours
- Make it an animal dance, with moves like a penguin waddle and a flamingo stand.
- Make a mirror dance: you face each other and copy every move at the same time.
- Make a story dance: the four moves tell a story, like waking up, going to the beach, swimming and falling asleep.

## Stuck?
- Your child can't think of moves: give a prompt ("Move like jelly," "Move like a robot running out of batteries").
- Counting to 8 is hard: use 4 counts per move.

## Show it off
Your child teaches the dance to the family after dinner, then everyone performs it together.

## Clean up
Your child puts the move cards in an envelope labeled with the dance's name.

## For the grown-up
This builds coordination, sequencing (the same skill used in reading and math), counting and confidence. Teaching someone else is a great way to check what you know. Clear sharp corners and have your child dance barefoot or in grippy socks. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the name of your dance, and what are the four moves?"
