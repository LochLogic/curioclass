title: Invent a New Animal
grade: 1
order: 15
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: paper, crayons or markers, a pencil, scissors (child can use safety scissors), glue stick, old magazines or catalogs
nice: play dough or clay, a stapler (grown-up)

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Scientists just discovered a brand-new animal that no one has ever seen. Guess what? You get to invent it! What does it look like? What does it eat? Where does it live? It's all up to you."

## Goals
- Your child invents an animal with a name, body parts, a home and a food.
- Your child makes an "animal fact page" and tells a story about the animal.

## Objectives
- Your child can explain how an animal's body parts help it live (wings to fly, fins to swim).
- Your child can describe a habitat.
- Your child can tell a short story with the animal as the main character.

## Get ready
Fold one sheet of paper in half to make a "fact page": the left side for a picture, the right for facts. Write these starters on the right side: Name, Lives in, Eats, Special power, Fun fact.

## Steps
1. Talk about real animals. Ask: "Why does a duck have webbed feet? Why does a giraffe have a long neck?" Body parts help animals live.
2. **Mix and match** (10 minutes). Your child picks parts from 2 or 3 real animals: an elephant trunk, butterfly wings, a turtle shell. Or cut parts from magazine pictures and glue them together.
3. **Draw it** (15 minutes). Your child draws the new animal big and bright on the left side of the fact page.
4. **Name it.** Combine animal names ("Elephanterfly") or make up a word ("Zoopsy"). Write the name at the top.
5. **The facts** (10 minutes). Your child tells you each fact and writes the easy words. Where does it live? What does it eat? What is its special power? Ask: "Why does it need that?"
6. **Draw its home.** On a second paper, your child draws where it lives: a cave, a cloud, the bottom of the sea.
7. **Tell a story** (10 minutes). Your child tells a story about one day in the animal's life: "One morning the Zoopsy woke up hungry..." You write it down.

## Make it yours
- Make the animal from play dough or clay and build its home in a shoebox.
- Invent a baby version and a grown-up version, and show how it changes.
- Invent a whole family of animals that live together in a new land.

## Stuck?
- Your child can't decide: roll a die (or pick numbers out of a hat) to choose from a list of 6 animal parts.
- Writing is hard: your child says the words, you write them, and your child copies one word.

## Show it off
Hold a "science discovery" announcement at dinner. Your child shows the fact page and tells the story.

## Clean up
Your child collects scraps, caps markers and puts the fact page in a folder.

## For the grown-up
This builds life science (how body parts help animals survive, a first-grade topic), creative writing and vocabulary. Kids learn real animal facts by explaining their made-up ones. **Ask at dinner:** "What's your animal's special power, and why does it need it?"
