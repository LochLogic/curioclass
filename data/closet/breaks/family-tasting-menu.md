title: Family Tasting Menu
grade: 11
order: 18
kind: Kitchen
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: High
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: whatever ingredients the kitchen has (check with a grown-up), small plates or saucers, spoons and forks, a cutting board, a knife, pots or pans as needed, paper and a pen
nice: herbs, a squeeze bottle for sauces, a tea towel draped over your arm

## The big idea
Fancy restaurants serve **tasting menus**: five or six tiny courses, each a surprise. You will plan and cook a
four-course tasting menu from what is already in the kitchen, with a theme, a printed menu and plating that would
make a chef nod. Then you serve it, course by course.

## Goals
- Four small courses, cooked and plated, served to at least one other person.
- A handwritten menu card with course names and a theme.

## Objectives
- I can plan a menu that balances flavors, textures and temperatures.
- I can time cooking so courses are ready in order.
- I can plate food with intention: height, color and space.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up which ingredients are fair game and which tools you can use. Take inventory: write what is in the
fridge, freezer and pantry. Check for allergies.

## Steps
1. Choose a theme (10 minutes): "A day in a garden," "Breakfast all day," "One ingredient four ways" (eggs, potatoes, apples).
2. Plan four courses: a **bite** (one mouthful, sharp flavor), a **small plate** (something warm), a **main**
   (the biggest, still small), a **sweet**. Vary texture: crunchy, creamy, crisp, soft.
3. Write a timeline backward from service time. Example: "6:00 serve bite; 5:30 roast potatoes; 5:10 make sauce."
4. Prep everything first: wash, chop, measure into small bowls. Chefs call it **mise en place**.
5. Cook (45 minutes). Taste as you go, adding salt or acid (lemon, vinegar) when something tastes flat.
6. Plate with care: an odd number of pieces, height in the center, a swipe of sauce, one bright garnish. Wipe the rim clean.
7. Serve one course at a time and introduce each in one sentence.

## Make it yours
- Do it as a blind tasting: guests guess the main ingredient of each course.
- Pick a color theme, like an all-green menu or all-orange.
- Make every course a remix of a childhood food, like grilled cheese as a tiny tart.

## Stuck?
- Courses are ready all at once: make two cold courses that can wait in the fridge.
- Something tastes bland: add salt, then acid, then something crunchy.

## Show it off
Set the table, hand out menu cards, and serve as the chef, explaining each dish.

## Clean up
Wash as you go, then do a final sweep: dishes, counters, stove, floor. A real chef leaves the kitchen spotless.

## For the grown-up
This builds cooking skills, planning, timing and creativity. Agree in advance on the stove, oven and knives, and
check allergies for everyone eating. **Ask at dinner:** "Which course would you put on a real restaurant menu?"
