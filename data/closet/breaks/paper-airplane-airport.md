title: Paper Airplane Airport
grade: 1
order: 7
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 6 to 10 sheets of printer paper, tape, a measuring tape or ruler, markers, a laundry basket or box, paper for signs
nice: paper clips, a hallway or yard, small stickers

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Welcome to the airport! Pilots need planes that fly far, planes that fly straight, and planes that can land on a target. Let's build a fleet and test them on the runway."

## Goals
- Your child folds at least 3 different paper airplanes.
- Your child tests them and finds out which flies farthest and which lands best.

## Objectives
- Your child can follow folding steps and then change a design.
- Your child can measure flight distance and compare numbers.
- Your child can explain one change that made a plane fly better.

## Get ready
Clear a hallway or go outside on a calm day. Tape a "runway" line on the floor where pilots throw from. Set a basket about 8 feet away as a landing target.

## Steps
1. **Build Plane 1: the Dart** (10 minutes). Fold paper in half the long way and open it. Fold the top corners to the middle line. Fold the new slanted edges to the middle again. Fold in half. Fold each wing down so its edge meets the bottom of the body. Your child names it and draws a logo.
2. **Test flight.** Throw from the runway line. Mark where it lands with a scrap of paper. Measure it with the measuring tape.
3. **Build Plane 2: the Glider** (10 minutes). Same as the Dart, but fold the tip down about 2 inches before the wing folds so the nose is blunt and the wings are wide.
4. **Test it the same way.** Which went farther? Which flew straighter? Write the distances on a chart.
5. **Your child's plane** (15 minutes). Your child changes something: bigger wings, wing tips folded up a pencil's width, a paper clip on the nose, a different shape. Ask: "What do you think will happen?"
6. **Airport challenges.** Who can land a plane in the basket? Who can fly through a doorway?
7. Award prizes: Longest Flight, Best Landing, Coolest Design.

## Make it yours
- Make it an airline: give planes passengers (drawn faces in the windows) and destinations.
- Build a control tower from a box, and you are the pilot asking for permission to land.
- Have a slow-flight contest: which plane stays in the air longest?

## Stuck?
- The plane nose-dives: fold the back edges of the wings up a tiny bit.
- It turns to one side: check that both wings are the same size and smooth out bends.

## Show it off
Hold an air show when the family gets home. Each person flies one plane and your child runs the tower.

## Clean up
Collect all the planes in a "hangar" box. Recycle the crumpled ones.

## For the grown-up
This builds measuring, comparing numbers, following steps and changing one thing at a time, which is how scientists test ideas. Folding builds fine motor control. Remind everyone not to throw planes at faces. **Ask at dinner:** "What change made your plane fly better?"
