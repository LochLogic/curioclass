title: A Book About Me
grade: k
order: 30
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: 5 sheets of paper, a stapler or yarn (grown-up), crayons or markers, a pencil, a mirror, a ruler or measuring tape
nice: family photos to glue in, stickers, a glue stick, a hole punch

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Every library has books about dinosaurs, trucks and princesses. But there is no book about YOU yet! Today you are the author. Let's make a book all about you."

## Goals
- Your child makes a 5-page book about themselves with a cover.
- Your child "reads" the book aloud to someone.

## Objectives
- Your child can draw a self-portrait looking in a mirror.
- Your child can tell facts about themselves: name, age, family, favorites.
- Your child can write their name and try writing a few letters or words.

## Get ready
Fold the 5 sheets in half and staple along the fold to make a 10-page book (grown-up). Write a short starter at the top of each page: "This is me." "My family." "I love to eat..." "When I grow up..." "My favorite thing to do is..."

## Steps
1. **The cover** (10 minutes). Your child looks in the mirror and draws their face: what color are their eyes and hair? Write "A Book About" and your child writes their name (or traces yours).
2. **This is me.** Measure your child with the tape. Write how tall they are. Your child draws themselves standing tall.
3. **My family.** Your child draws the people (and pets) they live with or love. You write the names they say.
4. **I love to eat...** Your child draws a favorite food. Ask: "What does it taste like?" and write their words.
5. **When I grow up...** Ask: "What do you want to be?" Let them dream big: astronaut, baker, dragon trainer. They draw it.
6. **My favorite thing to do** (10 minutes). Your child draws it and tells you a sentence to write.
7. **The last page.** Your child chooses what goes on the last page. A pet, a best friend, a place, a wish.

## Make it yours
- Make a book about a stuffed animal's life instead, written by your child.
- Add a handprint page: trace your child's hand and write their age in it.
- Make it a "superhero me" book, where your child has a superpower on every page.

## Stuck?
- Your child doesn't want to draw people: use stick figures, or let them glue in a photo.
- Your child gets tired: do the cover and two pages today, and finish tomorrow.

## Show it off
Your child "reads" the book to the family at dinner (they tell it from the pictures). Keep it on the bookshelf next to the real books.

## Clean up
Your child puts the crayons back and puts the book on the shelf.

## For the grown-up
This builds early writing (name, letters), self-awareness, and the idea that books are made by people, including them. It is lovely to save, and to make again next year to see how they've grown. Do the stapling yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "What is your favorite page in your book, and why?"
