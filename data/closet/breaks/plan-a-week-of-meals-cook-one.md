grade: 12
order: 3
kind: Kitchen
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper and a pen, the fridge and pantry, normal cooking tools, ingredients for one meal
nice: a cookbook, store flyers, a calculator

## The big idea
Pretend you have your own place next year and one rule: you cook five dinners a week without wasting food or going
broke. Chefs and home cooks beat this with a trick called **cook once, eat three times**: one batch of something that
becomes three different meals. Plan the week, then cook the anchor meal for the family tonight.

## Goals
- A five-dinner plan with a shopping list and cost estimate.
- One meal from the plan, cooked and served.

## Objectives
- I can plan meals that share ingredients to cut waste and cost.
- I can write a shopping list organized by store section.
- I can cook a full meal and time it so everything is ready together.

## Get ready
Take inventory: write down what is already in the fridge, freezer and pantry. Ask a grown-up about allergies,
tonight's budget and which appliances are okay.

## Steps
1. Choose an **anchor** (10 minutes): a big batch of one thing, like roasted chicken, a pot of beans, a tray of
   roasted vegetables, or seasoned rice.
2. Plan five dinners. Three use the anchor in different ways (example: roasted chicken night one, chicken tacos
   night two, chicken soup night four). Two are quick standbys (pasta, eggs, a grain bowl).
3. Write the shopping list by section: produce, dairy, protein, pantry. Cross off what you already have.
4. Estimate the cost. Guess prices from memory or ask a grown-up, then divide by the number of servings.
5. Cook the anchor meal (50 minutes). Read the full recipe first, prep everything, and start whatever takes longest.
6. Taste before serving. Salt, acid, a little fat, or something fresh usually fixes flat food.
7. Pack and label leftovers for night two.

## Make it yours
- Add a challenge: no more than 15 ingredients for the whole week.
- Plan a vegetarian week, or a week built around one cuisine.
- Make a "fridge rescue" night that uses whatever is left on Friday.

## Stuck?
- You cannot think of meals: list five things you like to eat, then find the one ingredient they share.
- The anchor takes too long: pick a faster one, like seasoned ground meat, beans or a big pot of grains.

## Show it off
Serve dinner and hand around the week's menu. Ask if anyone wants to be your "tenant" next week.

## Clean up
Wash everything, wipe the stove and counters, and label leftovers with the date.

## For the grown-up
This builds cooking, budgeting and planning, which are the skills for living on their own. Agree on the stove and
oven and check allergies before cooking. **Ask at dinner:** "What will this turn into on night two?"
