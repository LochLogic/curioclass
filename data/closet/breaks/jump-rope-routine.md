## The big idea
Jump rope teams do flips, tricks, and routines so fast the rope turns into a blur. You can start your own routine today. Learn five tricks, string them together, and perform a one-minute show that ends with a big finish.

## Goals
- You learn at least four jump rope tricks.
- You put them together into a routine and perform it.

## Objectives
- I can jump in a steady rhythm.
- I can link moves together in an order I choose.
- I can count beats to keep my routine on time.

## Get ready
Find a flat space with no low ceiling, lamps, or breakables nearby. Outside on pavement is best. Check the rope length: stand on the middle, and the handles should reach your armpits.

## Steps
1. Warm up (5 minutes). Jog in place, swing your arms, and do 10 jumps with no rope.
2. Learn the basic bounce. Jump with feet together, small and light, just high enough for the rope. Count to 20.
3. Learn the skier. Jump side to side, like skiing down a hill, while the rope turns.
4. Learn the bell. Jump forward and back while the rope turns.
5. Learn the side swing. Swing the rope to one side without jumping. Then swing it to the other side and jump through.
6. Invent your own trick (10 minutes). A spin? A one-foot hop? Give it a name.
7. Build your routine. Do each trick 8 times in an order you choose. Write it down: "Bounce 8, Skier 8, Bell 8, My Trick 8." Practice it three times.

## Make it yours
- Add a rhyme to jump to. Write your own: "Peanut butter, jelly jam, how many jumps? Count if you can!"
- Make a partner routine where two people jump one after the other, or side by side.
- Add a "freeze" move at the end, like a pose with the rope above your head.

## Stuck?
- You keep tripping: slow the rope down. Use your wrists, not your whole arms.
- No rope: jump an imaginary rope. Practice your footwork, then add the rope later.
- You're tired: take a water break and stretch. Tired legs trip more.

## Show it off
Perform your routine for your family after dinner. Say the name of each trick as you do it.

## Clean up
Coil the rope and put it away. Drink some water.

## For the grown-up
This builds coordination, rhythm, and stamina, plus the planning of putting moves in an order. Make sure the space is clear and, if indoors, that ceilings and lights are out of reach. A cord with no knots or hooks at the ends is safest. **Ask at dinner:** "What trick did you invent, and what is it called?"
