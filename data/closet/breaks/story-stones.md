title: Story Stones
grade: k
order: 13
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 8 to 10 smooth stones about the size of your child's palm (bigger than a golf ball), markers or paint, paper, a bowl or bag, a cloth bag or sock
nice: clear nail polish or glue for a shiny top (grown-up only), paint pens

## The big idea
Read aloud: "These are magic stones. Each one holds a piece of a story: a dragon, a house, a storm, a cupcake. When you pull them out of the bag, they tell a brand-new story every time!"

## Goals
- Your child makes a set of at least 8 story stones.
- Your child tells a story using stones pulled from the bag.

## Objectives
- Your child can draw a simple picture of a character, a place and an object.
- Your child can tell a story with a beginning, middle and end.
- Your child can connect two pictures with "and then..."

## Get ready
Collect stones on a walk or from a yard or garden center bin. Wash and dry them. Spread newspaper on the table. If you have paint, set out small cups of a few colors. Plan the categories on a sheet of paper: 3 characters, 3 places, 3 things.

## Steps
1. **Stone hunt.** If you collect them together, let your child pick the ones that "feel like stories."
2. Talk about stories: "Every story has someone in it, somewhere it happens, and something that happens." Show the category list.
3. **Draw the characters** (15 minutes). Your child chooses 3: maybe a cat, a robot and a princess. They draw one on each stone.
4. **Draw the places and things** (15 minutes). Places: a house, a tree, the moon. Things: a key, a cake, a rainbow. Let your child pick, and help with simple shapes if asked.
5. Let them dry. Drop them in the bag.
6. **Story time** (20 minutes). Your child pulls out one stone and starts: "Once upon a time there was a cat..." Pull another stone: "...and the cat found a key!" Keep going until all the stones are out or the story ends.
7. Take turns. You tell one story, then your child tells one with the same stones in a different order.

## Make it yours
- Make a "problem" stone (a storm, a lost shoe, a monster) that has to be in every story.
- Tell a "silly story" where every stone does something backward.
- Make a stone with a question mark: whoever pulls it invents a new character on the spot.

## Stuck?
- Drawing on stone is hard: draw the picture on paper, you cut it out, and glue it on.
- Your child freezes during the story: ask "What happens next?" or "How does the cat feel?"

## Show it off
After dinner, the family pulls stones from the bag and your child leads a family story, with everyone adding a line.

## Clean up
Store stones in the bag. Cap markers and wash brushes.

## For the grown-up
This builds storytelling, sequencing (first, next, last), vocabulary and drawing, the building blocks of reading and writing. Use stones bigger than a golf ball so they are not a choking risk. If you seal them with nail polish or glue, do it yourself, outdoors or by an open window. **Ask at dinner:** "Which stone is in your favorite story, and what happened to it?"
