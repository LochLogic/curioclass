title: Book Domino Chain Reaction
grade: 1
order: 22
kind: Build
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 15 to 30 hardcover or sturdy paperback books of similar size, a long hallway or open floor, a soft ball or toy car, cardboard tubes, paper, a pencil
nice: wooden blocks, a ruler for a ramp, a timer, a bell at the end

## The big idea
Read aloud: "One little push, and the whole line goes CLACK, CLACK, CLACK! Today you build a chain reaction, where one thing knocks over the next and the next. Can you make a line so long that it goes all the way across the room?"

## Goals
- Your child builds a book domino line that falls all the way to the end.
- Your child adds a starting trick and a finish that rings, pops or drops.

## Objectives
- Your child can find the right spacing so each book knocks over the next.
- Your child can test, find where it stopped, and fix it.
- Your child can explain cause and effect: "this pushed that, which pushed that."

## Get ready
Clear a long space of floor, ideally on a hard floor or flat rug. Pile the books nearby. Move pets and siblings away from the building zone for now.

## Steps
1. **Test two books.** Stand two books up on their bottom edges, close together. Push the first. Does it knock the second? Move them farther apart. Still? Find the farthest space that works. (Usually about half a book's height.)
2. **Build a line** (15 minutes). Your child stands books one after another at that spacing. Start with 10.
3. **Test it.** Push the first book. Where did it stop? Your child finds the gap and fixes it.
4. **Make it longer and curvier** (15 minutes). Add more books. Try a gentle curve. Ask: "What happens at the turn?"
5. **The starter.** Your child invents how it begins: a ball rolled down a ruler ramp, a toy car pushed down a book slope, a stuffed animal falling over.
6. **The finish.** Your child picks an ending: the last book knocks a cup off the table into a box, rings a bell, or knocks down a block tower.
7. **The big run.** Count down from 5 and start it. Did it work? Rebuild and try again.

## Make it yours
- Make a split path: the line splits into two and both sides fall at once.
- Add other "dominoes": tall boxes, cereal boxes, standing paper towel tubes.
- Make it a story: the books are a line of sleepy giants who wake each other up.

## Stuck?
- Books slide instead of falling: build on a rug or a towel, not slippery wood.
- The chain stops at the same place: that book is too far or tilted. Line it up again or move it closer.

## Show it off
Rebuild the chain and let a family member give the first push when they get home. Count down together!

## Clean up
Your child stacks books and returns them to the shelf by size: biggest on the bottom.

## For the grown-up
This builds cause and effect, early physics (pushes, falling, energy passing along a line), patience and problem solving. Use sturdy books you don't mind falling, and keep the line away from stairs. **Ask at dinner:** "Where did your chain keep stopping, and how did you fix it?"
