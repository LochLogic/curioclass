title: Giant Paper Maze
grade: 3
order: 24
kind: Puzzle
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 4 sheets of paper taped into one big sheet, a pencil, an eraser, markers, a ruler
nice: a coin or small toy to use as the walker, a timer

## The big idea
A tiny mouse wants to reach the Cheese Castle. But the path is full of traps, dead ends and sneaky cats! Draw a giant maze so hard that your family will get lost in it.

## Goals
- You draw a giant maze with one true path.
- Someone tries your maze and you time them.

## Objectives
- I can plan a path and hide it with dead ends.
- I can test my maze to make sure it works.
- I can tell a story with a map.

## Get ready
Tape 4 sheets together into one big square. Clear a big table or floor spot.

## Steps
1. Mark the START in one corner. Mark the GOAL in the far corner. Draw a picture of each.
2. Draw the true path light in pencil (10 minutes). Make it wiggle and turn. Go all over the paper.
3. Add dead ends. Draw paths that branch off and stop. Add at least 10.
4. Fill the gaps. Draw more walls and paths so the true path is hidden.
5. Add story traps. A cat in a dead end. A sticky spill. A secret tunnel that jumps across.
6. Test it yourself. Use a coin as the walker. Does the true path still work?
7. Go over the walls with marker. Erase your pencil path!
8. Name your maze, like "Escape from Kitty Kingdom."

## Make it yours
- Add a rule: you must pick up 3 keys on the way to unlock the goal.
- Make two mazes on one paper and race a family member.
- Make it a space maze with black holes and alien bases.

## Stuck?
- The maze is too easy: add more dead ends near the start.
- You blocked your own path: erase one wall to open a door.
- Lines get confusing: make walls dark and paths light.

## Show it off
Hand your maze to a grown-up with a timer. Can they beat it in under 2 minutes?

## Clean up
Put the markers away. Fold the maze to keep it, or hang it up.

## For the grown-up
Mazes build planning, spatial thinking and patience, and testing them is real debugging. Try it honestly; kids love to see you get lost. **Ask at dinner:** "Where was the sneakiest dead end in your maze?"
