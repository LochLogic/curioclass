title: Steady-Hand Circuit Game
grade: 11
order: 21
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a flashlight that takes 2 AA or D batteries (or 2 loose AA batteries and the bulb from an old flashlight), aluminum foil, tape, a wire coat hanger or a long piece of stiff wire, cardboard, scissors, paper clips
nice: insulated wire with bare ends, a small buzzer or LED from an old toy, electrical tape

## The big idea
You know the game: guide a loop along a twisty wire without touching it, or the buzzer screams. You are going to
build it from scratch, which means building a real circuit with a switch that you trigger by mistake. Along the way
you will learn why a circuit needs a complete loop and what an open or closed switch really is.

## Goals
- A working game where touching the wire lights the bulb (or sounds a buzzer).
- A circuit diagram on paper using real symbols.

## Objectives
- I can build a complete circuit with a power source, a load and a switch.
- I can draw the circuit with standard symbols.
- I can troubleshoot an open circuit by testing each connection.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Cut a cardboard base about 30 cm by 15 cm. Straighten the coat hanger and bend a 2 cm loop at one end.
Cut the rest into a 40 cm twisty track (or use foil rolled into a stiff rope).

## Steps
1. Test the power. Make a quick circuit: foil strip from the top of a battery stack to the bulb's base tip, bulb's
   metal side back to the bottom battery. It should light. That is a **closed circuit**.
2. Make wires from foil (10 minutes): fold strips 2 cm wide into thin ropes and tape them along the cardboard.
3. Build the track. Poke the ends of the twisty wire into the cardboard and tape one end to a foil wire leading to
   the battery.
4. Make the wand. Bend a loop around the track, then connect the wand's handle end to the bulb with a long foil wire
   so it can move freely.
5. Finish the loop: bulb back to the other end of the batteries. Now touching wand to track closes the circuit and
   lights the bulb.
6. Draw the circuit (10 minutes): battery, wire, switch (wand and track), bulb. Use real symbols.
7. Play. Time each run. Make the track harder with tighter curves if it is too easy.

## Make it yours
- Add a "safe zone" at the start and end made of tape so the wand can rest.
- Build two tracks in parallel and race a friend; whoever lights up first loses.
- Make the bulb a story: the track is a laser corridor, and the light means the alarm.

## Stuck?
- Nothing lights: check each joint. Press foil firmly onto metal and tape it.
- Bulb stays on: the wand is touching the track at rest. Bend the loop wider.

## Show it off
Challenge the family to beat your time after dinner, with a "laser corridor" story.

## Clean up
Take the batteries out so they do not drain, and recycle foil scraps.

## For the grown-up
This is basic electricity from physics class: circuits, switches and troubleshooting. Use only AA, C or D batteries,
never button or coin cells, and never anything from a wall outlet. If foil touches both battery ends directly with no
bulb, it gets warm fast, so stop and disconnect. **Ask at dinner:** "What makes a circuit open or closed?"
