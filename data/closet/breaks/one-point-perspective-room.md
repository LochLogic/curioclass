materials: white paper, a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, a black pen, colored pencils
nice: a larger sheet of paper, a fine black marker

## The big idea
Ever notice how a hallway seems to shrink as it goes away from you? Artists figured out the rule behind that about 600 years ago. It's called one-point perspective, and once you learn it, you can draw rooms that look like you could walk right into them. Today, you'll draw your dream bedroom (or a secret lair) in 3D.

## Goals
- You draw a room in one-point perspective with at least five objects inside.
- Your room looks deep, like you could step into it.

## Objectives
- I can use a vanishing point to make a drawing look 3D.
- I can draw boxes (furniture) that line up with the vanishing point.
- I can use shading to show light and depth.

## Get ready
Lay the paper horizontally. Sharpen your pencil and draw lightly at first; many lines will be erased.

## Steps
1. Draw the back wall: a rectangle in the middle of the page, about 8 cm wide and 6 cm tall.
2. Put a dot in the middle of that rectangle. That's your vanishing point (VP), the spot where everything seems to disappear.
3. Use the ruler to draw lines from the VP through each corner of the rectangle, out to the corners of the page. You now have a floor, ceiling, and two side walls.
4. Add a door or window on a side wall. Draw its vertical edges straight up and down, and its top and bottom edges as lines aimed at the VP.
5. Add furniture (20 minutes). Draw the front face of a bed or desk as a flat rectangle. Then draw lines from its corners back toward the VP. Cut them off with a line parallel to the front to make the back edge. That's a 3D box.
6. Add details that make it yours: a rug (a flat shape on the floor that narrows toward the VP), shelves, posters, a pet bed.
7. Erase extra guide lines. Go over the final lines in pen. Shade the sides of furniture away from the light.

## Make it yours
- Draw a secret lair version: a hidden room behind the bookshelf, a trap door in the floor, a slide instead of stairs.
- Draw a hallway that seems to go on forever, with doors on both sides.
- Put the VP off-center (near the left side) and see how the room changes.

## Stuck?
- Things look tilted: every vertical line should be straight up and down, and every depth line should aim at the VP.
- Furniture looks flat: you forgot to draw the lines back toward the VP from its corners.
- Too many lines: draw guides very lightly so they erase easily.

## Show it off
Show your room drawing and give a "tour" of what's inside. Point out the VP and see if your family can find it first.

## Clean up
Brush eraser crumbs into the trash and put your pencils away.

## For the grown-up
This is a classic art technique that also builds geometry: parallel lines, vanishing points, and spatial reasoning. It's how architects and game designers sketch spaces. **Ask at dinner:** "What's hidden in your dream room that we can't see?"
