## The big idea
Great speeches have changed history, and they all use the same tools. You'll write and deliver a 3-minute persuasive speech that tries to change your family's minds on something: a serious idea, a household rule, or something gloriously ridiculous like "cereal is a soup." Then the audience votes.

## Goals
- A 3-minute persuasive speech written on cue cards.
- A live delivery and a family vote before and after.

## Objectives
- I can use **ethos** (trust), **pathos** (emotion) and **logos** (logic).
- I can use a speech structure that builds to a call to action.
- I can deliver with eye contact, pauses and volume.

## Get ready
Choose your topic and your position. It should be something people might disagree with. Write it as one clear sentence: "Every family should have a no-phone dinner every night."

## Steps
1. **Pre-vote.** Ask your audience where they stand before you start writing, and write the count down.
2. **Brainstorm** (10 minutes). List every reason for your side. Then list the best argument *against* you. You'll need to answer it.
3. **Structure the speech** in five parts:
   - **Attention**: a hook, story or surprising fact.
   - **Need**: the problem.
   - **Solution**: your idea.
   - **Visualization**: show the world if they agree (or if they don't).
   - **Action**: tell them exactly what to do.
4. **Add the three appeals.** Ethos: why should they trust you? Pathos: a story or image that makes them feel. Logos: a fact, number or logical step.
5. **Answer the other side.** "Some people say... but..."
6. **Cue cards** (20 minutes). Write one main point per card, with a few key words, not full sentences.
7. **Practice** out loud 4 times with a clock. Pause after your strongest line. Look at your audience, not your cards.
8. **Deliver** it, then hold the second vote.

## Make it yours
- Hold a debate: someone else argues the other side, and the audience votes.
- Give the speech as a historical or fictional character arguing their cause.
- Make it a ridiculous topic delivered with total seriousness.

## Stuck?
- You can't think of reasons: interview a family member about why they'd agree.
- Nerves: plant your feet, take one slow breath, and pick one friendly face.

## Show it off
Make it a speech night: everyone speaks, and the votes are counted after each one.

## Clean up
Keep your cue cards. You'll use the structure again in school.

## For the grown-up
This builds rhetoric and public speaking skills used in English, debate and civics. Respond to your teen's arguments with real questions. **Ask at dinner:** "What was your best argument, and did it change any votes?"
