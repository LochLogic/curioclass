## The big idea
Before a play opens, a set designer builds a tiny model of the stage. It shows where the walls go, how the lights look, and where the actors stand. Pick a scene from a story you love, or one you invent. Then design its stage in a box.

## Goals
- You build a model stage set for one scene inside a shoebox.
- You light it with a flashlight to set the mood.

## Objectives
- I can plan a set with a backdrop, middle, and front.
- I can use color and light to show a mood.
- I can explain my design choices out loud.

## Get ready
Turn the shoebox on its side so the open part faces you. That is your stage. Pick your scene. Ideas: a haunted kitchen, a castle at sunset, a spaceship cockpit, a forest at night.

## Steps
1. Sketch your plan. Draw the stage from the front. What is in the back, the middle, and the front?
2. Make the backdrop (15 minutes). Draw it on paper sized to the back wall. Glue it in. A sky, a wall of books, a starry space window.
3. Make the side walls or "wings." Tape cardboard flats to each side. Paint or draw trees, curtains, or doors on them.
4. Build two or three set pieces, like a tiny table, a throne, or a rock. Fold paper into boxes and tape them.
5. Add a curtain at the front if you like. Glue a strip of paper or fabric across the top edge.
6. Light it up. Shine the flashlight from one side, then from above. Try tissue paper over the light for color.
7. Place your actors: toy figures or paper cutouts standing on folded paper tabs.

## Make it yours
- Make a set that changes. Slide a new backdrop in for "Act 2."
- Design a set for a play that has never been written. Write the first line the actor says.
- Make the whole set from one color, and use light to make it look different.

## Stuck?
- Pieces won't stand up: fold a small tab at the bottom and glue the tab to the floor.
- The backdrop is crooked: tape it at all four corners.
- The scene feels empty: add something small in the front, like a lamp or a pile of leaves.

## Show it off
Turn off the room lights. Shine the flashlight on your set and act out the first 30 seconds of the scene.

## Clean up
Pick up scraps and put away glue and scissors. Keep your set on a shelf.

## For the grown-up
This builds visual art skills, planning in three dimensions, and storytelling, the same way real theater designers work. Expect glue drying time. If you have fabric scraps or old ribbon, they make great curtains. **Ask at dinner:** "What mood did you want your set to show, and how did you make it?"
