grade: 10
order: 7
kind: Music
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pen, your voice, a table or pot for a beat
nice: any instrument at home, a phone to record for the family, a second singer

## The big idea
A great jingle gets stuck in your head for years after one listen. That's not an accident. It's a **hook**, built with a short melody, a rhyme and a business name placed exactly where the beat lands. Pick a small business near you (the bakery, the laundromat, the bike repair shop) or invent one, and write a 30-second jingle they'd be proud of.

## Goals
- A 30-second jingle with a hook, a verse and a tag line.
- A performance for your family.

## Objectives
- I can write a short, memorable melody using a few notes.
- I can fit words to a beat so the stressed syllables land on strong beats.
- I can write for an audience and a purpose.

## Get ready
Pick the business and write down three things about it: what it sells, what makes it special, and who comes there. Find a spot where you can sing out loud without feeling awkward.

## Steps
1. **Write the message** (5 minutes): one sentence that sums up why people should go there. "The bike shop that fixes it the same day."
2. **Find the hook.** Say the business name over and over, tapping a beat. Where do the strong syllables fall? "Su-ZY'S BAK-er-y" has three strong beats.
3. **Make a melody** for the name using just 3 to 5 notes. Hum many versions. A good hook often jumps up on the name and comes back down.
4. **Write the verse**: 4 lines that rhyme in pairs (AABB), each line about 8 syllables. Put a real detail in: "Fresh at six, the bread's still warm."
5. **The tag**: end with the hook again, plus where to find them. "On Maple Street, by the train."
6. **Structure it.** Hook (5 seconds), verse (15 seconds), hook plus tag (10 seconds). Time it with a clock.
7. Rehearse 5 times, with a beat on the table. Add a harmony or a backing "ooh" if you have a second voice.

## Make it yours
- Write the same jingle in three styles: a slow ballad, a rock anthem and a sea shanty.
- Write a jingle for a business from a fantasy world: a potion shop or a dragon-sitting service.
- Add a sound effect "logo": a bell, a whistle, a pop, at the end.

## Stuck?
- The melody won't come: sing the name the way you'd call a friend across a field. That's already a melody.
- The words don't fit the beat: count syllables, and cut or add little words like "the" and "so."

## Show it off
Perform it at dinner. The next day, ask if it got stuck in anyone's head.

## Clean up
Write the final lyrics and a simple pitch map (up, down, same) in your notebook.

## For the grown-up
Writing a jingle teaches songwriting (melody, rhythm and rhyme) and persuasive writing for an audience. If your teen wants to share it with a real local business, help them decide how. **Ask at dinner:** "Can you sing us the hook?"
