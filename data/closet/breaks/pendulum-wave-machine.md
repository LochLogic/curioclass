title: Pendulum Wave Machine
grade: 12
order: 14
kind: Science
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a broom handle or a long stick, two chairs to rest it on, string or thread, 10 to 12 identical small weights (metal nuts, washers, keys or same-size beads), a ruler or tape measure, scissors, tape
nice: a calculator, a second person to release the pendulums, a dark background

## The big idea
Line up a row of pendulums with carefully chosen lengths, release them together, and something hypnotic happens:
they form waves, snakes and spirals, fall into chaos, and then snap back into a perfect line. It is pure physics: each
pendulum's swing time depends only on its length. You will calculate the lengths, build it, and run the show.

## Goals
- A working pendulum wave with 10 to 12 pendulums.
- A calculation table showing how you chose each length.

## Objectives
- I can use the pendulum formula to calculate string lengths.
- I can measure and build precisely so a pattern works.
- I can explain why the pattern repeats.

## Get ready
Rest the broom handle across two chair backs, about 1 m off the floor. Clear the space below.

## Steps
1. Learn the rule. A pendulum's period (time for one full swing) is T = 2 pi times the square root of (L / g), where
   g is 9.8 m/s squared. Longer string, slower swing.
2. Pick a cycle time: 60 seconds works well. In 60 seconds, the longest pendulum does 50 swings, the next 51, the
   next 52, and so on.
3. Calculate lengths (20 minutes). For each pendulum, T = 60 / N, then L = g x (T / 2 pi) squared. For N = 50, T is
   1.2 seconds and L is about 35.7 cm. Do the same for N = 51 to 61.
4. Cut and tie strings (25 minutes). Measure each length from the stick to the center of the weight. Precision
   matters: within 2 or 3 mm.
5. Tie each string in a V shape (two points on the stick, 5 cm apart) so the pendulums swing in a single plane.
6. Line them up, lift all of them together with a ruler, and release.
7. Watch for 60 seconds. Note when patterns appear and when they all line up again.

## Make it yours
- Try a 30-second cycle and compare the patterns.
- Add a light behind them in a dark room and watch the shadows on the wall.
- Mess up one length on purpose and see how it breaks the pattern.

## Stuck?
- The pattern is messy: check each length again. Small errors grow quickly.
- Pendulums swing in circles: use the V-shaped tie so they stay in one plane.

## Show it off
Turn down the lights and give a two-minute show, explaining the math before you let go.

## Clean up
Coil and label the strings so you can rebuild it, and put the chairs back.

## For the grown-up
This builds physics, measurement and algebra: the pendulum equation from high school physics, put to work. **Ask at
dinner:** "What happens when they all line up again?"
