title: Paper Video Game Design
grade: 5
order: 15
kind: Game
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 4 to 6 sheets of paper, tape, a pencil, markers, a coin or small eraser for the player, a die (or numbered paper slips in a cup)
nice: graph paper, sticky notes for moving enemies

## The big idea
Video game designers start on paper long before any code. They draw levels, plan traps, and test ideas. Today you design a whole game with three levels and a boss fight. Then someone plays it on the floor with a coin and a die.

## Goals
- You design a three-level side-scrolling game on paper.
- Someone plays it from start to finish, and you watch to see what is fun.

## Objectives
- I can design levels that get harder little by little.
- I can write clear rules for how a player moves and wins.
- I can watch someone play and improve my design.

## Get ready
Tape two sheets of paper end to end for each level. Draw a path of about 25 squares across each one, like stepping stones.

## Steps
1. Invent your hero and your world. A snail racing to the ocean? A robot escaping a junkyard? Draw a title screen.
2. Write the rules. Roll the die and move that many squares. Land on a special square and something happens.
3. Design Level 1 (15 minutes). Make it easy. Add a few coins (+1 point), one trap (go back 2), and one power-up (roll again).
4. Design Level 2. Add a new hazard, like a gap you must roll exactly 3 to jump over.
5. Design Level 3: the boss level. Make a boss with 5 hit points. Each time you roll a 6, the boss loses one.
6. Add lives. The player starts with 3. Traps can cost a life.
7. Play-test it (20 minutes). Play it yourself, then fix anything too easy, too hard, or confusing.

## Make it yours
- Add secret warp squares that skip ahead to another level.
- Make it two-player, where you race or team up against the boss.
- Add a shop between levels where coins buy extra lives.

## Stuck?
- Levels go too fast: add more squares or more go-back traps.
- The boss is impossible: let a 5 also count as a hit.
- Out of ideas for hazards: think of weather, animals, or broken bridges.

## Show it off
Hand a family member the coin and die. Explain the rules in under one minute. Then watch them play without helping.

## Clean up
Fold your levels and keep them in a folder with the rules, so you can play again.

## For the grown-up
Game design builds planning, logic, probability (what are the chances of rolling a 6?), and clear rule writing. Watching someone else play teaches revision, which is the same skill as editing writing. Be a play tester when you can; honest, kind feedback is gold. **Ask at dinner:** "What part of your game did players like most? What did you change?"
