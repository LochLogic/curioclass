## The big idea
Lots of families in every town sometimes don't have enough food. Food banks help, but they need donations. You're going to plan a small food drive: set a goal, design a collection box, make flyers, and organize it like a real project manager.

## Goals
- A full plan for a food drive with a goal, a schedule and a drop-off plan.
- A decorated collection box and at least three flyers.

## Objectives
- I can set a goal and break it into steps with dates.
- I can design materials that persuade people to help.
- I can plan for problems before they happen.

## Get ready
Write down the food bank's most-needed list (common items: canned beans, canned fish, canned vegetables, rice, pasta, cereal, soup). Decide with your grown-up who you'll ask: family, neighbors, a building lobby, friends.

## Steps
1. **Set your goal.** A number you can reach: 30, 50 or 100 items. Make a thermometer drawing to track it.
2. **Make a schedule.** When does the drive start and end? When will you collect? When is the drop-off? Write it out with dates.
3. **Design the box (20 minutes).** Cut the flaps off a big box and cover it with paper. Write the food bank's name, your goal, and three needed items in big letters.
4. **Make flyers.** Big headline ("Help Fill the Box!"), what's needed, where the box is, and when it ends. Keep it short. Make at least three.
5. **Plan the ask.** Write a short speech you or your grown-up could say to neighbors: what, why, and how. Practice it.
6. **Plan for problems.** What if people donate things that are expired or open? (Thank them, check dates, and set those aside.) What if you don't reach the goal? (Every item counts.)
7. **Launch.** With your grown-up, put the box where people can reach it, hand out flyers, and update your thermometer.

## Make it yours
- Make it a challenge: two boxes, two teams, see which fills first.
- Add a "most needed" bonus: double points for items on the food bank's top list.
- Collect for a different need: pet food for an animal shelter, or warm socks for a shelter.

## Stuck?
- Not sure who to ask: start with the people you live with. Then ask your grown-up about the neighbors.
- Flyer is too wordy: if it takes more than 10 seconds to read, cut it.
- No food bank nearby: ask a school, library or community center where to donate.

## Show it off
Show your family the box and flyers, then celebrate the day you drop it off.

## Clean up
Put away supplies and keep your plan to use again next season.

## For the grown-up
This builds planning, persuasive design and civic responsibility. Please be the contact with the food bank and any neighbors, and go with your child for any door-to-door asking or drop-offs. **Ask at dinner:** "What's your goal for the food drive, and how will you reach it?"
