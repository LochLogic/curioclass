title: Ant Picnic Experiment
grade: 4
order: 21
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a paper plate, 5 tiny food samples (a crumb of bread, a drop of honey or jam, a bit of salt, a pinch of sugar, a crumb of cheese), a pencil, paper, a clock
nice: a magnifying glass, a toothpick for small amounts

## The big idea
Ants are tiny, but they're super picky eaters. Which food do they love most? Set up a picnic buffet for ants. Then watch, count and become an ant scientist.

## Goals
- You set up an ant buffet with 5 food choices.
- You find out which food ants like best.

## Objectives
- I can make a prediction and test it.
- I can count and record what I see over time.
- I can watch animals without disturbing them.

## Get ready
Find a spot where ants walk, like a sidewalk crack or a garden edge. Ask a grown-up if you may set up there. On a balcony? Put the plate near any crack.

## Steps
1. Draw 5 circles on the plate. Label each with a food name.
2. Put a tiny amount of each food in its circle. A crumb is enough.
3. Predict which food will win. Write it down.
4. Set the plate near the ants. Sit 3 feet away and watch.
5. Count ants on each food every 10 minutes (40 minutes). Make tally marks.
6. Use a magnifying glass to look closely. What do the ants do with the food?
7. Make a bar graph of your results.
8. Write a conclusion. "Ants liked ___ best. I think it's because ___."

## Make it yours
- Test the same foods at a different time of day.
- Try foods in water vs. dry. Which one wins?
- Draw a comic about the ants' picnic party.

## Stuck?
- No ants come: wait 20 more minutes or try a new spot.
- Ants swarm all foods: count the busiest one for 1 minute.
- Too windy: put the plate in a shady corner by a wall.

## Show it off
Show your graph at dinner. Ask the family to guess the winner first.

## Clean up
Pick up the plate and throw away the food. Don't leave food outside. Wash hands.

## For the grown-up
This builds observation, data collection, graphs and drawing conclusions. Stay nearby. Make sure your child knows not to touch the ants, since some can bite or sting, and not to use this near the house door. **Ask at dinner:** "Which food did the ants pick, and were you right?"
