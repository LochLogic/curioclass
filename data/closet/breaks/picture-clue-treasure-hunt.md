title: Picture Clue Treasure Hunt
grade: k
order: 19
kind: Game
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: paper, crayons or markers, tape, a small "treasure" (a snack, a sticker, a toy, a note), a shoebox for a treasure chest, scissors (grown-up only)
nice: an eye patch or scarf for a pirate hat, a cardboard tube spyglass

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Ahoy, pirate! A treasure chest is hidden somewhere in this house. You can't read the clues, but you can read pictures! Follow the picture clues one by one to find the treasure."

## Goals
- Your child follows a trail of 6 picture clues to find a treasure.
- Your child draws picture clues and hides a treasure for you to find.

## Objectives
- Your child can match a drawing to a real place (a picture of a bathtub means look in the bathtub).
- Your child can follow clues in order.
- Your child can draw a clue that someone else can understand.

## Get ready
While your child is busy, draw 6 clues on 6 pieces of paper. Each is a simple picture of a place: the fridge, a bed, the bathtub, a shoe, the couch, a plant. Number them 1 to 6 with dots so your child can count. Hide clue 2 at the place in clue 1, clue 3 at the place in clue 2, and so on. Put the treasure in the shoebox at the last place.

## Steps
1. **Pirate gear.** Your child makes a spyglass from a tube and a pirate hat from folded paper.
2. Hand over clue 1. Ask: "What is this a picture of? Where do we have one?"
3. **The hunt** (15 minutes). Your child runs to each place, finds the next clue, and figures out the picture. Let them count the dots to check the order.
4. Find the treasure and celebrate with a pirate cheer: "Arrr!"
5. **Switch!** Now your child makes clues for you (20 minutes). They pick 4 to 6 hiding places and draw each one. Help only if asked.
6. Your child hides the clues and a treasure (a stuffed animal or a drawing). You count to 60 with your eyes closed.
7. You follow your child's clues while they watch and give hints ("You're getting warmer!").

## Make it yours
- Make it a dinosaur egg hunt, or a lost puppy rescue, instead of pirates.
- Make the clues puzzle pieces: you cut each clue in half, and your child has to put it together first.
- Use clues that are shapes or colors ("find something red and round").

## Stuck?
- The picture is hard to read: add one color clue ("it's white and cold" for the fridge).
- Your child hides clues in the same spot twice: suggest one clue per room.

## Show it off
Your child sets up a treasure hunt for a family member after work and watches them follow it.

## Clean up
Collect all the clues in an envelope. Your child puts the pirate gear in a box for next time.

## For the grown-up
Reading pictures and symbols is a pre-reading skill: it is how kids learn that marks on paper stand for real things. Following clues in order builds sequencing. If you hide treasure food, keep it nut-free and check allergies. Avoid hiding places that are high, hot or near cleaning supplies. **Ask at dinner:** "Which clue was the trickiest?"
