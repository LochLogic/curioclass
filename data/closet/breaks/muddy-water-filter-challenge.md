title: Muddy Water Filter Challenge
grade: 5
order: 11
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: High
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a 2-liter plastic bottle, a clear jar or glass, dirt, water, sand, small gravel or pebbles, cotton balls or an old sock, a coffee filter or paper towel, a cup, a spoon, white paper
nice: activated charcoal from a fish tank kit, a second bottle for a better design, a timer

## The big idea
Imagine your team is stranded on an island. The only water is a muddy puddle. Can you build a filter that turns brown water clear? Engineers solve this problem for real around the world.

## Goals
- You build a layered water filter from things around the house.
- You test it, rate how clear the water is, and improve it.

## Objectives
- I can build a filter with layers that catch big and small bits.
- I can compare results with a clearness score from 1 to 5.
- I can explain why clear water still might not be safe to drink.

## Get ready
Work outside, in a tub, or over a tray. Ask a grown-up to cut the bottle in half for you. Fill a cup with water and stir in 2 spoons of dirt and a few leaf bits.

## Steps
1. Turn the top half of the bottle upside down. Set it in the bottom half like a funnel. Leave the cap off.
2. Plan your layers. Draw them first. What goes on the bottom? What goes on top?
3. Build it. A good start: coffee filter, then cotton, then sand, then gravel on top. Each layer about 3 cm thick.
4. Hold the muddy water against white paper. Give it a score of 1 (totally brown) to 5 (totally clear).
5. Pour slowly. Watch what each layer catches (10 minutes).
6. Score the water that comes out. Write it down.
7. Rebuild with a change: a thicker sand layer, a new order, or a sock layer. Test it with new mud (20 minutes).

## Make it yours
- Filter the water twice. Does a second pass help?
- Time how long each filter takes. Is the clearest filter also the slowest?
- Make "polluted" water with a drop of food coloring or a little cooking oil. Can a filter catch those?

## Stuck?
- Water rushes through too fast: add more sand or pack it down.
- Nothing comes out: your layers are too tight. Loosen the cotton.
- The water stays cloudy: tiny clay bits are hard to catch. Let it sit for an hour, then compare.

## Show it off
Line up three jars: the muddy water, your first try, and your best try. Explain each layer.

## Clean up
Dump the sand and gravel outside or in the trash, never the sink. Rinse the bottle for recycling.

## For the grown-up
This is a classic engineering challenge that also teaches about clean water. The key safety point: filtered water is never safe to drink, because germs pass through. Please do the bottle cutting yourself (a knife makes the first slit easily). Dirt and sand will clog drains. **Ask at dinner:** "Your water looked clear. Why still not drink it?"
