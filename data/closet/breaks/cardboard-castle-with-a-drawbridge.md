title: Cardboard Castle with a Drawbridge
grade: 1
order: 1
kind: Build
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a medium cardboard box (a shipping box), 4 paper towel or toilet paper tubes, a box cutter or sharp scissors (grown-up only), safety scissors, tape, string or yarn, a pencil, markers or crayons, paper
nice: a brass paper fastener, aluminum foil, toy figures or small stuffed animals as knights

## The big idea
Read aloud: "A dragon has been spotted near the kingdom! The royal family needs a castle with tall towers and a drawbridge that goes up and down. Only a master builder can make it. That's you!"

## Goals
- Your child builds a castle with towers and a working drawbridge that raises and lowers with a string.
- Your child defends the castle in a story with toy knights.

## Objectives
- Your child can plan a building by drawing it first.
- Your child can make a moving part work by testing and fixing it.
- Your child can describe where things are using words like above, below, beside and inside.

## Get ready
Tape the box closed and set it on its side or bottom, whichever looks more like a castle. Clear a table or floor space. Set out the tubes, tape and markers. Keep the box cutter out of reach when you are not using it.

## Steps
1. **Draw a plan** (5 minutes). Your child sketches the castle on paper: where the towers go, where the door is, how many windows. This is the builder's blueprint.
2. **Cut the drawbridge.** On the front of the box, your child draws a door about 4 inches wide and 6 inches tall. You cut the top and both sides only, so it folds down at the bottom.
3. **Make it work** (10 minutes). Poke a small hole near the top of the door and another in the wall above the doorway (grown-up). Your child threads string through both and ties a knot on the door. Pull the string: the bridge goes up! Let go: it comes down.
4. Test it 10 times. Does it stick? Your child figures out the fix (a longer string, a bigger hole, folding the bottom crease back and forth).
5. **Towers** (15 minutes). Tape a tube to each corner. Your child cuts small notches in the top with safety scissors to make battlements (the bumpy tops).
6. **Decorate** (20 minutes). Your child draws stones, windows, flags and a coat of arms. What animal or symbol is on the royal flag?
7. **The dragon attack.** Your child tells a story with toy figures: the dragon comes, the bridge goes up just in time, and then what happens?

## Make it yours
- Add a moat made of a blue towel, and a paper boat to cross it.
- Build a secret tunnel out the back from a tube and a small box.
- Make the castle belong to someone surprising: a cat queen, a robot knight, a friendly dragon.

## Stuck?
- The drawbridge falls off: tape the hinge line on the inside to make it stronger.
- Towers keep falling: tape them on the inside of the box corner too, or stand them on a tape "foot."

## Show it off
When the family gets home, your child raises the drawbridge and tells the story of the dragon attack.

## Clean up
Your child picks up scraps and puts the castle on a shelf to keep playing with.

## For the grown-up
This builds early engineering (a moving hinge and a pulley-like string), planning and spatial language. It is also great storytelling play. Do all cutting with the box cutter or sharp scissors yourself; your child can use safety scissors for notches. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you make the drawbridge go up?"
