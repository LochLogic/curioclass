title: Dream Treehouse Blueprint
grade: 3
order: 19
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 2 big sheets of paper (or 4 taped together), a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, colored pencils or markers
nice: graph paper, a blue marker for a "blueprint" look

## The big idea
If you could have any treehouse, what would it have? A slide? A secret door? A snack elevator? Be the architect. Draw the plans for your dream treehouse.

## Goals
- You draw a treehouse plan with at least 5 special features.
- You label every part so a builder could understand it.

## Objectives
- I can draw a plan with labels.
- I can use a ruler to draw straight lines.
- I can explain why I put each part where I did.

## Get ready
Tape your papers together on a table. Sharpen your pencil.

## Steps
1. Dream list (10 minutes). Write 10 things you want. Rope ladder? Telescope? Hammock? Zip line?
2. Pick your tree. Big oak? Giant palm? A tree in a jungle? Draw the trunk and big branches light in pencil.
3. Draw the main room on the strongest branches. Use the ruler.
4. Add levels or rooms. Where does the ladder go? Where is the lookout?
5. Add your 5 best features. Draw them clearly.
6. Label everything with arrows. "Snack elevator (pulls up with rope)."
7. Draw a small "inside" view. Show what one room looks like inside.
8. Go over the lines with marker. Color it in.

## Make it yours
- Design it for a different creature: a dragon, a robot, a family of mice.
- Add a secret room only you know about. Hide it in the drawing.
- Write rules for the treehouse, like "No grown-ups without the password."

## Stuck?
- Drawing looks messy: start again light in pencil. Press hard only at the end.
- Not enough room: add a second tree with a bridge.
- Can't draw a feature: draw a box and label it.

## Show it off
Give a "builder's tour." Point to each part and explain what it does.

## Clean up
Put pens away. Roll up your blueprint and put a band around it.

## For the grown-up
This builds planning, labeling, measuring and spatial thinking, the basics of design and architecture. Hang it on a wall; kids love to see their plans taken seriously. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the most important room in your treehouse?"
