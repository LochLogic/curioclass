## The big idea
You do not have to be a runner to start running. Most beginners quit because they go too fast and too far. You will
learn the walk-run method and build a four-week plan, then head out for your first mission run: a loop with a
secret goal you set yourself, like spotting five red doors or running to the far side of the park.

## Goals
- One completed walk-run session of about 25 minutes.
- A four-week plan, written out and stuck on your wall.

## Objectives
- I can use run-walk intervals to build fitness safely.
- I can find an easy pace using the talk test.
- I can plan a gradual increase over four weeks.

## Get ready
Tell a grown-up your route and when you will be back. Pick a safe loop with sidewalks or a track. Drink water.

## Steps
1. Warm up with 5 minutes of brisk walking and a few leg swings.
2. Run-walk (20 minutes): run 1 minute, walk 2 minutes, repeat 6 or 7 times.
3. Use the **talk test**: you should be able to speak in short sentences while running. If not, slow down.
4. Pick a mission during the run: count dogs, find a mural, or reach a landmark.
5. Cool down with 5 minutes of walking, then stretch calves, quads and hamstrings.
6. Write your plan (15 minutes). Week 1: three sessions of run 1, walk 2. Week 2: run 2, walk 2. Week 3: run 3, walk
   1. Week 4: run 5, walk 1. Rest a day between sessions.
7. Write a "why" at the top of the plan, in one sentence.

## Make it yours
- Plan a story run: each session you are a courier carrying an urgent message across a fantasy city.
- Make a playlist of songs at running tempo (about 160 to 180 beats per minute) for later.
- Plan a fun finish line in week four, with family cheering at the end.

## Stuck?
- Your shins hurt: slow down, land softly and shorten your stride. Take an extra rest day.
- You are bored: change the route or invite a friend.

## Show it off
Post your plan on the fridge and tick off each run. Invite the family to the week-four finish.

## Clean up
Stretch, drink water and put your shoes by the door for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds fitness, pacing and planning. Agree on the route and check-in time. Running in daylight and in visible
clothes is safest. **Ask at dinner:** "How did the run feel by the end?"
