title: Straw Rockets
grade: 5
order: 7
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper, tape, scissors, a pencil, drinking straws, a ruler or tape measure, a few books, a notebook page for results
nice: a wider straw or a paper tube to roll rockets on, a protractor, sticky notes for target markers

## The big idea
Real rocket scientists test, change, and test again. You can do the same thing with a straw and a scrap of paper. Blow into the straw and your paper rocket shoots across the room. Your mission: build the rocket that flies the farthest.

## Goals
- You build three or more paper rockets with different designs.
- You find the best launch angle and the best rocket shape.

## Objectives
- I can test one change at a time, like fin size or angle.
- I can measure and record each flight.
- I can explain why my best rocket flew farthest.

## Get ready
Find a long hallway or an open space outside. Make sure no one will walk into the flight path. Set a start line with tape.

## Steps
1. Cut a strip of paper about 12 cm by 8 cm. Wrap it around a pencil to make a tube. Tape the seam.
2. Slide it off. Fold and tape one end shut. That is the nose cone.
3. Cut out three or four small triangle fins. Tape them to the open end.
4. Slide the rocket onto your straw. Aim away from people. Blow hard and short!
5. Measure the distance. Write it in a chart with three columns: Rocket, Change, Distance.
6. Test angles (15 minutes). Stack books to hold the straw low, middle, and high. Which angle flies farthest?
7. Build two more rockets. Change only one thing on each: longer body, bigger fins, or no fins. Test each three times.

## Make it yours
- Add a "payload" (a small paper clip) and see how much weight your rocket can carry.
- Set a landing target 3 meters away and score points for hits.
- Design a two-stage rocket: a short tube inside a longer one.

## Stuck?
- The rocket won't slide on the straw: unroll it and roll it a little looser.
- Air leaks out the nose: add one more piece of tape across the top.
- Flights are all over the place: blow the same way every time, one short puff.

## Show it off
Hold a launch with a countdown. Show your chart and announce your champion rocket.

## Clean up
Collect every rocket, even crash landings. Throw away scraps and put the straws back.

## For the grown-up
This is the engineering design process: build, test, measure, improve. Your child also practices fair tests and simple data tables. Remind them never to aim at faces, and give each person their own straw. **Ask at dinner:** "What change helped your rocket fly farthest, and what did nothing at all?"
