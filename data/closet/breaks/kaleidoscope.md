materials: a paper towel tube, a cereal box, aluminum foil, plastic wrap, a small bag or wrap scraps, tiny colorful things (beads, sequins, paper bits, colored plastic scraps), tape, glue stick, scissors, a ruler, a pencil
nice: wax paper for a frosted end, reflective sticker paper, markers to decorate the tube

## The big idea
Look into a kaleidoscope and a few beads turn into a giant spinning star. The secret is mirrors. Light bounces between them again and again. You can build one with a tube and some foil, then discover a whole universe inside.

## Goals
- You build a working kaleidoscope with three mirror walls.
- You test which bits make the best patterns.

## Objectives
- I can explain that light reflects (bounces) off shiny surfaces.
- I can build a triangle-shaped mirror inside a tube.
- I can test and change my design to make clearer patterns.

## Get ready
Clear the table. Cut a flat panel from the cereal box. Smooth the foil out with your hand. Smoother foil makes sharper mirrors.

## Steps
1. Cut three strips of cardboard. Each one should be as long as the tube and about 3 cm wide. Measure the tube first.
2. Glue foil to one side of each strip, shiny side out. Smooth it with a ruler edge so there are no wrinkles.
3. Tape the strips together, foil on the inside, to make a long triangle. It should slide snugly into the tube.
4. Cover one end of the tube with plastic wrap. Tape it tight. That is the window.
5. Put a pinch of beads and bits on the window. Cover them with a second layer of plastic wrap, leaving a little room so they can tumble.
6. Cover the other end with cardboard. Poke a small eye hole in the center with a pencil.
7. Point it at a window or lamp, never the sun. Turn the tube slowly and watch (15 minutes of testing different bits).

## Make it yours
- Skip the beads. Look through the open end at real things, like a plant or your hand. That is a teleidoscope.
- Make a square mirror tube with four strips and compare the patterns.
- Draw the best pattern you see, as close as you can.

## Stuck?
- The pattern is blurry: the foil is wrinkled. Remake one strip with smoother foil.
- It is too dark: point it toward a bright window or lamp.
- The bits don't move: loosen the outer plastic wrap so there is more room.

## Show it off
Pass it around. Ask each person to describe the shape they see in just three words.

## Clean up
Sweep up loose beads and foil scraps. Put scissors and glue back.

## For the grown-up
This builds light science (reflection and symmetry) and careful measuring. Remind your child never to look at the sun through it. If little ones are in the house, keep beads and sequins up high; they are a choking risk. **Ask at dinner:** "How do three foil walls make so many copies of one bead?"
