title: Life Skills Olympics
grade: 12
order: 16
kind: Game
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 2+
materials: a timer, paper and pen for scores, a fitted sheet, a needle and thread with a loose button, a shirt to fold, a pair of shoes with laces, a can opener and a can, an envelope and paper, at least one competitor (sibling, friend or grown-up)
nice: medals made from cardboard, a "judges' table," a referee's whistle

## The big idea
Can you fold a fitted sheet? Sew a button in under three minutes? Write a polite email to a landlord? You are
hosting the Life Skills Olympics: timed, judged events where the real prize is not needing to call home next year.
Design the events, set the rules, and compete.

## Goals
- Six or more designed events with clear rules and scoring.
- A full competition, with medals and a final ranking.

## Objectives
- I can perform practical life skills under time pressure.
- I can design fair rules and scoring for a competition.
- I can coach someone through a skill they find hard.

## Get ready
Set up event stations around a room. Write each event, its rules and its scoring on a card.

## Steps
1. Design events (20 minutes). Ideas: **Fitted Sheet Fold** (neat rectangle, judged), **Button Sprint** (sew on a
   button, timed, must survive a tug), **Laundry Sort** (whites, darks, delicates), **Can Opener Relay**,
   **Envelope Address** (correct format, judged), **Grocery Math** (unit price of two items, fastest correct wins).
2. Write rules: time limits, how judging works, and a penalty for unsafe technique.
3. Practice each event once, alone.
4. Compete (40 minutes). Rotate stations. Score each event: 3 points for first, 2 for second, 1 for third.
5. Add a mystery event drawn from a hat at the end: "Iron a shirt," "Make a bed with hospital corners."
6. Award medals and give a coaching tip to whoever came last in each event.

## Make it yours
- Add a "cooking final": make toast and eggs, judged on timing and taste.
- Run a family tournament across the week with one event per day.
- Invent a joke event, like "Explain a Bill to a Grandparent."

## Stuck?
- A skill is too hard: demonstrate it first and give a 5-minute practice round.
- Scores are close: hold a tiebreaker with a sudden-death button sprint.

## Show it off
Hold a medal ceremony at dinner and post the scoreboard.

## Clean up
Fold everything, return tools to their homes, and put needles away safely.

## For the grown-up
This builds practical independence: sewing, laundry, cooking and money skills, with a lot of laughs. Use a manual can
opener and watch for sharp lids. **Ask at dinner:** "Which event would you be worst at?"
