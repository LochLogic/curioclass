title: Tiny Twig House
grade: 2
order: 6
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: sticks and twigs, leaves, small stones, bark, moss or grass, string or yarn, a paper bag or basket for collecting, a sheet of cardboard about the size of a placemat
nice: school glue, scissors, a small dish of water for a "pond", a magnifying glass

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Somewhere outside lives a family of tiny forest people, smaller than your thumb. Tonight a storm is coming, and they need a house. Only you can build it, using what you find on the ground."

## Goals
- A tiny house built only from nature, standing up on its own.
- A name and a story for the tiny family that lives there.

## Objectives
- Your child can collect and sort natural materials by size and use.
- Your child can solve building problems, like making walls stand or a roof stay on.
- Your child can describe the house using words like roof, wall, door and floor.

## Get ready
Head to a yard, a park, or a patch of trees with your child. Take only things already on the ground: no pulling live plants. If you have no outdoor space, collect on a walk, then build on the cardboard indoors or on a balcony.

## Steps
1. **Collect** (15 minutes). Your child gathers: 10 sticks about as long as a pencil, a handful of short twigs, big flat leaves, bark pieces, and a few pebbles. Ask them to sort the pile into "walls," "roof" and "fancy stuff."
2. **Pick a spot.** At the base of a tree, by a rock, or on the cardboard. Ask: "Where would a tiny person feel safe?"
3. **Build the walls** (15 minutes). Two easy ways: push sticks into the dirt in a square like a fence, or lean sticks against each other in a tent shape. Tie corners with string if they wobble.
4. **Add the roof.** Lay big leaves or bark across the top. Ask: "Will rain get in? How could you fix it?"
5. **Furnish it** (15 minutes). Your child decides what the family needs: a pebble path, a moss bed, an acorn-cap bowl, a bark table. Let them invent.
6. **Rain test.** Sprinkle a few drops of water over the roof. Is anyone inside getting wet? Your child fixes leaks.
7. **Name the family.** Your child writes their names on a leaf-shaped piece of paper or tells you their story while you write it down.

## Make it yours
- Build a whole village with a bridge, a well and a garden.
- Make a fairy door from flat bark and a pebble doorknob.
- Leave a tiny "welcome" note in the house and check tomorrow to see if the weather changed anything.

## Stuck?
- The walls keep falling: build in soft dirt, or press sticks into a ball of mud or play dough.
- The ground is too hard: build on the cardboard and glue pieces in place.

## Show it off
Give a guided tour to someone at home, using a stick as a pointer. If it's in a park, take a photo to show later.

## Clean up
Wash hands well. Leave the house to return to nature, or bring the cardboard version inside.

## For the grown-up
This builds fine motor skills, problem solving, early engineering and care for nature. Check the area for broken glass, sharp things and animal droppings first, and teach your child not to touch mushrooms, berries or anything they don't know. Wash hands after. **Ask at dinner:** "Who lives in your twig house, and what do they do all day?"
