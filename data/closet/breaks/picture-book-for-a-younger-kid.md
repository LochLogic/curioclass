title: Picture Book for a Younger Kid
grade: 11
order: 22
kind: Kindness
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 4 or 5 sheets of paper, a stapler or needle and thread, pencils, colored pencils or markers, scissors
nice: watercolors, collage paper, a hole punch and ribbon

## The big idea
Picture books look simple, but every word and picture is chosen with care. You are going to make one for a specific
younger kid you know: a sibling, cousin, neighbor or family friend. Put them in it, or put in the thing they love most,
and make a book they will want read again and again.

## Goals
- A finished 12- to 16-page picture book with words and pictures.
- A real child who gets to keep it.

## Objectives
- I can write for a young reader with rhythm, repetition and a clear arc.
- I can plan how pictures and words work together on each spread.
- I can design for a specific reader's interests and reading level.

## Get ready
Fold four sheets in half, nest them, and staple the spine for a 16-page book. Write the reader's name, age and three
things they love at the top of a scrap page.

## Steps
1. Plan the story (15 minutes). Kids love a **pattern** (three tries, a repeated line) and a **turn** (something
   goes wrong). Example: a dinosaur who wants to join every game, keeps getting turned away, then saves the day.
2. Make a **thumbnail plan**: tiny sketches of every spread on one page, with the text for each.
3. Write the text. Keep it to one to three sentences per page. Read it aloud for rhythm.
4. Draw the pictures (45 minutes). Let pictures show what the words do not say. If the text says "She was brave,"
   the picture can show her knees shaking.
5. Make the **page turn** count: put a question or a surprise on the right page so the reader has to turn.
6. Add a cover, a title and a dedication page.

## Make it yours
- Make it a lift-the-flap book with hidden surprises taped behind small flaps.
- Write it in rhyme, with one silly made-up word the kid can shout.
- Make the reader the hero, with their name and their pet.

## Stuck?
- The story is too long: cut it down to one problem and one solution.
- You think you cannot draw: use simple shapes, bold colors and collage. Kids love big, clear pictures.

## Show it off
Read it aloud to the kid, slowly, showing every page, and let them turn the pages.

## Clean up
Put art supplies back and wipe the table.

## For the grown-up
This builds writing, art and storytelling structure, and it is a real act of care for a younger child. **Ask at
dinner:** "What part of the book do you think they will love most?"
