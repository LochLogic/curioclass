title: Three-Course Meal from the Pantry
grade: 9
order: 3
kind: Kitchen
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: whatever is in your kitchen (with a grown-up's OK on what you can use), pots, a pan, a cutting board, a knife, measuring cups, plates, paper and a pen for the menu
nice: fresh herbs, a lemon, a squeeze bottle for sauces

## The big idea
You're the head chef, and the delivery truck never came. The challenge: a starter, a main and a dessert using only what's already in the house, including three "wildcard" ingredients somebody else picks for you. Great cooks aren't the ones with perfect groceries. They're the ones who can see a meal in a half-empty cupboard.

## Goals
- A three-course meal for at least two people, plated with care.
- A handwritten menu card that names each dish.

## Objectives
- I can plan a menu that balances flavors, textures and timing.
- I can use basic knife and stove skills safely on my own.
- I can work backward from serving time so everything is ready.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up which foods are off limits and check for allergies. Take inventory: write down every protein, grain, vegetable, fruit, sauce and spice. Ask someone to choose three wildcards you must use (say, canned chickpeas, an apple, peanut-free crackers).

## Steps
1. Sketch three dish ideas per course, then pick one each. Aim for contrast: if the main is rich, make the starter fresh and crunchy (10 minutes).
2. Write a **back-timed schedule**. If dinner is at 6:00 and pasta takes 12 minutes, water goes on at 5:35. Do dessert first if it needs to chill.
3. **Mise en place**: wash, chop and measure everything into bowls before you turn on the heat. Use the claw grip, fingertips tucked.
4. Cook the main (30 to 40 minutes). Taste as you go. Most dishes want salt, acid (lemon or vinegar) or heat, in that order of likely fixes.
5. Make the starter: a soup, a salad, or toast with a topping. Keep it small, three or four bites.
6. Plate like a restaurant: odd numbers of items, wipe the plate rim, add one bright color on top.
7. Write the menu card in your best handwriting, with a name for each dish ("Crispy Chickpea Crunch Salad").

## Make it yours
- Give the meal a theme: a dinner from a made-up country, or a meal for a starship crew.
- Cook a "one-ingredient three ways" menu, like potato soup, potato hash and a sweet potato pudding.
- Serve it blind: guests guess the wildcards from the taste.

## Stuck?
- Something is bland: add salt a pinch at a time, then a squeeze of lemon.
- Everything is ready at different times: keep finished food warm in a low oven or covered pot.

## Show it off
Serve the courses one at a time, and introduce each one like a server would.

## Clean up
Wash as you go, so the sink is half done before you eat. Wipe the stove when it's cool.

## For the grown-up
This builds real independence: planning, timing, knife and stove safety, and food chemistry. Give clear permission for the stove, oven and knives, and name any foods that are off limits or set aside for other meals. Check allergies before the wildcards are chosen. **Ask at dinner:** "Which wildcard was hardest to use, and what did you do with it?"
