## The big idea
Draw a monster's head. Fold it over. Someone else draws the body without looking. Then the legs. Unfold it and laugh! This is a drawing game that makes the strangest creatures ever.

## Goals
- You make at least 6 mix-up monsters.
- You name each one and write its story.

## Objectives
- I can draw parts of a creature that connect to other parts.
- I can follow the rules of a drawing game.
- I can write a short description of a made-up creature.

## Get ready
Fold each sheet into 3 parts, like a letter. Each part is one section: head, body, legs.

## Steps
1. Draw a head in the top section. Make it wild: 3 eyes, horns, a beak.
2. Draw the neck lines just past the fold, so the next person knows where to start.
3. Fold the top back so it's hidden.
4. Pass it to a partner. They draw a body. Playing alone? Draw 6 heads first, mix up the pages, then draw bodies.
5. Fold again and pass. Draw the legs.
6. Unfold! Look at your monster.
7. Name it. Write 3 facts: what it eats, where it lives, its superpower.

## Make it yours
- Draw people instead of monsters, like mixed-up superheroes.
- Make a "monster trading card" for each one.
- Add a fourth part: a tail or a hat.

## Stuck?
- Neck lines don't match: draw them bigger so the next person sees.
- Playing alone feels like cheating: close your eyes or set a timer.
- The monster is boring: add a weird thing like wheels or a hat.

## Show it off
Line up the monsters. Hold a "monster show" and let the family vote for the best.

## Clean up
Collect all drawings in a folder. Put pencils away.

## For the grown-up
This drawing game builds creativity, flexible thinking and descriptive writing. It's great with a sibling or any grown-up. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the funniest monster you made?"
