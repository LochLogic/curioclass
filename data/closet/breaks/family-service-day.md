title: Family Service Day
grade: 10
order: 10
kind: Kindness
time: 90-120 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pen, whatever the projects need (cleaning supplies, garden gloves, trash bags, paper and markers for cards, a bag for donations)
nice: a clipboard, a "service day" sign, snacks for volunteers

## The big idea
You're the volunteer coordinator. Your job is to plan and run a block of service with whoever is home (or solo) that leaves something better: a cleaner park corner, a neighbor's yard raked, a box of donations sorted, a stack of cards for a care home. Good service isn't random. It's planned like an event.

## Goals
- A service plan with 2 or 3 projects, a schedule and jobs for each person.
- At least one project completed, with a before-and-after.

## Objectives
- I can find a real need and plan a project to meet it.
- I can organize people into roles and keep to a schedule.
- I can reflect on the impact of our work.

## Get ready
Talk with a grown-up about what's possible today and who can help. List needs you know about: a neighbor who can't rake leaves, litter at the bus stop, a closet of outgrown clothes, a nursing home that collects cards.

## Steps
1. **Pick the projects** (10 minutes). Choose 2 or 3 that you can do in about an hour, with what you have. Mix outdoor and indoor if you can.
2. **Plan roles.** If people are helping, give each one a job: "Sam bags leaves, I rake, Mom carries." If you're solo, plan the order of your tasks.
3. **Make a schedule** in 15 or 20 minute blocks.
4. Get the supplies ready and make a short safety rule list: gloves for litter, no touching sharp objects, stay together.
5. **Do the work** (45 to 60 minutes). Keep the schedule loosely. Check in halfway.
6. Take a "before" and "after" snapshot (a sketch or a photo for the family).
7. **Debrief.** Gather everyone with a snack and ask: What was the hardest? What would we do next time?

## Make it yours
- Make it a monthly tradition with a different focus each time.
- Write thank-you notes to the people you served, or ask them what they'd like help with next time.
- Run a "service challenge": count bags of litter, cards written or items sorted, and set a goal.

## Stuck?
- Nobody's free to help: solo projects still count, like writing cards or sorting a donation box.
- The project's too big: shrink it. One side of the street, one closet, ten cards.

## Show it off
Share your before and after at dinner, and thank your volunteers by name.

## Clean up
Return supplies, wash gloves and tools, and take donations to the drop-off (with a grown-up).

## For the grown-up
This builds leadership, planning and civic responsibility. Please approve the projects and any locations outside the home, and handle contact with organizations. Gloves for any litter pickup, and no touching glass or needles. **Ask at dinner:** "What difference did today make, and for whom?"
