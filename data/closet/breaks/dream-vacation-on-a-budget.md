title: Dream Vacation on a Budget
grade: 5
order: 8
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, a pencil, colored pencils or markers, a calculator if you want one, the prices list on this sheet
nice: an atlas, globe or paper map, old travel magazines or brochures

## The big idea
Pack your bags! You have won a pretend prize: a 5-day trip anywhere in the world for your whole family. The catch? You only get 5,000 pretend dollars. Plan the trip, keep the budget, and write the diary of your adventure.

## Goals
- You plan a 5-day trip with a full budget that does not go over 5,000 dollars.
- You write a travel diary of the trip as if it really happened.

## Objectives
- I can add up costs and keep a running total.
- I can make choices when I can't afford everything.
- I can write a diary entry with real details about a place.

## Get ready
Clear a spot at the table. Fold two sheets of paper in half to make a little booklet. Use these made-up prices:
- Plane ticket: 400 per person. Train ticket: 150 per person.
- Hotel: 200 a night. Tent at a campsite: 40 a night.
- Meals: 50 per person per day. Big activity (a boat ride, a tour, a theme park): 100 per person.

## Steps
1. Pick your place. It could be a beach, a big city, a mountain, or somewhere you have always wondered about.
2. List who is coming. Count the people. That number matters for every cost.
3. Make your budget chart (15 minutes). Columns: Item, Cost, Running Total. Start with travel, then sleeping, then food.
4. Add activities until you get close to 5,000. Over budget? Cut something or switch the hotel for a tent.
5. Plan each day in your booklet. One page per day. Write where you go and what you do.
6. Write the diary (20 minutes). Pretend the trip happened. What did you eat? What went wrong? What was the best moment?
7. Draw a postcard on the back page with a short note home.

## Make it yours
- Add a surprise: a lost suitcase costs 150 dollars to replace. How do you change your plan?
- Plan two trips, one fancy and one cheap, and compare.
- Write the diary from your pet's point of view.

## Stuck?
- Way over budget: count people again, then try camping or a closer place.
- You don't know much about your place: write about what you imagine. Use clues from a map or book if you have one.
- Math gets messy: total each day first, then add the days.

## Show it off
Present your trip like a travel agent. Show the budget, read one diary page, and hand out your postcard.

## Clean up
Put away markers and keep your booklet in a safe spot. It's a great keepsake.

## For the grown-up
This mixes real math (adding, subtracting, money decisions) with geography and creative writing. Prices are made up, so no real money talk is needed. If your child picks a place with family meaning, this is a nice chance to share a story. **Ask at dinner:** "What did you have to cut from the trip, and was it hard to give up?"
