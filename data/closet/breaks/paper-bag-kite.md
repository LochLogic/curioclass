title: Paper Bag Kite
grade: 1
order: 27
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a paper lunch bag or a small paper grocery bag, crayons or markers, tape, string or yarn (about 6 feet), scissors (grown-up only), streamers made from crepe paper, tissue paper or strips of a plastic bag, a hole punch or pencil
nice: stickers, a bigger open space like a park or field

## The big idea
Read aloud: "The wind is an invisible friend who loves to play. It can push and pull and lift things into the sky. Let's build a kite out of a paper bag and run with the wind to see how high it flies!"

## Goals
- Your child builds a paper bag kite with streamers.
- Your child flies it and finds out what helps it fly best.

## Objectives
- Your child can describe the wind as calm, breezy or strong.
- Your child can test the kite and change one thing to make it fly better.
- Your child can explain that moving air fills the bag and lifts it.

## Get ready
Open the bag. Reinforce the open edge by folding it over once and taping it. Punch or poke a hole near the open edge on each of two opposite sides (grown-up). Cut streamers about 2 feet long.

## Steps
1. **Wind check.** Go outside or to a window. Ask: "Is the wind blowing today? How can you tell?" (Moving leaves, flags, hair.)
2. **Decorate** (15 minutes). Your child decorates the bag: a dragon face, a rocket, a fish, rainbows, their name.
3. **Add streamers.** Your child tapes 4 to 6 streamers to the bottom of the bag (the closed end). These are the tail.
4. **Add the string.** Tie a string to each hole, then tie the two strings together in the middle to make a "V." Tie the long flying string to the middle knot (grown-up).
5. **Fly it** (20 minutes). Your child holds the string and runs with the open end facing the wind. The bag fills with air and flies up behind them!
6. **Test changes.** Make the tail longer. Try running faster or slower. Try flying from a higher spot (a step or a small hill). Which works best?
7. **Wind dance.** Hold the kite still and let the wind move it. Your child copies the kite's movements with their body.

## Make it yours
- Make a kite creature, like a jellyfish with long ribbon tentacles.
- Have a kite parade with stuffed animals "flying" kites too.
- Make a wind sock from a second bag (cut off the bottom) and hang it to watch the wind all day.

## Stuck?
- No wind: running makes your own wind. Try a big open space.
- No yard or park: fly it on a balcony with the string tied to the railing, or run down a hallway with a fan blowing.

## Show it off
Take the family to fly the kite together after dinner or on the weekend.

## Clean up
Wind the string around a piece of cardboard so it doesn't tangle. Hang the kite on a hook.

## For the grown-up
This builds weather science (wind and air pushing things), testing and changing designs, and lots of running. Fly only in open spaces away from roads, trees and power lines, and never during storms. Stay with your child the whole time outdoors. **Ask at dinner:** "What made your kite fly the best?"
