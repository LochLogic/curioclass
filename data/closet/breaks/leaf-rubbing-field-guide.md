title: Leaf Rubbing Field Guide
grade: 4
order: 15
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1
materials: 6 or more leaves, paper, crayons with the paper peeled off, a pencil, a stapler or string, a bag for collecting
nice: a tree book from the library, a magnifying glass, a ruler

## The big idea
Every tree has its own leaf shape, like a fingerprint. Collect leaves and make rubbings. Then build your own field guide, a book that helps people name things in nature.

## Goals
- You make rubbings of at least 6 different leaves.
- You put them into a field guide with names and facts.

## Objectives
- I can tell leaves apart by shape, edge and veins.
- I can describe what I see with science words.
- I can sort things into groups.

## Get ready
Go outside with a grown-up nearby. Bring a bag. Pick up fallen leaves first. Ask before picking from a plant.

## Steps
1. Collect 6 to 10 leaves (20 minutes). Look for different shapes.
2. Sort them. Smooth edges or jagged edges? One leaf or many on a stem?
3. Put a leaf under a sheet of paper, bumpy side up.
4. Rub a peeled crayon sideways over the paper. The shape appears!
5. Label each rubbing. Name it if you know the tree, or make up a name, like "Spiky Star Leaf."
6. Write 3 facts under each rubbing: where you found it, its shape, and its size.
7. Make a cover. Staple the pages or tie them with string.

## Make it yours
- Add bark rubbings by holding paper against a tree trunk.
- Make a field guide to imaginary trees from another planet.
- Use 2 crayon colors on one leaf to make a sunset leaf.

## Stuck?
- The rubbing looks blurry: hold the paper still with one hand.
- No leaves outside: use houseplant leaves or herbs from the kitchen.
- Leaves are too dry and crumble: lay them flat in a book for a few minutes.

## Show it off
Give the family a "nature quiz." Show a rubbing. Can they match it to the real leaf?

## Clean up
Put leaves back outside or in compost. Put crayons in a box.

## For the grown-up
This builds observation, sorting, science vocabulary and nonfiction writing. Stay nearby outdoors, and make sure your child knows not to touch plants they don't know (like poison ivy). **Ask at dinner:** "What makes two leaves different?"
