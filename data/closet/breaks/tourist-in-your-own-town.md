title: Tourist in Your Own Town
grade: 12
order: 30
kind: Outside
time: 90-120 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a notebook, a pen, comfortable shoes, water, a snack, a little cash for the bus if needed
nice: a paper map, a friend, a camera or phone for photos

## The big idea
Tourists see the places locals stop noticing: the old church, the weird museum, the best viewpoint, the shop that has
been there for 80 years. Today you are a visitor from far away. Explore your own town as if you had never seen it,
and make a travel guide page that would make someone want to visit.

## Goals
- A visit to at least three places you have never really looked at closely.
- A handmade travel guide page with sketches, tips and a mini map.

## Objectives
- I can plan a safe route and outing on my own.
- I can observe places with fresh eyes and record details.
- I can write and design an inviting travel guide.

## Get ready
Tell a grown-up your route, who you are with and when you will check in. Pick three spots: a landmark, a place you
have never entered, and a local favorite food or view.

## Steps
1. Plan your route (10 minutes) and estimate how long each stop will take.
2. Go. At each stop, spend 10 minutes noticing: sounds, smells, the oldest thing you see, something funny.
3. Write a tip for visitors at each stop: "Go at sunset for the best view," "Ask for the cinnamon roll."
4. Sketch each spot quickly.
5. Rate each place from 1 to 5 stars.
6. Back home, design your guide page (30 minutes): a title, a mini map, three spotlights with sketches, and tips.

## Make it yours
- Do it in a theme: the best hidden benches, the weirdest street names, the cheapest snacks.
- Make it a guide for a visiting alien, explaining things like crosswalks and parking meters.
- Make a full guidebook of your town over the break, one area a day.

## Stuck?
- You cannot think of new places: walk a street you usually drive past, or go one stop past your usual one.
- It feels boring: talk to one shopkeeper or librarian and ask "what's something people miss here?"

## Show it off
Give the family a guided tour of one spot using your guide.

## Clean up
Empty your bag, file your notes, and put your shoes away.

## For the grown-up
This builds independence, planning, observation and writing. Agree on the route, check-in times and whether they can
take public transit. Do not have them go into anyone's home. **Ask at dinner:** "What place surprised you?"
