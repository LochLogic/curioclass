title: Backyard Weather Station
grade: 1
order: 5
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a clear plastic bottle, a ruler, tape, a marker, scissors (grown-up only), a paper plate, a long strip of plastic bag or crepe paper, a straw, a pencil, small stones or sand, paper for a weather log, crayons
nice: an outdoor thermometer, a compass, a clipboard

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Real weather scientists measure the rain, the wind and the clouds every day. Today you'll build your own weather station with a rain catcher and a wind sock. Then you can be the weather boss of your yard."

## Goals
- Your child builds a rain gauge and a wind sock and sets them up outside.
- Your child starts a weather log and records the weather for the rest of the break.

## Objectives
- Your child can measure in inches with a ruler.
- Your child can describe wind as calm, breezy or strong by watching the wind sock.
- Your child can draw weather symbols and record what they see each day.

## Get ready
Cut the top third off the plastic bottle (grown-up). Put a handful of stones in the bottom so it doesn't blow over. Pick a spot outside away from roofs and trees, or a balcony railing.

## Steps
1. **Rain gauge** (15 minutes). Turn the bottle top upside down and set it in the bottom part like a funnel. Tape a ruler to the side, or your child draws inch marks with the marker using the ruler. Mark 0 just above the stones; pour in water to the 0 line.
2. **Wind sock** (15 minutes). Your child decorates a paper plate ring (you cut the middle out). Tape 5 long strips of plastic bag or crepe paper to it. Hang it from a string.
3. **Set up the station.** Your child chooses the spot. Put the rain gauge where nothing drips into it and hang the wind sock where it can blow.
4. **Weather log.** Fold paper into 7 boxes, one per day. Your child draws symbols: sun, cloud, rain, wind.
5. **First reading** (10 minutes). Look at the sky, the wind sock and the rain gauge. Your child draws today's weather. Is the wind sock hanging (calm), waving (breezy) or straight out (strong)?
6. **Cloud check.** Name the clouds together: puffy (cumulus), flat and gray (stratus), thin and wispy (cirrus).
7. Your child makes a guess about tomorrow: "I think it will rain." Check tomorrow to see if they were right.

## Make it yours
- Add a weather vane with a paper arrow on a pencil to see which way the wind blows.
- Make a "puddle watch": draw chalk around a puddle and check how fast it shrinks.
- Have a weather contest: the family guesses tomorrow's weather, and the weather boss checks.

## Stuck?
- It isn't raining: pour a cup of water in and practice reading the ruler, then wait for real rain.
- No outdoor space: use a window sill and a wind sock hung by an open window, and record what you see.

## Show it off
Give a daily weather report to the family at dinner, using the log.

## Clean up
Tidy scraps. Empty the rain gauge after each reading and record it before you pour.

## For the grown-up
This builds measurement, observation, recording data over time and weather science (a first-grade topic). The cut edge of the bottle can be sharp; cover it with tape. Keep weather watching away from storms, and come inside when there is thunder. **Ask at dinner:** "What will the weather be tomorrow, and how do you know?"
