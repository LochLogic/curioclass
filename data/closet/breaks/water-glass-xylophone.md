title: Water Glass Xylophone
grade: 2
order: 8
kind: Music
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 6 to 8 glasses or glass jars the same size, water, a measuring cup, a metal spoon and a wooden spoon, a towel, paper and crayons
nice: food coloring, masking tape for labels

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Did you know water can sing? If you tap a glass, it rings. Add water and the sound changes. Today you are building your own musical instrument from glasses and water, and then you'll write a song for it."

## Goals
- A row of glasses that play different notes, from low to high.
- A short song your child made up and wrote down in colors.

## Objectives
- Your child can tell which sound is higher and which is lower.
- Your child can change the amount of water to change the sound.
- Your child can play a simple pattern and repeat it.

## Get ready
Set the glasses in a row on a towel on a sturdy table. Have water in a pitcher nearby. Your child stays sitting while playing, so nothing gets knocked over.

## Steps
1. **Tap an empty glass.** Use the metal spoon, gently. Listen. Now pour water halfway and tap again. Ask: "Higher or lower?"
2. **Fill the row** (15 minutes). Leave the first glass almost empty and add a little more water to each glass down the row, about a quarter cup more each time. Tap them in order. Is each one a little lower than the last?
3. **Tune it.** Your child listens and adjusts: add a splash or pour a little out until the notes step down evenly, like stairs.
4. **Color the notes.** Add a drop of a different food coloring to each glass, or put a colored tape label on each. Your child writes the color names in order on a paper.
5. **Play a pattern.** Try "Up the stairs" (play glasses 1 to 6), then "Down the stairs." Then try a pattern like red, red, blue, red.
6. **Write a song** (15 minutes). Your child makes up a short song and writes it with colored dots in the order to play. Five to 10 notes is plenty.
7. **Name it and practice.** Give the song a title. Play it three times until it's smooth.
8. **Try the wooden spoon.** How does the sound change? Your child picks the sound they like best.

## Make it yours
- Make a "rain song" with soft, quick taps and a "thunder" note on the emptiest glass.
- Play along to a song you both know, finding the notes by ear.
- Make a "secret song" and see if a grown-up can guess what it is.

## Stuck?
- All the glasses sound the same: make the water amounts more different, from nearly empty to nearly full.
- The sound is dull: tap near the rim, not the side, and don't touch the glass with your hand while it rings.

## Show it off
Hold a concert. Your child announces the song title, plays it, then teaches someone in the family to play it.

## Clean up
Pour out the water, dry the glasses, and wipe the table. Your child is in charge of the towel.

## For the grown-up
This builds listening, early music reading and real physics: less water means faster vibration and a higher note when you tap. Use sturdy glasses or jars, tap gently, and keep the setup low and on a towel. If a glass chips, you clean it up. **Ask at dinner:** "Why do you think the glass with the most water sounded the lowest?"
