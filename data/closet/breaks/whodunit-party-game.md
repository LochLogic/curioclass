title: Whodunit Party Game
grade: 6
order: 15
kind: Game
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 2+
materials: paper, index cards or paper squares, envelopes, pens, a "crime scene" spot in the house, 3 to 6 players to play it (family after work, siblings, friends who come over)
nice: costume pieces (hats, scarves, glasses), a flashlight, a magnifying glass

## The big idea
Someone has stolen the Golden Spatula! (Or the last slice of cake. Or Grandpa's lucky sock.) Every guest plays a suspect with a secret, and only one is guilty. You're the writer and the host. Plan the crime, hide the clues, and see if your family can solve your mystery.

## Goals
- You write a mystery with 3 to 5 suspects, clues, and one solution.
- Your guests play it and try to name the culprit.

## Objectives
- I can plan a mystery backward from the solution.
- I can write clues that point to the truth without making it too obvious.
- I can write characters with motives (reasons) and secrets.

## Get ready
Decide what was stolen and where. Pick one spot as the crime scene. Count your players; each one becomes a suspect.

## Steps
1. Decide the solution first. Who did it, why, and how? Write it down and seal it in an envelope.
2. Create suspects (20 minutes). For each, write a character card: name, job, a reason they might have done it, an alibi (where they claim they were), and one embarrassing secret that isn't the crime. Example: "Chef Ricotta. Wanted the spatula for her own cooking show. Says she was in the garden. Secret: she can't actually cook."
3. Make clues. Write 6 to 8 clue cards. Two point to the real culprit, two point toward an innocent suspect (red herrings), and the rest help rule people out.
4. Hide clues at the crime scene and around the house: a footprint drawn on paper, a torn note, a receipt with a time on it.
5. Plan rounds: Round 1, everyone reads their card aloud and introduces their character. Round 2, search for clues. Round 3, questioning, where everyone can ask anyone one question.
6. Run the game (40 minutes). Keep it moving. Answer questions in your host voice.
7. Final accusation: each player writes who they think did it. Open the solution envelope.

## Make it yours
- Make the host secretly guilty. (Plan it carefully so it's fair!)
- Set it somewhere wild: a space cruise, a 1920s train, a haunted museum.
- Give each player a secret mission besides solving the case, like "get someone to say the word 'banana.'"

## Stuck?
- It's too easy: add one more red herring, or make the key clue need two clue cards combined.
- It's too hard: add a "bonus clue" card that you hand out after round 2.
- Not enough players: the innocent suspects can be imaginary. Players play detectives instead.

## Show it off
That is the show! At the end, walk through what really happened, step by step, and how each clue fit.

## Clean up
Collect every clue so nothing gets found in a drawer next week. Keep the game in an envelope to play again with new people.

## For the grown-up
This builds logical reasoning, story planning, character writing, and confident hosting. Expect lots of planning time before the game; that's the real project. Playing it after dinner is the payoff. **Ask at dinner (or after the game):** "Which clue was the hardest to write?"
