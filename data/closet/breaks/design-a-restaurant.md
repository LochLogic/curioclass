title: Design a Restaurant
grade: 6
order: 24
kind: Pretend
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, markers or colored pencils, a pencil, a ruler, a notepad for taking orders, an apron or towel
nice: a table and chairs to set up as your restaurant, play money or paper bills, a small snack to "serve"

## The big idea
You're opening a restaurant, and you get to decide everything: the theme, the food, the name, the look, even the staff uniforms. A restaurant on the moon? A diner for dragons? A tiny café that only serves breakfast for dinner? Design it, then open your doors for one night.

## Goals
- You design a restaurant with a concept, a menu, and a floor plan.
- You run a pretend "opening night" with family as your first customers.

## Objectives
- I can design a business concept that fits together (name, food, decor).
- I can write a menu with descriptions and prices that make sense.
- I can stay in role and serve customers politely.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Write "Concept" at the top of a page. Decide your restaurant's big idea in one sentence: "A pirate seafood restaurant where the waiters talk like sailors."

## Steps
1. Name it. Brainstorm five names and pick the one that best fits your concept.
2. Write the menu (20 minutes). Include 3 appetizers, 4 main dishes, 2 drinks, and 2 desserts. Give each dish a fun name and a mouth-watering description: "Kraken Fries: crispy, golden, and slightly dangerous."
3. Set prices. Check that they make sense: desserts usually cost less than mains, and the fanciest item costs the most.
4. Draw the floor plan from above. Show tables, the kitchen, the entrance, the bathrooms, and one special feature (a fish tank, a stage, a slide).
5. Design the staff uniform and the logo.
6. Set up your restaurant for opening night. Lay out a "table," put a menu on it, and make a sign.
7. Open the doors. Greet customers, take orders on your notepad, and "serve" (real snacks, water, or just describe the food dramatically).

## Make it yours
- Add a "chef's special" that changes each night with a mysterious description.
- Create a restaurant review form and ask customers to rate food, service, and decor.
- Make it an unusual service style: food arrives on a train, or every dish is served by a "robot" (a sibling walking stiffly).

## Stuck?
- The concept feels plain: combine two ideas that don't go together, like a library and a pizza place.
- Prices are confusing: round them to whole dollars.
- No customers yet: serve stuffed animals and practice your lines until people get home.

## Show it off
Host your opening night after dinner. Hand out menus, take orders, and give a short welcome speech.

## Clean up
Clear the table, wash any dishes, and put your menu and floor plan in a folder.

## For the grown-up
This builds creativity, math (pricing and totals), descriptive writing, and social skills like serving and speaking politely. If your child serves real food, keep it simple (crackers, fruit, water) and check allergies. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the most popular dish on your menu?"
