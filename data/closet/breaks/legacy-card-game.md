title: Legacy Card Game
grade: 11
order: 4
kind: Game
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 40 to 60 index cards or cut paper rectangles, pens, an envelope or two, stickers or tape, a pencil
nice: a sealed "do not open until..." envelope, colored markers, a few dice

## The big idea
In a legacy game, every play changes the game forever. Cards get written on, torn up or unlocked, and the box
remembers what happened last time. You are going to design one: a small card game with secret envelopes that open
after certain events, so the fifth game is nothing like the first.

## Goals
- A playable card game with a core loop that works in 15 minutes.
- At least three sealed envelopes that permanently change the rules when triggered.

## Objectives
- I can design a simple core rule and balance it through playtesting.
- I can write triggers and unlocks that build tension over many plays.
- I can explain how a game's history can tell a story.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Pick a theme in one minute: a rival bakery war, two space colonies, a haunted lighthouse, a dragon
heist. The theme decides your card names.

## Steps
1. Design the core loop (20 minutes). Keep it simple: each turn, draw one card and play one card. Cards score points,
   steal points, or block. First to 10 wins.
2. Make 30 starter cards: about 15 scorers (1 to 3 points), 8 stealers, 7 blockers. Name them for your theme.
3. Playtest twice, alone (playing both hands) or with anyone home. Fix anything that wins too often.
4. Write your envelopes (25 minutes). Each one has a trigger on the outside, like "Open when someone wins with
   exactly 10" or "Open after the third game." Inside: new cards, a rule change, or a sticker that permanently
   alters a card.
5. Add permanence. Give the winner of each game one action: write their name on a card to give it +1 forever, or
   tear one card in half. Write down the rule.
6. Make a "history card" where every winner and every opened envelope is logged.
7. Play a round or two and open your first envelope for real.

## Make it yours
- Make a final envelope that ends the campaign with a winner of the whole series.
- Add a story card for each envelope that reveals one chapter of a mystery.
- Make it cooperative: both players fight the deck, and the deck gets meaner with every loss.

## Stuck?
- One card is too strong: raise its cost or add a rule that it must be discarded after use.
- Unlocks feel random: tie each one to something players care about, like a comeback or a perfect game.

## Show it off
Teach it to someone at home after dinner and play game one. Promise them the envelopes will make game two very different.

## Clean up
Rubber-band the deck, keep envelopes sealed in a box, and store the history card on top.

## For the grown-up
This is systems design: probability, balance, testing and fixing, plus storytelling across sessions. It is a great
game to play together over a whole break. **Ask at dinner:** "What's inside the envelope you hope we open first?"
