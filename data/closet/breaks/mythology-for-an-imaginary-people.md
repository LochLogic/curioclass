title: Mythology for an Imaginary People
grade: 8
order: 18
kind: Story
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, a pencil or pen, colored pencils or markers
nice: a big sheet of paper for a family tree of gods, a stapler for a booklet

## The big idea
Every ancient culture told stories to explain the world: why the sun rises, where rivers come from, why cats hate water. Invent a people who never existed, and then write the myths they would tell: their gods, their heroes, and the stories they tell by the fire.

## Goals
- A creation myth and at least one more myth or legend.
- A chart of at least four gods or spirits with their powers and symbols.

## Objectives
- I can write myths that explain something about the world.
- I can create characters with clear personalities and powers.
- I can show how a people's environment shapes their stories.

## Get ready
Invent your people first. Write a few lines: where they live (a frozen coast, a desert of glass, a floating island), how they get food, and what they fear most.

## Steps
1. **Create the gods or spirits (15 minutes).** Make four to six. Each needs a name, what they rule (storms, memory, cooking, luck), a symbol, and a personality flaw. A god of the tides who's forgetful. A spirit of bread who's jealous.
2. **Draw the family tree.** How are they related? Who are rivals? Who's in love? Who's not speaking to whom?
3. **Write the creation myth (20 minutes).** How did the world begin, in your people's version? Maybe the world was hatched from an egg, or sung into existence, or made from a giant's lost shoe. Keep it to one page.
4. **Write a "why" story.** Pick something in nature and explain it. Why does the moon have spots? Why does the wind howl at night? Why do owls stay awake? Example: "The owl once stole the moon's sleep, and now it can never close its eyes."
5. **Write a hero legend.** A human who did something great, or foolish. What did they want? Which gods helped or hurt them? What lesson does the story teach?
6. **Design a festival.** Your people celebrate one of these myths every year. What do they eat, wear and do?
7. **Illustrate.** Draw one scene from a myth, in the style of an ancient carving, a painted vase, or a woven tapestry.

## Make it yours
- Write a myth from the point of view of a god who thinks they're the hero, but clearly isn't.
- Make two peoples with competing myths about the same event.
- Write a modern myth for your town: why the traffic light on Main Street is always red.

## Stuck?
- Gods feel flat: give each one a want and a weakness.
- Myths feel random: connect them to the people's life. Fishing people care about the sea. Desert people care about water.
- Not sure how to start: begin with "Long ago, before the..." It always works.

## Show it off
Tell one of your myths out loud at dinner, like a storyteller around a fire. Lights low, voice dramatic.

## Clean up
Staple your pages into a booklet with a title and keep it with your art.

## For the grown-up
This builds creative writing, world-building, and an understanding of myths and why cultures tell them, which comes up in English and social studies. **Ask at dinner:** "Which god or spirit in your mythology would you most want to meet?"
