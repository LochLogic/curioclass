## The big idea
Flash fiction is a whole story in 500 words or less. That's about one page. No room for boring parts, no room for twelve characters. Just one moment that matters, and an ending that hits. Think of it as a short film you write instead of shoot.

## Goals
- You write a complete story of 500 words or fewer.
- You revise it to make it sharper, then read it aloud.

## Objectives
- I can build a story around one character, one place, and one change.
- I can cut words that don't do any work.
- I can write an ending that surprises or lands with a feeling.

## Get ready
Find a quiet spot. Number lines 1 to 25 down the side of a page; about 20 words per line means 500 words is roughly 25 lines.

## Steps
1. Pick a prompt (or make your own): "The elevator stopped between floors, and the lights went out." "Every night at 3 a.m., the dog stares at the same wall." "I found a note in my handwriting that I don't remember writing."
2. Answer three questions in one line each: Who is the main character? Where are they? What changes by the end?
3. Start in the middle of the action. Skip "One day I woke up." Your first line should drop the reader right into something odd.
4. Write the first draft fast (25 minutes). Don't stop to fix things. Get to the end.
5. Count your words. Over 500? Good, now you get to cut.
6. Revise (15 minutes). Cross out words like "very," "really" and "suddenly." Replace weak verbs: "walked slowly" becomes "crept." Cut any sentence that repeats something the reader already knows.
7. Rewrite the last line three different ways and pick the best. Then give the story a title that adds a hint.

## Make it yours
- Write it in exactly 100 words. Then 50. See what survives.
- Tell it backward, starting with the ending and finishing with how it began.
- Write it all as dialogue, no description at all, so the reader figures out the scene from voices.

## Stuck?
- Blank page panic: write the worst first sentence you can on purpose, then keep going.
- The story keeps growing: you have two stories. Pick the better one.
- The ending falls flat: end one beat earlier, right before the explanation.

## Show it off
Read it aloud to your family. Afterward, ask them one question: "What do you think happens next?"

## Clean up
Recopy the final version neatly, and keep your messy draft too. Writers keep drafts.

## For the grown-up
This builds core language arts skills: story structure, word choice, and revision, which is the part of writing kids skip most. Listen for the feeling of the story more than the spelling. **Ask at dinner:** "What did you cut that you were sad to lose?"
