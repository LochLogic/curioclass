title: Mystery Bag Guessing Game
grade: 1
order: 6
kind: Game
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 2+
materials: a pillowcase or cloth bag, 10 to 15 everyday objects (a spoon, a sock, a pinecone, a toy car, a sponge, an orange, a hairbrush, a key, a block, a cotton ball), paper, a pencil, a partner (a grown-up, a sibling or a friend over)
nice: a scarf for a blindfold, a timer

## The big idea
Read aloud: "There's something hiding in the Mystery Bag! You can't look. You can only feel it. Is it soft? Bumpy? Cold? Can you use your detective fingers to guess what's inside?"

## Goals
- Your child guesses mystery objects by touch alone.
- Your child fills a mystery bag and gives clues for a partner to guess.

## Objectives
- Your child can use describing words: smooth, rough, soft, hard, round, pointy, heavy, light.
- Your child can give clues without saying the answer.
- Your child can make a guess based on evidence.

## Get ready
Without your child seeing, gather 10 objects. Make sure nothing is sharp. Put 5 in the bag. Write the list on paper and fold it.

## Steps
1. **Practice words.** Ask your child to touch things around the room and describe them: "The couch is soft. The window is smooth and cold."
2. **Round 1** (10 minutes). Your child reaches into the bag without looking and holds one thing. Before pulling it out, they describe it: "It's long. One end is round. It's cold." Then they guess. Pull it out to check!
3. Keep score: a point for each right guess.
4. **Round 2: Clue master** (15 minutes). Your child secretly picks an object and puts it in the bag. They give you three clues: "It's soft. You wear it. It goes on your foot." You guess.
5. **Make it harder.** Your child must give clues with no "using" words, only how it feels: "It's soft, stretchy and has a hole."
6. **Round 3: Whole house** (15 minutes). Your child hunts the house for the trickiest mystery object they can find and puts it in for you.
7. Add up points. The winner picks the next game.

## Make it yours
- Do a smell mystery instead: a lemon, cinnamon, soap, an orange peel, with eyes closed.
- Do a sound mystery: shake things in a closed box.
- Make it a story: the objects were stolen from a museum, and the detective has to find them all.

## Stuck?
- Too hard: let your child look for 1 second after 3 guesses.
- Your child says the answer as a clue: practice "it's a thing that..." sentences together first.

## Show it off
Play with the family after dinner. Your child is the game host and picks the objects.

## Clean up
Your child returns each object to its place. Can they remember where each one came from?

## For the grown-up
This builds describing vocabulary, reasoning from clues, and taking turns, plus sense of touch. Giving clues without saying the answer is a big thinking skill. Keep sharp, breakable or very small objects out of the bag. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the trickiest mystery object?"
