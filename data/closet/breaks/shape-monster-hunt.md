title: Shape Monster Hunt
grade: k
order: 5
kind: Game
time: 45-75 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: paper, crayons or markers, scissors (grown-up only), tape, a paper bag or box, a paper towel tube
nice: a clipboard, a flashlight, stickers

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Shape Monsters are hiding all over the house! They look like circles, squares, triangles and rectangles. They are sneaky, but if you know your shapes, you can find them. Ready to hunt?"

## Goals
- Your child finds at least 12 shapes hiding in the house (or yard).
- Your child invents their own Shape Monster.

## Objectives
- Your child can name circle, square, triangle and rectangle.
- Your child can find each shape in real things (a plate is a circle, a door is a rectangle).
- Your child can sort what they find into groups.

## Get ready
On one sheet of paper, draw four big boxes and draw a shape in each: circle, square, triangle, rectangle. Give each shape monster eyes and teeth. This is the "Monster Map." Make a "monster scope" from the paper towel tube.

## Steps
1. Show the Monster Map. Introduce each monster in a funny voice: "I am Circle! I am round with no corners!" Trace each one with your child's finger and count the corners.
2. **Practice spotting.** Look around the room together. Ask: "Where is Circle hiding?" (A clock? A plate?)
3. **The hunt** (20 minutes). Your child walks through the house with the monster scope. Each time they find a shape, they say "Gotcha!" and you make a tally mark in that monster's box.
4. Let your child lead the route and choose which room to search next.
5. **Count the catch.** Which monster had the most hiding spots? Which was hardest to find? (Triangles are usually tricky!)
6. **Invent a monster** (15 minutes). Your child draws their own Shape Monster built from shapes: a square body, triangle ears, circle eyes. Help name it ("Trianglo the Crunchy").
7. Cut it out and tape it somewhere in the house for someone else to find.

## Make it yours
- Hunt outside: round wheels, rectangle windows, triangle roofs.
- Hunt for colors or letters instead of shapes.
- Make it a race: how many circles can your child find before you count to 60?

## Stuck?
- Triangles are hard to find: look at a folded napkin, a roof in a picture, a slice of bread cut corner to corner.
- Square or rectangle confusion: count the sides. If all four sides look the same length, it is a square.

## Show it off
Your child hides their own Shape Monster drawing for a family member to find after dinner, then tells where the other monsters were hiding.

## Clean up
Your child collects the monster drawings and puts them in an envelope for next time.

## For the grown-up
This is early geometry: naming shapes, counting sides and corners, and seeing shapes in the real world, all kindergarten math goals. Tally marks are a first step to counting in groups of five. **Ask at dinner:** "Where was the sneakiest place a Shape Monster was hiding?"
