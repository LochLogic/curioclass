title: Trivia Night About Your Friends
grade: 12
order: 29
kind: Game
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 2+
materials: index cards or paper, pens, a timer, snacks, players (friends over or family after dinner)
nice: a bell or buzzer, prizes, a scoreboard on a big sheet of paper

## The big idea
How well do your friends really know each other? Who once got stuck in a tree? Whose first job was the weirdest? You
are hosting a trivia night where every answer is about someone in the room. It is part game show, part roast, and
all about the strange, wonderful stories people do not usually tell.

## Goals
- Five rounds of trivia questions, with at least six questions each.
- A hosted game night with scores and a winner.

## Objectives
- I can write clear trivia questions with one correct answer.
- I can organize a game with rounds, rules and scoring.
- I can host a fun event and keep it moving.

## Get ready
Before the night, send each player a list of five questions to answer privately: a funny moment, a first job, a
hidden talent, a weird fear, a favorite food. Collect the answers in envelopes.

## Steps
1. Plan rounds (20 minutes): **Who Said It?** (match quotes to people), **True or False** (a surprising fact),
   **Guess the Fear**, **Childhood**, **Lightning Round**.
2. Write questions from the answers. Example: "Which player once ate a whole lemon on a dare?"
3. Set rules: teams or solo, points per correct answer, a bonus for knowing the full story.
4. Make a scoreboard.
5. Host it (40 minutes). Read the questions, keep time, and ask the subject to tell the story after each answer.
6. Award a prize: the "Best Friend Detective" trophy.

## Make it yours
- Add a round where people guess what someone would do in a strange situation.
- Make a picture round with baby photos.
- Host a family version about grandparents and parents.

## Stuck?
- Answers are too easy: ask for specific details, like the year or the place.
- Someone feels embarrassed: keep it kind and let anyone skip a question.

## Show it off
Post the final scoreboard and give out a trophy.

## Clean up
Put snacks away, wipe tables and keep the questions for a rematch.

## For the grown-up
This builds organizing, writing, hosting and listening skills, and it strengthens friendships. Keep questions kind,
with anyone free to pass. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the most surprising answer?"
