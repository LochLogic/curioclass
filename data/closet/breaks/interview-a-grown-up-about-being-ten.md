materials: paper, a pencil, a clipboard or a book to write on, markers

## The big idea
Your grown-ups were once kids too. What games did they play? What did they eat? Did they get in trouble? Become a reporter and interview a grown-up about being ten years old.

## Goals
- You write and ask at least 10 interview questions.
- You write a 1-page story about their childhood.

## Objectives
- I can write questions that get long answers, not just "yes" or "no."
- I can listen and take short notes.
- I can turn notes into a story with a beginning, middle and end.

## Get ready
Pick a grown-up to interview: a parent, grandparent, aunt, uncle, neighbor or family friend. Ask them for 30 minutes of their time. It can be in person or on a phone call they make.

## Steps
1. Write 10 questions (15 minutes). Start with "What," "How" and "Tell me about." Example: "What was your favorite toy when you were ten?"
2. Add 2 surprise questions. "What's something you did that you never told your parents?"
3. Do the interview (30 minutes). Ask each question. Write short notes, not every word.
4. Ask follow-up questions. If they say "We played outside," ask "What games?"
5. Thank them for their time.
6. Read your notes. Circle the 3 best stories.
7. Write a 1-page story. Start with a hook: "When my grandma was ten, she..."
8. Draw a picture of them as a kid.

## Make it yours
- Compare their life at ten with yours. Make a two-column chart.
- Interview 2 grown-ups and see how their answers differ.
- Make a "then and now" poster.

## Stuck?
- They give short answers: say "Tell me more about that."
- You can't keep up: ask them to wait while you write.
- Your story is too long: pick the funniest or most surprising part.

## Show it off
Read your story out loud at dinner. Watch your grown-up's face!

## Clean up
Keep your notes and story in a folder. It's a family treasure.

## For the grown-up
This builds listening, asking good questions and turning notes into writing. It's also a gift: kids rarely hear their grown-ups' childhood stories. Please be the interviewee, or help set up a call. **Ask at dinner:** "What surprised you most about being ten back then?"
