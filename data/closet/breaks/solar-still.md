## The big idea
You're stranded on a desert island with seawater all around and nothing to drink. You can't drink salt water. But the sun can separate the water from the salt for you. Build a **solar still**, the same idea survival experts use, and see how much clean water you can make before "rescue."

## Goals
- A working solar still that collects drops of fresh water.
- A measured amount of collected water and proof that it isn't salty.

## Objectives
- I can explain **evaporation** and **condensation** in the water cycle.
- I can explain why salt stays behind when water evaporates.
- I can test a variable and measure the result.

## Get ready
Find a sunny spot outdoors: a step, a balcony, a patio. A very sunny windowsill works if you can't go out. Mix "seawater": 2 teaspoons of salt in 2 cups of warm tap water.

## Steps
1. Taste a drop of your seawater (just a drop) so you know what salty is.
2. Pour the seawater into the big bowl, 2 to 3 cm deep. Add food coloring if you have it: it'll prove that only water travels.
3. Set the empty cup in the center. It must be taller than the water but shorter than the bowl's rim.
4. Stretch plastic wrap tight over the bowl and seal the edges.
5. Put the stone in the middle of the wrap, right above the cup, so the wrap dips into a cone pointing down.
6. **Wait and watch** (45 to 90 minutes). Check every 15 minutes. Draw what you see: fog, then drops, then drips into the cup.
7. Carefully remove the wrap and measure what's in the cup with a teaspoon. Taste a drop. Is it salty? What color is it?
8. Calculate how many hours you'd need to make one cup of fresh water at this rate.

## Make it yours
- Build two stills to test a variable: a dark bowl vs. a light one, or full sun vs. shade.
- Write a short survival log from your island, with your water totals day by day.
- Collect water from leaves instead: tie a clear bag over a leafy branch outside and compare.

## Stuck?
- No drops after 30 minutes: it needs more heat. Use warmer water or a sunnier spot.
- Drops fall back into the bowl: make the cone steeper with a slightly heavier stone.

## Show it off
Give your family a blind taste test: a drop of seawater and a drop of your still water. Which is which?

## Clean up
Pour the salt water down the sink, not on plants (salt harms them), and wash the bowls.

## For the grown-up
This is earth science and chemistry: evaporation, condensation, solutions and the water cycle. Only drinking-water ingredients are used, so a taste is safe. **Ask at dinner:** "Why doesn't the salt end up in the cup?"
