title: Family Time Capsule
grade: 1
order: 30
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a shoebox or a large jar with a lid, paper, crayons or markers, a pencil, tape, a measuring tape or string, small keepsakes (a drawing, a list, a photo)
nice: an envelope for each person, a sticker or ribbon to seal it

## The big idea
Read aloud: "A time capsule is a box full of things from today that you open far in the future! When you open it, you'll get to meet the you from right now. What do you want future you to know?"

## Goals
- Your child fills a time capsule with drawings, facts and a letter to their future self.
- Your child seals it and chooses a date to open it.

## Objectives
- Your child can tell facts about their life now: age, height, favorites.
- Your child can write a few words or a short letter.
- Your child can think about how things change over time.

## Get ready
Get a box or jar. Write these starters on separate papers: "My favorites," "Right now I am...," "Dear Future Me," "My family," "I hope..."

## Steps
1. **Talk about time.** Ask: "What were you like when you were a baby? What will you be like when you're 10?" Explain that the time capsule saves today.
2. **Measure.** Your child stands against the wall and you measure them with string or a tape. Cut a string their height, or write the number down. It goes in the capsule!
3. **Handprint and favorites** (15 minutes). Trace your child's hand on paper. On the "My favorites" page, your child draws or writes: favorite food, color, toy, book, animal, song.
4. **Letter to future me** (15 minutes). Your child tells you what to write: "Dear Future Me, I like to dance. Do you still like pizza?" They sign it.
5. **Family page.** Your child draws everyone they live with. Each family member can add a note or drawing later too.
6. **Decorate the capsule** (10 minutes). Your child decorates the box with a big label: "Do Not Open Until..."
7. **Seal it.** Put everything in. Your child chooses when to open it: in a year? On their birthday? Write the date on the box. Tape it shut and hide it somewhere safe.

## Make it yours
- Add a "guess the future" page: your child predicts what they'll be like in a year.
- Make a time capsule for a stuffed animal, telling about its life.
- Include a small object (a drawing, a leaf, a sticker) that means something today.

## Stuck?
- Writing is too hard: your child draws and you write their words.
- Your child wants to open it tomorrow: make one "mini capsule" to open at the end of the break, and the big one for a year.

## Show it off
Before sealing it, let your child show each item to the family. Everyone can add one thing.

## Clean up
Your child puts the supplies away and helps choose a safe hiding spot for the capsule.

## For the grown-up
This builds writing, self-awareness and an understanding of time (past, present, future), a first-grade social studies idea. It's a wonderful thing to open in a year. Put a reminder on your calendar. **Ask at dinner:** "What do you think you'll be like when you open the time capsule?"
