title: Radio Drama with Foley Sound
grade: 11
order: 8
kind: Pretend
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper and a pen, household sound makers (celery or a cabbage, a pair of shoes, a box of cornstarch or flour in a bag, cooking pots, a sheet of paper, an umbrella, gloves, keys, a bowl of water)
nice: a phone to record, family members or friends as extra voices, a blanket to build a sound booth

## The big idea
Before television, millions of people crowded around radios for stories told only with voices and sound effects.
The sound artists, called **Foley artists**, made horse hooves with coconut shells and broken bones with celery.
You are going to write and perform a ten-minute audio drama where every sound comes from things in your house.

## Goals
- A finished script of about four pages with sound cues marked.
- A live or recorded performance with at least eight Foley effects.

## Objectives
- I can tell a story using only sound, dialogue and narration.
- I can create convincing sound effects from everyday objects.
- I can write a script with clear cues so performers know when to act.

## Get ready
Gather possible sound makers on a table. Spend 10 minutes playing: crunch, shake, drip, slap. Write down every sound
that surprised you. Those are your toolkit.

## Steps
1. Pick a genre that loves sound: a storm at a lighthouse, a creaky haunted hotel, a heist, a space station alarm.
2. Plan three scenes, each with a clear place the listener can hear (rain on a roof, footsteps on gravel).
3. Write the script (30 minutes). Use this format: character name in capitals, then their line. Sound cues in
   brackets: [SFX: Door creaks open]. Example: "MARA: Did you hear that? [SFX: Three slow footsteps above.]"
4. Write for the ear. Characters should say names and places out loud ("Run, Theo, to the boathouse!") because the
   audience cannot see.
5. Build your Foley (20 minutes). Ideas: snapping celery for bones, cornstarch squeezed in a bag for footsteps in
   snow, a flapping umbrella for wings, gloves flapped for birds, keys for jail cells.
6. Rehearse with sound and voices together. If you are solo, play every part, using a different voice for each.
7. Perform it live behind a sheet or blanket, or record it in one take.

## Make it yours
- Make it an old-time radio show with a fake sponsor jingle for an imaginary product.
- Write a cliffhanger ending and promise episode two tomorrow.
- Perform it in total darkness for the audience, with only a flashlight on your script.

## Stuck?
- A sound does not work: listen with your eyes closed. If it is not clear, add a line of dialogue that names it.
- Too many parts for one person: cut to two characters plus a narrator.

## Show it off
Turn off the lights after dinner and play or perform it. The audience must keep their eyes closed.

## Clean up
Return the pots and shoes, compost the celery, and sweep any cornstarch.

## For the grown-up
This is drama, script writing and sound design, and it builds voice work, timing and creative problem solving.
Recording is optional; it works as a live show. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the hardest sound to fake?"
