title: Professor at Wizard School
grade: 5
order: 13
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pencil, markers, stuffed animals or toys as students, a stick or pencil for a wand, a towel or blanket for a robe, 3 clear cups, water, food coloring, cooking oil, salt, baking soda, vinegar, a spoon, a tray
nice: a pointy paper hat, a bell to start class, a sibling or grown-up as a student

## The big idea
A school for young wizards needs a new professor. That is you! You choose the class, write the lesson, and teach it to a room full of students. They might be stuffed animals, but they still need homework. Watch out. One of them always causes trouble.

## Goals
- You plan and teach one full wizard class with a demo and a test.
- You make a page from the class textbook.

## Objectives
- I can plan a lesson with a beginning, a demo, and a check at the end.
- I can explain how a "potion" works using real science words.
- I can speak in character in front of an audience.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up before using kitchen ingredients. Line up your students in rows. Set the potion cups on a tray at the "front of class."

## Steps
1. Pick your subject. Potions, Magical Creatures, Spell Writing, or one you invent, like Cloud Taming.
2. Make your professor. Pick a name, like Professor Thistlewick. Add one odd habit, like sneezing sparks.
3. Write the lesson plan (15 minutes). Three parts: what you will teach, your demo, and your test.
4. Make your demo potions. **Bubbling Lava:** fill a cup halfway with water, add color, pour in a thin layer of oil, and sprinkle salt. **Dragon Fizz:** put a spoon of baking soda in a cup and add vinegar.
5. Write the "real magic" for each one. Salt is heavier than oil, so it sinks and drags oil down. Baking soda and vinegar make a gas.
6. Make a textbook page. Draw a creature or a potion, with labels and three rules for students.
7. Teach the class (20 minutes). Ring the bell. Do the demos. Then give a quick test, and award points to the best student.

## Make it yours
- Write a spell-writing lesson where every spell must rhyme: "Wiggle, waggle, socks be found, show yourself above the ground."
- Invent a magical creature and teach how to care for it, including what it eats.
- Add a troublemaker student who "messes up" a spell. Write what goes wrong.

## Stuck?
- The lava potion stops: add another pinch of salt.
- Students are bored: let them "vote" on the next potion by raising paws.
- You forget your lines: write key words on a card and call it your "spell scroll."

## Show it off
Invite your family to a "parents' night." Teach them a short version of your class. Give each one a report card.

## Clean up
Pour the potions down the drain with lots of water. Wash the cups and wipe the tray.

## For the grown-up
Pretending to teach builds planning, clear speaking, and real understanding, since you have to know something well to explain it. The potions are kitchen science (density, a gas-making reaction). Please okay the ingredients; nothing else from under the sink. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the real science behind your best potion?"
