nice: a tarp or old sheet, a tape measure, a small trowel

## The big idea
Lost in the woods, the first thing survival experts build is a shelter, not a fire. A lean-to is one of the simplest: a slanted wall of sticks and leaves that keeps off wind and rain. Build one big enough for a stuffed animal (or you!) and test if it keeps your camper dry.

## Goals
- You build a lean-to shelter from fallen sticks and leaves.
- You test it with a "rainstorm" and see if your camper stays dry.

## Objectives
- I can build a structure using a frame and covering.
- I can choose materials based on strength and shape.
- I can test my build and improve it.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up where you can build: a yard, a garden corner, or a park with permission. Put on gloves. Look for a spot with a support like a fence, a low tree branch, or two sturdy forked sticks you can push into the ground.

## Steps
1. Find your ridge pole: one long, straight, sturdy stick. For a stuffed-animal shelter, about 60 cm. For a kid-sized one, about 1.5 meters.
2. Set up the frame. Rest one end of the ridge pole on a support (a forked stick, a low branch, a fence) and the other on the ground, or tie it between two supports with string.
3. Lean shorter sticks against the ridge pole, side by side, at a steep angle (about 45 degrees). Pack them close together.
4. Weave in thinner twigs sideways to fill gaps.
5. Pile leaves, pine needles, or grass on top, starting at the bottom and working up, like shingles on a roof. Aim for a layer at least as thick as your hand.
6. Put your camper inside. Pour a cup of water slowly over the roof. Check if the camper stayed dry (10 minutes of testing and fixing).
7. Add one improvement: a back wall, a leaf bed inside, or a door flap.

## Make it yours
- Build a tiny shelter village for small toy campers, each with a different design.
- Write a survival diary for your camper: "Night 1: The wind was fierce, but the lean-to held."
- Indoor or balcony version: lean a broom against two chairs, drape a sheet, and build a fort with the same angles.

## Stuck?
- The frame falls over: dig the ends of sticks into the ground, or tie joints with string.
- Water leaks through: add more leaves and make the roof steeper.
- Not enough sticks: build a smaller shelter. Even a 30 cm one teaches the idea.

## Show it off
Give a "survival school" tour. Show how the roof sheds water and explain why the angle matters.

## Clean up
Take the shelter apart and scatter the sticks and leaves where you found them. Wash your hands.

## For the grown-up
This builds engineering, problem solving, and outdoor skills, the same idea used in wilderness survival training. Please set where your child can go, and remind them to use only fallen wood (never break branches off living trees) and to avoid poison ivy or anything they don't know. Gloves help with splinters. No fires; this is shelter only. **Ask at dinner:** "Did your camper stay dry? What would you change?"
