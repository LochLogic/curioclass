title: Color Mixing Potions
grade: k
order: 18
kind: Science
time: 45-75 min
where: Either
mess: High
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 3 clear cups or jars, water, food coloring (red, yellow, blue) or washable markers, a spoon, an ice cube tray or muffin tin, a dropper or a spoon, paper towels, a baking pan, an old shirt
nice: a turkey baster, coffee filters, a white sheet of paper for testing

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Welcome to the Potion Lab! You have three magic colors: red, yellow and blue. If you mix them, new colors appear. Can you make purple? Can you make green? Let's mix some potions!"

## Goals
- Your child makes at least 3 new colors from red, yellow and blue.
- Your child invents and names their own potion.

## Objectives
- Your child can predict what color two colors will make.
- Your child can name the three primary colors.
- Your child can tell what they did in order ("first red, then blue").

## Get ready
Put on an old shirt. Set the baking pan on a table (or outdoors) and lay paper towels around it. Fill 3 cups halfway with water. Add 3 drops of red to one, yellow to another, blue to the third. No food coloring? Soak the tip of a washable marker in each cup for 2 minutes.

## Steps
1. Show the three colors. Say: "These are the primary colors. They are the magic starting colors."
2. **First potion.** Ask: "What will happen if we mix yellow and blue?" Your child guesses, then spoons some of each into a cup of the ice cube tray. Green!
3. **More potions** (15 minutes). Your child tries red and yellow, red and blue. Guess first every time. Lay a paper towel tip in each one to see the color soak up.
4. **Recipe card.** Draw a dot of each color on paper with a plus sign and an equals sign: yellow + blue = green.
5. **The secret potion** (10 minutes). Your child mixes any colors they want and names the potion ("Dragon Swamp Juice"). Ask: "What does your potion do?"
6. Try mixing all three. What happens? (It turns brown or muddy.)
7. Dip a paper towel or coffee filter into different potions and let the colors run together into tie-dye art.

## Make it yours
- Make a rainbow: line up the cups in rainbow order (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple).
- Make a light and dark potion: add water to make a color lighter, add more drops to make it darker.
- Play "Potion Shop," and you order a color your child has to make.

## Stuck?
- The colors are too dark to tell apart: pour some out and add more water.
- Spills: that's part of potion-making. Keep everything inside the baking pan.

## Show it off
Line up the potions and the tie-dye paper on the windowsill. Your child explains each recipe to the family.

## Clean up
Pour the potions down the drain. Your child rinses the cups. Food coloring can stain, so wipe surfaces right away.

## For the grown-up
This is early color science and art: primary and secondary colors, guessing and testing, and recording results. Food coloring stains fabric and some counters, so work on a tray and use old clothes. Keep the potions out of reach of younger siblings who might drink them. **Ask at dinner:** "Can you tell me how to make purple?"
