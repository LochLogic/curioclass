## The big idea
Coaches don't just shout "try harder." They plan **drills**: short, focused practice activities that build one skill at a time. You'll design a 45-minute practice for a rookie (a younger sibling, a cousin, a friend or a grown-up who wants to learn) and coach them to a skill they couldn't do before.

## Goals
- A written practice plan with a warm-up, three drills, a game and a cool-down.
- A rookie who improved a skill by the end.

## Objectives
- I can break a sport skill into steps and design drills for each.
- I can use **progression**: start easy and make each drill harder.
- I can give specific, encouraging feedback.

## Get ready
Pick your rookie and your skill: dribbling a ball, catching, jump rope tricks, throwing a frisbee, shooting at a target. Find a safe space (a yard, a park with permission or a big room for soft skills).

## Steps
1. **Test** (5 minutes). Have your rookie try the skill. Write down what they can and can't do.
2. **Plan the warm-up** (5 minutes of practice): jogging, arm circles, a fun tag game.
3. **Design three drills** that build on each other. For dribbling: Drill 1, dribble standing still 20 times with each hand. Drill 2, dribble walking between cones 2 m apart. Drill 3, dribble around cones while you try to tag the ball.
4. **Write the plan** with times, setup and coaching cues for each drill. Cues are short: "Fingertips, not palm." "Eyes up."
5. **Run the practice** (40 minutes). Show each drill, let them try, give one cue at a time. If they master a drill, make it harder. If they struggle, make it easier.
6. **End with a game** that uses the skill, like a dribbling relay or target challenge.
7. **Cool-down and retest.** Stretch, then retest the skill. Write down the improvement.

## Make it yours
- Run a "training camp" day with a camp name and an award at the end.
- Design a drill your rookie can practice alone this week.
- Switch roles: let them design a drill for you.

## Stuck?
- The rookie is frustrated: make the drill smaller, and celebrate the first success.
- The drill is too easy: add a time limit or a defender.

## Show it off
Have your rookie demo their new skill for the family at dinner, with you announcing.

## Clean up
Gather equipment and cones and put them back. Both of you drink water.

## For the grown-up
Coaching builds leadership, patience, communication and an understanding of how skills are learned, linking to health and PE. If they coach a younger child, a grown-up should be home, and outdoor sessions stay in agreed places. **Ask at dinner:** "What was your best coaching cue?"
