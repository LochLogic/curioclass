title: Life-Size Paper Twin
grade: 1
order: 28
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a big sheet of paper as tall as your child (paper grocery bags cut open and taped together, or the back of wrapping paper), tape, a pencil, crayons or markers, scissors (grown-up only for big cuts), glue stick, fabric scraps, yarn, old magazines
nice: washable paint, a measuring tape, a mirror

## The big idea
Read aloud: "What if you had a twin made of paper? Your paper twin will be exactly as tall as you. You get to decide what it wears, what it's holding, and whether it has a secret superpower. Lie down, it's time to trace!"

## Goals
- Your child makes a life-size paper twin of themselves.
- Your child decorates it to show who they are and hangs it up.

## Objectives
- Your child can name body parts and place them correctly.
- Your child can measure their own height and compare it to other things.
- Your child can make choices about color, clothes and details that show their personality.

## Get ready
Tape the paper together on the floor so it is a little longer than your child. Clear space around it.

## Steps
1. **Strike a pose.** Your child lies on the paper in any pose they want: arms up like a star, one leg bent, hands on hips.
2. **Trace** (5 minutes). You trace around your child with the pencil. Go slowly, it tickles!
3. **Measure.** Measure the paper twin with the measuring tape (or count how many of your shoes long it is). Write the number at the top: "I am 46 inches tall!"
4. **Body parts.** Your child draws the face using a mirror: eye color, hair, freckles. Ask: "Where do the elbows go? The knees?"
5. **Dress the twin** (25 minutes). Your child colors in clothes: their favorite outfit, a costume, or something they dream of wearing. Glue on fabric scraps or yarn hair.
6. **The extra.** Your child adds one surprise: a cape, a pet at their feet, a speech bubble with their favorite saying, a magic wand.
7. **Cut it out** (grown-up) or leave it on the paper. Tape it to a door or wall.

## Make it yours
- Make it a "future me" twin: what will you look like as a grown-up?
- Fill the twin with drawings of all your favorite things: foods inside the tummy, songs in the head.
- Make a twin for a stuffed animal too, and compare sizes.

## Stuck?
- Your child wiggles while being traced: trace quickly and fix lines later, or count to 30 together while you trace.
- No big paper: tape together printer paper sheets, or make a half twin from the waist up.

## Show it off
Hang the paper twin on the front door so it greets the family when they come home. Your child can stand next to it.

## Clean up
Roll up scraps and recycle them. Your child caps markers and puts crayons away.

## For the grown-up
This builds body awareness, measurement, self-expression and drawing. It's a lovely keepsake to compare to next year's height. Do any big cutting yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "What's one thing about your paper twin that's just like you?"
