title: Host an Improv Night
grade: 8
order: 23
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 2+
materials: paper, a pencil, index cards, a hat or bowl, a timer or clock, a "stage" area
nice: a few props (a hat, a scarf, a spoon), a doorbell or bell for "freeze"

## The big idea
Improv is acting with no script: you make it up on the spot. The most important rule is "yes, and": accept what your partner says, then add something. You're hosting an improv night for your family or friends, with games, a host's script and audience suggestions.

## Goals
- A show with at least four improv games, run by you as the host.
- A hat full of suggestion cards, used live.

## Objectives
- I can use "yes, and" to build a scene with a partner.
- I can explain the rules of a game clearly to players.
- I can think quickly and stay in character.

## Get ready
Set up a stage area with chairs for the audience. Find your players: a sibling, a friend over, or grown-ups after work. Fill a hat with 20 suggestion cards: places (a submarine, a dentist's office), jobs (a lion tamer), and problems (lost keys).

## Steps
1. **Warm up (5 minutes).** Play "Word at a Time": everyone tells a story, one word each, in a circle.
2. **Learn "yes, and."** Try a scene where your partner says "Look, a dragon!" Instead of "No, that's a dog," say "Yes, and it's eating our car!"
3. **Pick your games.** Some you can play: "Freeze" (two actors play a scene; someone yells "freeze," taps one out, takes their exact pose, and starts a new scene). "Expert" (one actor is an expert on a silly topic from the hat, and the audience asks questions). "Emotion Swap" (actors play a scene, and the host calls out a new emotion every 20 seconds). "Party Guests" (a host guesses each guest's secret identity).
4. **Write your host script.** Write a welcome, then a short explanation for each game, so you can introduce them in your own words.
5. **Rehearse one game.** Run "Expert" with one player to practice your hosting.
6. **Run the show (30 minutes).** Welcome the audience, explain each game, pull suggestions from the hat, and end each scene with a "scene!" and applause.
7. **Grand finale.** End with everyone on stage for one big scene with a suggestion from the youngest audience member.

## Make it yours
- Invent your own improv game with a new rule (everyone must rhyme, or speak only in questions).
- Make it a competition: two teams, and the audience votes on each scene.
- Do an improv "musical": the actors must sing every third line.

## Stuck?
- A scene stalls: the host calls "new problem!" and adds one: "The ship is sinking!"
- Someone's shy: give them a job like timekeeper or suggestion-puller first.
- Players say "no": stop the scene and remind everyone: "yes, and."

## Show it off
That's the show: perform it for the family on a weekend night with snacks.

## Clean up
Return props and chairs, and keep the suggestion hat for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds quick thinking, listening, confidence and collaboration, and it's real drama practice. It's also one of the best ways to help a nervous speaker relax. **Ask at dinner:** "Which improv game got the biggest laugh?"
