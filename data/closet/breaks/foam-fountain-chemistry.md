grade: 6
order: 7
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: High
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: an empty plastic bottle (a 500 ml water bottle is perfect), baking soda, vinegar, dish soap, warm and cold water, measuring spoons and cups, a funnel (or paper rolled into a cone), a baking pan or tray, a ruler, paper and a pencil, towels
nice: food coloring, a second bottle the same size, a kitchen thermometer, packets of dry yeast and sugar for the bonus round

## The big idea
You probably made a baking soda volcano when you were little. This is the grown-up version: a fountain of foam, and a real experiment. Your mission is to find the recipe that makes the tallest, longest-lasting foam tower, changing one thing at a time like an actual chemist.

## Goals
- You run at least four foam trials and measure every one.
- You find the best recipe and explain why it worked.

## Objectives
- I can name the independent variable (the one thing I change) and keep everything else the same.
- I can measure results and record them in a table.
- I can explain that baking soda and vinegar react to make carbon dioxide gas.

## Get ready
Work outside or in a bathtub; this gets foamy. Put the bottle in the middle of a baking pan. Make a results table with columns: Trial, What I Changed, Foam Height (cm), How Long It Lasted (seconds).

## Steps
1. **Baseline trial.** Use the funnel to put 2 tablespoons baking soda into the bottle. Add 1 teaspoon dish soap. Quickly pour in 1/2 cup vinegar. Measure how high the foam rises above the bottle top and how long it keeps flowing.
2. Rinse the bottle. Now pick your first variable to change. Options: more or less soap, warm vs. cold vinegar, more baking soda, mixing the soda with a little water first.
3. **Trial 2.** Change only that one thing. Keep every other amount exactly the same. Measure and record.
4. **Trials 3 and 4** (20 minutes). Test a second variable, or push your first one further (no soap, 1 teaspoon, 1 tablespoon).
5. Look at your table. Which change made the biggest difference? Which barely mattered?
6. Combine your best results into a "champion recipe" and run it once more.
7. Write a one-sentence conclusion: "Adding more ___ made the foam taller because ___."

## Make it yours
- Bonus round: in a separate bottle, mix 1 packet dry yeast, 1 tablespoon sugar, and 1/2 cup warm water with a squirt of soap. It makes a slow foam. Race it against your fast one.
- Try different bottle shapes (wide vs. narrow neck). Does the shape change the height?
- Add a drop of food coloring on each side of the bottle for a two-color fountain.

## Stuck?
- Hardly any foam: you need dish soap. That's what traps the gas into bubbles.
- Results are all over the place: measure with spoons and cups, not by guessing, and pour the vinegar the same speed every time.
- Running low on vinegar: use smaller amounts, like 1 tablespoon soda and 1/4 cup vinegar, for every trial.

## Show it off
Put on a "fountain show": run your champion recipe for the family and show the data table that proves it's the best.

## Clean up
Everything here can go down the drain with lots of water. Rinse the bottle and wipe the tray and the floor.

## For the grown-up
This is a real controlled experiment: one variable at a time, measured results, a conclusion based on data. That is the heart of middle school science. Everything used is kitchen safe, but vinegar stings eyes, so no face over the bottle. Please don't let this grow into mixing household cleaners; that can make dangerous gases. **Ask at dinner:** "What one change made the biggest difference to the foam?"
