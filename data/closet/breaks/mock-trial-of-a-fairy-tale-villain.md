title: Mock Trial of a Fairy Tale Villain
grade: 7
order: 19
kind: Pretend
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 2+
materials: paper, pens, a copy or memory of a fairy tale, stuffed animals or toys as witnesses and jury, a spoon or wooden spoon as a gavel, chairs
nice: a bathrobe or blanket as a judge's robe, a sibling, friend or grown-up after work to play the other lawyer

## The big idea
Order in the court! The Big Bad Wolf is on trial for blowing down two houses. Or maybe it's Jack, on trial for breaking into a giant's castle and stealing a hen. You're a lawyer. Build the case, call witnesses, and let the jury decide.

## Goals
- A full mock trial with opening statements, witnesses and closing arguments.
- A verdict from a jury, and you on the winning side (hopefully).

## Objectives
- I can build an argument using evidence and testimony.
- I can ask questions that bring out the facts I need.
- I can listen to the other side and respond to their points.

## Get ready
Pick a case. Good ones: The Three Pigs v. Wolf, Giant v. Jack, Bears v. Goldilocks (trespassing and porridge theft), or Stepsisters v. Cinderella. Decide who's prosecution (says they're guilty) and who's defense (says not guilty). Your partner takes the other side.

## Steps
1. **Know the facts.** Each lawyer writes down the story's events from their side. The wolf's lawyer might say, "My client had a cold. The sneeze was an accident."
2. **List your witnesses.** Each side picks two. A stuffed animal can be the third pig, a sneezing doctor, or a neighbor who heard everything. Write three questions to ask each one, and what they'll answer.
3. **Make evidence.** Draw exhibits: a "huff-and-puff" meter, a straw sample, a footprint map. Label them Exhibit A, B, C.
4. **Write an opening statement (10 minutes).** One minute long. Example: "Ladies and gentlemen of the jury, the evidence will show that the defendant planned this. He brought a bib."
5. **Hold the trial (30 minutes).** The judge (a grown-up, or switch off) bangs the gavel. Openings, then witnesses for each side. After each witness, the other lawyer gets to cross-examine: "Isn't it true you were eating porridge in someone else's house?"
6. **Closing arguments.** Sum up your best three pieces of evidence.
7. **The verdict.** The jury (family members or stuffed animals with a coin flip for each) decides. The judge announces the sentence: maybe the wolf has to rebuild every house in brick.

## Make it yours
- Hold an appeal: the loser gets a second trial with one new witness.
- Put a modern villain on trial: the person who left the empty milk carton in the fridge.
- Write the court newspaper's front page story about the verdict.

## Stuck?
- One side is way stronger: give the weaker side a surprise witness.
- Running out of things to say: ask "why" and "how do you know" questions to witnesses.
- No partner yet: prepare both sides yourself, then put the trial on when someone gets home.

## Show it off
Invite the family to be the jury and run the full trial in the living room.

## Clean up
Return the stuffed animals, chairs and robe, and keep your case file in case of an appeal.

## For the grown-up
This builds argument with evidence (a key skill in English and social studies), public speaking, and listening to the other side. It's also a fun way to talk about how real trials work. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the best piece of evidence for your side?"
