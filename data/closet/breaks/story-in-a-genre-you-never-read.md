title: Story in a Genre You Never Read
grade: 9
order: 2
kind: Story
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper or a notebook, a pen, a die or a slip-of-paper draw
nice: an index card for each trope, a timer

## The big idea
Every reader has a comfort genre. This is the day you leave it. You'll roll for a genre you'd never pick, study how it works from the inside, and then write a scene so good a fan of that genre would think you were one of them. Bonus points for breaking one of its rules on purpose.

## Goals
- A finished scene of 800 to 1,500 words in a genre new to you.
- A trope list you built yourself, with one trope you flipped.

## Objectives
- I can name the conventions (the expected moves) of a genre.
- I can use a convention on purpose and subvert another one.
- I can control tone through word choice and sentence length.

## Get ready
Roll a die or draw a slip: 1 western, 2 cozy mystery, 3 space opera, 4 gothic horror, 5 heist, 6 sports underdog. If you already love the one you rolled, roll again. Get paper and something to drink.

## Steps
1. Brainstorm the genre's **tropes**: what always shows up? For a western: a dusty town, a stranger who rides in, a showdown at noon, a code of honor. Write at least 8 (10 minutes).
2. Note the **tone** tools. Gothic horror uses long, heavy sentences and decaying houses. A heist uses short, fast sentences and a ticking clock. Write three style rules for yourself.
3. Pick the trope you'll **subvert**: the heist crew's genius is a grandmother, the space opera's chosen one refuses the job.
4. Build a character in five lines: name, want, fear, one weird habit, one secret.
5. Outline one scene: where it opens, what goes wrong, and the last line. One scene, not a whole book.
6. Draft fast without stopping to fix (35 minutes). If you get stuck, add a genre trope and keep going.
7. Revise one pass. Cut your weakest sentence on each page, and make your first line pull harder.
8. Title it the way the genre would: a western wants something like "Dust at Kettle Creek."

## Make it yours
- Mash two genres: roll twice and write a gothic horror heist.
- Write the same scene twice, once straight and once as a parody, and compare what changed.
- Tell it from the point of view of the side character the genre usually ignores.

## Stuck?
- Blank page: start in the middle of action, with a line of dialogue.
- It sounds fake: read one paragraph aloud and replace any phrase you've heard a thousand times.

## Show it off
Read it aloud to someone at home without naming the genre first. Can they guess it from the first page?

## Clean up
Keep your trope list in your notebook. It works for the next genre too.

## For the grown-up
Writing in an unfamiliar genre teaches the craft ideas English class names: genre, convention, tone and point of view. It stretches a reader as much as a writer. **Ask at dinner:** "Which rule of that genre did you break, and did it work?"
