title: Cardboard Loom Weaving
grade: 4
order: 8
kind: Art
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1
materials: a piece of cardboard about the size of a sheet of paper, a ruler, a pencil, scissors, yarn or string, strips of old cloth or ribbon, tape, a plastic fork or a paper clip as a needle
nice: a large plastic needle, a stick to hang it on

## The big idea
People have woven cloth for thousands of years. Your loom will be a piece of cardboard. Weave over, under, over, under and watch a pattern grow. Make a tiny rug, a bookmark or a wall hanging.

## Goals
- You build a cardboard loom.
- You weave a piece with at least 3 colors or textures.

## Objectives
- I can follow an over-under pattern.
- I can plan stripes and color patterns.
- I can finish my weaving so it doesn't come apart.

## Get ready
Mark dots every half inch along the top and bottom of the cardboard. Cut a short slit at each dot, about half an inch deep.

## Steps
1. Warp it. The warp is the set of strings that go up and down. Hook yarn in the first slit and tape the end on the back.
2. Run the yarn down to the bottom slit, then back up to the next top slit. Keep going across.
3. Tape the end on the back. Keep the warp tight, not stretched.
4. Weave row one. Tie yarn to your fork or paper clip. Go over, under, over, under.
5. Row two goes the opposite way: under, over, under, over.
6. Push each row down gently with your fingers.
7. Change colors or materials (30 minutes). Try cloth strips, ribbon, or yarn. Make stripes.
8. Finish. Flip the loom. Cut the warp strings in the middle on the back. Tie pairs together in knots.

## Make it yours
- Weave in a feather or a long leaf for nature texture.
- Make a tiny rug for a toy or doll house.
- Make a pattern: 3 rows blue, 1 row white, repeat.

## Stuck?
- The sides pull in: leave a little loop at the end of each row.
- You lost your pattern: look at the row below. Do the opposite.
- The warp slips out: tape it tighter on the back.

## Show it off
Hang it on a stick by the door so the family sees it first thing.

## Clean up
Wind leftover yarn into a ball. Put scissors away.

## For the grown-up
Weaving builds patterns, counting, focus and fine motor skills. It's also great for math: patterns and alternating sequences. Stay nearby for scissors. **Ask at dinner:** "What pattern did you weave, and what would you try next?"
