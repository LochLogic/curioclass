kind: Build
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a large cardboard box (at least 40 cm on each side), a smaller box or cardboard sheets, 3 paper cups or a sheet of cardstock for the claw, string, drinking straws, 2 long pencils or dowels, tape, glue, scissors or a craft knife, a ruler, small prizes (wrapped candy, small toys, folded paper notes)
nice: a hot glue gun, clear plastic from food packaging for a window, rubber bands, paper clips

## The big idea
Claw machines are famous for being nearly impossible to win. Yours will be fairer, or just as sneaky, your call. You'll engineer a working claw that opens and closes on a pull string, a gantry that moves it left and right, and a cabinet with a prize chute. Then you get to decide how hard to make it.

## Goals
- A cabinet with a claw that can move, drop, grab and lift a prize.
- A prize chute where won prizes slide out.

## Objectives
- I can build a **tendon-driven** gripper, where pulling a string closes the fingers.
- I can design a sliding mechanism that moves along a rail.
- I can test and adjust a machine until it works reliably.

## Get ready
Clear a big table. Cut away the front of the large box (leave a 3 cm frame) so you can see inside. Gather everything into one spot.

## Steps
1. **The claw** (25 minutes). Cut three fingers from cardstock, each 2 cm wide and 10 cm long. Score each one in two places to make knuckles. Tape a short straw piece along the inside of each finger segment, then thread a string through each finger's straws and tie it at the tip.
2. Tape the three fingers around a paper cup (the palm), pointing down. Gather the three strings through a hole in the cup's bottom and tie them to one pull string. Pull: the fingers curl. Let go: rubber bands on the outside of each finger pull them open.
3. **The gantry.** Push two pencils or dowels across the top of the box, front to back, as rails. Make a cardboard "trolley" with two straw sleeves that slide along a rail.
4. Hang the claw from the trolley on a second string, the lift string, that runs up through the trolley and out the top of the cabinet.
5. **The chute.** Make a sloped cardboard ramp in one front corner leading to a hole in the box's side. Build a low wall around it so prizes don't slide in by accident.
6. Fill the floor with prizes. Test the full cycle: move the trolley, lower the claw, close, lift, move to the chute, open (25 minutes).
7. Tune it. Too weak? Add a fourth finger or rubber grip strips. Too easy? Shorten the fingers or use slippery prizes.

## Make it yours
- Add a coin slot that only lets the trolley move after a coin drops in and trips a cardboard lever.
- Design it as an arcade for a story world: a claw machine on a pirate ship that sells "treasure."
- Make it two-player: two claws on two rails racing for the same prize.

## Stuck?
- The claw won't close all the way: the strings are rubbing. Use wider straws or put a dab of soap on the string.
- The trolley jams: sand or tape the rails smooth, and make the straw sleeves slightly wider than the rails.

## Show it off
Open the arcade after dinner. Each family member gets three tries, and the winner keeps the prize.

## Clean up
Pick up every cardboard scrap and keep the machine somewhere it won't get crushed.

## For the grown-up
This is mechanical engineering: linkages, tendons like the ones in our hands, and iterative testing, all part of engineering and physics. If they use a craft knife or glue gun, it's with your OK and on a cutting surface. **Ask at dinner:** "How does pulling one string close three fingers?"
