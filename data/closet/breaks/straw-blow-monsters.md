title: Straw Blow Monsters
grade: 1
order: 25
kind: Art
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: High
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: white paper (thicker is better), watercolor or washable paint thinned with water, drinking straws, a spoon, markers or crayons, newspaper, paper towels, an old shirt
nice: googly eyes and glue, a dropper, a hair dryer on cool (grown-up only)

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Inside every puddle of paint, a monster is hiding. To find it, you have to blow it out with a straw! Every monster comes out different: spiky, wobbly, long, or with ten legs. What will yours look like?"

## Goals
- Your child makes at least 4 straw-blown paint monsters.
- Your child names each monster and writes or tells one fact about it.

## Objectives
- Your child can control their breath to move paint (soft blow, hard blow).
- Your child can look at a random shape and imagine what it could be.
- Your child can add details to show a character's personality.

## Get ready
Put on the old shirt and cover the table with newspaper. Mix paint with water until it is thin like juice. Give your child their own straw.

## Steps
1. **Practice blowing.** Your child blows through the straw onto their hand: soft, then hard. Remind them: only blow out, never sip.
2. **Drop the blob** (5 minutes). Put a spoonful of paint in the middle of the paper.
3. **Blow the monster** (15 minutes). Your child points the straw at the blob and blows. The paint runs out in wiggly lines like legs and spikes. Blow in different directions. Try a second color on top.
4. **Let it dry.** Make 3 more while the first dries. Try one with lots of paint, one with little.
5. **Find the monster.** Turn each painting around. Ask: "Where is its face? Is that an arm or a tail?"
6. **Bring it to life** (15 minutes). Your child adds eyes (lots of eyes!), a mouth, teeth, feet, hats, with markers.
7. **Name and describe.** Your child names each monster and says one thing about it: "This is Splorp. He eats socks." Write it under the picture.

## Make it yours
- Make a monster family: mom, dad, kid, baby and a monster pet.
- Blow a "monster world" in a big painting with trees and paint-river roads.
- Write a short story where two monsters meet and become friends.

## Stuck?
- The paint won't move: add more water, and blow closer to the paint with a short, strong puff.
- Your child gets dizzy: take a break and breathe normally. Short puffs are better than long ones.

## Show it off
Make a "Monster Museum" on the fridge with the names and facts, and give the family a tour.

## Clean up
Throw away the straws and newspaper. Your child wipes the table and washes paint cups.

## For the grown-up
This builds breath control, fine motor skills and imagination, and naming the monsters builds vocabulary and early writing. Make sure your child only blows out and never sips; washable paint is safest. Let them rest if they feel lightheaded. **Ask at dinner:** "Tell me about your scariest (or silliest) monster."
