title: Paper Fortune Teller
grade: 3
order: 14
kind: Game
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 3 square sheets of paper (or regular paper cut square), markers or crayons, scissors, a pencil
nice: colored paper, stickers

## The big idea
Pick a color. Pick a number. Lift the flap. Your future is waiting! Fold a paper fortune teller and fill it with funny fortunes, dares or jokes. Then tell everyone's future.

## Goals
- You fold a fortune teller that opens and closes.
- You make at least 2 with different themes.

## Objectives
- I can follow folding steps in order.
- I can write short, funny sentences.
- I can count and spell to play the game.

## Get ready
To make a square, fold the top corner of a paper down to the side. Cut off the strip at the bottom. Unfold. You have a square.

## Steps
1. Fold the square corner to corner both ways. Unfold. You see an X.
2. Fold all 4 corners into the middle point. Now it is a smaller square.
3. Flip it over. Fold all 4 new corners into the middle again.
4. Fold it in half. Slide your thumbs and pointer fingers under the 4 flaps. Push them up to make points.
5. Color the 4 outside flaps 4 colors.
6. Write numbers 1 to 8 on the inside triangles.
7. Write 8 fortunes under the flaps (15 minutes). Make them funny! "You will meet a talking sandwich." "A dog will tell you a secret."
8. Play: Ask someone to pick a color. Spell it, opening each way. They pick a number. Count it. Then open the flap.

## Make it yours
- Make a dare teller: "Hop on one foot 10 times."
- Make a joke teller with a riddle under each flap.
- Make one for your pet, with fortunes about treats and naps.

## Stuck?
- It won't open: unfold it and fold each crease harder with your nail.
- The paper rips: use thinner paper and fold slowly.
- Fortunes won't fit: write tiny, or use one word and a picture.

## Show it off
Tell each person's fortune at dinner. Save the funniest one for last.

## Clean up
Throw away scraps. Keep your fortune tellers in a book so they stay flat.

## For the grown-up
Folding builds following directions, careful hands and early geometry (squares, triangles, halves). Writing fortunes is short, playful writing. **Ask at dinner:** "What fortune did you like writing best?"
