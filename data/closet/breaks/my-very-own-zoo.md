title: My Very Own Zoo
grade: k
order: 24
kind: Build
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: stuffed animals or toy animals, shoeboxes and other small boxes, blocks, paper, crayons, tape, scissors (grown-up only), bowls, a towel or blue paper for water, green paper or a green towel for grass
nice: craft sticks for fences, toilet paper tubes, a toy cash register for the ticket booth

## The big idea
Read aloud: "You are the zookeeper of a brand-new zoo! The animals have arrived, but they have nowhere to live. The penguins need cold water, the lions need sun, the monkeys need trees. Let's build them homes."

## Goals
- Your child builds a zoo with at least 4 animal homes.
- Your child gives a zoo tour and feeds the animals.

## Objectives
- Your child can match an animal to a home it needs (water, trees, a cave).
- Your child can sort animals into groups (by size, by where they live, by legs).
- Your child can explain a zoo rule and why it matters.

## Get ready
Pick a corner of the floor. Gather all the animals in a basket. Set out boxes, blocks and the towels.

## Steps
1. **Sort the animals.** Your child sorts them into groups: animals that swim, animals that climb, big animals, small animals. Let them choose how.
2. Ask: "What does each animal need to be happy?" Talk about water, food, shade, room to run.
3. **Build the homes** (30 minutes). Your child picks one box or block area for each group. A blue towel is a pond. Tubes stand up as trees. A box on its side is a cave. Blocks make fences.
4. **Signs.** Your child draws a sign for each home. You write the names: "Bear Cave," "Fish Pond."
5. **The ticket booth.** Make tickets from paper squares and a map of the zoo on one sheet.
6. **Feeding time** (10 minutes). Your child decides what each animal eats and "feeds" them with paper food or blocks.
7. **Zoo rules.** Your child makes 2 rules for visitors ("Don't feed the lions!") and you write them on a sign.

## Make it yours
- Make it a dinosaur park, a dragon sanctuary or a zoo for imaginary animals.
- Have an animal escape! Your child has to find it and build a better fence.
- Add a zoo hospital, a baby animal nursery or a gift shop.

## Stuck?
- The fences keep falling: lay blocks flat in a line instead of stacking them, or use tape on the floor.
- Too many animals: pick 4 favorites for the first homes, and add more later.

## Show it off
Sell tickets to the family when they get home and lead them on a zoo tour.

## Clean up
Animals go to bed in their basket. Your child stacks the boxes inside each other.

## For the grown-up
This builds sorting and classifying, animal habitats (a kindergarten science idea), planning and early map making. It is a great project to leave up for a few days and add to. **Ask at dinner:** "Which animal in your zoo has the best home, and why?"
