title: Finger Knit a Scarf
grade: 10
order: 25
kind: Art
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 2 or 3 old T-shirts you're allowed to cut up (or yarn if you have it), sharp scissors, a ruler
nice: a large-eye needle, different-colored shirts for stripes, a friend to knit with

## The big idea
You can knit with just your hands, no needles. And you can make yarn from old T-shirts: cut them into a long strip, stretch it, and it curls into a soft, chunky cord. You'll turn old clothes into a thick, cozy scarf, learning a craft that's thousands of years old.

## Goals
- T-shirt yarn cut from old shirts.
- A finished finger-knit scarf at least 100 cm long.

## Objectives
- I can cut a continuous strip of T-shirt yarn.
- I can finger knit using the over-and-under weave.
- I can join new yarn and finish the ends so they don't unravel.

## Get ready
Get permission for the shirts you're cutting. Lay one flat on a table and cut off the hem and everything above the armpits, so you have a tube.

## Steps
1. **Cut the yarn** (20 minutes). Fold the tube in half, side to side, but leave 5 cm unfolded at the top. Cut strips 2.5 cm wide from the folded edge up to (not through) the unfolded part. Open the tube. Cut diagonally across the uncut part, from the first strip to the second, and so on. You'll get one long strip.
2. **Stretch it.** Pull the strip a little at a time. The edges curl in and it becomes "yarn."
3. **Cast on.** Hold your left hand palm up. Tuck the yarn tail between your thumb and first finger. Weave the yarn over and under your four fingers, then back again, so each finger has one loop.
4. **Knit a row.** Weave over and under again, so each finger has two loops. Starting with your pinky, lift the bottom loop over the top loop and off the back of your finger. Do this for all four.
5. **Repeat** (45 minutes). Keep weaving and lifting. Pull the knitting down the back of your hand every few rows. Count rows to keep it even.
6. **Join new yarn**: tie the end of a new strip to the old one with a tight knot, and keep going. Hide the knot on the back.
7. **Bind off.** When it's long enough, move the loop from your pinky to your ring finger and lift the bottom loop over. Keep moving to your first finger. Cut the yarn, pull the tail through the last loop and tighten.

## Make it yours
- Stripe it with shirts of two or three colors.
- Make a matching headband with the leftovers.
- Knit two fingers wide for a thin "rope," and braid three of them into a thicker scarf.

## Stuck?
- Loops are too tight: relax your hand, and pull the knitting down more often.
- The yarn breaks: T-shirt yarn tears at seams. Knot it and keep going.

## Show it off
Wear it at dinner and tell everyone which shirts it was made from.

## Clean up
Sweep up fabric scraps, and save the bits for stuffing a pillow or toy.

## For the grown-up
Finger knitting builds fine motor skill, patterns and patience, plus reuse of old clothing. Scissors used on fabric should be sharp. **Ask at dinner:** "How many old shirts became your scarf?"
