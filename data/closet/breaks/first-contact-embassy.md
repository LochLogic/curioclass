title: First Contact Embassy
grade: 10
order: 19
kind: Pretend
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, index cards, pens or markers, a table and two chairs, a few household objects to trade (a spoon, a cup, a coin, a pinecone, a sock), clothes from your closet for a costume (with permission)
nice: a lamp to dim the room, a bell or pot lid, a second person to play the other side

## The big idea
A ship has landed in the park, and the beings inside don't speak any human language. Earth has sent one envoy to the first meeting: you. You'll invent an alien culture with its own customs and taboos, then run a first-contact meeting where neither side can use words the other understands. One wrong gesture could end the talks. One right one could start a friendship between worlds.

## Goals
- A one-page "culture file" for an invented alien people, with at least 5 customs.
- A first-contact meeting played out in character, ending in a signed (or drawn) agreement.

## Objectives
- I can build a believable culture with values, customs and a reason behind each one.
- I can communicate without shared words, using gestures, objects and drawings.
- I can stay in character and adapt when the other side surprises me.

## Get ready
Set the table as a neutral meeting ground: two chairs facing each other, a blank sheet of paper in the middle. Put your trade objects in a bag. If someone at home will play, give them 10 minutes alone with a blank culture file to invent their own side.

## Steps
1. **Invent the aliens** (20 minutes). Start with their world: is it dark, underwater, crowded, freezing? Then write 5 customs that follow from it. A dark world might mean light is rude, so a bright lamp is an insult. A crowded world might mean standing close is friendly.
2. Add **one taboo** (something they never do) and **one sacred thing** (something they treasure). Keep both secret from the other player.
3. **Invent a greeting** and three signals: yes, no and "I don't understand." Practice them until they feel natural.
4. **Write Earth's goals** on a card: three things you want from the meeting (peace, trade, a visit). Write one thing you won't give up.
5. **Hold the meeting** (30 minutes). No real words allowed. Use gestures, sounds, drawings on the paper and the trade objects. If you're solo, play both sides, switching chairs and keeping each side's secrets on its own card.
6. **Handle the crisis.** At some point, someone will break a taboo by accident. Find a way to repair it without words.
7. **Make an agreement.** Draw a treaty both sides can "sign" with a symbol: a picture of what each side gives and gets.
8. **Debrief out of character.** Reveal your secret customs and compare what each side thought was happening.

## Make it yours
- Make the aliens communicate only in rhythms, tapped on the table, and build a shared code as you go.
- Write the meeting up afterward as two reports: Earth's version and the aliens' version of the same event.
- Hold a second meeting a "year later," where both sides now know a few signals and the stakes are higher.

## Stuck?
- The meeting stalls: offer an object and act out what it's for. Trade is the oldest way to start talking.
- Solo play feels flat: give each side a different posture and sound, so switching chairs switches who you are.

## Show it off
Run a short meeting with a family member as a "volunteer envoy" who hasn't seen your culture file. See how long they take to figure out your taboo.

## Clean up
Return the trade objects and costume pieces. Keep the culture file and treaty together.

## For the grown-up
This is drama with real anthropology and communication ideas in it: how cultures form, how misunderstandings happen and how people repair them. It builds empathy, nonverbal communication and creative worldbuilding. **Ask at dinner:** "What custom did your aliens have, and what would have offended them?"
