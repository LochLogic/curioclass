title: Jar Terrarium
grade: 3
order: 8
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1
materials: a big clear jar or plastic bottle with the top cut off, small pebbles, soil, a spoon, small plants or moss or seeds, water in a cup, newspaper to work on
nice: a small toy for a tiny world, a spray bottle, activated charcoal from a fish store

## The big idea
What if you could hold a whole tiny world in your hands? A terrarium is a mini garden in a jar. It makes its own rain! Build one, and give it a tiny story too.

## Goals
- You build a living mini garden in a jar.
- You see water form on the jar walls like rain.

## Objectives
- I can build layers that help plants grow.
- I can tell what plants need to live.
- I can watch and write down what changes.

## Get ready
Lay newspaper on a table or work outside. Wash the jar. With a grown-up, go to a yard or park to dig a little soil. Find moss, small weeds or tiny plants.

## Steps
1. Make the bottom layer. Put pebbles in the jar about 1 inch deep. This catches extra water.
2. Add soil. Spoon in soil about 2 inches deep. Pat it flat.
3. Plan your world (10 minutes). Where do the plants go? Will there be a hill, a path, a pond of pebbles?
4. Plant. Poke a hole with your finger. Set the plant in. Press soil around it.
5. Add your extras: a flat rock, a twig log, a small toy.
6. Water a little. Use about 3 spoons of water. Do not flood it.
7. Close the lid or cover it with plastic wrap. Set it in bright light, but not hot sun.
8. Draw your terrarium. Check it tomorrow. Do you see drops on the glass? That is the water cycle!

## Make it yours
- Make it a dinosaur land or a fairy village with a tiny sign.
- Plant seeds from the kitchen, like a bean, and watch it sprout.
- Make two jars. Put one in light and one in a darker spot. Which grows better?

## Stuck?
- No plants outside: use seeds, or a cutting from a houseplant (ask first).
- The glass gets very foggy: open the lid for an hour.
- Plants droop: check if the soil is too dry or too wet.

## Show it off
Give your world a name. Show it to the family and tell them who lives there.

## Clean up
Shake the newspaper into the trash or yard. Wash the spoon and your hands.

## For the grown-up
This builds life science ideas: what plants need, layers, and the water cycle. Go with your child to collect soil and plants, and have them wash hands after. If you cut a bottle, do the cutting yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "Where does the water in the jar go, and where does it come back?"
