night_before: Ask your grown-up to call or email a local senior home or care center and ask if they accept letters from young people, and how they'd like them addressed. Many love "Dear Friend" letters.

## The big idea
Some people living in senior homes go days without getting any mail. A letter from a stranger who took the time to write can be the best part of their week. Write a stack of letters that make someone smile, laugh, and maybe write back.

## Goals
- At least four handwritten letters, each with a drawing or decoration.
- A package ready to mail or drop off.

## Objectives
- I can write a friendly letter that's personal but keeps my private details private.
- I can ask questions that invite someone to share their story.
- I can think about what someone else might enjoy reading.

## Get ready
Clear a table and set out your supplies. Write a list of things you could share: a funny thing your pet did, your favorite food, a book you love, what the weather's like, something you're learning.

## Steps
1. **Plan a great letter.** Strong letters have a friendly opening, two or three short stories or details about you, a question, and a warm goodbye.
2. **Write letter one (15 minutes).** Start with "Dear Friend." Example: "My name is Sam and I'm in seventh grade. This week I tried to teach my dog to sit. Instead she taught me to give her treats." Sign it with your first name only.
3. **Ask good questions.** People love sharing memories. Try: "What was your favorite game when you were my age?" or "What's the best advice anyone ever gave you?"
4. **Vary the letters.** Make each one different. One could be all jokes, one could describe your favorite place, one could include a short poem, one could be a "would you rather" with five questions.
5. **Decorate.** Add a drawing, a border, or a pressed leaf. Write big and clear so it's easy to read for people with weaker eyesight.
6. **Make envelopes.** Fold a sheet of paper around each letter and tape it, or use real envelopes. Write "For a Friend" on the front with a small drawing.
7. **Pack them up** in a big envelope with a note to the staff saying who they're from.

## Make it yours
- Make a "Postcard from my Town" series: draw a local place on one side and write about it on the back.
- Write a short story in parts and send one chapter in each letter.
- Make a puzzle letter: a word search or a riddle with the answer upside down.

## Stuck?
- Not sure what to say: describe your day in detail. Normal life is interesting to someone who isn't living it.
- Worried it's boring: add a question at the end. Questions make a letter feel like a conversation.
- No senior home nearby: write to an older neighbor or relative instead.

## Show it off
Read your favorite letter to your family before you seal it.

## Clean up
Put away the pens and keep leftover paper for when replies arrive.

## For the grown-up
This is kindness in action and real writing practice: audience, voice and the friendly letter. Please make the first contact with the care home and handle the return address. First names only from your child keeps their details private. **Ask at dinner:** "What question did you ask in your letters, and what do you hope they write back?"
