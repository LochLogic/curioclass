title: Pop-Up Card Engineering
grade: 6
order: 22
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: heavy paper or thin cardboard (cut-up cereal boxes covered with paper work), regular paper, scissors, glue stick, a ruler, a pencil, colored pencils or markers
nice: colored paper, a bone folder or the back of a spoon for crisp folds

## The big idea
Open a pop-up card and a castle springs up, a dragon lunges out, or a shark leaps from the fold. Paper engineers design these using a few clever folds. Learn three pop-up mechanisms, test them, then combine them into one card that makes someone say "Whoa."

## Goals
- You build three test pop-ups, each with a different mechanism.
- You design a final pop-up card with a scene that jumps out when opened.

## Objectives
- I can build a box fold, a V-fold, and a layered pop-up.
- I can measure and fold paper so a pop-up opens and closes flat.
- I can plan a 3D scene in layers, front to back.

## Get ready
Cut four pieces of heavy paper about 15 by 20 cm. Fold each in half the short way. Three are for tests, one is your final card.

## Steps
1. **Box fold.** On a folded test card, cut two parallel slits 3 cm long and 3 cm apart into the fold. Fold the flap between them back and forth, then open the card and push the flap through to the inside. It pops out as a box.
2. **V-fold.** Fold a small paper rectangle in half. Glue one side to the left page of a test card and the other to the right page, with its fold pointing toward the card's fold at an angle. Open slowly; it stands up like a tent.
3. **Layered pop-up.** Glue a drawing to the front of a box fold, then add a second box behind it with another drawing. Now you have a foreground and background.
4. Test each one five times. Do they close flat? Any tearing? Fix and note what worked.
5. Plan your final card (10 minutes). What's the scene? A volcano erupting, a monster under a bed, a city skyline, a birthday cake? Sketch which parts use which mechanism.
6. Build the final card (25 minutes). Draw and cut the pop-up pieces separately, then glue them onto the boxes and V-folds.
7. Glue the pop-up card inside a second folded cover card so the cuts don't show. Decorate the outside.

## Make it yours
- Add a pull-tab: a paper strip that makes something slide or wave when you pull it.
- Make a pop-up book with three pages that tell a short story.
- Design a card with a hidden surprise: a pop-up behind a flap that only appears on second look.

## Stuck?
- It won't close flat: your cuts are probably uneven. Measure both slits from the fold.
- The paper tears: use heavier paper, or reinforce the fold with a strip of tape on the back.
- The pop-up sticks out of the card: make the cuts shorter.

## Show it off
Give your card to someone for no reason, or for a birthday coming up. Watch their face when it opens.

## Clean up
Pick up all paper scraps, put the glue lid on, and put scissors away.

## For the grown-up
Pop-ups are a mix of art and geometry (parallel lines, angles, measuring) and design thinking. Expect a few failed tests; they're part of the engineering process. **Ask at dinner:** "Which pop-up trick was the hardest to get right?"
