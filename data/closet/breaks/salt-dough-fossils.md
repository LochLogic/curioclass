title: Salt Dough Fossils
grade: 5
order: 6
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: 2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, 3/4 cup water, a bowl, a spoon, a rolling pin or a smooth bottle, "fossil makers" (shells, leaves, a fork, toy animals, chicken bones that were boiled clean), a plate, paper, a pencil
nice: tea or coffee to brush on for an "old" look, a paintbrush, a magnifying glass

## The big idea
Millions of years ago, a creature stepped in soft mud. The mud hardened. Today, a scientist finds the print. That is a fossil! Today you make a set of fossils. Then someone has to guess what made each one.

## Goals
- You make at least six fossils: some prints and some "body" fossils.
- A family member tries to identify them like a real scientist.

## Objectives
- I can explain how a trace fossil (a print, like a footprint) forms.
- I can make a fossil and write a clue card for it.
- I can use clues to figure out what made a print.

## Get ready
Clear the table and dust it with a little flour. Wash your hands. Gather your fossil makers on a plate.

## Steps
1. Make the dough. Mix the flour and salt. Add the water a little at a time. Knead for 5 minutes until it is smooth, like soft clay.
2. Roll it out about 1 cm thick. Cut or tear it into six pieces, each about the size of your palm.
3. Make trace fossils. Walk a toy animal across one piece. Press a leaf into another. Drag a fork for "claw marks."
4. Make body fossils. Press a shell or a clean bone deep into the dough, then lift it out carefully.
5. Invent one fossil from a creature that never existed. Use a fork, a bottle cap, and a pencil tip to make its prints.
6. Write a clue card for each one (15 minutes). Example: "Three toes. Long stride. Probably ran fast."
7. Let them dry on a plate for two days. Or a grown-up can bake them at 120 C (250 F) for about 2 hours.

## Make it yours
- Make a "dig site." Bury dry fossils in a bin of sand or rice and dig them up with a spoon and brush.
- Tell a story with prints: a small creature's tracks, then a big one's, then only the big one's.
- Make a museum label for each fossil with a made-up age: "Found in layer 4. About 70 million years old."

## Stuck?
- The dough is sticky: add flour one spoon at a time.
- The prints are blurry: press straight down and lift straight up. Don't wiggle.
- Fossils crack while drying: they still look real. Real fossils crack too!

## Show it off
Set out your fossils with the clue cards face down. Can your family match each fossil to what made it?

## Clean up
Scrape dough bits into the trash, not the sink. Wipe the table and wash the bowl.

## For the grown-up
This is earth science: how fossils form, and how scientists read evidence. If you bake them, the oven is yours to run; this sheet is fine to air-dry instead. Salt dough is not food, so keep it away from pets (lots of salt is harmful to dogs). **Ask at dinner:** "What could a scientist tell about an animal just from its footprints?"
