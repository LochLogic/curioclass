title: Rainbow Fruit Kabobs
grade: 1
order: 13
kind: Kitchen
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: fruit in many colors (strawberries or watermelon for red, oranges or cantaloupe for orange, banana or pineapple for yellow, kiwi or green grapes for green, blueberries for blue, purple grapes for purple), a cutting board, a sharp knife (grown-up only), a butter knife, wooden skewers with the points snipped off or drinking straws, plates, paper and crayons
nice: yogurt for dipping, a cookie cutter for melon shapes

## The big idea
Read aloud: "A rainbow fell into the kitchen! Can you catch it and put it on a stick? We'll find a fruit for every color of the rainbow and make snacks so pretty, everyone will want one."

## Goals
- Your child makes rainbow fruit kabobs for the family.
- Your child invents and names a pattern kabob of their own.

## Objectives
- Your child can name the rainbow colors in order: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple.
- Your child can cut soft fruit with a butter knife.
- Your child can make and repeat a color pattern.

## Get ready
Wash hands and fruit. Cut hard fruit (melon, pineapple) into bite-size chunks (grown-up). Leave soft fruit (banana, kiwi) for your child to cut. Snip the sharp tips off wooden skewers or use straws. Set out a bowl for each color.

## Steps
1. **Rainbow check.** Ask: "What colors are in a rainbow?" Your child draws a rainbow on paper to use as a guide.
2. **Sort the fruit.** Your child puts each fruit in the bowl that matches its color. Which color has the most?
3. **Chef's job** (10 minutes). Your child cuts the banana and peeled kiwi into rounds with a butter knife. Show how: hold the fruit with fingers curled, like a claw, and saw gently.
4. **First kabob** (10 minutes). Your child slides fruit onto a skewer in rainbow order: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple.
5. **Your pattern.** Your child invents a new pattern: red, green, red, green, or big, small, big, small. Name it ("The Traffic Light," "The Watermelon Snake").
6. **Make a tray** (15 minutes). Your child makes one kabob for each person at home and lines them up on a plate like a rainbow.
7. Add a little cup of yogurt for dipping if you have it.

## Make it yours
- Make fruit creatures: a caterpillar of grapes, a snowman of melon balls.
- Make a "mystery kabob": your child closes eyes and you slide on fruit; they guess each one by taste.
- Open a kabob shop and give each kabob a price and a fun name.

## Stuck?
- Fruit slides off: put a firm fruit like melon or a grape at each end to hold it.
- Missing a color: use what you have, or skip it and make a "half rainbow."

## Show it off
Serve the rainbow tray as a snack or dessert when the family gets home. Your child explains the patterns.

## Clean up
Your child wipes the counter, puts peels in the compost or trash and washes the bowls.

## For the grown-up
This builds color words, patterns (a first-grade math idea), counting and safe kitchen skills like the claw grip with a butter knife. Do all sharp-knife cutting yourself, snip skewer points, and cut grapes and blueberries in half for younger siblings. Check allergies. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the name of the pattern you invented?"
