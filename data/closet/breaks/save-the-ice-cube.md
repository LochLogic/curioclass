title: Save the Ice Cube
grade: 4
order: 20
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 4 ice cubes the same size, 4 small containers or zip bags, insulation materials (foil, socks, cotton balls, newspaper, a towel), tape, a clock, a plate, paper and a pencil
nice: a ruler, a kitchen scale

## The big idea
A penguin's ice cube is melting! You have one hour to protect it. Wrap ice cubes in different materials to find the best insulator, something that slows down heat.

## Goals
- You protect 3 ice cubes with 3 different wraps.
- You find which wrap kept the ice frozen longest.

## Objectives
- I can explain what insulation does.
- I can set up a fair test with a control, one cube with no wrap.
- I can record results and compare them.

## Get ready
Get 4 ice cubes the same size. Set them on a plate in the freezer until you're ready.

## Steps
1. Plan 3 wraps. Example: cube 1 in a sock, cube 2 in foil, cube 3 in newspaper.
2. Cube 4 gets no wrap. That's your control.
3. Put each cube in its own bag or container. Then wrap it.
4. Set all 4 in the same spot. Write the start time.
5. Predict. Which one will last longest? Write your guess.
6. Check every 15 minutes (60 minutes). Peek inside. Write what you see.
7. After 1 hour, unwrap them all. Line them up. Which cube is biggest?
8. Write your result. "The best insulator was ___ because ___."

## Make it yours
- Design a "penguin house" from everything you learned.
- Try a two-layer wrap, like foil and then a sock.
- Race a family member. Whose ice cube survives longest?

## Stuck?
- They all melt fast: move them away from sunny windows.
- Hard to compare: weigh the leftover ice or measure the water.
- No zip bags: use cups with plates on top.

## Show it off
Show the family your results chart. Explain which wrap you'd use to keep a drink cold.

## Clean up
Pour out water. Dry the wraps. Put everything back.

## For the grown-up
This builds understanding of heat transfer and insulation, plus fair testing with a control. **Ask at dinner:** "What do you think keeps our food cold in a lunch bag?"
