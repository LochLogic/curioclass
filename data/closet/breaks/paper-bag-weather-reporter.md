title: Paper Bag Weather Reporter
grade: k
order: 21
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a paper grocery bag, scissors (grown-up only), crayons or markers, paper, tape, a wooden spoon or paper towel tube (microphone), a window or a door to the outside
nice: aluminum foil for the microphone top, a big map drawn on paper, a scarf or tie for the reporter outfit

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Good morning, everybody! It's time for the weather report! Is it sunny? Rainy? Is a snowstorm coming? You are the weather reporter, and everyone is waiting to hear what to wear today."

## Goals
- Your child makes a weather reporter vest and microphone.
- Your child gives a weather report for today and makes up a wild one.

## Objectives
- Your child can look outside and describe the weather with words like sunny, cloudy, windy, rainy.
- Your child can say what clothes go with the weather.
- Your child can speak to an audience in a clear, loud voice.

## Get ready
Make the vest: cut a paper grocery bag up the front middle, cut a neck hole in the bottom, and cut two arm holes in the sides (grown-up only). Cover the top of the spoon or tube with a ball of foil for a microphone.

## Steps
1. **Decorate the vest** (15 minutes). Your child draws weather on it: suns, clouds, raindrops, snowflakes, lightning. Write "Weather Team" on the back.
2. **Weather check.** Go to the window or step outside. Ask: "What is the sky doing? Is the wind moving the trees? Is it hot or cold?" Your child holds out a hand to feel.
3. **Make weather cards** (15 minutes). Your child draws 5 cards: sun, cloud, rain, snow, wind. These are the "map symbols."
4. Draw a big simple map on paper (your house, the park, Grandma's town). Your child tapes weather cards onto it.
5. **Practice the report.** Help your child plan 3 lines: "Today is ___. You should wear ___. Don't forget your ___!"
6. **On the air** (10 minutes). Your child stands in front of the map, holds the microphone, and gives the report. You are the camera person: count down "3, 2, 1, you're on!"
7. **Wild weather report.** Now your child makes one up: "Today it is raining spaghetti! Wear a pot on your head!"

## Make it yours
- Report the weather on another planet, like a planet where it rains glitter.
- Add a "dress the weather bear": your child dresses a stuffed animal for the forecast.
- Make a weather chart and report every day of the break, drawing the real weather each morning.

## Stuck?
- Your child is shy on camera: they report to a row of stuffed animals first.
- Your child can't remember the lines: tape the weather cards in order to the map as a reminder.

## Show it off
Your child gives the evening weather report to the family before dinner and tells everyone what to wear tomorrow.

## Clean up
Hang the vest on a hook. Your child stores the weather cards in an envelope taped to the map.

## For the grown-up
This builds weather science words (a kindergarten science topic), observation, and public speaking. Connecting weather to clothing is a real-life reasoning skill. Do all cutting, and keep the paper bag well away from your child's face and head. **Ask at dinner:** "What will the weather be tomorrow, weather reporter?"
