title: Trip to Anywhere Travel Agency
grade: 2
order: 4
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 2+
materials: paper, crayons or markers, scissors, tape, a table and two chairs, a small bag or backpack, a partner to be the customer (you, a sibling, or a grown-up after work)
nice: a globe or world map, old magazines or catalogs to cut up, a toy phone, a stamp and ink pad

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Welcome to Trip to Anywhere, the only travel agency that can send you anywhere at all. The moon? Sure. The bottom of the ocean? Easy. A land where everything is made of pancakes? Of course. You are the travel agent. Where will you send your customers?"

## Goals
- A travel agency desk with posters, tickets and passports your child made.
- At least two customers "sent" on trips, with tickets, packing lists and a pretend journey.

## Objectives
- Your child can describe a real or imaginary place using details about weather, food and things to do.
- Your child can write a short packing list that fits a place.
- Your child can use polite talk to help a customer.

## Get ready
Set up a table as the agency desk with two chairs facing each other. Fold a few sheets of paper in half to be blank passports. Cut 6 strips of paper about 3 inches by 6 inches for tickets.

## Steps
1. **Choose three destinations** (5 minutes). Your child picks three: one real place (the beach, a city, a jungle), one far-out place (the moon, a volcano island), and one totally made up (Pancake Land).
2. **Make posters** (20 minutes). One poster per place, with a big picture and a short line: "Visit the Moon! Jump 6 times higher!" Tape them up behind the desk.
3. **Make the tickets.** On each ticket, your child writes FROM, TO, and a time, like "Leaves at 3:00."
4. **Make a passport.** Your child draws your face inside and writes your name. Each trip gets a stamp (a drawn star works).
5. **Open for business.** You walk in as the customer. Your child greets you, asks where you want to go, and describes each place. Try customer questions: "Is it cold there? What do people eat?"
6. **Pack the bag.** Your child writes a 4-item packing list for your trip (for the moon: helmet, snacks, moon boots, camera) and finds pretend items around the house to put in the backpack.
7. **Take the trip** (10 minutes). Everyone "travels": count down for a rocket, sway for a boat. Your child is the tour guide and points out the sights.
8. **Switch.** Your child becomes the customer and you are the agent.

## Make it yours
- Add a weather report for each place, with a drawn sun, cloud or snowflake and a temperature.
- Make a souvenir shop where travelers buy one tiny item from each place.
- Plan a trip with three stops and draw the route on a map.

## Stuck?
- Your child runs out of things to say about a place: ask, "What does it sound like? What does it smell like?"
- No partner right now: stuffed animals can be customers, and your child writes their tickets.

## Show it off
Invite a family member for a "booking" after dinner. Your child sells them a trip and gives them a ticket to keep.

## Clean up
Take down the posters and keep them, the tickets and the passports in a folder for the next trip.

## For the grown-up
Pretend play like this builds speaking, describing, early geography and practical writing (tickets, lists, labels). It is also great practice in asking questions and listening. **Ask at dinner:** "If you could really go to one of your three places, which would it be, and what would you pack first?"
