title: Soundtrack for a Silent Scene
grade: 7
order: 14
kind: Music
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pencil, things that make sound: pots and lids, wooden spoons, a jar of rice or beans, rubber bands, a comb and wax paper, glasses of water, your voice, your hands
nice: a stopwatch or kitchen timer, a phone to record (just for the family to hear later), siblings to act or play

## The big idea
Watch a movie with the sound off and it's half as exciting. The music tells you how to feel: scared, sneaky, triumphant. You're the composer. Write a short silent scene and score it with sounds from around the house.

## Goals
- A one-to-two-minute silent scene with a written cue sheet.
- A live soundtrack, with music and sound effects, performed with the scene.

## Objectives
- I can use rhythm, speed and pitch to create a mood.
- I can write a cue sheet that matches sounds to moments.
- I can create a theme that repeats for one character.

## Get ready
Gather at least eight sound-makers. Test each one and sort them into three groups: low sounds, high sounds, and effects (scrapes, shakes, pops).

## Steps
1. **Write the scene.** Keep it short with a clear beginning, middle and twist. Example: a cat tries to steal a sandwich from a sleeping giant. Or a spy sneaks across a laser hallway. Write six to eight moments.
2. **Make a cue sheet (15 minutes).** A table with three columns: Time, What happens, Sound. Example: "0:20, the giant snores, low pot lid hum." Estimate times by acting it out slowly.
3. **Compose a theme.** Give your main character a short tune you can hum or tap: four to six notes. Try glasses of water with different amounts in them, or hum it. Use it whenever that character is in trouble.
4. **Set the mood.** Sneaky scenes use soft, slow taps. Chase scenes get faster and louder. A twist gets total silence, then a big crash. Mark these on your cue sheet.
5. **Add sound effects.** Footsteps (two cups tapped on a table), wind (blowing across a bottle), a door creak (dragging a wet finger on a balloon or a squeaky lid).
6. **Rehearse (20 minutes).** Act the scene in your head or have someone act it while you play. Get the timing tight.
7. **Perform.** Have someone act it out, or act it yourself while playing with one hand, while everyone watches.

## Make it yours
- Score the same scene twice: once as a comedy, once as a horror movie. Watch how the music changes everything.
- Write a scene with no human characters at all, like a storm moving across a city.
- Score a real moment from your day, like the morning rush to get out the door.

## Stuck?
- Sounds are too quiet: get closer to your audience, or switch to metal and wood instead of plastic.
- Timing keeps slipping: number every cue and have the actor freeze at each number until you play it.
- No actor: draw the scene as six comic panels and flip them as you play.

## Show it off
Turn off the lights, use a flashlight as a spotlight, and perform it as a "premiere" for your family.

## Clean up
Wash and put back every pot and glass, and sweep up any spilled beans or rice.

## For the grown-up
This builds music skills (rhythm, tempo, dynamics, motif) and storytelling, plus planning a performance with a written cue sheet. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you make the audience feel scared or excited with just sound?"
