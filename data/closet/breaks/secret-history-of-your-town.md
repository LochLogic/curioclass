title: Secret History of Your Town
grade: 12
order: 22
kind: Story
time: 75-120 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a notebook, a pen, a pencil, comfortable shoes
nice: a paper map of your area, a local history book, an older neighbor or family member to interview

## The big idea
Every town has places with no explanation: a staircase to nowhere, a street with a strange name, a bricked-up
window, a statue no one remembers. What if those were clues to a secret history that nobody tells? You will scout
three real oddities near home and write a short story that weaves them into a hidden past only you know.

## Goals
- Three real oddities found and sketched or described.
- A 1,000- to 2,000-word story that connects them in a secret history.

## Objectives
- I can observe and record real details of a place.
- I can blend fact and fiction into a believable story.
- I can structure a story around clues and a reveal.

## Get ready
Tell a grown-up where you will walk and when you will be back. Or, if you are staying in, pull three oddities from
memory or from the view out your window.

## Steps
1. Scout (25 minutes). Walk with fresh eyes. Note anything odd: a door that leads nowhere, an old painted sign, a
   carved date, a strangely placed tree. Sketch three.
2. Ask "what if?" for each one. Example: "What if the stair to nowhere was built for someone who never came back?"
3. Choose a narrator: a local historian, a teenager who found a diary, a ghost, a time traveler.
4. Connect the three clues into one secret (10 minutes): a buried treasure, a vanished family, a hidden society.
5. Draft the story (40 minutes). Use real street names and real details to make it convincing.
6. End with a reveal that sends the reader to look at the real place again.

## Make it yours
- Write it as a series of found documents: letters, newspaper clippings, a diary.
- Make a hand-drawn map with your secret locations marked.
- Turn it into a walking tour that you lead for your family.

## Stuck?
- You cannot find any oddities: look up at rooflines, signs and the second floors of old buildings.
- The story feels random: pick one emotion, like loss or greed, that ties all three clues together.

## Show it off
Lead the family on a walk to one of the real places and read the story out loud there.

## Clean up
Put away your notebook and keep the sketches with the story.

## For the grown-up
This builds creative writing, observation and an interest in local history. Agree on where they will walk. **Ask at
dinner:** "What is the strangest thing in our town?"
