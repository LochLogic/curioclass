title: Bird Watcher Notebook
grade: 2
order: 12
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: 4 sheets of paper folded and stapled into a notebook, a pencil, colored pencils, a window or an outdoor spot, a clipboard or a hardcover book to write on
nice: binoculars, a bird guide book from the library, bread crumbs or seeds, a blanket to sit on

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Birds are all around us, but most people never really look at them. Today you become a bird watcher, a scientist who watches quietly and writes down what they see. What birds live near you, and what are they doing?"

## Goals
- A bird notebook with at least 3 pages of sketches and notes.
- A tally of how many birds your child spotted.

## Objectives
- Your child can sit still and observe carefully for 5 minutes at a time.
- Your child can sketch a bird and label its beak, wings, tail and feet.
- Your child can write short notes about color, size and what the bird did.

## Get ready
Pick a spot: a window with a view of trees, a balcony, a yard, or a park bench. Make the notebook and write "BIRD NOTEBOOK" on the cover. On the first page, draw a simple bird and label the beak, wings, tail and feet together.

## Steps
1. **Be invisible.** Talk about how to watch birds: sit still, use a quiet voice, and don't make sudden moves.
2. **First watch** (10 minutes). Set a timer or count slowly. Every bird seen gets a tally mark on the back page.
3. **Pick one bird to study.** When a bird stays put for a bit, your child looks closely. Ask: "What color is it? Is it bigger than your hand? What's it doing?"
4. **Sketch it** (10 minutes). Quick drawing: an oval for the body, a circle for the head, a triangle for the beak. Then add color.
5. **Write notes.** Under the sketch, your child writes 2 or 3 short notes: "Brown. Hops. Eats on the ground." If you don't know the name, make one up: "Hopping Brown Bird."
6. **Listen.** Close eyes for one minute and count how many different bird sounds you hear. Your child writes the sound like a word: "chip chip," "coo-coo."
7. **Repeat** for two more birds. Then count up all the tally marks.

## Make it yours
- Make a "bird map" of the yard or view, showing where each bird was seen.
- Invent a bird that doesn't exist yet and draw it with labels for its special features.
- Do it again at a different time of day and compare.

## Stuck?
- No birds show up: sprinkle a few seeds or crumbs in view and wait quietly for 10 minutes.
- Birds fly away too fast: sketch from memory, or draw the shape of the bird in flight.

## Show it off
Share the notebook at dinner and challenge the family to guess which bird was "Hopping Brown Bird."

## Clean up
Put the notebook somewhere safe for the next watch, and bring in any blankets or crumbs.

## For the grown-up
This is real field science: observing, sketching, and recording. It builds patience, attention to detail, and early writing. If you go to a park, stay together. Wash hands after handling seeds. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the most interesting thing a bird did today?"
