title: The Tallest Tale
grade: 3
order: 22
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pencil, markers or crayons, a stapler or tape
nice: a grown-up to tell an old family story first

## The big idea
A tall tale is a story that gets bigger and bigger until it can't be true. "I sneezed so hard it blew the roof off the school!" Write a tall tale about yourself. The bigger, the better!

## Goals
- You write a tall tale about yourself with at least 3 huge moments.
- You make it into a little book with pictures.

## Objectives
- I can stretch the truth on purpose to make a story funny.
- I can use strong describing words.
- I can put events in order so they build up.

## Get ready
Fold 3 sheets of paper in half and stack them. Staple them on the fold. Now you have a 12-page book.

## Steps
1. Pick something true and small about you. You are fast. You are hungry. You love dogs. You have curly hair.
2. Make it huge (10 minutes). Write 3 ways to stretch it. Example: "I am so fast I beat my own shadow home."
3. Make each one bigger than the last. Small, then big, then gigantic.
4. Write the beginning: "Back when I was little, only eight years old..." Tell where you live and your super trait.
5. Write the 3 big moments. One per page or two. Use words like enormous, thundering, sizzling.
6. Write the end. How does the town remember you? Maybe they named a mountain after you.
7. Draw a picture on each page. Make things way too big on purpose.
8. Make a cover with a title, like "The Girl Who Ate a Whole Volcano."

## Make it yours
- Write a tall tale about your pet or a stuffed animal.
- Tell it out loud in a grand old storyteller voice.
- Make a tall tale "team-up." You meet another tall tale hero and have a contest.

## Stuck?
- Can't think of a trait: what do people say about you? Start there.
- It's not funny yet: make it even bigger! Then bigger again.
- Writing is slow: draw first, then write one sentence under each picture.

## Show it off
Read your tall tale out loud to the family. Ask, "Is it true?" and keep a straight face.

## Clean up
Put the book on the shelf with real books. Put pens away.

## For the grown-up
Tall tales are a real story type kids study in school. This builds exaggeration, describing words and story order. If you have a family story that grows each time it's told, share it first. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the biggest thing you did in your tall tale?"
