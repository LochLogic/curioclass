title: Flip Book Animation
grade: 2
order: 7
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: a small notepad or 20 index cards (or paper cut into 20 rectangles about 3 by 5 inches), a pencil, an eraser, a stapler or binder clip, colored pencils
nice: sticky notes (they make a ready flip book), a light window to trace through

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Did you know cartoons are just lots of drawings shown really fast? Today you are an animator. You will draw a tiny movie on paper, and when you flip the pages, your drawings will come alive and move!"

## Goals
- A flip book of at least 15 pages with a drawing that moves.
- A title page with your child's name as the animator.

## Objectives
- Your child can draw the same thing again and again with a small change each time.
- Your child can plan a short action with a start, a middle and an end.
- Your child can explain that many still pictures shown fast look like movement.

## Get ready
If you're using loose paper, stack 20 rectangles and staple or clip along the left edge. Sit at a table near a window. Sharpen the pencil.

## Steps
1. **Try a quick one** (10 minutes). On the last 5 pages of the pad, draw a ball rising a little higher on each page. Flip from back to front. It bounces! Explain that animators start at the back.
2. **Pick a movie.** Your child chooses something simple: a ball bouncing, a seed growing into a flower, a fish swimming across, a stick person jumping, a sun rising. Simple shapes work best.
3. **Draw the first page.** On the last page of the pad, draw the starting picture. Press firmly so it shows through the next page.
4. **Trace and change** (30 minutes). On the next page up, trace the picture through the paper, but move or change one thing a tiny bit (about a fingernail's width). Keep going, page by page. Hold the pages up to a window if it's hard to see through.
5. **Flip check.** After every 5 pages, flip to see how it moves. Too jumpy? Make smaller changes.
6. **Finish the action.** Your child decides how it ends: the flower blooms, the fish eats a worm, the stick person lands a flip.
7. **Color it.** Color the main thing the same color on every page.
8. **Make a title page.** On top, your child writes the movie name and "by" with their name.

## Make it yours
- Add a surprise ending, like the bouncing ball turning into a bird and flying away.
- Make a two-character movie where one chases the other.
- Draw a background (grass, a sky) on every page so the movie has a setting.

## Stuck?
- The picture jumps instead of moves: make each change smaller, like a tiny step.
- Tracing is hard: use thinner paper or tape the pages to a sunny window.

## Show it off
Hold a "movie premiere" after dinner. Dim the lights, have everyone sit down, and let your child flip the movie for each person.

## Clean up
Put pencils away and keep the flip book in a safe spot where it won't get bent.

## For the grown-up
Flip books build drawing control, patience, planning and the science of how animation works (our eyes blend quick pictures together). It's also a gentle lesson in small, steady changes. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the hardest part of making the drawing move?"
