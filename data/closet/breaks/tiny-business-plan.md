## The big idea
Every big company started as somebody's idea on a piece of paper. Today you're a founder. Invent a small business you could really run, or a wild one you wish existed, then plan it and pitch it to "investors."

## Goals
- A one-page business plan with a product, a price, and a profit estimate.
- A logo, a sign, and a two-minute pitch.

## Objectives
- I can figure out cost, price and profit.
- I can describe who my customers are and what they need.
- I can present an idea clearly and answer questions about it.

## Get ready
Brainstorm ten ideas in five minutes. Mix real ones (plant watering, bracelet making, pet sitting, a neighborhood newspaper) with wild ones (a robot dog walking company, a cloud repair service). Circle your favorite.

## Steps
1. **The problem.** Every business solves a problem. Write it in one sentence: "Busy neighbors' plants die when they go away."
2. **Your product.** What exactly do you sell? What makes yours better? Example: "Plant care visits, with a photo report drawn on a postcard."
3. **The math (15 minutes).** List your costs for one sale. Example: a friendship bracelet uses about 40 cents of thread. If you charge 3 dollars, your profit is 2 dollars 60 cents. If you sell 10 a week, that's 26 dollars. What would you do with it?
4. **Your customers.** Who buys this? Where would they hear about it? Write three ways to find customers.
5. **Name and logo.** Design a name and a simple logo that works in black and white. Make a sign on cardboard.
6. **Plan for problems.** What could go wrong? (Bad weather, a customer who doesn't pay, running out of thread.) Write a plan for each.
7. **Write a two-minute pitch.** Start with a hook question ("Has your plant ever died while you were on vacation?"), then the product, the price, and why you're the right person.

## Make it yours
- Make it an investor show: your family are investors who offer money for part of your profits.
- Design the business for a fantasy world: a dragon-sitting service or potion delivery. Do the same math.
- Make a full set of business papers: a price list, a flyer, and a receipt template.

## Stuck?
- Can't pick an idea: choose something you're already good at and someone you know needs.
- The math doesn't work, you'd lose money: raise the price, find a cheaper material, or sell more.
- Pitch feels awkward: practice it to a mirror three times. It gets easier every time.

## Show it off
Pitch to your family after dinner. They have to ask you three tough investor questions.

## Clean up
Put your plan and sign in a folder in case you actually want to launch it.

## For the grown-up
This is applied math (percent, profit, simple rates) and persuasive speaking, plus some real-world thinking about money. If they want to really launch something, that's your call on safety and who they deal with; neighbors and family friends are a good start. **Ask at dinner:** "If someone gave you 20 dollars to start your business, what would you spend it on first?"
