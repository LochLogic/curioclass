title: Play Dough Bakery
grade: k
order: 12
kind: Kitchen
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 2 cups flour, 1/2 cup salt, 2 tablespoons vegetable oil, about 3/4 cup warm water, a big bowl, a spoon, measuring cups, a rolling pin or smooth cup, cookie cutters or a plastic cup, a butter knife, plates, paper and crayons
nice: food coloring, cupcake liners, a muffin tin, a toy cash register

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Your bakery opens today! The customers want cookies, cupcakes and pizza. But first, the baker has to make the dough. Put on your apron, it's time to bake!"

## Goals
- Your child mixes a batch of play dough.
- Your child "bakes" and sells a menu of pretend treats.

## Objectives
- Your child can help measure with cups and spoons and count scoops.
- Your child can roll, pinch, cut and shape dough.
- Your child can count out pretend money and make a trade.

## Get ready
Wash hands and wipe the table. Put an apron or old shirt on your child. Set out the bowl, the ingredients and measuring cups. Make a sign for the bakery door.

## Steps
1. **Make the dough** (15 minutes). Your child scoops 2 cups of flour and 1/2 cup salt into the bowl, counting each scoop. You add the oil and warm water (and a few drops of color if you have it). Your child stirs, then kneads with hands until it is soft. Add a little flour if it is sticky.
2. **Name the bakery.** Your child names it and you write it on the sign.
3. **Plan the menu.** Ask: "What will your bakery sell?" Your child draws 4 to 6 menu pictures. You write the names and a price (1, 2 or 3 coins).
4. **Bake** (25 minutes). Your child rolls dough flat and cuts cookies, rolls balls for donuts, makes snakes for pretzels, flattens circles for pizza. Put cupcakes in liners or a muffin tin. Pop them in a pretend oven (a box or under the table) and count to 20.
5. Make paper coins: draw circles and you cut them out.
6. **Open the bakery.** You are the customer. Order from the menu. Your child serves you and counts the coins.
7. Switch: you be the baker and your child shops.

## Make it yours
- Bake a giant birthday cake for a stuffed animal, with dough candles and a song.
- Make a bakery for monsters: worm cookies, slime pie, eyeball donuts.
- Run a pizza shop instead, with toppings made from scraps of dough in different colors.

## Stuck?
- Dough is too sticky: add flour one spoonful at a time. Too crumbly: add water one spoonful at a time.
- Your child wants to eat it: remind them it is pretend food, and set out a real snack for the bakery break.

## Show it off
Set out the bakery display for the family. They get to "buy" one treat each when they get home.

## Clean up
Store the dough in a sealed bag or container; it keeps for a week or two. Your child wipes the table with a damp cloth.

## For the grown-up
This builds measuring, counting, fine motor strength and early money play. Play dough is salty and not for eating, so watch younger children. It contains wheat, so check allergies and use a gluten-free flour if needed. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the best thing your bakery made, and who bought it?"
