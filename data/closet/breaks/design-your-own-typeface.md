## The big idea
Every letter you have ever read was designed by someone. Type designers decide the height, weight, curves and
personality of every letter. You are going to invent a complete alphabet with its own voice, maybe sharp and
futuristic, maybe soft and handmade, and then use it to set a phrase that could only look right in your type.

## Goals
- A full 26-letter alphabet (capitals or lowercase) in your own consistent style.
- A sample phrase set in your typeface, like a title for a book or poster.

## Objectives
- I can use guidelines (baseline, x-height, cap height) to keep letters consistent.
- I can design repeated parts so the alphabet looks like a family.
- I can space letters by eye so words read smoothly.

## Get ready
Draw guidelines across a page: a **baseline** (where letters sit), an **x-height** line (top of a lowercase x), and
a **cap height** line (top of capitals). Leave about 1 cm between each.

## Steps
1. Choose a personality (5 minutes). Write three words: "spiky, fast, cold" or "round, friendly, bouncy."
2. Design the **key letters** first: lowercase n, o, a, e (or capitals H, O, A, E). These set the shape of the rest.
3. Decide your rules: serifs (little feet) or no serifs, thick or thin strokes, square or round ends.
4. Build the alphabet (40 minutes). Reuse parts: the bowl of b becomes the bowl of d, p and q; the arch of n becomes h and m.
5. Ink your best version with a pen.
6. **Kern** it. Write a word and adjust spacing between letters so the gaps look even, not measured. "AV" sits closer than "HH."
7. Set a phrase (15 minutes): a band name, a poster title, a quote. Draw it large.

## Make it yours
- Design a font for a specific place: a spaceship control panel, a haunted inn sign, a candy shop.
- Make letters from objects in the house, like paper clips or spaghetti, and draw them.
- Add a second weight, a bold version, to the same alphabet.

## Stuck?
- The letters do not look related: compare everything to your o and n and match their curves and stroke width.
- Spacing looks off: flip the page upside down so you see shapes, not words.

## Show it off
Make a sign for your bedroom door or the family fridge in your typeface and unveil it at dinner.

## Clean up
Keep your alphabet sheet flat in a folder.

## For the grown-up
This builds design, precision and visual consistency, the same thinking used in graphic design and typography.
**Ask at dinner:** "What personality did you give your letters?"
