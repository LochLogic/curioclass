## The big idea
What if YOU were the game piece? Draw a giant board game on the ground. Roll, hop and follow the silly squares. Then invite your family to play.

## Goals
- You draw a board with at least 30 squares.
- You add at least 6 action squares and play a full game.

## Objectives
- I can design a game path with a start and an end.
- I can write clear actions for game squares.
- I can test the rules and change them if needed.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up where you may draw. A driveway, patio or a safe sidewalk spot. No sidewalk? Use tape and paper squares inside, or paper on a big table.

## Steps
1. Draw START. Draw a winding path of squares. Each square should be big enough to stand in, about 1 foot wide.
2. Draw at least 30 squares. Make the path curve and twist.
3. Draw FINISH at the end. Give it a crown or a castle.
4. Add action squares (15 minutes). "Hop on one foot to the next square." "Go back 3." "Spin 3 times." "Do 5 jumping jacks."
5. Add a shortcut. A bridge that jumps 5 squares ahead.
6. Add a trap. "Lava! Back to start!"
7. Play! Roll the die. Walk that many squares. Do what the square says.
8. Change any rule that is too hard or too easy.

## Make it yours
- Make a theme: jungle, outer space, or a land made of sweets.
- Add a question square where someone asks you a riddle.
- Make it a race for two players at the same time.

## Stuck?
- Chalk is faint: wet the chalk tip in water first.
- The game is too long: add more shortcuts.
- No die: write 1 to 6 on 6 paper scraps and pick one from a cup.

## Show it off
Invite the family to play when they get home. You get to be the game master.

## Clean up
Put the chalk in its box. Spray or splash the drawing away if a grown-up asks.

## For the grown-up
This builds game design, counting and following rules, and it gets kids moving outdoors. Pick a spot well away from cars, and stay nearby. If your child goes past your yard, go with them. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the best action square on your board?"
