title: Pots and Pans Toy Parade
grade: k
order: 10
kind: Music
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 2 or 3 pots or pans, metal and wooden spoons, a plastic bowl, a baking sheet, a box with a lid, stuffed animals or toys, paper, crayons, a scarf or ribbon
nice: a paper towel tube for a horn, a cardboard box with wheels drawn on for a float

## The big idea
Read aloud: "The toys are having a big parade, and they need a marching band! You will be the drummer and the band leader. Can you make the beat so everyone marches together?"

## Goals
- Your child finds at least 5 different sounds in the kitchen.
- Your child leads a toy parade with a steady beat.

## Objectives
- Your child can hear and describe sounds: loud, soft, high, low.
- Your child can keep a steady beat while walking.
- Your child can play fast and slow, loud and soft on purpose.

## Get ready
Set out the pots, pans, bowl, baking sheet and box on the floor in a row. Line up the toys in a parade line along the hallway or across the room.

## Steps
1. **Sound hunt.** Your child taps each kitchen "drum" with a metal spoon, then a wooden spoon. Ask: "Which one is loudest? Which one sounds like rain? Which one goes BONG?"
2. Pick 2 favorite drums. Your child holds one or carries it in a scarf sling (tie a scarf through the pot handle).
3. **Find the beat** (10 minutes). Clap a slow beat together: 1, 2, 3, 4. Your child drums along. Then march in place to the beat.
4. **Band signals.** Make up signals: hands up high means loud, hands down low means soft, a spin means stop. Take turns being the leader.
5. Your child makes a parade sign on paper ("The Great Toy Parade!") and a flag on a spoon.
6. **The parade** (15 minutes). Your child drums and marches. The toys come along (you or a sibling carry them, or pull them in a box). Stop at each room for a "drum solo."
7. Finish with a big drumroll and a crash on the baking sheet.

## Make it yours
- Make it a rainstorm parade: start with soft taps like rain, then louder like thunder, then soft again.
- Your child makes up a marching chant: "Left, right, here we go, toys are marching, fast and slow!"
- Let each toy have its own special sound, and your child plays it when that toy "waves."

## Stuck?
- It is too loud for the house: put a dish towel over the pot, or drum on cardboard boxes and plastic bowls.
- Your child can't keep the beat: slow it way down and say "step, step, step" together.

## Show it off
Give the family a parade when they get home. They can join the line and follow the leader's signals.

## Clean up
Your child puts the pots and spoons back where they live in the kitchen. Toys go back to the shelf.

## For the grown-up
This builds steady beat, listening, and loud and soft (music class basics), plus coordination from marching while drumming. If neighbors share walls, use towels on the pots or try the box drums. **Ask at dinner:** "Which kitchen drum made the best sound?"
