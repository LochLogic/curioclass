title: Teddy Bear Picnic
grade: k
order: 17
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a blanket, stuffed animals, plastic plates and cups, a simple snack (crackers, cut fruit, cheese cubes), napkins, paper, crayons, a bag or basket
nice: a play tea set, a small speaker or a song you both know

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Today is the Teddy Bear Picnic! All the bears and bunnies are invited, but someone needs to send the invitations, pack the basket and plan the games. Will you be the picnic host?"

## Goals
- Your child plans and hosts a picnic for their stuffed animals.
- Your child makes invitations and leads one picnic game.

## Objectives
- Your child can match one plate, one cup and one napkin to each guest (one-to-one counting).
- Your child can share food out fairly.
- Your child can use polite words like please and thank you in play.

## Get ready
Pick a picnic spot: the yard, a balcony, or the living room floor. Wash hands. Cut the snack into small pieces and set it in a bowl. Gather the guests.

## Steps
1. **Invitations** (10 minutes). Your child folds paper in half and draws a picture on each invitation. You write: "Come to my picnic!" They "deliver" one to each guest.
2. **Pack the basket.** Your child counts the guests, then counts out the same number of plates, cups and napkins. Ask: "Do we have enough for everyone?"
3. Spread the blanket. Your child seats each guest and tells you their name.
4. **Share the food** (15 minutes). Your child serves each guest the same amount: one cracker each, then another. Ask: "Is it fair? Does everyone have the same?"
5. **Picnic talk.** Your child makes the animals talk. What do they say about the food? Who is grumpy? Who loves cheese?
6. **Picnic games** (15 minutes). Your child picks or invents one: a bear race (carry a bear on your head across the room), hide the bunny, or a dance.
7. End with a picnic nap: everyone lies down while you hum a quiet song.

## Make it yours
- Hold a picnic on the "moon" or under the "sea," with special food for those places.
- Make it a birthday picnic for one stuffed animal, with a paper crown and a song.
- A surprise guest arrives (you with a sock puppet), and your child has to make room and share.

## Stuck?
- The snack disappears too fast: give your child the job of "picnic server" so they serve before eating.
- Not enough guests: invite toys, a pillow with a face, or a drawing taped to a chair.

## Show it off
Take a "picnic picture" (a drawing or a photo) of all the guests to show the family.

## Clean up
Shake the blanket outside. Your child stacks the plates and wipes the crumbs.

## For the grown-up
This builds one-to-one counting, fair sharing (early division), manners and social play. Check allergies for the snack, keep it nut-free, and cut round foods like grapes into quarters for young children. **Ask at dinner:** "Who was the best guest at your picnic, and why?"
