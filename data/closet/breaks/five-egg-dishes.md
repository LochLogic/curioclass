title: Five Egg Dishes
grade: 10
order: 3
kind: Kitchen
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: about 8 eggs, butter or oil, salt, pepper, a small and a medium pot, a nonstick or well-oiled pan, a spatula, a whisk or fork, a slotted spoon, a bowl of ice water, a timer, plates
nice: vinegar, grated cheese, chopped herbs or scallions, toast

## The big idea
Chefs used to say a cook could be judged by how they make an egg. The same ingredient becomes five completely different dishes depending on heat, time and technique. You'll run a five-station egg lab, nail each technique, and serve a tasting plate for whoever is home.

## Goals
- Five dishes: boiled, scrambled, fried, poached and a rolled omelet.
- A tasting plate with a label for each dish.

## Objectives
- I can control heat to change how proteins set.
- I can time cooking to the minute for a specific result.
- I can use the stove safely on my own.

## Get ready
Get a grown-up's OK to use the stove, check for egg allergies, and make sure you can use 8 eggs. Set out everything you need. Fill a bowl with ice water.

## Steps
1. **Boiled** (12 minutes). Lower 2 eggs into boiling water with a spoon. Pull one at 6 and a half minutes (jammy yolk) and one at 10 minutes (firm yolk). Drop them into ice water to stop the cooking.
2. **Scrambled, low and slow.** Whisk 2 eggs with a pinch of salt. Melt butter in the pan on low. Stir constantly with a spatula for 3 to 5 minutes. Pull them off while they still look a bit wet: they keep cooking on the plate.
3. **Fried.** Heat oil on medium. Crack an egg into a cup first, then slide it in. Cook until the white is set and the edges are lacy (2 to 3 minutes). For "over easy," flip gently and cook 20 seconds more.
4. **Poached.** Bring a pot of water to a gentle simmer (small bubbles, not boiling). Add a teaspoon of vinegar. Stir to make a slow whirlpool and slip the egg in from a cup. Cook 3 minutes. Lift out with a slotted spoon.
5. **Rolled omelet** (10 minutes). Whisk 2 eggs. Pour into a buttered pan on medium-low. Stir fast for 10 seconds, then let it set. Add cheese or herbs, tilt the pan and roll it into thirds with the spatula.
6. Peel the boiled eggs under running water, starting at the fat end.
7. **Plate it.** Arrange all five on one big plate, with salt, pepper and a small label for each.

## Make it yours
- Make it a "world of eggs" plate, with one dish seasoned in a different style: chili oil, herbs, cheese or spices.
- Run a blind test: which scramble does your family like better, low and slow or fast and hot?
- Cook a sixth dish you invent, and name it after yourself.

## Stuck?
- The scramble is rubbery: the heat's too high. Turn it to low and pull it sooner.
- The poached egg looks like a cloud: the water's boiling too hard. Keep it at a gentle simmer and use the freshest egg.

## Show it off
Serve the tasting plate to whoever's home, and have them rank all five.

## Clean up
Soak egg pans in warm water right away. Wipe the stove when it's cool. Wash hands after handling raw egg.

## For the grown-up
This teaches technique, heat control and the chemistry of protein, plus real stove independence. Check for egg allergies before they start. Raw egg on hands or counters should be washed off with soap. **Ask at dinner:** "Which egg was the hardest to get right?"
