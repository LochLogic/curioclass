title: Cheer-Up Quest for a Friend
grade: 12
order: 27
kind: Kindness
time: 75-120 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, pens, envelopes, tape, small things from home (a snack, a drawing, a note, a favorite book), a bag to carry clues
nice: colored paper, stickers, a helper to deliver the first clue

## The big idea
Someone you know is having a rough week. Instead of a text, you are going to design a quest for them: a trail of
clues that leads them through small surprises, funny notes and memories, ending in something that says "you matter."
It is a scavenger hunt built from kindness.

## Goals
- A quest of five to eight clues, each with a small surprise.
- A final prize or message that makes your friend feel seen.

## Objectives
- I can design a sequence of clues that lead to a goal.
- I can choose surprises that fit one person.
- I can plan and carry out a kind act from start to finish.

## Get ready
Pick the friend or family member. Write down five things that make them smile: a food, a joke, a memory, a song, a
place.

## Steps
1. Choose the route (10 minutes): inside your home if it is for a family member, or around a friend's front door,
   mailbox or doorstep (with their family's permission).
2. Write clues (25 minutes). Riddles, inside jokes, mini puzzles. Example: "Find the place where we spilled the
   whole bowl of popcorn in seventh grade."
3. Prepare the surprises: a drawing, a favorite snack, a list of ten things you like about them, a mixtape lyric sheet.
4. Plan the finale: a letter, a handmade gift, or you waiting with their favorite food.
5. Set it up. Hide the clues in order and test the path yourself.
6. Start the quest by handing over the first envelope, or leaving it where they will find it.

## Make it yours
- Make it a quest for someone in your building or a grandparent, adjusted to what they enjoy.
- Make each clue a page in a short story where they are the hero.
- Invite their other friends to write one note each for the trail.

## Stuck?
- You do not know what to say: write a list of specific memories, then use the best ones.
- The friend lives far away: mail it as a quest in envelopes labeled "Open on Monday," "Open on Tuesday," and so on.

## Show it off
Do not share it online. Just ask your friend later which clue was their favorite.

## Clean up
Recycle scraps and collect any clues that were not found.

## For the grown-up
This builds empathy, planning and creativity. If the quest goes to a friend's home, make sure the friend's family
knows. **Ask at dinner:** "What did you put in the final envelope?"
