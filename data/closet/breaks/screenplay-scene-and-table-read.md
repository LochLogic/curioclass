## The big idea
Movies start as screenplays: a special format that tells actors what to say and directors what to film. You're writing a short scene, formatted like the real thing, then holding a "table read" where actors read it aloud for the first time, just like in a real studio.

## Goals
- A screenplay scene, about three to five pages, in real screenplay format.
- A table read with at least one other person.

## Objectives
- I can use screenplay format: scene heading, action, character, dialogue.
- I can write dialogue that shows character and moves the story.
- I can revise after hearing a scene read aloud.

## Get ready
Decide on two or three characters and a place. Simple is best: one location, one problem, a few minutes of time.

## Steps
1. **Learn the format.** A scene starts with a heading in capitals: INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT (INT means inside, EXT means outside). Under it, action lines in normal sentences describe what we see. Character names are in capitals in the middle of the line, with their dialogue underneath.
2. **Example:**
   INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT
   A dark kitchen. The fridge hums. MAYA (13) tiptoes in, holding a flashlight.
   MAYA: (whispering) If anyone finds out about the cake, we're done.
3. **Plan the scene.** Who wants what? What's in the way? How does it end? A good scene has a turn: something changes between the beginning and the end.
4. **Write it (40 minutes).** Keep action lines short: describe only what the camera sees and hears. Give each character a different way of talking. Cut "hi, how are you" small talk. Start late and end early.
5. **Add one surprise.** A secret is revealed, someone walks in, or the plan goes wrong.
6. **Prep the table read.** Make a copy for each reader or share one. Highlight each character's lines in a different color.
7. **Hold the table read.** Sit around a table. One person reads the action lines; others read their characters. Listen: which lines got a laugh? Which felt clunky? Mark them, then rewrite.

## Make it yours
- Write the same scene in two genres: as a comedy and as a thriller.
- Write a scene with almost no dialogue, told mostly through action.
- Write a scene that's a "lost" moment between two characters from a story you already love, but make the characters your own.

## Stuck?
- Dialogue sounds fake: read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it, change it.
- The scene feels flat: raise the stakes. What happens if the character fails?
- No readers yet: read every part yourself in different voices, or wait for family after dinner.

## Show it off
Hold a table read at dinner and give everyone a part. Take notes like a real director.

## Clean up
Keep your script and notes in a folder, marked "Draft 1."

## For the grown-up
This builds dialogue, structure and revision skills, and introduces a professional writing format. Hearing writing read aloud is one of the best ways to improve it. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the best line in your scene, and who said it?"
