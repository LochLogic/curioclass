title: Paper Roller Coaster with a Loop
grade: 5
order: 1
kind: Build
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a marble (or a small round bead or ball about 1.5 cm wide), cereal boxes or thin cardboard, paper, masking tape, scissors, a ruler, a pencil, a wall, a chair, or a stack of books to build against
nice: card stock, painter's tape (it peels off walls cleanly), a cup at the end to catch the marble, a timer

## The big idea
A real roller coaster has no engine on the ride. One big climb gives it all the energy it needs. Then gravity does the rest, even upside down! Can you build a paper track that sends a marble through a loop without falling out?

## Goals
- You build a marble coaster with a hill, a turn, and a full loop.
- The marble runs from start to finish three times in a row.

## Objectives
- I can explain that a higher start gives the marble more energy.
- I can test my track and fix the spot where it fails.
- I can measure and fold parts so they fit together.

## Get ready
Pick a spot near a wall, a bookshelf, or a sturdy chair. Ask a grown-up which wall is okay for tape. Clear the floor so the marble can't roll under furniture.

## Steps
1. Make track pieces (15 minutes). Cut strips of cardboard 5 cm wide. Fold up 1.5 cm on each long side. Now you have a U-shaped channel.
2. Make about eight pieces. Tape them end to end, with the higher piece always sitting on top of the lower one. That way the marble never hits an edge.
3. Build the first drop. Tape the start high on the wall or books, at least 60 cm up. Slope it down to the floor.
4. Test a straight run. Does the marble stay in? Fix bumps at the joints with smooth tape.
5. Make the loop. Bend one long track piece into a circle about 15 cm across. The track goes in, around, and out a little to the side. Tape the bottom down well.
6. Connect the loop near the bottom of the drop. Here is the rule: start the drop at least twice as high as the loop is tall (20 minutes of testing).
7. Add your own parts: a curve, a tunnel, a jump, or a cup "station" at the end.

## Make it yours
- Name your coaster and make a sign with its height and a warning: "Only for brave marbles."
- Add a second loop. How much higher must the start be now?
- Build a split track where the marble picks a path at random.

## Stuck?
- The marble falls out at the top of the loop: it is too slow. Raise the start, or make the loop smaller.
- The marble flies off a turn: add taller side walls on the outside of the curve.
- The marble stops: a joint is bumpy. Re-tape it so the top piece overlaps the lower one.

## Show it off
Invite your family to opening day. Do a countdown, then release the marble. Ask riders to guess if it will make the loop.

## Clean up
Peel tape off the wall slowly. Keep the track pieces in a box for a new design.

## For the grown-up
This is hands-on physics: height gives energy (potential energy), and speed lets the marble stay on the loop. It also builds measuring and patient fixing. Painter's tape is gentlest on walls. Marbles are a choking risk for children under 3, so pick them up if little ones are around. **Ask at dinner:** "How high did the start have to be for the marble to make the loop?"
