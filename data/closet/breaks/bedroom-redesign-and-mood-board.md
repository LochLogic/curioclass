title: Bedroom Redesign and Mood Board
grade: 10
order: 15
kind: Art
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a tape measure or ruler, graph paper or paper with a grid you draw, a pencil, scissors, glue or tape, a large sheet of cardboard or paper, old magazines, fabric scraps, colored paper, markers
nice: paint chip cards, string, a camera for before-and-after

## The big idea
Interior designers can make a room feel bigger, calmer or more creative without buying a thing. You'll measure your room, draw it to scale, test new layouts with paper furniture, and make a mood board that captures the feeling you want. Then, with permission, you'll move at least one thing for real.

## Goals
- A to-scale floor plan of your room with movable furniture cutouts.
- A mood board with your colors, textures and inspiration.
- One real change to your space.

## Objectives
- I can measure a room and draw it to scale.
- I can use design ideas like flow, focal point and zones.
- I can create a mood board that communicates a feeling.

## Get ready
Get permission to measure your room and to move furniture. Clear the floor. Sketch a rough room outline to write measurements on.

## Steps
1. **Measure** (15 minutes). Measure each wall, plus the doors, windows, closets and outlets. Measure each piece of furniture (length and width).
2. **Draw to scale.** Use 1 grid square = 10 cm (or 1 square = 6 inches). A 3.2 m wall is 32 squares.
3. **Cutouts.** Draw each piece of furniture to the same scale on separate paper and cut them out.
4. **Test three layouts** (20 minutes). Move the cutouts around. Think about **flow** (a clear path from the door), a **focal point** (what your eye goes to first) and **zones** (a sleep zone, a work zone, a chill zone). Leave about 75 cm for walking paths.
5. Pick a favorite and glue it down.
6. **Mood board** (30 minutes). Write three words for how you want the room to feel: "calm, creative, cozy." Cut images, colors and textures from magazines and fabric that match. Arrange them on cardboard, big things first.
7. **Make one real change.** Move a piece of furniture, rearrange a shelf or hang art in a new spot, using your plan.

## Make it yours
- Design the room for a character: a deep-sea scientist, a retired astronaut, a 200-year-old wizard.
- Make a "no cost" challenge: redesign using only what's already in the house.
- Design a dream version with no limits, then a real version you could do this week.

## Stuck?
- The room is too small for anything new: try putting things up on walls or under the bed to free floor space.
- Nothing fits your three words: choose one main color and find everything in that color family.

## Show it off
Give a "design presentation" to your family with the floor plan, mood board and the one change.

## Clean up
Put scraps in recycling and put your room back together if you only tested something.

## For the grown-up
This builds measurement, scale drawing (ratios and proportions from math class) and design thinking. Help or supervise when moving heavy furniture. **Ask at dinner:** "What are the three words for your room?"
