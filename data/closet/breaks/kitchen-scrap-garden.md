title: Kitchen Scrap Garden
grade: 2
order: 26
kind: Science
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: kitchen scraps (the bottom of a celery bunch, the root end of a green onion, carrot tops, a lettuce bottom), shallow dishes or jar lids, water, a sunny window, paper, a pencil, tape, toothpicks
nice: a ruler, a small pot of soil, a plant mister or spray bottle

## The big idea
Read aloud: "What if the leftovers we throw away could grow again? A plant scientist is needed to find out. Today you will plant kitchen scraps in water and watch to see which ones come back to life!"

## Goals
- A windowsill garden of at least 4 kitchen scraps in water.
- A plant journal to check and measure over the next week.

## Objectives
- Your child can set up an experiment and make predictions.
- Your child can measure and record growth using a ruler or tally marks.
- Your child can name what plants need: water, light, air.

## Get ready
Save scraps for a day or two. You cut each bottom piece 1 to 2 inches tall with a kitchen knife. Clean the dishes.

## Steps
1. **Meet the scraps.** Look at each one with your child. Ask: "Do you think this will grow? Why?"
2. **Plant them** (10 minutes). Set each scrap cut side up in a dish with about half an inch of water. Green onions can stand in a small glass. For a carrot top, push 3 toothpicks into its sides so it sits on a jar rim.
3. **Label.** Your child writes a name card for each scrap and tapes it to the dish: "Celery. Day 1."
4. **Predict.** Your child writes or draws which one they think will grow fastest.
5. **Make the journal** (15 minutes). Fold 4 sheets into a little book. Day 1 page: your child draws each scrap.
6. **Find the sunniest spot.** A windowsill is best. Your child arranges the garden.
7. **Check every day.** Change the water every day or two. Each day, your child draws or measures any new green and writes one sentence. Example: "The onion grew one inch!"

## Make it yours
- Do a "dark test": put one scrap in a cupboard and compare it with one in the sun.
- Name each plant and make up a personality for it.
- Try a scrap no one told you about, like an end of a potato or a pepper top, and see what happens.

## Stuck?
- Nothing grows after 3 days: some take longer. Celery and green onions are fastest. Keep water fresh.
- Water gets cloudy or smelly: rinse the scrap and the dish, and use fresh water.

## Show it off
After a week, give a "Garden Report" to the family with the journal and the plants.

## Clean up
Wipe up water drips, rinse any knives, and compost or throw away scraps that don't grow.

## For the grown-up
This builds plant science (roots, light, water), predictions and recording over time. You cut the scraps with a knife. Change water often so it doesn't spoil. **Ask at dinner:** "Which scrap is growing the most, and why do you think?"
