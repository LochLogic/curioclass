## The big idea
Little kids get bored too. You can be the hero who fixes that. Design activity kits for a younger child. It could be a sibling, a cousin, or a neighbor. It could be kids at a shelter your family knows. Every kit is full of things you made yourself.

## Goals
- You make two or three activity kits, each with at least three activities.
- You give them to a younger child or to someone who can share them.

## Objectives
- I can design activities that are the right level for a younger child.
- I can write short, simple directions a little kid can follow.
- I can think about what someone else would enjoy.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up who the kits are for and how old they are. Write the age at the top of a planning page. Set out paper and markers.

## Steps
1. Think back to when you were that age. What did you love? Write three ideas.
2. Make a coloring page. Draw a big, simple picture with thick lines: a dinosaur, a garden, a rocket.
3. Make a puzzle. Draw a picture on cardboard, then cut it into 6 to 8 big pieces. Put the pieces in an envelope.
4. Make a hunt card (10 minutes). Draw five small pictures: something red, something soft, something round. The child finds them at home.
5. Make one surprise activity of your own: a simple maze, a dot-to-dot, or a "draw the rest of this monster" page.
6. Pack each kit in an envelope or bag. Write the kit name on the front, like "Dino Fun Kit."
7. Write a short, friendly note to go inside. "Hi! I made this for you. Have fun!"

## Make it yours
- Make a kit with a theme, like "Ocean Day," where every activity fits the theme.
- Make one kit for a quiet place, like a doctor's waiting room, with no noisy parts.
- Add a story you wrote and drew, about 5 pages long, folded into a mini book.

## Stuck?
- Activities feel too hard: make them simpler and bigger. Little kids need big shapes.
- You're out of ideas: copy a game you played when you were small, in your own version.
- The puzzle pieces are flimsy: glue the paper to cardboard first.

## Show it off
Give the kits away with your grown-up, or show your family each kit before you seal it.

## Clean up
Put away the scissors and markers. Recycle your scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds kindness, empathy, and the skill of designing for someone else, plus writing simple directions. Please help choose who gets the kits and handle any delivery outside the home. For children under 3, leave out anything small enough to choke on. **Ask at dinner:** "Which activity do you think the little kid will love most?"
