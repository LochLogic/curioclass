title: Animal Track Detective
grade: 3
order: 12
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pencil, a ruler, a baking tray, flour or sand, a spoon, small toys with feet, a notebook
nice: a magnifying glass, play dough, a phone for a photo (a grown-up's)

## The big idea
Animals walk by and leave clues. Paw prints in the mud. Bird feet in the dirt. A trail by the fence. Be a detective and find out who was here!

## Goals
- You find and draw at least 3 tracks or animal signs.
- You make a track trap and see who leaves prints.

## Objectives
- I can tell different tracks by shape and size.
- I can measure a track with a ruler.
- I can use clues to make a good guess.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up where you may look. Mud, sand, snow and dirt are best. Bring your notebook and ruler.

## Steps
1. Learn the shapes. Draw these in your notebook: a dog print has 4 toes and claws. A cat print has 4 toes and no claws. A bird print looks like an arrow. A squirrel has long back feet.
2. Hunt for tracks (20 minutes). Check by puddles, gardens and fences. Look for other clues too: nibbled leaves, feathers, holes, droppings. Do not touch droppings!
3. Draw each find. Measure it with a ruler. Write where you found it.
4. Make your guess. Who left it? Write "I think a ___ because ___."
5. Set a track trap. Spread flour or sand flat in a tray. Put it outside overnight by a wall.
6. Indoors or no yard? Press toy animal feet into flour or play dough. Make a mystery trail for someone to solve.
7. Make a "Track Book" page for each animal you found.

## Make it yours
- Write the story of what the animal was doing. Was it chasing something?
- Make a fake creature track. Can the family guess what it looks like?
- Make your own footprint and your pet's footprint to compare.

## Stuck?
- No tracks anywhere: wet a patch of dirt with a cup of water and check later.
- Tracks are blurry: look for the clearest one in the trail.
- Not sure what made it: write two guesses. Detectives do that too.

## Show it off
Give a "detective report" at dinner. Show your drawings and name your best suspect.

## Clean up
Bring in the tray and shake the flour in the trash. Wash your hands well.

## For the grown-up
This builds observation, measuring and reasoning from evidence. Stay nearby outdoors, and go along if the hunt goes past your yard. Remind your child not to touch droppings or wild animals, and to wash hands after. **Ask at dinner:** "What clue helped you guess the animal?"
