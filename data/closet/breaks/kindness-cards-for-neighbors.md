title: Kindness Cards for Neighbors
grade: k
order: 23
kind: Kindness
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: paper (folded in half for cards), crayons or markers, scissors (grown-up only), glue stick, scrap paper, envelopes or plain paper to fold
nice: stickers, a hole punch for confetti, colored paper

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Some people near us might be lonely or tired or need a smile. You can be a secret kindness helper! Let's make cards that make people happy when they open them."

## Goals
- Your child makes at least 4 kindness cards.
- Your child decides who gets each card and helps deliver them.

## Objectives
- Your child can think about how someone else might feel.
- Your child can draw a picture that sends a message (happy, thank you, get well).
- Your child can write or copy a few letters, like their name or "HI."

## Get ready
Fold 4 to 6 sheets of paper in half. Set out crayons and scraps. Make a list together of people who might like a card: a neighbor, the mail carrier, a grandparent, a building worker, a family friend, a teacher.

## Steps
1. Ask your child: "How do you feel when someone is kind to you?" Then: "Who could use a happy surprise?" Pick 4 people from the list.
2. For each person, think about them together. "What does Mr. Lee like? He has a dog!" Plan one picture per card.
3. **Make the cards** (30 minutes). Your child draws on the front: a sun, a heart, a dog, a rainbow. Inside, you write the message they say ("You are nice. I like your dog."). Your child signs their name or draws a self-portrait.
4. **Add a surprise.** Your child chooses a fun extra: a folded paper flower glued inside, a pop-up heart (a folded strip glued in the middle), or a drawn joke.
5. Fold paper into envelopes or use real ones. Your child draws a picture on each one so you know who it is for.
6. **Deliver** (15 minutes). Hand them to people you know, tape one to a neighbor's door, leave one for the mail carrier, or mail them.

## Make it yours
- Make a "kindness rock" or "kindness sign" for your window that everyone walking by can see.
- Make cards for helpers you don't know by name, like the bus driver or the person who works at the store.
- Make a card for someone in your own home and hide it under their pillow.

## Stuck?
- Your child doesn't know what to draw: ask "What makes you smile?" and draw that.
- Your child wants to keep the cards: let them make one extra just for themselves.

## Show it off
Before delivering, line up the cards and let your child tell the family who each one is for and why.

## Clean up
Your child collects scraps in a box for the next project and caps all the markers.

## For the grown-up
This builds empathy, thinking about others, early writing and drawing with a purpose. Deliver only to people you know and trust, and go with your child; leave cards for strangers (like delivery workers) at your own door rather than approaching them. **Ask at dinner:** "Who do you think will be the happiest to get a card, and why?"
