title: Yogurt and Sourdough Starter
grade: 12
order: 6
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 4 cups milk, 2 tablespoons plain yogurt with live cultures, a pot, a thermometer if you have one, clean jars, a towel, flour (whole wheat or rye is best, but any works), water, a spoon, tape and a marker
nice: an oven with a light, a cooler, a rubber band

## The big idea
You are about to farm trillions of invisible creatures. Yogurt is milk transformed by bacteria; sourdough starter is
flour and water colonized by wild yeast and bacteria from the air and the grain. Today you set up both experiments,
and over the next week you will watch two tiny ecosystems come alive.

## Goals
- A jar of homemade yogurt incubating and a sourdough starter begun.
- A lab log you will update every day for a week.

## Objectives
- I can explain how bacteria and yeast change food through fermentation.
- I can control temperature to help the right microbes win.
- I can keep a lab log with observations over time.

## Get ready
Wash the pot, spoon and jars in very hot, soapy water and rinse well. Take the yogurt out of the fridge to warm a little.

## Steps
1. **Yogurt.** Heat the milk to about 180 F (82 C), just steaming with tiny bubbles. This changes milk proteins so
   yogurt sets thick.
2. Cool it to about 110 F (43 C): warm, like a hot bath. Test with a clean finger if no thermometer. Too hot kills
   the bacteria.
3. Stir in the starter yogurt, pour into jars, and keep warm for 6 to 12 hours: in an oven with only the light on,
   or wrapped in towels in a cooler.
4. **Sourdough starter.** In a clean jar, mix 50 g flour and 50 g water (about 1/3 cup flour and 3 1/2 tablespoons
   water). Stir hard, cover loosely, and mark the level with tape.
5. Start the lab log. Write the time, temperature, smell and look of each jar.
6. Every day for a week: discard half the starter, add 50 g flour and 50 g water, and mark the level. Bubbles and
   a rise mean wild yeast is winning.
7. Check the yogurt after 6 hours. Taste it. Longer means tangier.

## Make it yours
- Run a temperature test: one yogurt jar warm, one at room temperature. Compare.
- Name your starter and give it a backstory as a pet.
- Make a flavored yogurt with fruit, honey or jam.

## Stuck?
- The yogurt is runny: it needed more time or more warmth. Leave it longer.
- The starter is not bubbling: move it somewhere warmer and keep feeding it. It can take a week.

## Show it off
Serve a yogurt breakfast and show the family your bubbling starter and your lab log.

## Clean up
Wash the pot right away (milk sticks), refrigerate the yogurt, and keep the starter on the counter.

## For the grown-up
This builds microbiology and chemistry: fermentation, temperature control and lab records. Stove use is involved;
agree first. If the starter grows fuzzy mold or pink streaks, throw it out. Check dairy allergies. **Ask at dinner:**
"Which microbes are living in your starter?"
