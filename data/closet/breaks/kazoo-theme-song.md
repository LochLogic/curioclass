title: Kazoo Theme Song
grade: 3
order: 15
kind: Music
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a cardboard tube, wax paper, a rubber band, a pencil, scissors, paper and a pencil for writing
nice: a comb and tissue paper for a second kazoo, markers to decorate

## The big idea
Every hero has a theme song. When they walk in, the music starts! Build your own kazoo. Then write and play a theme song for you, your pet, or your family.

## Goals
- You make a kazoo that buzzes.
- You write and play a theme song with words.

## Objectives
- I can make a sound by humming into my kazoo.
- I can play loud, soft, high and low.
- I can make a tune with a pattern that repeats.

## Get ready
Get a tube from paper towels or toilet paper. Cut a square of wax paper a little bigger than the end of the tube.

## Steps
1. Put the wax paper over one end. Hold it with a rubber band.
2. Use the pencil tip to poke a small hole in the side of the tube. Make it about 1 inch from the wax paper.
3. Hum into the open end. Do not blow, hum! Do you hear a buzz?
4. Play with sounds (10 minutes). Hum high. Hum low. Hum loud and soft. Hum short "doo-doo" notes.
5. Pick who the song is for. You? Your cat? Your grandpa? Your stuffed bear?
6. Make up a short tune. Try 4 short notes, then 1 long note. Repeat it.
7. Write 4 lines of words. Example: "Here comes Maya, fast as a train. Never scared of thunder, never scared of rain."
8. Practice: sing the words once, then play the tune on the kazoo. Do this 3 times.

## Make it yours
- Write theme songs for every family member.
- Write a "villain" song that sounds sneaky and low.
- Make a song for something boring, like brushing teeth, and make it epic.

## Stuck?
- No buzz: hum, don't blow. Make sure the wax paper is tight.
- Tune won't stick: hum a tune you know, then change a few notes.
- Words won't rhyme: they don't have to! Just keep the beat.

## Show it off
Play each family member their theme song as they walk in the door.

## Clean up
Throw out scraps. Put the kazoo where you can find it.

## For the grown-up
This builds rhythm, pitch, patterns and playful word work. Kazoos can get loud, so pick a room where that is OK. **Ask at dinner:** "Can you play the theme song you wrote for me?"
