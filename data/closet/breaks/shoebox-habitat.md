title: Shoebox Habitat
grade: 2
order: 28
kind: Art
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: High
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: a shoebox, paper, crayons or markers, paint and brushes, glue, tape, scissors, natural things (leaves, sticks, rocks, sand), cotton balls, string, a toy animal or a paper animal
nice: clay or play dough, blue plastic wrap for water, a library book about animal homes

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Every animal has a home that gives it food, water and shelter. Today you will build a tiny world inside a shoebox for one animal. Will it be a jungle, a desert, the ocean or the Arctic?"

## Goals
- A shoebox habitat with a background, a floor and at least 4 details.
- A sign that tells what the animal needs to live there.

## Objectives
- Your child can name what animals need: food, water, shelter and space.
- Your child can choose materials to show a real habitat.
- Your child can write 2 or 3 facts about the habitat.

## Get ready
Turn the shoebox on its side so the open part faces you. Cover the table with newspaper. Put on old clothes for painting.

## Steps
1. **Pick a habitat** (5 minutes). Your child chooses one: rainforest, desert, ocean, Arctic, pond, forest. Talk about what lives there. A library book helps if you have one.
2. **Paint the background** (15 minutes). Sky, trees or water on the back and sides. Let it dry 10 minutes.
3. **Make the floor.** Sand for a desert, leaves and dirt for a forest, blue paper for the ocean, cotton balls for snow.
4. **Add 4 details** (25 minutes). Your child decides: trees from sticks and paper leaves, rocks, a cave from rolled paper, a pond from plastic wrap, a log, clouds from cotton balls hung by string.
5. **Add the animal.** Use a toy animal, or your child draws one on paper, cuts it out, and folds a tab at the bottom to make it stand.
6. **Find food, water, shelter.** Point to each. If something's missing, your child adds it.
7. **Write the sign.** On an index card: "This is a desert. The lizard hides under rocks. It eats bugs." Tape it to the top.

## Make it yours
- Make the animal a made-up creature and invent what it eats and where it sleeps.
- Show day and night: one half sunny, one half dark with paper stars.
- Add a "danger" in the habitat, like a hawk or a flood, and how the animal stays safe.

## Stuck?
- Things won't stick: use tape on the back, or glue and hold for 10 seconds.
- Paint takes too long to dry: color with crayons and markers instead.

## Show it off
Set the habitat on a table and give a "nature show" with your child narrating like an explorer.

## Clean up
Put lids on paint and glue, wash brushes, and roll up the newspaper. Your child wipes the table.

## For the grown-up
This builds life science (habitats and what animals need), planning and fact writing, which match second-grade science. You help with any cutting of cardboard. **Ask at dinner:** "What does your animal need to live, and where does it find it?"
