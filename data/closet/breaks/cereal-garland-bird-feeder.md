title: Cereal Garland Bird Feeder
grade: k
order: 22
kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: O-shaped cereal with no added sugar, a pipe cleaner or string, tape, an apple, a butter knife, scissors (grown-up only), a branch, fence or balcony railing, paper and crayons
nice: birdseed, a cardboard tube, a window where you can watch, a bird picture book

## The big idea
Read aloud: "The birds in our neighborhood are hungry! Let's make them a snack they can hang on to. Then we'll hide and watch who comes to eat. Who will be the first bird at the café?"

## Goals
- Your child makes a cereal garland and hangs it up for birds.
- Your child watches for birds and draws who comes.

## Objectives
- Your child can thread cereal one piece at a time.
- Your child can count pieces and make groups of 10.
- Your child can watch quietly and describe what a bird looks like.

## Get ready
Cut a piece of string about 24 inches long and wrap 2 inches of one end in tape for a needle, or use pipe cleaners. Tie one cereal piece to the other end so the rest don't slide off. Pour about 1 cup of cereal into a bowl. Cut a few apple slices about as thick as your thumb (grown-up).

## Steps
1. Talk about birds: "What do birds eat? Where do they live? How do they hold on to branches?"
2. **Thread the garland** (20 minutes). Your child threads cereal onto the string. Count each piece. After every 10, slide on an apple slice through its middle (poke a hole first, grown-up). How long can it get?
3. Make a second one with a pipe cleaner bent into a heart, a circle or a letter your child chooses.
4. **Hang it up.** Go outside together. Your child picks a spot birds can reach but cats can't: a branch, a fence, a balcony rail. Tie it on (grown-up).
5. **Bird watch** (20 minutes). Sit still by a window or a little way away. Use cardboard tube "spyglasses." Whisper what you see.
6. **Bird journal.** When a bird comes, your child draws it: what color, what size, what is it doing? You write their words. No bird yet? Draw the bird you hope will come.
7. Check the feeder later in the day and the next morning. Is the cereal gone?

## Make it yours
- Give the bird café a name and make a sign with a menu ("Today: Crunchy Os!").
- Make a feeder from a cardboard tube rolled in a little cereal stuck on with a spread of mashed banana.
- Make up a bird song for each bird you see, and sing it when it comes back.

## Stuck?
- No birds come: be patient, it can take a day or two for birds to find a new feeder. Keep checking.
- The string is too floppy for threading: use a pipe cleaner, which stays stiff.

## Show it off
Show the bird journal to the family and take them to see the feeder. Let your child point out "where the robin sat."

## Clean up
Sweep up spilled cereal (or leave it for the birds outside). Wash hands after handling the feeder.

## For the grown-up
This builds fine motor skills, counting by tens, patience and observation of living things. Use plain cereal with no sugar, chocolate or nuts, and skip peanut butter for anyone with nut allergies. Do the apple cutting and tying yourself. If you rent, a balcony railing works fine; take the feeder down after a few days so it doesn't get moldy. **Ask at dinner:** "What do you think the birds said about the café?"
