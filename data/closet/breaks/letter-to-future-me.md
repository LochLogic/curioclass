title: Letter to Future Me
grade: 5
order: 18
kind: Story
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 3 or 4 sheets of paper, a pen, an envelope, tape, markers or colored pencils
nice: a small box for extra keepsakes, a photo of you now, a ribbon

## The big idea
What if you could send a message to yourself, years from now? You at age 18 will be very curious about you at 10 or 11. Write a letter, make some predictions, and seal it. Then wait. It is a time machine made of paper.

## Goals
- You write a letter to yourself that you will open in the future.
- You seal it with a "Do not open until" date.

## Objectives
- I can write a letter with a greeting, details, and a closing.
- I can describe my life now with real details, not just "fine."
- I can make predictions and give reasons for them.

## Get ready
Find a quiet spot. Decide when you will open it: next year, at 13, or at 18. Write that date on the envelope in big letters.

## Steps
1. Start with a greeting. "Dear Future Me" works. Or try "Hey, 18-year-old me!"
2. Write about right now (10 minutes). Your favorite food, song, book, and game. Your best friends. What you worry about. What makes you laugh.
3. Describe one normal day in detail, from waking up to bedtime.
4. Ask your future self five questions. Example: "Do you still hate mushrooms?" "Did you ever learn to skateboard?"
5. Make five predictions with reasons. Example: "I think I will have a dog, because I always ask for one."
6. Write some advice for your future self. What do you hope you never forget?
7. Sign it. Add a drawing or a tracing of your hand. Seal it in the envelope.

## Make it yours
- Write a second, shorter letter from your future self back to you. What might they say?
- Add a "time capsule" box with a small drawing, a list of prices for things today, or a note from a family member.
- Make it a family project where everyone writes their own letter.

## Stuck?
- You don't know what to write: answer this: "If I could tell my future self one thing, it would be ___."
- It feels too personal: you're the only one who will read it. Be honest.
- Your hand gets tired: take a stretch break after each section.

## Show it off
Show the sealed envelope, not the letter. Let your family guess one of your predictions. Then hand it to a grown-up for safekeeping.

## Clean up
Put the pens away. Choose a safe spot for the envelope with your grown-up, and write that spot down somewhere.

## For the grown-up
This builds personal writing, reflection, and making predictions with reasons. Please respect the seal; the letter is private. A labeled spot in a drawer or with important papers keeps it from getting lost. **Ask at dinner:** "What's one prediction you made about your future self?"
