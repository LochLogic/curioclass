grade: 11

kind: Game
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 2+
materials: paper or index cards, pens, small items for goods (dried beans, buttons, paper clips, coins, pasta), a timer, two to five players (siblings, friends over, or the family after dinner)
nice: a die, envelopes for secret goals, colored paper for different currencies

## The big idea
Why does a bag of rice cost less than a diamond? Why do prices jump when something is scarce? You will design a
trading game where players run small island nations, each rich in some goods and short on others. Prices are not
fixed; they move with supply and demand. Can you get rich, or will a sudden storm wreck your plans?

## Goals
- A playable trading game with resources, secret goals and events.
- At least one full game played with real negotiation.

## Objectives
- I can explain supply and demand using examples from the game.
- I can design rules that make trading necessary.
- I can negotiate and adjust my strategy as conditions change.

## Get ready
Pick four goods and assign items: beans are grain, buttons are cloth, paper clips are iron, pasta is spice. Make one
"island card" per player.

## Steps
1. Design the islands (15 minutes). Each island starts with lots of one good and little of the others. Example: Island
   A has 12 grain, 2 cloth, 1 iron, 3 spice.
2. Write secret goal cards. Each player needs a mix to "build" things: a ship needs 4 iron, 3 cloth, 2 spice.
3. Write event cards: "Storm: lose half your grain." "Spice festival: spice is worth double." "Trade ban for one round."
4. Set rules: each round, draw one event card, then trade freely for 3 minutes, then each player builds what they can.
5. Play (40 minutes). Watch prices: how much grain does one iron cost? Write it down each round.
6. After the game, look at your price notes. What happened when something became scarce?

## Make it yours
- Add a bank and loans with interest, and see who goes bankrupt.
- Add a currency players can print, then an event that makes it worthless.
- Make it cooperative: all islands must build one lighthouse together before the storm season.

## Stuck?
- Nobody trades: make goals impossible without trading.
- One player gets too rich too fast: add an event that taxes the richest island.

## Show it off
Host a game night and post the final rich list on the fridge.

## Clean up
Sort goods back into containers and put cards in an envelope.

## For the grown-up
This builds economics, negotiation, math and game design. Players get a real feel for supply and demand. If little kids join,
skip buttons and paper clips and use pasta. **Ask at dinner:** "What made prices change in your game?"
