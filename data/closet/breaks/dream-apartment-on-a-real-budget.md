## The big idea
Ten years from now, you get the keys to your first apartment. But first, the puzzle: can you afford it? You'll pick a job and a salary, build a monthly budget, choose from three apartments on a "rental board," and design the space you'd actually live in. It's a math puzzle with a dream at the end.

## Goals
- A monthly budget that balances to zero (income minus expenses).
- An apartment choice that fits the budget, with a floor plan of how you'd set it up.

## Objectives
- I can calculate monthly take-home pay from a yearly salary.
- I can use the 30 percent rule for housing and build a balanced budget.
- I can make tradeoffs and explain my choices.

## Get ready
Get paper and a calculator. Write the rental board below at the top of a page.

**Rental board:** (A) Studio, 35 square meters, downtown: $1,300 a month. (B) One-bedroom, 55 square meters, 20 minutes by bus: $1,150. (C) Two-bedroom with a roommate, your share: $850, 40 minutes away.

## Steps
1. **Pick a job and salary.** Choose one: a nurse at $70,000 a year, an electrician at $58,000, a graphic designer at $52,000, a chef at $40,000. Or ask a grown-up what a job you like might pay.
2. **Take-home pay.** Divide the yearly salary by 12, then take about 25 percent off for taxes. $52,000 becomes about $3,250 a month.
3. **The 30 percent rule.** Housing should cost about 30 percent of take-home pay or less. Calculate your limit (10 minutes).
4. **Pick an apartment** from the board that fits. If none fit, you'll need to trade something.
5. **Build the budget** (20 minutes): rent, utilities ($120), food ($400), transport (bus pass $80 or car $450), phone ($50), savings (aim for 10 percent), fun, and an emergency fund. Every dollar gets a job. The total must equal take-home pay.
6. **Trade-offs.** If it doesn't balance, decide what to cut and write why.
7. **Design the space.** Draw your apartment to scale: 1 square = 50 cm. Lay out where everything goes.

## Make it yours
- Throw in a surprise: draw a card for a car repair ($600) or a raise ($200 a month). Rebalance.
- Compare two jobs and see how life changes.
- Write a one-page diary entry from your first week living there.

## Stuck?
- Nothing fits: try the roommate option, or the bus instead of a car. That's what real people do.
- The numbers feel fake: ask a grown-up what they think something costs where you live, and use that.

## Show it off
Present your budget and floor plan at dinner. Ask a grown-up if anything is missing.

## Clean up
Keep your budget page. It's a template you can use for real someday.

## For the grown-up
This is financial literacy: percentages, budgeting and trade-offs, all things high school math and personal finance cover. The numbers are rough examples; your local costs make it more real. **Ask at dinner:** "What did you have to give up to make your budget work?"
