## The big idea
Botanists identify trees using a **dichotomous key**: a series of yes or no questions that narrow things down. Smooth edges or toothed? Leaves in pairs or alternating? You'll collect leaves from 6 to 10 trees, press them, make rubbings, and write your own key so anyone can identify trees in your neighborhood.

## Goals
- A collection of leaves from 6 to 10 different trees, pressing in books.
- A handmade book with rubbings, notes and your own identification key.

## Objectives
- I can describe leaves using botanical terms: simple or compound, opposite or alternate, smooth, toothed or lobed.
- I can build a dichotomous key that separates every leaf in my collection.
- I can observe and record details carefully.

## Get ready
Tell someone where you're going. Collect only fallen leaves or ask permission before picking any. Avoid any plant with three leaflets together (it could be poison ivy).

## Steps
1. **Collect** (30 minutes). Find 6 to 10 different trees. Take 2 leaves from each, plus a note about the tree: bark texture, size, where it grows.
2. **Observe.** Lay out the leaves at home. For each, note: Is it **simple** (one blade) or **compound** (many leaflets on one stem)? Are the edges smooth, toothed (jagged) or lobed (with rounded fingers)? Does it have **opposite** or **alternate** leaf arrangement on the stem?
3. **Rubbings.** Put a leaf under plain paper, vein side up. Rub with the side of a crayon. One rubbing per page.
4. **Press.** Place one leaf from each tree between sheets of newspaper inside a heavy book. Stack more books on top. They'll be ready in about a week.
5. **Build your key** (20 minutes). Start with one question that splits your leaves into two groups. "1a. Leaf is compound: go to 2. 1b. Leaf is simple: go to 3." Keep splitting until each leaf has its own end point.
6. Test the key with a leaf someone else hands you.
7. **Assemble the book.** Staple pages together or tie them with string. Add the key at the back.

## Make it yours
- Give each unknown tree a name you made up, then try to find its real name later.
- Make a seasonal book: return to the same trees in spring and add new pages.
- Map where each tree grows on a hand-drawn neighborhood map.

## Stuck?
- Two leaves look the same: look at the arrangement on the stem and the bark.
- No trees nearby: use houseplants, shrubs or herbs from the kitchen.

## Show it off
Give your key to someone at home and challenge them to identify a leaf using it.

## Clean up
Return extra leaves outside, and leave the books stacked until the leaves are flat and dry.

## For the grown-up
This builds biology skills: classification, observation and scientific vocabulary. Teach or remind them to avoid plants they don't know that may cause rashes. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the first question in your key, and why?"
