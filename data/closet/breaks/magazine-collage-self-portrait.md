title: Magazine Collage Self-Portrait
grade: 12
order: 17
kind: Art
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: old magazines, newspapers, catalogs or junk mail, scissors, a glue stick, a sheet of heavy paper or cardboard, a pencil, a mirror
nice: a craft knife and cutting mat, colored paper, a fine black pen

## The big idea
Your face, built from a thousand scraps: a cheek made of a skyline, eyes cut from a sunset, hair from a weather map.
Collage artists build portraits from pieces whose colors and textures do the work of paint. You will build a
self-portrait that looks like you up close and says something about you from across the room.

## Goals
- A finished collage self-portrait on heavy paper.
- A short artist's statement explaining the choices.

## Objectives
- I can sort and use cut paper by value (light, middle, dark) to build form.
- I can choose imagery that adds meaning to a portrait.
- I can compose a portrait with strong contrast and focus.

## Get ready
Cover the table. Prop a mirror where you can see your face. Lightly sketch your face on the heavy paper: eyes about
halfway down the head, nose about halfway between eyes and chin.

## Steps
1. Hunt (20 minutes). Flip through magazines and tear out anything with good color, texture or meaning. Sort scraps
   into three piles: light, middle, dark. Sort by value, not color.
2. Map the shadows on your sketch: under the brows, beside the nose, under the chin.
3. Glue down the big areas first: background, then mid tones on the face.
4. Add darks into the shadows and lights onto highlights (40 minutes). Small pieces give detail; large pieces give calm.
5. Use meaning: a scrap with a word that matters to you, a city you want to live in, a sport, a song lyric.
6. Step back three meters. Does it read as a face? Add more contrast if not.
7. Write a 100-word artist's statement for the back.

## Make it yours
- Make it only in one color family, like all blue scraps, and let value do everything.
- Make a portrait of yourself in ten years.
- Make the background tell a story: where you came from on one side, where you are going on the other.

## Stuck?
- It looks like a mess: you need more contrast. Add the darkest darks you can find.
- You do not like how your face looks: make it stylized or abstract. It is still a portrait.

## Show it off
Hang it up and give an "artist's talk" at dinner with your statement.

## Clean up
Recycle magazine leftovers, wipe glue off the table, and put scissors away.

## For the grown-up
This builds art skills (value, composition, collage) and self-expression. If they use a craft knife, a cutting mat
keeps the table safe. **Ask at dinner:** "What scrap means the most to you?"
