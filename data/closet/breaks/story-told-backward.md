title: Story Told Backward
grade: 12
order: 2
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper or a notebook, a pen, index cards or scrap paper, tape
nice: a highlighter, a second reader

## The big idea
What if a story started with the ending, a broken vase, a stranger at the door, two people who will not speak, and
every scene after that moved backward in time until the reader finally saw the moment it all began? Reverse order
turns "what happens next?" into "how did it come to this?" and that question can be even more gripping.

## Goals
- A short story of five to seven scenes, told in reverse chronological order.
- A first scene that hooks and a final scene (the beginning) that lands like a reveal.

## Objectives
- I can structure a story out of time order and keep it clear.
- I can plant details early that gain meaning later.
- I can end on a beginning that recasts everything before it.

## Get ready
Get five to seven index cards. You will plan one scene per card, then shuffle their order.

## Steps
1. Brainstorm the end (10 minutes): a vivid, odd moment. Example: "Two old friends sit at opposite ends of a train
   car, both pretending not to see each other."
2. Plan the story forward first, one scene per card, from the start to that moment. What happened between them?
3. Flip the order. The story now opens with the last card.
4. Choose a time marker for each scene ("Three years earlier," a season, an age) so readers stay oriented.
5. Draft each scene (40 minutes), 150 to 250 words each. Every scene should answer one question and raise another.
6. Plant echoes: a line, an object, a gesture that appears in the opening and gets its meaning only in the last scene.
7. Make the last scene, the beginning, small and hopeful or small and ominous. Let readers connect it themselves.

## Make it yours
- Tell it through objects: each scene is the history of one item going back in time.
- Run two timelines at once: one forward, one backward, meeting in the middle.
- Write the whole thing in second person, as if the reader is living it in reverse.

## Stuck?
- It is confusing: make your time markers bigger and add one concrete detail in each scene that changes over time.
- The ending is flat: ask "what is the moment they could have chosen differently?" and end there.

## Show it off
Have someone read it, then ask them to tell you the story in forward order. See what they noticed.

## Clean up
Tape your cards into your notebook as a structure map.

## For the grown-up
This builds structure, foreshadowing and revision skills from high school and college writing courses, with a fun
challenge built in. **Ask at dinner:** "What happens at the very beginning of your story?"
