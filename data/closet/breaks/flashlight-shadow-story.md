title: Flashlight Shadow Story
grade: k
order: 16
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a flashlight, a blank wall or a white sheet, paper or thin cardboard (cereal box), scissors (grown-up only), tape, straws or wooden spoons for sticks, a pencil
nice: a lamp without a shade, colored see-through plastic (like a clean candy wrapper) for colored light

## The big idea
Read aloud: "When the lights go out, the shadow people come to life! A shadow is a dark shape where light is blocked. Let's make shadow puppets and tell a spooky, silly or brave story on the wall."

## Goals
- Your child makes 3 or 4 shadow puppets.
- Your child tells a shadow story on the wall.

## Objectives
- Your child can make a shadow by blocking light, and change its size by moving closer or farther.
- Your child can tell a story with a character and a problem.
- Your child can use the words light, dark, shadow, block.

## Get ready
Pick a room you can make dark. Set the flashlight on a stack of books about 6 feet from a blank wall, pointed at the wall. Practice with your own hands first: a dog, a bird.

## Steps
1. Turn off the lights and turn on the flashlight. Wave your hand in front. Ask: "What is that dark shape? Why is it there?" Talk about how your hand blocks the light.
2. **Shadow science.** Your child moves a hand closer to the flashlight. The shadow gets bigger! Farther away, it gets smaller.
3. **Plan the story** (10 minutes). Ask: "Who is in your story? What goes wrong?" Example: a little mouse who is scared of the dark meets a friendly owl.
4. **Make the puppets** (20 minutes). Your child draws each character on cardboard, big (about the size of a hand). You cut them out. Your child tapes each one to a straw or spoon.
5. Add a scenery piece: a house, a tree or a moon, taped to the wall or held by you.
6. **Rehearse.** Your child moves the puppets in front of the light while telling the story. Try making a character grow huge by moving it closer to the light.
7. **Perform** (10 minutes). Your child tells the whole story while you hold the flashlight. Then switch: you hold a puppet too.

## Make it yours
- Add colored light: hold see-through colored plastic over the flashlight for a red sunset or blue night.
- Your child makes a shadow guessing game: hold up a kitchen object, and you guess what it is.
- Use your bodies: stand in front of the light and act out the story as giant shadow people.

## Stuck?
- The shadow is blurry: move the puppet closer to the wall, and turn off more lights.
- Your child is scared of the dark: keep a small light on in the hall, or do it in daylight with the sun through a window.

## Show it off
Put on a shadow show for the family after dinner. Your child can make paper tickets.

## Clean up
Store the puppets in a folder. Your child returns the flashlight to its spot.

## For the grown-up
This mixes early science (how light makes shadows, and how distance changes them) with storytelling. Kids practice planning a story and saying it in order. Use a flashlight or a lamp only, never candles. If you use a lamp, keep the hot bulb away from paper and little hands. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you make your shadow get bigger?"
