title: Artifact from a Lost Civilization
grade: 11
order: 28
kind: Art
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: High
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: salt dough (2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, about 3/4 cup water) or air-dry clay, a fork, a toothpick, coffee or tea, a brush or paper towel, paper, a pen, a baking tray and oven (or a sunny windowsill)
nice: acrylic paint, sandpaper, a rope or string, twigs or pebbles

## The big idea
Archaeologists have just pulled a strange object out of the ground. Nobody knows who made it or what it was for. That
object is yours to invent. You will design a lost civilization, sculpt one of its artifacts, age it until it looks
ancient, and write the museum label as if it were real.

## Goals
- A sculpted and aged artifact: a tablet, a figurine, a coin, a tool or a mask.
- A museum label with its name, origin, date and a theory of its purpose.

## Objectives
- I can design a fictional culture with its own beliefs and symbols.
- I can sculpt and add texture and detail to clay.
- I can age an object convincingly and present it with a museum-style label.

## Get ready
Cover a table with newspaper. Mix the salt dough: flour and salt in a bowl, add water little by little, and knead
until smooth. Turn the oven on to its lowest setting (around 200 F or 95 C) if you will bake.

## Steps
1. Invent the civilization (10 minutes). Where did they live? What did they worship? What did they fear? Example:
   a desert people who worshiped the wind and feared silence.
2. Design three symbols for their writing or art.
3. Sculpt the artifact (25 minutes). A tablet is easiest: a 10 cm slab carved with symbols using a toothpick.
4. Add damage on purpose: a broken corner, a crack, a worn edge. Real artifacts are rarely perfect.
5. Dry it: bake 2 to 3 hours on low, or air-dry for a day or two.
6. Age it. Brush strong coffee or tea all over, then wipe it off so it stays in the cracks. Paint a little
   white or green for minerals.
7. Write the label (15 minutes): "Wind Tablet, Sennari culture, about 1200 BCE. Found at the dry lake site. Experts
   believe it was used in a ceremony to call the rain."

## Make it yours
- Make a set of artifacts from one tomb that tell a story together.
- Write a field notebook page from the archaeologist who dug it up.
- Leave a mystery: an object nobody can explain, and invite the family to theorize.

## Stuck?
- The dough cracks: add water a spoonful at a time and knead again.
- It looks too new: rub sandpaper over edges and add more coffee in the cracks.

## Show it off
Display it on a cloth with the label and give a museum curator's talk to the family.

## Clean up
Wipe the table, wash bowls and brushes, and turn the oven off.

## For the grown-up
This builds sculpting, worldbuilding, writing and an understanding of archaeology and how historians interpret
objects. The oven is used at low heat; agree on that first. **Ask at dinner:** "What was your civilization afraid of?"
