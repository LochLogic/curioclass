title: Make Your Own Jigsaw
grade: 2
order: 25
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: a piece of thin cardboard (a cereal box), white paper, glue stick, crayons or markers, scissors, a pencil, an envelope or zip bag
nice: colored pencils, a ruler

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Puzzle makers draw a picture and cut it into pieces so other people can put it back together. Today you get to make your own puzzle. What picture will you hide in all those pieces?"

## Goals
- A finished jigsaw puzzle with 12 to 20 pieces.
- Someone at home puts the puzzle together.

## Objectives
- Your child can make a detailed drawing that fills a page.
- Your child can draw piece shapes and cut along lines carefully.
- Your child can use color, edges and shapes to solve a puzzle.

## Get ready
Cut the front of a cereal box into a flat rectangle about the size of a sheet of paper. Glue white paper over the printed side with the glue stick, pressing out any bumps.

## Steps
1. **Plan the picture** (5 minutes). Your child chooses: their house, a favorite animal, a beach scene, a rocket in space. Fill the whole page; empty spaces are hard to solve.
2. **Draw and color** (25 minutes). Bright colors and lots of details make a good puzzle. Your child colors every corner.
3. **Draw the piece lines.** Flip it over. Your child draws 3 lines across and 4 lines down to make a grid. Add a bump or a dip in the middle of each line to make jigsaw "tabs."
4. **Cut it out.** Your child cuts the strips first, then each piece. Cardboard is thick, so help with the hard spots. Go slow on the tabs.
5. **Solve it yourself.** Mix up the pieces and let your child try. Tip: find the corners and edges first.
6. **Make the box.** Your child draws a small copy of the picture on the envelope and writes the title and "12 pieces" (or however many).

## Make it yours
- Make a puzzle with a secret message written on the back that only shows when it's solved.
- Make a two-sided puzzle with a picture on both sides.
- Make a puzzle for a younger kid with 6 big pieces, or a hard one with 30.

## Stuck?
- Cardboard is too hard to cut: use thick paper or a paper plate instead.
- Pieces are too similar: add more colors and details, or make fewer pieces.

## Show it off
Give the puzzle to someone in the family as a challenge. Time how long it takes them.

## Clean up
Put every piece in the envelope, pick up paper scraps, and put the scissors away.

## For the grown-up
This builds drawing, hand strength and careful cutting, plus the problem solving of putting pieces together (shapes, colors and edges). Use kid scissors and help with the thick parts. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the hardest part of your puzzle to put back together?"
