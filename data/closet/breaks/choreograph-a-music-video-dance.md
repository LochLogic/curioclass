title: Choreograph a Music Video Dance
grade: 6
order: 21
kind: Move
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a clear space to dance, paper, a pencil, a song you know by heart (you can hum or sing it, or count "5, 6, 7, 8" out loud)
nice: a radio or any music player, props (a hat, sunglasses, a scarf, a chair), a mirror

## The big idea
Music videos aren't just dancing: they're scenes, outfits, locations, and moments people remember. You're the choreographer and the star. Make a dance to a song you love, then perform it as a "live music video" that moves from room to room like scene changes.

## Goals
- You choreograph at least one minute of dance with your own moves.
- You perform it as a "live music video" with at least two different scenes.

## Objectives
- I can count music in 8-counts and fit moves to the beat.
- I can create and remember a sequence of movements.
- I can use space, levels (high, middle, low), and facial expression to perform.

## Get ready
Clear space so you won't knock anything over. Pick a song (or a beat you can clap). Fold a page into 8 boxes; each box is one 8-count.

## Steps
1. Listen or hum through your song twice. Count "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8" along with the beat. Notice where the chorus starts.
2. Make a move bank (10 minutes). Invent or remember 10 moves and name them: "the robot arm," "slide and freeze," "the window wipe," "spin and drop."
3. Choreograph the chorus first. Fill four 8-counts with moves. Write the move names in your boxes.
4. Choreograph a verse. Use different moves so the chorus stands out when it comes back.
5. Add levels and travel: one move low to the floor, one jump, and one move that travels across the space.
6. Plan your "video" (10 minutes). Pick two or three locations at home (hallway, kitchen doorway, backyard) as scenes. Decide which part of the dance happens where, and add one prop moment.
7. Rehearse five times. The last two times, add facial expressions. Sell it!

## Make it yours
- Add a slow-motion section where every move is done at half speed.
- Make one shot a "close-up": just your hands dancing in a doorway.
- Teach one 8-count to a family member and make them your backup dancer.

## Stuck?
- You forget the order: practice one 8-count at a time, then chain them together.
- Moves feel awkward: make them bigger. Big moves look confident even if they're silly.
- No music player: sing the song yourself, or ask someone to clap the beat.

## Show it off
Premiere your live music video: lead your audience from scene to scene and perform each part where it "was filmed."

## Clean up
Put any furniture you moved back where it belongs and return props.

## For the grown-up
This builds musical timing (counting beats), memory, creativity, and performance confidence, and it's a good workout. The "music video" part is all live, with no camera needed. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the name of your best move? Can you show us?"
