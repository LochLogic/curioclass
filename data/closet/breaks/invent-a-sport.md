title: Invent a Sport
grade: 4
order: 4
kind: Move
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a soft ball or rolled-up socks, a laundry basket or bucket, a pillow, tape or chalk, paper, a pencil
nice: a timer, a whistle, a frisbee or paper plate

## The big idea
Someone invented soccer. Someone invented basketball. Today, you invent a brand-new sport! Pick your gear, make the rules and play the first game ever.

## Goals
- You create a new sport with at least 5 rules.
- You play it and change the rules to make it better.

## Objectives
- I can write fair rules that everyone can follow.
- I can test a game and fix what is not fun.
- I can explain how to score points.

## Get ready
Find a safe space. A yard, a park with a grown-up, or a cleared room with soft gear only. Gather 3 or 4 pieces of gear.

## Steps
1. Mix up two sports. Example: bowling plus basketball becomes "Bowl-ketball."
2. Pick your gear. Use 2 or 3 things, like a sock ball and a basket.
3. Set up the field. Mark the start, the goal and the edges with tape or chalk.
4. Write 5 rules (10 minutes). How do you score? What's not allowed? How does a turn end? How do you win?
5. Play alone first. Try 3 rounds. Is it too hard? Too easy?
6. Change one rule. Play again.
7. Make it work for 2 or more players. Who goes first? Do players take turns?
8. Name your sport and design a team logo.

## Make it yours
- Add a silly rule, like "every point must be scored while hopping."
- Make it a team sport with a pass rule.
- Write a short announcer script for a big game.

## Stuck?
- The game is boring: add a time limit or a bonus point zone.
- The game is too hard: move the goal closer.
- Players argue: write the rule more clearly on the rule sheet.

## Show it off
Teach the family your sport after dinner. Hold the first official match.

## Clean up
Peel up tape or wash away chalk. Put the gear back.

## For the grown-up
This builds writing clear rules, fairness, and lots of movement. Indoors, use only soft balls and keep away from glass and lamps. If your child plays in a park, go with them. **Ask at dinner:** "Which rule did you change, and why?"
