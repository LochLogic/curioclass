grade: 11
order: 9
kind: Move
time: 75-120 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a bike and helmet or good walking shoes, paper, a pencil, a clipboard or a book to press on, water, a watch or phone for time
nice: a paper street map of your area, colored pencils, a friend to scout with

## The big idea
Cities pay planners to figure out the best routes for bikes and walkers: the quiet streets, the steep hills, the
spot where everyone gets stuck at a crossing. Today you are the scout. Ride or walk a loop near home, rate every
stretch, and turn it into a hand-drawn route map someone else could follow.

## Goals
- A completed loop of 3 to 10 km (bike) or 2 to 5 km (walking) that starts and ends at home.
- A hand-drawn map rating each stretch for safety, effort and scenery.

## Objectives
- I can plan a route and estimate distance and time.
- I can observe road conditions and rate them by clear rules.
- I can draw a map with a legend, scale and directions.

## Get ready
Tell a grown-up your loop, how long you will be gone, and when you will check in. Check bike tires and brakes, or lace
up your shoes. Make a three-symbol legend: green (calm), yellow (careful), red (avoid).

## Steps
1. Sketch your planned loop from memory (10 minutes). Guess the distance and time before you go.
2. Head out. Stop at each turn or major intersection and note the time.
3. Rate each stretch from 1 to 5 for three things: **safety** (traffic, crossings), **effort** (hills, bumps), and
   **scenery** (trees, views, interesting buildings).
4. Note hazards and perks: a blind corner, a broken sidewalk, a water fountain, a shady bench, a great view.
5. Head home and compare: how far off were your guesses?
6. Draw the final map (25 minutes). Color each stretch by your safety rating. Add a rough scale ("one finger width =
   about 300 m"), a north arrow and your legend.
7. Write three tips for anyone using the route. Example: "Walk the bike across Elm; the light is very short."

## Make it yours
- Design a theme route: "best trees," "most dogs per kilometer," or "flattest way to the library."
- Plan a challenge version for later with one big hill and a timed segment.
- Scout two routes to the same place and decide which one a nervous beginner should take.

## Stuck?
- You cannot estimate distance: count your steps or pedal turns for one block, then multiply.
- You get lost from your plan: write down street names as you go, then fix the map at home.

## Show it off
Give a grown-up a five-minute "planning meeting" with your map, then lead them on the route on a weekend.

## Clean up
Put the bike away, refill your water bottle, and pin the map somewhere useful.

## For the grown-up
This builds fitness, map reading, estimation and civic awareness of how streets are designed. Agree on the area,
the time limit and the check-in. A helmet is a must on a bike. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the most dangerous spot,
and how would you fix it?"
