materials: paper of different kinds (lined, plain, scrap, envelopes, sticky notes), pens in different colors, a large envelope or folder
nice: cold tea and a brush to "age" paper, string, old stamps or stickers, a typewriter-style font for headings drawn by hand

## The big idea
An **epistolary** story is told entirely through documents: letters, diary pages, notes, receipts and postcards. The reader becomes a detective, putting the story together from the pieces. You'll write and physically make a stack of documents that tells a full story, and hand it to someone in an envelope.

## Goals
- A set of 8 to 12 handmade documents that tell a complete story.
- A packet someone else can read and piece together.

## Objectives
- I can tell a story through multiple voices and document types.
- I can reveal plot through what characters say and don't say.
- I can make each document's form fit its writer and purpose.

## Get ready
Gather different paper types. Decide on two or three characters who would write to each other.

## Steps
1. **Premise** (10 minutes). Ideas: a lighthouse keeper writing to their sister as strange lights appear at sea; two pen pals in different centuries who discover their letters are crossing time; a missing cat told through posters, notes and a neighbor's diary.
2. **Plot it in documents.** List 8 to 12 pieces in order: who wrote it, who it's for, and what it reveals. Each one should add a new clue or twist.
3. **Give each writer a voice.** One uses long, careful sentences. One writes in a rush with no punctuation. One crosses things out.
4. **Use the gaps.** What happens between documents? Readers will fill it in. The biggest event might never be written about directly.
5. **Write and make the documents** (50 minutes). Use different paper for each writer. A receipt is a narrow strip. A diary page has a date. A telegram is short and in capitals.
6. **Age them** if it fits: brush cold tea on paper, let it dry, crumple and flatten.
7. Put them in order in an envelope, or deliberately out of order so readers have to sort them.

## Make it yours
- Make it a mystery: the reader must figure out who did it from the documents alone.
- Hide one document somewhere in the house with a clue to find it.
- Write it as messages between two characters who never meet.

## Stuck?
- It's all one voice: give each writer a quirk, like one always signs off differently.
- The story is confusing: add one document that clearly states a date or event to anchor the reader.

## Show it off
Hand the envelope to someone at home and watch them read. Ask them to tell you the story back.

## Clean up
Recycle scraps and keep the finished packet somewhere dry.

## For the grown-up
Epistolary writing builds English skills like voice, structure and inference, and it's a classic form in literature. Tea for aging paper should be cool. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the one thing the letters never say directly?"
