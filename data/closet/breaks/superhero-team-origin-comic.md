title: Superhero Team Origin Comic
grade: 5
order: 9
kind: Story
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 3 or 4 sheets of paper, a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, markers or colored pencils, a black pen
nice: a stapler, sticky notes for planning

## The big idea
Every superhero team has a first day. Before the capes, before the base, they were just ordinary people. Then something happened. Your job: invent a brand new team of heroes. Then draw the comic of how they met and got their powers.

## Goals
- You invent a team of three or four original heroes, each with a power and a weakness.
- You draw a 2 to 4 page comic that tells the team's origin story.

## Objectives
- I can plan a story in panels (the boxes in a comic).
- I can use speech bubbles, captions, and sound words.
- I can create characters with powers that work better together.

## Get ready
Rule your pages with a ruler. Make 6 boxes on each page, two across and three down. Leave a little gap between boxes.

## Steps
1. Invent your heroes (15 minutes). Give each one a name, a power, a weakness, and an everyday job. Example: "Static Sam. Shocks things. Weak to water. Works at a laundromat."
2. Make their powers fit together. One hero is strong but slow. Another is fast but weak. Together they win.
3. Pick the event that gave them powers. A strange storm? A science fair gone wrong? A mysterious old library book?
4. Plan the story on sticky notes or scrap paper. One sentence per panel. Page 1: normal life. Page 2: the event. Page 3: the first big problem. Page 4: they team up and win.
5. Sketch your panels lightly in pencil (30 minutes). Big actions get big boxes.
6. Add words. Speech bubbles for talking. Captions in boxes for "Meanwhile..." Sound words like ZZAP and KRUNCH.
7. Ink over the pencil with pen. Then add color.

## Make it yours
- Give the villain an origin too, from the same event.
- Make heroes with silly powers, like talking to houseplants or never losing socks.
- Draw a cover for "Issue 1" with the team name in big letters.

## Stuck?
- Drawing people is hard: use stick figures with big expressive faces. Many comics start that way.
- Too much story: cut it down to the four main moments, one page each.
- Words don't fit in the bubble: write the words first, then draw the bubble around them.

## Show it off
Read your comic out loud to your family. Do the voices for each hero.

## Clean up
Cap the markers and stack your pages. Staple them into a book if you have a stapler.

## For the grown-up
Comics build real writing skills: planning a plot, writing dialogue, and showing a story through pictures. They also build teamwork thinking, since the heroes need each other. Expect some pages to be messy; the story matters more than the drawing. **Ask at dinner:** "Why does your team need each member? What would go wrong without one?"
