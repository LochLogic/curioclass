title: Field Guide to My Block
grade: 6
order: 10
kind: Outside
time: 60-120 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper (6 to 8 sheets folded and stapled into a booklet), a pencil, colored pencils, a clipboard or a hard book to write on
nice: a magnifying glass, a small bag for fallen leaves, binoculars, a field guide book from home or the library

## The big idea
Scientists make field guides so other people can recognize what they find. Your block, building, or street is full of living things nobody has written a guide for yet. Become the official naturalist of your neighborhood and write the first field guide to it.

## Goals
- You find and document at least 10 living things (plants, animals, fungi) near your home.
- You make a field guide booklet with a page for each one.

## Objectives
- I can observe and sketch living things in detail.
- I can describe living things by features like shape, color, size, and habitat.
- I can organize information into a guide others could use.

## Get ready
Get a grown-up's permission for where you can go: your yard, your block, a nearby park, or a courtyard. Make the booklet and write "Field Guide to [your street name]" on the cover.

## Steps
1. Walk slowly and look in layers (15 minutes): up (birds, trees), middle (bushes, fences, walls), and down (sidewalk cracks, grass, under leaves).
2. Pick your first 10 finds. Mix it up: some plants, some animals, maybe a mushroom or lichen (the crusty stuff on rocks and bark).
3. Make a page for each. Sketch it, then add four facts: Size (compare to something, like "as big as my thumb"), Color, Where Found, and When Seen.
4. Add a "how to recognize it" tip. Example: "Look for the leaves that come in groups of three."
5. Give a name. Use the real name if you know it. If not, invent a descriptive one, like "Purple Sidewalk Flower" or "Fence Spider with Striped Legs."
6. Add a rarity rating: Common (seen 5+ times), Uncommon (2 to 4), Rare (just once).
7. Finish with a map page showing where you found things, and a table of contents.

## Make it yours
- Add a "Most Wanted" page for something you've heard of but haven't spotted yet.
- Make a night guide: what do you see or hear at dusk from a window or doorway?
- Write it in the voice of a dramatic explorer: "Day 3. The ants have noticed me."

## Stuck?
- You can't find 10 things: look at weeds in cracks, bugs on walls, and birds on wires. Even moss counts.
- Animals don't hold still: sketch quickly from memory and fill in details later.
- You're stuck inside: do a window guide, documenting what you can see from one window over an hour.

## Show it off
Take your family on a "guided tour" of your block using your field guide. Can they spot your rare find?

## Clean up
Return anything you picked up (only take fallen leaves, never living plants). Wash your hands.

## For the grown-up
This is real life science: observation, classification, and scientific illustration. Agree on boundaries ahead of time and ask your child to tell you before leaving the yard. Remind them to look but not touch insects or plants they don't know (some sting or cause rashes). **Ask at dinner:** "What was the most surprising thing living on our block?"
