where: Indoors

materials: an old piece of clothing you're allowed to change (jeans, a jacket, a plain shirt, a tote), fabric scraps from worn-out clothes, a needle, thread (contrast colors look best), scissors, pins or clips, chalk or a pencil
nice: embroidery thread, a thimble, an iron (with a grown-up's OK)

## The big idea
A rip doesn't have to be the end. **Visible mending** turns holes into art with bold stitches and patches you show off instead of hide. You'll take a worn or boring piece of clothing and flip it into something one of a kind, with techniques tailors have used for centuries.

## Goals
- One piece of clothing repaired or redesigned with at least one patch and decorative stitching.
- A sample cloth with three stitches you practiced.

## Objectives
- I can thread a needle, knot thread and sew a running stitch and a whipstitch.
- I can plan a design that fits the shape of the clothing.
- I can attach a patch so it lasts through washing.

## Get ready
Get permission for the piece you're changing. Wash your hands. Cut a 15 cm square from a scrap for your sample cloth. Thread a needle with an arm's length of thread and tie a knot at the end.

## Steps
1. **Practice on the sample** (15 minutes):
   - Running stitch: up, down, up, down in a straight line, stitches about 5 mm long.
   - Whipstitch: loop over the edge of a fabric, good for attaching patches.
   - Backstitch: each stitch goes back to meet the last one, making a solid line.
2. **Plan the design.** Lay the clothing flat. Chalk where patches or stitching will go. Consider a patch on a knee, stitched lines across a pocket, or a stitched star on a back.
3. Cut a patch 2 cm bigger than the hole on all sides. Round the corners so they don't peel.
4. Pin the patch in place (on the outside for a bold look, or behind for a peek-through look).
5. Sew the patch with whipstitch around the edge (25 minutes). Keep your other hand inside the clothing so you don't sew the front to the back.
6. **Decorate** with running stitches in a grid, rays or rows across the patch. Contrast thread on dark denim looks great.
7. Knot off on the inside: make a tiny stitch, pass the needle through the loop twice, pull tight and trim.

## Make it yours
- Design a patch that tells a story: a map of your neighborhood, a symbol only your friends get.
- Turn the clothing into something else: a shirt into a tote bag, or jeans into shorts with a stitched hem.
- Make a matching pair: patch two items with the same design for you and a friend.

## Stuck?
- The thread keeps knotting: use a shorter piece, and let the needle dangle every few stitches to untwist.
- The patch puckers: you're pulling too tight. Stitch more loosely, and smooth the fabric as you go.

## Show it off
Wear it at dinner and give a "designer interview" about your choices.

## Clean up
Stick needles back in a pincushion or a scrap of fabric, count them, and sweep for dropped pins.

## For the grown-up
This is textile art and practical repair: hand sewing, design, and a habit of fixing instead of tossing. Needles are sharp; a pincushion and a count at the end help. If they use an iron, it's with your OK. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the story behind your patch?"
