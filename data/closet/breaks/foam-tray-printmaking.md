materials: clean foam trays (from fruit, vegetables or baked goods, washed with soap), scissors, a dull pencil or ballpoint pen, washable paint, a flat plate or baking sheet for rolling paint, a small sponge or brush, paper, a large spoon, newspaper
nice: a small foam roller or rolling pin, colored paper, thicker paper like cardstock

## The big idea
Printmaking lets you make the same picture again and again. You'll carve a design into a foam tray, ink it and press it onto paper to make a **relief print**, where the raised surface carries the ink and the carved lines stay white. Artists have made posters, book art and protest signs this way for hundreds of years.

## Goals
- A finished printing plate carved from foam.
- An "edition" of at least 5 prints, numbered and signed.

## Objectives
- I can design for printing, knowing the image will come out reversed.
- I can ink a plate evenly and burnish (rub) a clean print.
- I can number an edition the way printmakers do.

## Get ready
Cover the table with newspaper. Cut the curved edges off a tray to get a flat piece about 10 x 15 cm. Squeeze a little paint onto a plate.

## Steps
1. **Sketch** (10 minutes). Draw your design on paper the same size as your foam. Keep it bold: thick lines and big shapes print better than tiny details.
2. **Reverse it.** Any letters must be drawn backward on the foam, because the print flips. Hold your sketch up to a window and trace the back of it to see the mirror image.
3. **Carve.** Press into the foam with a dull pencil. Press hard enough to make a groove, but not through. Go over lines twice to deepen them (15 minutes).
4. **Ink.** Dab or roll a thin, even layer of paint over the whole plate. Too much and the grooves fill in.
5. **Print.** Lay the paper on the plate. Rub with the back of a spoon in small circles over every part. Peel slowly from one corner.
6. Make 5 or more prints, re-inking each time. The first one is usually a test.
7. **Edition.** In pencil, below each print, write the number like "3/5" on the left and sign on the right.

## Make it yours
- Do a two-color print: print the whole plate in yellow, carve more lines, then print in blue over the top, lining up the corners.
- Make a series of four plates that tell a short story.
- Print on something else: a card, a cloth bag, or wrapping paper.

## Stuck?
- The print is blotchy: use less paint and rub harder with the spoon.
- Lines filled in: carve deeper, and wipe extra paint off the plate before printing.

## Show it off
Hang the whole edition in a row like a gallery, and give one print to each family member.

## Clean up
Rinse the plate and plates in water while the paint is wet, and wipe the table. Keep the plate to print again.

## For the grown-up
This teaches relief printmaking, design in reverse, and the idea of an artist's edition, all part of high school art. Use only clean trays from produce or baked goods, not from raw meat. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did your letters have to be backward?"
