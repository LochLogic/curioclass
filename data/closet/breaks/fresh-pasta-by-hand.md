grade: 10
order: 18
kind: Kitchen
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: High
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 200 g (about 1 and 2/3 cups) all-purpose flour, 2 eggs, a pinch of salt, a fork, a clean counter or big board, a rolling pin (or a clean, smooth bottle), a knife, plastic wrap or a bowl to cover the dough, a large pot, a colander, butter or olive oil, grated cheese or garlic
nice: semolina flour, a bench scraper, fresh herbs, black pepper

## The big idea
Some of the world's best food has only two ingredients. Flour and eggs, plus your hands, become silky noodles in under two hours. Fresh pasta cooks in 2 minutes, tastes completely different from dried, and makes any dinner feel like an event. Today you're the pasta maker.

## Goals
- Fresh, hand-rolled and hand-cut pasta for 2 to 3 people.
- A simple sauce and a served dinner course.

## Objectives
- I can use the well method to mix dough by hand.
- I can knead until the dough is smooth and elastic, and explain what gluten does.
- I can cook fresh pasta to the right texture on my own.

## Get ready
Get a grown-up's OK for the stove and check for egg or wheat allergies. Clean and dry a big work surface. Wash your hands.

## Steps
1. **The well.** Pile the flour on the counter and make a crater in the middle, wide enough for the eggs. Crack in the eggs and add salt.
2. **Mix.** Beat the eggs with a fork, then slowly pull in flour from the inside wall of the well. Keep the wall up until it gets thick, then fold everything in with your hands.
3. **Knead** (8 to 10 minutes). Push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back and turn it. It starts shaggy and becomes smooth, like play dough. If it's sticky, add a pinch of flour. If it's dry and cracking, wet your hands.
4. **Rest.** Wrap the dough and let it sit for 30 minutes. The **gluten** (stretchy protein) relaxes, so it rolls without shrinking back. Plan your sauce while you wait.
5. **Roll** (15 minutes). Cut the dough in half. Roll each half from the middle outward, turning it often, until it's thin enough to see your hand through it, about 1 mm.
6. **Cut.** Dust the sheet with flour, roll it loosely into a log, and slice across into ribbons, 5 mm for thin, 1 cm for wide. Shake them out.
7. **Cook.** Boil salted water, drop in the pasta, and cook 2 to 3 minutes. Taste one. Save a cup of pasta water, then drain.
8. Toss with butter or oil, cheese and a splash of pasta water to make it silky.

## Make it yours
- Shape something new: bow ties (pinch rectangles in the middle) or filled pillows (a spoonful of ricotta between two sheets).
- Color the dough with a spoon of pureed spinach or tomato paste.
- Invent a sauce from what's in the fridge and give it a name.

## Stuck?
- The dough won't come together: add water a teaspoon at a time.
- The noodles stick together: use more flour when cutting, and cook them right away.

## Show it off
Serve it as the main course for the family and explain how it was made.

## Clean up
Scrape dried dough off the counter with a bench scraper or spatula before wiping, so it doesn't smear. Wash the pot and board.

## For the grown-up
This builds kitchen technique, food science (gluten, starch) and independence at the stove. Check for egg and wheat allergies first. Raw egg means washing hands and surfaces with soap. **Ask at dinner:** "What happened to the dough while it rested?"
