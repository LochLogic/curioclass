title: Memory Box of High School
grade: 12
order: 25
kind: Art
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a sturdy box (a shoebox works), old photos, tickets, notes and keepsakes, paper, pens, glue, scissors, tape
nice: paint, fabric, ribbon, small envelopes, a craft knife and cutting mat

## The big idea
High school ends in a blur. A memory box captures it before it fades: a handmade box full of the small things that
mattered, each one labeled with the story behind it. You will turn a plain box into a work of art that you will want
to open in 20 years.

## Goals
- A decorated box filled with at least ten labeled items.
- A hidden compartment with a letter to open later.

## Objectives
- I can choose objects that represent important moments.
- I can design a box with a clear theme inside and out.
- I can write short, specific captions for each item.

## Get ready
Gather keepsakes from drawers, backpacks and bulletin boards. Clear a table.

## Steps
1. Sort the keepsakes (15 minutes). Choose ten or more that each tell a story.
2. Choose a theme: a map of your four years, a museum, a time capsule, a treasure chest.
3. Decorate the outside (25 minutes): collage, paint, drawing, your name and the years. Make it bold.
4. Divide the inside into sections with cardboard dividers: freshman, sophomore, junior, senior.
5. Write a caption for each item on a small card: what it is, the date, and one sentence about why it matters.
6. Make a hidden compartment in the lid or a false bottom.
7. Write a letter to yourself to open in ten years and seal it in the hidden spot.

## Make it yours
- Add a "soundtrack" card with a song for each year.
- Make it a pop-up box where opening the lid lifts a scene.
- Ask friends to add one item each with a note.

## Stuck?
- You do not have many keepsakes: draw or write memories on cards instead, like "the night the power went out at prom."
- It looks cluttered: use fewer, bigger items and give each one space.

## Show it off
Give the family a guided tour of the box, item by item.

## Clean up
Put away art supplies and store the box where it will stay dry.

## For the grown-up
This builds design, writing and reflection, and it often turns into a meaningful keepsake. **Ask at dinner:** "What
item in the box has the best story?"
