time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 8 or more sheets of paper, scissors, tape, a pencil, a ruler, an index card or a piece of paper about 8 by 12 cm
nice: a highlighter, newspaper for giant strips, colored paper so you can track the sides

## The big idea
Can a piece of paper have only one side? Can you cut a loop in half and get one bigger loop? Can you walk through an index card? These sound impossible. They're not. Today you explore the weirdest shapes in math, make predictions, and get proven wrong in the best way.

## Goals
- You build and cut at least five strange paper loops, predicting each result first.
- You turn your best discoveries into a short "math magic" show.

## Objectives
- I can make a prediction, test it, and explain what surprised me.
- I can describe a Mobius strip, a loop with only one side and one edge.
- I can follow precise steps for cutting and taping.

## Get ready
Cut 10 strips of paper, each about 3 cm wide and 28 cm long (the long side of a sheet). Make a chart: Loop, My Prediction, What Happened.

## Steps
1. **Plain loop.** Tape a strip into a ring with no twist. Draw a line down the middle all the way around. Notice there's an inside and an outside.
2. **Mobius strip.** Take a new strip, give one end a half twist, then tape it. Draw a line down the middle without lifting the pencil. Where does it end up? (Spoiler: on "both" sides, because there's only one.)
3. **Cut it in half.** Predict first: what will you get if you cut along the middle line of the Mobius strip? Write it down, then cut. Write what happened.
4. **Cut it in thirds.** Make a new Mobius strip and cut along a line one-third of the way from the edge, going around twice until you get back to where you started. Predict, cut, record.
5. **Two twists.** Make a loop with two half twists (a full twist). Cut down the middle. Predict, cut, record.
6. **The square.** Make two plain loops and tape them together crosswise, like a plus sign, at one spot. Cut each loop down the middle. Look at what you get (15 minutes for steps 4 to 6).
7. **Walk through a card.** Fold the index card in half the long way. Cut slits from the fold almost to the edge, about 1 cm apart. Then flip it and cut slits from the open edge between the first cuts, almost to the fold. Open it carefully and cut along the fold, except the two end strips. Unfold it into a huge ring and step through!

## Make it yours
- Try a loop with three half twists. Does it have one side or two?
- Make a Mobius strip from a long strip of colored paper and write a never-ending sentence on it.
- Invent your own experiment: tape two Mobius strips together, or cut in quarters, and see what happens.

## Stuck?
- The cuts get messy: start each cut by folding the strip gently and snipping a small hole.
- The card ring breaks: your slits went too close to the edge. Stop about 5 mm short.
- Results don't match a friend's: check that you made a half twist, not a full twist.

## Show it off
Put on a "math magic" show. Hand someone a Mobius strip and ask them to predict the cut, then cut it in front of them. Finish by stepping through the index card.

## Clean up
Gather paper scraps into recycling and tape your best loops into your chart as evidence.

## For the grown-up
This is topology, a branch of math about shapes that bend and twist, and it's a great lesson in predicting, testing, and being happily wrong. It uses only paper and scissors. **Ask at dinner:** "What happened when you cut the Mobius strip in half, and why is it so strange?"
