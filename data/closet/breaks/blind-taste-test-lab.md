grade: 6
order: 23
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 2+
materials: 5 or 6 foods cut into small cubes the same size (good ones: apple, pear, raw potato, cucumber, carrot, a tiny piece of raw onion), small cups or a muffin tin, toothpicks, a scarf or sleep mask for a blindfold, a cutting board, a kitchen knife, paper and a pencil, water for rinsing, a partner (a sibling, a friend, or a grown-up after work)
nice: two kinds of the same drink (two juices, two brands of crackers), a clothespin or just pinched fingers for the nose

## The big idea
Is taste really in your tongue? Scientists say most of what we call "flavor" actually comes from smell. If that's true, then with your nose pinched, an apple and a raw onion might taste... the same. Set up a lab and find out.

## Goals
- You run a blind taste test with your nose open and with your nose pinched.
- You compare results and decide how much smell matters to taste.

## Objectives
- I can design a fair test with a control (the "nose open" round).
- I can record data and calculate a percent correct.
- I can explain how smell and taste work together to make flavor.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up for permission to use a knife and check food allergies for everyone tasting. Wash your hands. Make a data table: Sample, Nose Open Guess, Nose Pinched Guess, Correct?

## Steps
1. Cut each food into same-size cubes, about 1 cm. Same size and shape means the taster can't tell by feel.
2. Put each food in a numbered cup. Write the secret key (1 = apple, 2 = potato, and so on) on a separate paper and hide it.
3. Write a hypothesis: "With their nose pinched, my tester will get ___ out of 6 right."
4. **Round 1: nose pinched.** Blindfold your tester. They pinch their nose, chew, and guess. Record each guess. They sip water between samples.
5. **Round 2: nose open.** Mix up the cup order. Same test, but now they can smell. Record the guesses.
6. Score both rounds. Figure out the percent correct: correct answers divided by total, times 100.
7. Switch jobs. Now you're the tester. Compare your results with your partner's.

## Make it yours
- Test whether looks trick taste: color plain water with food coloring and ask what "flavor" it is.
- Try the jelly-bean-style challenge with two juices or two kinds of cracker.
- Test more people and make a bar graph of nose-open vs. nose-pinched scores.

## Stuck?
- Tester guesses right by texture: cut softer foods smaller, or choose foods with similar crunch (apple, pear, potato).
- Tester peeks: use a sleep mask or turn them to face the wall.
- No partner right now: set it up so someone else hands you samples, or test the "nose pinch" idea on yourself, eyes closed.

## Show it off
Present your results like a science fair: hypothesis, data table, and your conclusion. Then let your family try round 1.

## Clean up
Wash the knife and board right away, compost or toss scraps, and wipe the counter.

## For the grown-up
This is a controlled experiment about the senses, with real data and percentages. Please check every taster's food allergies, okay the knife use, and check in while they cut. Raw potato and onion are safe in tiny pieces but not tasty, which is part of the fun. **Ask at dinner:** "With your nose pinched, what could you NOT tell apart?"
