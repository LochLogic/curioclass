title: Fantasy Map and World Book
grade: 6
order: 4
kind: Story
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a large sheet of paper (or 4 taped together), 4 or 5 sheets of paper for the world book, pencil, eraser, black pen, colored pencils or markers
nice: tea to stain the map an "old" color, a stapler, a ruler

## The big idea
Every great fantasy story has a map in the front. Rivers, mountains, ruins, and a place nobody dares to go. Today you invent a whole world: draw the map, then write the book that explains it. Who lives there, what happened long ago, and what's hiding in that dark forest?

## Goals
- You draw a detailed map of an invented land with at least 12 named places.
- You write a short world book with history, creatures, and a legend.

## Objectives
- I can draw a map that follows real geography (rivers flow downhill to the sea).
- I can invent names and places that fit together.
- I can write in different styles: a history, a guide, a legend.

## Get ready
Lay out the big paper. Decide if your land is an island, a continent, or a valley between mountains. Lightly sketch the coastline first; make it wiggly, since real coasts are never smooth.

## Steps
1. Draw the coastline and big features: mountain ranges (small upside-down Vs), forests (bunches of little trees), deserts, lakes.
2. Add rivers. Start them in the mountains and run them downhill to the sea or a lake. Rivers join together; they don't split as they head downstream.
3. Place towns where people would actually live: on rivers, coasts, and crossroads. Add a castle, a ruin, a port, and one forbidden place.
4. Name everything (15 minutes). Pick a sound for each region so names feel related. A cold northern land might have names like Krask, Vornhold, and Skell; a sunny south might have Lumari and Sol Avesh.
5. Ink the map with pen, then color it. Add a compass rose, a scale bar, and a title cartouche (a decorated label box).
6. Write the world book (30 minutes). Page 1: A short history (who settled the land, one war or disaster). Page 2: Three creatures. Page 3: The legend of the forbidden place.
7. Make a cover and staple it together.

## Make it yours
- Draw a "secret" version of the map that shows hidden tunnels, treasure, or a buried city.
- Write a page from a traveler's diary crossing your land.
- Invent a rule of magic and explain where on the map it's strongest.

## Stuck?
- The map looks empty: add roads connecting towns, then small details like bridges and lone towers.
- Names sound random: pick two or three letter combos for each region and reuse them.
- No ideas for the legend: ask "Why does nobody go there anymore?" and answer it.

## Show it off
Give a "guided tour" of your world. Point to places on the map and read the legend aloud by flashlight for drama.

## Clean up
Roll up the map or store it flat inside the world book. Put away the markers.

## For the grown-up
This is creative writing and geography in one: landforms, how rivers and settlements really work, and writing in different genres. The world often gets bigger over the next few days; that's a great sign. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the story behind the forbidden place?"
