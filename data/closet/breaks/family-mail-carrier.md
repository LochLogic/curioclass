title: Family Mail Carrier
grade: 1
order: 21
kind: Kindness
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: paper, envelopes (or paper folded into envelopes), crayons or markers, tape, a shoebox, a bag for a mailbag, scissors (child can use safety scissors)
nice: stickers for stamps, a hat for the mail carrier, a hole punch

## The big idea
Read aloud: "The Family Post Office is open! Everyone in the house gets their own mailbox, and you are the mail carrier. Your job is to write happy letters and deliver them. Who will get the first piece of mail?"

## Goals
- Your child makes a mailbox for each person at home.
- Your child writes and delivers at least 4 kind letters or cards.

## Objectives
- Your child can write a short message with a greeting and their name ("Dear Mom, ... Love, Sam").
- Your child can think about what would make someone else happy.
- Your child can match names to mailboxes.

## Get ready
Gather a small box, bag or folded paper pocket for each person in the house (and pets!). Fold sheets of paper into cards. Make a list of everyone who will get mail.

## Steps
1. **Build the mailboxes** (15 minutes). Your child decorates a box or paper pocket for each person and writes their name on it. Tape them to bedroom doors or line them up on a shelf.
2. **The post office.** Set up a shoebox with a slot cut in the top (grown-up) as the main mailbox.
3. **Plan the letters.** For each person, ask: "What do you love about them? What would make them smile?" Write a word or two to remember.
4. **Write the letters** (25 minutes). Your child writes "Dear ___," then a message: "You make the best pancakes." "Thank you for reading to me." Help with spelling, and let them draw a picture too.
5. **Stamps and envelopes.** Your child draws a stamp in the corner of each envelope (a tiny picture), writes the name on the front, and seals it.
6. **Deliver!** (10 minutes). Put on the mail carrier hat and bag. Your child walks the "route" and puts each letter in the right mailbox.
7. **Mail back.** Write your child a letter too and put it in their mailbox to find.

## Make it yours
- Write a letter to someone outside the house (a grandparent, a friend) and mail it for real.
- Make the post office deliver packages: a drawing, a folded paper star, a "coupon for a hug."
- Write letters from a pet or a stuffed animal to the family.

## Stuck?
- Writing is tiring: your child says the message, you write it in light pencil, and they trace it.
- Your child can't think what to say: give starters like "I like when you..." or "Thank you for..."

## Show it off
When the family gets home, your child tells them to check their mailboxes and watches them read.

## Clean up
Your child puts the supplies in the post office box. Keep the mailboxes up for the rest of the break.

## For the grown-up
This builds early writing (letter format, a first-grade skill), gratitude and thinking about others. Keep the mailboxes up and write back: kids write more when they get mail too. If you mail a real letter, help address it and walk to the mailbox together. **Ask at dinner:** "What did you write in your letters, and who do you think liked theirs best?"
