title: Comic Book Issue One
grade: 6
order: 19
kind: Story
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: 4 sheets of paper folded in half and stapled into an 8-page booklet (or just 6 to 8 loose pages), a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, a black pen, colored pencils or markers
nice: a fine black marker, sticky notes for planning

## The big idea
Issue #1 of any comic has one job: hook readers so hard they *need* issue #2. No superheroes this time. Think mystery, sci-fi, fantasy, horror, or a funny slice of life about a kid with a weird problem. You'll write and draw a full first issue that ends on a cliffhanger.

## Goals
- You create a 6-page comic plus a cover.
- The last page ends on a cliffhanger that makes readers want more.

## Objectives
- I can plan a story across pages, with a big moment on each page turn.
- I can use different panel sizes and shots (close-up, wide shot) to control pacing.
- I can write dialogue and captions that move the story forward.

## Get ready
Make your booklet. On a separate page, write your genre and a one-line pitch: "A girl finds out her new school is run by talking crows."

## Steps
1. Plan with thumbnails (15 minutes). Draw 6 tiny page outlines on one sheet. Sketch quick stick figures for each panel. This is how pros plan.
2. Know your shots. Wide shot: shows the whole scene. Medium shot: characters from the waist up. Close-up: a face or an object, for emotion or a clue.
3. Plan the page turns. Put a mini-surprise at the bottom of each right-hand page so the reader turns to find out what happens.
4. Draw panels in pencil (40 minutes). Use 3 to 6 panels per page. Give big moments a big panel.
5. Add lettering: speech bubbles, thought bubbles, caption boxes, and sound effects. Write the words first, then draw the bubble around them.
6. Ink your lines in pen, erase pencil, and add color or gray shading.
7. Draw the cover last: the title, "Issue #1," and one image that teases the mystery without giving it away.

## Make it yours
- Hide a clue in an early panel that only makes sense on the last page.
- Tell one page with no words at all, only pictures.
- Add a fake "letters page" at the back with made-up reader letters, and your answers.

## Stuck?
- Your story is too big: issue #1 only needs the setup and the first problem.
- Characters look different in every panel: give each one a signature feature (a striped scarf, spiky hair, glasses).
- The ending isn't exciting: end on a reveal, a question, or someone in danger.

## Show it off
Hand your comic to a family member to read silently. Watch their face on the page turns. Then ask: "What do you think happens in issue #2?"

## Clean up
Store your comic flat. Keep the thumbnails; they're your plans for the next issue.

## For the grown-up
Comics build serious writing skills (plot, pacing, dialogue) and visual storytelling. This is a full-afternoon project and may spill into the next day, which is great. **Ask at dinner:** "Where does your cliffhanger leave the main character?"
