title: Restaurant Day
grade: 1
order: 4
kind: Pretend
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: paper, crayons or markers, a notepad or scrap paper for orders, a towel for the waiter's arm, plates, cups, napkins, a simple real snack or lunch (bread, cheese, fruit, crackers, vegetables), a butter knife
nice: an apron, a small bell, a tablecloth, a vase with a flower

## The big idea
Read aloud: "A brand-new restaurant is opening today, and you are the owner, the chef and the waiter! You'll make the menu, take the orders and serve real food to your customers. What will your restaurant be called?"

## Goals
- Your child runs a pretend restaurant with a menu and serves a real snack or lunch.
- Your child takes orders, serves them and makes a bill.

## Objectives
- Your child can make a menu with pictures, words and prices.
- Your child can listen to an order and remember or write it.
- Your child can add small numbers to make a bill.

## Get ready
Wash hands. Decide together what real food is on the menu: 4 or 5 simple things you already have. Prep anything that needs a sharp knife or heat ahead of time (grown-up). Set a table with a tablecloth or towel.

## Steps
1. **Name the restaurant.** Ask: "What kind of restaurant? Fancy? A diner? A dinosaur café?" Make a sign for the door.
2. **Make the menu** (15 minutes). Fold paper in half. Your child draws each food and writes or copies the names. Add prices: 1 to 5 dollars. Give each dish a fun name ("Cheese Mountain," "Rainbow Crunch").
3. **Set the table.** Your child places a plate, cup and napkin for each customer, plus a flower or a drawn centerpiece.
4. **Open for business.** You arrive as the customer. Your child greets you, shows you to your seat and hands you the menu.
5. **Take the order** (10 minutes). Your child writes your order on the notepad (pictures are fine). Then they "go to the kitchen" and make it: arrange crackers, spread butter with a butter knife, pour water.
6. **Serve.** Your child serves the food with the towel over their arm and says, "Enjoy your meal!"
7. **The bill.** Your child adds up what you ordered and writes the total. You "pay" with paper money and they make change.
8. Switch roles and you serve your child.

## Make it yours
- Make it a restaurant for monsters, with dishes like "Swamp Soup" (green grapes in a bowl).
- Add a drive-through window from a cardboard box.
- Serve the stuffed animals too, and make their special menu.

## Stuck?
- Adding is tricky: make every price 1 or 2 dollars and count on fingers together.
- Your child wants to skip to eating: serve first, eat together, and do the bill as dessert.

## Show it off
Make it a family dinner: your child serves the family a "restaurant night" with the same menu.

## Clean up
The staff clears the table, wipes it and puts dishes in the sink. Restaurants always clean up!

## For the grown-up
This builds writing for a purpose, adding money, listening and following directions, and real kitchen helping. Check allergies before planning the menu and keep it nut-free. Do any knife work beyond a butter knife and any cooking yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the most popular dish at your restaurant?"
