where: Indoors

materials: paper, a pencil, a ruler, an eraser, a black pen or marker
nice: colored pencils, thin and thick markers, a light source to trace through

## The big idea
Graphic memoir is a true story told in comics. It doesn't need explosions; some of the best are about one ordinary day that turned out to matter. You'll pick one real day from your life (the best, the worst, the weirdest) and tell it in a two-page comic.

## Goals
- A two-page comic about one real day, with 10 to 16 panels.
- A title and a final panel that lands with a feeling.

## Objectives
- I can use panels, gutters and captions to control time.
- I can choose which moments to show and which to skip.
- I can tell a true story with honesty and a point of view.

## Get ready
Fold two sheets of paper in half for a mini book, or work flat. Draw light rectangle borders 1.5 cm from the edges.

## Steps
1. **Pick the day** (5 minutes). The first day at a new school, the day the dog came home, the day everything went wrong at the bus stop. Write three you could tell, then circle one.
2. **List the beats**: 8 to 12 moments in order. Star the one most important moment.
3. **Thumbnail** both pages tiny (10 minutes). Plan panel sizes: the starred moment gets the biggest panel. Small panels speed time up; one wide panel slows it down.
4. Use the **gutter** (the space between panels) on purpose. Readers fill in what happens between panels, so you can skip boring parts.
5. Draw yourself simply. A consistent symbol (a hood, a hairstyle, glasses) is more important than realism.
6. Mix **captions** (your older voice looking back: "I didn't know yet that...") with **speech bubbles** (what people said that day). Write the words before you draw the bubbles.
7. Pencil, then ink (40 minutes). Erase pencil and add shading or one color for mood.
8. Write the title on page one.

## Make it yours
- Tell the same day from someone else's point of view on page two.
- Draw it with no words at all, and let pictures carry everything.
- Draw yourself as an animal or creature, and give everyone else in the story their own creature too.

## Stuck?
- You can't draw people well: draw from behind, from far away, or as simple shapes. Graphic memoirs are full of stick figures.
- Too much happened: cut until only one feeling is left.

## Show it off
Let someone read it while you watch their face. Don't explain anything until they finish.

## Clean up
Store pages flat and put pens and markers away with caps on.

## For the grown-up
Graphic memoir combines narrative writing with visual storytelling: structure, pacing and voice, central skills in English class. True stories can be personal; let your teen choose whether to share. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did you pick that day?"
