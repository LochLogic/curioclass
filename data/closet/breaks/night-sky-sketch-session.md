materials: a sketchbook or paper on a clipboard, a pencil, a warm jacket, a blanket or chair, a flashlight covered with red cellophane or a red sock (red light keeps your night vision)
nice: binoculars, a star chart from a library book, a flask of something warm, a friend or grown-up

## The big idea
For thousands of years, people learned the sky by drawing it. Astronomers before photography kept careful sketches of
the Moon, planets and comets. Tonight you will sit outside, let your eyes adjust, and sketch what you see like an
observer from another century, then invent your own constellations.

## Goals
- Two sky sketches: one wide view of a section of sky and one close-up of the Moon or a bright star cluster.
- At least one invented constellation with a name and a short story.

## Objectives
- I can let my eyes dark-adapt and use averted vision to see faint stars.
- I can map star positions relative to a landmark on the horizon.
- I can record observations with time, date and conditions.

## Get ready
Tell a grown-up where you will be. Pick the darkest spot you can safely get to: a yard, balcony, doorstep or window
with the lights off. Write the date, time, weather and Moon phase at the top of the page.

## Steps
1. Turn off all lights and wait 15 minutes. Your eyes will see far more stars by the end.
2. Draw the **horizon line** with landmarks: a roof, a tree, a power pole. These anchor your map.
3. Sketch the wide view (20 minutes). Draw bright stars as bigger dots, faint ones as tiny dots. Measure gaps with
   your fist held at arm's length (about 10 degrees).
4. Use **averted vision**: look slightly to the side of a faint star and it pops out.
5. Sketch a close-up of the Moon (if up) or a bright cluster. Shade craters or note color differences between stars.
6. Invent a constellation from your stars and write its myth in five sentences.
7. Come back in an hour, or another night, and see how everything moved.

## Make it yours
- Pretend you are the first astronomer on a new planet and name everything you see.
- Draw the same patch of sky every night of the break and track a planet's movement.
- Map light pollution: how many stars can you see from different spots?

## Stuck?
- City lights block most stars: sketch the Moon and the brightest stars and planets, or the shapes of clouds.
- It is cloudy: sketch the night skyline instead and try again another night.

## Show it off
Take a grown-up outside and point out your invented constellation and tell its story.

## Clean up
Bring everything inside, fold the blanket, and keep your sketches in order by date.

## For the grown-up
This builds astronomy, observation and drawing skills, and patience. Night outdoors means staying close, telling you
where they are, and coming in at a set time. Use the red light, not a phone screen, to keep night vision. **Ask at
dinner:** "What did you notice after your eyes adjusted?"
