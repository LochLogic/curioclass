grade: 12
order: 15
kind: Puzzle
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: string or shoelaces, a few beads, buttons or washers, cardboard, scissors, a pencil, wire coat hangers or paper clips, pliers
nice: thicker wire, keyrings, a friend to test your puzzles on

## The big idea
You have seen those maddening puzzles: a ring trapped on a twisted wire, a bead that seems impossible to free. They
are called **disentanglement puzzles**, and every one has a hidden path. You will solve a classic, then design two of
your own, and test them on family members who will absolutely say "this is impossible."

## Goals
- Two original puzzles that can be solved and reset.
- A solution diagram for each one, kept secret.

## Objectives
- I can analyze a puzzle by tracing loops and paths.
- I can design a puzzle that looks harder than it is.
- I can write clear step-by-step instructions to solve it.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Straighten one coat hanger with pliers. Cut a piece of cardboard 10 cm by 5 cm.

## Steps
1. Build a classic (15 minutes): the **cardboard and bead puzzle**. Poke two holes in the cardboard 2 cm apart, plus
   one larger hole in the middle. Thread string through the side holes and tie a bead on each end so it cannot slip
   through. Loop the string through the center hole. Now try to move a bead from one side of the string to the other.
2. Solve it. Hint: pull the loop through the middle hole and pass the bead through the loop. Draw each step.
3. Design your own (30 minutes). Ideas: a ring trapped on two wire loops, a washer on a shoelace with knots, a
   button that must travel through three holes.
4. Test it on yourself: can you solve it? Can you reset it?
5. Name each puzzle and rate its difficulty from 1 to 5.
6. Write a solution card, folded and taped shut.

## Make it yours
- Make a "puzzle box" set of three with increasing difficulty.
- Make a giant version with a rope and a hula hoop or a big ring.
- Make one with a story: free the trapped prisoner from the tower.

## Stuck?
- You cannot solve your own puzzle: you may have made it truly impossible. Simplify it by removing one loop.
- The wire is too stiff: use thinner wire or bend it around a broom handle for smooth curves.

## Show it off
Give a puzzle to each person at dinner. Time them.

## Clean up
Put the pliers away, collect wire snippets, and store puzzles in a bag.

## For the grown-up
This builds spatial reasoning and the mathematical idea of topology (how loops and shapes connect). Bent wire ends
can be sharp, so file or tape them. **Ask at dinner:** "Which puzzle took you longest to solve?"
