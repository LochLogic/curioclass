time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: a clear space about 2 m by 2 m, comfortable shoes or socks on a non-slip floor, water, paper and a pen
nice: a speaker or radio for music, a mirror, a friend to learn it with

## The big idea
What happens if you mix a slow ballet move with a hip-hop bounce and a folk dance clap? Choreographers do this to
make dance feel new. Build a one-minute routine that mashes up at least three styles into one flowing piece with its
own story.

## Goals
- A one-minute routine combining at least three dance styles.
- A performance of the routine from memory.

## Objectives
- I can describe and perform moves from different dance styles.
- I can build smooth transitions between very different moves.
- I can count music in eights and match moves to counts.

## Get ready
Clear a space and move anything breakable. Warm up for five minutes: roll shoulders, swing arms, march, do 10 slow
squats and gentle stretches. Pick a song or a beat you can clap.

## Steps
1. Pick three styles (5 minutes): ballet, hip-hop, line dance, robot, salsa steps, a sport move, or your family's
   dance. Write one move you know for each.
2. Learn to count. Most songs move in groups of eight beats. Clap "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8."
3. Build each style's section (20 minutes): two counts of eight for each style. Mark moves on paper by count.
4. Design transitions. Find a move that connects: a spin, a body roll, a freeze. A transition can be funny, like a
   ballet arm that turns into a robot.
5. Add a story arc: the dancer starts stiff (robot), finds joy (salsa), and floats away (ballet).
6. Run it three times with music. Fix any transition that trips you.
7. Perform it from start to finish without stopping.

## Make it yours
- Add a style from a decade: a 1920s swing kick, a 1970s disco point, a 1990s shuffle.
- Teach the routine to someone in 15 minutes and perform it as a duo.
- Make it outdoors in a park or yard and use the space: a bench, a step, a tree.

## Stuck?
- Moves feel disconnected: slow everything to half speed and find the shared rhythm.
- You keep forgetting the order: name each section and say the names out loud as you dance.

## Show it off
Perform it after dinner with music, and teach the chorus move to the audience.

## Clean up
Put furniture back and drink some water.

## For the grown-up
This builds fitness, coordination, rhythm and choreography. Make sure the space is clear and the floor is not
slippery. **Ask at dinner:** "Which move was hardest to connect to the next one?"
