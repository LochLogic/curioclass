## The big idea
Tabletop skirmish games use small armies, a ruler and dice to play out battles on a tabletop full of hills and ruins. You'll build your own version from scratch: design paper miniature fighters, give them stats, build a battlefield from household junk, and fight a scenario.

## Goals
- Two small warbands of 4 to 6 paper miniatures each, with stat cards.
- A tabletop battlefield and one scenario played to the end.

## Objectives
- I can design units with different stats that are balanced against each other.
- I can use dice probability to set hit chances.
- I can write a scenario with a clear victory condition.

## Get ready
Clear a table or floor area about 60 x 90 cm. Collect boxes, cups and books for terrain.

## Steps
1. **Pick a setting** (5 minutes): knights and goblins, robots in a junkyard, rival explorers in a jungle temple.
2. **Make miniatures.** Draw each fighter about 4 cm tall on folded cardstock, so the front and back show. Glue a coin or a cardboard square at the base so it stands up (25 minutes).
3. **Stats.** Give each one: Move (in cm), Attack (dice to roll), Defense (number to beat), Health. Example: Scout, Move 15, Attack 1 die, Defense 3, Health 2. Brute, Move 8, Attack 2 dice, Defense 5, Health 4.
4. **Combat rule.** Roll Attack dice. Each roll equal to or higher than the target's Defense does 1 damage. A die hitting on 4+ has a 50 percent chance; on 6 it's about 17 percent. Use this to balance.
5. **Point costs.** Give each unit a value, like Health + Attack + (Move divided by 5). Each side gets the same total.
6. **Build terrain.** Boxes are buildings that block line of sight. Cups are rocks that give +1 Defense to anyone behind them.
7. **Scenario.** Write a goal: "Hold the bridge for 5 rounds" or "Capture the relic and get it back to your edge." Play it, solo or against someone (30 minutes).
8. After the game, fix one unbalanced unit.

## Make it yours
- Add a hero for each side with a special power.
- Run a three-game campaign: the winner of each fight gets a bonus in the next.
- Make one side huge and weak, the other small and strong, and see if it balances.

## Stuck?
- One unit always wins: raise its point cost or lower one stat.
- Games take too long: shrink the table or cut each side to 3 units.

## Show it off
Set up a battle for someone at home and teach them in three minutes.

## Clean up
Store the warbands in an envelope, flat. Keep stat cards with them.

## For the grown-up
Designing a skirmish game builds probability, measurement, balancing and rule-writing, with plenty of art along the way. **Ask at dinner:** "Which unit in your game is the most powerful, and how did you balance it?"
