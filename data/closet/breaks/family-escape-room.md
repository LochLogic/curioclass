title: Family Escape Room
grade: 5
order: 4
kind: Puzzle
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: paper, pens, an envelope or two, a small box or bag with a zipper, tape, a book, a timer or clock, a "treasure" (a snack, a homemade award, or a note)
nice: a combination lock, a flashlight, sticky notes

## The big idea
Your family is trapped! Well, pretend trapped. They have 20 minutes to solve a chain of puzzles and find the key to escape. You are the game designer. Every clue is yours. Can you make it hard, but not impossible?

## Goals
- You build a 4 or 5 puzzle escape room in one room of your home.
- Your family plays it, and you run the clock.

## Objectives
- I can design puzzles where each answer leads to the next clue.
- I can test my puzzles and fix the ones that are too hard or too easy.
- I can give hints without giving away the answer.

## Get ready
Pick one room. Ask a grown-up which spots are okay for hiding clues. Choose a story, like "a wizard locked the living room" or "the cat stole the TV remote."

## Steps
1. Plan the chain on paper (15 minutes). Draw five boxes in a row. Each box is a puzzle. The answer to one tells you where the next clue is.
2. Write puzzle one: a riddle. Example: "I have pages but I am not a tree. Look inside me." The next clue goes in a book.
3. Write puzzle two: a secret code. Swap each letter for the next one (A becomes B). "DPVDI" means COUCH.
4. Make puzzle three a hunt. Cut a clue into four pieces and hide them. Put together, they say the next spot.
5. Add one or two more puzzles of your own. A math lock works well. "Lock code: legs on a spider, then fingers on one hand." That is 85.
6. Put the treasure in the final box or bag. Hide every clue. Walk the whole chain yourself to test it (15 minutes).
7. Write three hint cards. Each hint gives a little help, not the answer.

## Make it yours
- Add a twist. One clue is a fake, and the real one is hidden under it.
- Give the players a role, like spies or space explorers, and write a one-paragraph "mission briefing."
- Make a two-team version with two chains that meet at the end.

## Stuck?
- A puzzle is too hard: add a picture hint or make the code shorter.
- Players finish in five minutes: add a puzzle that needs two clues put together.
- You run out of hiding spots: tape clues under chairs, inside shoes, or behind a picture frame.

## Show it off
Read the mission briefing out loud. Start the timer. Watch, give hints, and cheer when they escape.

## Clean up
Collect every clue so nobody finds one in a shoe next week. Keep your puzzles in an envelope.

## For the grown-up
Designing an escape room builds logic, reading, writing, and planning, and it means thinking from someone else's point of view. Agree on hiding spots ahead of time so nothing fragile gets moved. Playing it is a great after-work family game. **Ask at dinner:** "Which puzzle was hardest to make work, and how did you fix it?"
