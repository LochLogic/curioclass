## The big idea
A mandala is a circle filled with repeating patterns that spin out from the center. People across many cultures have made them for hundreds of years. They look complicated, but they're built from simple geometry, one ring at a time. Design your own, then make one from nature.

## Goals
- At least one finished mandala with eight or more repeating sections.
- A nature mandala made outside or on a tray.

## Objectives
- I can divide a circle into equal parts using geometry.
- I can create patterns with rotational symmetry.
- I can plan a color scheme that ties a design together.

## Get ready
Find a flat, quiet table. Trace a large plate on your paper. Mark the exact center (fold the paper in half twice and the crease crossing is the center).

## Steps
1. **Divide the circle.** Fold the paper along the creases you already made, then fold diagonally twice more. Open it up: you have 8 equal slices. For 12, use a protractor and mark every 30 degrees.
2. **Draw rings.** Trace smaller circles from the center: a cup, a coin, a bowl. You'll have about four or five rings.
3. **Start in the center.** Draw a small shape: a flower, a star, a dot pattern.
4. **Fill ring by ring (30 minutes).** In each ring, pick one shape: petals, triangles, waves, dots, scales. Repeat it exactly in every slice. Each ring should be different from the one next to it.
5. **Ink it.** Go over your favorite lines with a black pen and erase the pencil.
6. **Color it.** Choose a palette of three to five colors. Try warm colors in the center and cool ones outward, or alternate colors ring by ring.
7. **Make a nature mandala (15 minutes).** Outside or on a tray, use leaves, pebbles, petals, sticks, beans or pasta. Start with one object in the center and build rings outward.

## Make it yours
- Make a "story mandala": every ring represents a part of your life or a season of the year.
- Use only one color in many shades: a mandala in every blue you can make.
- Make it giant: draw one in sidewalk chalk on a driveway or patio.

## Stuck?
- Shapes don't line up: use the fold lines as guides for each slice.
- It looks busy: leave some rings simple, like plain dots, to give the eye a rest.
- Running out of ideas: look at real things for patterns: a sunflower, a tire, a snowflake.

## Show it off
Hang your mandala in a window and make a photo or drawing of your nature mandala before the wind takes it.

## Clean up
Return nature items outside, cap your pens, and keep your mandala flat.

## For the grown-up
This is geometry (angles, circles, rotational symmetry) and design, and many kids find it calming. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you make every slice match?"
