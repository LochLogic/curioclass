title: Program the Human Robot
grade: 5
order: 30
kind: Puzzle
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 2+
materials: paper, a pencil, masking tape or string, a stuffed animal or small object, a partner (a sibling, a friend, or a grown-up after work)
nice: index cards, a timer, a blindfold or scarf for the hard level

## The big idea
Robots can't think. They do exactly what they're told, nothing more. Your job is to be the programmer. Write the
commands that steer a "robot" (your partner) across a room to rescue a stuffed animal. One wrong step and the robot
walks into the couch!

## Goals
- You write a program that gets the robot from start to finish with no mistakes.
- You and your partner switch jobs, so you both get to program and both get to be the robot.

## Objectives
- I can write step-by-step commands in the right order.
- I can find a bug (a mistake) in my program and fix it.
- I can use a loop, like "repeat 3 times: step forward", to make a program shorter.

## Get ready
Clear a path in a room. Use tape or string to make a grid on the floor: 5 squares by 5 squares, each about one big
step wide. If you can't use tape, use sheets of paper as squares. Put the stuffed animal in one square and mark a
start square.

## Steps
1. Make your command cards. Write each one on its own card or scrap: FORWARD 1, TURN LEFT, TURN RIGHT, PICK UP.
2. Agree on the robot rules. The robot only moves when it hears a command. A turn means turn in place, no step.
3. Write your first program on paper (10 minutes). Plan every move from start to the stuffed animal. Read it over
   before you run it.
4. Run it! Read the commands one at a time. The robot does exactly what you say, even if it's wrong.
5. Did the robot crash? That's a **bug**. Find the line that went wrong, fix it, and run the program again.
6. Make it harder. Add "walls" (pillows) the robot can't step on, and write a new program around them (15 minutes).
7. Shorten it with a **loop**. If you wrote FORWARD 1 three times in a row, write REPEAT 3: FORWARD 1 instead.
8. Switch jobs. Now your partner programs and you are the robot.

## Make it yours
- Give the robot a personality. It beeps on every turn or talks in a robot voice. It can only follow commands, though!
- Invent a new command, like SPIN or JUMP, and decide exactly what it means.
- Make a delivery mission: the robot picks up the stuffed animal and brings it back to start.

## Stuck?
- Lefts and rights get mixed up: tape an L and an R on the robot's hands.
- The program is too long to keep track of: number every line so you can say "the bug is on line 6."
- No partner right now: be your own robot. Write the program, then walk it yourself exactly as written.

## Show it off
Challenge a grown-up to be your robot after dinner. Can they follow your program with no help?

## Clean up
Pull up the tape, put the pillows back, and keep your command cards in an envelope for next time.

## For the grown-up
This is "unplugged" coding: the same thinking as computer programming (order of steps, debugging, loops) with no
screen. It also builds left and right, direction words, and patience. Painter's tape comes off floors cleanly; test a
corner first on rugs. If the robot wears a blindfold, keep the grid clear of anything they could trip on. **Ask at
dinner:** "What was the worst bug in your program, and how did you find it?"
