title: Toy Museum Tour
grade: 2
order: 11
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: 8 to 12 toys or special objects, index cards or paper folded into small tents, a pencil, markers, a table or shelf, tape, paper for tickets
nice: a shoebox for a "rare item" display case, a scarf or towel to drape over tables, a paper towel tube for a pointer

## The big idea
Read aloud: "The Museum of Amazing Toys is opening, and you are the head curator. A curator chooses the treasures, writes the signs, and gives the tours. Visitors are coming soon. What will they see?"

## Goals
- A museum with at least 8 objects, each with a label your child wrote.
- A guided tour given to a visitor.

## Objectives
- Your child can sort objects into groups and explain why they belong together.
- Your child can write a short label with a name and a fact.
- Your child can speak clearly to an audience and answer a question.

## Get ready
Clear a table, a shelf or the floor along a wall. Drape a towel over it for a fancy look. Fold index cards in half to make standing labels.

## Steps
1. **Choose the collection** (10 minutes). Your child picks 8 to 12 toys or treasures: a favorite stuffed animal, a shell, a toy car, a drawing.
2. **Make rooms.** Sort them into 2 or 3 groups, like "Animals," "Things That Go," and "Things I Made." Each group gets its own area with a sign.
3. **Write the labels** (20 minutes). Each label has a name and one fact. Example: "Mr. Buttons. A stuffed bear. He has been to the beach three times." Help spell, but let your child sound words out first.
4. **Pick the star.** Every museum has one special treasure. Your child chooses it, puts it in a shoebox display case, and writes "DO NOT TOUCH."
5. **Make tickets.** Cut strips of paper and write "MUSEUM ADMIT ONE." Your child decides on a price (one hug works).
6. **Practice the tour** (10 minutes). Your child walks the path and says one or two sentences about each object. Use the tube as a pointer.
7. **Open the doors.** You're the visitor. Hand over your ticket, follow the tour, and ask at least two questions.

## Make it yours
- Add a gift shop where visitors get a drawing of the star treasure to take home.
- Make it a mystery museum: one object is a fake, and visitors must guess which.
- Pick a theme, like a Dinosaur Museum or a Museum of Tiny Things, and only use matching objects.

## Stuck?
- Label writing feels long: your child writes just the name, and you write the fact as they say it.
- Your child gets shy during the tour: they can be a "robot tour guide" and use a funny voice.

## Show it off
Give a tour to each family member who comes home, and ask them to sign a guest book (a sheet of paper on a clipboard).

## Clean up
Return each toy to its home and keep the labels in an envelope in case the museum reopens.

## For the grown-up
This builds sorting and grouping, writing facts, and public speaking. Curating also shows kids that their own things have stories worth telling. **Ask at dinner:** "Which object in your museum has the best story, and what is it?"
