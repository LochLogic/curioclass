title: Character Day Costume
grade: 2
order: 10
kind: Pretend
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: old clothes, scarves, towels, paper bags, cardboard (cereal boxes), scissors, tape, markers, paper, string or yarn
nice: aluminum foil, paper plates, face paint, yarn for hair, a mirror

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Today you invent a brand-new character that no one has ever seen. Maybe a cloud knight, a forgetful wizard, or a dog who runs a bakery. Then you make the costume and BECOME that character for the rest of the day."

## Goals
- A full costume with at least three parts your child designed.
- A character card that tells the character's name, power and favorite food.

## Objectives
- Your child can invent a character with clear traits.
- Your child can plan a design on paper and then build it.
- Your child can speak and move like their character.

## Get ready
Pull out a pile of old clothes, scarves and recycling. Clear the floor and set out tape, scissors and markers.

## Steps
1. **Invent the character** (10 minutes). Ask three questions: "What kind of creature or person are they? What can they do that's special? What's one silly thing about them?" Write the answers down.
2. **Draw the plan.** Your child draws the character from head to toe and labels each part: "hat," "cape," "shield."
3. **Make the head piece** (20 minutes). Ideas: a paper bag mask with eye holes (you cut), a cardboard crown, a headband with paper ears.
4. **Make the body piece** (20 minutes). A towel or scarf cape, a paper bag vest (cut up the front and arm holes), a cardboard shield or a foil belt.
5. **Make one prop.** Every character needs something to carry: a wand from a rolled paper tube, a tray of paper cupcakes, a foil sword.
6. **Write the character card.** On an index card or half sheet: NAME, POWER, FAVORITE FOOD, CATCHPHRASE. Example: "Sir Drizzle. Power: makes it rain lemonade. Catchphrase: Splish and splash!"
7. **Become them** (20 minutes). Your child puts it all on, practices a walk and a voice in the mirror, and spends time in character.

## Make it yours
- Invent a sidekick from a stuffed animal and make it a matching costume.
- Write a one-page adventure about the character and act it out.
- Design a villain for the character to face, and play it yourself.

## Stuck?
- Your child can't decide on a character: mix two things together, like a dragon plus a librarian.
- Tape won't hold on cloth: tie pieces with string or yarn instead.

## Show it off
Hold a "character entrance" for the family: your child walks in, says the catchphrase, and answers questions in character.

## Clean up
Put clothes back, recycle scraps, and keep the costume in a bag for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds creativity, planning, labeling and confidence in speaking. Inventing an original character, instead of copying one from a show, stretches imagination further. You do all cutting of eye holes and any tight spots. Never put plastic bags over a child's head. **Ask at dinner:** "If your character came to dinner, what would they say about the food?"
