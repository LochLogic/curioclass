where: Indoors

materials: a notebook or paper, a pen or pencil, index cards or sticky notes
nice: a favorite novel to look at for how first chapters work, a quiet spot with a door

## The big idea
Every novel you've ever loved started with one person writing one chapter. That's the hardest part, and also the best: you get to invent the world, meet the main character, and set off the trouble. Today you write chapter one of your own book.

## Goals
- A finished first chapter, about 1,000 to 2,000 words.
- A one-page plan for where the book goes next.

## Objectives
- I can write an opening line that hooks the reader.
- I can introduce a character with a clear want and a problem.
- I can end a chapter so the reader has to keep reading.

## Get ready
Find a quiet place and set a "do not disturb" sign. Make three cards: Character, Want, Trouble.

## Steps
1. **Build your main character (10 minutes).** On the Character card, write their name, age, one thing they're great at, one flaw, and a secret. Specific beats general: not "she's brave" but "she once climbed the school roof to get a lost shoe."
2. **The want and the trouble.** What does your character want more than anything? What's in the way? Example: "Jae wants to win the town's scavenger hunt. His rival is his own older sister."
3. **Pick the opening moment.** Don't start with the alarm clock. Start when something is already happening or about to change. The rule: enter late.
4. **Write a hook first line.** Try a strange fact ("The day I was supposed to become invisible, I overslept."), a question, or a line of dialogue mid-argument. Write five options and pick the best.
5. **Draft the chapter (45 minutes).** Show the character doing something, not you explaining them. Bring in the world through details, not a lecture. Put the trouble in by the middle of the chapter.
6. **End on a hook.** The last line should leave a question hanging: a door opens, a message arrives, a lie is discovered.
7. **Plan ahead.** On one page, list five big things that will happen in the rest of the book, ending with how it might end.

## Make it yours
- Write it in first person from someone unexpected: the villain, the sidekick, the house the story happens in.
- Start in the middle of the action and skip backward in chapter two.
- Write a prologue first: a short, mysterious scene from 100 years earlier.

## Stuck?
- The blank page is scary: write the worst possible first sentence on purpose, then keep going. You can change it later.
- The chapter feels slow: cut the first paragraph. Most first drafts start a little too early.
- Not sure what happens: give your character a choice and pick the one that makes things worse.

## Show it off
Read your first page out loud to someone at home and stop at a cliffhanger. Make them beg for chapter two.

## Clean up
Number your pages, clip them together with your plan, and put them in a folder labeled with the title.

## For the grown-up
This builds the craft of fiction (character, conflict, pacing, hooks) and the stamina of sustained writing, which is huge for high school English. **Ask at dinner:** "Who is your main character, and what do they want?"
