title: Inventor's Notebook
grade: 5
order: 24
kind: Build
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: a notebook or 6 sheets of paper folded and stapled into a booklet, a pencil, colored pencils, a ruler, recycling (boxes, tubes, lids), tape, string, rubber bands
nice: graph paper, sticky notes, a magnifying glass for "close inspections"

## The big idea
Every invention starts with an annoyance. Someone got tired of a problem and fixed it. What bugs you every day? Lost socks? Cold toast? Your sibling stealing the last cookie? Fill a real inventor's notebook and build a model of your best idea.

## Goals
- You fill a notebook with at least five invention ideas.
- You build a working model or a prototype of your favorite.

## Objectives
- I can name a problem and think of more than one fix.
- I can draw a labeled diagram of how something works.
- I can build a simple prototype (a first test model) and improve it.

## Get ready
Make your notebook. Write "Inventor's Notebook" and your name on the cover. On page 1, write the date. Real inventors date every page.

## Steps
1. Walk around your home with the notebook (10 minutes). Write down every small problem you notice. Aim for ten.
2. Pick your top five. For each one, write one sentence: "The problem is ___."
3. Brainstorm three fixes for each problem. Silly is fine. "A sock-finding robot dog" is a real start.
4. Pick your best idea. Draw it big and label every part with arrows. Example: "This lever pushes the cookie jar lid closed."
5. Build a prototype from recycling, tape, string, and rubber bands (30 minutes). It does not need to be perfect. It needs to show how it works.
6. Test it. Write what worked and what didn't. Draw one change.
7. Give it a catchy name and write a one-line slogan.

## Make it yours
- Invent something for a pet, a plant, or a grandparent.
- Design a "bad invention" on purpose, like an umbrella with holes, and write why it fails.
- Make a pretend patent page with a drawing and the words "Invented by" and your name.

## Stuck?
- You can't find problems: ask someone at home, "What's annoying you lately?"
- Your prototype falls apart: use more tape at the joints, or make it smaller.
- The idea is too big to build: build just one part of it.

## Show it off
Give a two-minute "inventor's pitch." Show the drawing, demo the prototype, and say your slogan.

## Clean up
Put leftover recycling back in the bin. Keep your notebook. Add to it whenever you spot a new problem.

## For the grown-up
This builds the engineering design process (find a problem, brainstorm, build, test) and labeled diagrams, which show up in science class. Encourage wild ideas; the notebook is for thinking, not for being right. **Ask at dinner:** "What problem did you solve, and what would you change in version two?"
