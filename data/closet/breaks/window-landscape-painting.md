title: Window Landscape Painting
grade: 12
order: 5
kind: Art
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper (heavy paper or cardboard if using paint), any paint you have (watercolors, poster paint, or acrylic), brushes, a cup of water, a palette or plate, paper towels, a pencil
nice: an easel or a box to prop the paper, masking tape, colored pencils for detail

## The big idea
The view from one window changes every hour: morning light, clouds rolling in, streetlights at dusk. Painters like
the Impressionists painted the same view again and again just to catch light. You will paint the view from your
window with real landscape techniques, then decide how true or how strange to make it.

## Goals
- A finished landscape painting of the view from a window.
- A small "color study" of the same view done quickly in a different light.

## Objectives
- I can use atmospheric perspective: far things are lighter, cooler and less detailed.
- I can paint in layers, from big shapes to small details.
- I can mix colors to match what I see.

## Get ready
Choose your window and set up so the light falls on your paper, not in your eyes. Tape your paper to a board.
Squint at the view: what are the three or four big shapes?

## Steps
1. Sketch lightly (10 minutes): the horizon line, the big shapes (sky, buildings, trees, ground). Do not draw details.
2. Paint the sky first, from top to bottom, getting lighter near the horizon.
3. Paint the far background in pale, cool colors (blues, lavenders). This is **atmospheric perspective**.
4. Paint the middle ground with stronger colors and some detail.
5. Paint the foreground last (30 minutes) with the darkest darks and the most detail: leaves, window frames, fences.
6. Add light: a few bright touches where the sun hits.
7. Do a 10-minute color study of the same view on a scrap. Paint fast and loose.

## Make it yours
- Paint the view as it might look 500 years ago or 500 years from now.
- Paint it as if it is underwater, or after a snowstorm in the middle of summer.
- Include the window frame and part of your room, so the painting shows inside and outside.

## Stuck?
- Colors look muddy: wait for each layer to dry before painting on top, and rinse your brush more often.
- The view is boring: crop it. Paint one tree, one rooftop or one corner.

## Show it off
Hang the painting next to the window and let the family compare.

## Clean up
Rinse brushes until the water runs clear, dry them flat, and wipe the table.

## For the grown-up
This builds painting technique, observation and color mixing. Use acrylics on a covered surface; they do not wash out
of clothes. **Ask at dinner:** "What changed in the view while you were painting?"
