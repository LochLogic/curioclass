title: Neighborhood History Walking Tour
grade: 10
order: 30
kind: Outside
time: 90-120 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a clipboard or notebook, a pen, a hand-drawn map, comfortable shoes, water
nice: a long-time neighbor or relative to interview, a camera, colored pencils, a library book about your town

## The big idea
Every street holds clues: a date carved on a building's cornerstone, a faded painted sign on a brick wall, a street named after someone nobody remembers. You're the historian. You'll walk your neighborhood, gather clues, interview someone who's lived there a long time, and build a 6-stop walking tour you lead for your family.

## Goals
- A map of 6 tour stops with a fact or story for each.
- A guided walking tour for your family.

## Objectives
- I can find primary evidence in buildings, signs and street names.
- I can gather an oral history through an interview.
- I can turn research into a short, engaging script.

## Get ready
Tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back. Plan a loop of about 1 to 2 km near home that you're allowed to walk. Dress for the weather.

## Steps
1. **Clue hunt** (40 minutes). Walk slowly and look up. Look for:
   - Cornerstones or dates on buildings.
   - **Ghost signs**: old painted ads on walls.
   - Old features: horse rings, carriage steps, a filled-in doorway, a bricked-up window.
   - Street names, plaques and memorials.
2. **Sketch and note** each clue: where it is and what it says.
3. **Interview** (15 minutes) a neighbor you know, a grandparent or a family member who's lived nearby a long time. Ask: "What was here when you were young? What's changed? What's the strangest story you know about this street?"
4. **Pick 6 stops** with the best stories. Order them in a loop.
5. **Write a script** for each stop: 4 or 5 sentences with a hook, the history and a "look at this" detail.
6. Draw the tour map with numbered stops.
7. **Lead the tour.** Walk the family through, using your script.

## Make it yours
- Do a "lost places" tour of things that used to be there but are gone.
- Add a mystery stop: a clue you couldn't explain. Ask your audience for theories.
- Do a "100 years from now" tour, imagining how each stop will change.

## Stuck?
- Your street feels too new: look for the oldest thing you can find, even a tree or a fence. Every place has a story.
- You can't go out much: do a tour of your building or home, with each room's history.

## Show it off
Lead the tour on a weekend for the family. Hand out your map.

## Clean up
Put away your notes, and keep the map: future tours can grow from it.

## For the grown-up
This builds history skills: primary sources, oral history and turning research into a story. Your teen should tell you their route, stay in agreed areas and only interview people they already know. **Ask at dinner:** "What's the oldest thing you found on our street?"
