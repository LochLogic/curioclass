materials: several large, sturdy cardboard boxes (delivery boxes work), packing tape or masking tape, a ruler or yardstick, a marker, scissors, a box cutter or craft knife (with permission), heavy books for testing
nice: wood glue or white glue, a cushion for the seat

## The big idea
Can cardboard hold a person? Yes, if it's designed right. Engineers make paper and cardboard furniture strong by using clever shapes. Design and build a chair that holds you without collapsing. No wood, no nails, just cardboard and your brain.

## Goals
- A cardboard chair that holds you sitting down for at least 30 seconds.
- A sketch of your design with notes on why it's strong.

## Objectives
- I can use structural shapes (tubes, triangles, layers) to make cardboard stronger.
- I can test a design with weight before trusting it.
- I can measure and cut accurately for a real project.

## Get ready
Get permission to use the boxes and the box cutter. Lay down an old towel or cardboard to cut on. Measure the height of a chair seat you like (usually about 45 cm) and the width (about 40 cm).

## Steps
1. **Test cardboard strength.** Cut a strip about 5 by 20 cm. Try bending it along the inside lines (flutes) and across them. Which way is stronger? Rolled into a tube? Folded into a triangle?
2. **Sketch your design (15 minutes).** Ideas that work: a cube with an inside grid of slotted panels like an egg crate; tight cardboard tubes standing up under the seat; triangle columns; layers glued flat together.
3. **Measure and mark.** Use a ruler and marker to draw every piece before cutting. Aim for a seat 40 by 40 cm, about 40 to 45 cm tall.
4. **Cut carefully (30 minutes).** Always cut away from your body, on a safe surface. Use scissors for curves and small cuts, and the knife for straight long cuts.
5. **Assemble.** Slot or tape pieces together. Tape every edge. If you have glue, layer two pieces for the seat.
6. **Test with books.** Stack heavy books on the seat. Add more and more. Watch for bending or crushing. Fix weak spots with more support underneath.
7. **The real test.** Sit down slowly with your hands on a table or with a grown-up nearby. Does it hold?

## Make it yours
- Build a stool instead, or a chair with a backrest and armrests.
- Make it a throne: add a tall back and decorations.
- Build it with no tape or glue: only slotted pieces that lock together.

## Stuck?
- The chair crumples: the cardboard is running the wrong way. Make the flutes stand up and down like columns.
- It wobbles: make sure the legs are all the same height. Measure and trim.
- Not enough cardboard: use many small tubes instead of big panels. Tubes are very strong.

## Show it off
Invite your family to test sit and give it a strength rating.

## Clean up
Pick up scraps, put the box cutter away with the blade closed or retracted, and recycle the leftovers.

## For the grown-up
This is structural engineering: how shape, not just material, makes something strong. Box cutters are fine at this age with your permission, a safe surface, and a reminder to cut away from the body; check in during the cutting. Have them test with books first and sit slowly. **Ask at dinner:** "What shape made your chair strong enough to hold you?"
