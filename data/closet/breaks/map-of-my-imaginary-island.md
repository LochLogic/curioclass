title: Map of My Imaginary Island
grade: 2
order: 21
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: With you
players: 1
materials: a large sheet of paper (or 4 sheets taped together), a pencil, crayons or markers, a ruler, a cup to trace circles
nice: a tea bag for an "old map" look (grown-up brews and cools it), a string to tie the scroll, a real map or atlas to look at

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Far out in the ocean is an island nobody has ever found, because it only exists in your imagination. Today you are the explorer who discovers it and makes the very first map. What will you find there?"

## Goals
- A map of an imaginary island with at least 8 labeled places.
- A short story about one adventure that happens on the island.

## Objectives
- Your child can use map tools: a compass rose, a key and labels.
- Your child can invent places and give them names that match what they are like.
- Your child can tell or write a short adventure that follows a path on the map.

## Get ready
Lay the big paper on a table. Show your child a real map if you have one, and point out the compass rose (N, S, E, W) and the key.

## Steps
1. **Draw the island shape.** Your child draws a big wobbly shape that fills most of the paper. A shape like an animal or a shoe is fun.
2. **Add the land** (15 minutes). Mountains (triangles), forests (little trees), a river (a wavy blue line from the mountains to the sea), a lake (trace a cup). Your child decides where.
3. **Add the special places** (15 minutes). Every island needs mystery: a volcano, a hidden cave, a waterfall, a castle, a village, a shipwreck, a spot marked X.
4. **Name everything.** Your child writes a name for each place. Names should tell something: "Grumbling Volcano," "Whisper Forest," "Lake Sparkle." Aim for 8 labels.
5. **Add map tools.** Draw a compass rose in a corner with N, S, E and W. Make a key in a box: a little tree means forest, a triangle means mountain.
6. **Plan an adventure.** With a dotted line, your child draws the path an explorer takes from the boat to the X.
7. **Tell the story** (15 minutes). Your child tells what happens along the path while you write it, or writes 3 to 5 sentences themselves.

## Make it yours
- Add creatures that live in each part of the island and draw them on the map.
- Make the map look old: wipe a cooled tea bag over it, let it dry, then roll it up like a scroll.
- Add a danger zone that explorers must avoid, and a secret shortcut.

## Stuck?
- Your child can't think of places: ask, "What would you want on your own island? What would be scary? What would be fun?"
- Labels crowd together: write them along the coast or the edges, with a line pointing to the place.

## Show it off
Give the family a "guided expedition": your child traces the path with a finger and tells the adventure.

## Clean up
Put crayons away, roll the map and tie it with string.

## For the grown-up
Mapmaking builds early geography (compass directions, a key, symbols), planning and storytelling. Writing names that describe places is great vocabulary practice. **Ask at dinner:** "If you could live in one place on your island, where would you live?"
