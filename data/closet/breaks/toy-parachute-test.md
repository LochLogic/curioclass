title: Toy Parachute Test
grade: 4
order: 16
kind: Science
time: 60-90 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: a plastic grocery bag, a paper napkin, a piece of cloth, string, tape, scissors, a small toy figure, a ruler, a timer or a clock with seconds
nice: coffee filters, a notebook, a stopwatch

## The big idea
A toy jumper needs a safe landing! Make three parachutes from different materials. Test them to find the slowest, safest one. You're the lab scientist.

## Goals
- You make at least 3 different parachutes.
- You find out which one falls the slowest.

## Objectives
- I can run a fair test, where only one thing changes.
- I can time and record results.
- I can explain air resistance, the push of air against falling things.

## Get ready
Find a safe drop spot. Stand on the floor and hold your arm high. Do not climb on chairs. A grown-up can drop from higher.

## Steps
1. Cut 3 squares, each 12 inches wide: one from the bag, one from the napkin, one from the cloth.
2. Cut 4 strings, each 12 inches long, for each parachute.
3. Tape one string to each corner of a square.
4. Tie the 4 strings together. Tape the toy to the knot.
5. Drop parachute 1 from your highest reach. Time it. Write it down.
6. Drop each parachute 3 times. Write all the times.
7. Find the average, or middle time, for each one.
8. Which won? Why do you think so?

## Make it yours
- Cut a small hole in the top of one parachute. Does it fall straighter?
- Try a bigger and smaller version of the winning material.
- Make a "rescue mission": land the toy on a paper target.

## Stuck?
- It falls too fast to time: try a bigger square.
- It gets tangled: fold it carefully before each drop.
- It tips over: make all 4 strings the same length.

## Show it off
Hold an "Air Show" for the family. Announce each parachute like a stunt pilot.

## Clean up
Fold the parachutes. Pick up string scraps.

## For the grown-up
This builds science process skills: fair tests, timing, averages and drawing conclusions. Please do any high drops yourself, and keep plastic bags away from little ones. **Ask at dinner:** "Which parachute fell slowest, and why?"
