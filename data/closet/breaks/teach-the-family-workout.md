## The big idea
Personal trainers don't just know exercises: they know how to teach them, how to make them easier or harder for anyone, and how to keep people going when they want to quit. Design a 20-minute workout and coach your family through it.

## Goals
- A 20-minute circuit workout with at least six stations.
- Cue cards with an easy, medium and hard version of every move.

## Objectives
- I can design a workout with a warm-up, main part and cool-down.
- I can explain and demonstrate good form clearly.
- I can adjust an exercise so people of different abilities can do it.

## Get ready
Clear a space, inside or out, about 3 by 3 meters. Fill a water glass. Make six index cards, one per station.

## Steps
1. **Plan the shape.** Warm-up (4 minutes), circuit (12 minutes), cool-down (4 minutes).
2. **Pick six moves.** Mix upper body, lower body, core and cardio. Example: squats, wall push-ups, plank, jumping jacks, lunges, step-ups on a low step.
3. **Make three levels (15 minutes).** On each card, write an easy, medium and hard version. Push-ups: wall push-ups (easy), knee push-ups (medium), full push-ups (hard). Lunges: hold a chair (easy), regular lunges (medium), jump lunges (hard).
4. **Write form tips.** One or two per card: "Knees behind toes," "Back flat like a table." Practice each move in front of a mirror so you can demonstrate it perfectly.
5. **Set the timing.** 40 seconds of work, 20 seconds of rest, then move to the next station. Two full rounds equals 12 minutes.
6. **Do a test run.** Do the whole workout yourself. Too hard? Too easy? Fix it.
7. **Write a pep talk.** Coaches motivate. Write three lines to shout during the hardest station: "Ten more seconds! You've got this!"

## Make it yours
- Give it a theme: "Superhero Training Camp" or "Zoo Workout" where every move is an animal.
- Make a partner workout: high-fives between reps, wheelbarrow walks, passing a ball.
- Design a "no-noise" version for apartments: no jumping, all slow and controlled.

## Stuck?
- Someone can't do a move: point them to the easy version or invent a new one.
- The timing is hard to track: have one person be the timekeeper and call "switch."
- Nobody wants to join: start it yourself in the living room. Energy is contagious.

## Show it off
Lead the workout for your family when they're home, as the coach.

## Clean up
Put away the cards in an envelope, return the towel, and drink some water.

## For the grown-up
This builds fitness, leadership, and the idea of adjusting a challenge so everyone can join in, which is a big part of good teamwork. Jump in as a participant, or make sure anyone with a health condition picks the easy versions. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the hardest station for everyone, and who did the best?"
