title: Live Radio Show
grade: 4
order: 12
kind: Pretend
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pencil, a cardboard tube or hairbrush for a microphone, things to make sound effects (a pot, a spoon, paper to crumple, a cup of water)
nice: a phone to record (a grown-up's), a blanket fort studio, a timer

## The big idea
You're on the air! Long ago, families gathered around the radio for news, songs and stories. Today you run the station. Write and perform a 10-minute radio show with no pictures, only sounds.

## Goals
- You write a radio show with at least 4 parts.
- You perform it live for an audience.

## Objectives
- I can write for listeners who can't see anything.
- I can make sound effects to set a scene.
- I can use my voice to change characters.

## Get ready
Set up a "studio." A blanket fort, a closet or a corner. Gather sound effect things.

## Steps
1. Name your station. Example: "Radio Pancake, 104.5 FM."
2. Plan 4 parts (10 minutes). Pick from: news, weather, a song, a short story, a fake ad, an interview, a sports report.
3. Write a short script for each part. Keep each one to 2 minutes.
4. Plan sound effects. Rain is paper crumpling. Footsteps are fists tapping a table. Thunder is shaking a baking sheet.
5. Practice your announcer voice. "Good evening, listeners!"
6. Run through the whole show once. Time it.
7. Fix any part that runs too long.
8. Make a "ON AIR" sign for the door.

## Make it yours
- Make a call-in show. A family member "calls" and asks a question.
- Make a scary radio story with creaky door sounds.
- Make a "news from the future" show.

## Stuck?
- You run out of ideas: do a weather report for an imaginary planet.
- Sound effects are hard: say "SPLASH!" in a funny voice. That's okay too.
- You feel shy: perform from behind a door so the audience only hears you.

## Show it off
Have the family close their eyes and listen. Perform the whole show live.

## Clean up
Put sound effect things back. Fold the blanket.

## For the grown-up
This builds writing for an audience, speaking fluently and creative listening. A recording on your phone makes a great keepsake, but the show works without it. **Ask at dinner:** "What sound effect worked the best?"
