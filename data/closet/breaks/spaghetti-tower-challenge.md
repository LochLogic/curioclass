title: Spaghetti Tower Challenge
grade: 4
order: 7
kind: Build
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: 20 sticks of dry spaghetti, tape, string (about 3 feet), scissors, a ruler or tape measure, a small ball of foil (the size of a large grape) for the top
nice: a timer, mini marshmallows or gumdrops if you have them

## The big idea
Engineers race to build the tallest tower using spaghetti. The catch? It has to hold a "jewel" on top without falling. You get 20 sticks, some tape and some string. Ready, set, build!

## Goals
- You build a free-standing tower that holds a foil ball on top.
- You beat your own height record at least once.

## Objectives
- I can use triangles to make a strong shape.
- I can test a design and change it after a fail.
- I can measure and record heights.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Count out 20 spaghetti sticks. Cut 10 pieces of tape. Roll the foil ball.

## Steps
1. Try a shape. Tape 3 sticks into a triangle. Press on it. Now tape 4 into a square. Which is stronger?
2. Sketch 2 tower plans (10 minutes). Use triangles!
3. Build round one in 18 minutes. Use a timer if you have one.
4. Put the foil ball on top. Let go. Does it stand?
5. Measure from the table to the top. Write it down.
6. Look at what broke or bent. Write one fix.
7. Build round two with your fix. Can you go taller?
8. Try string as "cables" to pull the tower straight.

## Make it yours
- Build a bridge instead and see how many coins it holds.
- Build a tower that leans like a famous one but still stands.
- Challenge someone at home. Who builds tallest in 10 minutes?

## Stuck?
- Sticks snap: tape them in pairs to make them stronger.
- The tower leans: make the base wider.
- The top wobbles: add a triangle brace near the top.

## Show it off
Line up all your towers from shortest to tallest. Give the family a tour.

## Clean up
Sweep broken spaghetti into the trash. Roll up leftover string.

## For the grown-up
This is a classic engineering challenge that teaches shapes, stability and trying again. Dry pasta is sharp when it snaps, so it's best to sweep up rather than walk barefoot. **Ask at dinner:** "Why do you think triangles worked better than squares?"
