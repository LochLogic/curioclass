materials: paper, pencils, black markers or pens, colored pencils or markers, scissors, tape, an empty cereal box or other packaging, a ruler
nice: tracing paper, colored paper, a small box to make a product package

## The big idea
Every company you know has a team that decided exactly how it looks: the logo, the colors, the letters and even the way it talks. Today you're that team for a company that doesn't exist: a dragon-proof umbrella maker, a bakery on the moon, a shoe repair shop for giants. You'll build a full brand from scratch.

## Goals
- A finished logo, a color palette and a type style for your company.
- One "real" mockup: the logo on a package, a sign or a shirt design.

## Objectives
- I can design a logo that's clear at large and small sizes.
- I can choose colors and letterforms to fit a brand's personality.
- I can write a short tagline and brand voice.

## Get ready
Invent your company in one sentence: "Glimmer and Grind is a coffee cart that only serves people who work at night." List five words that describe its personality, like cozy, sleepy, magical, trustworthy, quirky.

## Steps
1. **Logo sketches** (15 minutes). Draw 15 tiny logo ideas in boxes the size of a postage stamp. Try three kinds: a **wordmark** (just the name in special letters), a **symbol** (a picture), and a **combination**.
2. Pick three and redraw them at about 8 cm wide. Then shrink one to 1 cm. Can you still tell what it is? Simple shapes survive.
3. Choose one and make it final: draw it carefully with a ruler, and fill in with black marker.
4. **Palette.** Pick one main color, one accent and one neutral. Explain each choice in one sentence ("deep blue for night, gold for the lamp light").
5. **Type.** Pick a lettering style for headings: blocky, rounded, tall and thin, or hand-drawn. Write the alphabet in that style, A to Z.
6. **Brand voice.** Write a tagline (under 7 words) and three sample sentences of how your company talks on a sign.
7. **Mockup** (25 minutes). Turn an empty cereal box inside out and redesign it as your product's package, using your logo, colors and type.

## Make it yours
- Give the company a rival with an opposite brand, and design both side by side.
- Design a mascot that follows all your brand rules.
- Rebrand the company for 100 years from now and show how it changed.

## Stuck?
- The logo is too busy: remove one element at a time until it breaks, then put the last one back.
- All your ideas look the same: try one with no letters at all.

## Show it off
Lay out a "brand board" with the logo, palette, type and mockup. Pitch it to your family as if they're the company's owners.

## Clean up
Recycle scraps and keep the brand board flat in a folder or on your wall.

## For the grown-up
This is graphic design: shape, color theory, type and audience, the same thinking used in art, marketing and design careers. It's a good look at how ads work on all of us. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did you pick those colors for your company?"
