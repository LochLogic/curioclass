time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors

materials: paper or a notebook, a pen, a bowl or hat, scrap paper, a timer or clock
nice: a friend or two to enter the contest too, a grown-up to be the judge

## The big idea
Short story contests often hand writers a random prompt and a hard word limit, then start the clock. The pressure
squeezes out ideas you would never plan. Run your own contest: draw a genre, a character, an object and a line of
dialogue from a hat, then write a complete story of exactly 1,000 words or fewer before time runs out.

## Goals
- A complete story under 1,000 words that uses all four random prompts.
- A final cut that is tighter and sharper than the first draft.

## Objectives
- I can build a full story arc in a tight space.
- I can cut words without losing meaning or voice.
- I can turn random constraints into a surprising, unified story.

## Get ready
Write five slips for each category and fold them into four piles. Genres: heist, ghost story, romance, western,
sci-fi. Characters: a retired magician, a night guard, a twin, a dog walker, a mayor. Objects: a jar of buttons, a
key with no lock, a broken watch, a map, a lunchbox. Dialogue: "You were never supposed to find this," "Run," "I
thought you'd be taller," "It's still warm," "Not again."

## Steps
1. Draw one slip from each pile. No redraws.
2. Plan for 10 minutes. Write three sentences: who wants what, what stops them, how it ends.
3. Start the clock: 45 minutes to draft. Begin in the middle of the action.
4. Use the object at least twice, and make it matter more the second time.
5. Fit the line of dialogue in where it hits hardest, often near the end.
6. Count words. Count one line and multiply by your number of lines for a quick estimate.
7. Cut (15 minutes): remove every "very," "just" and "suddenly," every scene that does not change something, and any
   line that explains a feeling instead of showing it.

## Make it yours
- Run a family contest with the same slips, then read the entries blind and vote.
- Add a fifth prompt: a setting or a weather condition.
- Write it in exactly 100 words, then expand the same story to 1,000.

## Stuck?
- You cannot connect the prompts: make the object the reason the character is in the genre's world.
- It is too long: cut the opening paragraph. Most stories start one paragraph too early.

## Show it off
Read it aloud at dinner, and let the family guess which four prompts you drew.

## Clean up
Keep the slips in an envelope for the next contest and file your story.

## For the grown-up
This builds creative writing, structure and editing skills under pressure. A word limit forces strong choices.
**Ask at dinner:** "Which prompt was hardest to use?"
