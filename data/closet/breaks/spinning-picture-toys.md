title: Spinning Picture Toys
grade: 6
order: 5
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: thin cardboard (a cereal box), white paper, glue, scissors, string or 2 rubber bands, a pencil, markers, a round lid or bowl to trace (about 15 cm across), a ruler, a mirror
nice: a pushpin and a pencil with an eraser for a spinner handle, a protractor

## The big idea
Before movies existed, people made pictures move with spinning paper toys. Your eyes hold onto an image for a split second, so when pictures flash by fast, your brain blends them into motion. You'll build two of these old-school animation toys and make your own drawings come alive.

## Goals
- You build a thaumatrope, a two-sided spinning card that blends two pictures into one.
- You build a spinning animation disk that plays a short looping animation in a mirror.

## Objectives
- I can explain persistence of vision, the brain holding an image for a moment.
- I can draw a sequence of frames that change a little each time.
- I can build and test a moving image device.

## Get ready
Clear a table. Trace a circle about 15 cm across onto cardboard and cut it out. Cut a second, smaller circle about 6 cm across for the thaumatrope.

## Steps
1. **Thaumatrope.** On the small circle, draw a bird on one side and an empty cage on the other. Flip the second one upside down before you draw it (turn the card top to bottom), or the bird will show up upside down.
2. Punch or poke a hole on each side of the circle. Thread string through each hole. Twist the strings, then pull to make it spin. The bird should appear inside the cage.
3. Design your own thaumatrope pair: a fish and a fishbowl, a face and a mustache, a head and a hat.
4. **Animation disk (30 minutes).** On the big circle, divide it into 8 equal slices like a pizza (use the ruler and draw lines through the center).
5. Cut a narrow slit about 1 cm wide and 2 cm deep at the outer edge of each slice line.
6. In each slice, just inside the slits, draw one frame of a short loop. Simple works best: a ball bouncing, a heart beating, a stick figure jumping. Change it a little each frame.
7. Push a pin through the center into a pencil eraser. Stand facing a mirror with the drawings toward the glass. Spin and look through the slits.

## Make it yours
- Make a loop that tells a joke, like a cat that jumps, misses, and falls.
- Try 12 frames instead of 8 for smoother motion.
- Make a thaumatrope with words, like "I LOVE" on one side and "PIZZA" on the other.

## Stuck?
- The thaumatrope pictures don't line up: make sure the second image is drawn upside down compared to the first.
- The disk looks blurry: your slits are too wide. Make them thinner or spin faster.
- The motion jumps: make smaller changes between frames.

## Show it off
Host a tiny "film premiere." Let each family member spin the toys and guess what the loop is before you tell them.

## Clean up
Put scissors and pins away safely, and keep your toys in an envelope.

## For the grown-up
This builds art skills (sequencing, drawing motion) and the science of how vision works. These toys came before cartoons and movies, so there's some fun history too. Keep pushpins away from younger kids. **Ask at dinner:** "How does your brain get tricked into seeing motion?"
