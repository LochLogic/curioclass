grade: 11
order: 12
kind: Story
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper or a notebook, a pen, a highlighter or second pen
nice: a timer, a friend or family member to read it aloud with you

## The big idea
No narration. No "she said." No description of the room. Just two people talking, and the reader still knows where
they are, who they are, what they want and what just went wrong. It is one of the hardest constraints in writing, and
when it works it feels like eavesdropping on something you should not hear.

## Goals
- A finished short story, 600 to 1,200 words, told only in dialogue.
- A reader who can tell you the setting and the secret without being told.

## Objectives
- I can reveal setting, character and conflict only through what people say.
- I can use subtext, where characters mean more than they say.
- I can give two characters voices so different that tags are not needed.

## Get ready
Find a quiet spot. Write at the top of the page: Who are they? Where are they? What does each one want? What are they
not saying?

## Steps
1. Choose your two characters (10 minutes). Give each a want that clashes with the other's. Example: two astronauts
   on a station, one wants to go home, the other knows they cannot.
2. Write a voice sheet: three habits for each speaker. One talks in short bursts and changes the subject; the other
   asks a lot of questions and uses formal words.
3. Plan the hidden thing. One character knows something the other does not.
4. Draft (30 minutes). Start in the middle of a conversation. Plant the setting through detail: "Stop tapping the
   glass. Nothing's out there but stars."
5. Build tension through dodges, interruptions and silence ("..." or a short line that does not answer).
6. End on a line that lands the reveal or changes everything. Do not explain it.
7. Highlight any line that is there only to explain. Cut it or rewrite it so it sounds like a person talking.

## Make it yours
- Make it a phone call, so one side is missing and the reader guesses the other half.
- Add exactly one line of narration at the very end and make it count.
- Write it as an interrogation where the suspect is cleverer than the detective.

## Stuck?
- You cannot tell who is talking: make one character use a nickname for the other or speak in a distinctive rhythm.
- It sounds stiff: read it aloud. Cut anything people would not actually say.

## Show it off
Do a table read with someone at home, each of you taking a part, after dinner.

## Clean up
Keep your voice sheet and draft together in your notebook.

## For the grown-up
This is advanced writing craft: subtext, characterization and economy, the same skills used in plays and screenplays.
**Ask at dinner:** "What was the secret in your story, and how did you hide it?"
