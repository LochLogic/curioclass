title: Travel Journal from a Place You've Never Been
grade: 8
order: 26
kind: Story
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper folded into a booklet, a pencil or pen, colored pencils, glue or tape, scissors, scrap paper, envelopes or receipts
nice: an atlas, encyclopedia or travel book from the shelf or library, old maps, tea or coffee to "age" the paper (ask first)

## The big idea
Travelers keep journals full of sketches, tickets and stories. You're going on a trip without leaving home. Pick a place you've never been, real or made-up, and write the journal of your three days there, complete with souvenirs and maps.

## Goals
- A travel journal with three days of entries.
- At least four "souvenirs": tickets, postcards, maps or receipts you made.

## Objectives
- I can write vivid descriptions using all five senses.
- I can build a believable setting from details.
- I can tell a story in a series of journal entries.

## Get ready
Pick your destination. A real place you've learned about in books (a mountain town, an island, a desert city), or a fictional one, like a city built on the back of a whale. If you pick a real place, read about it in a book or ask someone who's been there, so your details are true and respectful.

## Steps
1. **Make the journal.** Fold 4 sheets of paper in half and staple. Make the cover look worn: crumple and flatten it, or (with permission) brush with cold tea.
2. **Plan your trip.** Day 1: arrival. Day 2: the big adventure. Day 3: departure. What will happen each day? Leave room for something to go wrong.
3. **Write Day 1 (15 minutes).** Start with arrival. Use all five senses: "The air smelled like rain and frying onions. Somewhere, a bell rang 13 times."
4. **Make souvenirs (20 minutes).** A ticket stub (a strip of paper with a seat number), a postcard with a drawing, a receipt from a food stall, a hand-drawn map with your route. Glue them into the journal.
5. **Write Day 2.** The big adventure. Meet someone, get lost, try a new food, see something amazing. Include one sketch.
6. **Write Day 3.** How did the trip change you? What will you remember?
7. **Add finishing touches.** Doodles in the margins, a pressed leaf, a "packing list" on the inside cover.

## Make it yours
- Write it as a time traveler: a journal from a real place 500 years ago.
- Write it as a famous explorer you invent, sending notes back to a society of scientists.
- Leave a mystery: the last page is torn out, with only one strange line left.

## Stuck?
- Not sure what to write: describe the food. Food always makes a trip feel real.
- The place feels flat: give it one weird rule, like "everyone whistles instead of saying hello."
- Running out of ideas: write a letter home in the middle of the journal.

## Show it off
Hand the journal to someone at home and let them read it like a found treasure.

## Clean up
Put away supplies and keep your journal on a shelf.

## For the grown-up
This builds descriptive writing, narrative, and geography and cultural curiosity. If they choose a real place, encourage them to learn from books or from people who've been there, so it's grounded in real life rather than stereotypes. **Ask at dinner:** "Where did you go, and what's one thing you saw there?"
