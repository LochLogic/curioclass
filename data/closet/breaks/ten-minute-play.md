title: Ten-Minute Play
grade: 9
order: 18
kind: Pretend
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1+
materials: paper, a pen, a stapler or paper clips, chairs for a "stage"
nice: a few props, a lamp for stage lighting, two or three people to read parts

## The big idea
A ten-minute play is a complete story on stage: one place, a few people, one big change. Theaters hold whole festivals of them. You'll write one, then gather whoever's home for a **table read**, where actors read the script aloud for the first time. Hearing your words in someone else's voice is a strange and great feeling.

## Goals
- A finished script of about 8 to 10 pages in play format.
- A table read with at least one other person, or a solo read where you voice everyone.

## Objectives
- I can write in correct play format with character names, dialogue and stage directions.
- I can give each character a clear want and an obstacle.
- I can build a scene to a turning point, then a change.

## Get ready
Set up a writing spot. Write the format at the top of the page so you remember it: character name in capitals, dialogue below, stage directions in parentheses.

## Steps
1. **Premise** (10 minutes). Pick two characters and one place they can't easily leave: two strangers stuck in an elevator, a knight and a dragon at a job interview, siblings cleaning out a grandparent's attic.
2. Give each character a **want** that clashes with the other's. The knight needs the job; the dragon is the interviewer and hates knights.
3. Plan in three beats: the setup (page 1 to 3), the rising fight (page 4 to 7) and the **turn**, where something is revealed or someone changes (page 8 to 10).
4. Write (45 minutes). One page of script is about one minute on stage. Keep stage directions short: "(She slams the door. It doesn't close.)"
5. Read it aloud to yourself. Cut any line that explains feelings the actors could show.
6. **Table read.** Hand out parts. Read it straight through without stopping. Take notes on where people laughed and where they lost the thread.
7. Make one round of changes based on what you heard.

## Make it yours
- Add a third character who enters halfway through and flips everything.
- Write it with no dialogue at all, only stage directions, as a silent play.
- Write it so the last line is the same as the first line, but it means something totally different.

## Stuck?
- The characters just talk: give one of them a secret and a reason to hide it.
- It ends too soon: add an obstacle right when the problem seems solved.

## Show it off
Stage it in the living room with a lamp as a spotlight. Even scripts in hand count.

## Clean up
Staple the final draft and file it. Put any props back.

## For the grown-up
Playwriting teaches structure, character and dialogue, all central to English and drama classes, and a table read builds confidence. If they ask you to read a part, say yes. **Ask at dinner:** "What does each character want, and who gets it?"
