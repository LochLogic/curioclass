title: String Telephone
grade: 3
order: 11
kind: Science
time: 45-75 min
where: Either
mess: Low
grownup: Nearby
players: 2+
materials: 4 paper or plastic cups, string (about 20 feet), 2 paper clips, a pencil, a partner (a sibling, a friend over, or a grown-up after work)
nice: yogurt cups, a tin can with no sharp edges, fishing line, yarn

## The big idea
Can you talk to someone across the room with no phone? Yes! Sound can travel down a string. Build a string telephone and test which one works best.

## Goals
- You build a phone that carries a whisper across the room.
- You find out which string and cup work best.

## Objectives
- I can explain that sound is a shake, called a vibration.
- I can test two kinds of phones and compare them.
- I can keep a test fair by changing one thing at a time.

## Get ready
Find a long room or hallway. Clear a path. Find your partner.

## Steps
1. Poke a small hole in the bottom of each cup with a pencil.
2. Push the string through one cup. Tie it to a paper clip inside so it can't slip out.
3. Do the same at the other end with the second cup.
4. Walk apart until the string is tight. Talk into your cup. Your partner holds theirs to their ear.
5. Test it. Whisper a secret word. Can your partner hear it? Now let the string go loose. What happens?
6. Build phone number two with different string or cups. Test both with the same words.
7. Try fun tests (15 minutes). Talk around a corner. Pinch the string. Hum instead of talk.
8. Write a short report. Which phone won? Why do you think so?

## Make it yours
- Make a three-way phone. Tie a third string to the middle.
- Invent a phone code: one tug means "hello," two tugs mean "come here."
- Run your phone between two blanket forts.

## Stuck?
- You can't hear: pull the string tighter and keep it off the furniture.
- The knot pulls through: use a bigger paper clip or tie a big knot.
- No partner yet: hang one cup by a radio or a ticking clock. Listen at the other end.

## Show it off
Call a grown-up from the next room. Tell them a joke on the string phone.

## Clean up
Wind up the string. Stack the cups. Keep the phones in a bag.

## For the grown-up
This builds ideas about sound and vibration and the habit of comparing two designs fairly. Kids need a partner here, so it is a good after-work activity together. Keep string out of doorways so no one trips. **Ask at dinner:** "What happened to the sound when the string was loose?"
