kind: Outside
time: 60-90 min
where: Outdoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: string or yarn about 4 meters long, 4 sticks or pencils, a ruler or tape measure, paper, a pencil, a clipboard or a book to write on, a spoon for gentle digging
nice: a magnifying glass, a white plate to set bugs on for a closer look, colored pencils

## The big idea
Biodiversity means how many kinds of living things share one place. Scientists count it by marking a small square and looking very, very closely. You might think a patch of grass is boring. Look closer. It could be a whole city!

## Goals
- You mark a 1 meter square and count every kind of living thing in it.
- You make a field record with drawings and a total count.

## Objectives
- I can observe closely and record what I see.
- I can sort living things into groups, like plants, insects, and spiders.
- I can compare two squares and explain why one has more life.

## Get ready
Pick a spot: a lawn, a garden edge, a park with a grown-up, or a weedy sidewalk crack. Push 4 sticks into the ground in a square, 1 meter on each side. Tie the string around them.

## Steps
1. Sit still at the edge for 2 minutes. Watch for anything moving. Write it down before it leaves.
2. Make a chart: Plants, Insects, Spiders and Others, Signs of Life. Signs of life are clues like nibbled leaves or a feather.
3. Search the top layer (15 minutes). Look at every leaf. Count how many different kinds of plants you see.
4. Search the ground. Gently lift a rock or log and look under it. Put it back the same way.
5. Dig one spoonful of soil and spread it on a white plate. Look for tiny movers. Then return it.
6. Draw your three most interesting finds. Label each one, or give it a nickname like "tiny red speed bug."
7. Pick a second square in a different spot, like shade or near a path. Count again and compare (20 minutes).

## Make it yours
- Come back at a different time of day. Does the square change?
- Pretend you are an alien scientist writing a report on "Earth life, Square 1."
- Give a prize to the most surprising creature, with a funny award name.

## Stuck?
- You see nothing: get closer. Lie on your belly with your face 20 cm from the ground.
- Bugs move too fast: count it once, then watch it go. You don't need to catch it.
- No yard: use a planter, a balcony box, or a patch of park with a grown-up.

## Show it off
Show your chart and drawings. Ask your family to guess how many kinds of life you found before you reveal it.

## Clean up
Put rocks and logs back where they were. Pull up the sticks and wind up the string. Wash your hands.

## For the grown-up
This is real field science: observing, sorting living things, and comparing habitats. Any outdoor patch works, even a crack in the pavement. Remind your child to look but not touch anything that stings or bites, and to wash hands after. If your child heads past your yard, go with them. **Ask at dinner:** "Which square had more life, and why do you think so?"
