kind: Build
time: 90-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1
materials: a shoebox or small cardboard box, extra cardboard, 2 wooden skewers or long straight sticks, 2 straws, tape, glue, scissors, a pencil, a ruler, a bottle cap, markers
nice: a hot glue gun (grown-up only), clothespins to hold glue while it dries, beads to keep parts from sliding

## The big idea
An automaton is a moving machine with a hidden mechanism. You turn a handle, and a figure on top bobs, waves, or hops. Inside is a cam, a lumpy wheel that pushes things up and down. Build your own cardboard machine and bring a character to life!

## Goals
- You build a box machine with a crank that makes a figure move.
- You design your own character and its motion.

## Objectives
- I can explain how a cam turns spinning into up-and-down motion.
- I can change the cam's shape to change the motion.
- I can fix a machine part that sticks or slips.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up to poke two holes in the long sides of the box. They go across from each other, halfway up. Ask for one hole in the center of the box top, just big enough for a straw.

## Steps
1. Cut two cardboard circles about 6 cm wide. These will be your cams.
2. Poke a skewer hole off center in each circle, about 1.5 cm from the edge. Off center is the secret. It makes the bump.
3. Push the skewer through one side of the box, through a cam, and out the other side. Glue the cam to the skewer.
4. Make the follower. Tape a small cardboard square (3 cm) to the bottom of a straw. Drop the straw down through the top hole so the square rests on the cam.
5. Turn the skewer. The straw should rise and fall! Add a bottle cap to the end of the skewer as a handle.
6. Design your figure (20 minutes). A fish jumping waves? A dragon popping from a nest? Draw it on cardboard, cut it out, and tape it to the top of the straw.
7. Decorate the box as the scene. Add a second cam and straw for a second moving part if you have time.

## Make it yours
- Try an egg-shaped cam instead of a circle. How does the motion change?
- Make two figures that move at different times, like a cat chasing a mouse.
- Write a tiny story that your machine acts out with each turn.

## Stuck?
- The straw sticks: make the top hole a little bigger, or trim the follower square.
- The cam slips on the skewer: add more glue or wrap tape around the skewer for grip.
- The figure barely moves: move the skewer hole closer to the edge of the cam.

## Show it off
Put on a show. Turn the crank slowly and tell the story, then let your family try the handle.

## Clean up
Throw away scraps and put the glue and scissors back. Let the glue dry overnight.

## For the grown-up
This builds real engineering ideas: cams, followers, and how machines change one kind of motion into another. It also takes patience and fixing. Please poke the holes yourself with a sharp pencil or skewer, and handle any hot glue yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "Can you explain how the off-center hole makes your figure move?"
