time: 60-90 min
where: Either

players: 1+
materials: a flashlight, paper, a pencil, a blanket or two, pillows, red, orange or yellow paper or tissue paper, tape
nice: a paper towel tube, a jar of water for spooky sound effects, a stuffed animal audience
night_before: Ask who in the family can be your audience after dinner.

## The big idea
Long ago, people told stories around fires at night. The best ones had a slow build, a strange sound, and a big surprise. Tonight you will build a pretend campfire and tell a story that gives your family shivers. Not too scary. Just enough.

## Goals
- You write a short campfire tale with a surprise ending.
- You tell it by flashlight to an audience.

## Objectives
- I can plan a story with a beginning, a build-up, and a twist (a surprise turn).
- I can use my voice to show feeling, like whispering or pausing.
- I can add sound effects at the right moment.

## Get ready
Pick a corner for the "camp." Spread out a blanket and pile some pillows around it. Lay out paper and a pencil.

## Steps
1. Build the fire. Crumple the colored paper into flame shapes. Tape them around the flashlight, pointing up. Turn it on and see it glow.
2. Pick your setting. Choose one: an old lighthouse, a quiet lake, a snowy cabin, or a place you invent.
3. Plan your story on paper (15 minutes). Use three boxes: Beginning, Build-up, Twist. Write a few words in each.
4. Add three "creepy clues" to the build-up. Example: a door that was closed is now open. Footprints that stop in the middle of a field.
5. Write the twist. Funny twists are great. Maybe the monster just wanted its lost sock back.
6. Choose two sound effects. Tap the floor slowly for footsteps. Blow across a jar for wind.
7. Practice telling it out loud twice (15 minutes). Slow down before the twist. Pause. Then say it.

## Make it yours
- Tell the story from the monster's side, so the people are the scary ones.
- Leave the ending open. Let the audience vote on what happened next.
- Make it a story chain: you tell one part, and each listener adds a sentence.

## Stuck?
- No ideas: start with "Nobody ever went past the old ___ after dark. Except me."
- The story feels too long: cut it down to three clues and one twist. Short is spookier.
- Someone gets too scared: switch to a funny twist right away.

## Show it off
After dinner, dim the lights and invite your family to the campfire. Hold the flashlight under your chin for the twist.

## Clean up
Fold the blankets, put pillows back, and keep your paper fire for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds story structure (beginning, rising action, twist), planning, and speaking with expression. The "fire" is only paper and a flashlight; keep real flames and candles out of it. If your child scares easily, the funny-twist version is just as much fun. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the creepiest clue in your story, and how did you come up with it?"
