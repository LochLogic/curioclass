title: Write a Cryptic Crossword
grade: 12
order: 11
kind: Puzzle
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: graph paper (or paper ruled into a grid), a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, a pen
nice: a dictionary or thesaurus, a friend who likes puzzles to test it

## The big idea
In a cryptic crossword, every clue is a tiny riddle with two halves: a plain definition and some wordplay that
leads to the same answer. "Hear silent ramble (6)" means LISTEN: "hear" is the definition, and "silent" rambles
(rearranges) into LISTEN. Once you see the trick, they are addictive. Learn the main clue types and build a small
cryptic of your own that will stump your family.

## Goals
- A finished 9 by 9 crossword grid, filled with real words.
- Eight or more cryptic clues that a solver can crack.

## Objectives
- I can write clues using anagrams, hidden words, charades and double definitions.
- I can build a grid where every word crosses others.
- I can test a clue for fairness: one definition, one piece of wordplay, one answer.

## Get ready
Draw a 9 by 9 grid with 1 cm squares. Shade a pattern of black squares that is symmetrical: if you shade one, shade
its mirror across the center too. Aim for words of 3 to 9 letters.

## Steps
1. Learn four clue types (15 minutes). Solve each example before reading the answer.
   - **Anagram:** "Hear silent ramble (6)" is LISTEN. Words like "ramble," "broken" or "wild" signal a rearrangement.
   - **Hidden word:** "Small house found in some cab in town (5)" is CABIN, hiding in "cab in."
   - **Charade:** pieces in a row. "Vehicle with a pet makes a rug (6)" is CAR plus PET, CARPET.
   - **Double definition:** two meanings, one word. "Tree's hand (4)" is PALM.
2. Fill the grid with words first (20 minutes). Place the longest words, then work outward. Pencil and erase freely.
3. Write a clue for each answer (40 minutes). The definition always goes at the very start or very end of the clue.
4. Polish the **surface**: each clue should read like a normal, slightly odd sentence that hides how it works.
5. Number the grid and write Across and Down lists with letter counts in brackets.
6. Solve-test every clue cold the next hour. Is there only one possible answer?

## Make it yours
- Hide a theme: every answer relates to your town, your friends or a book you love.
- Hide a secret message in the first letters of the Across answers.
- Make a mini 5 by 5 cryptic for the family to solve at breakfast.

## Stuck?
- The grid will not fill: swap a tricky long word for two shorter ones, or move a black square (and its mirror).
- A clue is too hard: make the wordplay indicator more obvious and the definition more direct.

## Show it off
Give the puzzle to someone at dinner, with one free hint card per person.

## Clean up
Keep your clue notes and a clean inked copy of the puzzle.

## For the grown-up
This builds vocabulary, wordplay, logic and precise writing. Cryptics are a classic word-lover's hobby, and solving
your teen's puzzle together is half the fun. **Ask at dinner:** "What's your sneakiest clue?"
