grade: 7
order: 11
kind: Art
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: printer paper, a pencil, an eraser, a ruler, colored pencils or markers, a black pen, scraps of fabric or old magazines, glue or tape
nice: tracing paper, a sheet of cardboard for a mood board

## The big idea
Fashion designers don't make one outfit. They make a collection: six or more looks that all tell the same story. Pick a wild theme, design a whole line of clothes, and present it like a real designer.

## Goals
- A collection of at least six outfit sketches that share a theme.
- A mood board with colors, textures and a collection name.

## Objectives
- I can draw a fashion figure using a proportion guide.
- I can design several items that look like they belong together.
- I can explain my design choices to an audience.

## Get ready
Pick your theme. Some ideas: clothes for living on Mars, a storm-chaser team, a school uniform in 100 years, a pirate crew that's also a rock band. Write it at the top of a page.

## Steps
1. **Make a mood board (15 minutes).** On cardboard or paper, glue fabric scraps, magazine colors and little sketches that fit your theme. Choose a palette of four colors and stick to it.
2. **Draw a figure template.** Fashion figures are tall and simple. Draw a line down the page and divide it into nine equal parts. The head takes the top part, the shoulders sit at the bottom of part two, the waist at part four, the knees at part six or seven. Keep it light and simple: this is a hanger for clothes, not a portrait.
3. **Trace it six times.** Use tracing paper, a window, or just redraw it lightly so every look is on the same body.
4. **Design the looks (40 minutes).** Every look needs one "signature" detail that repeats across the collection, like a lightning-bolt seam or big pockets. Mix it up: one cozy look, one formal, one for action, one that's totally over the top for the finale.
5. **Add detail.** Show fabric with texture marks: tiny dots for knit, wavy lines for silk, cross-hatching for denim. Add a close-up box of one special detail, like a button or zipper.
6. **Ink and color.** Go over the outlines in black pen, erase the pencil, then color with your palette.
7. **Name each look.** Write a short label under each: "Look 4: Dust Storm Parka, waterproof, with a built-in face scarf."

## Make it yours
- Design the collection for a client: a grandparent, a dog, or a person with a superpower.
- Make one look real: restyle something from your closet (with permission) to match your sketch.
- Make it sustainable: every item must be designed from things a house would throw away.

## Stuck?
- Figures look stiff: tilt the hips one way and the shoulders the other way. Instant pose.
- Everything looks the same: change the silhouette. Try one look that's long, one that's short, one that's puffy.
- Can't draw faces: don't. Many designers leave faces blank.

## Show it off
Line up your sketches on a wall like a runway, then give a two-minute "designer's talk" to your family about the collection.

## Clean up
Put the fabric scraps in a bag, cap your markers, and keep your sketches flat in a folder.

## For the grown-up
This builds drawing skills (proportion, line, texture) and design thinking: making a set of choices that hold together around an idea. **Ask at dinner:** "What was your collection's theme, and which look would you actually wear?"
