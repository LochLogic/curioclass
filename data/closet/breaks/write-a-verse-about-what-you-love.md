title: Write a Verse About What You Love
grade: 9
order: 24
kind: Music
time: 60-90 min
where: Indoors
mess: Low
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper, a pen, a table or box to tap a beat on
nice: a phone timer or metronome, a second person to keep the beat

## The big idea
A verse is 16 bars of rhythm, rhyme and voice, and the best ones sound like nobody else could have written them. Yours will be about something you love: a person, a place, a game, a food, a feeling. You'll build your own beat, write to it, and perform it with real flow.

## Goals
- A 16-bar verse (or song verse) with a hook.
- A performance over a beat you made yourself.

## Objectives
- I can count bars and fit syllables to a beat.
- I can use **multisyllabic rhymes** and internal rhyme.
- I can vary flow by changing speed, pauses and where words land.

## Get ready
Make a beat on a table: knock (low) on 1 and 3, slap (high) on 2 and 4. Keep it at a medium speed, about 90 beats per minute. That's about the speed of a calm walk.

## Steps
1. **Brainstorm** (10 minutes). Write your topic in the middle of a page. Around it, write 20 specific details: smells, sounds, names, places, inside jokes. "Grandma's kitchen" becomes "steam on the window, cumin, the radio in Spanish, the chair with the wobble."
2. **Rhyme bank.** Take your best details and list rhymes for them. Go past one-syllable rhymes. "Kitchen" rhymes with "missing," "listen," "glistenin'." "Window" with "slow flow" and "info."
3. **Write the hook**: 4 bars you'll repeat. Make it simple and catchy.
4. **Write 16 bars** (25 minutes). One bar is four beats, which is about 8 to 12 syllables at this speed. Read each line over your beat as you go.
5. Use **internal rhyme**: rhymes inside lines, not just at the end. "The cumin in the room is like a perfume blooming."
6. Change the flow in the middle. Slow down for four bars, or put your rhymes on the off-beats.
7. Mark breath spots with a slash. Practice the whole verse with the beat 5 times.

## Make it yours
- Write it from the point of view of the thing you love, like your old sneakers talking about you.
- Write two verses: one about what you love, and one about something that got in its way.
- Change the genre: turn it into a slow ballad or a chant for a stadium.

## Stuck?
- Lines are too long for the beat: cut filler words like "really," "just" and "so."
- It sounds forced: never use a rhyme you don't mean. Rewrite the line around a better word.

## Show it off
Perform it for someone at home with a second person keeping the beat. Then teach them the hook.

## Clean up
Copy the final verse into a notebook. Keep the rhyme bank for next time.

## For the grown-up
This builds poetry skills (rhyme, rhythm, imagery and voice) and music skills like tempo and meter, the same skills in English and music classes. Let them perform without judging the genre. **Ask at dinner:** "Can you rap the hook for us?"
