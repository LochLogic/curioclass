title: Junk Robot
grade: 1
order: 10
kind: Build
time: 60-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: With you
players: 1+
materials: clean recycling (boxes, tubes, plastic bottles, egg cartons, lids), tape, glue, aluminum foil, markers, paper, scissors (grown-up does any tough cutting), string or yarn
nice: brass paper fasteners for moving arms, pipe cleaners, bottle caps

## The big idea
Read aloud: "Beep boop! The Robot Factory has run out of parts, so it needs a brilliant inventor to build a new robot from junk. Your robot can do anything you invent: cook, clean, dance, fly. What will your robot do?"

## Goals
- Your child builds a robot from recycling with a body, head, arms and a special feature.
- Your child invents what the robot does and presents it.

## Objectives
- Your child can join materials together in a sturdy way.
- Your child can plan a design and change it when something doesn't work.
- Your child can explain what each part of the robot is for.

## Get ready
Wash and dry recycling. Spread it all out on a table or floor so your child can see the choices. Set out tape, glue and foil.

## Steps
1. **Robot job.** Ask: "What is your robot's job?" A homework robot? A pancake-flipping robot? A robot that tickles? Write the job at the top of a paper.
2. **Draw a plan** (5 minutes). Your child sketches the robot and labels parts: "arms to flip pancakes," "big eyes to see dust."
3. **Build the body** (20 minutes). Your child picks a box for the body and a smaller box for the head. Tape them together. Add tube arms and legs.
4. **Make it strong.** Shake it gently. Does anything fall off? Add more tape, or tape around the whole joint.
5. **Special features** (20 minutes). Your child adds the parts the robot needs for its job: a spatula hand, a foil antenna, buttons, a control panel, a storage door.
6. **Moving parts.** If you have paper fasteners, poke a hole (grown-up) and attach the arms so they swing.
7. **Name it and program it.** Your child names the robot and writes or tells 3 "commands" the robot understands, like "DANCE," "SPIN," "SAY HELLO."

## Make it yours
- Build a robot pet instead, like a robo-dog that fetches socks.
- Make a robot that's broken and needs fixing, and invent the tools to fix it.
- Your child becomes the robot: they follow your commands in a robot voice.

## Stuck?
- Tape won't stick to plastic: use more tape wrapped all the way around, or swap for a cardboard piece.
- The robot falls over: give it bigger feet (a flat box) or make it sit.

## Show it off
Hold a Robot Expo when the family gets home. Your child demonstrates the robot and its commands.

## Clean up
Return unused recycling to the bin. Your child puts tape and markers away.

## For the grown-up
This builds engineering thinking, planning, fine motor skills and creative problem solving. Check recycling for sharp edges (cans, torn plastic) and leave out anything that held cleaning products. Do any hard cutting and poking yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "What would your robot do if it were real?"
