title: Mug Cake Science
grade: 5
order: 10
kind: Kitchen
time: 45-75 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: Nearby
players: 1+
materials: 2 microwave-safe mugs, flour, sugar, cocoa powder, baking powder, salt, milk, vegetable oil, measuring spoons, a fork, a microwave, oven mitts, a ruler, a spoon
nice: chocolate chips, a toothpick, a timer

## The big idea
Why do cakes puff up? A secret ingredient makes tiny gas bubbles. You will bake two mug cakes, one with the secret and one without, and see what happens. Then you get to eat the evidence.

## Goals
- You make two mug cakes, one with baking powder and one without.
- You measure and compare them, then eat them.

## Objectives
- I can follow a recipe and measure carefully.
- I can run a fair test where only one ingredient is different.
- I can explain what baking powder does in a cake.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up if you can use the microwave. Check with them about allergies. Wash your hands. Set out everything on the counter. Label the mugs A and B with tape.

## Steps
1. In each mug, mix 4 tablespoons flour, 2 tablespoons sugar, 1 tablespoon cocoa, and a pinch of salt. Stir with a fork.
2. Add 1/4 teaspoon baking powder to mug A only. Mug B gets none. This is the only difference.
3. Add 3 tablespoons milk and 1 tablespoon oil to each mug. Stir until there are no dry lumps.
4. Make a guess. Write it down: "I think mug A will be ___ cm taller."
5. Microwave mug A for 70 seconds. Use mitts to take it out. Hot! Then do mug B.
6. Let both cool for 3 minutes. Measure how tall each cake is with a ruler. Taste a bite of each.
7. Write down what you see. Which is fluffier? Which is dense, like a brownie? Which do you like better?

## Make it yours
- Try a third mug with double baking powder. Is more always better?
- Invent a flavor: add chocolate chips, a pinch of cinnamon, or a spoon of jam in the middle.
- Turn it into a "cake contest" where family members rate each one from 1 to 5.

## Stuck?
- The cake is gooey in the middle: microwave 15 more seconds at a time.
- The cake is rubbery: it cooked too long. Try 10 seconds less next time.
- It overflowed: use a bigger mug or a little less batter.

## Show it off
Serve tiny tasting bites to your family. Show your measurements and ask them to guess which had the secret.

## Clean up
Soak the mugs in warm water, wash the spoons, and wipe the counter. Put every ingredient back.

## For the grown-up
This is kitchen chemistry: baking powder releases carbon dioxide gas when wet and heated, which puffs the cake. It also practices measuring fractions. Please check allergies first (wheat, milk). Mugs get very hot in the microwave, so make sure your child uses mitts, and give the okay before they start. **Ask at dinner:** "What did the baking powder do, and how could you tell?"
