mess: Medium

players: 1+
materials: milk (or half-and-half), sugar, vanilla, salt, ice cubes (about 3 cups per batch), a small zip-top bag, a large zip-top bag, measuring cups and spoons, a towel or winter gloves, a spoon
nice: a kitchen thermometer, cocoa powder, crushed cookies or fruit, a second set of bags for a test

## The big idea
How do you make ice cream without a freezer? Shake it! Salt makes ice super cold, colder than plain ice. That cold pulls heat out of the milk until it freezes. Get ready to shake, shake, shake and then eat your science.

## Goals
- You make a serving of real ice cream in a bag.
- You find out what salt does to ice.

## Objectives
- I can measure ingredients carefully.
- I can test ice with and without salt to compare.
- I can explain why the salty ice gets colder.

## Get ready
Ask a grown-up about allergies first. Wash your hands. Lay a towel on the counter. Measure everything before you start.

## Steps
1. In the small bag, put 1 cup milk, 1 tablespoon sugar, and 1/2 teaspoon vanilla. Squeeze out the air. Seal it tight.
2. Fill the large bag halfway with ice. Add 1/3 cup salt.
3. Put the small bag inside the big bag. Seal the big bag.
4. Wrap the bag in a towel or put on gloves. It will get very cold!
5. Shake for 5 to 10 minutes. Toss it, roll it, dance with it. Check every 2 minutes.
6. Want a test? Make a second big bag with ice but no salt. Hold one in each hand for 30 seconds. Which is colder? (Use a thermometer if you have one.)
7. When it is firm, take the small bag out. Rinse the outside with cold water so salt doesn't get in. Eat it!

## Make it yours
- Invent a flavor: add 1 teaspoon cocoa, a spoon of jam, or a pinch of cinnamon to the small bag.
- Race a family member. Who can make ice cream fastest?
- Make an ice cream shop menu with your flavors and silly names.

## Stuck?
- It is still soupy: add more ice and salt, and keep shaking 3 more minutes.
- It tastes salty: the small bag leaked. Seal it better and double-bag it next time.
- Your hands are freezing: switch to gloves or pass the bag to a helper.

## Show it off
Serve tiny tasting spoons to your family. Explain the salt secret while they eat.

## Clean up
Pour the salty water down the sink, throw away the bags, and wipe the counter.

## For the grown-up
This is kitchen chemistry: salt lowers the freezing point of water, so salted ice gets colder than 0 C and can freeze milk. Please check for milk or other allergies, and use a dairy-free milk if needed (it will be softer). The bag gets cold enough to hurt bare hands, so a towel or gloves matters. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did we need the salt?"
