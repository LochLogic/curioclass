title: Cause Zine
grade: 11
order: 17
kind: Story
time: 75-120 min
where: Indoors
mess: Medium
grownup: On your own
players: 1
materials: paper (letter size), scissors, glue stick, pens and markers, old magazines or newspapers, a stapler or needle and thread
nice: a photocopier, colored paper, stamps or stencils

## The big idea
Zines are handmade, photocopied magazines that people have used for decades to spread music scenes, art and causes
that mainstream media ignored. Pick something you care about, from animal shelters to bike lanes to a local band, and
make a bold, small zine that makes people care too.

## Goals
- A finished 8-page zine about one cause, folded from a single sheet or stapled.
- A plan for where it goes: the fridge, a friend, a community board.

## Objectives
- I can choose a clear message and design pages around it.
- I can mix words and images to persuade and inform.
- I can use a layout that guides a reader through the pages.

## Get ready
Fold a sheet into an 8-page mini zine: fold in half lengthwise, unfold, fold in half the other way twice, cut the
middle crease, and fold into a booklet. Make one blank test version first.

## Steps
1. Pick your cause (5 minutes). Write one sentence: "I want readers to ____ because ____."
2. Plan the pages: cover, the problem, a story or fact, why it matters, what you can do, a call to action, back cover.
3. Gather materials. Cut headlines, letters and images from magazines. Collect facts you already know or can ask
   someone about.
4. Lay out each page (35 minutes). Mix big text, collage and drawings. Leave white space. A single strong image can
   say more than a paragraph.
5. Write the call to action with something easy to do in under a day: "Bring an old towel to the shelter," "Turn off
   the hall light."
6. Make a master copy. If you have a copier, make five more; if not, hand-copy one more for a friend.

## Make it yours
- Make a zine for a fictional cause, like protecting a rare species of dragon or saving a haunted library.
- Add a fold-out poster in the middle.
- Make it interactive with a fill-in page, a cut-out bookmark or a quiz.

## Stuck?
- You do not know what cause to pick: list five things that annoy you, then flip one into a cause.
- Pages feel cluttered: cut half the text and make the strongest line huge.

## Show it off
Hand copies to family members at dinner and ask each one to do the call to action this week.

## Clean up
Recycle magazine scraps, put the glue and scissors away, and wipe glue off the table.

## For the grown-up
This builds persuasive writing, design and media literacy. Zines are a long tradition of independent publishing.
Help them find a safe place to share copies if they want to. **Ask at dinner:** "What do you want people to do after
reading it?"
