MA.K.T1	Kindergarten civics: being a good citizen in the classroom
MA.K.T1.1	Follow rules and expectations with little reminding, and with help ask and answer questions about why rules exist.
MA.K.T1.2	Take on jobs at school and home, follow through on them, and be helpful and respectful to others.
MA.K.T1.3	With help, point to characters in stories and texts who show fairness, caring, responsibility, or authority, or who show how rules are made and followed.
MA.K.T1.4	Ask and answer questions and use books to learn about national symbols, songs, and texts of the United States.
MA.K.T1.4a	Explain why the United States flag has its colors, stars, and stripes.
MA.K.T1.4b	Explain why the bald eagle is the national emblem of the United States.
MA.K.T1.4c	Explain why "The Star-Spangled Banner" is the national anthem.
MA.K.T1.4d	Explain what the words of the "Pledge of Allegiance" mean.
MA.K.T2	Kindergarten geography: how places connect
MA.K.T2.1	Describe where people, objects, and places are using position words such as near, far, left, right, behind, and between.
MA.K.T2.2	With help, explain how maps and globes are alike and different.
MA.K.T2.3	Name the parts of an address: street name and number, city or town, state (Massachusetts), and country (United States).
MA.K.T2.4	With help, find the school's city or town on a state map and the school itself on a street map of that town.
MA.K.T2.5	Use maps, photographs, or drawings to show and explain where important places are near home or school and how they relate.
MA.K.T2.6	Make maps, drawings, and models that show the physical features of familiar places.
MA.K.T3	Kindergarten history: shared traditions
MA.K.T3.1	Describe how civic holidays honor important events or people in history.
MA.K.T3.2	Compare and contrast the traditions and celebrations of people from different cultural backgrounds.
MA.K.T3.3	Put events from personal life, nature, and texts in time order using words about sequence and time.
MA.K.T3.3a	Use sequence words such as first, next, and last to order actions.
MA.K.T3.3b	Use time words such as now, then, long ago, before, after, today, tomorrow, and yesterday to place events in time.
MA.K.T4	Kindergarten economics: work and buying and selling
MA.K.T4.1	With help, describe things people do when they work inside and outside the home, using experience and texts.
MA.K.T4.2	With help, ask and answer questions about buying, selling, and trading, and explain how people choose among things they need and want.
MA.K.T4.3	With help, give examples of goods and services people pay for with money they earn.
MA.1.T1	Grade 1 civics: groups, leaders, elections, and citizens
MA.1.T1.1	Explain the benefits and meaning of belonging to a group, follow a group's rules and duties, and explain the reasons for those rules.
MA.1.T1.2	Investigate the different roles members play in a group and explain how each role helps reach a shared goal.
MA.1.T1.3	Explain that a leader is also a group member who takes on extra responsibility, such as inspiring others, organizing tasks, and guiding decisions.
MA.1.T1.4	Analyze examples of leaders from history, daily life, and texts, and describe the qualities of a good leader.
MA.1.T1.5	Give examples of why groups with differing views need ways to decide, and explain how voting can make fair decisions or choose leaders.
MA.1.T1.6	Explain that an election is a kind of voting in which people choose their leaders.
MA.1.T1.7	Name some leaders chosen by elections, such as the President, the Governor of Massachusetts, or a team captain, and explain what they do.
MA.1.T1.8	Explain that people in a town, city, or nation are citizens whose rights and duties include electing leaders for set terms and judging whether to re-elect them.
MA.1.T1.9	Explain that people born in the United States are citizens, others become citizens later, and some residents who are not citizens still belong to the community with rights and responsibilities.
MA.1.T1.10	Evaluate the qualities of a good citizen or community member using examples from history, texts, news, and personal experience.
MA.1.T2	Grade 1 geography: places to explore
MA.1.T2.1	Explain that a map represents space and helps people find locations and features.
MA.1.T2.2	Use the cardinal directions to find and describe places on a map, locate major cities and capitals, and investigate why they became important.
MA.1.T2.3	Explain that a capital is the city where a state or nation's government is centered.
MA.1.T2.4	Locate and explain physical features such as continents, oceans, rivers, lakes, and mountains on maps, and make maps of local places.
MA.1.T2.5	Explain that people in different parts of the world can have different ways of living, customs, and languages.
MA.1.T3	Grade 1 history: unity and diversity in the United States
MA.1.T3.1	Use evidence to explain how people in the United States are united, such as by a shared national history, and how they are diverse in backgrounds, beliefs, and traditions.
MA.1.T3.2	Explain the meaning of symbols, phrases, and songs that show pride in belonging to the United States.
MA.1.T3.2a	Explain the meaning of national symbols such as the flag, the bald eagle, the White House, and the Statue of Liberty.
MA.1.T3.2b	Explain the meaning of national words and mottoes, such as "E pluribus unum" ("Out of Many, One") and the "Pledge of Allegiance."
MA.1.T3.2c	Recognize the tunes and words of patriotic songs such as "America the Beautiful" and "The Star-Spangled Banner."
MA.1.T3.3	Record patterns in seasonal events or personal experiences using a calendar and words and verb tenses about past, present, and future.
MA.1.T4	Grade 1 economics: resources and choices
MA.1.T4.1	Explain how natural resources in a place connect to local industries and jobs such as fishing, farming, or mining.
MA.1.T4.2	Tell the difference between renewable and non-renewable resources.
MA.1.T4.3	Explain that people are a resource too, since skills and knowledge from school and work lead to new inventions and more goods and services.
MA.1.T4.4	Explain what being employed means and define income, wages, and salary.
MA.1.T4.5	Give examples of goods that people buy and use.
MA.1.T4.6	Give examples of services people do for one another.
MA.1.T4.7	Give examples of choices people make when buying goods and services, and explain that limited money forces those choices.
MA.1.T4.8	Analyze examples of voluntary buying choices, such as picking a company that treats workers well or protects the environment.
MA.1.T4.9	Compare reasons people save money, such as a future purchase, a donation, or an emergency.

MA.3.T1	Grade 3: Massachusetts cities and towns, past and present
MA.3.T1.1	Use a current Massachusetts map with directions, scale, legend, and title to locate and describe the student's own town, its geographic features, and historic landmarks.
MA.3.T1.2	Research the origins and people of the town or city, including Native Peoples, colonial founders, and enslaved and free workers, and describe its population and immigrant groups now and in the past through interviews.
MA.3.T1.3	Explain why schools, towns, and cities have governments, what they do, how Massachusetts local governments are organized, and how people take part in their communities.
MA.3.T2	Grade 3: geography and Native Peoples of Massachusetts
MA.3.T2.1	Use a physical map of North America to locate the Northeast region and identify its important rivers, lakes, shoreline, capes, bays, and mountains.
MA.3.T2.2	Locate the six New England states on a political map of the United States.
MA.3.T2.3	Explain the diversity of Native Peoples, past and present, in Massachusetts and New England.
MA.3.T2.3a	Name at least three Native groups of the region, such as the Massachusett, Narragansett, Nipmuc, and Wampanoag.
MA.3.T2.3b	Locate the tribal territories within Massachusetts.
MA.3.T2.3c	Explain how physical features influenced where traditional settlements were located.
MA.3.T2.3d	Describe contributions of a tribal group near the school, such as foods, crafts, words, place names, and useful knowledge.
MA.3.T3	Grade 3: European explorers meet Native Peoples of the Northeast
MA.3.T3.1	Locate North America, the Atlantic, and Europe, and explain how Native Peoples first met Europeans and why Europeans sailed west in the 1500s and 1600s.
MA.3.T3.2	Trace on a map the voyages of European explorers of the Northeast coast, such as John Cabot, Verrazano, John Smith, and Champlain.
MA.3.T3.3	Explain how one explorer described Native Peoples and the new lands, and compare an early 1600s map of New England with a current one.
MA.3.T4	Grade 3: the Pilgrims, Plymouth Colony, and Native communities
MA.3.T4.1	Explain who the Pilgrims were, why they left Europe, how they traveled and governed Plymouth Colony, and analyze their relationships with the Wampanoag and Abenaki/Wabanaki.
MA.3.T4.1a	Explain the purpose of the Mayflower Compact and the idea of self-government.
MA.3.T4.1b	Describe challenges Pilgrim men, women, and children faced, such as building homes, farming, adjusting to a new place, and keeping their community together.
MA.3.T4.1c	Describe contacts with Samoset and Massasoit, the events leading to a harvest thanksgiving, and later relations between Europeans and Native Peoples in southeastern Massachusetts.
MA.3.T5	Grade 3: Puritans, Massachusetts Bay Colony, Native Peoples, and Africans
MA.3.T5.1	Compare the roles and decisions of leaders of the Puritans and the Pilgrims, such as John Winthrop and William Bradford, with those of Native leaders such as Massasoit and Metacom.
MA.3.T5.2	Explain why many Puritans migrated to Massachusetts, how they spread west, and what this meant for Native Peoples, including lost land, disease, conversion, and conflicts such as King Philip's War.
MA.3.T5.3	Use paintings, artifacts, buildings, or texts to analyze daily life, housing, schooling, and work of Puritans in Massachusetts Bay, including servants and enslaved people.
MA.3.T5.4	Explain that slavery was legal in all the European colonies of the 1600s and 1700s, including Massachusetts, which had both free and enslaved Africans.
MA.3.T5.5	Explain how sea trade and bartering helped build colonial Massachusetts's economy, using historical society and museum materials.
MA.3.T5.5a	Describe the fishing and shipbuilding industries.
MA.3.T5.5b	Describe Atlantic and Caribbean trade, especially the Triangular Trade that sold enslaved Africans and moved goods made by slave labor.
MA.3.T5.5c	Describe how the seaport cities of New Bedford, Newburyport, Gloucester, Salem, and Boston developed.
MA.3.T6	Grade 3: Massachusetts in the 1700s and the American Revolution
MA.3.T6.1	Use a historical map to explain how far the Province of Massachusetts extended, and explain why its towns and cities grew in the 1700s.
MA.3.T6.2	Analyze how events, places, and people in Massachusetts in the early 1770s led to the start of the American Revolution, using maps, paintings, and texts.
MA.3.T6.3	Analyze how colonists' sense of injustice led them to declare independence, and what the Declaration of Independence shows about its writers' beliefs.
MA.3.T6.4	Explain that the new nation wrote the Constitution as a plan of government, that the Bill of Rights spells out citizens' rights, and that full citizenship was then limited to white male property owners over 21.
MA.3.T6.5	Explain that states have governments too, and that the 1780 Massachusetts Constitution, mainly written by John Adams, is the oldest working constitution in the world and grants basic rights.
MA.4.T1	Grade 4: North America geography and map skills
MA.4.T1.1	Use a physical map of North America with directions, scale, key, and title to locate major rivers, lakes, oceans, mountains, deserts, and other physical features.
MA.4.T1.2	Locate Canada and its provinces, Mexico and its states, the Caribbean nations, and the United States on a political map, and explain terms like continent, country, state, province, and county.
MA.4.T1.3	Research Canada or Mexico using maps, atlases, digital sources, and articles, then present on topics such as its people, languages, government, cities, environment, and industries.
MA.4.T2	Grade 4: ancient civilizations of North America
MA.4.T2.1	Evaluate competing theories about how people first reached North America, such as a land bridge or a sea route, and the evidence dating early populations to about 15,000 years ago.
MA.4.T2.2	Use maps of Native culture regions and photographs to identify archaeological evidence of major civilizations, such as stone tools, pottery, mounds, and cliff dwellings.
MA.4.T2.3	Explain how archaeologists research the past, through digs, artifacts, climate data, and teamwork, to build theories about migration, settlement, and culture in prehistory.
MA.4.T2.4	Give examples of preserved Native archaeological sites, such as Mesa Verde and Cahokia Mounds, and explain why they matter to the full story of American life.
MA.4.T3	Grade 4: early European exploration and conquest
MA.4.T3.1	Explain how historians studying European voyages to the Americas use archaeology, maps, illustrations, and texts of the time, which are called primary sources.
MA.4.T3.2	Explain who the Vikings were and describe evidence of their early encounters with Native Peoples on the North American Atlantic coast.
MA.4.T3.3	Trace European explorations of North America and the Caribbean in the 1400s and 1500s, evaluate why nations sent them (empire, Asian trade routes, colonies, resources), and make a timeline of landings and conquests.
MA.4.T4	Grade 4: how the United States grew and its regions today
MA.4.T4.1	Describe how canals, roads, and railways built in the 1800s helped the United States expand westward.
MA.4.T4.2	Give examples of how the United States gained states and territories from 1791 to 1898, such as the Louisiana Purchase, the Mexican War, the Oregon Territory, Alaska, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico.
MA.4.T4.3	Compare reasons men and women in the East moved west in the 1800s, and describe pioneer life on the frontier, including wagon trains and western settlements.
MA.4.T4.4	Explain that many groups came to the United States by choice from other places while others, such as people from Africa, were brought against their will.
MA.4.T4.5	Explain that by the mid 1800s Americans were deeply divided over slavery and its spread into the West, which led to the Civil War from 1861 to 1865.
MA.4.T4a	Grade 4 regions: the Northeast
MA.4.T4a.1	Locate the Northeast states on a political map of the United States.
MA.4.T4a.2	Use atlases and maps to make a Northeast map showing cities, capitals, physical features, and a title, scale, compass, and key.
MA.4.T4a.3	Explain the benefits of statehood over being a British colony in the 1700s, and build a class timeline of when each Northeast state joined the United States.
MA.4.T4a.4	Ask questions, research, and analyze how people adapted to the Northeast environment and how its features and resources shaped settlement, urban growth, industry, and trade.
MA.4.T4a.5	Describe the region's diverse cultures, including contributions of Native Peoples, Africans, early European settlers, later immigrant groups, and more recent arrivals from around the world.

MA.5.T1	Grade 5: early colonization and growth of the colonies
MA.5.T1.1	Explain early relations between English settlers and Native Peoples, including disease, differing views of land ownership, and conflicts such as the Pequot War and King Philip's War.
MA.5.T1.2	Compare why different colonies were founded and research one colony's founder, such as William Penn, Roger Williams, or John Winthrop.
MA.5.T1.3	Analyze why English colonists most shaped the language and political ideas of the future United States, even though other European nations explored North America.
MA.5.T1.4	Locate the 13 colonies, describe how regional climate affected crops in the Northern, mid-Atlantic, and Southern colonies, and describe varied sources of labor.
MA.5.T1.5	Describe the origins of slavery, its legality in all colonies through the 1700s, and how common slave ownership was, including among many early national leaders.
MA.5.T1.6	Describe the Triangular Trade and the harsh conditions of the Middle Passage for enslaved Africans.
MA.5.T1.7	Compare living and working conditions of enslaved and free Africans in the 1700s colonies, and explain how some enslaved people sought freedom.
MA.5.T1.7a	Describe how enslaved people were treated as property, denied education, abused, and punished for escape, and how these conditions led to rebellions.
MA.5.T1.7b	Explain that many enslaved Africans became skilled artisans and could be hired out to work.
MA.5.T1.7c	Explain how some Africans were freed after service or by grant, and how some in the North sued for freedom, such as Elizabeth Freeman, Quock Walker, and Prince Hall.
MA.5.T1.7d	Explain that Northern states adopted gradual emancipation, with Massachusetts outlawing slavery in 1783, and that free African Americans could have families, property, and jobs.
MA.5.T2	Grade 5: causes of revolution, the Revolutionary War, and forming a government
MA.5.T2.1	Explain the reasons for the French and Indian War, how its costs changed British imperial policy, and the key British policies and colonial responses.
MA.5.T2.1a	Identify the main British policies: the Proclamation of 1763, the Sugar, Stamp, Townsend, and Tea Acts, and the Intolerable Acts.
MA.5.T2.1b	Explain the slogan "no taxation without representation."
MA.5.T2.1c	Describe the roles of the Stamp Act Congress, the Sons of Liberty, the Boston Tea Party, the Suffolk Resolves, and the early battles at Lexington, Concord, and Bunker Hill, ending with the British leaving Boston.
MA.5.T2.1d	Describe women's roles in boycotts, writing for liberty, running farms and businesses, raising funds, and supporting the Continental Army.
MA.5.T2.2	Use a historic map of 1770s Boston to locate key sites and analyze the roles of Massachusetts figures such as Samuel Adams, Crispus Attucks, Paul Revere, and Phillis Wheatley.
MA.5.T2.3	Explain how colonial governments, such as legislatures, town meetings, and charters of rights, developed and helped lead to the Revolution.
MA.5.T2.4	Read the Declaration of Independence and explain its main argument, its reasons for independence, and its key ideas on equality, natural and legal rights, and the rule of law.
MA.5.T2.5	Describe the impact of later Revolutionary War events, locate them on a map, and explain why America won and Britain lost.
MA.5.T2.5a	Describe the Battles of Trenton and Saratoga.
MA.5.T2.5b	Describe the Continental Army's winter at Valley Forge.
MA.5.T2.5c	Describe the Battle of Yorktown.
MA.5.T2.6	Explain that many Americans stayed loyal to Britain or neutral, and that Native Peoples and free and enslaved Africans fought on both sides.
MA.5.T2.7	Compare the impact of leaders such as John Adams, Franklin, King George III, Jefferson, Hamilton, Madison, Washington, and Lafayette during the Revolution and early Republic.
MA.5.T2.8	Explain why the Articles of Confederation were adopted in 1781, their weaknesses as a plan of government, and why they failed.
MA.5.T2.9	Analyze the causes of Shays' Rebellion and explain why it was a key event leading to the Constitutional Convention.
MA.5.T3	Grade 5: principles of United States government
MA.5.T3.1	Read the Preamble and parts of the Constitution and explain how they reflect individual rights, equality, rule of law, general welfare, limited government, and representative democracy.
MA.5.T3.2	Explain how the framers divided and shared power among three branches, what each branch does, and how checks and balances work.
MA.5.T3.3	Describe the responsibilities of federal, state, and local government, such as protecting rights, law enforcement, and schools.
MA.5.T3.4	Analyze the major Constitutional Convention debates, including power, state size, the Senate, and slavery, and explain how the 3/5 Compromise kept the states united but reinforced slavery.
MA.5.T3.5	Explain that the new Constitution did not extend voting and property rights to women.
MA.5.T3.6	Read the Bill of Rights, explain the freedoms it guarantees, and research and argue with evidence why one of the first ten Amendments was included in 1791.
MA.5.T4	Grade 5: the growth of the Republic
MA.5.T4.1	Identify the first three Presidents (Washington, Adams, Jefferson), summarize key developments of their terms such as political parties and the Louisiana Purchase, and evaluate their leadership.
MA.5.T4.2	Evaluate the importance of the Louisiana Purchase and trace the Lewis and Clark expedition with Sacagawea from 1803 to 1806.
MA.5.T4.3	Describe the causes of the War of 1812 and how wartime events built American nationalism.
MA.5.T4.3a	Explain British trade restrictions and impressment as causes of the war.
MA.5.T4.3b	Describe major events of the war, including the USS Constitution, the burning of the Capitol and White House, and the Battle of New Orleans.
MA.5.T4.4	Locate New England places important to textiles, machinery, whaling, shipping, and the China trade in the 1700s and 1800s, and give short- and long-term benefits and costs of these industries.
MA.5.T4.5	Explain 1800s conflicts between Native Peoples and governments over land and self-government.
MA.5.T4.5a	Describe Tecumseh's 1810 call for Native Peoples to unite against loss of their land.
MA.5.T4.5b	Describe Andrew Jackson and the Indian Removal Act of 1830, which forced Southeastern Native communities west of the Mississippi.
MA.5.T4.5c	Describe the Mashpee Revolt of 1833, a Massachusetts dispute over self-government.
MA.5.T4.5d	Explain the significance of the Trail of Tears in 1838 for the Cherokee and other Southeastern Native communities.
MA.5.T5	Grade 5: the struggle for civil rights for all
MA.5.T5.1	Trace how Northern states abolished slavery while it expanded westward, and explain the 1808 ban on importing slaves and the continuing internal slave trade until the mid 1800s.
MA.5.T5.2	Identify the major causes of the Civil War, such as slavery, western competition, and the rise of the Republican Party, and its key outcomes, such as the end of slavery and Reconstruction.
MA.5.T5.3	Explain the ideas and roles of people who fought slavery and sought rights for African Americans, such as Harriet Tubman, Frederick Douglass, and Sojourner Truth.
MA.5.T5.4	Identify the major military leaders and battles of the Civil War, such as Grant, Lee, Gettysburg, and Vicksburg.
MA.5.T5.5	Describe Abraham Lincoln's role in building the Republican Party and his Civil War actions, including the Emancipation Proclamation and the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments.

MA.ECON.T6.2	Identify what makes up the U.S. money supply.
MA.ECON.T6.3	Explain the role of banks and other financial institutions in the U.S. economy.
MA.ECON.T6.4	Describe how the Federal Reserve System is organized and what it does.
MA.ECON.T6.5	Compare the credit, savings, and investment services that financial institutions offer consumers.
MA.ECON.T6.6	Research and track financial investments such as stocks, bonds, and mutual funds.
MA.ECON.T7	Trade
MA.ECON.T7.1	Explain the benefits of trade among individuals, regions, and countries.
MA.ECON.T7.2	Distinguish absolute from comparative advantage and explain why most trade rests on comparative advantage.
MA.ECON.T7.3	Define trade barriers such as quotas and tariffs.
MA.ECON.T7.4	Explain why countries sometimes set up trade barriers.
MA.ECON.T7.5	Explain the difference between the balance of trade and the balance of payments.
MA.ECON.T7.6	Compare trends in labor productivity in the United States and other developed countries.
MA.ECON.T7.7	Explain how exchange rate changes affect the purchasing power of people in the U.S. and abroad.
MA.ECON.T7.8	Evaluate the arguments for and against free trade.
MA.PFL.T1	Earning and spending income
MA.PFL.T1.1	Explain how people choose jobs they qualify for based on factors such as satisfaction, pay, benefits, chances to grow, and location.
MA.PFL.T1.2	Explain how the labor market sets wages and how recessions or industry shifts can change a worker's income or cause unemployment.
MA.PFL.T1.3	Analyze how federal income tax rates have affected people at different income levels from 1950 to today.
MA.PFL.T1.4	Describe how advertising and social media affect buying decisions, using data on the effects of media on purchases.
MA.PFL.T1.5	Give examples of ways to pay, such as cash, cards, checks, or rent-to-own, and analyze the costs and benefits of each.
MA.PFL.T1.6	Explain the roles of state and federal governments in protecting consumers.
MA.PFL.T2	Saving money
MA.PFL.T2.1	Recognize that banks are businesses that lend depositors' money to borrowers.
MA.PFL.T2.2	Explain how principal, interest, and compound interest relate to one another.
MA.PFL.T2.3	Explain the difference between the real (inflation-adjusted) and nominal interest rate on savings.
MA.PFL.T2.4	Research government policies such as retirement accounts and education savings plans and analyze how well they encourage saving.
MA.PFL.T2.5	Analyze how well agencies such as the Federal Reserve, the CFPB, the FDIC, and state banking departments protect the banking system and consumers.
MA.PFL.T2.6	Create a savings or investment plan for a future goal such as college or retirement.
MA.PFL.T3	Using credit and making investments
MA.PFL.T3.1	Recognize that a credit card purchase is a loan, that fees may apply, and that card interest rates tend to be higher than on other loans.
MA.PFL.T3.2	Explain why some banks offer low introductory credit rates that rise after a late or missed payment.
MA.PFL.T3.3	Explain what credit bureaus, credit ratings, and credit reports are, what can lead to credit denial, and how reports and scores are used.
MA.PFL.T3.4	Research the long-term consequences of not repaying loans, such as damaged credit, repossession, wage garnishment, and bankruptcy.
MA.PFL.T3.5	Explain a consumer's right to full disclosure of loan terms and to a free copy of their own credit report.
MA.PFL.T3.6	Create a credit plan for buying a major item such as a car or home, comparing interest rates.
MA.PFL.T3.7	Explain what a financial asset is and why asset values rise and fall over time.
MA.PFL.T3.8	Explain how buyers and sellers in financial markets set asset prices and so affect rates of return.
MA.PFL.T3.9	Analyze how diversifying an investment portfolio across different assets lowers risk for an individual investor.
MA.PFL.T3.10	Analyze the role of the Securities and Exchange Commission in regulating financial markets.
MA.PFL.T4	Protecting and insuring assets
MA.PFL.T4.1	Explain the purposes of main types of insurance, then compare cost and coverage from several companies to find the best fit for a given consumer.
MA.PFL.T4.2	Explain the problems of identity theft and ways to protect personal information in online transactions, email scams, and telemarketing.
MA.NML.T1	Freedom of the press and media literacy
MA.NML.T1.1	Evaluate why a free flow of information matters in a democratic society.
MA.NML.T1.2	Explain why press freedom is protected in the First Amendment and the Massachusetts Constitution, and that it means publishing views without government control or censorship.
MA.NML.T1.3	Give examples of how a free press offers competing information and views about government, policies, and politics.
MA.NML.T1.4	Give examples from history or today of government control of information or censorship.
MA.NML.T1.5	Tell news from opinion and explain the different jobs of news articles, editorials, editorial cartoons, and op-ed pieces.
MA.NML.T2	History of journalism
MA.NML.T2.1	Explain how the spread of information has changed over time and how key technologies, from the printing press to the Internet, drove those changes.
MA.NML.T2.2	Analyze one aspect of American journalism through a case study, such as newspapers in the Revolution and ratification debates or the African American press.
MA.NML.T3	Challenges of media literacy today
MA.NML.T3.1	Evaluate the benefits and challenges that digital news and social media bring to a democracy, such as speed, volume, and number of outlets.
MA.NML.T3.2	Explain the practices investigative journalists use to develop and check the facts in their stories.
MA.NML.T3.3	Explain how new technologies widen the influence of media and of corporate or public interest groups.
MA.NML.T3.4	Explain how changes in the news industry, such as ownership consolidation and the shift to digital, affect news consumers.
MA.NML.T3.5	Explain how becoming a discerning news consumer can change individual lives and strengthen democratic government.
MA.NML.T4	Analyzing the news and other media
MA.NML.T4.1	Explain why it matters to check who is behind a website, including sponsors and authors, as well as bias, evidence, and other perspectives.
MA.NML.T4.2	Explain methods for evaluating information and opinion in print and online media, including checking credibility, editorial messages, claims, and evidence.
MA.NML.T4.3	Analyze how assertion differs from verification and how evidence differs from inference.
MA.NML.T4.4	Evaluate and take apart news reports, social media posts, and opinion pieces on a public policy issue, judging the evidence, sources, and other perspectives.
MA.NML.T5	Gathering and reporting information with digital media
MA.NML.T5.1	Gather, organize, and synthesize information with digital tools, using advanced searches or original data collection such as interviews and surveys.
MA.NML.T5.2	Write a factual report and an editorial on a public event or policy, and explain how the two kinds of writing differ.
MA.NML.T5.3	Use digital tools such as photography, drawing, and video software to communicate visually in reporting or opinion pieces.
MA.NML.T5.4	Use digital tools to design and produce a significant digital product, such as a multipage website, portfolio, or podcast.
MA.NML.T5.5	Collaborate online with outside experts and others on a substantial project, such as a policy debate or community service project.
