# words: after each id, a tab and one line in Curio Class's own words (ours, not NCDA's), a bare verb phrase addressed to the student. The line keeps the indicator's stage (K: know or name, A: do or show, R: judge or revise) and its subject. A personal example may always be answered for an invented worker (STANDARD.md section 6).
# use: FOR PLANNING ONLY. Never quoted as NCDA's words on a public page. Public pages say "the national career development guidelines" with the id. Plan: docs/courses/career-education/STANDARD.md.
CD1.K1	Explain why planning a career is never finished in one go.
CD1.A1	Point to steps in your own plan that move you toward a goal.
CD1.R1	Judge which of your planning steps actually moved you forward.
CD1.K2	List the parts and steps of a career plan.
CD1.A2	Write a first career plan of your own.
CD1.R2	Review your plan and revise the lines that no longer fit.
CD1.K3	Name near and far goals for school, work and the way you want to live.
CD1.A3	Show steps you have taken toward a near goal and a far goal.
CD1.R3	Revisit your goals and change the ones that have shifted.
CD1.K4	Name habits that help a person steer a career, such as bouncing back and adapting.
CD1.A4	Use those steering habits in a task and show where you used them.
CD1.R4	Rate your own career-steering habits and pick one to grow.
CD1.K5	Explain how changes in you or in the job market can change a plan.
CD1.A5	Show a change in your plan and say what caused it.
CD1.R5	Judge how well your plan keeps up with changes in you and in work.
CD2.K1	Describe how you tend to make choices, such as boldly or carefully.
CD2.A1	Recall a past choice that shows how you decide.
CD2.R1	Weigh what works and what does not in the way you decide.
CD2.K2	Name the steps of one way to make a decision.
CD2.A2	Make a choice by following a decision model step by step.
CD2.R2	Compare decision models and say which suits you.
CD2.K3	Explain how facts about yourself, jobs and schooling make a choice better.
CD2.A3	Use facts about yourself, jobs and schooling to make a choice.
CD2.R3	Judge whether you used enough good facts in a choice.
CD2.K4	List the options and likely results for one decision.
CD2.A4	Show how looking at several options changed a choice you made.
CD2.R4	Judge how widely you search for options before deciding.
CD2.K5	Explain how what you value, believe and put first shapes your choices.
CD2.A5	Point out where your values show in a choice you made.
CD2.R5	Weigh how much your values steered a choice, and whether you want that.
CD2.K6	Explain how school, work and home life can shape a decision.
CD2.A6	Show how school or work experience shaped a choice, yours or an invented worker's.
CD2.R6	Judge how much your experiences have shaped your choices.
CD2.K7	Explain how a stereotype can shrink the choices a person sees.
CD2.A7	Find a time a stereotype narrowed a choice, yours or a worker's.
CD2.R7	Plan ways to catch and set aside stereotypes when choosing.
CD2.K8	Explain that luck and timing can change how a choice turns out.
CD2.A8	Tell of a time when luck played a part in a choice.
CD2.R8	Judge how much luck shaped earlier choices and what you can still control.
CD2.K9	Explain why most career choices involve giving something up.
CD2.A9	Name trade-offs you may have to accept in a career choice.
CD2.R9	Judge how well you handle trade-offs.
CD3.K1	Explain why good career information matters to a plan.
CD3.A1	Show where career information changed your plan.
CD3.R1	Check your plan against current, fair information and update it.
CD3.K2	Name the kinds of career information (jobs, training, hiring, the economy) and where to find them.
CD3.A2	Use several kinds of career information in one job study or plan.
CD3.R2	Judge how well your plan combines job, training, hiring and economic facts.
CD3.K3	Explain why career sources differ in accuracy, fairness and age.
CD3.A3	Spot a career source that is old, one-sided, missing facts or wrong.
CD3.R3	Rate the sources you plan to use for accuracy, fairness, date and completeness.
CD3.K4	Name several ways jobs are grouped, such as the career clusters.
CD3.A4	Use a way of grouping jobs to find options for your plan.
CD3.R4	Judge which way of grouping jobs helps you most.
CD3.K5	List jobs you could consider whatever group you belong to.
CD3.A5	Look seriously at a job few people like you hold.
CD3.R5	Judge how open you are to jobs outside the usual pattern for your group.
CD3.K6	List the gains and the hurdles of working in a job uncommon for your group.
CD3.A6	Decide for yourself whether a job uncommon for your group belongs in your plan.
CD4.K1	Describe school-based, job-specific and general work skills.
CD4.A1	Use your skills in a task that models getting, keeping and growing in a job.
CD4.R1	Rate your work skills and plan how to build the weak ones.
CD4.K2	Name the steps of a job search: resume, cover letter, application, interview and leads.
CD4.A2	Write your own resume and a letter to an employer, fill in an application and do a mock interview.
CD4.R2	Judge your own resume, application and interview against criteria.
CD4.K3	Explain why skills such as problem solving and honesty matter at school and work.
CD4.A3	Show general work skills, such as dependability and problem solving, in a task.
CD4.R3	Rate your general work skills with evidence from your tasks.
CD4.K4	Explain how skills carry from one job to another.
CD4.A4	Show which of your skills would carry over to a different job.
CD4.R4	Judge how your carry-over skills widen your options.
CD4.K5	Explain how being willing or able to move affects job chances.
CD4.A5	Decide where you would be willing to live and work, and why.
CD4.R5	Weigh how your choice about moving fits your career goals.
CD4.K6	List the upsides and the risks of working for yourself.
CD4.A6	Decide whether working for yourself belongs in your plan.
CD4.R6	Weigh how that decision about working for yourself fits your goals.
CD4.K7	Name ways to present yourself well to an employer.
CD4.A7	Present yourself to an employer through a portfolio, resume or interview.
CD5.K1	Name needs in society that could shape your plan.
CD5.A1	Show how your plan could adjust as society's needs change.
CD5.K2	Name economic conditions that could shape your plan.
CD5.A2	Show how your plan could adjust as the economy changes.
CD5.K3	Name hiring trends, read from dated outlook figures, that could shape your plan.
CD5.A3	Show how your plan could adjust as hiring trends change.
ALL1.K1	Explain how doing well in school connects to personal and career goals.
ALL1.A1	Set out in your plan the school results your goals need, and your steps to reach them.
ALL1.R1	Check your progress toward the school results your goals need.
ALL1.K2	List ways to improve your school work.
ALL1.A2	Try one way of improving your school work and show it.
ALL1.R2	Review how you improve your school work and plan the next step.
ALL1.K3	Describe study habits that help learning.
ALL1.A3	Practise a study habit and show what it changed.
ALL1.R3	Rate your study habits and plan to strengthen one.
ALL1.K4	Describe the conditions and strategies that help you learn, with no fixed learning-style label.
ALL1.A4	Use what helps you learn to improve a piece of school work.
ALL1.R4	Judge which of your learning strategies work and change the ones that do not.
ALL1.K5	Explain why a plan helps you do better in school.
ALL1.A5	Write a short plan for doing better in school.
ALL1.R5	Check what your school plan achieved and revise it.
ALL1.K6	Explain how attitude and habits affect school results.
ALL1.A6	Show attitudes and habits that help your school work.
ALL1.R6	Judge which of your attitudes and habits help or hold you back.
ALL1.K7	Explain how school success opens more job options.
ALL1.A7	Show how your school work widens your job options.
ALL1.R7	Judge how well your school skills will carry into work.
ALL1.K8	Explain how finding and using information helps you learn.
ALL1.A8	Show how finding and using information helped your school work.
ALL1.R8	Rate how well you find and use information and plan to improve.
ALL2.K1	Explain why a changing economy means learning new skills all through life.
ALL2.A1	Show how something you are learning now prepares you for changing work.
ALL2.R1	Judge whether your skills are ready for a changing, mixed workplace.
ALL2.K2	Explain how seeing yourself as a learner shapes who you are.
ALL2.A2	Give an example of how learning is part of who you are.
ALL2.R2	Trace how particular learning moments changed how you see yourself.
ALL2.K3	Explain why taking charge of your own learning matters.
ALL2.A3	Carry out a piece of learning on your own.
ALL2.R3	Judge how well you learn on your own.
ALL2.K4	Name what it takes to move to the next stage of school, such as high school.
ALL2.A4	Prepare for the move to high school with the steps it needs.
ALL2.R4	Judge how ready you are for the step after high school.
ALL2.K5	Name routes for learning after school: colleges, trade schools, apprenticeships, the military, online study and training on the job.
ALL2.A5	Show the steps you are taking toward one route for learning after school.
ALL2.R5	Weigh how a route for learning after school fits your goals.
ALL2.K6	Name particular programs, such as high school pathways, college majors and apprenticeships.
ALL2.A6	Choose a program of study and show how it fits your plan.
ALL2.R6	Judge how a program of study prepares you for a changing economy.
ALL2.K7	Describe learning outside a classroom that builds skills.
ALL2.A7	Show learning you do outside class.
ALL2.R7	Judge how well your learning in and out of class fit together.
