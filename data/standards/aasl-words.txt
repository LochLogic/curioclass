# words: added 2026-11-21. After each id, a tab and one skill phrase in Curio Class's own words (an action and an object, 8 to 15 words; ours, not AASL's; never quoted as AASL's on a page). Decided by Cory 2026-11-21 (Desk q-fix-library-1).
AASL.I.A.1	Turn a topic you care about or are studying into questions to research.
AASL.I.A.2	Say what you already know about a topic before you start looking.
AASL.I.B.1	Look for evidence that answers your research question, not just related facts.
AASL.I.B.2	Plan the steps and sources that will fill what you still do not know.
AASL.I.B.3	Make a product that shows what your research taught you.
AASL.I.C.1	Read, watch or listen to classmates' work and respond to what it says.
AASL.I.C.2	Give a classmate specific, kind advice that would make their work better.
AASL.I.C.3	Use the advice you get to revise your own research product.
AASL.I.C.4	Share finished research with real people who have a reason to care.
AASL.I.D.1	Keep asking new questions after one answer is found.
AASL.I.D.2	Stay with one research question over weeks, through several rounds of searching.
AASL.I.D.3	Put what research taught you to use on a real problem or decision.
AASL.I.D.4	Look back on how a search went to choose what to do next.
AASL.II.A.1	Notice and name what different classmates add to a shared piece of work.
AASL.II.A.2	Spot the point of view a source takes and weigh it before agreeing.
AASL.II.A.3	Explain how your own background shapes the way you read a topic.
AASL.II.B.1	Work alongside people who see a question differently than you do.
AASL.II.B.2	Compare how several sources with different views treat the same question.
AASL.II.B.3	Show more than one side fairly in what you make and present.
AASL.II.C.1	Take part in a discussion backed by what your sources actually say.
AASL.II.C.2	Add to a talk where people hold different views, and listen as well.
AASL.II.D.1	Choose to learn with and from people whose lives differ from yours.
AASL.II.D.2	Ask others how they see a topic and show you want to know.
AASL.II.D.3	Think about where you stand among the many communities a topic touches.
AASL.III.A.1	Look for chances to widen and deepen a question by researching with others.
AASL.III.A.2	Build a new idea with a research group that no member had alone.
AASL.III.A.3	Let what the group found shape how you choose to solve a problem.
AASL.III.B.1	Pick a fitting way to talk and share files with a research team.
AASL.III.B.2	Link up with other learners to build on what each already knows.
AASL.III.C.1	Ask others for comments on your work and answer them thoughtfully.
AASL.III.C.2	Bring other people's views into your own research on purpose.
AASL.III.D.1	Speak up with ideas and findings when a research group talks.
AASL.III.D.2	Treat helping your group learn as part of your own job.
AASL.IV.A.1	Decide when a question needs research rather than a guess.
AASL.IV.A.2	List the kinds of sources likely to hold the answer you need.
AASL.IV.A.3	Choose which sources to use, and say why each one earns its place.
AASL.IV.B.1	Search more than one kind of source, not just the first site.
AASL.IV.B.2	Gather information from sources that see the question in different ways.
AASL.IV.B.3	Question each source's maker and purpose, and check what it says, before trusting it.
AASL.IV.B.4	Sort notes by topic, importance or another plan you can explain.
AASL.IV.C.1	Use and judge sites that many people write and edit together.
AASL.IV.C.2	Add to a shared, group-written site with credit for others' work.
AASL.IV.C.3	Compare with classmates what several group-written sites say on one question.
AASL.IV.D.1	Keep rechecking a collection of sources for quality and usefulness.
AASL.IV.D.2	Map how ideas from different sources connect into one picture.
AASL.IV.D.3	Show others how you chose and sorted your sources so they can check.
AASL.V.A.1	Read many kinds of texts and write for many different purposes.
AASL.V.A.2	Catch and test your own assumptions and mistaken ideas while researching.
AASL.V.A.3	Research a question of your own just to learn more about it.
AASL.V.B.1	Solve a problem by designing, building, testing and improving a solution.
AASL.V.B.2	Keep going on a self-chosen making project through trial and error.
AASL.V.C.1	Say what you wonder about a topic you are studying or enjoy.
AASL.V.C.2	Work out with others a new way to find something out.
AASL.V.C.3	Come up with fresh answers to a problem together with a group.
AASL.V.D.1	Try again in a new way when a task gets hard.
AASL.V.D.2	Name a skill you can grow with practice and help.
AASL.V.D.3	Take criticism calmly and use it to get better.
AASL.VI.A.1	Use information, devices and media responsibly when you learn.
AASL.VI.A.2	Explain what fair and honest use of information and media means.
AASL.VI.A.3	Judge whether information is accurate, fair in its context and fit for the task.
AASL.VI.B.1	Use and copy others' work only in honest, permitted ways.
AASL.VI.B.2	Name who made each work you use and respect their rights.
AASL.VI.B.3	Label your own work so others can credit it correctly.
AASL.VI.C.1	Share and remix others' work only as its license allows.
AASL.VI.C.2	Publish what you found in a form that suits its audience.
AASL.VI.D.1	Set up your own ways of finding and using information that work.
AASL.VI.D.2	Look back on whether your research was made honestly and fairly.
AASL.VI.D.3	Help others use information safely, honestly and within the law.
