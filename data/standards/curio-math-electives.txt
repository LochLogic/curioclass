# level: one outcome a row owns, CUR-PC.<n> (Precalculus), CUR-ST.<n> (Statistics), CUR-CA.<n> (Calculus); <n> is the teaching order within the course
# note: these outcomes cover what the three courses teach beyond the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics; the Common Core (+) codes the electives teach are owned by the same rows and are not repeated here. Statistics and Calculus are written at first-year college level, Calculus covering both semesters.
# note: every statement is in Curio Class's own words and grouping. No published course framework, exam description or textbook wording is copied.
CUR-PC.1	Analyse a function from any family met so far (polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, piecewise): state its domain and range, where it rises and falls, its turning points, symmetry and end behaviour, and say which family fits a given graph, table or description and why.
CUR-PC.2	Find every zero of a polynomial of degree 3 or 4 with integer coefficients, real and complex, using the factor theorem, rational candidates and conjugate pairs, and write the polynomial as a product of linear factors.
CUR-PC.3	Read a rational function fully from its factored form (zeros, holes, vertical and horizontal or slant asymptotes, sign on each interval), and solve polynomial and rational inequalities with a sign chart, checking the answer on a graph.
CUR-PC.4	Use the product, quotient and power properties of logarithms and the change of base to rewrite expressions, and solve exponential and logarithmic equations exactly, rejecting inputs outside the domain.
CUR-PC.5	Model data and situations with exponential, logarithmic and logistic functions, straighten curved data by taking logarithms of one or both variables, and judge which model fits and how far it can be trusted.
CUR-PC.6	Find the values of all six trigonometric functions from the unit circle, and graph them, including secant, cosecant and cotangent as reciprocals, with changes of amplitude, period, phase shift and midline, and write a rule from a graph.
CUR-PC.7	Prove trigonometric identities by a chain of equal expressions (one side rewritten into the other, or each side rewritten into the same expression), and use the double-angle, power-reducing and half-angle formulas to find exact values and to simplify.
CUR-PC.8	Solve trigonometric equations exactly where special angles allow and with technology otherwise, giving every solution in a stated interval and the general solution with its period.
CUR-PC.9	Find the dot product of two vectors, use it to find the angle between them, decide when they are perpendicular and project one vector onto another, with work and force examples.
CUR-PC.10	Locate points with polar coordinates, convert points and equations between polar and rectangular form, and explain why one point has many polar names.
CUR-PC.11	Sketch and recognise graphs of polar equations (circles, lines, cardioids and other limaçons, roses, spirals) by building a table of values and reasoning about symmetry and the largest and smallest radius.
CUR-PC.12	Describe a curve or a motion with parametric equations, graph it with its direction of travel, eliminate the parameter where possible, and write parametric equations for a line, a circle and a projectile.
CUR-PC.13	Write and graph parabolas, ellipses and hyperbolas in standard form from their features, complete the square to put a general second-degree equation in standard form, and name the conic it describes.
CUR-PC.14	Write sums in sigma notation, find the sum of an infinite geometric series when the ratio lies between -1 and 1, and explain why an infinite geometric series with any other nonzero ratio has no finite sum.
CUR-PC.15	Find the average rate of change of a function over shorter and shorter intervals, and estimate the instantaneous rate at a point as the slope the secant lines approach, read from tables and graphs and in context.
CUR-PC.16	Describe informally what value a function approaches as the input nears a number or grows without bound, using tables and graphs, including one-sided behaviour, asymptotes and holes, and say whether a function is continuous on an interval.
CUR-ST.1	Summarise categorical data with frequency and relative frequency tables, bar charts and two-way tables, compute marginal and conditional relative frequencies, and describe whether two categorical variables are associated.
CUR-ST.2	Display the distribution of a quantitative variable (dot plots, histograms, stemplots, box plots) and describe it by shape, centre, spread and unusual values (outliers by a stated rule), choose resistant or non-resistant summaries to fit the shape, and compare two or more distributions in context.
CUR-ST.3	Locate a value within a distribution with percentiles and standardised scores, describe how adding a constant or multiplying by one changes centre and spread, and, using density curves as areas, find areas and cut-off values under any normal model with tables or technology, checking first whether a normal model is reasonable.
CUR-ST.4	Fit a least-squares regression line, interpret its slope and intercept in context, use the correlation and the coefficient of determination to describe the strength of a linear relationship, and predict without extrapolating far.
CUR-ST.5	Judge a linear model with a residual plot, find outliers and influential points and describe their effect, and straighten a curved relationship by transforming a variable before fitting a line.
CUR-ST.6	Choose and carry out a sampling method (simple random, stratified, cluster, systematic), and name how a sample can be biased (undercoverage, nonresponse, response wording, voluntary response, convenience) and what each does to an estimate.
CUR-ST.7	Design an experiment with comparison, random assignment, replication and, where fitting, blocking, matched pairs, a placebo and blinding, and state the scope of inference: to which population, and whether cause can be claimed.
CUR-ST.8	Find probabilities with simulation, the addition and general multiplication rules, conditional probability and independence, using tree diagrams and two-way tables, and with counting methods where outcomes are equally likely.
CUR-ST.9	Find the mean and standard deviation of a discrete random variable and of a linear change of it, and of the sum or difference of two independent random variables.
CUR-ST.10	Recognise binomial and geometric settings, compute their probabilities, means and standard deviations, and use a normal model to approximate a binomial when the counts are large enough.
CUR-ST.11	Describe the sampling distribution of a sample proportion and of a sample mean (centre, spread and shape), explain why spread shrinks as samples grow and why the shape of the mean's distribution approaches normal, and tell bias from variability in an estimator.
CUR-ST.12	Build and interpret a confidence interval for one population proportion, check its conditions, explain what the confidence level means, and choose a sample size for a wanted margin of error.
CUR-ST.13	Carry out a significance test for one population proportion: state hypotheses, check conditions, find and interpret a p-value, and give a conclusion in context at a stated significance level; say what a p-value does not tell (the probability the null hypothesis is true, or the size or importance of an effect), and tell statistical from practical significance.
CUR-ST.14	Describe the two kinds of error a test can make and their consequences in context, define the power of a test, and explain how significance level, sample size and the size of the true effect change it.
CUR-ST.15	Build a confidence interval and carry out a significance test for the difference between two population proportions, from two independent samples or two randomly assigned groups, and say what each design lets you conclude.
CUR-ST.16	Use t distributions to build a confidence interval and carry out a significance test for one population mean and interpret them in context, checking conditions and the effect of skew and outliers in small samples.
CUR-ST.17	Recognise paired data, and analyse the differences with one-sample t procedures, explaining why pairing can make a comparison more precise.
CUR-ST.18	Build a confidence interval and carry out a significance test for the difference between two population means from independent samples or a randomised two-group experiment, interpret them in context, and explain how a two-sided test at a level and an interval at the matching confidence agree.
CUR-ST.19	Carry out chi-square tests for goodness of fit, for homogeneity of several populations and for independence of two categorical variables, choosing the right one from how the data were gathered.
CUR-ST.20	Build a confidence interval and carry out a significance test for the slope of a population regression line, interpret them in context, checking the conditions with residuals, and read the output of statistical software.
CUR-ST.21	Plan and carry out a whole statistical investigation: pose a question, design the data collection, gather and analyse the data, choose and carry out an inference procedure, and report the findings and their limits in writing.
CUR-CA.1	Explain a limit as the value a function's outputs approach as the input approaches a number, from either side, write it in limit notation, and estimate limits from graphs and tables, telling when a limit does not exist.
CUR-CA.2	Find limits exactly with the limit laws, algebraic rewriting (factoring, rationalising, common denominators) and a squeeze argument, and find limits at infinity and infinite limits to describe asymptotes.
CUR-CA.3	Define continuity at a point and on an interval with limits, classify discontinuities (removable, jump, infinite), choose a constant to make a piecewise function continuous, and use the intermediate value theorem to show a value is reached.
CUR-CA.4	Define the derivative at a point as the limit of difference quotients and as the slope of the tangent line, write the tangent line, find where a function is not differentiable, explain why a differentiable function is continuous but not the reverse, and sketch the graph of a derivative from the graph of a function.
CUR-CA.5	Differentiate with the power, constant multiple, sum, product and quotient rules, and differentiate sine, cosine, the other trigonometric functions, exponential and logarithmic functions.
CUR-CA.6	Differentiate composite functions with the chain rule, and find derivatives of inverse functions, inverse trigonometric functions included, from the derivative of the original function.
CUR-CA.7	Differentiate relations given implicitly, find slopes and tangent lines on curves such as circles, and find second and higher derivatives.
CUR-CA.8	Interpret a derivative as a rate of change with units in context, and use derivatives to describe straight-line motion: position, velocity, speed and acceleration, and when an object speeds up or slows down.
CUR-CA.9	Solve related rates problems: name the quantities that change, write an equation linking them, differentiate it with respect to time and interpret the result.
CUR-CA.10	Approximate a function near a point with its tangent line and judge whether the estimate is over or under, and use derivatives (the rule of L'Hospital) to find limits of the forms 0/0 and infinity over infinity, and of products and differences rewritten into them.
CUR-CA.11	State and apply the mean value theorem and the extreme value theorem, find critical points, and use the first derivative to find intervals of increase and decrease and relative and absolute extrema.
CUR-CA.12	Use the second derivative to find concavity and points of inflection and to classify critical points, and connect the graphs of a function, its first derivative and its second derivative in both directions.
CUR-CA.13	Solve optimisation problems in context: build the function to be made largest or smallest, find its domain from the situation, and justify that the answer is the absolute extreme.
CUR-CA.14	Approximate accumulated change and area with left, right, midpoint and trapezoidal sums from formulas and tables, write the definite integral as the limit of such sums, and use the properties of definite integrals.
CUR-CA.15	Explain and use both parts of the fundamental theorem of calculus: a function defined as an accumulation of a rate has that rate as its derivative, and a definite integral can be evaluated with any antiderivative.
CUR-CA.16	Find indefinite integrals of the basic functions, and integrate by substitution, adjusting the limits of a definite integral.
CUR-CA.17	Integrate with integration by parts, partial fractions with distinct linear factors, polynomial division and completing the square, and integrals of the squares of sine and cosine with the double-angle identity, and choose a method from the form of the integrand.
CUR-CA.18	Evaluate improper integrals over unbounded intervals or with unbounded integrands as limits, and decide whether each converges.
CUR-CA.19	Write differential equations from verbal descriptions of rates, check a proposed solution, sketch and read slope fields, and approximate solutions step by step with Euler's method.
CUR-CA.20	Solve separable differential equations with initial conditions, and model exponential growth and decay and logistic growth, reading the carrying capacity and the time of fastest growth.
CUR-CA.21	Use definite integrals for net change and accumulation in context, for the average value of a function, and for straight-line motion: displacement from velocity and total distance travelled.
CUR-CA.22	Find the area of a region between curves, integrating with respect to x or to y and splitting the region where the curves cross.
CUR-CA.23	Find volumes of solids with known cross sections and of solids of revolution by discs and washers, around either axis or another horizontal or vertical line.
CUR-CA.24	Find the length of a curve given as the graph of a function.
CUR-CA.25	Differentiate and integrate parametric and vector-valued functions: slope and concavity of a parametric curve, velocity, speed and acceleration of motion in the plane, total distance travelled and arc length.
CUR-CA.26	Find slopes of polar curves and the area of regions bounded by polar curves, including the region between two polar curves.
CUR-CA.27	Find limits of sequences, and decide whether an infinite series converges, from its partial sums and with the geometric, nth term, integral, p-series, direct comparison and limit comparison tests.
CUR-CA.28	Use the alternating series test and its error bound and the ratio test, and tell absolute from conditional convergence.
CUR-CA.29	Build Taylor and Maclaurin polynomials for a function at a point, use them to approximate values, and bound the error with the Lagrange error bound.
CUR-CA.30	Find the radius and interval of convergence of a power series, write the Taylor series of the common functions, and make new series by substitution, differentiation, integration and multiplication by a power.
