# ELA grades 6 to 8: edges to other bands

Cross-band assumes and hands_on cells in ela-68.csv are left empty; the stitcher writes them from these lists. The
grade 5 skill named is the standard it rests on; use the grades 3 to 5 band's row id that owns it.

## Models the band needs from grades 3 to 5

Grade 6 rows have these in model_in, so the grades 3 to 5 band must hand them on (have them in a grade 5 row's
model_out): claim-evidence-reasoning, main-idea-details, story-map, narrative-arc, figurative-language,
context-clues, word-parts, text-structure, essay-structure, paragraph-frame, writing-process, sentence-combining,
research-cycle, citation, discussion-protocol. If the earlier band does not use one of them, the grade 6 row that
uses it should move it to model_out only (introducing it in grade 6).

Grade 6 introduces argument-map (e6-trace-argument), rhetorical-situation (e6-author-purpose) and point-of-view
(e6-narrator-pov, a new shared name: who tells, what they know, what the reader comes to know). If an earlier band
already introduces any of these, add it to that row's model_in so the row carries it.

## Needs from earlier grades

- e6-cite-evidence: quoting accurately to explain what a text says and infers (RL.5.1, RI.5.1).
- e6-theme-summary: a theme from how characters respond to challenges, and a summary (RL.5.2).
- e6-plot-episodes: comparing characters, settings and events with specific details (RL.5.3).
- e6-word-meaning-lit: figurative language, similes and metaphors in context (RL.5.4; L.5.5a).
- e6-structure-lit: how chapters, scenes or stanzas fit together into the whole (RL.5.5).
- e6-narrator-pov: how a narrator's or speaker's point of view shapes how events are described (RL.5.6).
- e6-read-vs-watch: how visual and multimedia elements add to a text's meaning (RL.5.7).
- e6-compare-genres: comparing stories in the same genre on similar themes (RL.5.9).
- e6-reading-range: reading at the high end of the grades 4 to 5 band independently (RL.5.10; RI.5.10).
- e6-central-idea-summary: two or more main ideas, key details, a summary (RI.5.2).
- e6-idea-development: relationships between individuals, events or ideas in history and science texts (RI.5.3).
- e6-technical-words: general academic and domain words of a grade 5 topic (RI.5.4; L.5.6).
- e6-section-structure: comparing the overall structures of two texts (RI.5.5).
- e6-author-purpose: several accounts of one event and the points of view they represent (RI.5.6).
- e6-media-integrate: drawing on several print and digital sources to locate an answer (RI.5.7; SL.5.2).
- e6-trace-argument: how an author uses reasons and evidence for particular points (RI.5.8; SL.5.3).
- e6-compare-accounts: integrating information from several texts on one topic (RI.5.9).
- e6-argument-writing: opinion pieces with reasons and information, logically grouped (W.5.1).
- e6-informative-writing: informative pieces with related information grouped, headings, transitions (W.5.2).
- e6-narrative-writing: narratives with dialogue, description, pacing and transitions (W.5.3).
- e6-produce-publish: clear writing for task, purpose and audience; typing two pages in one sitting (W.5.4; W.5.6).
- e6-revise-edit: planning, revising and editing with guidance; combining and expanding sentences (W.5.5; L.5.3a).
- e6-short-research: short research from several sources; summarising notes and listing sources (W.5.7; W.5.8).
- e6-text-response-writing: drawing evidence from texts and writing routinely (W.5.9; W.5.10).
- e6-collab-discussion: discussions prepared from reading, with agreed rules and roles (SL.5.1).
- e6-present-claims: reporting on a topic in a logical order at an understandable pace; adding media (SL.5.4; SL.5.5).
- e6-pronouns: grade 5 grammar and usage (conjunctions, verb tense, avoiding tense shifts) as the base for pronoun work (L.5.1).
- e6-punctuation-spelling: commas after introductory elements and in a series; spelling by grade 5 patterns (L.5.2).
- e6-word-strategies: context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, reference works (L.5.4); synonyms, antonyms, homographs (L.5.5c).

## Hands on to later grades

The grade 9 to 10 standards are band codes (RL.9-10.x) used in English 9 and English 10; each line names what
the grade 8 row gives the first grade 9 row on the same strand.

- e8-strongest-evidence: choosing and defending the strongest evidence (to RL.9-10.1, RI.9-10.1: strong and thorough evidence, and where the text leaves matters uncertain).
- e8-theme-relationship: theme development tied to character, setting and plot; objective summary (to RL.9-10.2: how a theme emerges and is shaped by specific details).
- e8-dialogue-incidents: how dialogue and incidents drive action and reveal character (to RL.9-10.3: complex characters who develop and advance the plot).
- e8-word-choice-lit: impact of word choice, analogy, allusion and irony on meaning and tone (to RL.9-10.4: cumulative impact of word choices on tone, sense of time and place).
- e8-compare-structures: comparing two texts' structures and their effect on meaning (to RL.9-10.5: an author's choices about structure, order and time).
- e8-dramatic-irony: point of view across characters and reader (to RL.9-10.6: point of view or cultural experience in world literature).
- e8-production-fidelity: judging a production against its text (to RL.9-10.7: a subject in two artistic mediums, what each stresses or leaves out).
- e8-myth-made-new: a modern text drawing on and transforming source material (to RL.9-10.9: how an author draws on and transforms source material in a specific work).
- e8-reading-range: reading the high end of the 6 to 8 band independently (to RL.9-10.10, RI.9-10.10: the grades 9 to 10 band).
- e8-central-idea-supporting: a central idea and its supporting ideas; objective summary (to RI.9-10.2: how a central idea emerges and is refined by details).
- e8-connections-distinctions: how comparisons, analogies and categories link ideas (to RI.9-10.3: the order of points, how they are introduced and developed, connections between them).
- e8-word-choice-info: word choice, analogy and allusion in nonfiction (to RI.9-10.4: cumulative impact of word choices, for example legal or technical language).
- e8-paragraph-structure: the role of sentences in a paragraph (to RI.9-10.5: how ideas are developed and refined by particular sentences, paragraphs or larger parts).
- e8-conflicting-viewpoints: how an author answers conflicting views (to RI.9-10.6: how an author uses rhetoric to advance a point of view or purpose).
- e8-medium-choice: judging mediums and the motives behind presentations (to RI.9-10.7, SL.9-10.2: accounts of a subject in different mediums; credibility and accuracy of sources).
- e8-delineate-argument: soundness, sufficiency and irrelevant evidence (to RI.9-10.8, SL.9-10.3: false statements and fallacious reasoning; seminal U.S. documents).
- e8-conflicting-texts: separating factual conflict from interpretation (to RI.9-10.9: seminal U.S. documents and related themes).
- e8-argument-writing: claims with counterclaims, cohesion and formal style (to W.9-10.1: precise claims, counterclaims developed fairly with strengths and limits).
- e8-informative-writing: broader categories, well-chosen evidence, varied transitions (to W.9-10.2: complex ideas, connections and distinctions, syntax that links major sections).
- e8-narrative-writing: reflection and linked events (to W.9-10.3: multiple plot lines, a coherent whole).
- e8-produce-publish: writing for task, purpose and audience; presenting relationships between ideas with technology (to W.9-10.4, W.9-10.6: publishing and updating shared writing products).
- e8-revise-purpose-audience: revising for purpose and audience; voice and mood for effect (to W.9-10.5, L.9-10.3: what is most significant for a specific purpose and audience; style manuals).
- e8-self-generated-research: self-generated questions and several avenues; standard citation (to W.9-10.7, W.9-10.8: sustained research, synthesis of sources, advanced searches).
- e8-text-response-writing: strongest evidence in routine writing (to W.9-10.9, W.9-10.10).
- e8-collab-discussion: decision-making, connecting several speakers, qualifying views (to SL.9-10.1: propelling conversations, consensus and voting, responding to diverse perspectives).
- e8-present-reasoning: sound reasoning and integrated media (to SL.9-10.4, SL.9-10.5, SL.9-10.6: a clear and distinct perspective; strategic use of digital media).
- e8-verbals-voice-mood: verbals, voice and mood (to L.9-10.1: parallel structure; phrases and clauses of several kinds).
- e8-pause-omission: comma, ellipsis and dash; quoting with omissions (to L.9-10.2: semicolons and colons).
- e8-word-strategies: roots, affixes, references, checking a guess (to L.9-10.4, L.9-10.5: patterns of word changes; nuance in word meanings).
