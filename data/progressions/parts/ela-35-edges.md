# ELA grades 3 to 5: edges to other bands

Rows in parts/ela-35.csv. Cross-band assumes and hands_on cells are left empty there; the stitcher writes them from
these lists. The grade 2 skill is named by the standard it rests on (the K to 2 part was not written when this was
drafted); use the K to 2 row id that owns it. Grade 6 ids are from parts/ela-68.csv as read 2026-11-13.

## Models the band needs from K to 2

Grade 3 rows carry these in model_in and model_out, so a grade 2 row must hand each on (have it in model_out):
sound-spelling-map, syllable-types, word-parts, context-clues, story-map, main-idea-details, paragraph-frame,
writing-process, research-cycle, discussion-protocol, sentence-combining. If K to 2 does not use one, the grade 3
rows that use it should move it to model_out only (introducing it in grade 3); the likely introducing rows are
e3-long-words (sound-spelling-map, syllable-types, word-parts), e3-word-strategies (context-clues), e3-central-message
(story-map), e3-main-idea (main-idea-details), e3-opinion (paragraph-frame), e3-writing-process (writing-process),
e3-short-research (research-cycle), e3-discussion (discussion-protocol), e3-sentences (sentence-combining).

This band introduces: figurative-language (e3-literal-nonliteral), narrative-arc (e3-story-parts), text-structure
(e3-text-connections) in grade 3; claim-evidence-reasoning (e4-reasons-evidence), essay-structure (e4-informative),
citation (e4-research), rhetorical-situation (e4-writing-tasks) in grade 4; argument-map (e5-reasons-to-points) in
grade 5. The grades 6 to 8 part also introduces argument-map (e6-trace-argument) and rhetorical-situation
(e6-author-purpose); since this band hands them on from grade 5, those grade 6 rows should add them to model_in.
No new model names were added; all are from the shared list.

## Needs from earlier grades

- e3-text-questions: asking and answering who, what, where, when, why, how questions about key details (RL.2.1; RI.2.1).
- e3-central-message: recounting fables and folktales and naming the central message (RL.2.2).
- e3-character-actions: how characters respond to major events and challenges (RL.2.3).
- e3-literal-nonliteral: how words and phrases supply rhythm and meaning; shades of meaning (RL.2.4; L.2.5).
- e3-story-parts: the overall structure of a story, beginning and ending (RL.2.5).
- e3-point-of-view: differences in characters' points of view; an author's main purpose (RL.2.6; RI.2.6).
- e3-pictures-and-words: using illustrations and images to understand a text (RL.2.7; RI.2.7).
- e3-same-author: comparing two versions of the same story (RL.2.9).
- e3-reading-range: reading the grades 2 to 3 band with scaffolding at the high end (RL.2.10; RI.2.10).
- e3-main-idea: the main topic of a multiparagraph text and the focus of paragraphs; recounting key ideas from a read-aloud (RI.2.2; SL.2.2).
- e3-text-connections: the connection between events, ideas or steps; how reasons support an author's points (RI.2.3; RI.2.8).
- e3-text-features: text features to locate key facts (RI.2.5).
- e3-compare-two-texts: comparing the most important points of two texts on one topic (RI.2.9).
- e3-academic-words: words in a text on a grade 2 topic; using words acquired from reading (RI.2.4; L.2.6).
- e3-word-strategies: sentence context, known prefixes, known roots, compound words, glossaries (L.2.4).
- e3-long-words: long and short vowels, vowel teams, two-syllable long-vowel words, common prefixes and suffixes, irregular words (RF.2.3).
- e3-fluency: reading grade-level text aloud with accuracy, rate and expression; audio recordings of stories or poems (RF.2.4; SL.2.5).
- e3-opinion: opinion pieces with reasons and linking words (W.2.1).
- e3-informative: informative pieces with a topic, facts and definitions, and a conclusion (W.2.2).
- e3-narrative: narratives of a short sequence with details and time words (W.2.3); formal and informal English (L.2.3).
- e3-writing-tasks: writing to a topic with guidance (W.2.5; W.2 types). Grade 2 has no W.4 or W.10, so the need is the grade 2 writing types.
- e3-writing-process: revising and editing with guidance; digital tools to publish (W.2.5; W.2.6).
- e3-short-research: shared research; gathering information from provided sources (W.2.7; W.2.8).
- e3-discussion: agreed rules, building on others' talk, asking for clarification; questions about what a speaker says (SL.2.1; SL.2.3).
- e3-speaking-reports: telling a story or recounting an experience in coherent sentences (SL.2.4; SL.2.6).
- e3-word-forms: collective nouns, irregular plurals, irregular past verbs, adjectives and adverbs (L.2.1a to L.2.1e).
- e3-sentences: producing, expanding and rearranging simple and compound sentences (L.2.1f).
- e3-conventions: capitals, commas in letters, apostrophes, spelling patterns, beginning dictionaries (L.2.2).

## Hands on to later grades

- e5-quote-accurately: quoting accurately for what a text says and infers; drawing evidence in writing (to e6-cite-evidence; e6-text-response-writing).
- e5-theme-response: a theme from how characters meet challenges or a speaker reflects; a summary (to e6-theme-summary).
- e5-compare-characters: comparing characters, settings or events with specific details (to e6-plot-episodes).
- e5-figurative: interpreting similes, metaphors and idioms in context; synonyms, antonyms, homographs (to e6-word-meaning-lit; e6-word-strategies).
- e5-whole-structure: how chapters, scenes or stanzas fit the overall structure (to e6-structure-lit).
- e5-narrator-influence: how a narrator's or speaker's point of view shapes the telling (to e6-narrator-pov).
- e5-visual-multimedia: how visual and multimedia elements add to meaning, tone, beauty (to e6-read-vs-watch).
- e5-genre-compare: comparing stories of one genre on similar themes (to e6-compare-genres).
- e5-reading-range: independent reading at the high end of the grades 4 to 5 band (to e6-reading-range).
- e5-several-main-ideas: two or more main ideas, a summary, summarizing a read-aloud (to e6-central-idea-summary; e6-media-integrate).
- e5-relationships: interactions between individuals, events and ideas (to e6-idea-development).
- e5-compare-structures: comparing the overall structures of texts (to e6-section-structure).
- e5-multiple-accounts: several accounts of one event and the points of view they represent (to e6-author-purpose).
- e5-locate-sources: finding answers across several print and digital sources (to e6-media-integrate).
- e5-reasons-to-points: which reasons and evidence support which point; a speaker's claims and support (to e6-trace-argument).
- e5-integrate-several: integrating several texts on one topic (to e6-compare-accounts).
- e5-academic-words: grade 5 academic and domain words, words of contrast and addition (to e6-technical-words).
- e5-word-strategies: context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, reference works (to e6-word-strategies).
- e5-long-words: reading multisyllabic words by syllables and morphology (to e6-word-strategies and e6-technical-words, as word parts; RF ends in grade 5).
- e5-fluency: accurate, expressive oral reading of grade-level text (no grade 6 standard; grade 6 keeps a fluency opener).
- e5-opinion: opinion pieces with logically ordered reasons backed by facts and details (to e6-argument-writing).
- e5-informative: informative pieces with a focus, grouped information, links across categories (to e6-informative-writing).
- e5-narrative: narratives with dialogue, description, pacing and transitions (to e6-narrative-writing).
- e5-writing-tasks: clear writing fitted to task, purpose and audience; routine writing (to e6-produce-publish; e6-text-response-writing).
- e5-writing-process: planning, revising, rewriting, editing; two typed pages in one sitting (to e6-revise-edit; e6-produce-publish).
- e5-research: short research from several sources, summarized notes, a list of sources (to e6-short-research).
- e5-discussion: prepared discussion with roles, elaboration and conclusions (to e6-collab-discussion).
- e5-presenting: reporting and presenting an opinion in logical order with media; adapting speech (to e6-present-claims).
- e5-grammar: conjunctions, perfect tenses, tense shifts; expanding, combining and reducing sentences (to e6-pronouns; e6-revise-edit).
- e5-conventions: series and introductory commas, titles of works, spelling (to e6-punctuation-spelling).
