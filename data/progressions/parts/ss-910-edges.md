# Social studies grades 9 to 10: edges across bands

Cross-band assumes and hands_on cells in ss-910.csv are left empty; the integrator fills them from these lists.

## Models grade 9 uses that an earlier grade must hand on

timeline, map-scale, map-key, region, latitude-longitude, cause-effect-chain, source-sourcing, corroboration,
claim-evidence, continuity-change, branches-of-government. Grade 9 introduces perspective (wh1-forms-of-power) and
supply-demand (wh1-farming-and-cotton) unless an earlier grade already hands them on; if one does, add it to those
rows' model_in.

## Needs from earlier grades

- wh1-inquiry-questions: grade 6 to 8 compelling and supporting questions (C3 D1, 6-8 band).
- wh1-sources-of-the-past: grade 6 to 8 sourcing of a primary source (maker, date, audience, purpose).
- wh1-sources-and-interpretations: grade 6 to 8 primary and secondary source distinction and comparing two accounts.
- wh1-historical-argument: grade 8 written claim with evidence from more than one source.
- wh1-graphic-displays: grade 6 to 7 map reading (scale, key, physical and political maps) and drawing a timeline to scale.
- wh1-geography-shapes-contact: grade 6 to 7 physical geography of world regions (each region's "physical and political geography" topics).
- wh1-ways-societies-interact: grade 7 Rome and Han China to about 500 CE and the early Silk Road (7.T2b, 7.T4c); chronology picks up there.
- wh1-belief-systems: grade 6 to 7 origins of Judaism (6.T3e), Hinduism and Buddhism (7.T1b), Confucianism (7.T2b), Christianity (7.T4c), Islam (6.T3f).
- wh1-religions-spread: the same grade 6 to 7 origins rows.
- wh1-empire-power-culture: grade 7 fall of Rome in the west and Gupta India (7.T4c, 7.T1b).
- wh1-african-kingdoms: grade 6 Sub-Saharan African states to 1000 CE (6.T4b), including Axum.
- wh1-song-kamakura: grade 7 ancient China, Japan and Korea (7.T2b, 7.T2c).
- wh1-american-empires: grade 6 ancient societies of Central and South America (6.T5c).
- wh1-european-kingdoms: grade 7 Rome's fall and early Christianity (7.T4c).
- wh1-classical-learning: grade 7 Greek and Roman learning (7.T4b, 7.T4c).
- wh1-philosophies-of-government: grade 8 philosophical foundations of government (8.T1).
- wh1-enlightenment: grade 8 Enlightenment ideas behind the founding (8.T1).
- wh1-constitutional-democracy: grade 8 Constitution, branches and Bill of Rights (8.T2, 8.T3, 8.T5).
- wh1-slavery-across-world and wh1-atlantic-slave-trade: grade 5 slavery in the colonies and the Middle Passage (the grade 5 row on colonial slavery).
- wh1-colonial-americas: grade 4 to 5 European exploration and colonial encounters with Native nations.
- wh1-farming-and-cotton: an earlier grade's scarcity, trade and specialization (grade 6 to 7 economics, if handed on).

## Hands on to later grades

- wh1-constitutional-democracy: world setting of the Constitution and Bill of Rights, to US History I (USI.T1 to T2) and Government. Skips grade 10 because grade 10 is world history; US history resumes in grade 11.
- wh1-enlightenment and wh1-philosophies-of-government: natural rights, social contract and kinds of government, to Government (GOV.T1). Skips grade 10 for the same reason.
- wh1-atlantic-slave-trade: the Atlantic system and its source limits, to US History I (slavery and the Civil War). Skips grade 10 (grade 10 also builds on it in wh2-atlantic-revolutions and wh2-imperialism-causes).
- wh2-atlantic-revolutions: the American Revolution in comparison with France and Haiti, to US History I.
- wh2-industrial-revolution and wh2-industrial-cities: industrialization's causes and effects, to US History I industrial America.
- wh2-industrial-ideas-reform: labor, socialism and reform, to US History I (labor and Progressives).
- wh2-imperialism-causes and wh2-imperialism-impact: 19th century empire, to US History I (US expansion overseas).
- wh2-wwi-causes and wh2-total-war: causes and conduct of the First World War, to US History I (USI.T7).
- wh2-great-depression: causes, data and competing economic explanations, to US History II and Economics (macroeconomics, monetary policy).
- wh2-totalitarian-regimes, wh2-road-to-war, wh2-wwii-war-and-diplomacy, wh2-holocaust, wh2-atomic-bombs: the Second World War in world view, to US History II.
- wh2-wwii-consequences: UN, human rights and international law, to Government (international relations) and US History II.
- wh2-cold-war-blocs, wh2-cold-war-europe, wh2-cold-war-world: the Cold War worldwide, to US History II.
- wh2-decolonization and wh2-social-movements: means of changing societies and late 20th century movements, to US History II civil rights and Government (civic action).
- wh2-globalization and wh2-postwar-prosperity: trade, growth and its effects on workers, to Economics (comparative advantage, D2.Eco.14; trade policy).
- wh2-terrorism and wh2-middle-east-since-1980: to US History II (since 2001) and Government.
- wh2-global-problems: one informed-action cycle, to Government (D4.8 deliberation and action).
- wh2-historians-interpretations: critique of secondary history and source credibility, to US History I and II.
- wh2-research-argument: a full sourced research argument with critique and revision, to US History I and II.
