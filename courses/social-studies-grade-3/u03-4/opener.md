# Section 3.4 opener: trade and how places depend on each other

## What the section does

Four days of 40 minutes. Day 61 reads origin labels and a map legend, and the class ledger gets its third line. Day 62 follows one made-up order history in two directions (goods one way, money the other) and shows that dependence grows with more orders. Day 63 places Ghana, a country in West Africa today, and reads a summary of a news report about the Techiman market. Day 64 compares two records, takes one likeness and one difference, and names what a source does not tell.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **People trade because places make and grow different things.** An origin label says where something was made or grown; the map legend says what the arrow stands for.
- **Dependence runs both ways.** A grower needs a buyer; a maker needs the grower's goods. Local work does not end because of trade; a snack made here from limes grown far away still needs local workers.
- **A seller cannot charge just anything,** because buyers may choose another seller.
- **Ghana today** is a country on the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa, bordering Ivory Coast and Burkina Faso. An old empire with the same name lay far to the northwest. The day says "long ago" and gives no dates; grade 6 studies it.
- **Techiman** is a city in the Bono East Region. A trader and a market user speak for themselves; one source does not speak for everyone.

## The errors in plain words

P is the idea that an arrow explains dependence by itself. I is the idea that trade ends local work. R merges today's Ghana with the old empire. J takes one source for every market. K gives no detail from the source. M returns from 3.2.

## Supplies

Origin label cards, the legend blank, the lime order history, the arrows blank, the Techiman Traders Record, our own-words summary of a real newspaper report (days 63 to 65), the Local Production Record, the compare table, a hardware shop card and a fish stall card.

## A room with nothing

A hand-drawn map, chalk arrows and a pair of cards read aloud. No child says where a family's things come from. A label never names a child's family or country.
