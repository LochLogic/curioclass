# Day 1: A person and a place

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the town page in full first: the person is no longer living, the page names a deed and what it changed, and it is linked and credited. If none is ready, use the fallback card. Post chart paper for the board. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 1 to 2 (key-exits-1-2.md) out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 6 min. Hook: a place and its symbol

Whole class. Show the landmark photograph beside its symbol on the town map. Say who made the photograph and when, from its credit. Review: in kindergarten you asked who made a source and when.

**Ask.** What do you notice? What do you wonder? Expect: any look or any question. A statement gets "What could we ask about it?"

Post the big question on the board: "Why is our town where it is?" Review: in grade 1 you learned that a big question is too large to answer at once. Say: "It has no one answer."

#### 15 min. A person tied to the place

Say who made the page and when. Read it aloud once. Say: "A biography is the true story of one person's life, told by someone else. A leader sees something that needs changing and acts."

Pairs fold plain paper into three boxes and label them from the board: WHO (the person), WHERE (the place tied to them), WHO CARES TODAY (who uses or visits that place now). They fill the boxes with your help.

**Ask.** Who is the person? Which place is tied to them? Who uses or visits that place today? Expect, with the fallback card: Frederick Law Olmsted; the Back Bay Fens in Boston; people who visit the park.

Two pairs share. Any pair may pass.

#### 5 min. Wonder cards

Each child writes one question about the person or place and names one thing that could answer it: the page, a map or someone to ask. Review: in grade 2 you learned a wonder card, one question and one source. Mark each card on the record sheet: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Those not yet seen get the question-and-source cards on day 2.

#### 10 min. Start-of-year check

Not a test; nothing is scored. Read the Jo Card once, then each ask once ([[s01-R1]], [[s01-R2]], [[s01-R3]]). Most children write a sentence or label a drawing for about 7 minutes. Up to 3 children who prefer to speak come to the side table, about 45 seconds an ask. Others who prefer to speak tell a scribe. Record each child's explanation on the one scale.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket

Every child writes on the half slip ([[s01-E1]], [[s01-E1b]]). Read the Sign Card aloud once, then each ask once. Collect the slips; then open the key. The card is first seen here.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the page. No photograph: use a place from your town's own website. No exit slip: write on plain paper.

**Skip.** The class board, the pair share and the record sheet.

- **10 min. A place.** Look at a photograph of a place in your town.
  **Say.** "What do you notice? What do you wonder?" Expect: any look or any question. Then write: "Why is our town where it is?"
- **13 min. A person.** Read a short page about a person from your town who is no longer living. Fold paper into three boxes: who, where, who cares today.
  **Say.** "Who is it? Which place? Who uses it today?" Expect: a name, a place and a group.
- **7 min. A wonder.** Review (grade 2): your child writes one question and one thing that could answer it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E1]] [[s01-E1b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: read the first half of the biography and fill only who and where. Longer: a second person from the same page.

### Words and supports

Words: biography, leader, place, big question. Frame: "I wonder ___. I could find out from ___." Second frame: "The person is ___. The place is ___. It matters to ___." A pointed card or a home language is an answer.
