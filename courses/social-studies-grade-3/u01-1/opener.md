# Section 1.1 opener: why our town matters to others

## What the section does

Two days of 40 minutes. Day 1 opens the unit's big question, Why is our town where it is? The teacher shows a photograph of a public landmark beside its symbol on the town map, then reads a short biography of a local person no longer living, from a free, credited town page. If no page is ready, use the fallback card (biography-fallback-card.md). Children write or draw a question of their own on a wonder card. A short start-of-year page (the Jo Card) follows, not scored. Day 2 is about who else cares about the big question and why, and which words in "a good place for a town" people can read two ways. The class writes five supporting questions together and starts a day 2 record that comes back on day 22.

## How the days run

Day 1: hook, biography, a before, an action and an after, the Jo Card, wonder cards, the exit. Day 2: a short repair, a worked example with the library volunteer strips, pairs sorting draft question cards, the garden question cards, the day 2 record, the exit. Each day's exit is one printed card (cards-exits-1-2.md) that every child answers in writing; the day's six are also heard.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A big question matters to other people, not only to us.** Different people care about where a town is for different reasons.
- **A person's action has a before and an after.** A leader sees a need, acts, and something changes.
- **Some words can be read two ways.** In "a good place for a town", good can mean near water or safe from floods. A map fact, such as where the river runs, has one reading.
- **A fact can be checked; a priority is what someone wants most.**
- **A supporting question helps answer the big question in steps.**

## The errors in plain words

None of the unit's error letters is taught on these two days. If a child names only the feature and no action, ask what the person did and what changed. Wrong ideas are read from "someone might say" cards, never from a named child.

## Supplies for the section

A free, credited photograph and page for the town landmark, the town map, the biography page or fallback card, wonder cards, the Jo Card, the before, action and after boxes, chart paper for the question board, the library volunteer strips, draft question cards, garden question cards, the day 2 record, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Say the biography aloud. Draw three boxes on the board for before, action and after. Write the questions on paper strips and sort them on the floor. Children answer the exit on a half sheet the teacher copies from the board.

No child reports on home, family, money, religion or how they came to live here. A child may pass in talk.
