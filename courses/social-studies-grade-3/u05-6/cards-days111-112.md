# Days 111 and 112 cards

Print "made up, for practice" on invented cards. Read every line aloud once. A stop card is on each desk, and a child may pass.

## Day 111 repair (O and AB from day 110)

A fresh made-up caption beside a record of the classroom. Each child names one change and one thing that stayed.

## Retrieval

What the class read in grade 3 unit 1 (check with the English plan; ask only what it states). Words from grade 2: enslaved people, enslavers, forced. Slavery was legal and was cruel.

## The colony table

| Colony | Began | Region | Slavery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virginia (Jamestown) | 1607 | South | Africans were brought by force in 1619; slavery became law in Virginia in 1662. |
| Massachusetts (Boston) | 1620 and 1630 | North | Legal. |

Ask: which is in the North, which in the South? Slavery was part of life in both, and the law allowed it in both. In 1619 people were taken from their homes and brought to Virginia by force. Sources: Boston exhibit (U5R); National Park Service, African Americans at Jamestown (U5AE).

## Who was enslaved

Enslaved Bostonians worked in homes, shops, farms, shipyards and at sea. Before 1700 most enslaved people in Boston were Native American. They were held against their will. (City of Boston Archaeology, U5R.)

## Some Africans were free

Card 1, made up: Mira lived in a made-up port. She was free. She ran a small shop.
Card 2, made up: Tomas lived in the same port. He was enslaved. He worked on the wharf.

The class says which was free and which was enslaved, and that Africans in a port like this were both (R).

## Day 112 repair (H or R)

For H: fresh made-up northern records. Pairs find the place and say what each record says. For R: a fresh free person's record beside a fresh enslavement record.

## Who wrote it?

On 25 May 1774 a great number of Black people of Massachusetts sent a petition to the Governor, Council and House of Representatives. (Founders' Constitution, U5Q.) Who made it, when, to whom.

## The request

Clause: "we have in common with all other men a naturel right to our freedoms without Being depriv'd of them by our fellow men."

Plain summary: like everyone else, we have a natural right to be free, and no one should take it from us.

Hard words explained: naturel means natural. Depriv'd means taken away. The class names the action (a petition) and the purpose (freedom).

## Lives

The petitioners wrote as people with rights. Enslaved people worked in skilled crafts and at sea, as well as in homes and on farms. (U5R.) Venture Smith's own words from grade 2 come back. The lives are told as lives.
