# Section 5.6 opener: slavery in the colonies and the words of enslaved people

## What the section does

Two days of 40 minutes. Day 111: slavery was legal in colonies in the North and the South; who was enslaved; some Africans were free. Day 112: a petition written in 1774 by enslaved people, read as their own words.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Legal in the colonies the pages name.** Slavery was legal in Massachusetts (sources.csv U5R); the date of that law is held back for the day 120 law card, so do not teach it. In late August 1619 about twenty Africans were brought to Virginia by force; slavery was not yet a law there and was recognized in Virginia law in 1662 (U5AE). Say people were taken from their homes; never "workers who came" or "arrived". The plan's wording "legal in every colony" is reworded to these two colonies, one northern and one southern, because no page read names the others. Nothing is said about St. Augustine, Plymouth or New Amsterdam on slavery.
- **Who was enslaved.** Enslaved Bostonians worked in household service, unskilled work, skilled craft and seafaring, in farming, shipbuilding, distilling and construction. Before 1700 most enslaved people in Boston were Native American (U5R). They were held against their will. Say the words enslaved people and enslavers; never "slaves" or "owners". No graphic detail.
- **Some Africans were free.** The exhibit names real free Black residents, but the one it names, Zipporah Potter Atkins, is held for day 120 and was read in a page summary only. On day 111 the free-person card is invented and labelled made up.
- **The petition.** On 25 May 1774 a great number of Blacks of the province petitioned the Governor, Council and House of Representatives of Massachusetts (U5Q). Day 112 teaches one clause: "we have in common with all other men a naturel right to our freedoms without Being depriv'd of them by our fellow men." The other clause, about an act of the legislative, is held for the E112 exit. The family lines are never used. No slur is read.
- **Lives, not only suffering.** The pages read give occupations and the petition. Do not add claims about faith or family customs that no page read states.

## The errors in plain words

H is thinking slavery was only in the South. R is treating all Africans in Boston as enslaved. O and AB are held from day 110: a change and a thing that stayed; a child's own home.
