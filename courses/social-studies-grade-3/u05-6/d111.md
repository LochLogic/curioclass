# Day 111: Slavery in the colonies

## Teacher page

### Before class
This day tells hard history. Put a stop card on each desk; any child may point to it or pass. No role play and no acting. Hand back day 110's written tasks with a cue. Draw the colony table on the wall with two rows, Virginia and Massachusetts. Print the half exit slip.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (O and AB from E110)
If many missed day 110's exit (error O, naming only changes; error AB, describing a home), all 5 minutes go here; if few, those children use the side table. Read, made up: in 1650 a child fetched water from a well in a bucket; now water comes from a faucet. Each child writes [[s05-W111a]]. No child describes their own home.

#### 4 min. Retrieval
Review: in grade 2 you learned the words enslaved people and enslavers; we never say "slaves" or "owners". Day 109: an enslaved person was held for life and denied freedom. Say plainly: slavery was legal then, and it was cruel.

#### 8 min. Legal in every colony
The colony table has two rows: Virginia, Jamestown, 1607, the South; Massachusetts, Boston, 1630, the North (bands on the wall strip). **Ask.** "Which is in the North, which in the South?" Expect: Massachusetts north, Virginia south. Say: in late August 1619 about twenty Africans were brought to Virginia against their will, captives taken from a ship; slavery was recognized in Virginia law in 1662. In Massachusetts slavery was legal too. The people taken from their homes were forced, never workers who came. Each child writes [[s05-P111a]].

#### 8 min. Who was enslaved
Read, from the City of Boston Archaeology slavery exhibit: enslaved Bostonians worked in homes, shops, farming, shipbuilding and at sea. Before 1700 most enslaved people in Boston were Native American. They were held against their will. No graphic detail. Each child writes [[s05-P111b]].

#### 8 min. Some Africans were free
Read both made-up cards once. Mira ran a small shop and could leave town when she liked. Tomas worked on the wharf and could not leave. **Ask.** "Which was free, and which was enslaved?" Expect: Mira free, Tomas enslaved. Say: Africans in a port like this were both free and enslaved. Free is new today, set against enslaved. The exhibit also tells of free Black residents of Boston.

#### 5 min. Record
Each child writes [[s05-P111c]]. Frame: "___ was free." "___ was enslaved." Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each, and list their names.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Charlestown Labor Card, new today. Read it once. Each child writes [[s05-E111]] and [[s05-E111b]]. Key: key-exits-111-112.md.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the table on the board and read every card once.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall strip: write the table on paper. You are the partner.

**Skip.** The pairs and the wall strip.

- **7 min. Repair.** [[s05-W111a]] **Say.** "What stayed the same?" Expect: people need water.
- **12 min. Colony and work.** Read the facts aloud. [[s05-P111a]] [[s05-P111b]]
- **11 min. Free and enslaved.** [[s05-P111c]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E111]] [[s05-E111b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the table, the free and enslaved cards and the exit. Longer: add one more kind of work.

### Words and supports
Words: free, enslaved, legal, colony. Frame: "___ was free." "___ was enslaved." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 112 repairs error H (slavery only in the South) or error R (all Africans in Boston enslaved), then reads a petition by enslaved people.
