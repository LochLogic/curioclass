# Day 93: Why ships sailed west

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hang a world map with a compass rose and key. Write the five year cards of the repair row on the board. Keep key-exits-93-96.md away until slips are in. Keep a class list for who was heard.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (AA from E92)
Hand back day 92's slip and practice (s05-E92, s05-P92a, s05-P92b, s05-P92c, s05-P92d, s05-P92e, s05-P92f) marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Show the wrong, "The 1800s are the eighteenth century," beside the right: 1800 to 1899 is the nineteenth, so count one more. Each child says the century aloud for each year on the board: 1790, 1805, 1990, 2024, 1850.

#### 5 min. Locate
Review (unit 1): the compass rose and the key. Point to Europe, the Atlantic and North America on the map; the class says each name aloud. Seloy, the Timucua town from day 90, is on the North American coast. Each child writes [[s05-P93a]] using the word bank.

#### 8 min. Why sail west
Read the four reason cards aloud, made up for practice: trade (some Europeans hoped to buy and sell goods across the sea); riches (some hoped to find things worth a lot of money); land (some hoped for land to farm and build on); religion (some hoped to live and worship as their faith asked). A hope is not a promise. Review (grade 2): a chain strip, "Because ___, they moved ___. Afterward ___." Link each card to the strip: a hope, a voyage (a trip by ship), a meeting. Say plainly that people already lived on the coasts the ships reached. Each child writes [[s05-P93b]] and [[s05-P93c]].

#### 10 min. First meetings
New term: a first meeting is when people from two places meet for the first time. Read Salt Cove (made up): a ship reaches Salt Cove, where fishers live. The fishers watch it come in. The sailors want fresh water. Fill the strip together: what each group saw (a strange ship; a town with drying racks); what each learned (news of the other's land); what each wanted (water; to know who the visitors were). Chain: a reason (water), a voyage, a meeting, an effect (each learns about the other). **Ask.** "Could we say the ship discovered Salt Cove?" Expect: no, people already lived there.

#### 10 min. Pairs
Each pair reads the Fern Point card (made up): a ship reaches Fern Point, where people live in a town on the shore. The visitors want to trade cloth. The townspeople want to see the cloth. Afterward, the town and the visitors agree to meet again each spring. Each child fills the strip for it, [[s05-P93d]]. Hear six pairs and list their names. Post the answer to "Why sail west?" on the question board.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Encounter Card, new today. Read the card once. Each child writes [[s05-E93]] and [[s05-E93b]]. Key: key-exits-93-96.md.

**Shared shape.** Practice names a reason, a voyage and a meeting from a made-up cloth trade whose result is given; the exit is a new meeting card and asks one effect and why it sailed.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the strip on the board; children copy it.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall map: use an atlas or a map on a screen.

**Skip.** The pair round; you are the partner.

- **8 min. The map.** Find Europe, the Atlantic and North America. [[s05-P93a]]
- **10 min. Reasons.** Read the four reasons. [[s05-P93b]] [[s05-P93c]] **Say.** "Did anyone already live there?" Expect: yes, on every coast the ships reached.
- **12 min. Fern Point.** [[s05-P93d]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E93]] [[s05-E93b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one reason card, not two. Longer: write a new meeting card and fill its strip.

### Words and supports
Words: voyage, trade, riches, first meeting, Europe, Atlantic. Frame: "A ship sailed because ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow
