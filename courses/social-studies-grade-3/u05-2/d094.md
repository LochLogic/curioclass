# Day 94: Tracing a route

## Teacher page

### Before class
Print two route maps for each child. Project the map. Keep key-exits-93-96.md away until slips are in. Keep a class list for who was heard.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (Z from E93)
Hand back day 93's slip and practice (s05-E93, s05-P93a, s05-P93b, s05-P93c, s05-P93d) marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Read two accounts of one coast, made up. A visitor's letter: "I saw a bay. I saw smoke from many fires. I wrote to tell my friends at home." A townsperson's note: "This is our town. Our fields run to the shore. We fish from the point." Each child writes [[s05-P94a]]. Show the wrong, "The land was empty," beside the right: fields and a town show people live there.

#### 5 min. Map-key and compass, returned
Review (unit 1): the legend and the north arrow. On this map an arrow is a route and a dot is a place. West is to the left. New term: a route is the line a voyage follows, drawn with an arrow whose tail is at the start dot and head at the end dot. Draw one example arrow, from the Florida dot to the Plymouth dot.

#### 8 min. Worked trace: Verrazzano
New term: an explorer sailed new routes and reached lands new to him. In 1524 Giovanni da Verrazzano sailed for the king of France, crossed the Atlantic, and sailed along the coast from Florida to New Brunswick (source U5L). On the projected map, trace it; children draw it on tracing paper over their map: [[s05-P94b]]. Say aloud the start (France), the direction across the water, and the end (New Brunswick). Cabot is not used today.

#### 10 min. Pairs: Henry Hudson
Henry Hudson reached New York Bay in 1609 (source U5T). Each pair uses a fresh map. Each child draws the route with the tail at the Europe dot and the head at New York Bay, [[s05-P94c]], then writes which way it points across the Atlantic, [[s05-P94d]]. Check a rotating six pairs, about a minute each, and list their names. If an arrow ends at Plymouth (error AE), show a route to another coast, up to five minutes.

#### 5 min. French and Dutch labels
The map has two labels, labels only, no history: New France (the French settlement of Quebec began in 1608, source U5L) and New Netherland (the Dutch founded New Amsterdam in 1625, source U5T). Pairs find each and say which direction it lies from Plymouth. Each child writes [[s05-P94e]] and [[s05-P94f]].

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Cabot Route Card, new today: a map with a dot at England, a dot at the Northeast coast and a blank line between them. Read: "John Cabot sailed from England in 1497." Each child draws [[s05-E94]]. Key: key-exits-93-96.md.

**Shared shape.** Model and practice trace a source's route over tracing paper on a labelled map; the exit is a new voyage on a printed card, drawn once with a pencil.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the coast on the board with chalk arrows; children copy it.

### At home

**Swap.** No tracing paper: draw the arrow in pencil on the map.

**Skip.** The pair round and the class check.

- **8 min. Two accounts.** Read both aloud. [[s05-P94a]]
- **10 min. Verrazzano.** Draw the arrow. [[s05-P94b]] **Say.** "Where is the tail, and where is the head?" Expect: the tail at France, the head at New Brunswick.
- **12 min. Hudson and labels.** [[s05-P94c]] [[s05-P94d]] [[s05-P94e]] [[s05-P94f]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E94]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: Hudson's route only. Longer: draw a made-up voyage between two dots of your own.

### Words and supports
Words: route, explorer, voyage, start, end. Frame: "The route starts at ___ and ends at ___." Pointing and a scribe are answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow
Day 95 opens with error B (an arrow run the wrong way) from today's exit, with a fresh made-up voyage, then asks who made a source, when and why.
