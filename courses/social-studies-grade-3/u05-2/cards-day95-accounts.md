# Day 95 cards: an account beside a nation

Read each card aloud once. Children underline two details on each. The exit lines are not here; they are held back in cards-exits-93-96.md.

## Repair (B from day 94): a fresh route, made up

Pell Harbor voyage, made up: a boat leaves the town of Pell Harbor and sails to the island of Gull Rock. Each child draws the arrow with the tail at the start dot and says the start and the end.

## Sourcing: three questions

Who made it? When? Why?

A flyer (made up): Come to Lantern Beach! Soft sand and warm water. A town's own page (made up): Lantern Beach is where our lifeguard hut stands and where we hold the spring clean-up. Same beach, two voices.

Promotion: a writer's picture of a place meant to make readers want to come.

## Smith (a visitor, 1616)

John Smith wrote A Description of New England in London in 1616 for readers who might pay for settlers. (sources.csv U5N.) Plain summary read aloud: he wrote that where the people lived, the ground was very rich and fertile.

Exact words for the teacher to show: "the ground is exceeding fat & fertill." (Smith used a harsh word for Native people just before these words. Do not read it or show it.)

Questions: who made it, when, why. He wanted readers to come and settle.

## The Massachusett Tribe's own page

The Massachusett Tribe at Ponkapoag is a nation that lives today. (sources.csv U5H, paraphrase.) Plain summary read aloud: women owned and tended the planting fields and preserved the harvests. Women built and owned the long houses.

## The two-column strip

Left column: Smith. Right column: the Massachusett Tribe. Rows: what it shows about the land and people; what it leaves out.
