# Section 5.2 opener: why sail west, routes, an account and a map

## What the section does

Four days of 40 minutes. Day 93: where Europe, the Atlantic and North America are, why some Europeans sailed west, and what happened when a ship met people already living on a coast. Day 94: tracing a route from a start dot to an end dot. Day 95: one visitor's description of the coast beside the Massachusett Tribe's own page. Day 96: an old map beside a new one.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Why sail west.** Trade, riches, land and religious goals are the reasons the standard names. A reason is a hope, not a promise. Say aloud that people already lived on every coast. The word "discovered" is not used.
- **A route** is a line with a tail at the start and a head at the end. Verrazzano sailed in 1524 for the king of France along the coast from Florida to New Brunswick (sources.csv U5L). Henry Hudson reached New York Bay in 1609 (U5T). On day 94 the class names "New France" and "New Netherland" as map labels only (Quebec 1608, U5L; New Amsterdam 1625, U5T).
- **Sourcing.** Who made it, when, and why. John Smith was a visitor writing in 1616 for readers at home who might invest or settle (U5N). The Massachusett Tribe at Ponkapoag is a nation that lives today and keeps its own records (U5H).
- **An old map beside a new one.** Shared features stay; labels change. A new label or a blank space does not mean nobody lived there.

## The errors in plain words

Z is the idea that the land was empty. B is an arrow drawn the wrong way round. C is trusting one writer to tell it all. AE is ending every route at Plymouth.

## Supplies

A world map, one blank route map for each pair (blank-day94-map.md), tracing paper, pencils. The cards are in cards-day93-reasons.md, cards-day94-routes.md, cards-day95-accounts.md and cards-day96-maps.md. The exit cards, with the passages and maps held back for them, are in cards-exits-93-96.md. The keys are in key-exits-93-96.md. No tool.

## A room with nothing

Draw the coast on the board and use chalk arrows. Hold two pages to a window for the overlay. Read every card aloud once.

## Unsettled facts for the day writers

- The plan names Smith's 1616 map and Smith's 1614 voyage. Neither was read (the map has no image page found; the voyage is not on a page read). Day 94 teaches Verrazzano and Hudson instead, and day 96 uses invented map pairs until a map page is read.
- Spell it Verrazzano on the cards (the plan spells it Verrazano). Cabot is only on the day 94 exit, and Cabot's exact landfall is cut (disputed).
- Smith's teaching lines are exact in sources.csv U5N. Do not use the "thirtie habitations" line, which is the exit line.
- No Native nation is made up anywhere. Invented towns are labelled made up.
