# Day 95: One account beside another

## Teacher page

### Before class
Print the Smith card and the Massachusett Tribe card for each child. Keep key-exits-93-96.md away until slips are in. This day carries the sensitivity read: say every nation by its own name, in the present tense where it lives on.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (B from E94)
Hand back day 94's slip and practice (s05-E94, s05-P94a, s05-P94b, s05-P94c, s05-P94d, s05-P94e, s05-P94f) marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Read a fresh made-up voyage: a boat leaves the town of Pell Harbor and sails to the island of Gull Rock. Each child draws it, [[s05-P95a]], and says the start and the end. Show the wrong, an arrow with its head at the start dot, beside the right: the tail is at the start.

#### 5 min. Sourcing
Review (unit 1): who made it, when and why. Read a flyer (made up): "Come to Lantern Beach! Soft sand and warm water." Then the town's own page (made up): "Lantern Beach is where our lifeguard hut stands and where we hold the spring clean-up." Same beach, two voices. New term: promotion is a writer's picture of a place, meant to make readers want to come.

#### 10 min. Smith
John Smith wrote A Description of New England in London in 1616, for readers who might pay for settlers (source U5N). Plain words: the ground where the people lived is very rich. Show his words: "the ground is exceeding fat & fertill." He used a harsh old word for the people; we do not repeat it. Children underline two details on the card. Each child writes [[s05-P95b]] and [[s05-P95c]]. **Ask.** "Who made it, when, and why?" Expect: Smith, a visitor, in 1616, so readers would come and settle.

#### 10 min. The Massachusett Tribe's page
The Massachusett Tribe at Ponkapoag is a nation that lives today, with a tribal council, and it keeps its own records (source U5H). Read one paragraph, in our words: women owned and tended the planting fields and preserved the harvests; women built and owned the wetus and long houses. Children underline two details. Each child writes [[s05-P95d]]. **Ask.** "Who made this page, and why might they tell it?" Expect: the Massachusett Tribe, to tell its own story.

#### 8 min. Pairs
Each pair fills the two-column strip: what each account shows about the land and people, and what each leaves out. Each child writes [[s05-P95e]] and [[s05-P95f]]. Hear six pairs and list their names. Post the answer to "Whose account and map?" on the question board.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Accounts Exit Pair, new today. Read both passages once, with the plain summary. Each child writes [[s05-E95]]. Key: key-exits-93-96.md.

**Shared shape.** Model and practice read one Smith line and one Massachusett page paragraph and ask who made each and what each leaves out; the exit gives two new lines and asks what each shows about the land.

### Other rooms
No printer: write both short passages on the board; children answer on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No class cards: read both passages from the page.

**Skip.** The pair strip and the board.

- **5 min. Sourcing.** Read the flyer and the town page. **Say.** "Who made each, and why?" Expect: a seller wants visitors; the town tells its own story.
- **12 min. Smith.** [[s05-P95b]] [[s05-P95c]]
- **13 min. The Massachusett page.** [[s05-P95d]] [[s05-P95e]] [[s05-P95f]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read both passages once. [[s05-E95]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: Smith's card only. Longer: ask what a third writer would add.

### Words and supports
Words: account, promotion, visitor, nation, source. Frame: "Smith shows ___, and the page shows ___." Pointing and a scribe are answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow
Day 96 opens with error C (an explorer's account tells the whole story) from today's exit, with the flyer and the beach page, then lays an early map over a new one.

### Credit
Ours. Smith's words are public domain (source U5N). The Massachusett Tribe paragraph is in our words from the Tribe's own pages (source U5H). Lantern Beach and Pell Harbor are made up.
