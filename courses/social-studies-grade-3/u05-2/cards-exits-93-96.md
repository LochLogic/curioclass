# Exit cards, days 93 to 96

Print "made up, for practice" on every invented card. The teacher reads each stem aloud once. Answers may be spoken, drawn or written.

## Day 93: Encounter Card (made up)

A ship sails west because its owners hope to trade in far lands. It reaches a coast where people live. Traders swap tools for corn.

Ask 1: Encounter Card. Name one effect of this meeting.
Ask 2: Encounter Card. Why did the ship sail west?

## Day 94: Cabot Route Card

A map with a dot at England, a dot at the Northeast coast and a blank line between them. Read aloud: John Cabot sailed from England in 1497.

Ask: Cabot Route Card. Draw his route with an arrow.

## Day 95: Accounts Exit Pair

Read aloud once. Smith, 1616: "where wee founde some habitations and corne fields: they report a great Riuer, and at least thirtie habitations, doo possesse this Countrie." Plain summary: Smith heard of a great river and at least thirty homes with cornfields.

Massachusett Tribal Life, as a nation's own words: children kept birds away from the fields.

Ask: Accounts Exit Pair. Smith and Massachusett Tribal Life. What does each show about the land?

## Day 96: Coast Exit Pair (made up)

Early map labels: Round Water; Gull Point; Elm Brook; Fish Houses (a drawn row of houses).
Current map labels: Larch Bay; Gull Point; Elm Brook; Pier Lane.

Ask 1: Coast Exit Pair, invented. Name one place that is on both maps.
Ask 2: Coast Exit Pair. Name one label that is different.

The keys are in key-exits-93-96.md. Do not give them to children.
