# Day 18: What changed and what stayed on our map

## Teacher page

### Before class
Find an early map of the area from a library, historical society or state archive. Read its credit and date; add nothing it does not say. Have the town map and the answer overlay, with one landmark on both maps marked. If no early map exists, use a dated archive map of a nearby place beside its modern map, and say so. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 16 to 18 (key-exits-16-18.md), out of the room until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 7 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 17's exit tickets and written tasks with the written cue. If many made error Q, show a dated pair on the board (a field in 1950, a school on it in 2000); pairs name what people did. If many made error P, pairs link a new fence to what people did by a pond. If M or N was heard more, it takes the 7 minutes: read one line from a nation's page about the past and one about today; pairs say the nation lives here today.

#### 5 min. Maker, date and why
New words: early map and modern map, each with its date. Review: in grade 2 you asked who made it and when, and said "the record does not say." Read the early map's credit aloud. Say who made it, when and why; read its legend, north arrow and date as on day 7.

**Ask.** Who made this map, when, and why? Expect: the maker and date from its credit; "the map does not say" for the rest.

#### 14 min. Early beside modern
Pairs set the early map beside the town map and line up one landmark; the scales differ, so say "about." Review: changed and stayed the same, from grade 2. Pairs fill the Changed and Stayed chart: three changes and two things that stayed, each with its map's date. They mark one blank place on the early map. Each child writes [[s01-P18b]]. Visit pairs in turn with the overlay.

**Ask.** What changed, what stayed, and how do you know? Expect: a feature and the date of the map that shows it.

#### 4 min. Swap and check

Pairs swap charts. Does each change name its map's date? Is one thing that stayed named? Say "yes" or point to the gap.

#### 5 min. Supporting question 4
Each child writes [[s01-P18]] from the chart; four look-fors on the record sheet: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Add the class's answer to the question board.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket
Give each child the Millpond Map Pair beside the half slip; read both questions once. Every child writes [[s01-E18]] and [[s01-E18b]]. The pair is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The chart uses real maps of our town and asks for three changes and two that stayed; the exit shows two made-up maps and asks for one of each, with no chart.

### Other rooms
No printer: lay two sheets edge to edge, or draw the pair on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No early map: use an old and a new photograph of a street.

**Skip.** The swap and check.

- **10 min. Two maps.** Set an old and a new map of a place near you side by side.
  **Say.** "What does each map show, and what date does it have?" Expect: a feature and a date.
- **20 min. Changed and stayed.** Find one change and one thing that stayed.
  **Say.** "What does a blank place on the old map tell us?" Expect: it does not say what was there.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E18]] [[s01-E18b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one change and one thing that stayed. Longer: a second change, with its date.

### Words and supports
Words: early, modern, changed, stayed, "the map does not say". Frame: "Between ___ and ___, people ___ the ___, but the ___ stayed."

### Tomorrow
If many wrote that nothing was there in 1900 (error I), day 19 sets a dated map beside a short account from a nation of the place; if many wrote that everything is new (error R), it marks a change and a feature that stayed on a fresh dated pair. Day 19 models a claim from two very short sources.
