# Section 5 opener: how people use and change land and water

## What the section does

Three days, 16 to 18. Day 16 asks what people changed on the land and what they wanted the change to do, and sorts changes made by people from changes made by nature. Day 17 looks at how a group's culture and the land are tied, with the frame "this group, so it", using made-up cases and then a nation's own page once the nation passages have passed the sensitivity read. Day 18 sets an early map of our town beside the current map and fills a Changed and Stayed chart with map dates.

## How the days run

Short repair, worked example, pairs work with cards, then a printed exit card (cards-exits-16-18.md). The day's six are also heard.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **People change land and water, for reasons.** A change has an action and an aim.
- **Nature changes land too.** A sort of made-by-people and made-by-nature is a skill, not a trick.
- **A culture and the land are tied.** A group's practice can help explain how it cares for or uses a place.
- **A new development does not end everything before it.** Some things change and some stay; each claim has a map date.
- **Native nations are named by their own names and live today.** Describe what a nation's own page says, in the present tense. Where the page is silent, write that the page does not say. Nothing ceremonial or sacred is used.

## The errors in plain words

- **Q:** people never change land.
- **R:** a new development ends everything before it.
- **M:** Native nations exist only in the past.
- **N:** all Native nations have the same practices.
- **I:** unmarked land was not empty.

## Supplies for the section

The Land Change Cards, sort cards, the Nation Card, the frame card, Dry Hollow and Elm Lane cards, the early map of our town beside the current map, the Changed and Stayed chart, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Describe each card aloud, draw before and after on the board.
