# Section 2.3 opener: rules, rights and taking part

## What the section does

Five days of 40 minutes. Day 33 traces one school rule and one town law back to a problem, a maker and what each protects, with a four-box strip. Day 34 shows that every right comes with responsibilities: a group agrees a rule, and each person's duty keeps a freedom for others. Day 35 shows how a democracy depends on people taking part (vote, go to a meeting, serve on a board, volunteer, stay informed), through a short biography of a person who took part. Day 36 names one benefit and one difficulty of working together in a family, a school, a workplace, a volunteer group and a government. Day 37 compares how a decision is made in a class, at a town meeting or council, and in an election.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a five-minute return and repair for the most-missed letter of the day before, then teaches, then ends with one exit card (cards-exits-33-37.md). The day 34, 36 and 37 records are practice; each is kept for written feedback before day 46.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A rule has a maker and a reason.** Saying "it was always there" removes both.
- **A duty and a freedom come as a pair in a group rule.** My duty to listen is how your freedom to speak stays. A right is not a prize for obeying. A broken rule gets a small, fair fix, never the loss of a right.
- **Speaking at a meeting is not voting.** Residents may speak at public comment; the elected members decide. A child can learn, ask questions, volunteer with an adult, or speak at public comment with an adult.
- **Cooperation has costs in every setting, and the costs differ.**
- **An election chooses a person. A vote on a plan decides a matter.**

## The errors in plain words

S is the idea that rules were always there. M is the idea that a right is earned by obeying. D is the idea that only voters belong. Y is the idea that leaders know every need without asking. K is the idea that a person who speaks at a meeting also votes. E is the idea that working together is easy. Z is one difficulty named for every setting. P is the idea that every vote chooses a person. Wrong ideas come from "someone might say" cards, never a named child.

## Supplies for the section

A four-box strip, the school rule page, one town law page (or the invented fallback), rule and problem cards, group rule cards, the biography page (or the invented fallback card), five ways cards, two service decision cards, ten five-setting cards and five mats, three deciding cards, the three records, exit cards, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Chart paper, index cards, a strip drawn by hand, the town's pages printed, an invented biography card if no local page is ready.

No child describes their own home or says who in their home votes. A child may pass in talk.
