# Day 33: Why a rule was made

## Teacher page

### Before class
Fill the rule page: our school's hall rule from the handbook, and one law from our town's code page with maker, date and link. Read that page first. Choose a law not about noise or music: the exit uses a noise rule. With no law page ready, use the Tumbleford law. Set cards 5 and E aside. Keep the teacher key, key-exits-33-37.md, out of the room until the exit slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 32's exit slips. Teach the most-missed letter, wrong then right. Error K: "The people who watch the meeting vote" beside "They may speak; the council votes." Error B: "Every town has a mayor who makes the rules" beside "Our town's page names the council; the other town's names Town Meeting." If many made error K, sort speaking and voting on a made-up Brindle notice.

#### 8 min. A rule's story
Review (day 26): groups make rules to keep people safe, settle a disagreement or protect a freedom. Review (grade 1): a law is a rule for everyone in a place. Review (kindergarten): a problem is a place that needs fixing, never a child. New: the four-box strip, with boxes problem, who made it, the rule and what it protects. Model it on our school's hall rule; say the maker from the page, or "the page does not say." Show a "someone might say" card: "Nobody made it; rules were always there." **Ask.** What problem led to the rule? Expect: children bumped or fell. Children watch.

#### 12 min. A town law
Review (day 25): who made it, when and why. Read the law page aloud once, with its maker, date and link. **Ask.** How do you know the problem? Expect: the page says, or does not say. Pairs fill the strip: [[s02-P33a]], [[s02-P33b]], [[s02-P33c]], [[s02-P33d]]. Where the page does not say, write "the page does not say." With Tumbleford, say aloud that it is made up.

#### 5 min. A small, fair fix
If someone breaks the hall rule, the fix should match the problem. Model two: pick up what was dropped and walk the hall again; repair what was spoiled. Review (grade 1): a right is something every person here may do or have; a broken rule does not end one. Each child writes [[s02-P33e]].

#### 7 min. Match the rule
Pairs match the five rule cards to the five problem cards. Each child writes [[s02-P33f]], then picks one rule and writes [[s02-P33g]]. The practice page stays in the folder.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Rule Record Card, first seen here. Read the card and the two asks once. Every child writes [[s02-E33]] and [[s02-E33b]].

**Shared shape.** Practice fills a strip from our pages or matches loose cards; none is about night noise. The exit is a made-up numbered town record.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the four boxes on the board and read the cards aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No town law page: use the Tumbleford law from the rule page.

**Skip.** Sharing with other pairs.

- **5 min. A rule's story.** Read your child's school hall rule. Name its problem and what it protects.
  **Say.** "What problem led to this rule?" Expect: what went wrong before it.
- **14 min. A town law.** Read the law page. [[s02-P33a]] [[s02-P33b]] [[s02-P33c]] [[s02-P33d]]
  **Say.** "How do you know the problem?" Expect: the page says, or does not say.
- **6 min. A fair fix.** [[s02-P33e]]
- **8 min. Match the rule.** [[s02-P33f]] [[s02-P33g]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card and asks once. [[s02-E33]] [[s02-E33b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: boxes 1, 2 and 4 only; skip the second match question.

### Words and supports
Words: problem, maker, rule, law, protect. Frame: "The problem was ___. The rule protects ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 34 shows that every right comes with duties. If many made error S on day 33's exit (a rule taken to have no maker or reason), day 34's Return and repair sets that answer beside a right one. If many wrote the music as what Rule 12 protects, set that beside neighbors' sleep.
