# Teacher key, day 135 sheet

Teacher only. Keep apart from packet-day135.md. Score each item once: 2 when every look-for appears, 1 when some appear without a false claim, 0 when absent or contradicted. Write the missing part and its letter, not just a total. Read each answer for meaning, not spelling.

- Read-aloud word notes (teacher says these before each packet; not on the sheet). Packet A: Representative Houses were groups of lawmakers the colonists chose; dissolved means ended; rights are what people are owed; alter means change; abolish means end; "these ends" are the people's rights. Packet B: Article I, Section 8 lets Congress set up post offices and roads for the mail; Article II, Section 3 means the President sees that laws are carried out; Article III, Section 2 means the courts decide cases; the states voted on the plan in their own conventions and nine of thirteen had to agree; the First Amendment means the people may ask the government to fix wrongs. Packet C: a rule about voting does not settle whether a person is a citizen. Packet D: the Council and House were the lawmakers. Own-state card: good behavior means as long as they act well.
- Q1: the elected Houses spoke for the people; ending them took away their voice and harmed their rights. Not "they disliked all rules" (C).
- Q1b: change or end the government and start a new one. Not "nothing; the ruler decides" (T).
- Q1c: the people. Not "the ruler" (T). The idea that power rests on the people's agreement is read in Q5c.
- Q2: it sets up the government. Not "it gave reasons for independence" (E).
- Q2b: they list rights and protections. Not "they set up Congress" (E).
- Q2c: petition the government to fix wrongs, or assemble. Not "a gift the government can take back" (O).
- Q2d: white men over 21 who owned property; each state set its own rules. Not "all citizens" (D).
- Q2e: some women and free Black men who met the property rules voted until 1807. Not "no woman ever voted" (J).
- Q3: courage or perseverance: asking officials to correct an injustice. Not "they were angry" (S).
- Q3b: equality, or liberty for everyone. Not "they felt sad" (S).
- Q4: the President (Article II, carries out the laws). Not "Congress, because it makes laws" (x).
- Q4b: President versus Governor, one House versus a Congress, or country versus state. Not "the Declaration made both plans" (P).
- Q5: consent means people have a voice or a say in how they are governed. Not "a kind leader" (Q) or "everyone likes every law" (I).
- Teacher background for packet C: the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians is a sovereign nation today with its own laws, elections and government (ebci.gov, U6I); use it for a fresh written move, not on the sheet.
- Q5b: a detail from Packet A, such as the Houses being ended. Not "the Declaration is famous" (G).
- Q5c: it links the detail to the claim, for example that rule with no voice for the people is not consent. Not a detail with no reasoning (G).
- Q5d: a detail from Packet D, such as the petitioners asking the lawmakers for freedom as a natural right. Not "both mention America" (G).
- Q5e: Packet D shows people left out of the promise asking that it protect them too, while A is about the colonists' own representation; both packets named. Not "they say the same thing" (G).
- R1 (day 125 record, revised): a named person beyond the class and a reason. Not "only the rulers care" (N).
- R2 and R2b (day 128 record, revised): delegates drafted it at a convention in Philadelphia in 1787; the states approved it. Not "the Declaration made it" (P).
- R3 (day 131 sort, revised): no; a person can be a citizen and not be allowed to vote. Not "yes, no vote means not a citizen" (D).
- R4 (day 134 strip, revised): spaced by years; 1787 and 1791 are close together, 1807 is much farther. Not equal steps whatever the years (H).
- R4b (day 134 strip, revised): the overlap is named: the plan and the rights list came while New Jersey's wider voting went on. Not "New Jersey's voting ended with the Constitution" (M).
