# Day 133: Write a claim about consent

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 132's slips and cards with one cue. Write the Reading Club card large. Put the six answers from the question board where all can see. Keep the Detail Card hidden until the exit. Keep key-exits-133-136.md. The stop card and the right to pass hold. Describe the petition in your own words; never read it as a quote.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (F or L from E132, whichever more missed)
Hand back day 132's slips and cards (s06-E132, s06-E132b, s06-P132a). If many wrote that a symbol proves everyone was free (error F), set a made-up caption beside a made-up record: the Harbor Light, 1880, said to welcome every boat; a 1881 harbor record shows boats from one village turned away. Use "The symbol says ___; the record says ___." If many thought all must salute (error L), give two ways to study a symbol, and say anyone may decline. The other goes to the small-group table.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Read the six answers from the question board aloud. Review: on day 117 you used a claim sheet: a claim, a detail from each of two sources and a limit. Show the blank frame.

#### 7 min. Gather
For three minutes link one detail to the Reading Club claim "The club shares its books": taking each book home in turn fits; meeting on Tuesdays does not. Then take the frame down. Each child lists two details from the unit and the source of each, [[s06-P133a]]: the Declaration's consent line, an article, the speech clause, a New Jersey rule or a symbol caption. Hear a rotating six, one detail each.

#### 6 min. Check against the petition
Say in your words, not as a quote: in January 1777 Prince Hall and seven other Black men in Massachusetts sent a petition to the Council and House. They said all people have a natural right to freedom. We know the words only through a handwritten copy (U6P). Ask: what was the promise, and what was the practice? Do not compare it with Britain; that is held for day 135.

#### 9 min. Write the claim
Each child finishes the frame and names a gap between a promise and what happened, [[s06-P133b]]. Words, a drawing or dictation count. Consent means people agree to how decisions are made, not that everyone likes every law and not a kind leader.

#### 6 min. Hear another
Review: the norms card from unit 2: listen, say it back, give a reason. Pairs read their claims; each partner names the strongest detail and why. Each child makes one change. Walk the room.

#### 4 min. Exit
Read the Detail Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E133]] and [[s06-E133b]]. Key: key-exits-133-136.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice writes a claim from details of the unit and names a gap; the exit gives a new claim with two details and asks which supports it.

### Other rooms
No board: read the six answers from a paper list.

### At home

**Swap.** No frame: say the claim aloud and write it.

**Skip.** The repair and the pair talk.

- **5 min. Answers.** Read the six answers. **Say.** "What does consent mean?" Expect: people agree to how decisions are made.
- **10 min. Details.** [[s06-P133a]]
- **15 min. The claim.** [[s06-P133b]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E133]] [[s06-E133b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: write one detail and the claim. Longer: a second gap between promise and practice.

### Words and supports
Words: claim, detail, source, gap. Frame: "I think consent means ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." Read every card aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 134 revises the day 125, 128 and 131 records and a fresh strip, from your written notes. If many wrote a claim with no detail (error G), the G children take a fresh pair of details at the small-group table. It hands back E133, E133b, P133a and P133b.
