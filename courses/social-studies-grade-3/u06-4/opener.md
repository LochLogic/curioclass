# Section 6.4 opener: the claim, the timeline, the check and the response

## What the section does

Four days of 40 minutes. Day 133: write a short claim that answers the unit question, with two details and their sources, and hear a partner. Day 134: revise the day 125, 128 and 131 records and the strip after the written notes, and meet one fresh overlap. Day 135: the end-of-unit check from four packets and an own-state card, unaided. Day 136: the response.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A claim needs details that bear on it** (G). A town's name is not evidence; neighbors choosing their council is. A claim is not "consent matters because the Declaration is famous".
- **Consent is not kindness** (Q) and not liking every law (I).
- **A timeline is drawn to scale.** 1787 and 1791 are four years apart; 1807 is much farther (H). New Jersey's wider voting went on while the Constitution and the Bill of Rights came (M).
- **Day 135 is the check.** Packets A to D and the own-state card are used on no teaching day. Packet D's comparison and packet A's sentence on altering a harmful government are held (sources.csv U6AP, U6P). Only revised records are scored for D1.1, the origins, the three questions and the timeline.
- **Day 136.** Power from the people does not mean everyone voted (R): the Massachusetts glosses say where power comes from and who may elect, and a fresh task repairs each child's missed idea (U6R).
- **The petition** is described in words, never quoted: Prince Hall and seven other Black men, a manuscript copy dated 13 January 1777 (U6P). Four of the eight signers are known to have been free; do not say all were.

## The errors in plain words

G is a claim with no detail or reasoning. H is spacing cards evenly whatever the years. I is thinking consent means everyone likes every law. M is thinking a new government ended every other development. Q is thinking consent means kindness. R is thinking power from the people means everyone voted.

## Supplies

Paper, pencils, rulers, the strip, the evidence list, the blank claim frame from unit 5 (used on day 133 only for the first example), the packets (packet-day135.md; key-day135.md is teacher only), the exit cards in cards-exits-133-136.md and the key in key-exits-133-136.md.

## A room with nothing

Draw the strip with a ruler; copy the four packets large; the teacher reads every packet aloud once. No student timer.

## Unsettled facts for the day writers

- Day 133 describes the petition only as men asking for freedom as a natural right; the comparison with Britain's treatment of the colonies is packet D.
- Day 134 gives each child a written cue on the strip; the revision is scored, not the first attempt.
- Day 135: the check is 35 unaided minutes; the teacher reads each packet aloud once just before its prompts. The sheet is scored after class and each missing part is given its letter.
- Interim review: unit 5's blank claim frame and unit 2's norms card are named as earlier work; claim them only if the ledger lists them at stage written, otherwise teach as new and list.
