# Day 44: A small answer that helps

## Teacher page

### Before class
Copy the frame card and the Brindle band shell card from cards-day44-45.md; print the six board answers (days 27 to 43). Fill each pair's folder: the Local Government Record, class edition, the Class Decision Record, the day 33 rule, the day 37 chart, our state notes and the table from day 43. Keep the teacher key (key-exits-44-46.md) away until slips are in. Sources: U2B2, U2N.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 43's slips (s02-E43, s02-E43b, s02-E43c); E43c misses get a written cue. Teach the more-missed of error U, error T and error I, wrong then right, whole class. Error U: "Every government has three branches" beside "A nation sets up its parts in its own way." Error T: "They had a government long ago" beside "The Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin governs itself today." Error I: "The tribe is a town" beside "This nation governs itself today and wrote its own plan." The others go to the side table.

#### 6 min. The board and an oral model
Read the six board answers aloud. Review: claim, evidence and reasoning (unit 1). Say, from the Brindle band shell card (made up): "I think the council should decide the band shell rules because the Brindle Council Page shows the council may make and change them, which means the council has the final say." Put up the frame card: I think ___ because the ___ shows ___, which means ___. Say the new words: **link** is how a smaller answer helps the big question. **People a rule touches** are the people who use it or follow it.

#### 12 min. Gather
Pairs open the folder and choose two records that answer one supporting question from the board. Side table, up to 6 children: five minutes on another of U, T and I, then five on error AD: "It was always there" beside "Delegates wrote it in 1787; the states ratified it." In the last two minutes, hear two children, 45 seconds each; the rest are read on the record and cued for day 46.

#### 9 min. The inquiry record
Children fill the day 44 record: [[s02-P44a]], [[s02-P44b]] and [[s02-P44c]]. Five minutes writing; partners say the link, one minute each; the record goes in the folder. Day 46 revises it after a written cue.

#### 5 min. Say a claim aloud
Pairs say "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___" with the record in hand, one minute each. Then switch partners once and say it again.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the quarter slip with the Cedarbank Card, first seen here. Read the card and the ask once. Children write [[s02-E44]].

**Shared shape.** The Brindle model is a claim with a detail; the exit asks which of two new records answers a supporting question. The exit is a new case: the Cedarbank Card.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the six board answers on chart paper; children copy the record from the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you are the partner. No folder: use any two papers about your town.

**Skip.** The side table.

- **5 min. Read.** Read the board question aloud. **Say.** "Who should make the rules in our town?" Expect: any answer; the work is to find evidence.
- **12 min. Choose.** Pick a small question and a paper that answers it.
- **9 min. The record.** [[s02-P44a]] [[s02-P44b]] [[s02-P44c]]
- **5 min. Say it.** "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___." Switch roles.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card and ask once. [[s02-E44]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one record, not two. Longer: say the link in two different ways.

### Words and supports
Words: link, people a rule touches, record, claim, evidence. Frame: "This helps because ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 45 writes one sentence: a claim that names the Local record and the Class record, each with a detail. If many made error W (an answer that does not link) or error V (a liking, not a claim) on day 44's exit, day 45's Return and repair sets the wrong beside a right answer. The records are revised on day 46.

### Credit
Ours. The Brindle and Cedarbank cards are invented for practice. The 1787 fact: National Archives, Constitution of the United States, transcript (source U2B2); the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin: the Tribe's own pages (source U2N).
