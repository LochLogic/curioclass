# Section 2.5 opener: constitutions, our state's and the nation's

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 41 teaches what a constitution is: a basic plan that sets up a government, says what it may and may not do, and may list rights. One safeguard is shown at introductory depth: the speech line of the First Amendment. Congress proposed it, the states ratified it, and it protects speaking freely. Day 42 finds, on our state's own official pages, the year the plan was adopted, one part of the government and one right. Massachusetts 1780 or Colorado 1876 is the worked example, whichever is not our state. Day 43 sets our state beside the United States (three parts, delegates in 1787, ratified by the states) and one other plan, the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin's own constitution, read from its own pages.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a five-minute return and repair, a short retrieval, then teaching or application, a record (practice, kept for day 46), and an exit card (cards-exits-41-43.md).

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A constitution is a plan, not a list of rules.** It sets up who does what, and what may not be done. A class rule list has rules but no parts and no limits.
- **A government has limits.** "The plan says what it may and may not do." "It was always there" removes the makers (AD): delegates wrote the United States plan in 1787 and the states ratified it.
- **States differ.** Ours is read from its own official page. The state's own grade 3 framework calls the Massachusetts plan of 1780 the oldest functioning constitution; say "often called" to stay careful.
- **The Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin is a nation with its own constitution.** It is not a town (I) and not a copy of a state (U). Its government governs today (T). The pages name a Tribal Legislature and a Tribal Judiciary, and an article on separation of powers. Say what the pages say and no more. The people and their governance did not begin with recognition; if restoration comes up, say plainly that federal policy tried to end recognition and members worked to restore it.
- **Rights come with responsibilities, but a right is not a prize** (M).

## Cautions

No costume, ceremony, sacred material, identity simulation or child as spokesperson. No child is asked their heritage. A sensitivity read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) must be finished before day 43 runs. Constitutions change; historic promises did not give equal rights in practice (unit 6 returns to it). Not here: how a bill becomes a law, national powers in detail, the Bill of Rights beyond the speech line, informed action.

## The errors in plain words

H is a constitution taken as a rule list. AB is a government taken to have no limits. R is one state's year taken for every state. M is a right taken as a prize. AD is a plan taken to have no makers. T is a nation taken as past only. I is a nation taken as a town. U is one template taken for every government. F and O come from day 40 and are repaired on days 41 and 42.
