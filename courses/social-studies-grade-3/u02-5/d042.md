# Day 42: Our state's plan

## Teacher page

### Before class
Fill the State card for our state from its own official pages (default Massachusetts: the Massachusetts Legislature's Constitution page, source U2D): the year, three parts, and one right clause in plain words. The exit clause is a different one; keep it on the key. For the Colorado card use source U2F. Keep the teacher key (key-exits-41-43.md) away until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 41's exit slips. Teach the more-missed of error H and error AB, wrong then right, whole class. Error H: "Our class rule list is a constitution" beside "A constitution sets up a government and says what it may not do." Error AB: "A government can do anything" beside "The plan says what it may and may not do." The other letter goes to a fresh card sort later this week.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Review (day 26): the governor is the leader of a state. Review (day 28): a branch is one part of a government with its own job. Read aloud: a state has lawmakers, a governor and judges. Children write [[s02-T42]].

#### 8 min. The method
Say a state has a constitution too. Point to the three-job strip: sets up, may and may not, rights. Model with Colorado's plan, the other state: find when it was adopted, which means made official; one part of its government; one right. Read the Colorado card. Colorado's plan was approved by voters on 1 July 1876. Read one part from the page's headings and say its job. Name the right: its Bill of Rights is the part that lists rights. Each state wrote its own plan in its own year. **Ask.** What year did Colorado's plan come? Expect: 1876.

#### 14 min. Our state
Pairs read the State card for our state; read one clause aloud once. They fill the three boxes: [[s02-P42a]], [[s02-P42b]] and [[s02-P42c]]. First, a side table of up to 6 children for 5 minutes, for the second half of day 40's exit error F repair (equal speaking turns on a fresh case), if needed. Then visit 8 pairs, about 1 minute each; the other 7 pairs are seen on day 43.

#### 6 min. What the right protects
Each child says one sentence to a partner: "People use this right to ___." Say that a right belongs to everyone in the state; nobody earns it.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the State Rights Exit Card, first seen here. Read the card and the first two asks once. Children write [[s02-E42]] and [[s02-E42b]]. Then read the third ask once; children write [[s02-E42c]] in the Exit box on the student page.

**Shared shape.** The Colorado example and our card show a year, a part and a right in use; the exit gives an unused clause and asks what right it protects.

### Other rooms
No printer: read the State card aloud twice; children write the three boxes on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you are the partner. No printer: read the card aloud.

**Skip.** The side table, the visits.

- **3 min. Retrieval.** [[s02-T42]]
- **8 min. The method.** Read the Colorado card. **Say.** "What year did it come?" Expect: 1876.
- **14 min. Our state.** Read our state's card. [[s02-P42a]] [[s02-P42b]] [[s02-P42c]]
- **6 min. What the right protects.** Your child says: "People use this right to ___."
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card and asks once. [[s02-E42]] [[s02-E42b]] [[s02-E42c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: two boxes. Longer: find a second right on the card.

### Words and supports
Words: adopted, state, right, constitution. Frame: "Our state's plan was adopted in ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 43 sets our state beside the United States and one other plan. If many made error R (every state wrote its plan in 1780) or error M (a right is a prize) on day 42's exit, day 43's Return and repair sets it beside a right answer. The notes stay in the folder.
