# Day 43: Three plans side by side

## Teacher page

### Before class
Copy the two plans chart and the Menominee summary card from cards-day43.md. Keep the teacher key (key-exits-41-43.md) away until slips are collected. Name the nation in full each time: the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin. Sources: U2B, U2B2, U2N, U2O.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 42's exit slips. Teach the more-missed of error R and error M, wrong then right, whole class. Error R: "Every state wrote its plan in 1780" beside "Each state wrote its own plan in its own year." Error M: "A right is a prize you earn" beside "A right belongs to everyone in the state." Error M also gets a side table of up to 5 children for 5 more minutes in the Menominee part.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Point to the three-job strip. Children write [[s02-T43]]. A miss goes to the side table.

#### 9 min. Two plans side by side
Fill the first column of the two plans chart with the class: our state's lawmakers, governor and judges, from day 42 and the three baskets. Then the United States: Congress makes laws, the President carries them out, courts decide cases. People at the Constitutional Convention, called delegates, signed it on 17 September 1787; the states ratified it (source U2B2). **Ask.** Did anyone always have these plans? Expect: no, people wrote them and then they were adopted.

#### 11 min. One other plan
Say the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin is a nation that governs itself today. Read the summary card aloud: it has a written constitution, with an article on a Tribal Legislature, another on a Tribal Judiciary, and a part on keeping powers separate; the legislature has nine members elected by Tribal members; the plan has been amended. Credit as on the card. This is a different plan from a state's, set up in the nation's own way. Pairs say how its parts are set up; record on the chart's third column. No costume or ceremony; no child speaks for a nation.

#### 7 min. The table
Children fill [[s02-P43a]], [[s02-P43b]] and [[s02-P43c]] on the day 43 table, 5 minutes. Up to 5 children speak a column at the side table, one minute each. The tables stay in the folder for day 46.

#### 2 min. Close the board
Add the answer to "What limits government?" on the unit 2 question board: a constitution says what a government may and may not do.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Menominee Card, first seen here. Read the card and the first two asks once. Children write [[s02-E43]] and [[s02-E43b]]. Then read the made-up town line and the third ask once; children write [[s02-E43c]] in the Exit box on the student page.

**Shared shape.** The summary card gives a nation's legislature and judiciary article; the exit card gives its court system and asks what it shows.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the chart on the board; children copy three columns.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you are the partner. No chart: draw three columns.

**Skip.** The side table, the baskets.

- **3 min. Retrieval.** [[s02-T43]]
- **9 min. Two plans.** Read our state's card and the United States parts. **Say.** "Did anyone always have these plans?" Expect: no, people wrote them.
- **11 min. One other plan.** Read the Menominee summary. The nation governs today.
- **7 min. The table.** [[s02-P43a]] [[s02-P43b]] [[s02-P43c]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card and asks once. [[s02-E43]] [[s02-E43b]] [[s02-E43c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: two columns. Longer: a power and a limit.

### Words and supports
Words: constitution, delegates, nation, today. Frame: "The nation's plan ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 44 starts the unit's last claim work. If many made error U (every government must copy a state's three parts), error T (a nation taken as past only) or error I (a nation taken as a town) on day 43's exit, day 44's Return and repair sets it beside a right answer. The tables are revised on day 46.
