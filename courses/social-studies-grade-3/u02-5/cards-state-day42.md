# State cards (day 42)

Teacher reads every clause aloud once. Fill the overlay for our state before the day, from the state's own official pages. Each card carries the maker, the page and the date read.

## Our state card, teaching edition (default: Massachusetts)

- Adopted: voters approved the Massachusetts plan in 1780. It is often called one of the oldest working written constitutions in the world.
- Parts it sets up: a legislative part (lawmakers), an executive part (the governor), a judicial part (judges).
- One right: the people have a right to meet together peacefully to talk about the common good and to ask leaders to fix wrongs (Declaration of Rights, Article XIX).
- Source: the Massachusetts Legislature's page for the Constitution (text and rights articles) and the state's History and Social Science Framework (2018), grade 3 topic 6, which gives 1780 and calls it the oldest functioning constitution; read 2026-11-21.

If our state is not Massachusetts, the teacher fills the same three lines from our state's own official page, with an original summary and a short clause.

## Worked example, the other state (default: Colorado 1876)

- Adopted: Colorado's plan was drafted in 1876, approved by voters on 1 July 1876 and took effect with statehood on 1 August 1876.
- Parts it sets up: a General Assembly with two chambers (lawmakers), a governor who leads the executive part, courts.
- One right: its Bill of Rights lists rights people keep.
- Source: the Colorado General Assembly's page and the state constitution.

Say "often called" when you say Massachusetts's plan is among the oldest. Each state wrote its own plan in its own year.
