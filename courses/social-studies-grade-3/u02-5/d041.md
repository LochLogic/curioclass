# Day 41: What a constitution is

## Teacher page

### Before class
Print the Willowmere plan card, the three-job strip, the Council card, someone might say, the speech safeguard strips and the day 41 cards. Keep the teacher key (key-exits-41-43.md) away until slips are collected. Speech line: source U2C.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 40's exit slips. Teach the more-missed of error F and error O, wrong then right, whole class. Error F: "Hugo's friends asked the loudest, so it counts" beside "Each person speaks in turn; the rules are the same." Error O: "Count the popular child three times" beside "One ballot each, counted once." If error O: tally two fresh cases. Case 1: five ballots, each marked once. Case 2: one ballot dropped in twice. Which count? Expect: each ballot once.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Review (grade 1): the Constitution is the paper that says how our government is set up, from 1787. Review (day 28): a branch is one part of a government with its own job. Read the slip once. Children write [[s02-T41]] and [[s02-T41b]]. A miss goes to the side table.

#### 9 min. Teaching first
Say Willowmere is made up. Read its plan: it sets up a council and a mayor; it says what the council may do and may not do; it lists one right. A constitution is a basic plan that sets up a government, says what it may and may not do, and may list rights. Fill the three-job strip with the class: sets up, may and may not, rights. Beside it, read our class rule list: rules, but no parts and no limits. **Ask.** Is our rule list a constitution? Expect: no, it has no parts and no limits. Then read the someone-might-say card and test it against the strip.

#### 10 min. One safeguard
A safeguard is something that protects. The speech safeguard is one line in the Bill of Rights, which Congress proposed in 1789 and the states ratified in 1791. Proposed means suggested. Ratified means the states said yes, so it counted. The line says Congress may not make a law "abridging the freedom of speech", which means cutting it off. Pairs lay the three-card strip in order: Congress proposed it; the states approved it; it became part of the Constitution. **Ask.** Why does this line protect speaking freely? Expect: Congress may not cut it off.

#### 8 min. The card
Children fill [[s02-P41a]], [[s02-P41b]] and [[s02-P41c]] on the day 41 card, 5 minutes. Up to 6 children speak it at the side table, one minute each. The cards stay in the folder for day 46.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the quarter slip with the Council Card, first seen here. Read it once. Children write [[s02-E41]].

#### 2 min. Close
Say tomorrow's work: our own state's plan.

**Shared shape.** The Willowmere card shows a plan's parts and limits; the exit gives a council with no limit and asks why a plan matters.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the strip on the board; children write on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you are the partner. No strip: draw three boxes.

**Skip.** The side table.

- **3 min. Retrieval.** [[s02-T41]] [[s02-T41b]]
- **8 min. What a constitution is.** Read Willowmere. **Say.** "What may the council not do?" Expect: what the plan says it may not.
- **10 min. One safeguard.** Say: Congress proposed the speech line; the states said yes.
- **9 min. The card.** [[s02-P41a]] [[s02-P41b]] [[s02-P41c]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s02-E41]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: skip the retrieval. Longer: draw Willowmere.

### Words and supports
Words: constitution, safeguard, proposed, ratified. Frame: "A constitution ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 42 finds three things in our state's plan. If many made error H (a rule list taken for a constitution) or error AB (a government with no limits) on day 41's exit, day 42's Return and repair sorts four fresh cards. The cards stay in the folder.
