# Day 60: What interest is

## Teacher page

### Before class
Read day 59's slips for error W and error AA and pick the more-missed. Draw the table: Bank Birch pays $2 a year on $100, Bank Cedar pays $6. If math has not yet taught sums within 1,000, show the adding with counters. Have the question board ready: today adds its answer on what financial institutions do. Keep key-exits-56-60.md away until slips are in. The student page stays in the child's folder.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Day 59's slips (s03-E59, s03-E59b, s03-E59c). Whole class, the more-missed. Error W: "a credit union belongs to the bank" beside "its members own it". Error AA: "insurance keeps your savings" beside "insurance pays the losses its agreement lists". Use a fresh card: Fern Credit Union, where neighbors who joined save and borrow. The other, with error N, goes to the side table during Compare two offers.

#### 8 min. Why interest?
Review: you learned interest in grade 1, a little extra a bank pays for keeping savings there. New: interest is payment for using money. A baker (made up) borrows $100 for a year and pays back the $100 and $6. The $6 is interest. Banks lend out savings, and savings accounts normally pay interest; public pages, checked. Interest is not a gift: someone pays it (error AB).
**Ask.** Who pays the interest, and who gets it? Expect: the baker pays; the lender gets it.

#### 10 min. Deposit and interest counters
Each pair lays the $100 card down and puts counters beside it, not on it: the $100 is the deposit, the counters are the interest. The whole $106 is not the interest (error H). Pairs trace a saving: $100 on the card, two counters at Bank Birch for a year. Each child writes [[s03-P60c]] and [[s03-P60d]]. Hear a rotating six pairs.

#### 10 min. Compare two offers
Show the table. Same $100, same year, so compare by dollars: Birch two counters, Cedar six, and Cedar pays $4 more. Offers over different years cannot be compared yet: hold the year equal (error O). Pairs use the $100 card and up to eight counters, then work the next bank. Each child writes [[s03-P60b]]. Visit a rotating six pairs; the side table meets for 5 minutes.

#### 4 min. Say it
"Interest is payment for ___." Partners say it, one minute each way. Each child writes [[s03-P60a]], then one cue on the page for day 66: what I will check. Hear a rotating six. The page stays in the folder.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Oak and Fir cards, first seen here. Read the cards and each ask once. Children write [[s03-E60]] and [[s03-E60b]] on the slip and [[s03-E60c]] in the exit box. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The table compares $2 and $6, then pairs work back from a difference; the exit gives $1 and $3. The Oak and Fir cards are new.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the table on the board; use a sheet of paper for the $100 and bottle caps for dollars.

### At home

**Swap.** No counters: use eight coins from a game. Nothing real is used.
**Skip.** The cue.

- **10 min. Model.** Say: "A baker borrows $100 and pays back $106. The $6 is interest." Lay a card for $100 and six coins beside it.
- **20 min. Banks.** Bank Birch pays $2 a year on $100. Your child writes [[s03-P60c]], [[s03-P60d]], [[s03-P60b]] and [[s03-P60a]].
  **Say.** "Who pays interest, and who gets it?" Expect: the borrower pays; the lender gets it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the cards and each ask once. [[s03-E60]] [[s03-E60b]] [[s03-E60c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: the baker and the net-maker, not the banks. Longer: Bank Cedar pays $6; Bank Pine pays $9; how much more?

### Words and supports
Words: interest, deposit, borrow, lend, pays, same year. Frame: "Interest is payment for ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 61 opens with the more-missed of error H (the whole $106 is the interest) and error AB (interest is a free gift), 5 minutes, with error O at the side table. Day 61 takes no new interest content; it begins what is made here and what comes from far away.
