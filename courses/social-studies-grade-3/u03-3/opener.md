# Section 3.3 opener: limits, choices and money

## What the section does

Five days of 40 minutes. Day 56 shows scarcity and the benefit and cost of a choice, with a bird feeder case. Day 57 ranks three choices, picks one and names the opportunity cost as the next best, and the savings ledger gets its second line. Day 58 sorts rewards and penalties (incentives) and what each might make people do. Day 59 meets banks, credit unions and insurance. Day 60 defines interest and compares two made-up banks.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Scarcity** means limited time or money, so not every choice can be made. A limit is never laziness.
- **Benefit and cost.** A benefit is what a choice gives; a cost can be money or something given up.
- **Opportunity cost** is the next-best choice you give up, not the whole list and not the money price.
- **A reward or penalty might change what people do.** It does not guarantee it. A reward is a gain for choosing something, a penalty a cost for choosing it.
- **A bank keeps savings and lends. A credit union is owned by its members, who elect its board and whose savings become other members' loans. Insurance pools many people's payments to cover the losses its agreement lists.** Insurance is not a savings account and does not cover every loss.
- **Interest** is payment from a borrower to a lender for using money. A bank normally pays interest on savings. Interest is not the whole balance and is not a free gift: someone pays it.

## The errors in plain words

C is the idea that scarcity means laziness. Y is the idea that every choice can be had. D is the idea that the price is the opportunity cost. Z is the idea that every rejected choice is the cost. Q mixes a reward and a penalty. E takes an incentive as a guarantee. W makes a credit union belong to someone besides its members. AA treats insurance as savings. G says a bank gives only gifts. N says insurance covers every loss. H takes the balance for the interest. AB takes interest as a free gift. O compares offers without holding years and amounts equal.

## Supplies

Benefit and cost card, three choice cards, sort cards 1 to 4, the bank cards, a coverage card, the interest cards, a net-maker card, the savings ledger.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn cards, three coins for the ledger and the teacher reading each card aloud. Amounts are made up. No child says what a family spends, saves or owns.
