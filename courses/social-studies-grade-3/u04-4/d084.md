# Day 84: A nation that governs itself

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 83's exit by error G (every nation shared the same thing) or O (shared long ago, and that is all). Print the table, strip and card. Have the unit 2 cards for our town and state to hand. Keep key-exits-83-85.md out until slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 83's slips (s04-E83, s04-E83b) and written task (s04-P83a to s04-P83f), each marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Spend four minutes on the more-missed of error G or O. G: "Every nation shared the same thing" beside "In the Penobscot Nation's Homeland unit, students study maps with place names in Penobscot and learn what they mean, its page says." O: "They shared names long ago, and that is all" beside "That unit is on the Nation's page now." The other letter meets you at the side table. Review (unit 2): government means the leaders and workers who make a community's rules.

#### 8 min. Sovereignty
New word. Say it once: "Sovereignty is a nation's right to govern itself." Governments talk to one another, and laws set some limits; those are yours to hold, not this lesson's. If asked, say you will find out. Read the Eastern Band card aloud twice. Its page says Cherokee is a sovereign nation with its own laws, elections, government and institutions. Children write [[s04-P84a]]. Do not use the Raleigh visit; it is the exit.

#### 10 min. Compare four governments
Pairs fill the governments table for the Eastern Band, the Navajo Nation, our town and our state: who leads, who decides, who it speaks for. Use only what each card says; where silent write "the page does not say." Do not make them look alike. Children write [[s04-P84b]]. Hear six pairs. On the question board answer "Who governs today?" Children write [[s04-P84e]].

#### 15 min. Say who each government speaks for
Each child fills the strip with whom a tribal, a state and the federal government each speak for: its own people, the people of the state, the people of the country. Children write [[s04-P84c]] and [[s04-P84d]]. Mark as before: the reason must name self-government (laws, elections), not size.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Eastern Band Notice, first seen here. Read it and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E84]] and [[s04-E84b]]. Key: key-exits-83-85.md. Mark as before.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice compare what pages say about four governments and fill a strip; the exit gives one new notice and asks what shows a government acting, and whether a law changed.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the table and strip on the board; read the card aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed table: draw a three-row table by hand.

**Skip.** The town and state columns.

- **8 min. The card.** Read the Eastern Band card. Say sovereignty once. [[s04-P84a]] **Say.** "What does sovereign mean?" Expect: it governs itself.
- **12 min. The table.** [[s04-P84b]] [[s04-P84e]]
- **15 min. The strip.** [[s04-P84c]] [[s04-P84d]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the notice once. [[s04-E84]] [[s04-E84b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: the strip only, [[s04-P84c]] and [[s04-P84d]]. Longer: add a column for our school.

### Words and supports
Words: sovereignty, government, tribal, state, federal, leader. Frame: "A tribal government is a government because ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 85 asks questions of two sources on one topic and lays events before and after. It opens with error F (a tribal government is a town department) if many made it today.
