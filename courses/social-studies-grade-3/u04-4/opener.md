# Section 4.4 opener: what nations share, how they govern, how sources agree

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 83 reads what a nation near us shares today, from the nation's own page, and a short original biography of a language master of the Penobscot Nation. Day 84 teaches sovereignty, a nation's right to govern itself, and compares named governments without forcing one pattern on them. Day 85 asks questions of two sources on one topic, checks whether they agree, and lays six events before and after one another.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A contribution is continuing knowledge and work,** not a gift owed to settlers. Each card names the nation, the kind of contribution and the source. A contribution does not belong to every nation at once.
- **The biography says only what its source says:** her role and her teaching today. It does not guess at her family or at sacred knowledge.
- **Sovereignty means a nation's right to govern itself.** Nations existed before the United States and still govern. Governments talk to one another. Legal limits exist, and the teacher holds them.
- **Compare real governments.** The Eastern Band's page names a Principal Chief and a Tribal Council. The Navajo Nation's page names Council delegates in the Council Chambers. Do not impose one pattern.
- **Asking starts the checking.** A question is not agreement. Two copies of one account are one teller. Two accounts made by separate people that agree are stronger.

## The errors in plain words

G is saying every nation gave the same thing. U is saying Native peoples gave nothing useful. O is saying the sharing is past only. F is calling a tribal government a town department. E is saying a question proves an answer. Q is a question about looks only.

## Supplies

The River card, the Dana card, four contribution cards, the contribution table, the Eastern Band card, the governments table, the authority strip, the Bridge Day cards, six event cards and the exit cards for days 83 to 85. The two day 78 Cherokee cards are used again on day 85.

## A room with nothing

Read each card aloud. Draw the table on the board. Children point to a card and say its nation. A spoken answer with a named source is a full answer. No child is asked about home or family.
