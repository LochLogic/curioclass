# Day 83: contribution cards

Read each card aloud. Each names its nation and its source. Present tense. Say: "The Nation shares this, its page says."

**Card 1. The River card.** The Penobscot Nation shares a River unit on its own curriculum page. The unit teaches that the Penobscot River is the heart of Penobscot culture. It teaches what the Penobscots have used rivers for: food, drinking water and travel. Source: the Penobscot Nation's own curriculum page.

**Card 2. Carol A. Dana.** Carol A. Dana is a language master with the Penobscot Nation's Cultural and Historic Preservation Department. For decades she has taught the Penobscot language to young learners and to adults. She helped make a standard writing system. She helped make a Penobscot-English dictionary. She recorded audio lessons. Source: a University of Maine news page, 1 April 2026.

**Card 3. The cedar-bark basket.** Norma Hottowe Pendleton, Makah, made a canoe basket of cedar bark in 1984 to 1985. It is plaited in a twill pattern. Source: the National Museum of the American Indian catalog record.

**Card 4. Wild rice.** David Grignon, the tribal historic preservation officer of the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin, says in a 2014 interview that in the fall they had wild rice. Source: Wisconsin Public Television, 25 August 2014.

The before, action, after boxes are filled on the board for the River card and the Dana card.
