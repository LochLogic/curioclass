# Day 109: Servants and enslaved people

## Teacher page

### Before class
This day tells hard history. Put a stop card on every desk and say at the start that any child may point to it, or pass, at any time, with no reason. No role play and no acting: no child speaks as either person. Hand back day 108's written tasks with a cue. Update the question board, five supporting questions. Print the exit slip.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (G from E108)
If many missed day 108's exit (error G, one picture is every home), keep all 5 minutes here; if few, those children use the side table. Read the made-up claim: "Every family had one room." Read the made-up caption: one house with two rooms. Each child writes [[s05-W109a]].

#### 5 min. Words
Review: you learned the words enslaved people and enslavers in grade 2; we never say "slaves" or "owners". Say plainly: slavery was legal then, and it was cruel. New: a servant is a worker bound to serve for a set time, and the set time is a service term. Held for life and denied freedom are new today: an enslaved person was held for life and denied freedom.

#### 10 min. A servant
Read the made-up card: Ned, a servant, has a four-year term, daily work, and is free to leave when it ends. He and the family he serves agreed on the four years. Fill the Work Terms Table row with the class: how long, who decides, free after. Each child writes [[s05-P109a]].

#### 10 min. Enslaved people
Say: enslaved people were held for life and denied freedom. In Boston they worked in homes, shops, farming, shipbuilding and at sea. Before 1700 most enslaved people in Boston were Native American. No real person's record is read. Fill the same table row for enslaved people: the enslaver decided. Each child writes [[s05-P109b]]. **Ask.** "How is Ned's line different?" Expect: a set time, then free; the other was for life.

#### 8 min. Pairs: sort
Pairs read six made-up cards and write S for a service term or E for enslaved. Repair a wrong sort on the spot with the table. 1 Dara works four years to pay a debt, then leaves. 2 Eli is held for life. 3 Fenn works three years, then is free. 4 Gale is denied freedom and cannot leave. 5 Hope serves a set term and then starts a shop. 6 Ira is held for life and cannot choose where to live. Each child writes [[s05-P109c]]. Hear a rotating six pairs, about 45 seconds each, and list their names.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the New Work Terms Card, new today. Read it once. Each child writes [[s05-E109]]. Key: key-exits-106-110.md.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the table on the board; a stop card is a folded paper with a hand drawn on it.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you are the partner, and your child may stop at any time.

**Skip.** The pairs.

- **6 min. Repair.** [[s05-W109a]] **Say.** "Does one house show every home?" Expect: no.
- **13 min. Two lives.** Read the words and Ned's card. [[s05-P109a]] [[s05-P109b]]
- **11 min. Sort.** [[s05-P109c]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E109]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the table rows and the exit. Longer: say how Ned's line differs from the enslaved line.

### Words and supports
Words: servant, service term, enslaved people, enslavers, held for life, denied freedom. Frame: "A servant ___. An enslaved person ___." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 110 repairs error Q (servant and enslaved are the same) by filling the table again from two new cards, then compares life then and now.
