# Section 5.5 opener: spreading colonies, leaders, daily life, work, then and now

## What the section does

Five days of 40 minutes. Day 106: why Puritans came, how the colonies spread, and four effects on Native nations with each cause kept apart. Day 107: leaders compared, Ousamequin, John Winthrop and Metacom. Day 108: homes and schooling in an early colony. Day 109: servants and enslaved people. Day 110: what changed and what stayed.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Puritans.** The Winthrop Fleet carried about 1,000 colonists to Massachusetts in 1630. By the mid-1640s more than 20,000 colonists lived in the colony. They wanted their religious practices free from the king and the Anglican Church. Belief is described, not judged (sources.csv U5Z). Puritans are a different group from the Pilgrims of day 100. John Winthrop led the first wave in 1630 and was governor 12 of the colony's first 20 years (U5Z).
- **Spreading.** Groups of families petitioned for land grants of roughly 40 square miles, and new towns spread inland from the coast (U5Z). Name the nations the pages name, in the present tense: the Massachusett (U5H) and the Wampanoag (U5E, U5F).
- **Four effects.** Disease wiped out whole villages and land shrank after English arrival (Aquinnah page, U5F). Conversion: praying towns were villages for Native people who had converted, set up by the minister John Eliot, often on the colony's border; the towns followed Puritan law and had schools that taught English (U5AA, Britannica). One of them, Ponkapoag, was a self-governing praying town from 1657, and the Massachusett Tribe at Ponkapoag lives and governs today (U5H). War: after King Philip's War survivors fled north and settled among the Abenaki (U5G). Do not use the 1675 Mashpee entry, which was the E103 exit. Do not describe the Deer Island confinement. Name King Philip's War once, with no battle detail.
- **Metacom** (also Metacomet, called King Philip) was sachem of the Wampanoag from 1662 to 1676, was a son of Massasoit, and led his people through the war of 1675 to 1678, which is named for him (U5AC, Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe). His reasons are not on our page, so none are taught.
- **Houses and school.** The houses at the museum village are timber-framed re-creations of what the first houses may have looked like (U5W). Boston Latin School was founded in 1635. A 1642 law required parents to see that children learned to read. Most children learned with a hornbook. Schooling was aimed mainly at boys (U5X).
- **Servants and enslaved people.** Slavery was legal in Massachusetts. The date of the law is held back for the day 120 law card (U5R); do not teach it. Enslaved Bostonians worked in homes, shops, farming, shipbuilding and at sea. Before 1700 most enslaved people in Boston were Native American (U5R). Say plainly that slavery was cruel. No role play, no simulation. A stop card on each desk.

## The errors in plain words

K is thinking a new colony replaced an older one at once. N is giving one cause for every effect. F is calling a seizure a choice. AC is thinking one leader rules all. G is treating one picture as every home. Q is treating servant and enslaved as the same. AB is a child describing their own home. O is naming only changes, never what stayed.
