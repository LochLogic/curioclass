# Day 106: Why Puritans came, and what followed

## Teacher page

### Before class
Write one note and one letter (shown, shown with help, not yet seen) on each sheet of day 105's assessment. Draw on the wall strip the bands Colony T (1590 to 1640) and Colony U (1620 to 1650), both made up. Draw a coast with Salem as a dot and a blank inland area. Print the exit slip. Keep key-exits-106-110.md away until slips are in.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (K from E104 5, mid-unit response 5)
K, 5 minutes: If many missed day 104's exit (error K, a new colony ended the older one), keep this whole part on it. Pairs find T and U on the strip. Each child writes [[s05-W106a]]. If few missed it, those children use the side table.
Response, 5 minutes: each child reads the note and letter on the day 105 sheet and fixes the one cell the note names in colored pencil, writing [[s05-W106b]]. A child whose note names a 0 meets the teacher with a fresh map or strip. The fixes stay in the folder.

#### 6 min. Why Puritans came
Review: you learned about the Pilgrims on day 100, the church group on the Mayflower. Add a 1630 band for Massachusetts Bay to the strip. Read: Puritans were colonists who wanted their religious practices free from the king and the Anglican Church. About 20,000 came in the 1630s. They were a different group from the Pilgrims. Belief is described, not judged. Post "What undermined it?" from day 90 on the question board. Each child writes [[s05-P106b]].

#### 7 min. How they spread
Say: groups of families asked for land, and new towns spread from the coast inland. The Massachusett and Wampanoag nations lived there then and live today. Trace one arrow with the class: tail at the Salem dot, head inland. **Ask.** "Which way does the arrow point?" Expect: inland, away from the sea. Each child writes [[s05-P106c]].

#### 10 min. Four effects, four causes
Pairs fill the cause chart. Read each card once. Review: you learned a cause and an effect on day 93 ("Because ___, they moved ___."). Cards: disease wiped out whole villages, caused by contact with newcomers; land shrank, caused by new towns claiming it; praying towns, villages that the minister John Eliot set up for Native people who had converted, with Puritan law and schools that taught English, caused by the minister's plan; one war, named once, King Philip's War, taught in grade 5, with survivors fleeing north to live among the Abenaki. Say: the Massachusett Tribe at Ponkapoag governs itself today; its praying town governed itself from 1657. No religion is judged. **Ask.** "Did the nations choose disease or land loss?" Expect: no; each came from what the newcomers brought or did. One cause for one effect. Hear a rotating six pairs, about 45 seconds each, and list their names. Each child writes [[s05-P106d]].

#### 5 min. Record
Each child writes [[s05-P106a]]. The teacher writes a cue after class; practice, revised on day 119.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the New Cause Card, new today. Read it once. Each child writes [[s05-E106]]. Key: key-exits-106-110.md.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the chart on the board and read the cards aloud once.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall strip: draw two lines on paper.

**Skip.** The strip and the pairs.

- **8 min. Strip.** Draw 1590 to 1640 and 1620 to 1650 as two lines. **Say.** "Was the first one there when the second began?" Expect: yes, 1620 to 1640. [[s05-W106a]]
- **10 min. Why and where.** Read the page aloud. [[s05-P106b]] [[s05-P106c]]
- **12 min. Effects.** Read the cards. [[s05-P106d]] [[s05-P106a]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E106]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the strip, the chart and the exit. Longer: each child draws the arrow on their own map.

### Words and supports
Words: Puritan, effect, cause, praying town. Frame: "One effect was ___ because ___." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow
