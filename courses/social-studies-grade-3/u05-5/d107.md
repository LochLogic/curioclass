# Day 107: Leaders compared

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hand back day 106's written tasks and exit slips with a cue. Make two leader record cards per pair. John Winthrop: Massachusetts Bay; led the first wave of 1630; governor 12 of the colony's first 20 years. Metacom, also called King Philip: Wampanoag; sachem from 1662 to 1676; son of Massasoit; led his people through the war of 1675 to 1678, named for him; the page gives no reason, so the why box stays blank. Boxes: role, decision, why, answers to.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (N and F from E106)
If many missed day 106's exit (error N, one cause for every effect; error F, the nation chose it), all 10 minutes go here; if both, 5 each. If few, those children use the side table. Say: made up, a town's well dried up and a barn burned. Each child writes [[s05-W107a]]. Then pairs say who caused each loss and who chose it: the people in the town did not choose the dry well. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each.

#### 4 min. Retrieval
Review: on day 98 you learned that two people can see one event from two sides (perspective), and on day 103 about Ousamequin, the Great Sachem of the Wampanoag. **Ask.** "Who saw the 1621 visit from the Wampanoag side?" Expect: Ousamequin.

#### 8 min. Ousamequin's life
Read aloud: Ousamequin was the Great Sachem of the Wampanoag in 1620. English records call him Massasoit. Sachems cared for their people, settled disputes and oversaw talks with other groups. In summer 1621 two colonists visited his town, Pokanoket, to cement an alliance. This is a short biography, a second source beside the colonial record. Review: before, action and after boxes (grade 3, unit 4). Fill them with the class. After: the Wampanoag live today; the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah) has an elected council and teaches the Wampanoag language. Each child writes [[s05-P107c]].

#### 10 min. Pairs: Winthrop and Metacom
Pairs fill each leader card from the facts above. **Ask.** "In which years did each lead?" Expect: Winthrop in the 1630s and 1640s; Metacom in the 1670s. They led in different years, so they were not talking with each other. Hear a rotating six pairs, about 45 seconds each, and list their names. Each child writes [[s05-P107a]].

#### 6 min. Record
Each child writes [[s05-P107b]]. Frame: "Winthrop ___ because ___. Metacom ___." Practice; cue after class; revised on day 119.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the New Leader Pair, new today. Read it once. Each child writes [[s05-E107]]. Key: key-exits-106-110.md.

**Shared shape.** The pair cards name a role, a choice and whom each leader answers to; the exit gives two made-up leaders and asks how their choices compare.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep one biography and the exit. Longer: add one thing Ousamequin's people did.

### Words and supports
Words: leader, sachem, answer to. Frame: "Winthrop ___ because ___. Metacom ___." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers.
