# Day 91: A timeline with equal spaces

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hang the wall strip, 1500 to 1700, one mark for ten years (1500 is mark 0). Cut the Alder Bay cards. Write the Pine Street Bench card on the board. Keep the keys away until slips are in.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair, with retrieval
Hand back day 90's slips and record (s05-E90, s05-E90b, s05-P90a, s05-P90b, s05-P90c, s05-P90d) marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. 5 minutes of retrieval: review (grade 2), you put events in order and asked someone who was there. **Ask.** "The record says beans were planted in May and picked in September. What happened first? Who could tell us about it?" Expect: planting; someone who was there. If many miss it, put two picture cards in order and ask again. 5 minutes for Y: the Pine Street Bench card (made up): the town plans a bench at the bus stop. Pairs name a group it touches, and why. If few made error Y on day 90's exit, they work this at the side table while the rest do retrieval. If both are many, retrieval takes all 10 and Y moves to the pairs part.

#### 4 min. Hook: two strips
Show the four Alder Bay cards (made up): market field 1520, first bridge 1570, mill 1590, school 1670. Strip A: the cards one hand apart each. Strip B: spaced by years, one mark for ten years. **Ask.** "Which strip shows how long there was between them?" Expect: strip B. Show the wrong beside the right: "spaced one hand apart each" beside "1570 to 1590 is two marks and 1590 to 1670 is eight."

#### 9 min. The strip
Place the four cards on the wall strip, counting tens aloud: 1520 at mark 2, 1570 at 7, 1590 at 9, 1670 at 17. **Ask.** "How many tens are between the bridge and the mill?" Expect: two tens, twenty years. Equal space means equal years, so the space tells how long. Children copy two cards onto their own strips: [[s05-P91a]].

#### 7 min. Decades and centuries
New words: a decade is one mark, ten years; a century is ten decades, a hundred years. Count the hundreds, then one more: 1800 to 1899 has eighteen hundreds done, so it is the nineteenth century (source U5M). Show the wrong, "The 1800s are the eighteenth century," beside that right. Pairs say the century aloud for 1815, 1999 and 2026. Expect: nineteenth, twentieth, twenty-first. Hear six pairs.

#### 8 min. Pairs
Each child marks three Pell Harbor cards (made up) on a strip: dock 1515, school 1545, bakery 1695. A year ending in 5 sits halfway between two marks. [[s05-P91b]] Pairs say how many tens apart each two cards are; each writes [[s05-P91c]]. Hear a rotating six pairs and list their names.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out a fresh strip and the quarter slip with the Hollis Cove Strip Card, new today. Read it once. Each child marks both cards on the strip and hands both in: [[s05-E91]]. Key: key-exits-90-92.md.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice mark made-up cards on a ten-year strip and count tens; the exit is a new town's two cards, marked alone on a fresh strip.

### Other rooms
No printer: rule a strip on paper with 21 marks.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall strip: use the paper strip on the table.

**Skip.** The class talk and the pair round; you are the partner.

- **8 min. Remember.** Read the beans question. **Say.** "What happened first?" Expect: planting.
- **10 min. The strip.** Place two Alder Bay cards: [[s05-P91a]]. **Say.** "How many tens between 1570 and 1590?" Expect: two tens.
- **12 min. Pell Harbor.** [[s05-P91b]] [[s05-P91c]] Say the century of 1815: the nineteenth.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E91]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: use two Pell Harbor cards. Longer: a second strip for a town of your own, made up.

### Words and supports
Words: decade, century, strip, mark. Frame: "___ is ___ tens after ___." Pointing and a scribe are answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow
Day 92 practises placing events and centuries. It opens with error A (cards spaced one step apart, whatever the years) from today's exit, building a fresh strip.
