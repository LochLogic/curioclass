# Day 90: Our big question

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hang the question board: big question, five blank lines. Print the supporting-question record and the half exit slip; set out record folders. Keep key-exits-90-92.md away until slips are in. Keep a class list.

### The day

#### 5 min. Retrieval (grade 2, why people move)
Hand back day 89's redo cards, read and never scored: s04-F89a, s04-F89b, s04-F89c, s04-F89d, s04-F89e, s04-F89f, s04-F89g, s04-F89h, s04-F89i, s04-F89j, s04-F89k, s04-F89l, s04-F89m, s04-F89n, s04-F89o, s04-F89p, s04-F89q, s04-F89r. Review (grade 2): "Because ___, they moved ___." Read the Okafor card (a family chose to move for a new job) and the Lindqvist card (a family had to leave when the town closed their building), both made up. **Ask.** "Why might a family move, and how is being made to move different?" Expect: for work or safety; being made to move takes away choice. If many miss it, set the cards side by side; ask again.

#### 7 min. Hook and the word colony
Say once: a colony is a settlement far from the home country, which claims it; a colonist settles in one. Judge no one. Show the shore card (a drawn shoreline, a town, a boat). Read once: "In 1565 Spanish colonists settled at Seloy, a Timucua town in what is now Florida." Pairs talk for 2 minutes: how could settlement change life for people already there? A child may pass. Native nations live and govern today. Write the big question on the board.

#### 8 min. Co-write the board
Review (unit 1): the question board. Turn five prompts into children's questions, each with its answer day: why sail west (93); whose account and map (95 to 96); what made cooperation possible (103); what undermined it (106); who worked and who benefited (109 to 116).

#### 4 min. Worked record (made up)
Review (unit 2): the record. Say the case is made up. Fill one aloud for "Could skaters and walkers share the new path?" Question: who uses it most? Fact needed: when each group is there. Two readings of "share": use the path together, or each keep a part. Link: the answer tells which could work.

#### 10 min. Each child's record
Each child picks one board question and fills the four boxes alone: [[s05-P90a]] and [[s05-P90b]]. Confer with a rotating six, a minute each, and list their names. A child may say it aloud while you write.

#### 4 min. Matters to others
Review (unit 1): "This matters to ___ because ___." Show the wrong beside the right: "Only our teacher needs the answer" beside "The people who walk the path need it, so they know if it will be safe." Pairs say who else needs our big question answered, and why. Each writes [[s05-P90c]] and [[s05-P90d]], kept in the record folder.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Mill Creek Dock Card, new today. Read it once. Every child writes [[s05-E90]] and [[s05-E90b]]. Key: key-exits-90-92.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice use the made-up path and our big question; the exit is a new dock card and asks which other group it touches and why.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the dock card on the board; children answer on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No class board: write the big question on paper.

**Skip.** The pair talk and the class board; you are the partner.

- **5 min. Moving.** Read the Okafor and Lindqvist cards. **Say.** "Why might a family move, and how is being made to move different?" Expect: for work or safety; being made to move takes away choice.
- **8 min. Seloy.** Say colony. Read the Seloy line. **Say.** "How could settlement change life for people already there?" Expect: any honest idea.
- **18 min. Your record.** [[s05-P90a]] [[s05-P90b]] [[s05-P90c]] [[s05-P90d]]
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Read the Mill Creek card once. [[s05-E90]] [[s05-E90b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: hear fewer board offers. Longer: a second record for another board question.

### Words and supports
Words: colony, colonist, settle, share. Frame: "This matters to ____________ because ______________________________." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow
Day 91 builds a timeline. It opens by handing back today's slips and repairing error Y (only our teacher needs the answer) with a fresh question card.

### Credit
Ours. The Seloy fact is in our words from the Florida Museum's St. Augustine timeline. The families, the path and the dock card are made up.
