# Section 5.1 opener: the big question and the timeline

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 90 opens "Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?" The class writes five supporting questions with their answer days, and says who besides the class needs the answers. Day 91 builds a timeline with equal spaces for equal years. Day 92 practices placing events, saying which came first and how far apart, and counting centuries.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A colony** is a settlement far from the home country that the home country claims. Say it as a word for a kind of place, never as a story of one place yet. Plymouth and the other places come in their turn.
- **Whose homeland a settlement entered** is the first thing the question asks. A town named Seloy, home to Timucua people, stood where the Spanish settled in 1565 (sources.csv U5A). Say the nation's name, in plain words.
- **A timeline is a scale.** One mark means ten years. Equal spaces mean equal years, not equal numbers of events. A decade is ten years, a century is a hundred.
- **Counting centuries.** Count the hundreds, then one more. 1815 is in the nineteenth century (sources.csv U5M).
- **Sharing is read two ways:** use the land together, or each keep a part.

## The errors in plain words

Y is the idea that only the teacher needs our answer. A is spacing cards evenly when the years are not even. AA is calling the 1500s the fifteenth century.

## Supplies

The wall strip, 1500 to 1700, ten-year marks (rolled paper works). A paper strip for each pair. The question board. The supporting-question record (blank-day90-record.md). The card sets in cards-day91-strips.md and cards-day92-rounds.md, and the exit cards in cards-exits-90-92.md. No tool. No photograph: the shore image the plan named is cut, because no free picture with a rights line was read. Use the drawn shoreline on the day 90 card.

## A room with nothing

A strip on rolled shelf paper or a string with pegs, one mark each hand-span. Fold paper strips and mark them with a ruler. Cut the cards from index paper. Say aloud that every case with a made-up town is made up. No child is asked about their own family's history.

## Unsettled facts for the day writers

The shore fact on day 90 is only this: in 1565 Spanish colonists under Pedro Menendez settled at Seloy, a Timucua town (U5A). Nothing else about Seloy is taught on day 90; the food line is held back for day 97 (U5R2).
