# Day 76: Why there?

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 75's exit by error A (states named as nations) and T (all the shading taken as lands held today). Draw the chain strip on the board. Keep key-exits-75-76.md out until the slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 75's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on the more-missed of A or T, wrong answer beside right. A: "Maine, Rhode Island and Massachusetts are the three nations" beside "Penobscot, Narragansett and Wampanoag are nations; Maine is a state." T: "The whole shaded area is where the Tribe lives today" beside "The square marks lands held today; the shading is the homeland." Whole class if many; the other letter meets you at the side table during the river case.

#### 8 min. Settlement explanation
New today: a seasonal camp is a place people stay in one season. Review (grade 2): to settle is to make a home in a place and stay. Read the Narragansett card aloud with its credit; the page tells of earlier times, and the Tribe lives and governs today. Model the chain on the board: the feature is the shore; the practice is moving to the shore in summer; so the place is a summer home near the shore. Say: "People moved to the shore in summer, so they lived near the shore." The shore alone did not decide: people and the shore together explain it. Where the card is silent, we write "the page does not say."

#### 10 min. River case
Read the Penobscot River card aloud with its credit. Pairs fill the chain strip: the feature, the river; the practice, what the page says people used it for; the place. The card does not say where people lived, so the place box says "the page does not say." Move among pairs and hear six, a minute each. Write the page's words for the practice on the board. Children write [[s04-P76c]].

#### 15 min. Explain connections
Each child fills a second strip for the shore. Write: [[s04-P76a]] and [[s04-P76b]]. Then one sentence: [[s04-P76d]]. The two features are a coast and a river; do not list things people ate or made. Pointing, drawing arrows and a scribe are all answers.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Alder Bend Card, first seen here. Read the card and the ask once. Every child writes [[s04-E76]]. Key: key-exits-75-76.md. The written strips are judged for a feature from the page, a practice from the page, and no claim that the feature alone decided; mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice go from a page's practice to where people lived; the exit gives a made-up modern town and asks how a river connects two places.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the two cards on the board and draw the strip by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: read the two cards from the teacher's page aloud.

**Skip.** The second strip.

- **8 min. The shore.** Read the Narragansett card. **Say.** "What did people do, and where did they live?" Expect: moved to the shore in summer.
- **10 min. The river.** Read the Penobscot card. [[s04-P76c]]
- **15 min. Your chain.** [[s04-P76a]] [[s04-P76b]] [[s04-P76d]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E76]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one strip, the shore; keep [[s04-P76a]] and [[s04-P76d]]. Longer: write a chain for a place in our region where a page says what people did.

### Words and supports
Words: feature, practice, seasonal camp, settle, the page does not say. Frame: "People ___, so they lived near ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
