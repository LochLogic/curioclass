# Day 75: Three nations, three marks

## Teacher page

### Before class
Day 74 ended section 4.1. The course check (days 69 to 71) says who needs the map key again; count them. Keep key-exits-75-76.md out until the slips are in. No child is asked whether they or their family belong to a nation.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 74's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark. Review (day 7): the legend trace. Show the fresh map (a made-up island). Say: "The map shows ___. I can find it because the legend says ___." Pairs say it for the marsh and the hill. If more than a third needed the map key on the course check, teach it to the whole class now; otherwise a small group meets you while the rest repeat the trace.

#### 8 min. Three regional nations
Read each card aloud twice with its credit. Each nation is named by its own name and lives and governs today: the Penobscot Nation, the Narragansett Indian Tribe, the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah). Say the three names aloud twice with the class. Set the state names apart: Maine, Rhode Island, Massachusetts are states. **Ask.** Which word is a nation, Penobscot or Maine? Expect: Penobscot. Then answer the board question: "Whose homelands are here?" These three are the ones our cards name. Other nations live in our region too.

#### 10 min. Homeland and present-land maps
Show the three-mark legend. New today: present lands are the lands a nation holds today. On the Penobscot map, a teaching sketch, shading marks the river area the Penobscots have relied on (approximate, no hard edge; the Nation's own map is the authority). The square marks Indian Island, where the Nation's offices are today; it is one place, not the lands the Nation holds. The dashed line is a state border, another map. Review (grade 2): a border is where one place ends. Pairs trace: "The shading shows ___; the square shows ___." Hear six pairs, a minute each. Then each pair writes [[s04-P75a]].

#### 15 min. Name from the card
Pairs reread the cards. Each child answers from a detail on a card: Chief and Council, a winter and a summer home, the island of Noepe. Write the nation's name: [[s04-P75b]], [[s04-P75c]], [[s04-P75d]]. Then ring the river-area mark on the Penobscot map: [[s04-P75e]]. Pointing, a scribe and speech are all answers. Walk the room.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the exit map, first seen here. Read the two panels and asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E75a]] and [[s04-E75b]]. Key: key-exits-75-76.md.

**Shared shape.** The practice names a nation from a detail on its card and rings a mark on the Penobscot map; the exit names nations from placed dots and reads a new made-up map.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the legend and a copy of the Penobscot map on the board; children use paper markers.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: say the three names and one detail for each.

**Skip.** Ringing the river-area mark.

- **8 min. Three nations.** Read the cards. **Say.** "Which are nations, which are states?" Expect: Penobscot, Narragansett and Wampanoag are nations.
- **10 min. Three marks.** Read the legend. [[s04-P75a]]
- **15 min. Name them.** [[s04-P75b]] [[s04-P75c]] [[s04-P75d]] [[s04-P75e]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the map once. [[s04-E75a]] [[s04-E75b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: name two nations; keep [[s04-P75b]] and [[s04-P75d]]. Longer: find a fourth nation near us on its own page.

### Words and supports
Words: nation, homeland, present lands, legend, state, border. Frame: "The shading shows ___; the square shows ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 76 explains how rivers, coasts and land placed towns and seasonal camps. It opens with whichever of error A (states named as nations) or T (all the shading taken as lands held today) more children made today.
