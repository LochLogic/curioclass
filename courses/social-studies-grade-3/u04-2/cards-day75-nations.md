# Day 75: three regional nations

Teacher reads each card aloud twice with its credit. Each nation is named by its own name. Say the three names aloud twice. State names are set apart.

**Card 1. The Penobscot Nation.** The Penobscot Nation is in Maine. It calls the Penobscot River the heart of its culture. For thousands of years its people have relied on Maine's rivers for food, drinking water and travel. Today the Nation has a Chief and a Council. Its offices are at Indian Island, Maine. Credit: the Penobscot Nation's own pages.

**Card 2. The Narragansett Indian Tribe.** The Narragansett Indian Tribe is a federally recognized Tribal Nation in Rhode Island. Its early history tells of two homes: a winter home and a summer home. In summer the tribe moved to the shore. Credit: the Narragansett Indian Tribe's own Early History and Tribal Government pages.

**Card 3. The Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah).** The Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah) is on the island of Noepe. Many people call the island Martha's Vineyard. Wampanoag ancestors have lived at Aquinnah and across Noepe for at least 10,000 years. The Tribe's lands today are at the southwestern end of the island. Credit: the Tribe's own page.

Nations: Penobscot, Narragansett, Wampanoag. States: Maine, Rhode Island, Massachusetts.
