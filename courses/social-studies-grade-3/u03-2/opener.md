# Section 3.2 opener: money, profit and the businesses near us

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 53 shows why a community uses money: a three-person swap fails when nobody wants what the next person has, and a payment solves it. The class also opens the unit's made-up savings ledger. Day 54 teaches profit (what is left after costs), the idea that a seller hopes for a profit when choosing what to make, and one NPS-based account of Maggie L. Walker in three boxes (before, action, after). Day 55 builds the Local Production Record from our own town's businesses and uses the three baskets again.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Money makes exchange easier.** A trade without money needs a match, where each person wants what the other has. Money means a maker can sell to one person and buy from another. Money does not mean nobody gives anything up: paying gives up money. Barter is shown as one comparison; the plan never says every people once swapped goods this way.
- **Sales are the money buyers pay. Profit is what is left after costs.** Profit is a count and says nothing about a person. A seller hopes for a profit, and that hope can shape what she makes and what she asks. A hope is not a promise of sales.
- **Maggie L. Walker** (a real person): the National Park Service says the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank opened in 1903 and she was its first president, that she urged members to pool their money and lend it among themselves, and that Richmond then was a Jim-Crow city where she led for civil rights. The account names no further barrier and invents none.
- **Local businesses** are read from our own town's pages. A table row names what the business makes or does, whether it is a good or a service, and one natural, one human and one capital resource.

## The errors in plain words

B is the idea that money means nobody gives up anything. F is the idea that sales are profit. M is the idea that the highest sale price gives the best result. AD is the idea that saved money is gone, which the ledger answers: it is kept. V, A and E return from 3.1 and 3.3.

## Supplies

Wants cards (made-up people), a fresh trade card, the savings ledger blank, cost and sales cards, the Walker boxes, the Local Production Record (two made-up cards, or cards from two or more of our town's business pages), a survey row blank.

## A room with nothing

Paper strips for the wants cards, a hand-drawn ledger on the board, coins from a jar, and the teacher reading each card aloud.

No child reports on home, family money or a parent's job. Every amount is made up and is labeled so. No child is asked what they have or what they save.
