# Day 54: Hoping for profit

## Teacher page

### Before class
Read day 53's slips for error B and error AD and pick the more-missed. Copy the account from cards-day54-walker.md and read it twice before class. Keep key-exits-53-55.md away until slips are in. The student page stays in the child's folder.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Day 53's slips (s03-E53) and the class savings ledgers (s03-P53d). Whole class, the more-missed. Error B: "money makes the boots free" beside "paying gives up money, so someone gives up something". Error AD: "saved money disappears" beside "saved money is kept for the goal". The other goes to the side group in the counters work.

#### 12 min. Profit with counters
Review: you learned price and cost in grade 1. New words: sales, the money buyers pay, and profit, what is left after costs. Model a made-up candle stall: a candle costs $3 to make and sells for $8, so $8 less $3 leaves $5, the profit. Pairs use counters to find the profit on the key ring card, then work the cookies card on paper. Each child writes [[s03-P54a]] and [[s03-P54b]]. Visit a rotating six pairs. A side group of up to six takes error AD for 5 minutes.

#### 6 min. Hope shapes choices
A fancier candle costs $14 and the stall asks $20; none sells. A seller hopes for profit, so she may change what she makes or what she asks. Hoped-for profit may not come true: the highest price is not always best, error M.
**Ask.** What does a seller hope for? Expect: money left after costs.

#### 10 min. A seller's choices
Review: you learned biography and before, action, after in unit 1. Read the account of Maggie L. Walker aloud once, about 4 minutes, in the words of cards-day54-walker.md. Say only what it says: Richmond then was a Jim-Crow city, she led for civil rights and she urged members to pool their money and lend it among themselves. Name no other barrier.
**Ask.** What did she choose to start, and what did people lack? Expect: a bank, the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank in 1903; a bank of the kind she called for. Pairs fill before, action and after, 6 minutes. Each child writes [[s03-P54d]], [[s03-P54e]] and [[s03-P54f]].

#### 4 min. Say it
Each child says "A seller hopes to ___, so she chooses ___" to a partner, then writes [[s03-P54c]]. Hear a rotating six. Speaking or dictating counts.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Market Card, first seen here. Read the card and each ask once. Children write [[s03-E54]] and [[s03-E54b]]. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice finds one profit at a time; the exit sets two sellers side by side and asks which leaves more. The Market Card is new.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the cards on the board; use pebbles for counters.

### At home

**Swap.** No counters: use coins or buttons. No class: you are the partner.

**Skip.** The side group.

- **10 min. Profit.** Show a candle: costs $3, sells for $8. Your child writes [[s03-P54a]] and [[s03-P54b]].
  **Say.** "What is left after costs?" Expect: the profit.
- **12 min. Walker.** Read the account once. Your child writes [[s03-P54d]], [[s03-P54e]] and [[s03-P54f]].
  **Say.** "What did she choose to start?" Expect: a bank.
- **8 min. Say it.** Your child writes [[s03-P54c]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card and each ask once. [[s03-E54]] [[s03-E54b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one cost and price card. Longer: a seller who might lower a price, and why.

### Words and supports
Words: cost, price, sales, profit, choose, bank. Frame: "A seller hopes to ___, so she chooses ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 55 opens with the more-missed of error F (sales taken as the profit) and error M (the stew because it sells for more) from today's exit. The other goes to the survey side table.

### Credit
Ours. The candle stall and Market Card are invented for practice. The Walker account is our own wording of three National Park Service pages on Maggie L. Walker (nps.gov).
