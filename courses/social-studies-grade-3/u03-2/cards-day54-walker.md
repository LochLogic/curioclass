# Maggie L. Walker, before, action, after (teacher reads aloud, about four minutes)

Maggie L. Walker was a real person who lived in Richmond, Virginia. Richmond then was a Jim-Crow city, and she was a leader for civil rights. In 1901 she called for a bank that would turn small savings into larger sums. In 1903 the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank opened, and she was its first president. She urged members to pool their money and lend it among themselves. The bank went on for many years: it later joined two Richmond banks and kept operating until 2009.

Read it once. Then fill three boxes with the class:

- **Before:** in 1901 she called for a bank that would turn small savings into larger sums; there was not yet one of the kind she asked for.
- **Action:** she chose to start the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank.
- **After:** people could pool their money and lend it among themselves in a bank run by their own community.
