```
   PINE LANDING (town)                      GULL BAY (town)
```

## Page 2: Record B, Narragansett Record

Facts from the Narragansett Indian Tribe's own website, in our words.

The Narragansett Indian Tribe is a Tribal Nation in Rhode Island. It governs itself today. A Chief Sachem and a Tribal Council lead the tribe. The tribe's offices are in Charlestown, Rhode Island.

The tribe has an Education Department. It offers tutoring, counseling and workshops for Native children. Children of other federally recognized tribes can use these services too.

Source: the Narragansett Indian Tribe's own website.

Ask: Record B. Who leads the Narragansett Indian Tribe today?
Ask: Record B. Describe one thing the Narragansett Indian Tribe shares.
Ask: Name the source that shows that contribution.
Ask: Records A and B. Write one claim that answers our unit question.
Ask: Record A. Give a detail from A that supports your claim.
Ask: Record B. Give a different detail from B that supports your claim.

## Page 3: Record C, Sugar Camp Record (made up, for practice)

Maple County is a made-up place. Print "made up, for practice" at the top.

Each spring, families in Maple County tap sugar maple trees. They camp near the trees in a sugar camp. They boil the sap at a mill beside the creek. Boiling needs creek water and wood. In summer the families go back to their farms.

Ask: Record C. In which season do people use the sugar camp?
Ask: Record C. What do people do at the sugar camp?
Ask: Record C. Where does the sugar camp stand?
Ask: Record C. How do the maples and the boiling explain that?

## Page 4: Record D, Treaty Record

**The treaty passage.** From the treaty of 12 May 1854, as the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin shows it. Read aloud once.

"Articles of agreement made and concluded at the Falls of Wolf River, in the State of Wisconsin, on the twelfth day of May, one thousand eight hundred and fifty-four, between the United States of America, by Francis Huebschmann, superintendent of Indian affairs, duly authorized thereto, and the Menomonee tribe of Indians."

The paper uses the government's old spelling and wording, "Menomonee tribe of Indians." The tribe's name today is the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin.

**The tribe's later history.** The Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin's own history page says the tribe made seven treaties with the United States during the 1800s. This page was written later, looking back.

Ask: Record D. Ask a question that the treaty passage could answer.
Ask: Record D. Is the treaty passage primary or secondary for the agreement?
Ask: Record D. Say why the treaty passage is that kind.
Ask: Record D. Ask a question that the tribe's later history could answer.
Ask: Record D. Is the tribe's later history primary or secondary for the agreement?
Ask: Record D. Say why the later history is that kind.
