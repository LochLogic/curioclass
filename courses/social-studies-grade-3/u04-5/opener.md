# Section 4.5 opener: the claim, the review, the unseen records

## What the section does

Four days of 40 minutes. Day 86 closes the unit question: each child chooses one detail from the Local Homeland Record and one from the Local Contribution Record, hears another supported emphasis (knowledge, settlement or governing) and writes a claim. Day 87 returns the written note on the day 72 card, revises it in a new color, and reviews the nations, the sort, the map and what we gave and how we govern. Day 88 is the unit assessment, from records the class has not seen. Day 89 is the response: a note, a grouped reteach and a fresh redo item.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A claim needs a detail from each source.** "The cards prove my answer" is not a claim with evidence. A claim names what a record shows. Two lines from one card are one source, not two.
- **Another emphasis can be supported too.** Knowledge, settlement and governing are three honest ways to answer the unit question.
- **The packet records are new.** They are A (a made-up regional map record), B (a Narragansett Indian Tribe record), C (a made-up county sugar camp record) and D (a treaty and the tribe's later history). Nothing in them is taught or reviewed before day 88.
- **The treaty passage is a quotation of an 1854 government paper.** It is read aloud once. Its kind (primary) comes from how it was made, not from its age.
- **Day 89 follows the letters.** Each child hears one diagnosed letter from the register and does one fresh item.

## The errors in plain words

H is a claim with no detail. V is two details counted as two sources. R is saying only the teacher needs the answer. The other letters return from days 72 to 85.

## Supplies

The blank frame card, the supporting-question check card, the day 87 review cards, the day 88 packet (five pages), the answer key, the redo cards, the written moves, the record sheet from section 4.1.

## A room with nothing

Read the frame card aloud. Read each packet page once aloud. Offer enlarged high-contrast maps, tactile markers, speech, dictation or a scribe. No student timer. Keep the packet and its cases out of teaching and review.
