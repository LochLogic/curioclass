Not seen by children before day 23. Kept in the teacher folder until then, collected afterwards and shown on day 24. Invented cases print "invented for practice". Sources checked 2026-11-21.

## L: local town map pair

The teacher builds L from the current town map, the early town map and a photograph. It has a legend with a historic landmark symbol, a physical feature (a river bend or lake), a north arrow and a bar.

## G: park map (invented for practice)

A made-up park on a grid of three columns A to C west to east and three rows 1 to 3 south to north. Words: "The park entrance is in the southwest corner. The pond is in the northwest corner. The pavilion is in the northeast corner. North is up. One square is 15 metres." Children place the three places and add a key, a north arrow, a scale bar and a title.

## W: world grid

A world outline with the equator and the prime meridian drawn, columns A to F west to east, rows 1 to 3 south to north, and a dot for Maputo, Mozambique.

## C: three climate cards (invented for practice)

## P1: Elwha River restoration (National Park Service, source 23B)

Reworded for children. For many years dams blocked the Elwha River and kept salmon from swimming upstream. Removal began on September 17, 2011. The Glines Canyon Dam came down in 2014, after the Elwha Dam. The park service lists the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe as a partner that helped free the river.

## P2: Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe (the tribe's own pages, sources 23C and 23D)

The Elwha Tribe's fisheries page says, in its own words, that salmon are "intricately woven into the culture of the Elwha Tribe" (a short quotation, credited to the tribe). Today the tribe's Natural Resources Department counts returning adult salmon, traps young fish as they travel to the sea and surveys spawning sites. The tribe's history page says much of its salmon fishing was done in the Elwha River long ago. The Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe works on the river today.

## Flood Garden Card (invented for practice)

A made-up street of gardens beside a stream flooded each spring. The families moved their gardens above the flood line.

## Boat Parade Card (invented for practice)

A made-up town held a boat parade on the water every year. The parade moved to the bridge and now crosses on foot. It is still held every year.
