# Day 20: Questions that help answer the big one

## Teacher page

### Before class
Post the five supporting questions on the board: Where are we on Earth? What does our town map show? How did land and water help place towns? How have people changed this place? Whose needs does a good location serve? Have the landmark photograph, the nation's passage and the two town maps ready. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 19 to 22 (key-exits-19-22.md), out of the room until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 19's exit slips and written task with the written cue, 2 minutes. Eight minutes for the more-missed error. If many made error K on day 19's exit (sources named, no detail): pairs link one detail from each source of a new pair, the Library Corner Pair, invented for practice, to a claim. The map shows a book drop by the east door; the photograph shows a box by that door with a book sticking out. If many made error G (a claim past the sources): pairs sort three Fountain Square cards under shows, suggests and cannot tell: "There is a stop on the south side." "People wait for the bus." "The stand was built to keep people dry."

#### 7 min. Two readings of a supporting question
Read the board's five questions. Review: on day 2 you read two readings: one word people can mean two ways, each checkable. Model two made-up questions. "Where is the safest place for a bus stop?" Safest could mean away from cars, or well lit. "Which path is nearest the market?" Nearest could mean fewest steps, or least time. A fact can be found out; a priority is a choice.

**Ask.** What word could mean two things here, and which part is a fact? Expect: safest or nearest, then a fact such as where cars drive or how far the market is.

#### 10 min. My question record
Each child picks one question from the board and fills the record on Side 1 ([[s01-P20]], [[s01-P20b]], [[s01-P20c]], [[s01-P20d]]), then a partner reads it back. The small group takes its repair first, 5 minutes. Give one cue to about 8 children, a minute each. The record is kept in the folder.

#### 8 min. Two sources
Read the Source Table's four rows aloud. Each child fills the rows for two sources they choose (what it shows; one detail we could use), then writes [[s01-P20e]]. A child who asks may use the claim frame card.

**Ask.** Which two sources did you pick, and what does each add? Expect: both named, each with a detail.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket
Give each child the half slip. Read both asks once. Every child writes [[s01-E20]] and [[s01-E20b]] about a board question they did not pick today. The slip is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The exit uses a board question the child did not pick, with no record in hand.

### Other rooms
No printer: copy the record's four lines and the Source Table's three columns on lined paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No board: write five questions about your town on a page.

**Skip.** The repair, the cue and the Source Table.

- **10 min. Two readings.** Read the question "Which path is nearest the market?"
  **Say.** "What word could mean two things, and what is a fact we need?" Expect: nearest; how far the market is.
- **20 min. A record.** Pick a question and fill the four lines ([[s01-P20]] [[s01-P20b]] [[s01-P20c]] [[s01-P20d]]), then write the sentence ([[s01-P20e]]).
  **Say.** "How does that help explain why the town is here?" Expect: a link to place or a choice.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E20]] [[s01-E20b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one fact, not two.

### Words and supports
Words: supporting question, fact, priority, two readings. Frame: "Knowing ___ helps explain why the town is here because ___." Pointing, drawing or a scribe is an answer.

### Tomorrow
If many gave an unrelated fact (error O), day 21 opens by linking one group or one needed fact to a supporting answer; if many called a direction a word with two readings (error J), it sorts one mapped fact and two supported priorities. Day 21 writes the closing claim.
