# Day 22: Read the cue, revise and review

## Teacher page

### Before class
Write one cue on each child's day 10 school-grounds map, day 2 record and day 20 question record: present, partial or missing, one cue each, from the feedback cue sheet, never a grade and never another child's name. Lay each child's three pieces together. Keep the overlay (teacher): the triangle; the lower side; about 3 bars; Southeast; a lake; the ford or the wagon ruts. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 19 to 22 (key-exits-19-22.md), out of the room until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Read the feedback
Each child reads the cue on the map first, then on the two records. No one asks the teacher what a cue means; reread it. Say that these three revisions are scored on day 23, and that the first tries were practice.

#### 15 min. Revise
Each child revises alone and answers no questions aloud. The school-grounds map first, 10 minutes: features in place, then the four parts (title, legend, north arrow, bar). Then the day 2 record, 3 minutes, and the day 20 question record, 2 minutes, only where marked partial or missing. The teacher answers no content questions. Each child writes [[s01-P22]]. The revised pieces are handed in and scored on day 23.

#### 18 min. Retrieval and repair
Hand back day 21's slips with the written cue, and return the revised piece from this day's first part to the folder for day 23. Five minutes for the more-missed error on day 21's exit. If many made error K (a map named, no detail): link one detail from each of two sources to a claim, using the Fountain Square pair. If many made error G (the maps prove the reason): sort the three Fountain Square cards from day 20 under shows, suggests and cannot tell. The other error goes to a small group of up to 6 in the next 13 minutes.

Thirteen minutes: pairs work the six fresh cards on Side 2, invented for practice, one at a time: a legend, a north arrow, a bar, a region, a feature kind and a source detail. Review: the legend, arrow and bar from days 7 and 8.

Check: after every two cards, your partner reads the overlay line. Same answer, or find the slip together.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Give each child the quarter slip with the fresh bar map; read the ask once. Every child writes [[s01-E22]]. The map is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** Card 1 compares two bar counts from words. The exit shows a new map and asks for one whole-bar count.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the six cards on the board and answer on lined paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No overlay: your adult reads each answer after you write it.

**Skip.** The revision of school pieces and the repair.

- **10 min. Read and fix.** Look at a map you drew. Choose one thing a cue would ask for.
  **Say.** "What does each symbol mean? Where is north?" Expect: the legend and the arrow.
- **20 min. Six cards.** Do the cards on Side 2; your adult reads each answer after you write it.
  **Say.** "How did you know?" Expect: the key, the arrow or the bar.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E22]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: four cards, keeping the bar and the arrow. Longer: make a seventh card and swap with a partner.

### Words and supports
Words: bar, grid cell, equator, hemisphere, latitude, region, key. Pointing or a scribe is an answer.

### Tomorrow
Day 23 is the end-of-unit check, read aloud and answered alone, with nothing on the wall. If many wrote steps for bars (error D), day 24's map conventions group lays a strip along a fresh route.
