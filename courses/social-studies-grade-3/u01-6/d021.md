# Day 21: Why is our town where it is?

## Teacher page

### Before class
Have each pair's town map and early map, with their dates and credits. Write the word card for the wall: claim, evidence, reasoning. Take the claim frame card down and keep it in the folder; a child who asks for it gets it and is marked "shown with help". Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 19 to 22 (key-exits-19-22.md), out of the room until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 20's slips with the written cue, 1 minute. If many made error O on day 20's exit (an unrelated fact): link one group or one fact to the answer for "How did land and water help place towns?" Pairs name who needs the answer, or a fact that would help. If many made error J (a direction called a word with two readings): sort three cards, "North is where the arrow points," "Nearest could mean fewest steps or least time," "Safest could mean away from cars or well lit." The other error goes to a small group of up to 6 in part 3.

#### 7 min. Say the claim
Read the question: "Why is our town where it is?" Each child says a claim to a partner, using the Local Current Map and the early map, then the partner asks, "Why do you think that?" The word card holds claim, evidence and reasoning, and no frame. Review: a claim is what you say is true, and evidence is a detail in a named source. Pairs take about 2 minutes each, with the question.

**Ask.** Which detail from which map supports your claim? Expect: a named map and a detail, then how it helps.

#### 14 min. Write the claim
Each child writes on Side 1: the claim, a detail from each map, and one thing the maps cannot tell ([[s01-P21]], [[s01-P21b]], [[s01-P21c]], [[s01-P21d]]). The claim names a physical feature and one human choice. It stays practice. Give written cues to about 8 children. The small group takes its repair first, 5 minutes. The sheets stay in the folder.

#### 9 min. Share
Groups of six sit together. Each child reads a claim aloud, 1 minute each, and listeners name the evidence they heard and its map. Hear one group yourself; the record names who was heard.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket
Give each child the Town Exit Pair beside the half slip; read both asks once. Every child writes [[s01-E21]] and [[s01-E21b]]. The pair is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The closing claim uses our own two maps and ends with what they cannot tell. The exit is a new made-up pair, with no frame and no partner.

### Other rooms
No printer: read the claim question and write the four boxes on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No town maps: use a library map and one photograph of your town.

**Skip.** The repair and the group share.

- **10 min. Say it.** Look at a map of your town.
  **Say.** "Why do you think the town is here?" Expect: a claim with a feature and a human choice.
  **Say.** "Why do you think that?" Expect: a detail from a named map.
- **20 min. Write it.** Fill the four boxes ([[s01-P21]] [[s01-P21b]] [[s01-P21c]] [[s01-P21d]]).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E21]] [[s01-E21b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: the claim and one detail. Longer: a second claim from the other pair of details, or "What else would help show it?"

### Words and supports
Words: claim, evidence, reasoning, "the maps cannot tell". A spoken claim, a drawing or a scribe is an answer. The claim frame is offered on request.

### Tomorrow
If many wrote that the maps prove the reason (error G), day 22's retrieval sorts claim cards into shows, suggests and cannot tell; if many named a map with no detail (error K), it links one detail from each of two sources. Day 22 returns written feedback and the children revise before the check.
