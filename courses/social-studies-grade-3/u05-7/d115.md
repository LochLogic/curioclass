# Day 115: Two harbors

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hand back day 114's written tasks and exit slips with a cue; keep a stop card on each desk. Have the day 94 map out. Print the student page and the exit slips with the Two-Harbor Exit Map; the key is key-exits-113-116.md. Tern Landing and its people are made up; say so aloud. The Salem and New Amsterdam pages do not describe the water; do not add detail they lack.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (T from E114 10, or U 5)
If many missed day 114's exit on T (people mixed up with goods), take 10 minutes: read a made-up route card: one ship carries tools to a harbor and brings back rope; a different ship carries people who were taken from their homes and forced aboard. Say: people are never goods. Each child writes [[s05-W115a]]. If many missed U (every ship sailed one triangle), take 5 minutes: read a made-up route that ran back and forth between two harbors, then each child writes [[s05-W115b]]; then 5 minutes of retrieval: harbor, cargo, and barter in your own words. If few missed either, those children use the side table.

#### 9 min. Salem
Read the Salem card once. Review: a harbor is a sheltered place where ships can stop (grade 1). A port is a town where ships come and go. Each child writes [[s05-P115a]]. Say: ships carried goods out and goods back; the people who loaded and sold them made the port work. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each.

#### 9 min. New Amsterdam
Review: on the day 94 map, New Netherland was the Dutch colony's label. Read the New Amsterdam card once. Our page does not say who lived near the bay before; do not guess. Each child writes [[s05-P115b]]. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each; list their names.

#### 10 min. Pairs
Read the Tern Landing card once (made up). Pairs put the three steps in order: [[s05-P115c]]. Then each child writes [[s05-P115d]]. Hear a rotating six pairs, about 45 seconds each, and mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the Two-Harbor Exit Map, new today. Read it once aloud. Each child writes [[s05-E115]] and [[s05-E115b]]. Key: key-exits-113-116.md. Hand the slips back on day 116.

### Other rooms
No printer: copy the cards onto the board; read each aloud once.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you read each card aloud; your child may skip any part.

**Skip.** The repair you did not need.

- **7 min. Repair.** Read the route card. [[s05-W115a]] [[s05-W115b]] **Say.** "What moved by force?" Expect: people.
- **12 min. Two real ports.** Read the Salem and New Amsterdam cards. [[s05-P115a]] [[s05-P115b]]
- **12 min. Tern Landing.** Read the card. [[s05-P115c]] [[s05-P115d]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E115]] [[s05-E115b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep one real port, the Tern Landing steps and the exit. Longer: your child draws Tern Landing and labels the three steps.

### Words and supports
Words: port, wharf, fur trading station, harbor. Frame: "First ___, then ___, then ___." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 116 opens by returning the exit slips: connect harbor, labor and trade on a fresh port record. Then children trace a documented route on a map and write why ships and goods gathered at ports.

### Credit
Ours. Salem follows the National Park Service article (U5S); New Amsterdam follows the National Park Service page on early American places (U5T); both checked 2026-11-19. Tern Landing is made up.
