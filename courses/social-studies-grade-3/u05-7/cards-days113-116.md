# Days 113 to 116 cards

Print "made up, for practice" on invented cards. Read every line aloud once.

## Day 113 repair (S from day 112, and retrieval from day 53)

S: a fresh made-up petition detail: a town's tenants ask the landlord to fix a roof. Name the action and the purpose.
Retrieval: A fisher wants bread, but the baker does not want fish. How could money help? Money is accepted by everyone, so the wants need not match.

## Fishing

Fishing was a major trade, and sailors carried fish to far ports. (Britannica, Triangular trade, U5AB.) A harbor is a sheltered place where ships can load.

## Shipbuilding

A shipyard builds boats from timber. Boats help fishers and carriers. Fishers and carriers give work to the yard.

## Barter

Barter is a trade of goods or services with no money. Clare the sailmaker, made up, has a sail and wants corn. A farmer has corn and wants a sail. They swap, no coins. Pairs trace one more swap, no coins (I).

## Link card (made up)

Fen Barrel Works makes barrels. Seth packs salted cod in them. Dora sells the barrels of cod at the dock. Pairs link the barrel maker, the packer and the seller (AD).

## Day 114 repair

AD: a fresh made-up card: a net maker, a trawler and a market seller. I: trace one fresh swap, goods for goods.

## A simplified network, labeled so

A map with three dots: New England, the Caribbean and West Africa. Goods arrows: fish to the Caribbean; sugar and molasses back; rum to West Africa. Say separately: people were taken from their homes and forced onto ships. (U5AB.) Tail at the start dot. Say each leg's compass direction. Ask what the picture leaves out.

## Goods and people

Goods: fish, lumber, sugar, molasses. Say separately, never in the same list: people are never goods; Africans were taken from their homes and forced onto ships. (T.)

## Who made the sugar

Sugar and molasses were made on Caribbean plantations by the forced labor of enslaved people. Salem merchants sent fish, livestock and lumber to those plantations. (National Park Service, U5S.)

## One triangle?

Ships took many routes. No New England trader is known to have sailed a full triangle in order. The picture is simplified. (U5AB.)

## Day 115 repair (T or U)

T: a fresh made-up route card: a ship carries tools to a harbor and takes away rope, and a different ship carries people who were taken from their homes and forced aboard. Say people are never goods. U: the simplified network beside a made-up route that ran back and forth between two harbors.

## Salem

Salem merchants sent fish, livestock and lumber to the Caribbean, and ships came back with sugar, molasses and coffee. (U5S.) Fill the chain: what the water gives, what the people do, what moves.

## New Amsterdam

In 1609 Henry Hudson reached New York Bay. In 1624 the Dutch set up a fur trading station on Governor's Island. In 1625 they founded New Amsterdam in Lower Manhattan. (National Park Service, U5T.) On the day 94 map it is labeled New Netherland. The same chain.

## Pairs: Tern Landing, made up

Tern Landing has a long dock, a sheltered cove and a market. Fill the chain: harbor, work, trade. Say why workers were needed there (W).

## Map return

Tail at the start dot. A legend with two marks: a circle for goods and an arrow with its tail at the start dot. People are named in words, never as a mark among goods.

## A documented route

In 1764 the schooner Pembroke came back to Salem from Dominica, a Caribbean island, with sugar, coffee and molasses. (National Park Service, U5S.) The page does not say where the trip began. Trace one arrow on tracing paper over a printed map, from Dominica to Salem. Mark the goods with circles. Name each leg's direction.

## Record

Why did ships and goods gather at ports? Sea access and trading skills; and some people were also moved by force.
