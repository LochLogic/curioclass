# Day 116: Trace a route

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hand back day 115's written tasks and exit slips with a cue. Print the student page, the route map (dots for Salem and Dominica) and the Route Card slips; key: key-exits-113-116.md. Pike Cove and Marlow Quay are made up; say so. Board legend: a circle for goods, an arrow with its tail at the start. People are named in words, never as a mark. The page gives no outbound port.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (W from E115)
If many missed day 115's exit on W (water alone built a port), take 10 minutes: read the made-up Pike Cove record, which has a calm bay, one wharf and a Friday market. Each child fills the three blanks [[s05-W116a]]. If few missed it, those children work here for 5 minutes at the side table while the rest start the map.

#### 4 min. Map return
Review: a legend tells what each mark means (day 7); a compass rose shows north, south, east and west (day 93). Say: an arrow's tail is at the start dot. Read the board legend. Each child writes [[s05-P116b]].

#### 12 min. Trace a route
Read the Pembroke card once. This is a real record. Lay tracing paper over the route map. Draw one arrow from Dominica to Salem; the page does not state the outbound leg, so draw nothing for it. Mark the goods with circles. The sugar, coffee and molasses were made by the forced labor of enslaved people, so say that once, plainly. Each child writes [[s05-P116c]] and [[s05-P116d]]. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each.

#### 8 min. Record
Each child writes [[s05-P116a]] on the record page, using the Pembroke map and the frame. This is the day 116 record. It goes in the record folder with a written cue after class and is revised on day 119. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each, and mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 4 min. Share
A rotating six read their lines aloud. The class adds one line to the question board under "Who worked and who benefited?" If time is short, drop this and the map return, give 8 minutes to the route and 8 to the record.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the Route Card, new today. Read it once aloud. Each child writes [[s05-E116]]. Key: key-exits-113-116.md. Hand the slips back on day 117.

### Other rooms
No tracing paper: draw the arrow on the map itself. No printer: draw two dots on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you read each card aloud; your child may skip any part.

**Skip.** The Pike Cove repair, if day 115 went well.

- **6 min. Repair.** Read the Pike Cove record. [[s05-W116a]]
- **16 min. The route.** Draw two dots, Salem and Dominica. Read the card. [[s05-P116b]] [[s05-P116c]] [[s05-P116d]] **Say.** "Which way did the ship sail, from Dominica to Salem?" Expect: north.
- **10 min. Why ports.** [[s05-P116a]]
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E116]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the route and the record; drop the repair and share. Longer: add a second route your child invents, labelled made up.

### Words and supports
Words: route, legend, forced labor, port. Frame on the page. Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 117 opens by returning the exit slips and records, then gathers evidence from colonial and Native sources for the unit question.

### Credit
Ours. The Pembroke card follows the National Park Service article on Salem (U5S), checked 2026-11-19, and its source record for Dominica. Pike Cove and Marlow Quay are made up.
