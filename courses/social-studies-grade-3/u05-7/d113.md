# Day 113: Fish, boats and swaps

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hand back day 112's written tasks and exit slips with a cue. Draw a made-up cove on the board: curved shore, still water, a dock. Print the student page and the exit slips with the Port Work Card; the key is key-exits-113-116.md, teacher only. Clare, Ivo, Maud, Fen Boat Yard, Seth, Dora, Rafe and Wyn are made up; say so aloud.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (S from E112 5, day 53 retrieval 5)
First, S (5 min). If many missed day 112's exit (enslaved people did nothing), read one more request from the same 1774 petition, once: "that we may obtain our Natural right our freedoms." Summary on the page: we ask to get our natural right to be free. A petition is a written request to officials (day 112). Each child writes [[s05-W113a]] and [[s05-W113b]]. If few missed it, those children work here at the side table and the rest start the retrieval.
Second, retrieval (5 min). Review: you learned barter on day 53, a trade with no money. Ask: a potter wants bread, but the baker does not want pots. How could money help? Each child writes [[s05-W113c]].

#### 8 min. Fishing
Review: harbor, a sheltered place where ships can stop (grade 1, day 49); industry, all the work that gets or makes one kind of thing, such as fishing (grade 2, day 37). Point to the cove. New: the fishing industry is all the work of catching and selling fish; cargo is goods a ship carries. Read the fishing card once. Each child writes [[s05-P113a]]. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each.

#### 8 min. Shipbuilding
New: shipyard and timber. Read the shipyard card once. The page we read does not name the yard's crafts, so we do not either. Each child writes [[s05-P113b]]. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each.

#### 6 min. Barter
Review: barter, a trade with no money (day 53). We have no page that says how many coins people had, so our made-up cases give one person no coins. Say: wrong, "everything was paid for with coins"; right, "a sail could be paid for in corn." Read the Clare swap and fill the first row of the barter table together. Each child writes [[s05-P113c]].
Check: Clare gives a sail; the farmer gives corn.

#### 6 min. Link card
Read the link card once; pairs talk for one minute, then each child writes [[s05-P113d]] alone. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each, and mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. This is the day 113 record. It goes in the record folder with a written cue and is revised on day 119.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the Port Work Card, new today. Read it once aloud. Each child writes [[s05-E113]] and [[s05-E113b]]. Key: key-exits-113-116.md. Hand the slips back on day 114.

### Other rooms
No printer: copy the cards onto the board; read each aloud once.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you read each card aloud; your child may stop at any time.

**Skip.** The petition repair, if day 112 went well.

- **8 min. Repair.** Read the request. [[s05-W113a]] [[s05-W113b]] [[s05-W113c]] **Say.** "What did they ask for?" Expect: to be free.
- **10 min. Fish and boats.** Read the two cards. [[s05-P113a]] [[s05-P113b]]
- **12 min. Swaps.** Read the Clare swap. [[s05-P113c]] [[s05-P113d]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E113]] [[s05-E113b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the fishing card, the link card and the exit. Longer: your child draws the made-up cove and labels where each worker stands.

### Words and supports
Words: fishing industry, cargo, shipyard, timber, barter. Frame: "The ___ goes to ___." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 114 opens by returning the exit slips: link a builder, a fisher and a seller on a fresh card, and trace one swap of goods for goods. Then it turns to sea routes across the Atlantic and Caribbean, including people carried by force.
