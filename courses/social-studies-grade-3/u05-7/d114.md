# Day 114: Sea routes and the people carried by force

## Teacher page

### Before class
This day tells hard history. Put a stop card on each desk; any child may point to it or pass. No role play. Hand back day 113's written tasks and exit slips with a cue. Draw three dots on a map: New England, the Caribbean, West Africa. Print the exit slip. Say "enslaved Africans" and "people carried by force", never goods or cargo. Pell, Orrin, Jo, Hana, Tomas and Isle Corra are made up.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (AD 5 and I 5, from E113)
First, AD (5 min). Read the made-up card: Pell makes nets. Orrin fishes with a Pell net. Jo sells his fish at the market. Each child writes [[s05-W114a]]. Second, I (5 min). Read: Hana has eggs and wants a hat; Tomas makes hats and wants eggs. Wrong: "a swap needs coins"; right: "eggs for a hat is barter." Each child writes [[s05-W114b]]. If few missed one, those children use the side table.

#### 8 min. The triangle
Review: compass rose and key on a world map (day 93); tail of an arrow at the start dot. Show the simplified network: a picture of some sea routes, not every voyage. Read each goods arrow once: salted fish from New England to the Caribbean; sugar and molasses back; rum to West Africa. Then say separately: the picture also shows people who were taken from their homes and forced onto ships. Later writers called this picture the triangle trade. Each child writes where the fish arrow ends [[s05-P114a]], then its direction [[s05-P114b]]. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each.

#### 8 min. Goods and people
Say: goods are things made or grown and traded; fish, lumber, sugar and molasses were goods. Separately: people are never goods. Africans were taken from their homes and forced onto ships. Say it once, calmly; no graphic detail. Review: enslaved people were held for life and denied freedom (day 109). Each child writes which two goods came back from the Caribbean [[s05-P114c]].

#### 8 min. Who made the sugar
Read the sugar card: Salem merchants sent fish, livestock and lumber to Caribbean plantations, and ships came back with sugar, molasses and coffee. A plantation is a large farm. Enslaved people produced the sugar by forced labor, work they could not refuse. Each child writes [[s05-P114d]]. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each; list their names.

#### 4 min. One triangle?
Say: historians say no New England trader is known to have sailed one full triangle in order; ships took many routes. The picture is simple, so it leaves things out. Each child writes [[s05-P114e]].

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the slip with the Route Card, new today. Read it once aloud. Each child writes [[s05-E114]] and [[s05-E114b]]. Key: key-exits-113-116.md. Hand the slips back on day 115.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the three dots and the arrows on the board; read the cards aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No class: you read each card aloud; your child may stop at any time and skip any part.

**Skip.** The repair you did not need.

- **8 min. Repair.** Read the net card and the swap. [[s05-W114a]] [[s05-W114b]] **Say.** "Who gave what?" Expect: eggs for a hat.
- **12 min. The picture.** Draw the three dots and the arrows. [[s05-P114a]] [[s05-P114b]] [[s05-P114c]]
- **10 min. Sugar.** Read the sugar card. [[s05-P114d]] [[s05-P114e]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s05-E114]] [[s05-E114b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the sort, the sugar card and the exit. Longer: your child adds one more route the picture leaves out.

### Words and supports
Words: Caribbean, plantation, forced labor, simplified network. Frame: "The picture leaves out ___." Read aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 115 opens by returning the exit slips: name the goods on a fresh route and say, in its own sentence, who was forced aboard, or set the triangle beside a fresh route. Then it turns to two harbors, Salem and New Amsterdam.
