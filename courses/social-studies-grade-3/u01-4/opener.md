# Section 4 opener: five regions and the land and water in ours

## What the section does

Five days, 11 to 15. Day 11 sorts states into the classroom's five regions with the key (Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, West), starting with our state and one beside it. Day 12 places the states that touch our state on an outline of our region. Day 13 labels all of a region's states and rejects names that do not belong. Day 14 reads a physical map with its own key for rivers, lakes, mountains, shoreline, capes and bays, then reads our town on it. Day 15 asks how land and water explain where a town is, using the town map, a satellite view and a made-up pass.

## How the days run

Short repair, worked example, pairs work, then a printed exit card (cards-exits-11-15.md). The day's six are also heard.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Region, state and landform are different kinds of name.** A region holds states; a state is one place on the political map; a river or a bay is a landform.
- **We use the classroom five-region key.** It is ours, and other maps may group states differently.
- **A region is complete only when all its states are named.**
- **A physical map has its own key.** Brown triangles may show mountains on one map and nothing on another.
- **Land and water help explain where a town is** (a crossing, a harbor, a pass), but a photograph cannot prove it alone.

## The errors in plain words

- **F:** region, state and landform names are not interchangeable.
- **S:** naming some states does not complete a region.
- **P:** listing features does not explain a relationship.
- **G:** a feature seen is not a proved conclusion.

## Supplies for the section

The federal political map and five-region key, the Region Card, outlines and name banks, state cards, the physical map of our region, land and water picture cards, the Feature Card, the Gull Cove card, the Saddle Notch card, the satellite view, the Our Town Card, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw a rough region outline on the board, write the key on paper, use the town map and describe the land aloud.
