# Day 13: Our region again, and another

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put up the US political map and five-region key. Use the outlines and name banks as a base: print a fresh outline of our region for each pair and one of another region: the Midwest, or the Northeast if ours is the Midwest. Cut the six state cards. Plan a plain bank for each outline: only that region's states. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 11 to 15 (key-exits-11-15.md), out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 12's exit tickets with the written cue. If many made error S (named some states and stopped): pairs trace our state's border on the wall map and name each state it touches. If many made error F (only if a slip shows it): sort four new labels into state, region and landform from the printed maps. The other error goes to a small group of up to six during part 2.

#### 12 min. Our region again

No clocks. Review: on day 12 you walked from state to state and counted to the key's list. Pairs take turns: one covers the labels and points, the other names the state. Each child takes six points in turn, about half a minute a child. Then each child labels a fresh outline from the plain bank, and the partner checks it against the key. Ease shows when a child names a state without the key beside them; no one is timed.

#### 12 min. Another region

The teacher models the walk on a region that is not ours, the Midwest or the Northeast, with no bank: copy each name from the key's list and count aloud. Pairs label the other outline the same way, key beside them, and write "We counted ___." The small group meets at the side table and labels half an outline, then swaps.

#### 6 min. Which region?

Six state cards: Oregon, Georgia, Ohio, Nevada, Alabama and Iowa. Each pair lays every card under its region on the key and finds each name in a list. Cards are practice; the teacher cues three pairs.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Slips out. Read the new outline's directions once. Every child writes on the quarter slip ([[s01-E13]]). The outline and its bank are first seen here.

**Shared shape.** Practice labels outlines from a plain bank or the key's list, and the cards sort a state into its region. The exit's bank mixes real states with a region name and a landform. That mix, and the Southwest, are first seen at the exit.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw outlines on paper and write the key's lists on the board. One projector: show the key; children use paper outlines.

### At home

**Swap.** No outlines: sketch a region and cover its labels with paper.

**Skip.** The pair turns and the six cards.

- **10 min. Name them.** Cover the names on a map of our region, point, and say each state.
  **Say.** "What is this state?" Expect: the name, then check it against the key.
- **20 min. Another region.** Choose one region that is not ours and write its states from the key.
  **Say.** "How many states does the key list?" Expect: the number printed, and we counted the same.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E13]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: if the day folds into day 12, drop part 4 and half of part 2; keep the exit. Longer: label a third region's states with no key.

### Words and supports

Words: region, state, count, key. Frame: "We counted ___; the key lists ___." Pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells whether children keep names that are not states off the map. If many wrote a region or landform on a state (error F), day 14 opens by sorting labels into state, region and landform; if many labelled only some states (error S), it checks each on the key. Day 14 reads a physical map for rivers, lakes, coast and mountains.
