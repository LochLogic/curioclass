# Day 12: Every state of our region

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put up the US political map and five-region key. Print New England's outline from the wall map. Make the outlines and name banks: a name bank for our region that holds only its states, one card each. Count our region on the key: Northeast 11, Southeast 12, Midwest 12, Southwest 4, West 11. Pick four labels for the repair: Iowa, Northeast, Lake Michigan, Appalachian Mountains. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 11 to 15 (key-exits-11-15.md), out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 11's exit tickets with the written cue. If many made error F (a state's name or a landform given as the region): lay the physical map over the political map and sort the four labels into state, region and landform. If many made error B (went by color): match a star, a square and a triangle to a made-up key. The other error goes to a small group of up to six in part 3.

#### 9 min. Six states, one method

Source 12A: New England is six states: Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Connecticut. On the key they sit inside the Northeast. Model the walk on the wall map: start at an edge state, move to the next state beside it, say each name from the bank, and count to six. Pairs trace the same walk on their outline.

#### 15 min. Every state of our region

Pairs label the outline of our region from the name bank with the walk: start at an edge, move neighbor to neighbor. Then they check each state against the key's list and write: "We counted ___; the key lists ___." For a Northeast class, the six are a start and five more follow: New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware and Maryland. The teacher gives each of the 15 pairs one cue; practice, not scored. The small group meets at the side table for its repair.

**Ask.** How do you know each state you labeled is in our region? Expect: it is on the key's list for our region, and the count matches.

#### 6 min. Check and swap

Pairs swap outlines with another pair, find one state to fix or one label to add using the key, and give it back.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Slips out. Read the new outline's directions once. Every child writes on the quarter slip ([[s01-E12]]). The outline is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The walk names every state from a bank and counts to the key's list. The exit gives a new outline with no bank and asks for every state of the region in its place.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw our region's outline on the board; pairs copy it and write the names. One projector: show the key and the wall map; children use paper outlines.

### At home

**Swap.** No outline: sketch our region and use an atlas.

**Skip.** The pair swap.

- **10 min. Six states.** Read New England's six names aloud and walk a finger from state to state.
  **Say.** "Where did you start, and what did you do next?" Expect: at an edge, then to the next state beside it.
- **20 min. Our region.** Write the name of each state in our region on a sketch and count them.
  **Say.** "How do we know every state is there?" Expect: we counted and the key's list matches.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E12]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs label half the region and trade for the rest. Longer: label a second region with no bank.

### Words and supports

Words: region, state, walk, count, key. Frame: "We counted ___; the key lists ___." Pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells whether children know which states touch ours. If many named one touching state and stopped (error S), day 13 opens by checking each state on the key and placing the missing labels; if many named a region or a landform (error F), it sorts labels as day 12 did. Day 13 practices our region and adds another.

### Credit
