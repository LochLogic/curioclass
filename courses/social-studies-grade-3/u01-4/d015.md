# Day 15: How land and water could place a town

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put up the shows, suggests, cannot tell card; keep the answer overlay at hand. Lay out the town map and a satellite view of a different part of town; have its credit ready. Choose one paragraph from the town's history page; add nothing it does not say. Write three Saddle Notch strips. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 11 to 15 (key-exits-11-15.md), out of the room until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 14's exit tickets and written tasks (the exit box on the student page) with the written cue. If many made error F, sort six labels into state, region and landform; if many made error B, match three symbols to a key. The other goes to a small group in part 3.

#### 8 min. A pass

Review: on day 9 you sorted claims into shows, might suggest and cannot tell. Read the Saddle Notch card aloud (invented for practice). Pairs sort three strips under the wall card's headings: a road runs through the low gap (shows); the gap might help travellers cross the hills (might suggest); who built the inn (cannot tell). The teacher hears four pairs.

**Ask.** What does the map show, and what can it not tell? Expect: it shows the road and the gap; it cannot tell why the inn stands there.

#### 14 min. Our town

Show the town map beside the satellite view and read its credit aloud.  Review: on days 7 and 8 you read direction and bars on the town map. Pairs find one physical feature near the town and say which way it is from the school. Read one paragraph of the town's history page exactly as written; say any Native nation's name as the page gives it. Pairs fill the Our Town Card: "The map shows ___. It might help explain ___. The page says ___" or "The page does not say." Cue six pairs, one each.

#### 8 min. Supporting question 3

Each child writes one sentence for "How did land and water help place towns?" ([[s01-P15]]), with the frame: The map shows ___. It might help because ___. It cannot tell ___. Then groups of six read aloud, one turn each, at one minute a child; the teacher hears one group, and reads the rest from the folder. Add the answer to the question board.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the map and photograph aloud once. Every child writes on the quarter slip ([[s01-E15]]). Hillford is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The Saddle Notch strips are sorted, not explained. The exit asks each child to explain, from a new map and photograph of a different town, what could help explain it. No practice card shows a road crossing a river.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the strips by hand. One projector: show the map and the satellite view.

### At home

**Swap.** No town page: use the town's own web page.

**Skip.** The groups reading aloud.

- **10 min. Look.** Look at a map and a photograph of a place near you.
  **Say.** "What does the map show?" Expect: a feature printed on it.
- **20 min. Might and cannot.** Fill one frame for a river, lake or hill near the place.
  **Say.** "What can the map not tell us?" Expect: why each family came.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E15]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the group reading. Longer: a second feature near the town.

### Words and supports

Words: pass, shows, might help, cannot tell. Frame: "The map shows ___. It might help because ___. It cannot tell ___."

### Tomorrow

The exit tells whether children can link one feature to how it could help, with could or might. If many wrote that everyone came for the water (error G), day 16 opens with three claim cards sorted into shows, might suggest and cannot tell; if many listed features with no link (error P), it links a fresh map feature to a photograph. Day 16 is how people changed the land.
