# Day 14: Land and water in our region

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put up the US political map and five-region key. Pair a physical and a political map of a region not ours: the Northeast with its sample overlay (overlay-answer-sample.md), or the Midwest if ours is the Northeast, using printed labels only. Find our region's river, lake, mountains and any cape or bay. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 11 to 15 (key-exits-11-15.md), out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 13's exit tickets with the written cue. If many made error F (a region or landform written on a state): sort four labels into state, region and landform. If many made error S: check each state on the key. The other goes to a small group in part 3.

#### 12 min. Land and water words

Review: in grade 2 you learned a physical map shows land and water. Review from grade 1: river, lake, mountains (a long row of high land), harbor (a sheltered place where ships can stop). New words, one picture card each: shoreline (where land meets the sea or a lake), cape (land that sticks out into water), bay (water that curves into land), pass (a low way through mountains). The political map names states; the physical map names land and water. For the Northeast, one fact each: Hudson River (Source 14B), Lake Champlain (Source 14A), Cape Cod (Source 14C), Chesapeake Bay (Source 14D), Appalachian Mountains (Source 14E). Pairs match each card to a symbol on the map's key, not a colour.

**Ask.** Is this a cape or a bay, and how does the key show it? Expect: the kind from the key's symbol, not the colour.

#### 10 min. Our region's features

Pairs use the physical map of our region. On the Feature Card they write the kind, the name copied from the map's label, and how the key shows it, for one river, one lake and the mountains, then the shoreline and a cape or bay; with no coast, "no coast." Cue each pair once; practice, not scored. The small group fills the river line.

#### 8 min. What could it do for a town?

Show the Gull Cove card (invented for practice); read only its harbor and pier. Model: "The map shows a harbor. That might help because ships can stop. The map cannot tell who came first." Review: on day 9 you used shows, might suggest and cannot tell. Each pair says the same for one feature on its card; the teacher hears four pairs.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the panel's directions once. Every child writes places A and B on the half slip ([[s01-E14]], [[s01-E14b]]) and places C and D in the exit box on the student page ([[s01-E14c]], [[s01-E14d]]). The Fenmark Panel is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** Practice names kinds of land and water on real printed maps and matches pictures to symbols. The exit uses a made-up panel with four lettered places. No picture card or model shows the exit's panel, and the Gull Cove card is read only for its harbor.

### Other rooms

No printer: use a school atlas and board drawings. One projector: show the maps and key.

### At home

**Swap.** No physical map: use an atlas page.

**Skip.** Gull Cove.

- **10 min. Words.** Say each land and water word and point to a picture of it.
  **Say.** "How does the key show a river?" Expect: the symbol in the key, not the colour.
- **20 min. Our region.** Find one river, one lake and the mountains on a physical map of our region and write their names.
  **Say.** "Where did you copy the name from?" Expect: the map's own label.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E14]] [[s01-E14b]] [[s01-E14c]] [[s01-E14d]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: match fewer cards; keep the Feature Card. 

### Words and supports

Words: river, lake, mountains, shoreline, cape, bay, pass, harbor. Frames: "The map shows ___. It might help because ___. The map cannot tell ___."

### Tomorrow

If many called a cape a bay, or the reverse (error F), or went by colour (error B), day 15 opens by sorting six labels or matching symbols to a key.
