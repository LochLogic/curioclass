# Day 11: Our state and its region

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put up the US political map and five-region key; have the map's credit ready. Choose the model state: Washington, or Michigan if our region is the West. Draw a board grid, columns A to F, rows 1 to 6. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 11 to 15 (key-exits-11-15.md), out of the room until slips are collected.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Five minutes: hand back day 10's exit tickets with the written cue. If over a third put a place on the wrong side (error L), all rebuild the grid; otherwise up to six do it at the side table. Review: on day 5 you named a grid cell, letter first. Mark B4 and children write its name; mark F2 and one child says it.

Five minutes, new: two places both have cold winters. **Ask.** Why might we put them in one group? Expect: they share something; the people there are not the same. Anyone who cannot say joins the small group in part 4.

#### 7 min. Our state among the fifty

Read the wall map's credit aloud. Review: in kindergarten you learned a state is a part of our country; in grade 2, a political map shows borders. Find our state by its printed name; pairs trace its border.

**Ask.** How do you know this is our state? Expect: its name is printed on it, and a border goes round it.

#### 8 min. A group of states

New: a region is a group of states that sit together and share something. Read the key: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, West. It is our classroom key; other maps group states differently; one region does not make people alike. Model with the model state: find it, read the key, find its name in one list, say the region. Lay the transparent physical map over it and read one water or land place beside it from its labels.

**Ask.** Our state is yellow and so is that far state. One region? Expect: colours only tell neighbours apart; check the key's lists.

#### 10 min. Our region

Pairs fill the Region Card: our state, our region, what the map shows beside the region (a lake, an ocean or another country), and in the third box cross out "next to ours" and write "in our region," one more state from the key's list. Cue six pairs; practice, not scored. The small group takes six minutes at the side table: made-up place pairs such as Pine Cove and Birch Hill (both have cold winters); they say what each pair shares, then fill two boxes.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the exit map's directions once. Every child writes on the half slip ([[s01-E11]], [[s01-E11b]]).

**Shared shape.** The Region Card starts from our own state on the wall map and fills a sentence. The exit names a new state to find, key beside it, and asks for the region, then a circle in a list. Missouri and Tennessee are not used before it.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the key onto paper. One projector: show the map and key.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall map: use a page of a US atlas.

**Skip.** The class repair and the pair talk.

- **10 min. Our state.** Find our state on a map by its printed name.
  **Say.** "How do you know this is our state?" Expect: its name is printed and a border goes round it.
- **20 min. Our region.** Read the five-region key together. Find our state's name in one list and say the region.
  **Say.** "Is a state in our region because of its colour?" Expect: no, check the key's lists.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E11]] [[s01-E11b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the Region Card keeps its first two boxes.

### Words and supports

Words: state, region, border, key. Frame: "Our state is ___. It is in the ___ region."

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who can read a region from the key. If many wrote a state's own name or a landform (error F), day 12 sorts four new labels; if many went by color (error B), it matches three symbols to a key. Day 12 labels every state of our region.

### Credit

Ours. The key and place pairs are invented by Curio Class; the map's credit is read aloud.
