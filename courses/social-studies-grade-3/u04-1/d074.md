# Day 74: One question, four sources

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 73's exit by error D (the oldest is primary) and M (primary must be true). Put the four cards in a set for each pair. You may show the museum's own page for the basket; children do not act out a speaker or make a replica. Keep key-sorts-73-74.md and key-exits-72-74.md out until the matching and slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 73's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on the more-missed of D or M, wrong answer beside right. D: "The oldest paper is primary for every question" beside "The Harvest Fair photograph is primary for who took part; the Stall Plan is primary for where stalls were planned." M: "It is primary, so it must be true" beside "It is firsthand; we still ask what it cannot tell." Whole class if many; otherwise a side table while the rest re-read the strip.

#### 8 min. The relationship test, one new source
The question for today: "How do these sources show Native people's use of plants?" Read card 2 aloud with its credit: a canoe basket made of cedar bark by Norma Hottowe Pendleton, Makah, in 1984 to 1985, kept by the National Museum of the American Indian. Fill the strip on the board: maker, date, was the maker there. It is primary for how she made a basket in that time. It is not primary for how people used cedar in earlier times, because she was not there. Say what it cannot tell.

#### 10 min. Who made it, and when
Read cards 1, 3 and 4 aloud with their credits: David Grignon's interview, the museum label and the Curio Class textbook page. Pairs match each of the four cards to its maker and year, and say whether that maker was there. Hear six pairs, a minute each, and note their names. Review (day 73): an oral history is primary for what the teller knows.

#### 15 min. Say what each can and cannot tell
Each child writes the maker of each card: [[s04-P74a]], [[s04-P74b]], [[s04-P74c]], [[s04-P74d]]. Pairs then say one thing each card can tell and one it cannot. Then each writes one question that one card could answer: [[s04-P74e]]. Words only. A child who needs it tells you at the side table.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Snow Records Card, first seen here. Read the card and the two asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E74]] and [[s04-E74b]]. Key: key-exits-72-74.md.

**Shared shape.** The practice matches four cards to their makers, and the exit asks which of two records is firsthand in a new case.

### Other rooms
No printer: write each card on the board and read it aloud with its credit.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: use any one photo and one story you can read.

**Skip.** The question of your own.

- **8 min. One source.** Read card 2. **Say.** "Was the maker there?" Expect: she made the basket herself, so primary for her craft.
- **10 min. Four cards.** Match each to its maker.
- **15 min. Makers.** [[s04-P74a]] [[s04-P74b]] [[s04-P74c]] [[s04-P74d]] [[s04-P74e]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E74]] [[s04-E74b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: the matching drops to eight minutes; keep every card in [[s04-P74a]] to [[s04-P74d]]. Longer: write a second question one card answers as the other kind.

### Words and supports
Words: primary, firsthand, secondary, oral history, maker. Frame: "For the question ___, this is primary or secondary because ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.
