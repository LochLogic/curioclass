# Section 4.1 opener: our question about the Native nations of our region

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 72 opens the unit question, "How have the Native nations of our region shaped this place, then and now?" The class sets a local map beside a short card from the nearest nation's own page. Children write the five supporting questions with their days and say who besides the class needs the answer. Day 73 teaches how to tell a primary source from a secondary one: by how it relates to the question. Day 74 applies that to four sources about plants and people.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A homeland is the land a nation's people have known as home for a very long time,** in the nation's own words. A climate region is a weather word. A homeland comes from the nation's own account.
- **The nations are here today.** Each is named by its own name, in the present tense, and governs itself. No card says "were" about a nation that lives on.
- **A source is primary or secondary for a question.** Primary means made by someone who was there, or who tells what they know themselves. Secondary means made by someone who was not there and looks back. The same map can be both, for two questions.
- **Primary does not mean true.** Always ask what the source cannot tell. A source is never primary just because it is old.
- **Oral history** is primary for the telling and for what the speaker knows. Whether a speaker saw an older event is a separate question.

## The errors in plain words

R is the idea that only the teacher needs our answer. K is the idea that a nation is gone, so no one needs it. D is the idea that an old source is primary for everything. M is the idea that a primary source must be true. Q is a question about looks, not about the past.

## Supplies

A local town physical map, the nearest nation's card (the teacher prints it from the nation's own page; a Northeast sample is in cards-day72-hook.md), the question board, the supporting-question record, the importance record, the relationship strip, the six sort cards, the four day 74 print cards with their credits, and the exit cards.

## A room with nothing

Draw the map and the strip by hand. Read each card aloud. Hold up the four day 74 cards one at a time. Children answer by speech, a labeled drawing or pointing. No child is asked about home, family or what a family believes.
