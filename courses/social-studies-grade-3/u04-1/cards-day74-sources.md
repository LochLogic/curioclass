# Day 74 cards: four sources, one question

The question for all four cards: "How do these sources show Native people's use of plants?" Read each card aloud with its credit. Children do not act out a speaker or make a replica.

**Card 1. Interview.** David Grignon is the tribal historic preservation officer of the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin. In the interview "Menominee History" on Wisconsin Public Television, made 25 August 2014, he says that in the fall they had wild rice, and that winter was a time of fishing and hunting. Credit: Wisconsin Public Television, PBS Wisconsin; our words.

**Card 2. Canoe basket.** A basket made by Norma Hottowe Pendleton, Makah. She made it in 1984 to 1985. It is made of cedar bark, plaited in a twill pattern. It is 28 by 28 centimeters. The National Museum of the American Indian keeps it. Credit: National Museum of the American Indian, catalog record 25/5373.

**Card 3. Museum label.** Written by the museum, not by the maker. It says: a canoe basket, Makah, made by Norma Hottowe Pendleton in 1984 to 1985, of cedar bark, twill-plaited. The museum bought it from her in 1985. Credit: National Museum of the American Indian.

**Card 4. Textbook page.** Written for Curio Class on 15 November 2026. It says: Plants give food and things to make. The Menominee and Makah cards show two nations' uses of plants. This page looks back at the other three cards. It was not made by anyone in them.

Children match each card to its maker and year, then say what each card can and cannot tell.
