# Day 73: Who was there?

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 72's exit by error R (only the teacher needs it) and K (the Nation is gone). Set out the cards. Keep key-sorts-73-74.md and key-exits-72-74.md out until the matching and slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Retrieval and repair
Hand back day 72's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark. Review (grade 2): who made it, and when. Pairs read the Seed Pot Records (made up) and write a line each: [[s04-P73a]], [[s04-P73b]], [[s04-P73c]]. Two minutes. Then three minutes on the more-missed of R or K, wrong answer beside right. R: "Only our teacher needs to know" beside "Families need to know the Nation lives today." K: "The Nation is gone, so nobody needs it" beside "The Nation lives and governs today", pointing to "today" on day 72's card.

#### 8 min. Photograph and map sourcing
Ask: "Who took part in Pond Day?" Show the Pond Day photograph (a parent who helped, 4 May) and the Pond Map (the class, 20 May). Fill the strip on the board. Say the rule once: a source is **primary** for a question if it was made by someone who was there, or who tells what they know themselves; we also say **firsthand**. It is **secondary** if made by someone who was not there and who looks back. The photograph is primary for Pond Day. The map was drawn later, so it is secondary for "Who took part?" but primary for "Where is the pond on 20 May?" One source, two questions. Review (day 9): what a source cannot tell. Primary does not mean true. Fill "The source shows ___, so ___."

#### 10 min. Compare records
Pairs add two cards to the strip: the Jug Card (a potter, 1970) and Boat Builder's Telling. An **oral history** is a telling a person gives about what they know; it is primary for what the teller knows. Pairs say a question for the telling; each child writes [[s04-P73d]] for the jug. Hear six pairs, a minute each, and note their names. If many missed the Seed Pot Records, take that group aside with two fresh records.

#### 15 min. Match makers
The question for all six cards: "Who took part in the Harvest Fair?" Read each card once. Pairs match each card to its maker (a visitor, a helper, a reporter, a book printer, a baker, the fair committee) and say whether that maker was at the fair. Then each child writes [[s04-P73e]] and [[s04-P73f]], and [[s04-P73g]] for card 6: one card can answer a different question. Reasons are about the maker, never age or looks.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Meeting Records Card, first seen here. Read the card and the two asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E73]] and [[s04-E73b]]. Key: key-exits-72-74.md.

**Shared shape.** The model gives one source two questions, the practice matches each card to its maker and a question it can answer; the exit chooses between two records.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the six cards on the board and draw the strip by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: use any photo and any note you have.

**Skip.** The oral history.

- **5 min. Two records.** Read the Seed Pot Records. [[s04-P73a]] [[s04-P73b]] [[s04-P73c]]
- **8 min. Primary.** **Say.** "Who made it, and were they there?" Expect: a maker who was there makes a primary source.
- **10 min. The jug.** [[s04-P73d]]
- **12 min. Six cards.** Match each to its maker. [[s04-P73e]] [[s04-P73f]] [[s04-P73g]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E73]] [[s04-E73b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: match four cards; keep [[s04-P73e]] and [[s04-P73g]]. Longer: a second question the same source answers the other way.

### Words and supports
Words: primary, firsthand, secondary, oral history, maker, question. Frame: "For the question ___, this is primary or secondary because ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
