# Day 4: The equator and the prime meridian

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make the overlay from sources 4A to 4G (city coordinates, read 2026-11-21). For our town, fill the dot from the town's own free page. Write the three sentence cards: Tokyo, Japan; Cape Town, South Africa; Mexico City, Mexico. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 3 to 6 (key-exits-3-6.md) out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Label two continents and one ocean on a blank outline: South America, Europe, Pacific Ocean. If many made error A on day 3's exit (s01-E3, s01-E3b), this is the repair instead: sort continent, ocean and hemisphere labels on a fresh globe view.

#### 10 min. Two lines

Hold one string loop at the equator of the globe and one over the prime meridian. Review: you met the equator in grade 2, the line round the middle. The prime meridian runs from pole to pole through Greenwich, England; people agreed on it (NOAA, source 1A). The children draw the equator in red and the prime meridian in blue on the World Outline Card. Say: "A hemisphere is a half of the globe." They label the four halves: northern, southern, eastern, western. Review: north, south, east and west from grade 1.

#### 14 min. Where in the world?

The class works a chart: place, continent, north or south of the equator, east or west of the prime meridian. Model Lima, Peru: South America, south, west. Pairs do Oslo, Norway; Sydney, Australia; Vancouver, Canada; then our town. Show the overlay to check.

**Ask.** How do you know Sydney is south and east? Expect: it is below the equator line and right of the prime meridian line.

#### 6 min. A location sentence

Each child takes one of the three city cards and says the sentence to a partner, then writes it: "___ is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres." Walk the room; hear any child who asks.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the card aloud once, then each ask once. Every child writes on the half slip ([[s01-E4]], [[s01-E4b]]), then answers in the exit box on the student page ([[s01-E4c]]). The card is first seen here.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in. With nothing: a ball and string; draw the lines on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No globe: use a ball and string. No chart: draw four columns.

**Skip.** The pair work and the overlay.

- **8 min. Two lines.** String round a ball for the equator, then pole to pole.
  **Say.** "Which line splits north from south?" Expect: the equator.
- **12 min. A chart.** Fill the chart for Lima and Oslo with the overlay.
- **10 min. A sentence.** Write one city sentence.
  **Say.** "Say it aloud first." Expect: a continent and two hemispheres.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E4]] [[s01-E4b]] [[s01-E4c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: chart two cities. Longer: a second sentence about our town.

### Words and supports

Words: equator, prime meridian, hemisphere, northern, southern, eastern, western. Frame: "___ is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres." A pointed card is an answer.

### Tomorrow

If many named an ocean or a hemisphere as the continent (error A), day 5's warm-up sorts the labels again. Then a letter-and-number grid and the words latitude and longitude.
