# Day 3: A globe and a flat map

## Teacher page

### Before class

The teaching Globe View Card must print the Pacific Ocean as well as its other labels; add it by hand if not. Put each pair's two cards and a blank outline in an envelope. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 3 to 6 (key-exits-3-6.md) out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Day 2's exit and exit box (s01-E2, s01-E2b, s01-E2c) came back. Read the more-missed letter from a card, then its right example. Error O: "I like bikes" beside "A rack in the doorway could block people, so the people who keep it clear care." Error J: "North could be the south door if I like" beside "North is where the arrow points; 'best' can mean nearest or out of the way." The other letter goes to a small group of up to 6 during the outline part, 5 minutes: sort one mapped fact and two priorities on cards.

#### 10 min. Retrieval, then repair

Pairs hold a Globe View Card and a flat world card. Review: in grade 2 you saw that a flat map must stretch, fold or cut. Four minutes: find South America and the Pacific Ocean on each card.

**Ask.** Does Greenland look the same size on both? Expect: no, it is drawn too large on the flat card.

Six minutes of repair: lay matching outlines side by side. Whole class if more than a third missed; otherwise a small group while the others begin the blank outline.

#### 7 min. A model and a picture

Turn the globe. Two volunteers trace Africa. Say: "A globe is a model of Earth, a small copy that is round like the real thing. A flat map is a picture of one view." Review: in kindergarten you said one way a map and a globe differ. Point to supporting question 1 on the board: Where are we on Earth?

**Ask.** Why can a flat map not copy a globe exactly? Expect: a round surface must stretch or cut to lie flat.

#### 10 min. Seven continents, five oceans

Read the whole word bank aloud once; the Southern Ocean is the water round Antarctica (NOAA, source 2A). Pairs write ten names in the numbered circles. Leave two circles empty today, the ones for Asia and the Indian Ocean. Give each pair one cue as you pass; practice, no score.

#### 4 min. Both ___, but ___

Review: in kindergarten you used "Both ___, but ___". Each pair writes one sentence on how a globe and a flat map are used, not on what the flat map changes: "Both show Earth, but the globe turns and the flat map lies flat."

#### 4 min. Exit ticket

Read each ask once. Every child circles Asia and writes on the half slip ([[s01-E3]], [[s01-E3c]]), then circles the Indian Ocean in the exit box on the student page ([[s01-E3b]]). The cards are first seen here.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in. With nothing: a ball, string and a wall map.

### At home

**Swap.** No globe: use a ball and a flat map from a book. No card set: draw a circle and a rectangle.

**Skip.** The pair work, the small group and the board.

- **8 min. Ball and map.** Roll a ball, then lay the map flat.
  **Say.** "Why can a flat map not copy a ball exactly?" Expect: a round shape must stretch or cut to lie flat.
- **12 min. Names.** Say the seven continents and five oceans from the word bank; label ten on the outline.
- **10 min. Sentence.** Write "Both show Earth, but ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E3]] [[s01-E3b]] [[s01-E3c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: label five names. Longer: find a place you know on both cards.

### Words and supports

Words: globe, model, flat, continent, ocean. Frame: "Both ___, but ___." A pointed card is an answer.

### Tomorrow

If many circled an ocean as a continent, or the reverse (error A), day 4 opens by sorting continent, ocean and hemisphere labels on a fresh globe view. Then the equator and the prime meridian.
