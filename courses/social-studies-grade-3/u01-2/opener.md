# Section 2 opener: where we are on Earth

## What the section does

Four days of 40 minutes, days 3 to 6. Day 3 compares a globe with a flat world map: a flat card changes sizes, shapes and edges, and the globe does not. Children name the seven continents and five oceans. Day 4 draws the equator and the prime meridian on a world outline, so each place can be placed in a northern or southern and an eastern or western hemisphere. Day 5 reads a letter-and-number grid, first on the classroom wall, then on a world grid card. Day 6 asks why places at the same distance from the equator can be warm and wet or warm and dry, using two park cards, and ends with a Where We Are card for our own town.

## How the days run

A short repair, a worked example, pairs work with a card, then a printed exit card (cards-exits-3-6.md) that every child answers in writing. The day's six are also heard.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A globe is round; a flat card must stretch, bend or cut.** Neither is wrong; each is best for a job.
- **Continents are the big land bodies; oceans are the big water bodies.** The Southern Ocean is the water round Antarctica.
- **The equator** is the line round the middle of Earth. Latitude lines run east to west. **Longitude** lines run north to south. The prime meridian is the line of 0 degrees longitude.
- **A place is in two hemispheres at once:** north or south of the equator, east or west of the prime meridian. We use the words, not the numbers.
- **A grid cell is read letter first, then number.**
- **Sunlight is more direct near the equator.** How much rain a place gets is a different fact from how warm it is.

## The errors in plain words

- **A:** continent, ocean and hemisphere are not the same word.
- **L:** a grid cell is read letter, then number.
- **E:** latitude alone does not decide climate; rain matters as well.

Wrong ideas are read from "someone might say" cards.

## Supplies for the section

A globe, the Globe View Card and flat world card, blank outline with word bank, two string loops, the World Outline Card, the location chart, the classroom grid map, the World Grid Card, a ball and a torch, the two park cards, the Where We Are card, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Use a ball and a wall map. Draw the grid on the floor with tape or chalk. Describe the park cards aloud.
