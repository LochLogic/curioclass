# Day 5: A letter and a number

## Teacher page

### Before class

Mark the classroom grid: columns A to D west to east, rows 1 to 4 south to north. Keep cell C3 clear of every teaching object. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 3 to 6 (key-exits-3-6.md) out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Two minutes: on a fresh outline, give the continent and two hemispheres of one place from day 4's cards that the child did not write (Tokyo, Cape Town or Mexico City). If many made error A on day 4's exit (s01-E4, s01-E4c), the other three minutes are the repair: sort the labels again.

#### 10 min. A letter and a number

Say: "A grid cell is a letter and a number, the letter first." Put a star on B3 and a paper shape on C1; children call each cell. Then place a token on A4, then D3. Find the door: the letter first, then the number. Pairs name the cell of the sink and the cell of the clock.

**Ask.** Which comes first, the letter or the number? Expect: the letter, then the number.

#### 10 min. Latitude and longitude

On the globe and a printed outline: lines that run east and west are latitude; lines that run from pole to pole are longitude (NOAA, sources 1A and 1B). Children draw one line of each on an outline and label them. The equator is a line of latitude; the prime meridian is a line of longitude. No numbers: they wait for grade 4.

#### 10 min. The world grid

On a World Grid Card, letters run across and numbers up. Place Lima, Peru (B2) and Oslo, Norway (D3). Pairs find the cell of Sydney (F1), Vancouver (A3), Tokyo (F3) and Cape Town (D1). Then each pair names a cell and the place in it.

**Ask.** Where do cells and lines agree? Expect: the equator runs through the middle of row 2; the prime meridian runs between columns C and D. Pairs mark the row that holds the equator.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read each ask once. Every child writes on the half slip ([[s01-E5]], [[s01-E5b]]). The grid is first seen here.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in. With nothing: draw the grid on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No class grid: draw a grid of the table. No tokens: use coins.

**Skip.** The pair work and the wall grid.

- **10 min. A grid.** Draw a four-by-four grid of the table; put a spoon on B3.
  **Say.** "Name the cell." Expect: B3, the letter first.
- **10 min. Two kinds of line.** On a ball or map, trace latitude east to west, and longitude pole to pole.
- **10 min. World grid.** Find Sydney and Vancouver on the grid.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E5]] [[s01-E5b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two places on the world grid. Longer: our town's cell.

### Words and supports

Words: grid, cell, column, row, latitude, longitude. Frame: "The cell is ___: the letter, then the number." A pointed cell is an answer.

### Tomorrow

If many wrote the number first, or counted rows from the north (error L), day 6's warm-up rebuilds the columns and rows on a fresh grid and places a new cell. Then sunlight and rain.
