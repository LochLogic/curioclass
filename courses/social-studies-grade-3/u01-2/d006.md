# Day 6: Sunlight, rain and where we are

## Teacher page

### Before class

Find our town's two weather facts, sun and rain, on a free page and credit it; fill them on the teacher copy of the Where We Are card. Darken the room a little for the torch. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 3 to 6 (key-exits-3-6.md) out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Place a star on a fresh grid and name its cell, one minute. If many made error L on day 5's exit (E5), this is the repair: rebuild the labelled columns and rows, then place a new cell.

#### 8 min. Sunlight on a ball

Shine a torch straight on the middle of a ball, then on the top, slanted. The spot is small and bright at the middle and spread out near the top. Two volunteers hold the ball; the class draws both spots. Say: "Near the equator the sun's light is more direct and warms more. Near the poles the same light spreads out and warms less" (NASA, sources 6E and 6F). This is not the seasons.

#### 10 min. Two places, one latitude

Death Valley National Park and Great Smoky Mountains National Park lie at about the same latitude (source 6D). Read the park cards aloud: Death Valley gets under 2 inches of rain a year and its summers can reach 120 degrees Fahrenheit; the Great Smoky Mountains lowlands get about 55 inches (National Park Service, sources 6A to 6C). Four mountain ranges stand between Death Valley and the sea. Review: in grade 2 the ridge made one side dry.

**Ask.** Does warm weather tell us how much rain a place gets? Expect: no, warmth and rain are two different facts.

#### 12 min. Where are we on Earth?

Pairs fill the Where We Are card for our town: continent, two hemispheres, cell on the world grid, how sunny, how rainy. Then each child writes one sentence for supporting question 1 on the board.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the Lowlands Card once, then each ask once. Every child writes on the half slip ([[s01-E6]], [[s01-E6b]]). The card is first seen here.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in. With nothing: a window or lamp and a ball; describe the park cards aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No torch: use a lamp and an orange. No card set: say the park facts.

**Skip.** The pair card and the class board.

- **8 min. Light on a ball.** Shine a lamp on an orange, straight on, then slanted.
  **Say.** "Where is the spot smaller and brighter?" Expect: straight on.
- **10 min. Two parks.** Read the park facts.
  **Say.** "Does warm weather tell us how much rain?" Expect: no, they are two facts.
- **12 min. Our town.** Write: "Our town is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E6]] [[s01-E6b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the second park fact. Longer: a place with a different rainfall.

### Words and supports

Words: direct, spread out, warm, cold, dry, wet, rain. Frame: "Our town is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres, and it is ___ and ___." A pointed card is an answer.
