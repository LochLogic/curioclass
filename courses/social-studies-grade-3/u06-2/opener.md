# Section 6.2 opener: the national plan and the list of rights

## What the section does

Two days of 40 minutes. Day 128 shows where the Constitution came from (a convention in Philadelphia in 1787, approved by the states), sorts its first sentences into three jobs and sets the national plan beside the state's, with an origins record. Day 129 teaches that the Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments, added in 1791, reads the speech and press clause closely, and sorts details to the three documents.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Three documents, three jobs.** The Declaration (1776) gives reasons; the Constitution (1787) sets up a government; the Bill of Rights (1791) lists rights that limit it (E). Teaching crops are the opening sentence of each article and the speech and press clause (sources.csv U6C, U6D).
- **Where the Constitution came from.** A convention met in Philadelphia in May 1787 and delegates signed on 17 September 1787; conventions in the states voted, and nine of thirteen were needed (U6E). The Declaration did not make it (P). Do not tell the debates.
- **Three jobs, three bodies.** Congress makes laws, a President carries them out, courts decide cases. The plan splits the work so one person does not do it all (K).
- **The state beside the nation.** The default state is Massachusetts: two branches of lawmakers, a Governor, and courts (U6R). A teacher in another state swaps in the class's own passages from unit 2's chart.
- **A right is a limit on government** (O), not a gift. A right on paper and a right protected for everyone were not always the same; day 130 asks who.
- **No belief is asked of any child.** The religion phrase is read with a gloss only on the day 129 exit.

## The errors in plain words

E is thinking the three documents do the same job. K is thinking the President makes every law. O is thinking a right is a gift the government can take back. P is thinking the Declaration made both plans.

## Supplies

Paper, pencils, the 1787 order strip (three cards), the job baskets from unit 2, the unit 2 state chart, the origins record sheet, detail cards. Cards: cards-day128.md and cards-day129.md; exit cards: cards-exits-128-129.md; key (teacher only): key-exits-128-129.md.

## A room with nothing

Copy the three crops large on paper. Use three paper baskets labeled makes laws, carries out laws, decides cases.

## Unsettled facts for the day writers

- Do not print Massachusetts Declaration of Rights Articles V or IX on day 128; they are the day 136 glosses. Day 128 prints only the history caption (U6R).
- The packet B crops (Article I Section 8, Article II Section 3, Article III Section 2, Article I Section 2, the petition clause) and the religion phrase are held; no teaching day reads them.
- Interim review: grade 2 countries and unit 2's three-job strip are named as earlier work; claim them only if the ledger lists them at stage written, otherwise teach as new and list.
