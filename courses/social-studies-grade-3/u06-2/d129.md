# Day 129: The Bill of Rights lists rights

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 128's slips and records with one cue. Write the six provisions on cards. Do not show the Phrase Card before the exit; no child is asked what they believe. Keep key-exits-128-129.md with you.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 128's slips and records (s06-E128, s06-E128b, s06-P128a); the records return to the folder for day 134. If many wrote that the President or one person does every job (error K), sort six made-up provisions to the three baskets: a council votes on a bridge (makes laws); a mayor sees the bridge law kept (carries out); a court decides who owns a dock (decides cases); a council sets dock fees; a mayor opens the new dock; a court hears a dock quarrel. Few missed: those children take the cards at the side table. Error P children take the order strip for five minutes.

#### 8 min. Why a list
Amendment is new: a change or addition. The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments, added to the Constitution after it began. Congress proposed amendments in 1789; ten were approved on 15 December 1791. Their introduction spoke of extra clauses to explain and limit the government. Review (grade 1): a right is something every person here may do or have. Place 1791 on the strip, one centimeter after 1790. **Ask.** "Why add a list of rights?" Expect: to say what the government may not do.

#### 8 min. One right, closely
Read the First Amendment words once: "abridging the freedom of speech, or of the press". Gloss: the government may not stop people from speaking or printing their ideas. Pairs say one thing the clause lets a person do. Each child then shows one made-up case, [[s06-P129a]].

#### 5 min. A right is a limit
Complete a two-column card on the board: the right, speech and press; what it stops the government from doing, stopping people's ideas. A right is not a gift to take back (error O). Say plainly: a right on paper and a right kept for everyone were not always the same. Day 130 asks who.

#### 6 min. Three documents
Each child draws three cards on the strip by their years and writes each one's job, [[s06-P129b]]: 1776, reasons for independence; 1787, a plan of government; 1791, a list of rights. Hear a rotating six, one card each, and name them on the record.

#### 3 min. Exit
Read the Phrase Card aloud once with its words to know, then each ask once: [[s06-E129]] and [[s06-E129b]]. Key: key-exits-128-129.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice makes a case from the speech clause and places three documents by year; the exit gives a new phrase to explain and place by its job.

**Swap.** No strip: draw a line on paper.

**Skip.** The repair and the pair talk.

- **5 min. Why a list.** Read the Bill of Rights part. **Say.** "Why add a list of rights?" Expect: to say what the government may not do.
- **10 min. One right.** Read the speech words. [[s06-P129a]]
- **10 min. Three documents.** [[s06-P129b]]
- **5 min. A limit.** **Say.** "What may the government not do?" Expect: stop people from speaking or printing ideas.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E129]] [[s06-E129b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the speech case and the three years. Longer: sort the three-documents detail cards to independence, a plan or a list of rights.

### Words and supports
Words: amendment, Bill of Rights, right, limit. Frame: "The government may not ___." Read every card aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow
