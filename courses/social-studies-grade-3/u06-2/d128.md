# Day 128: Where the national plan came from

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 127's slips and pages with one cue. Set out three paper baskets. The cards give Massachusetts; swap in your state's plan and caption from unit 2's chart if you teach elsewhere. Keep key-exits-128-129.md with you. Kestrel Isle is made up; do not show it before the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 127's slips and pages (s06-E127, s06-E127b, s06-P127a, s06-P127b, s06-P127c). If many wrote "whoever is in charge decides" (error T), read a fresh made-up pair: Captain Rue asks the village what rule to make; Captain Dov only says "obey". **Ask.** "Which has the people's agreement?" Expect: Rue. If many wrote "the leader owns their rights" (error O), set a fresh clause beside the limit it puts on a ruler.

#### 5 min. Retrieval
Review (grade 1): our state sits inside our country. Review (unit 2, day 41): a constitution is a basic plan that sets up a government, says what it may and may not do, and may list rights. **Ask.** "What is a constitution for?" Expect: it sets up a government and limits it.

#### 8 min. Where the national plan came from
Lay the three order cards: delegates met in Philadelphia in May 1787; they wrote a plan and signed it on 17 September; the states voted on it in their own conventions, and nine of thirteen had to say yes. Place 1787 on the strip, seven centimeters after 1780. **Ask.** "Did the Declaration make the plan?" Expect: no (error P); it gave reasons in 1776, and the Constitution sets up a government.

#### 8 min. The three parts
Review (unit 2, days 28 and 29): three jobs in three baskets. Read each opening sentence once and place it yourself while the class names its job aloud: Article I, Congress makes laws; Article II, a President carries out laws; Article III, courts decide cases. Say plainly: the plan splits the work so one person does not do it all (error K).

#### 5 min. Beside our state
Read the state's passage from unit 2's chart: two houses of lawmakers, a Governor and courts. Say one way alike (three jobs) and one different (state or country). Pairs fill the state table, [[s06-P128b]], and check it against the chart; the page stays in the folder.

#### 5 min. Origins record
Read the state caption and the national caption once. Each child fills two rows, [[s06-P128a]]: who drafted and who approved each plan. Hear a rotating six, about 50 seconds each, and name them on the record. The record is kept; it comes back on day 134 with a note.

#### 4 min. Exit
Read the Kestrel Isle Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E128]] and [[s06-E128b]]. Key: key-exits-128-129.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The class places each opening sentence and the pairs fill a state table; the exit gives a new card to name the job and the national body.

### Other rooms
No baskets: draw three boxes on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No baskets: three labeled sheets of paper.

**Skip.** The retrieval and the repair.

- **5 min. A plan.** **Say.** "What is a constitution for?" Expect: it sets up a government and limits it.
- **10 min. Where it came from.** Read the three cards in order. **Say.** "Did the Declaration make the plan?" Expect: no, delegates wrote it and the states approved it.
- **10 min. Three jobs.** Read the three sentences; name each job.
- **5 min. Our state, and the record.** [[s06-P128b]] [[s06-P128a]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E128]] [[s06-E128b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: read one crop only. Longer: find the three jobs in your town.

### Words and supports
Words: Constitution, delegate, convention, branch, approve. Frame: "___ drafted it and ___ approved it." Read every card aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 129 adds the list of rights. If many wrote that the President or one person did every job (error K) on E128, its return part sorts fresh provisions to the three baskets for ten minutes. Error P: a small group with the order strip.
