# Section 5.3 opener: St. Augustine, Jamestown and the 1622 war

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 97: St. Augustine, 1565, placed on the strip, with a Spanish leader and the Timucua, whose town Seloy stood there. Day 98: Jamestown, 1607, and Wahunsonacock of the Powhatan, with the Pamunkey's own history. Day 99: the 1622 war told from two papers, with the maker and purpose of each named first.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Who answered to whom.** A leader card has name, nation, role, decision, interest and who each answered to. A box stays blank when the source is silent; say so aloud. This is a lesson: a card is only as full as the record.
- **St. Augustine.** On 8 September 1565 Pedro Menendez de Aviles and 800 Spanish settlers founded it (sources.csv U5B). The Spanish settled at Seloy, a Timucua town, from September 1565 to May 1566, and turned its council house into a fort (U5A). Say "Timucua" by that name. No Timucua leader is on the page for Seloy, so none is named; no Timucua decision is taught.
- **Jamestown.** James Fort was founded in 1607 by the Virginia Company (U5L). Wahunsonacock (called Powhatan) led about thirty groups; the Pamunkey were one of six core tribes of Tsenacomoco (U5C). The Pamunkey Indian Tribe was recognised by the United States in 2015 as a sovereign nation and lives on its reservation today (U5D). Say it in the present tense.
- **The 1622 war.** A second war began in 1622 (U5C). A 1646 treaty ended the third (U5C). The Waterhouse report of 1622 is only a label card: the Virginia Company's official response to the attack, meant to reassure investors and migrants (U5O). No passage of it was read.
- **Sourcing, again.** Who made it, when, why. The war is named plainly. No role play.

## The errors in plain words

AC is the idea that one leader rules everyone. C is trusting one account as the whole story. X is a cause with no date: put the dates in order before saying what caused what.

## Unsettled facts for the day writers

- The plan asks for Menendez's title and a Timucua leader's decision; both are cut (not on a page read). Menendez's role is only "led the settlers"; his decision is only the founding.
- The plan pairs Wahunsonacock with John Smith; Smith's role at Jamestown is not on a page read, so day 98 uses the Virginia Company beside Wahunsonacock and an invented pair.
- The Virginia Company's aims are not on a page read. Reuse the day 93 reason cards without claiming the company's aims.
- Wahunsonacock's aims (land, security) are cut. The siege and the food pressure are held back for E98; do not teach them. Dossier B drops the Seloy food line and the siege line.
- Do not name a Native nation that was not named by its own page.
