# Exit cards, days 97 to 99

Print "made up, for practice" on invented cards. The teacher reads each stem aloud once. Answers are spoken, pointed or written.

## Day 97: Seloy Exit Card

Read aloud once. After a while, the Spanish colonists turned to their Timucua hosts for food. (Florida Museum, sources.csv U5A.)

Ask: Seloy Exit Card. What could the Timucua decide about that food?

## Day 98: Pamunkey Exit Card

Read aloud once. In the winter of 1609 to 1610, colonists pressed the Powhatan towns for food. Wahunsonacock ordered a siege of the English fort. (Pamunkey history, U5C.)

Ask: Pamunkey Exit Card. Why did Wahunsonacock order a siege of the fort?

## Day 99: Pier Rule Pair (made up)

Paper 1, by the pier owner: Skiffs may not tie up at the pier after sunset. This rule keeps the pier safe.
Paper 2, by the skiff club: The sunset rule is unfair. Skiffs need a place to tie up after dark.

Ask 1: Pier Rule Pair. Why might Paper 1, by the owner, defend the rule?
Ask 2: Pier Rule Pair. Why might Paper 2, by the skiff club, push for change?

The keys are in key-exits-97-99.md. Do not give them to children.
