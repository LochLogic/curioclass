# Day 81: The Makah Tribe and the shore

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 80's exit by error B (the state edge is the homeland edge). Print a chain strip for each child, the cards and the Penobscot map for each pair. No costumes, no sacred objects and no staged hunt today. Keep key-exits-77-82.md out until the slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 80's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on error B, wrong answer beside right, using the Penobscot map from day 75. B: "The state border is where the homeland ends" beside "The dashed line is the state's mark. The shading is the homeland. They are different marks." Children point to each on the map. If many missed it, keep a small group at the side table through the pairs part.

#### 8 min. Makah case
Read the Makah card twice with its credit. It is one nation, not every nation of the West. Present tense: the Makah Tribe's offices are at Neah Bay, Washington, and its page lists a Makah Tribal Council. The page names five permanent villages, all on the shore at the northwest corner of the continental United States. Say: "A village by the shore is a place. Why there? We need the shore and what people did." Review (4.2): the chain, feature, practice, place.

#### 10 min. Deah village account
Read the Deah account twice with its credit: Deah is one of the five villages, and today it is called Neah Bay. On the board: the feature is the shore; the practice is making canoes, fishing and drying fish; the place is the village. Pairs fill the chain strip. Hear six pairs, a minute each. Children write [[s04-P81a]] and [[s04-P81b]].

#### 15 min. Explain shore settlement
The "both parts" test: a feature and a practice, never one alone. Read two claims aloud and each child answers what is missing: [[s04-P81c]] and [[s04-P81d]]. Pointing, drawing arrows and a scribe are all answers.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Shore Case, first seen here. It says made up, for practice. Read the card and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E81]] and [[s04-E81b]]. Key: key-exits-77-82.md. Mark each slip shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice go from one nation's page to its village and test two claims for a missing part; the exit gives a made-up modern town and asks the child to explain it.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the Deah account on the board and draw the strip by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: read them aloud from the teacher's page.

**Skip.** The second claim, [[s04-P81d]].

- **8 min. The Tribe.** Read the Makah card twice. **Say.** "Where are the villages?" Expect: on the shore.
- **10 min. The village.** Read the Deah account twice. [[s04-P81a]] [[s04-P81b]]
- **15 min. The test.** [[s04-P81c]] [[s04-P81d]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E81]] [[s04-E81b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: [[s04-P81a]], [[s04-P81b]] and [[s04-P81c]]. Longer: say one thing the Deah account does not tell.

### Words and supports
Words: Makah Tribe, village, shore, canoe, feature, practice. Frame: "The ___ and the ___ together explain ___." Pointing, drawing arrows and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 82 compares the five cases and places three nations of our region. It opens with whichever of error C (the feature decided) or J (every nation does the same) more children made today.
