# Day 79: The Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 78's exit by error I (everyone chose to leave) or P (every Cherokee person left). Print the Midwest outline for each child, the card and a strip for each pair. Keep key-exits-77-82.md out until the slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 78's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on the more-missed of I or P, wrong answer beside right. I: "Everyone chose to leave" beside "The United States government forced people to leave." P: "Every Cherokee person left" beside "Many were forced out; the Eastern Band's people stayed in North Carolina." The other letter meets you at the side table during the pairs part.

#### 8 min. Menominee case
Read the card twice with its credit. It says where the Nation governs today and where its people once lived. This is one nation, not every nation of the Midwest. Present tense: the Nation governs from Keshena today. Review (unit 2): the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin has its own constitution; that is a reminder only. Say what the page does not say: it gives places, and it does not say why people lived there. Where we do not know, we write "the page does not say." We do not fill the gap ourselves.

#### 10 min. Homeland and ways of life
Pairs fill a chain strip from the card. The place box takes what the card names; the feature and practice boxes take "the page does not say." Then read aloud one line from another source already read, with its maker and date: David Grignon, the Menominee tribal historic preservation officer, says in the 2014 Wisconsin Public Television interview that in the fall they had wild rice (review, 4.1 day 74). Pairs may add it to the practice box with his name. Hear six pairs, a minute each. Children write [[s04-P79b]] and [[s04-P79c]].

#### 15 min. Explain locations
Each child shades the Menominee homeland on the Midwest outline from the card: [[s04-P79a]]. The shading crosses state lines and has no hard edge. Then they finish the sheet's two lines: the card says why it lies there ("the page does not say") and the chain with its blanks. The College account stays unseen until the exit.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Shawano Lake Card, first seen here. Read the card and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E79]] and [[s04-E79b]]. Key: key-exits-77-82.md. Mark each slip shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice take one nation's card for where it governs and lived; the exit gives a college's passage about one lake and asks why people camped there and what it shows.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the outline of four states on the board; read the card aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed outline: draw a box and label it Wisconsin.

**Skip.** The chain, [[s04-P79c]].

- **8 min. The card.** Read it twice. **Say.** "Where does the Nation govern today?" Expect: Keshena, Wisconsin.
- **10 min. The page.** [[s04-P79b]] [[s04-P79c]]
- **15 min. The outline.** [[s04-P79a]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E79]] [[s04-E79b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: [[s04-P79a]] and [[s04-P79b]]. Longer: say one thing a second page could tell us that this card does not.

### Words and supports
Words: Nation, homeland, Wisconsin, Keshena, the page does not say. Frame: "The page says ___. It does not say ___." Pointing, shading and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 80 takes the Navajo Nation. It opens with error J (every nation does the same) from today's exit if many children made it: two nations' own pages side by side.

### Credit
Ours. Facts: the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin's Brief History page, and Wisconsin Public Television's 2014 interview with David Grignon. The Shawano Lake Card is in our words from the College of Menominee Nation.
