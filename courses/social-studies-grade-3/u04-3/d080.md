# Day 80: The Navajo Nation and the San Juan River

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 79's exit by error J (every nation does the same, or all Menominee people lived at one lake). Print a chain strip and the legend for each child, the two cards for each pair. Use only the card's first two paragraphs: the schematic in the card file is not printed today. Keep key-exits-77-82.md out until the slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 79's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on error J, two nations' own pages side by side. Wrong: "Every nation lives the same way." Right: "The Penobscot page tells of rivers. The Menominee page names a village and a reservation. Each page tells about one nation." Then the Menominee card again: "It names where the Nation governs; it does not name where every person lived."

#### 8 min. Navajo case
Read the Navajo card twice with its credit. The Navajo Nation is in the Southwest and governs itself today; the Council delegates meet in the Council Chambers. Review (grade 1): the Navajo Nation governs itself. Children write [[s04-P80d]]. Nothing sacred is taught: no mountains, stories, designs or ceremony. Review (4.2): the legend has three marks, the homeland shading, the square for lands held today and the dashed state border. A state border is one mark for the state and the homeland shading is another. A nation's homeland can cross state lines. Say it once, with no practice.

#### 10 min. Land and water
Read the San Juan River account twice with its credit. The legal detail stays with you. On the board: the feature is the river; the practice is irrigation, carrying water through a network of irrigation facilities; the place is the farm fields. Pairs fill the chain strip with those three. Hear six pairs, a minute each. Children write [[s04-P80a]] and [[s04-P80b]].

#### 15 min. Explain locations
Each child writes one sentence about how the river and irrigation together explain where the fields are: [[s04-P80c]]. The river alone is not an answer; the water and what people do with it are. Pointing, drawing arrows and a scribe are all answers.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Navajo Exit Locator, first seen here. Read the passage, the picture's label and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E80]] and [[s04-E80b]]. Key: key-exits-77-82.md. Mark each slip shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice go from one card's river and irrigation to where the fields lie; the exit gives a sentence about states and a small picture, and asks what the passage names and whether state edges mark a homeland edge.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the river account on the board and draw the strip by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: read them aloud from the teacher's page.

**Skip.** The second ask of the strip, [[s04-P80b]].

- **8 min. The Nation.** Read the Navajo card twice. [[s04-P80d]]
- **10 min. The river.** Read the account twice. **Say.** "What does the river water do for the fields?" Expect: irrigation carries it to them. [[s04-P80a]] [[s04-P80b]]
- **15 min. Your sentence.** [[s04-P80c]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the passage and label once. [[s04-E80]] [[s04-E80b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: [[s04-P80a]] and [[s04-P80c]]. Longer: say what the account does not tell about the fields.

### Words and supports
Words: Navajo Nation, Council, river, irrigation, fields, state border. Frame: "The river and the ___ together explain ___." Pointing, drawing arrows and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 81 takes the Makah Tribe. It opens with error B (the state edge is the homeland edge) from today's exit if many children made it: a state outline laid over a nation's own map.
