# Day 82: Five cases, one question

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 81's exit by error C (the bay decided) or J (every group lives by a bay). Draw a rough US outline on the board. Print the table and the New England outline for each child, the chains and cards for each pair. Keep key-exits-77-82.md out until the slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 81's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on the more-missed of C or J, wrong answer beside right. C: "The bay decided" beside "People keep boats there and fish, so they live by it." J: "Every group lives by a bay" beside "One page tells how one village lives." The other letter meets you at the side table during the pairs part.

#### 8 min. Compare worked cases
Read the five one-line chains aloud, one per nation, and write each nation's name by its place on the US outline: the Penobscot Nation, Cherokee Nation, Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin, Navajo Nation and Makah Tribe. Each is one example, never its whole region. Revise region aloud: you learned the five-region key in unit 1 as a classroom tool; a nation's homeland can cross state lines. Ask: "What did land and what people do explain in each?" Take two answers.

#### 10 min. Locate three regional nations
Pairs place three numbered dots on the New England outline from the day 75 cards: the Penobscot Nation, the Narragansett Indian Tribe and the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah). The Aquinnah card's page says its people have lived on the island of Noepe for at least 10,000 years. Children write [[s04-P82d]]. Hear six pairs, a minute each. On the question board, answer "Why settle here?": land and what people do, together.

#### 15 min. Explain settlement comparisons
Pairs pick one nation of our region and one other case, then fill the compare table. A nation's card that is silent gets "the page does not say." Children write [[s04-P82a]], [[s04-P82b]] and [[s04-P82c]]. Judge the sentence with four look-fors: a feature from each case, a practice from each, one thing the same or different, and no claim about all nations. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Potter Card and the Dairy Farm Card, first seen here. Both say made up, for practice. Read the cards and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E82]] and [[s04-E82b]]. Key: key-exits-77-82.md. Mark each slip shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice compare real nations' cards in a table and on a map; the exit gives two made-up places, explains one, then compares the two.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the table on the board; read the chains aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed outline or table: draw a five-row table by hand.

**Skip.** The dots on the outline.

- **8 min. Five cases.** Read the five chains from the teacher's page. **Say.** "Which two used water?" Expect: any honest pair, such as the Penobscot and Navajo Nations.
- **10 min. Our region.** Place the three dots. [[s04-P82d]]
- **15 min. The table.** [[s04-P82a]] [[s04-P82b]] [[s04-P82c]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read both cards once. [[s04-E82]] [[s04-E82b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one nation, [[s04-P82a]] and [[s04-P82c]]. Longer: pick a different pair and write a second sentence.

### Words and supports
Words: feature, practice, place, same, different, region. Frame: "The ___ and the ___ are the same, but ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.
