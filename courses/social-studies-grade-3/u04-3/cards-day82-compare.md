# Day 82: five one-line chains and the compare table

Each is one example, never its whole region. Say: homelands cross state lines, and the five-region key is a classroom tool.

1. **Penobscot Nation, Northeast.** People rely on the river for food and travel.
2. **Cherokee Nation, Southeast.** The Nation once held much of the Southeast. The United States forced the people to leave. Today the Nation governs in eastern Oklahoma.
3. **Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin, Midwest.** Menominee historic preservation officer David Grignon says, in a 2014 interview, that in the fall the Menominee had wild rice. The Tribe's page says the Menominee once lived across Wisconsin and nearby states. Today the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin governs from Keshena.
4. **Navajo Nation, Southwest.** The San Juan River waters the farms in its valley.
5. **Makah Tribe, West.** The Tribe's page tells how people at Deah made canoes and fished. The Tribe is at Neah Bay today.

Then place three nations of our region on the New England outline from day 75: the Penobscot Nation, the Narragansett Indian Tribe and the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah). The board answers "Why settle here?"
