# Section 4.3 opener: five nations, five places

## What the section does

Six days of 40 minutes. Each of days 77 to 81 takes one nation as one example, never as the whole of its region: the Penobscot Nation (Northeast), the Cherokee Nation and the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians (Southeast, today in Oklahoma and North Carolina), the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin (Midwest), the Navajo Nation (Southwest) and the Makah Tribe (West). Each card is in our words from the nation's own page, in the present tense. Day 82 compares the cases on a map and places the three regional nations again.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Each case rests on its page and no more.** Where a page says nothing, we say "the page does not say".
- **Land and practice together explain a place.** The stream, the shore, the lake: each needs a practice beside it. A feature alone never decides.
- **Removal is said plainly.** In 1838 the United States forced the Cherokee people from their homelands, and many died. No detail, no reenactment. A child may look away or pass.
- **Cherokee nations are separate.** The Cherokee Nation and the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians each speak for their own people. There is more than one Cherokee nation.
- **A dot shows presence only.** A state edge is not a homeland edge.
- **Nothing sacred.** No sacred places, stories, designs or ceremonies; no costumes or staged hunts.

## The errors in plain words

K is saying a nation's work is past only. C and L are the feature errors. I is saying people chose to leave. P is saying every Cherokee person left. J is saying every nation does the same. B is taking a state edge for the homeland edge. S is taking a worked nation for a regional one.

## Supplies

Five nation cards, two period maps, a Midwest outline, a Navajo schematic, the compare table and a US outline. Exit cards for days 77 to 82. The chain strip from section 4.2.

## A room with nothing

Read each card aloud twice. Draw the chain on the board. Children point to a feature, a practice and a place. A spoken chain is a full answer.
