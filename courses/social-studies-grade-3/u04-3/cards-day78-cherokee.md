# Day 78: two Cherokee cards and two maps

Read aloud twice. Say the removal plainly, with no detail. A child may look away or pass. There is more than one Cherokee nation. These are two of them, each named by its own name. The grade 2 Homeland and Removal Map is only a reminder.

**Card 1. The Cherokee Nation.** The Cherokee Nation once held a large territory across much of the Southeast. In 1838 the United States government forced the Cherokee people to leave. This is called the Trail of Tears. About one in four of the people died. Today the Cherokee Nation governs itself. It uses its treaty rights and its own authority over the Cherokee Nation Reservation in eastern Oklahoma. Credit: the Cherokee Nation's own History page.

**Card 2. The Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians.** After the Trail of Tears, some Cherokee people remained in the East. They rebuilt in North Carolina. Today they are the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians. They govern themselves at Cherokee, North Carolina, in the Qualla Boundary. The page says Cherokee is a sovereign nation with its own laws, elections and government. Credit: the Eastern Band's own Government page.

Pairs fill: who, where, when, for both cards.
