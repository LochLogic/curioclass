# Day 81: Makah case card and the Deah account

One nation, one example. Present tense. No costumes, no sacred objects and no staged hunt. Read aloud twice.

**The Makah Tribe.** The Makah Tribe's offices are at Neah Bay, Washington. The Tribe's page lists a Makah Tribal Council. The page names five permanent Makah villages. All of them stand on the shore at the northwest corner of the continental United States. Credit: the Makah Tribe's own page.

**The village of Deah.** Deah was one of the five villages. Today it is called Neah Bay, where the Tribe's offices are. The Tribe's page tells how, in earlier times, people made canoes from cedar for halibut and salmon fishing. Halibut were caught, dried or smoked and stored for winter. Credit: the Makah Tribe's own page, in our words.

Pairs fill the chain: shore, boats and fishing, village.

The "both parts" test: a feature and a practice, never one alone.
