# Day 77: The Penobscot Nation, then and now

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 76's exit by error C (the river decided), J (every nation does the same) or L (a feature listed alone). Print the Penobscot card and map for each pair. The exit slip holds the Stream Card; keep key-exits-77-82.md out until the slips are in.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 76's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on the most-missed of C, J or L, wrong answer beside right. C: "The river decided where they lived" beside "People moved to the river for food, so they lived near it." J: "Every nation uses the water the same way" beside "One page tells how one nation uses its water." L: "There is a shore" beside "People moved to the shore in summer, so they lived near it." The other letters meet you at the side table during the pairs part.

#### 8 min. Penobscot case
Each case starts from the nation's own page, so the nation speaks for itself. The Penobscot Nation is one example, not every nation. Read the card twice with its credit; it is told in the present tense, because the Nation is here today. Review (4.2): the chain, feature, practice, place. On the board: the feature is the rivers; the practice is relying on them for food, drinking water and travel; the place is Maine's rivers. Say: "The Nation relies on Maine's rivers." The page does not say where the homeland ends. Then the Chief and Council: that is how the Nation governs itself today. Review (4.2): where a page is silent, we write "the page does not say." Model one silence: the card does not say how many people live at Indian Island.

#### 10 min. Map and account
Pairs set the card beside the Penobscot map from day 75 and fill the chain strip. Move among pairs and hear six, a minute each. Write the card's words for the practice on the board. Children write [[s04-P77a]] and [[s04-P77c]]. The strip answers both; a pointed arrow or a scribe is an answer.

#### 15 min. Explain settlement
Each child writes one chain from the card on a fresh strip: [[s04-P77d]]. Then one sentence about today: [[s04-P77b]]. A sentence about the Chief and Council or about Indian Island is right. A sentence in the past tense only is not yet seen: ask "Is the Nation here today?"

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Stream Card, first seen here. Read the card and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E77]] and [[s04-E77b]]. Key: key-exits-77-82.md. Mark each slip shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice go from a card's river to where the Nation is; the exit gives a different page, about a stream, and asks why the work is there and whether it is now.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the card on the board and draw the strip by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed card: read it aloud from the teacher's page.

**Skip.** The second strip.

- **8 min. The card.** Read the Penobscot card twice. **Say.** "What does the Nation rely on the river for?" Expect: food, drinking water, travel.
- **10 min. The strip.** [[s04-P77a]] [[s04-P77c]]
- **15 min. Your chain.** [[s04-P77d]] [[s04-P77b]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E77]] [[s04-E77b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: one chain, [[s04-P77d]], and the sentence. Longer: read the card again and say one thing the page does not say about the Nation.

### Words and supports
Words: Nation, river, feature, practice, today, the page does not say. Frame: "The Nation ___ the rivers, so ___." Pointing, drawing arrows and a scribe are all answers.
