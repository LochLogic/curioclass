# Day 78: Homeland and lands today

## Teacher page

### Before class
Tally day 77's exit by error K (the work is long ago only), C or L. Print the two maps and the legend for each child, the two cards for each pair. Keep key-exits-77-82.md out until the slips are in. Read card 1 over once yourself: it says removal plainly, and you stop there.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 77's exit tickets and the written task, each with a mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Then five minutes on the most-missed of K, C or L, wrong answer beside right. K: "The Nation fished here long ago" beside "The Nation works on this stream now." C: "The stream decided" beside "The Nation rebuilds lake outlets and streams, and expects more harvest as fish grow." L: "There is a stream with fish" beside "Fish live in these lakes and streams, and the Nation expects more harvest." The other letters meet you at the side table during the pairs part.

#### 8 min. Cherokee case
Read card 1 twice with its credit. Say plainly, once: "In 1838 the United States government forced the Cherokee people to leave their homelands." Stop there: no detail, no acting out, no pictures. A child may look away or pass, and nobody is asked about their own family. Review (grade 2): removal is when the government forces a nation to leave its homeland; you met the Cherokee Nation's forced move then. Then the Nation today: it governs itself in eastern Oklahoma. There is more than one Cherokee nation; each speaks for its own people. Review (4.2): the legend's homeland shading and lands-held-today square. On the board: shading is the homeland; the square is lands held today.

#### 10 min. Removal and remaining communities
Read card 2 twice with its credit: the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians governs itself in North Carolina today. Pairs fill who, where and when for both cards. Move among pairs and hear six, a minute each. Children write [[s04-P78a]] and [[s04-P78b]]. Write the cards' words for "where" on the board.

#### 15 min. Compare maps
Each child gets the two maps, period-labelled, and the legend. Read map 1 and map 2 aloud with their labels. Each child marks what lies outside the lands held today: [[s04-P78c]]. Then one fact about the forced move: [[s04-P78d]]. Pointing, shading and a scribe are all answers.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Choctaw Card, first seen here. Read the card and the asks once. Every child writes [[s04-E78]] and [[s04-E78b]]. Key: key-exits-77-82.md. Mark each slip shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The model and practice read two real Cherokee cards and two maps; the exit gives a real nation of another region, the Choctaw, and asks why a Nation's lands are elsewhere and whether all left.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the two maps on the board with the three marks; read the cards aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed maps: draw two outlines and the three marks by hand.

**Skip.** The marking, [[s04-P78c]].

- **8 min. Card 1.** Read it plainly. Your child may look away or pass. **Say.** "Where does the Cherokee Nation govern today?" Expect: eastern Oklahoma.
- **10 min. Card 2.** [[s04-P78a]] [[s04-P78b]]
- **15 min. The maps.** [[s04-P78c]] [[s04-P78d]]
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** Read the card once. [[s04-E78]] [[s04-E78b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: card 2 and [[s04-P78b]] only; keep [[s04-P78c]]. Longer: say what the maps do not show.

### Words and supports
Words: homeland, lands held today, removal, forced, Cherokee Nation, Eastern Band. Frame: "The homeland was ___; today ___." Pointing, shading and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 79 takes the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin. It opens with whichever of error I (people chose to leave) or P (every Cherokee person left) more children made today.
