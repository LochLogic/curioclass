# Day 142: Who has lived here

## Teacher page

### Before class
Read the Local Nation Account and the town's own record of its beginning (Boston: the Massachusett Tribe at Ponkapoag's own page and the town's record, teacher-supplied). Start the wall strip with equal marks for equal years. Keep key-exits-141-145.md. Ask no child about ancestry.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 141's slips and records with their notes: s07-E141, s07-P141r1, s07-P141r2, s07-P141a, s07-P141b, s07-P141c, s07-P141d, s07-P141e. If many gave a reason with no one else in it (error I), read this made-up card aloud: Mo says the class garden matters because "I like flowers." Who else would care, and why? Others read their notes. Up to six who missed the day 141 retrieval join you at the side table for five minutes on the legend and the nation's page.

#### 4 min. Retrieval
Say: Review: in unit 6 you labeled a source with its maker, date, audience and purpose. Read this made-up card: the Fern Mill poster, made by the Fern Mill Club in 2010 to invite visitors to a walk. Children say the four labels back. Then one line each: a community is people living and working in a place together; a country is a nation such as the United States (you learned country in kindergarten).

#### 10 min. The nation's own page
Read the Local Nation Account aloud once, with its maker and date. Say aloud that the nation is a living nation today, in the present tense, where its page says. Pairs talk; each child fills the Nation Card: [[s07-P142a]], [[s07-P142b]], [[s07-P142c]]. Where the page is silent, write "the page does not say." No one speaks for a nation.

#### 8 min. The town's record
Read the town's own record aloud once. Label its maker, date, audience and purpose on the wall. Teach two words: a founder is a person the record names as starting the town; made a town means the town's legal start on a date. Say: the date a town was made is when it began as a town, not when people first lived there (error A).

#### 7 min. Two accounts
Pairs use the two-accounts frame. Model row one; pairs finish what each tells and leaves out. Ask: what does each tell that the other does not? Cut four fact lines from the two pages onto cards; each child matches them, [[s07-P142e]]. Put the town's made-a-town year on the wall strip, and the nation's card under it with an arrow to today.

#### 3 min. Research note
[[s07-P142d]] Practice, revised on day 158. Collect it.

#### 3 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip. Read the made-up Founding Card once, then each ask once: [[s07-E142]] and [[s07-E142b]]. Key: key-exits-141-145.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice matches fact cards to the page that says them; the exit gives two made-up records of one place and asks whether one must be wrong.

### Other rooms
No wall strip: lay paper cards on a rope or table.

### At home

**Swap.** No second page: use the nation's page and the town's record the teacher sent.

**Skip.** The wall strip and the side table.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the two pages aloud. **Say.** "What does each page tell, and what does it not say?" Expect: the nation's page tells who lives here; the record tells when the town was made.
- **12 min. Nation Card.** [[s07-P142a]] [[s07-P142b]] [[s07-P142c]]
- **8 min. Two accounts.** [[s07-P142e]] [[s07-P142d]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s07-E142]] [[s07-E142b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: fill the Nation Card and the research note only. Longer: find one more thing each account leaves out.

### Words and supports
Words: nation, founder, made a town. Frames: "The page says ___; it does not say ___." "The nation's page says ___. The town record says ___." Read every page aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 143 reads a historical map of our state at one date. The exit tells you who still takes the town's date as the start of all life there (error A); they get the day 143 repair. It hands back E142, E142b and P142a to P142e.
