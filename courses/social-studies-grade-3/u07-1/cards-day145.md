## Local Record cards (Boston)

Print the two cards below for Boston classes. A class outside Boston uses cards its teacher has read from a solid source, in the same two-or-three-line shape.

Record Card 1 (Phillis Wheatley). Phillis Wheatley was born in West Africa. She became enslaved as a child. In 1761, John and Susanna Wheatley bought her when she arrived in Boston. She was a person who was forced; she could not choose where to live. Later the Wheatleys freed her. (Teacher line: National Park Service, Phillis Wheatley, U7Q.)

Record Card 2 (Apollos Rivoire). Apollos Rivoire was a French Huguenot, a French Protestant. He emigrated to Boston at thirteen. He worked as a goldsmith and passed his trade and his name, Paul Revere, to his son. (Teacher line: National Park Service, Paul Revere, U7R. The page does not say why he came. Say so; children can tell that the card does not say he was taken by force.)

## Labor History Card (Boston)

Earlier work. In early Massachusetts, most families felt they needed their children's labor on the farm or in a shop. Around age 13, a young man went to work as an apprentice in a trade. (Teacher line: Paul Revere House, U5X.)

Work now. In 2025, jobs in health care and education were more than a quarter of Boston's jobs. Only about 1 job in 100 was in manufacturing. Boston has more than two dozen colleges and universities. (Teacher line: City of Boston, U7P.)

Write one sentence: earlier work, later work and a reason the work changed.
