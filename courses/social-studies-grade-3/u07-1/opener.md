# Section 7.1 opener: who lived here and why the town grew

## What the section does

Five days of 40 minutes. Day 141 opens "Who has made our community what it is?" and writes five supporting questions. Day 142 sets a Native nation's own page beside the town's own record. Day 143 reads a historical map of the state. Day 144 asks why a town grew where and when it did. Day 145 follows why people moved and how work changed.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A founding date is not the start of all life there** (A). A town's record says when the town was made. A Native nation's own page says who lived there before and who lives there now. Native nations are named by their own names, in the present tense, and the teacher supplies the local page. No child speaks for a nation.
- **A map sheet shows only what its title and edge say** (C). A map from one date may leave a district out, and a state's outline has changed (Q). Massachusetts is a worked case: Maine was part of Massachusetts until 1820 (sources.csv U7M).
- **Water alone does not explain growth** (O). Ports and rivers mattered with trade, jobs and roads.
- **People moved for different reasons.** Some chose to move. Enslaved people were people who were forced to move, and they were not paid (B, J).

## The errors in plain words

A is thinking the town's founding date is when life began there. B is thinking everyone who came chose to. C is thinking a map sheet shows the whole state. D is thinking one view is everyone's. I is a reason with no one else in it. J is thinking all past workers were free. O is thinking water explains growth by itself. Q is thinking the state always had today's outline.
