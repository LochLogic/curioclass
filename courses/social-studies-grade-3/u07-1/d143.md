# Day 143: Read a map from long ago

## Teacher page

### Before class
Choose two dated sheets of our state from a public archive: a teaching sheet and a second sheet for pairs. Neither is the sheet held for day 159. Check each maker, date, title and crop, and say only what the sheet shows. Write one note on each day 142 record. Keep key-exits-141-145.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 142's slips and records with their notes: s07-E142, s07-E142b, s07-P142a, s07-P142b, s07-P142c, s07-P142d, s07-P142e. If many thought the town's date is when life began there (error A), read this made-up pair: Bell Hollow was made a town in 1850; the Dunmore Library book says families gathered at the spring before 1850 and gather there today. Children set three cards on a strip: before, the town begins, today. The families belong in all three. Others read their notes.

#### 5 min. Retrieval
Say: Review: in unit 1 you read a map's title, legend and scale bar. Show the current outline of our state. Pairs find the title, the legend and the scale bar, and each says what the title says the map is of.

#### 10 min. Teach
Show the teaching sheet. Read its maker, date and title aloud. Ask: what does the title say this sheet shows, and does it say what it leaves out? Trace the edge with a finger: where does the drawing stop, the edge of the sheet or the edge of the state? Say: read the title and the edge before you trust a sheet. Lay the current outline beside it. Ask where they differ; say the sheet's date shows a different reach (error Q). Put the sheet's date on the wall strip. If Native nations lived there then, say so from a page you have read, naming each by its own name, and say that each lives on today where its own page says.

#### 8 min. Pairs
Pairs fill the map strip frame for the second sheet: title, date, edge, and what the title leaves out, or "the title says nothing." Each child marks the edge on their own copy, [[s07-P143e]]. Say the frame aloud: "The sheet shows ___ and leaves out ___."

#### 9 min. Map record
Each child writes [[s07-P143a]], [[s07-P143b]], [[s07-P143c]] and [[s07-P143d]] on the page, with the current outline beside it. A scribe is offered. Collect them; mark with four look-fors: the date, the reach with the sheet's limit, one difference from today, and what the sheet leaves out. Practice, revised on day 158.

#### 3 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip. Read the made-up Map Title Card once, then each ask once: [[s07-E143]] and [[s07-E143b]]. Key: key-exits-141-145.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice reads two real dated sheets of our state and writes their date, reach, limit and a difference; the exit gives only the title of an invented sheet and asks what it shows and which words say so.

### Other rooms
No printer: draw the title and edge on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No second sheet: use the one the teacher sent.

**Skip.** The repair card and the wall strip.

- **5 min. Title.** Find the title and date on the sheet. **Say.** "What does the title say this map shows?" Expect: the place and, if it says, what it leaves out.
- **10 min. Edge.** Trace the edge. **Say.** "Is this the edge of the sheet or the edge of the state?" Expect: look at the title and compare with today's outline.
- **15 min. Record.** [[s07-P143a]] [[s07-P143b]] [[s07-P143c]] [[s07-P143d]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s07-E143]] [[s07-E143b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: write the date and one thing the sheet leaves out. Longer: find a second difference from today's outline.

### Words and supports
Words: historical map, title, edge. Frames: "The sheet shows ___ and leaves out ___." "The title says ___." Read every title aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 144 asks why our state's first towns grew where and when they did. The exit tells you who took a sheet's edge for the state's edge (error C); they get the day 144 repair, and error Q joins it. It hands back E143, E143b and P143a to P143e.
