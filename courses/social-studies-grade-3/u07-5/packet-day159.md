# Day 159 packet: map A, cards B to E and sheets

Used on no teaching day. Print "made up, for practice" on card D and packet E. The key is key-day159.md (teacher only). Q1 to Q5 are on the child's sheet; the child may write or draw.

## Map A (a historical map of our state at one date)

The teacher prints the Buell and Carleton 1784 map of Massachusetts (sources.csv U7B). Our note below goes on the sheet.

Note on A: Boston stands on a harbor. Ships carried cod and grain out and brought sugar and molasses in. Enslaved people were forced to make the sugar and molasses. In 1784 Maine was a district of Massachusetts. Maine became its own state in 1820.

Q2. How did the state's reach then differ from today's outline?

Q2b. Note on A. Why did Boston grow where it did?

## Card B (an original summary)

Boston's trade brought sugar and molasses in. Enslaved people were forced to make the sugar and molasses. Lewis Hayden ran a clothing store in Boston and housed people seeking freedom.

## Card C (an original summary, from a recorded memory)

A woman says her East Boston neighborhood was Irish, Jewish and Italian. The synagogue is gone and is now a parking lot. Latino and Asian families came later.

Q1. Name two groups who shaped this town.

Q1b. Write what a card says about one of your groups.

Q1c. Card C. Name one change in who lived there.

Q3. Ask one question about a person who changed how people lived.
