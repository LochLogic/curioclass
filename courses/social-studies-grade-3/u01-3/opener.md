# Section 3 opener: the four parts of a map

## What the section does

Four days of 40 minutes, days 7 to 10. Day 7 teaches the legend and reads a made-up map, then the real town map. Day 8 teaches the north arrow and the scale bar and uses them to say which way and about how far, in whole bars; the bar is counted, never measured in a number. Day 9 reads a landmark photograph with its caption and sorts statements into what a source shows, what it might suggest and what it cannot tell. Day 10 asks each child to draw a map of four places on the school grounds with all four parts. That map is kept for written feedback and revised on day 22.

## How the days run

Short repair, worked example on an invented map or the town map, pairs work with a card, then a printed exit card (cards-exits-7-10.md) that every child answers in writing. The day's six are also heard.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A map has four parts, each with one job.** The title says what the map shows. The legend says what each symbol stands for on this map only. The north arrow says which way is north on this page. The scale bar says how far a length on the map is on the ground.
- **North is where the arrow points, not the top of the page.**
- **A scale bar is a length counted in bars.** Lay a paper strip the length of one bar end to end. Never read it as footsteps.
- **A symbol is not the place.** A colour does not give a symbol its meaning; the legend does.
- **A caption and a photograph show what was there; they might suggest more.** They cannot tell what no one wrote.

## The errors in plain words

- **B:** colour alone gives a symbol its meaning.
- **C:** the top of the page is always north.
- **D:** a scale bar means footsteps.
- **G:** a visible feature does not prove a conclusion the source does not support.
- **I:** unmarked land was not empty.

Wrong ideas are read from "someone might say" cards.

## Supplies for the section

The four-part map card for the wall, the town map at full size with a bar checked against a ruler, paper strips, the Maple Park Map, route cards, the answer overlay (teacher), landmark statement cards, the wall card shows, suggests, cannot tell, the school-grounds frame, the Fern Hollow Trail card, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the map on the board, cut strips from paper, read the caption aloud.
