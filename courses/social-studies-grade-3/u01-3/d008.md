# Day 8: How far, and what nature made

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write six route cards from the real town map, public places only; count each with a strip and write the counts on the overlay only. Write the five symbol cards (beach, river, bridge, ball field, shelter), marked "invented for practice", and five mixed cards: two directions, two distances, one feature. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 7 to 10 (key-exits-7-10.md), out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Take the most-missed of day 7's errors: error B (a symbol picked by its color), error C (the top of the page called north) or error D (bars read as steps). Read its "someone might say" card, the right idea, then one fresh item together. For B, match three new symbols to a legend. For C, turn the paper and the north arrow together. For D, lay a strip along a fresh route and count. The other letters go to a small group of up to 6 in part 2. Hand back day 7's exit tickets and written task (the exit box) with the written cue.

#### 12 min. Distances in whole bars

Review: as on day 7, lay the strip end to end and say "about ___ bars". Pairs count six route cards. After the first three routes, each pair checks against the overlay; then the last three, different routes.

Check: a count within one bar of the overlay is close enough; if not, count again with the strip.

The small group takes its repair at the side table, 5 minutes.

**Ask.** A route comes to about 7 bars. Is that 7 steps? Expect: no, it is 7 bar lengths on the ground.

#### 10 min. Physical features

A physical feature is a land or water place that nature made. Sort the five symbol cards under "made by nature" and "made by people": beach and river go with nature; bridge, ball field and shelter with people. Then pairs find the town's physical features by their legend symbols (water or shoreline, where the town has them) and say where one is from the school: "The ___ is ___ of the school, about ___ bars away."

**Ask.** Is a river a physical feature? Expect: yes, it is water that nature made.

**Ask.** Is a bridge? Expect: no, people built it.

#### 8 min. Mixed cards

Five quick cards: two directions, two distances, one feature. A partner checks each answer against the overlay; the child writes it on the student page.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the Reed Pond card once, then each ask once. Each child writes the first two answers on the half slip ([[s01-E8]], [[s01-E8b]]) and the third in the exit box on the student page ([[s01-E8c]]). The card is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The route cards use the real town map with none of the exit card's places; the exit card is a new map, first seen at the child's own turn.

### Other rooms

With one projector, show the overlay after each three routes. With no printer, write routes on the board and cut strips from paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No town map: use a park or library map. No strip: cut paper as long as the bar.

**Skip.** The mixed cards and the group repair.

- **15 min. Bars.** Count three routes between places with the strip.
  **Say.** "About how many bars is it? Is that steps?" Expect: a count with bars; no.
- **12 min. Nature made it.** Look at the legend.
  **Say.** "Which symbols are land or water that nature made?" Expect: water, shoreline.
- **5 min. One sentence.** Write: "The ___ is ___ of the ___, about ___ bars away."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E8]] [[s01-E8b]] [[s01-E8c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter, or if the day folds into day 9: drop the mixed cards and half the routes. Longer: a bent route counted in two pieces.

### Words and supports

Words: physical feature, made by nature, made by people. A pointed symbol is an answer.

### Tomorrow

Day 9 finds historic landmarks on the town map. If many read bars as steps on the exit (error D), it opens by laying a strip along a fresh route and counting again.
