# Day 7: Title, legend, arrow and bars

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the town map at full size; check its bar against a ruler. As in grade 2, cut a strip to one bar for each pair. Write the overlay: the school's symbol, the nearest water (or park) and a bar count between two public places. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 7 to 10 (key-exits-7-10.md), out of the room until the slips are collected.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Three minutes: on the Maple Park Map each child touches the title, legend, arrow and bar as the teacher names each job. You learned the four-part map card in grade 2.

Five minutes, the more-missed of two repairs. Error E from day 6: Death Valley gets under 2 inches of rain a year, the Great Smoky Mountains lowlands about 55 (sources 6A and 6C), at about the same latitude (source 6D). Or match each map part to its job. The other goes to a small group in part 4.

**Ask.** Two places at about the same latitude: must they get the same rain? Expect: no.

Two minutes: hand back day 6's exit tickets with the written cue.

#### 8 min. Title, legend, arrow

Show our town's current official map; read its credit aloud. Review: the title says what the map shows; the key says what each symbol stands for. The key's second name is the legend, the map card's word. Model: "The map shows ___. I can find it because the legend says ___." The north arrow is the compass rose's one direction. Turn the paper: the arrow turns, and north stays where it points. Read a card: "Someone might say, 'Up is north.'" Beside it: "North is where the arrow points."

**Ask.** The paper is turned. Where is north now? Expect: where the arrow points.

Pairs find the school's symbol and the nearest water and say which way it is.

#### 11 min. Scale in whole bars

Review: you learned in grade 2 to lay equal strips end to end on a scale. The bar stands for a ground distance; read its label. New: count whole bar lengths and say "about". The teacher counts one pair of places aloud with a strip. Pairs count two routes on the Maple Park Map: swings to shelter, shelter to grove. Read a card: "Someone might say, 'Three bars means three steps.'" Beside it: "Three bars is about three bar lengths on the ground."

**Ask.** About how many bars is the school from the nearest water? Expect: the overlay's count, with the word bars.

#### 6 min. What the map shows

Pairs say, then write: "On this map the ___ is ___ of the ___, about ___ bars away." The small group takes its repair.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Read the Clover Court card once, then each ask once. Each child writes two answers on the half slip ([[s01-E7]], [[s01-E7b]]) and the third in the exit box on the student page ([[s01-E7c]]). The card is first seen here.

**Shared shape.** The Maple Park Map has its arrow up; the exit card is a new map, first seen at the child's own turn.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the map on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No town map: use a park or library map. No strip: cut paper as long as the bar (grade 2 review).

**Skip.** The class repair and the pair talk.

- **10 min. Four parts.** Find the title, legend, arrow and bar on the map.
  **Say.** "What is the legend for?" Expect: it says what each symbol stands for.
- **12 min. Bars.** As in grade 2, lay the strip end to end between two places and count.
  **Say.** "About how many bars apart? Is a bar a step?" Expect: a count with the word bars; no, a bar is a length on the ground.
- **10 min. One sentence.** Write: "On this map the ___ is ___ of the ___, about ___ bars away."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-E7]] [[s01-E7b]] [[s01-E7c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: count one route, not two. Longer: a second pair of places.

### Words and supports

Words: legend, north arrow, bar length, about. A pointed symbol is an answer.

### Tomorrow
