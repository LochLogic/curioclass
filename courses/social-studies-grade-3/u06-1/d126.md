# Day 126: Why independence?

## Teacher page

### Before class
Count who missed the flag or the eagle on day 124's readiness page (s06-RD1, s06-RD1b; errors A and B). Mark day 125's slips with a cue. Print the strip. Keep key-exits-125-127.md with you. The kindergarten review pictures are made up; the Harbor Card is our summary. Do not show it before the exit.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 125's slips (s06-E125) and cards (s06-P125a) with one cue each; the cards return to the folder for day 134. If many wrote "only the mayor cares" (error N) or "be kind" (error Q), take five minutes first: a fresh made-up law card and one person it reaches. If a third or fewer missed A or B, those children take the repair with you; the rest read the four-box label quietly. If more, the whole class takes it. Review (kindergarten): a national symbol stands for our country. Each child writes [[s06-P126a]], then the heron banner caption [[s06-P126b]]. Hear each group child about 45 seconds. If N or Q and A or B are both many, N or Q moves to day 127. The pages stay in the folder.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Review (grade 2): you asked a teller questions in an interview. **Ask.** "What does a date help us know about what someone said?" Expect: when it was said.

#### 8 min. Maker, date, audience, purpose
Show the label. Review (unit 5): who made it, when and why. Read each box once: a committee of the colonists' leaders; 1776; the king and the wider world; to give reasons for separating. Audience is new: who a source was written for. Children fill the boxes.

#### 9 min. A grievance
A grievance is a complaint listed as a reason for acting. Read the taxation card once: "For imposing Taxes on us without our Consent." Review (unit 2): taxes are money many people put in for what everyone uses. The writers said taxes were set without their agreement. **Ask.** "Why did this feel unfair?" Expect: no one asked them (link to consent). Say plainly: the colonists did not want no rules (error C); they wanted a say. The war is later, in grade 5. **Ask.** "What did the colonists want instead?" Expect: a government they had a voice in. Pairs say it in their words.

#### 7 min. The strip
Review (unit 5, day 91): equal marks for equal years. This strip runs 1700 to 1810, one centimeter a year. Plymouth, 1620, is 80 centimeters left of the start: point to the arrow. The class places 1776 six centimeters after 1770; check with a ruler. Each child draws a card at 1776 and fills the label under it, [[s06-P126c]]. Partners point to a mark and say its year.

#### 3 min. Exit
Read the Harbor Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E126]] and [[s06-E126b]]. Each child writes and marks the strip. Key: key-exits-125-127.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice places 1776 and explains the tax complaint aloud; the exit gives a new 1774 card to explain and place.

### Other rooms
No printer: rule a paper strip.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed strip: rule paper, one centimeter a year.

**Skip.** The pair talk.

- **5 min. The label.** Read the four boxes. **Say.** "Who was it written for?" Expect: the king and the wider world.
- **10 min. The complaint.** Read the taxes line. **Say.** "Why did this feel unfair?" Expect: taxes were set without their agreement.
- **10 min. The strip.** [[s06-P126c]]
- **5 min. Symbols.** [[s06-P126a]] [[s06-P126b]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E126]] [[s06-E126b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: skip the retrieval. Longer: a second grievance.

### Words and supports
Words: grievance, audience, strip, symbol. Frame: "The colonists wanted ___ because ___." Read every card aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 127 reads what the Declaration says about rights and consent. If many marked 1774 one step after the last card (error H), its return part places a fresh dated card by its year. "No government at all" (error C) is the C part again.

### Credit
Ours. The taxation words are public domain, from the Declaration (National Archives). The Harbor Card is our summary of the 1774 Resolves (Avalon Project).
