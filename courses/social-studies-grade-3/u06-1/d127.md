# Day 127: What the Declaration says

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 126's slips (s06-E126, s06-E126b) and strips (s06-P126c) with one cue each. Post two word cards. The voice sort, Arden Lake and the six phrases are made up: say so. Keep key-exits-125-127.md with you. Do not show the Town Leader Card before the exit. Read no other part of the Declaration.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 126's slips, strips and labels. If many marked 1774 one step after the last card (error H), place a fresh made-up card, a bridge opened in 1783, by its year: three centimeters after 1780. Few missed: those children use the side table. If N or Q was carried from day 126, do that repair first.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Review (day 125): consent means agreeing to how decisions are made. Two pairs say why the Room 5 corner, version two, had consent.

#### 10 min. From complaint to a government they chose
Review (day 126): the taxes card. This is the C part, for the whole class every time. Read four made-up lines once; each child sorts them, [[s06-P127a]]: wanted no rules, or wanted a voice. Take a reason for one line. Each child then finishes [[s06-P127c]]. **Ask.** "What did the colonists want instead of no rules?" Expect: a government they had a voice in. A child who says no rules (error C) hears line 1 again.

#### 10 min. The words
Read each Declaration line twice, with its gloss. "All men are created equal": all are meant to be equal. Review (grade 1): equality means each vote counts the same. "Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness": rights people have. "Deriving their just powers from the consent of the governed": governments get their power from the people's agreement. Say plainly: in 1776 many people, including enslaved people and women, were not treated as equal. Day 130 asks who. Tell the Arden Lake card (made up): the neighbors chose the lake council, and the council wrote a rule, no motors on the lake. **Ask.** "Where did the council's power come from?" Expect: from the neighbors who chose it. Review (unit 2, day 26): agreement can mean everyone votes, or people choose others to decide; Arden Lake is the second.

#### 8 min. Principle and virtue
Two words go on the wall. A democratic principle is an idea about how a people's government should work, such as equality or consent. A civic virtue is a good habit shown by what a person does, such as fairness. Each child sorts six made-up phrases, [[s06-P127b]]. Pairs say one example each.

#### 4 min. Exit
Read the Town Leader Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E127]] and [[s06-E127b]]. Each child writes. Key: key-exits-125-127.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice sorts lines and phrases and finishes a claim about the colonists; the exit gives a leader no one chose and asks what is missing.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the lines on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall: hold the two word cards.

**Skip.** The pair talk.

- **5 min. Retell.** **Say.** "What does consent mean?" Expect: agreeing to how decisions are made.
- **10 min. A voice.** [[s06-P127a]] [[s06-P127c]] **Say.** "Did they want no rules?" Expect: no, a voice.
- **10 min. The words.** Read the Declaration lines twice, and the Arden Lake card. **Say.** "Where did the council's power come from?" Expect: the neighbors chose it.
- **5 min. Two words.** [[s06-P127b]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E127]] [[s06-E127b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: read each line once. Longer: a second made-up council.

### Words and supports
Words: rights, equal, consent, council, democratic principle, civic virtue. Frame: "The colonists wanted a say because ___." Read every card aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
Day 128 shows where the national plan came from. If many wrote "whoever is in charge decides" (error T) or "the leader owns their rights" (error O) on E127, its return part gives a fresh ruler who asks beside one who only commands, or a fresh clause beside its limit.
