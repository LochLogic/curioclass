# Day 125: Whose agreement counts?

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hand back day 124's exit slips. Keep day 124's readiness results for day 126 (read them from the record). Print the student page, a card for each child and quarter-page exit slips. Write the six questions on strips; do not post them yet. Room 5 and the shop are made up: say so. Keep key-exits-125-127.md with you; never print it on a slip.

### The day

#### 6 min. Hook
Show the hook card: the Declaration of Independence, written in 1776. Read the consent phrase aloud once, then our two lines: people agree to be governed; a government needs their agreement. Review (grade 1): July 4 remembers it. **Ask.** "Whose agreement does a government need?" Pairs name a person for one minute; take three answers. No answer is wrong yet.

#### 9 min. Consent, taught
Tell the Room 5 reading-corner story (made up). Version one: the teacher announces who sits where. Version two: the class talks and agrees on where each group sits. **Ask.** "Which one had the children's agreement?" Expect: version two. Post the word card: consent means agreeing to how decisions are made. **Ask.** "In version two, did every child get a first choice?" Expect: no; consent is not liking every rule (error I). Review (unit 2, day 26): fair rule-making read two ways, everyone votes on each rule, or chosen people decide. Read that card aloud; both ways can be consent.

#### 6 min. Who else cares?
Model once with the shop card (made up): a shop owner hears of a new rule about opening hours. Say aloud: "A customer cares because that is when the customer can buy bread." Then pairs name two other people the rule reaches, [[s06-P125b]], and write them; hear three pairs. Review (unit 2, day 26): who a question matters to. The page stays in the folder.

#### 6 min. Co-write
Read the six supporting questions on the strips and post each under the big question with its day: why independence (126), what the writers believed (127), what makes a government plan (128), what rights were listed (129), whose voices counted (130 and 131), what symbols promise (132). The class words each; you write them.

#### 9 min. Supporting-question card
Review (unit 5, day 90): the four-box record. Each child fills the card, [[s06-P125a]]: a question from the board, a fact it needs, one idea people read two ways, and how it connects to the big question. Words, a drawing or a scribe are fine. Hear a rotating six, one minute each, and name them on the record. The card is kept; it comes back on day 134 with a note.

#### 4 min. Exit
Read the Bus Driver Card aloud once (made up): [[s06-E125]]. Each child writes on the slip. A scribe is offered. Key: key-exits-125-127.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The shop model names people a rule reaches; the exit asks why one person cares whose agreement counts.

### Other rooms
No board: hold the strips and read them aloud. No printer: copy the four boxes by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No board: write the six questions on paper.

**Skip.** The pairs and the co-write.

- **5 min. Hook.** Read the Declaration line from the card. **Say.** "Whose agreement does a government need?" Expect: the people's.
- **10 min. Consent.** Tell the Room 5 story. Ask which version had the children's agreement.
- **10 min. Who else cares.** [[s06-P125b]]
- **10 min. The card.** [[s06-P125a]] Your child may talk while you write.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E125]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: post three of the six questions. Longer: a reason for each person on the shop card.

### Words and supports
Words: consent, government, rule, agree. Frame: "___ cares because ___." Read every card aloud; pointing, drawing and a scribe are answers; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow
E125 shows who sees why a rule reaches a person's life. If many wrote "only the mayor cares" (error N) or "be kind" (error Q), day 126's return part opens with a fresh law card and one person it reaches. Day 126 asks why the colonists felt treated unfairly, and the timeline.
