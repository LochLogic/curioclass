# Teacher key, course check 2 (days 122 and 123)

Teacher only. Keep apart from packet-check2.md. Score each part once: 2 when every look-for appears, 1 when some appear without a false claim, 0 when absent or contradicted. Write the missing part by name (sources, timeline, treaty, claim), not a total. Spelling and handwriting are not scored.

- Day 122 read-aloud notes. Primary means made at the time by someone who was there; secondary means made later by someone telling about it. A source is primary or secondary for a question by when it was made and by whom, not by how old it is.
- E1: primary. E1d: written in 1926 by the builder, who was there. Not "it is old".
- E1b: secondary. E1e: written in 2021, long after. Not "it is about the bridge".
- E1c: any question the page could help answer, such as who helped build the bridge or why it was built.
- E2: the 1920s. E2b: the 1800s, the nineteenth century. E2c: founding, bridge, library.
- Day 123 read-aloud notes. A treaty is an agreement between nations. Cede means give up. The nation is named by its own name, the Makah Tribe, and it lives at Neah Bay today. Say plainly that Source A is our wording of the 1855 treaty and Source B is our wording of the Makah Tribe's own page.
- E3: land, their rights to land. Not "whaling rights", which they kept.
- E3b: to fish and to hunt whales and seals at their usual places. Not a right to make laws.
- E3c: Makah ways of life. A child may say how this changed Makah life, in the card's words.
- E4: a claim that answers: they kept the right to fish and to hunt whales and seals at their usual places. Not "I like whales".
- E4b: a detail from Source A, the right to fish and hunt whales and seals at usual places. Not "the treaty is old".
- E4c: a detail from Source B, that the Makah made the treaty to keep whaling rights, or to protect their people's health, education and welfare. Not a detail from Source A only.
