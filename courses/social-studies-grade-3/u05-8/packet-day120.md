# End-of-unit sheet: two dossiers

Print two pages. The teacher reads the card a task names, aloud, just before that task. Pencil only. No talk. Every card is made up unless it says real. The key is in key-day120.md.

## Dossier A: Harbor Town Record

Made up, for practice. The law card and the alliance card are real.

Card A1. Governor T led a made-up colony. In a hard year he ordered a fort built to protect the colony.
Ask: Governor T's card. Why did he make this choice for his colony?

Card A2. A new mill dam raised the river. The water covered the town's cornfield.
Ask: Dossier A, cause card. Name the effect and its cause.

Card A3. Pim is a servant. He works two years, then goes free. Jory is enslaved. He is held for life.
Ask: Dossier A, work record. How was a servant's life different from an enslaved person's?

Card A4. A drawing of a kitchen in a made-up colony in 1650. A girl hauls up water in a bucket from the well. A woman churns butter by hand.
Ask: Dossier A, drawing. Name one thing about work or home that has changed since then.

Card A5. Law card, real. In 1641 Massachusetts made slavery legal. (City of Boston Archaeology.)
Ask: Dossier A's law card and B's Atkins record. Was slavery legal only in southern colonies?

Card A6. Port X has deep, sheltered water. Ships load at its docks. Shipwrights and traders work there.
Card A7. Port Y has a sheltered river mouth. Ships load at its quay. Net makers and sellers work there.
Ask: Dossier A, Port X caption. Why did Port X grow around a harbor?
Ask: Dossier A, Port Y caption. Why did Port Y grow around a harbor?

Card A8. A simplified trade diagram, made up. Port M to Port N: tools. Port N to Port O: people carried by force and sold. Port O to Port M: tobacco grown by enslaved people.
Ask: Trade diagram, made up. What happened to people on the Port N to Port O leg?

Card A9. Alliance card, real. In summer 1621 two colonists visited Ousamequin, the Wampanoag Great Sachem, at Pokanoket to cement an alliance. (Pilgrim Hall.) Today the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah) has an elected council. (Aquinnah history page.)
Task 8 uses this card.

## Dossier B: Neighbors' Record

Made up, for practice. The Atkins card and the Seloy card are real.

Card B1. Captain U led a made-up fishing town. She chose to trade fish for tools. She kept the town's fishing grounds.
Ask: Captain U's card. Why did she make these choices for her town?

Card B2. Two towns quarreled over a boundary. In the fight that followed, a storehouse burned.
Ask: Dossier B, cause card. Name the effect and its cause.

Card B3. Atkins record, real. Zipporah Potter Atkins lived from 1645 to 1705. She was a free Black resident of Boston. In 1670 she bought a house. (City of Boston Archaeology.)
Ask: Dossier B, Atkins record. What does it show about Africans in Massachusetts?

Card B4. Seloy card, real. In 1565 Spanish colonists settled at Seloy, a Timucua town in what is now Florida. The Spanish made a Timucua council house into a fort. Relations worsened, and in 1566 the Timucua attacked it. (Florida Museum.)
Task 8 uses this card.

Task 8. Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?
Ask: Write a claim.
Ask: Give one detail from A and one from B.
Ask: Name both sources and say how each supports your claim.
Ask: How did a supporting question help you answer the big question?

Check that every task has an answer. Hand in the sheet and the folder.
