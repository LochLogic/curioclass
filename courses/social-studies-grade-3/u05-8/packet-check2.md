# Course check 2: the cards for days 122 and 123

Print each card on its own page. The Lantern Bay cards are made up, for practice: print that at the bottom. The teacher reads each card aloud once, just before its questions. The key is in key-check2.md (teacher only).

## Day 122, card 1: the bridge sources

Lantern Bay is a made-up town. The question is: how did Lantern Bay get its first bridge?

Source A. A diary page, June 4, 1926, by Odd Lindahl, who built the bridge. He wrote it that day, for himself. "The crew set the last beam today. Tomorrow the first cart will cross."

Source B. A museum label, written in 2021, for visitors. "Lantern Bay's first bridge opened in 1926. Before that, people crossed the bay by ferry."

## Day 123, cards 3 and 4: the treaty

The Makah Tribe lives at Neah Bay, Washington, today. In 1855 the Makah and the United States made a treaty.

Source A. The Treaty of Neah Bay, made on January 31, 1855, by the United States and the Makah. In our words: The Makah give up their rights to land. The United States agrees to pay thirty thousand dollars. A piece of land is kept for the tribe. The Makah keep the right to fish, and to hunt whales and seals, at their usual places. Congress agreed to the treaty in 1859. (Source C2A, the treaty text.)

Source B. The Makah Tribe's own page, About our History, 2025, written for readers today. In our words: The Makah made the treaty to keep their whaling rights and to protect the health, education and welfare of their people. Government agents, missionaries and teachers tried to change Makah ways of life. (Source C2B.)
