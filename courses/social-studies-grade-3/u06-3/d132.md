# Day 132: What a symbol says and what a record says

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 131's slips with one cue. Write the five captions on cards. Draw a year line on the board. Keep the Monument Card hidden. Keep key-exits-130-132.md. Say plainly: no child has to sing, salute or recite; a court decided in 1943 that schools cannot force it (U6S).

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair, with retrieval (D or S from E131 and the record)
Hand back day 131's slips and sort (s06-E131, s06-E131b, s06-P131a); the sort returns on day 134 with a note. If many wrote that a denied vote means not a citizen (error D), sort two made-up cases: a town lets only boat owners vote for a harbor master (voting); a town book counts Lena, born there, as one of its people (citizenship). If many wrote that a virtue is a feeling (error S), sort four phrases: she kept asking until a court heard her; the law treats everyone the same; he felt proud; officials answer to the people. The other goes to the six heard during the record. Review: in kindergarten a symbol is a small picture that stands for a place; in grade 1 you met the Statue of Liberty, a gift from France raised in 1886.

#### 17 min. Five cards
Read each caption aloud once, name its maker, and tell why it was made and what it stands for. A child writes each year on the board line. The Liberty Bell (4 min): ordered in 1751 to call lawmakers together; later abolitionists took inspiration from it, and in 1835 an anti-slavery publication named it. The Statue of Liberty (4): a gift from France, dedicated 1886, with a broken shackle and chain at its foot. The anthem (3): words written after the September 1814 attack on Fort McHenry. Independence Day (3): Congress adopted the Declaration on July 4, 1776; people have celebrated since. The 1777 flag resolution (3): Congress chose thirteen stripes and thirteen stars. Name later meanings as later.

#### 8 min. Supported discussion
Five minutes: the Statue beside a dated record. Frame: "The symbol says ___; the record says ___." The symbol says liberty; the record says that in 1886 the Black press said it was not yet real for African Americans. A symbol shows an ideal; a dated record shows what happened. Three minutes: two ways to study a symbol, read its caption or draw it and say what it stands for. Anyone may decline.

#### 7 min. Record
Each child puts five made-up Pine Ford captions in order by the year made, oldest first, [[s06-P132a]]. Pointing and dictation count. Hear a rotating six, one caption each. A row left unfinished is finished at the side table on day 133, not for a mark.

#### 3 min. Exit
Read the Monument Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E132]] and [[s06-E132b]]. Key: key-exits-130-132.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice orders five made-up captions by year; the exit gives one new caption and asks why it was made and what it proves.

### Other rooms
No year line: write five years on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: read the captions from the page.

**Skip.** The repair and the discussion.

- **10 min. Symbols.** Read two captions from the cards. **Say.** "What does it stand for?" Expect: an idea from the caption.
- **15 min. Order them.** [[s06-P132a]]
- **5 min. Ideal and record.** **Say.** "Does a symbol show what happened?" Expect: it shows an ideal; a record shows what happened.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E132]] [[s06-E132b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the Statue and the ordering. Longer: a second symbol's later meaning.

### Words and supports
Words: symbol, caption. Frame: "The symbol says ___; the record says ___." Read every caption aloud; pointing is an answer.

### Tomorrow
Day 133 writes a short claim that answers the unit question with evidence. If many wrote that a symbol proves everyone was free (error F) or that all must salute (error L), its return part repairs one. It hands back E132, E132b and P132a.

### Side 2

Made up for practice. Pine Ford captions.

1. The Pine Ford Bell, ordered in 1850 to call townspeople to meetings.

2. The Pine Ford banner, 1900: a blue wave for the river.

3. Pine Ford Day, every May since 1860, remembers the first town meeting.

4. The Pine Ford song, written in 1920, is about the river.

5. The Pine Ford charter, 1840: a paper listing the town's rules.
