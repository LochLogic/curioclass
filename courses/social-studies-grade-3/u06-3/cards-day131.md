# Cards for day 131

Print "made up, for practice" on every invented card. The teacher reads each card aloud once. Pockets: citizenship, voting, equal rights, and one for "none of the three".

## One together (made up, for practice)

A 1798 rule lets people from the north end of town use the dock free and makes people from the south end pay. Sort it with the frame: "This answers the ___ question because ___."

## The sort: four fresh cases (student page)

1. A 1802 county rule lets people there two years vote for the mill board. (made up, for practice)
2. A 1801 town book counts each child born in town as one of its people. (made up, for practice)
3. A 1803 market law says every seller pays the same stall fee, whatever the village. (made up, for practice)
4. Our summary of the Oneida Indian Nation's own page. The Haudenosaunee Grand Council let each Nation choose its own path. (real, original summary)

Put each case in a pocket and write one reason. The Oneida card is a nation's own decision, not a state election; it answers none of the three. Say: a nation choosing its own path is a decision made by that nation, a fourth kind of question. Item: [[s06-P131a]]

## Say why

Pairs explain one card. The teacher says that a vote, or being denied a vote, is not an answer to the citizenship question. The unit's question comes back: whose agreement counted?
