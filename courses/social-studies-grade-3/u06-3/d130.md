# Day 130: Who was left out of voting

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 129's slips with one cue. Print "made up, for practice" on every invented card. Keep both exit cards hidden until the exit. Keep key-exits-130-132.md. The stop card and the right to pass hold; no child reports on home, family or any group.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (O and E from E129)
Hand back day 129's slips and records (s06-E129, s06-E129b, s06-P129a, s06-P129b). If many wrote that the government can pick one faith (error O), read a made-up clause: "The town council may not stop a person from printing a flyer." Pairs say the right and what it stops the council doing. If many sorted details wrongly (error E), each sorts three of the three-documents detail cards they did not use on day 129. Few missed: those children use the side table.

#### 3 min. Retrieval
Review: on day 126 you used the four-box label: maker, date, audience, purpose. Say the four boxes together; name each card's maker as it is read.

#### 6 min. Voting was set by each state
Read the first card. Each state set its own voting rules; in most, voters were white men over 21 who owned property. Say plainly: many were left out. Enslaved people could not vote; you learned in grade 2: people held in slavery, forced to work, not paid, not free to leave. Read the nation card; maker: the Oneida Indian Nation, on its own page, there today. A nation's choice is its own decision, not a state election.

#### 8 min. New Jersey
Read the New Jersey card: for a time its rule named property, not sex or color. Each child draws a band from 1776 to 1807 under 1787 and 1791, [[s06-P130b]]. Two made-up cards: Isaac, a free Black man, 1802, owns land worth over fifty pounds; Hannah, 1810. Place each year. 1802 is inside the band, so Isaac met the rule. 1810 is after it: a law of 1807 let only white men who were citizens and paid taxes vote. The rule was an exception and ended.

#### 6 min. Elizabeth Freeman
Maker: the National Park Service. Read the card: born into slavery about 1744, in 1781 she went to a Massachusetts court and a jury decided she was free. Review: a civic virtue is a good habit shown by what a person does. Perseverance, new, is going on with something hard. Each child writes the line, [[s06-P130c]].

#### 8 min. Three questions
Read the three-questions card, each question with a made-up case. Each child writes a line for each, [[s06-P130a]]. The card stays in the folder until day 134. Hear a rotating six, one line each.

#### 4 min. Exit
Read the Oneida Card and the New Jersey Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E130]] and [[s06-E130b]]. Key: key-exits-130-132.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice places years on a band and writes lines for three questions; the exit reads a new message from Congress and a woman in 1790.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: keep the band, the Freeman line and the three lines. Longer: a child places one more made-up year on the band.

### Words and supports
Words: citizenship, voting, equal rights, perseverance. Frame: "She ___ to get ___." Read every card aloud; pointing and a scribe are answers.

### Tomorrow
