# Day 131: Three different questions

## Teacher page

### Before class
Mark day 130's slips and records with one cue. Print "made up, for practice" on every invented card. Set out four pockets on the board: citizenship, voting, equal rights, none of the three. Keep the Voting Law Card hidden until the exit. Keep key-exits-130-132.md. The stop card and the right to pass hold.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (U or J from E130, whichever more missed)
Hand back day 130's slips and records (s06-E130, s06-E130b, s06-P130a, s06-P130b, s06-P130c); the three-questions card stays in the folder. If many wrote that a nation's choice is a state election (error U), read the Oneida card and a made-up state voting rule, and match each to who decided. If many wrote that no woman ever voted (error J), place two made-up cards on the strip: Cato, a free Black man, 1805, owns land worth over fifty pounds; Ruth, 1808, owns land worth over fifty pounds. The other repair goes to the side table during the sort.

#### 4 min. Recall
Review: on day 130 you learned citizenship, voting and equal rights. Pairs take turns saying one question each from their card, then swap.

#### 5 min. One together
Read the made-up dock rule. The class sorts it with the frame "This answers the ___ question because ___." Equal rights: the law should treat people the same, and this rule does not. Ask for a reason, not a show of hands.

#### 14 min. The sort
Each child sorts four cases, [[s06-P131a]]. Three are made up; the fourth is our summary of what the Oneida Indian Nation says on its own page; the nation is there today. Say: a nation choosing its own path is a decision made by that nation, a fourth kind of question. Read each card aloud once, then children work alone, writing a pocket and one reason each; pointing and a scribe count. Thirty turns will not fit, so hear a rotating six at the board, one card each, and name them on the record. The record returns on day 134 for revision.

#### 8 min. Say why
Pairs explain one card to each other; the class hears three. Say plainly: a rule about voting answers the voting question. It does not by itself answer who is a citizen. Return to the board: whose agreement counted then?

#### 4 min. Exit
Read the Voting Law Card aloud once, then each ask once: [[s06-E131]] and [[s06-E131b]]. Key: key-exits-130-132.md. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Shared shape.** The practice sorts four fresh cases to pockets with a reason; the exit gives one new voting law and asks about one person and about the law.

### Other rooms
Four boxes drawn on paper take the place of pockets.

### At home

**Swap.** No pockets: draw four boxes.

**Skip.** The repair and the Recall.

- **5 min. Recall.** **Say.** "Name the three questions." Expect: citizenship, voting, equal rights.
- **20 min. The sort.** Read each card aloud. [[s06-P131a]]
- **5 min. Say why.** **Say.** "Which card was hardest? Why?" Expect: a reason.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-E131]] [[s06-E131b]]

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: sort the first three cases and keep the reasons. Longer: a child writes a fifth made-up case and a partner sorts it.

### Words and supports
Words: citizenship, voting, equal rights. Frame: "This answers the ___ question because ___." Read every card aloud; a color for each pocket helps.

### Tomorrow
Day 132 reads five symbol captions and asks what a symbol says and what a record says. If many wrote that a vote denied means not a citizen (error D), its return part sorts two fresh cases into citizenship or voting. It hands back E131, E131b and P131a with a written note; the sort comes back on day 134.
