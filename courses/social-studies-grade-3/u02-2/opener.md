# Section 2.2 opener: who decides here, and how it is paid for

## What the section does

Five days of 40 minutes. Day 28 names the leaders at each level and the three jobs of a state and national government: make laws, carry them out, decide cases about laws. The national government is the worked example. A made-up country, Bayview, is sorted by pairs. The nation nearest the school is read from its own page, by its own name, in the present tense. Day 29 compares three real town and city forms (Boston, Boulder, Lexington). Day 30 shows who governs the public schools: a school committee and the superintendent it hires. Day 31 shows how taxes and fees pay for services and that a government collects the money, then spends it. Day 32 finds our own town's government on its official pages and compares it with a town in another state.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a five-minute return and repair for the most-missed letter of the day before, then teaches, then ends with one exit card (cards-exits-28-32.md) that every child answers in writing. Day 32 is a short, compressible block.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The three jobs are a pattern for states and the nation, not for every government.** A town may split the jobs another way. A tribal nation sets up its own government and is named by its own name.
- **A government has people who decide and people who run daily work.** Elected means chosen by voters. Hired means chosen to do a job.
- **Towns set up their own form.** A mayor and a council, a council and a manager, or a Select Board and a Town Meeting are three forms among others. No class is told that every town copies one.
- **School governance is policy for all the schools,** not classroom management.
- **A tax is paid by many people for what many use. A fee is paid by the person who uses one service.** A government collects the money, then spends it. Property tax is one source of school money. Schools also get money from the state and the country.

## The errors in plain words

A is the idea that one leader holds every job. B is the idea that every town copies one model. C is the idea that services cost nothing. L is the idea that taxes are gifts. K is the idea that everyone at a public meeting casts the deciding vote. I (a tribal nation is a town) and T (nations exist only in the past) are heard in talk on day 28. Wrong ideas come from "someone might say" cards.

## Supplies for the section

Three baskets, job cards, the Bayview cards, the nation card, three real form cards, role cards, school decision cards, a jar and counters, tax and fee cards, three-box strips, the class edition of the Local Government Record, packets from our town and a town in another state, exit cards, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Chart paper, index cards, a jar and counters, the town's pages printed.

No child is asked to speak for a nation. No child says what their family pays or whether a home is owned or rented. Cases name offices and titles, never an officeholder's name or party.
