# Day 30: Who governs the public schools

## Teacher page

### Before class
Set out the six decision cards, the three column labels (OUR TEACHER, OUR PRINCIPAL, THE COMMITTEE OR SUPERINTENDENT), the committee boxes and the role chips. Find your district's own page on its school committee, or use the state school advisory (Source U2K, Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education). Learn from the page whether your committee is elected or appointed. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 28 to 32 (key-exits-28-32.md), out of the room until the exit cards are collected.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 29's exit slips. Practice pages stay in the folder. Teach the most-missed letter, wrong answer then right. B: "Every town has a mayor" beside "Towns set up their own form: Boston has a mayor, Boulder a manager, Lexington a Select Board." A: "The manager votes on the laws" beside "The council votes; the manager, who was hired, runs the daily work." X: "She is called the manager" beside "A manager is hired to run the town's daily work."

#### 9 min. Who decides here at school?
Pairs sort the six decision cards onto the three columns. Say: class rules are the teacher's and the class's; a rule for the school building is the principal's; policy, a rule for all the schools, is the committee's. Each child writes [[s02-P30a]] and [[s02-P30b]].

#### 13 min. Committee and superintendent
Read the district's role paragraph aloud once, saying its maker, date and link first (review: who made it, when and why, day 25). Read titles only, no names. New: the school committee sets policy and hires the superintendent; the superintendent runs the daily work of all the schools. Pairs fill the two boxes: [[s02-P30c]], [[s02-P30d]]. Say whether your committee is elected or appointed, as the page says; each child writes [[s02-P30e]]. Then read the Cedar Hill card (made up) as a second example. **Ask.** Who votes on the Cedar Hill calendar? Each child writes [[s02-P30h]].

#### 10 min. Elected or hired?
Review (day 29): elected is chosen by voters, hired is chosen to do a job. On the Our towns chart, point to Boulder's manager and Lexington's Town Manager, both appointed, hired by a council or board, and to the elected councils. Pairs sort the role chips (committee member, superintendent, principal) and say how each gets the job, as the page says; where the page does not say, they write "the page does not say." Each child writes [[s02-P30f]]. A child who finishes writes [[s02-P30g]].

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the School Card, first seen here. Read it and the two asks once. Every child writes [[s02-E30]] and [[s02-E30b]].

**Shared shape.** The decision cards and Cedar Hill go from a decision to who makes it; the exit asks which person in a new district holds each job.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the six decisions on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write the decisions on slips of paper. No district page: use the state school advisory.

**Skip.** The role chips.

- **10 min. Who decides?** Your adult reads each decision card. [[s02-P30a]] [[s02-P30b]]
  **Say.** "What does a school committee do?" Expect: sets policy for all the schools and hires the superintendent.
- **13 min. Committee and superintendent.** Your adult reads the district page aloud. [[s02-P30c]] [[s02-P30d]] [[s02-P30e]]
- **9 min. Cedar Hill.** Read the Cedar Hill card. [[s02-P30h]] [[s02-P30f]] [[s02-P30g]]
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Read the School Card once. [[s02-E30]] [[s02-E30b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: [[s02-P30a]] to [[s02-P30d]] only. Longer: find which jobs in your district are elected and which are hired.

### Words and supports
Words: committee, superintendent, policy, elected, hired. Frame: "The ___ hires the ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 31 shows how taxes and fees pay for services. If many made error A on day 30's exit (a building leader taken for the superintendent), day 31's Return and repair sets that answer beside a right one.
