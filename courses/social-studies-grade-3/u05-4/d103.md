# Day 103: The Wampanoag and Plymouth, 1621

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hang the wall strip. Copy the two leader cards onto the board. Keep key-exits-100-104.md away until slips are in. Keep a class list for who was heard. The harvest card is Winslow's, the English side only: no costume, no play-acting, and not called the first Thanksgiving.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair (V from E102)
Hand back day 102's slip (s05-E102) marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Write the wrong, "Only men's work mattered," beside the right, "Everyone's work mattered." Pairs read the Lund record, made up, and name each person's work. Each child writes [[s05-P103b]] and [[s05-P103c]]. If few made error V, they work at the side table.

#### 4 min. Timeline
Review (day 91): one mark is ten years. Place 1621 on the wall strip, one year after 1620, almost on the same mark. Each child writes [[s05-P103d]] on the strip in the record folder.

#### 8 min. Samoset and Ousamequin
Review (kindergarten day 41): the Wampanoag are a nation living in Massachusetts today. Read the three cards once. A sachem is a leader of a nation; Ousamequin, called Massasoit in English records, was the Great Sachem of the Wampanoag. An alliance is an agreement between groups to help one another; two made-up clubs agree to share a pool. Fill two leader cards with the class: role and one decision. Bradford has no decision on our page; a blank is honest (review, day 97). **Ask.** "What made the alliance possible?" Expect: both sides agreed. Each child writes [[s05-P103e]] and [[s05-P103f]].

#### 8 min. The 1621 harvest
Review (kindergarten day 42): you heard about a harvest meal in 1621. Read the harvest card once. Wampanoag people live today and tell their own history. Each child writes [[s05-P103g]].

#### 8 min. Later relations
Read the label first: the card comes from a modern essay on the Sipayik Tribal Government website. Read it once. Later years brought other agreements, and one gathering does not tell what came after; later relations need other evidence. The Abenaki and Wabanaki nations each have their own history. Add to the question board: the alliance and the gathering are evidence for sharing the land, and later relations need more. Each child writes [[s05-P103h]].

#### 5 min. Ordering record
Each child puts the three dated cards in order and writes the line under them: [[s05-P103a]]. It goes in the record folder; the teacher gives a written cue after class, and it is revised on day 104. The other practice answers stay in the record folder. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each, and list their names.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Later Relations Exit Card, new today. Read it once. Each child writes [[s05-E103]]. Key: key-exits-100-104.md.

**Shared shape.** Model and practice fill leader cards, read a harvest card and a Wabanaki card and order three dated cards; the exit is a new card about a later war and asks what it shows about one gathering.

No printer: copy the cards onto the board and read each aloud once.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: the Lund record to one question. Longer: ask what another leader card could hold.

### Words and supports
Words: alliance, sachem, harvest, later, evidence. Frame: "First ___, then ___, then ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers. Read every card aloud once.

### Tomorrow
Day 104 returns every record with a written cue and gives time to revise; error D (one gathering made peace permanent) from today's exit is repaired by rebuilding the ordering record from a new sequence.

### Credit
Ours. The leader, harvest and Wabanaki facts are in our words from Pilgrim Hall Museum, Plimoth Patuxet Museums, the National Park Service and the Sipayik Tribal Government. The Lund record and the clubs are made up.
