# Section 5.4 opener: Plymouth, the Compact and the mid-unit check

## What the section does

Six days of 40 minutes. Day 100: Plymouth, 1620; who crossed, why, and how. Day 101: the Mayflower Compact and the idea of self-government. Day 102: hardships and who faced them. Day 103: the Wampanoag, Samoset, the 1621 alliance, a harvest gathering, and later relations. Day 104: written feedback and independent revision. Day 105: the mid-unit check.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The Mayflower.** About 102 passengers and crew. Leiden Separatists planned the crossing and others joined at Southampton. Some were hired (John Alden, a cooper). Many families came with children (sources.csv U5J). The ship left Plymouth, England on 16 September 1620 and anchored near the tip of Cape Cod on 11 November 1620 in the calendar of the time (U5L, U5P); one calendar is used throughout the unit, never 21 November. The Separatists worshiped in their own way, apart from the Church of England (U5AD; the museum's Who Were the Pilgrims page gives no reason for leaving). Plymouth stood on Wampanoag land (U5E).
- **The Compact** was signed at Cape Cod on 11 November 1620 and agreed to form a civil body politick and make just and equal laws for the colony (U5P). It was an agreement among its signers; forty-one names are signed. Say "it reached the people who signed" and let the class say who did not. Do not say who could vote.
- **Hardships.** About half died in the first winter; Bradford wrote that six or seven sound people fetched wood, made fires, cooked and washed for the sick (U5J). The Bradford line is held for the E102 card's idea only if the day writer needs it; teaching rows use invented family records.
- **The Wampanoag.** Ousamequin was the Great Sachem in 1620; the English called him Massasoit. In summer 1621 Stephen Hopkins and Edward Winslow visited Pokanoket, guided by Tisquantum, to cement an alliance (U5I). Samoset, an Abenaki sagamore from Maine, greeted Plymouth in English on 16 March 1621 (U5K). A harvest gathering lasted three days in autumn 1621, and Winslow wrote that Massasoit came with some ninety men (U5Y). The only eyewitness account was written from the English side; no Wampanoag eyewitness account of it survives in our sources. It is not called the first Thanksgiving. No costume and no reenactment.
- **Wabanaki.** The Wabanaki Confederacy allied with the French; the page names King William's War (1688 to 1698) and Dummer's Treaty (1727) (U5G). Name the Abenaki and Wabanaki nations by their names and in the present tense. The 1675 entry is held for E103.

## The errors in plain words

AF is giving one reason for everyone on the ship. M is calling everyone on the ship a Pilgrim. E is thinking the Compact governed everyone. V is thinking one task fell to one group. K is thinking a new colony replaced an older one at once. D is thinking one gathering made peace permanent. L is thinking all Native nations share one history.
