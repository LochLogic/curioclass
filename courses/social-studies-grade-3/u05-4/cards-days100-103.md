## The Compact, a labeled summary

Made by Curio Class from the Avalon text (U5P). Signed at Cape Cod on 11 November 1620, in the calendar of that time, the day the ship anchored. The people who signed agreed to form a body to make just and equal laws for the colony.

## Samoset and Ousamequin

Samoset was an Abenaki sagamore from Maine. He greeted Plymouth in English in March 1621. (Wikipedia, Samoset, U5K.)
Ousamequin was the Great Sachem of the Wampanoag. English records call him Massasoit. In summer 1621 two colonists visited his town, Pokanoket, with a guide, to cement an alliance. (Pilgrim Hall, U5I.)

Alliance: two groups agree to help each other. Two clubs, made up, agree to share a pool.

Leader cards. Ousamequin: Wampanoag; Great Sachem; decision: made an alliance with Plymouth. William Bradford: Plymouth colonist; leader; decision: the page names none, so leave blank.

## The harvest gathering

In autumn 1621 a harvest gathering lasted three days at Plymouth. Edward Winslow, a colonist, wrote that Massasoit came with some ninety men. The only eyewitness account we have was written from the English side. (Plimoth Patuxet Museums and Pilgrim Hall Museum, U5Y.) It is not called the first Thanksgiving. No costume and no play-acting. The Wampanoag live today and tell their own history.

## Later relations: the Wabanaki

The Wabanaki Confederacy allied with the French. A treaty, Dummer's Treaty, was made in 1727. (A modern essay on the Sipayik Tribal Government website, U5G.) The Wabanaki nations live in Maine today. (National Park Service, Acadia, U5AF.)
