# Day 100: Who crossed on the Mayflower, and why

## Teacher page

### Before class
Hang the wall strip, 1500 to 1700, ten-year marks. Ready three bands: St. Augustine from 1565, Jamestown from 1607, Plymouth from 1620. Copy the six passenger cards onto the board. Keep key-exits-100-104.md away until slips are in. Keep a class list for who was heard.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair (C from E99)
Hand back day 99's slip and practice (s05-E99, s05-E99b, s05-P99a, s05-P99b, s05-P99c, s05-P99d, s05-P99e, s05-P99f, s05-P99g) marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Write the wrong, "One paper tells all of it," beside the right, "Each maker's paper tells what helps that maker." Review (day 99): the label comes first. Pairs read the Bike Rack Pair, made up, and say who made each paper and why. Each child writes [[s05-P100b]] and [[s05-P100c]]. If few made error C, they work at the side table.

#### 4 min. Timeline and bands
Review (day 91): one mark is ten years. Place 1620 on the wall strip. New: a band is a line from a colony's start year onward. Draw the three bands; the first two are still going. **Ask.** "Which colonies were there in 1620?" Expect: all three at once. Each child writes [[s05-P100d]] with the strip in the record folder.

#### 9 min. Who came and why
Read the six passenger cards once each. About 102 passengers, and crew, sailed in 1620. A Separatist is a person who left a church to form their own. Pilgrim is the name for the church group; hired workers are not Pilgrims. Pairs sort the cards into church members, hired workers and families, and say each group's reason. The page gives families no reason, so that box stays blank; a blank is honest. **Ask.** "Why did each group cross?" Each child writes [[s05-P100e]].

#### 7 min. The crossing
Review (day 94): a route is an arrow from start to end. On the wall map, trace the route with the class: tail at Plymouth, England, head near Cape Cod. Two places share the name Plymouth. Say the direction and ocean aloud: west across the Atlantic. The ship left in September 1620 and anchored near Cape Cod in November. The colony of Plymouth stood on Wampanoag land. Each child writes [[s05-P100f]].

#### 8 min. Record card
Each child fills the Record card: [[s05-P100a]]. It stays in the folder; the teacher gives a written cue after class. Hear a rotating six, about 45 seconds each, and list their names.

#### 2 min. Exit
Hand out the half slip with the Passenger Comparison, new today. Read it once. Each child writes [[s05-E100]] and [[s05-E100b]]. Key: key-exits-100-104.md.

**Shared shape.** Model and practice sort cards into groups and name a group's reason in a table row; the exit is a new pair of passengers and asks one reason in a sentence and why a hired worker is not a Pilgrim.

### Other rooms
No printer: copy the cards, the colonies table and the Route Card onto the board, and read each aloud once.

### At home

**Swap.** No strip: draw a line and mark 1565, 1607 and 1620.

**Skip.** The Bike Rack Pair.

- **7 min. Two papers.** Read both papers aloud. **Say.** "Does one paper tell all of it?" Expect: no, each tells part. [[s05-P100b]] [[s05-P100c]]
- **10 min. Who came.** Read the two cards. [[s05-P100e]]
- **8 min. Strip and ship.** [[s05-P100d]] [[s05-P100f]]
- **5 min. Record card.** [[s05-P100a]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read the Passenger Comparison once. [[s05-E100]] [[s05-E100b]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: the Bike Rack Pair to 5 minutes; the rest stays. Longer: make up two passengers and sort them.

### Words and supports
Words: passenger, Separatist, Pilgrim, hired, voyage, band. Frame: "___ came because ___." Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 101 opens with error AF (everyone crossed for one reason) and error M (everyone was a Pilgrim) from today's exit, ten minutes, then places the Compact on the strip.

### Credit
