# Section 2.1 opener: why we have rules and governments

## What the section does

Three days of 40 minutes. Day 25 opens the unit's big question, Who should make the rules in our town? The class looks at its own rule for taking turns to speak beside a dated paragraph from the town's official page (the Local Government Record, teaching edition). Who made each, who can change it, and who it reaches. Six invented people near Oakridge Pond show that a rule reaches many people with different needs. Day 26 gives a short repair for the unit 2 readiness gaps (leaders, equal ballots, group roles), then why groups make rules, fair rule-making read two ways, and the six supporting questions. Each child starts the supporting-question record (boxes A to D). Day 27 teaches the four levels of government (school, town, state, country) and what each looks after.

## How the days run

Day 25: hook, the town's record, whose needs count, wonder cards, the exit. Day 26: return and repair, why groups make rules, fair rule-making in two ways, co-writing the six supporting questions, the record, the exit. Day 27: return and repair, four levels, what each level does, the record as a source, the exit. Each day's exit is one card (cards-exits-25-27.md) that every child answers in writing; some are also heard at the side table.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A big question matters to other people, not only to us.** A rule reaches people who ride, walk, work and play in a place, not only the people who vote.
- **A government is leaders and workers, not a building.** They make a community's rules, carry them out and give services everyone uses.
- **Each level looks after needs of its own size.** A school, a town, a state and a country each have leaders. No level is worth more than another.
- **A leader has powers (what the leader may do) and duties (what the leader must do).**
- **Groups make rules for reasons:** to keep people safe, to settle a disagreement, to protect a freedom.
- **Fair rule-making can be read two ways:** everyone votes on each rule, or chosen people (representatives) study the choices and decide. Each has a cost. Neither is the right one.

## The errors in plain words

W is an answer that does not link to the question (a liking). D is the idea that only voters belong, care or help. A is the idea that one leader does every job. S is the idea that rules were always there. V is the idea that fair means what I like. Wrong ideas are read from "someone might say" cards, never from a named child.

## Supplies for the section

The class turn-taking rule chart, a dated paragraph from the town's official page, paper circles for four levels, picture cards, chart paper for the question board, the day 26 record, exit cards, the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw four circles on the floor with chalk or yarn. Read the town record aloud from the printed page. Write the six supporting questions on the board. Children answer the exit on a half sheet the teacher copies from the board.

No child reports on home, family make-up, money or religion. A child may pass in talk. The class is a group with rules, not a government.
