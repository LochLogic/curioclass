# Day 26: Why groups make rules

## Teacher page

### Before class
Fill the record sheet from day 24's readiness answers (R1 to R8). Write one cue on each day 25 slip. Set out the unit 2 question board, the draft question cards, unit 2, the electing helpers sequence and the Oakridge Picnic cards. Fold each student page over the Exit box. Keep the teacher key, exit cards days 25 to 27 (key-exits-25-27.md), out of the room until the exit cards are collected. Draft key: u01-1/key-exits-1-2.md.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair
Hand back day 25's slips with one cue; day 27 teaches errors W and D from day 25's exit.

Review: Return and repair (grade 2). 4 min: read the four grade 1 slips on side 1 aloud once: [[s02-T26]], [[s02-T26b]], [[s02-T26c]], [[s02-T26d]]. Error A is taught on day 27; error D goes to a day 34 small group.

5 min: the plant-corner sequence. If over a third missed a readiness item, whole class; otherwise up to 6 children at the side table, 45 seconds each, while the others sort the eight draft cards into "helps our big question" and "does not". **Ask.** Who won, and why is a second ballot unfair? Expect: Pia, 6 to 2; each voter gets one ballot. Then the fresh card, Dani 5, Lou 3. 1 min to gather.

#### 6 min. Why groups make rules
Pairs. Review (kindergarten, grade 1): a rule keeps us safe, is fair or helps us learn. New: a rule can settle a disagreement or protect a freedom (something a person is free to do, such as speak). Model a made-up pool rule: walk, do not run. "It keeps swimmers safe." Pairs use the swing and club cards to finish [[s02-P26e]] and [[s02-P26f]]. **Ask.** Why do groups make rules? Expect: to keep people safe, settle a disagreement, protect a freedom.

#### 6 min. Fair rule-making, two ways
Pairs. Oakridge Picnic is made up. Card 1: all 20 children vote on every game. Card 2: 3 representatives (people others choose to decide for them) ask everyone and decide. Pairs finish [[s02-P26g]], [[s02-P26h]], [[s02-P26i]], [[s02-P26j]].  **Ask.** What could "fair" mean to two people? Expect: everyone votes, or chosen people study the choices and decide. If a child says fair means what I like (error V), ask how to check.

#### 6 min. Co-write the supporting questions
Review: the question board (day 2). The class rewords each of the six draft strips in one line, and you write each on the board with its day: Why have governments (27)? Who decides locally (32)? What do rules protect (34)? How can people participate (37)? How can we decide fairly (40)? What limits government (43)? Set each day 25 wonder card under its strip; they stay in the folder.

#### 9 min. The record
Each child copies one supporting question onto side 2 and finishes boxes A to D: [[s02-P26a]], [[s02-P26b]], [[s02-P26c]], [[s02-P26d]]. A sentence, a drawing or telling the teacher all count; up to 5 children tell at the side table, 1 minute each. Practice: it stays in the folder for a written cue before day 46.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket
Hand out the half slip with the Tanner Street Card, first seen here. Read it and the asks; every child writes [[s02-E26]] and [[s02-E26b]]. Then unfold the Exit box on side 2, read the Park Card once, and each child writes [[s02-E26c]].

**Shared shape.** The swing and club cards finish one reason; the exits are new cases.

### Other rooms
No printer: write the slips on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw the swing and the club on paper. No class: your adult is your partner and takes the second view.

**Skip.** The sorting and the side table.

- **5 min. Look back.** Review from grade 1: your adult reads the four slips ([[s02-T26]] [[s02-T26b]]).
  **Say.** "Who leads a country, a state and a city?" Expect: President, governor, mayor.
- **8 min. Why rules.** [[s02-P26e]] [[s02-P26f]]
  **Say.** "Why do people make rules?" Expect: to stay safe, settle a disagreement, protect a freedom.
- **8 min. Two ways.** [[s02-P26g]] [[s02-P26h]]
- **10 min. The record.** [[s02-P26a]] [[s02-P26d]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-E26]] [[s02-E26b]] [[s02-E26c]] Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: boxes A and D only. Longer: a third meaning.

### Words and supports
Words: freedom, disagreement, representative, fair. Pointing, drawing and a scribe are all answers.

### Tomorrow
Day 27 teaches why schools, towns, states and the country have governments. If many made error S ("rules were always there") or error x on day 26's exit, day 27's Return and repair sets that answer beside a right one.
