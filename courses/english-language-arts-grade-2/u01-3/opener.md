# Section 1.3 opener: three schools in three countries (days 13 to 15)

## What the section does

Days 13 to 15 turn from the story to a text that gives information. The class hears three short articles about school days in Japan, Finland and Brazil, one a day. On day 13 children recount the key ideas of an article they have heard. On day 14 they explain what a photograph shows that the words do not say. On day 15 they follow a diagram and its words to see how a school day works. Each country is shown as an ordinary place with its own school day; do not rank, joke or call any school strange. Writing begins on day 16.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes: a 60-minute topic block and a 60-minute foundations block. The topic block holds a short opening repair, one or two words, the reading with its map and picture, a model, partner work and a five-minute exit. Cycle 3 (ee, ea and y) runs in the foundations block, with the reader *The Green Beanbag*; on day 15 it ends with a 15-minute written check and a fresh response for the day 11 six. Every exit is one question you read aloud, and you hear the day's rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A recount keeps the key ideas in order.** Cover the text and say first, then, last. A small detail may be left out.
- **A photograph clarifies the words.** The words say what a library is; the photograph shows more of how a place looks. Ask what the photograph shows that the words do not.
- **A diagram is read in parts.** Read the title, the labels and the arrows, then say the order.
- **Facts about schools are facts about a time and a place.** Say "in 2025" when the article does, and "in many schools" when it says many.

## The errors, in plain words

- **N:** a few words that name the topic but lose the key ideas ("it is about bags"). Cover the text and tell three ideas in order.
- **C:** a photograph named without saying what it shows. Ask what the photograph shows that the words do not.
- **A:** an order that contradicts the diagram. Follow the arrows with a finger and say the labels.
- **L, M, P, Q:** vowel sounds and spellings, as in sections 1.1 and 1.2.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet. A child who cannot write may draw, point or tell; a scribe never supplies the answer.
