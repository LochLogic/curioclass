# Day 13: Learning together

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print article 02-a, Learning Together, in large print with its map and photograph, one for each pair, and the strips (a sheet folded in three). Note the commonest retry letter from day 12. Today's six are the third group on the record sheet. Their oral card: seem, jeep, meal, treat, penny, candy; and "Lee can see a funny jeep." Write the twelve sort words on cards: peel, speed, steep, sleep, steam, dream, cream, heat, bunny, muddy, dusty, lucky.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Look at your day 12 notes and repair the commonest retry letter for the whole class; a child may pass, and no one is the example. Error B: lay "Leo asks Mira to play cards" beside "Mira says, 'No, thank you.'" and "Mira is shy"; children point to the card that says what she does. Error A: read "Mira walked across the room to Leo," show where on the question words card and ask "Where did she go?"; each child puts a finger on the line that says. Error C: show the chapter 4 picture, cover the words, and say the frame "The picture shows ___ so Mira ___ ." If no letter is common, partners say the six question words and ask each other one about chapter 4.

#### 4 min. Words

Two new words, each acted out. Cooperate: to work together to get something done. Two pairs pile a heap of cards together; say "We cooperate." Helpers: the pupils whose turn it is to do a job; this is the grade 1 sense of taking turns. Choose two helpers to hand out the pencils for the day. Each child says one sentence with a word to a partner.

#### 8 min. Read article 02-a, Learning Together

Say aloud first: "This article is about school days in Japan. It comes from a visit to a school in Tokyo that Japan's education ministry wrote about in March 2026. Schools differ, so the article says many where that is true." Show the map, then read the whole article (about 214 words) once, about three minutes, and say many as the article does. Pause after "the pupils clear up too" and after "the places that everyone shares," and say "I wonder ..." for one child to add. Show the photograph and read its caption; say only what the caption says. Read the words to know. Say: "Today we ask a question of the article and tell its key ideas in order." Ask nothing about children's own lunches, classroom jobs or homes.

#### 12 min. Ask and answer with a located detail

Review: in unit 1 you used the six question words on the card; keep the card in view. Say: "A question the article can answer is a text-dependent question. Its answer sits in a line we can point to." Teach two roles. The asker says a question with a question word. The partner finds the line, puts a finger on it and says the frame "The article says ___ ."

Model both roles with the article in view, finger under the lines, about three minutes each.

**Ask.** "Why do schools do this?" (you ask; reread the paragraph that begins with that question and answer from it). Expect: cleaning helps pupils learn to cooperate and to respect the places everyone shares.

**Ask.** "Who serves the meal in many schools?" (a child asks; you find the line). Expect: the pupils serve the meal themselves.

**Ask.** "What do the pupils do when the meal is over?" (a child asks; another child finds the line, or you do). Expect: they clear up.

Each time, point out the line and say "The article says ___ ." Then close the article and ask the last question again from memory; the answer is the same, but the line proves it.

#### 12 min. Recount

Review: in grade 1 you told a story first, next, last. Today the strip says first, then, last, for an article. Show the folded strip, say recount means to tell the key ideas in order, and leave out the small details. Cover the article. Model it aloud, one box a time, drawing a small picture in each: First, pupils serve lunch and clear up. Then, pupils clean the school as a group. Last, this helps pupils cooperate and learn together. Say a small detail is left out: the helpers bring the food to the classroom.

Write "It is about lunch" beside the three ideas.

**Ask.** "Which one tells the key ideas in order?" Expect: the three ideas; "it is about lunch" names the topic but loses the ideas (error N).

Then the class tells the article again together, with a different child for each box; the frame is "First ___, then ___, last ___ ."

#### 14 min. Partners practise both roles

Pairs have article 02-a and the strip. Round one, 6 minutes: partners take turns being the asker and the one who finds the line; each asks a question with a question word, the partner points and says "The article says ___ ," then they swap. Round two, 8 minutes: article covered, each child recounts to the partner with the strip, three minutes each, then the partner says one idea they heard. Walk round and hear your six, about twenty seconds each, on the record sheet. If a recount is only a topic (error N), say "Tell me what the pupils do first."

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card (s01-X13) aloud once and then once more, and ask: "Tell me the key ideas of the card in order." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means the ideas in order using words of the card. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What does the card say?" Not yet seen means a topic or a wrong order, or none.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Retrieve ee and ea. Write need and beak, and children map the team in each (ee or ea, one long e), blend and spell them on tiles. If a child reads the team with a short vowel (error M), map the whole team first, then blend. Send any ee or ea miss from the reader on day 12 here.

#### 15 min. Reread and sort

Reread, 4 minutes: partners read The Green Beanbag, a page each; map said, to and the on the wall and say them plainly, so no one guesses from the picture or the first letter (error P). Sort, 4 minutes: pairs sort the twelve word cards into three columns, ee, ea and y, on the mat and say where the team or the y sits: ee peel, speed, steep, sleep; ea steam, dream, cream, heat; y bunny, muddy, dusty, lucky. Spell, 4 minutes: say each sentence twice for children to write: "The bunny hops in the deep mud." and "We steam the meat." They underline the long e in each word. Find the wrong word, 3 minutes: write "A muddy bunny hid in the deap hole." Children say the wrong word and fix it (deep; error Q, a sound spelling that does not carry the pattern over).

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each. Tiles: spell steep, cream, penny and speed. Sort: sort the twelve cards again and add one word of their own to each column. Reread: partners read The Green Beanbag with expression. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 3 oral card (group 3 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle); the others work in stations. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Say: "Say one word with ee, ea or y to your partner and tell where the team sits." Expect: in the middle of a word, or y at the end.

### Other rooms

No printer: read the article from the screen and draw the map on the board. No strip: fold scrap paper into three. No wall: draw the sound-spelling-map on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the screen. No strip: fold paper in three. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The partner roles and the stations.

- **10 min. The article.** Read 02-a aloud.
  **Say.** "Why do many Japanese schools have pupils clean?" Expect: it helps them learn to cooperate and respect places everyone shares.
- **10 min. Tell it in order.** Cover the article. Your child draws First, Then and Last in the strip and tells it.
  **Say.** "What happens first, then, last?" Expect: pupils serve lunch, clean together, learn to cooperate.
- **10 min. Ee, ea and y.** Write steam, speed and bunny. Your child reads each and says where the long e sits.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X13]] Read the card twice; your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the second partner round and keep the model, one recount and the exit. Longer: a child recounts the article to a younger child and adds one detail left out.

### Words and supports

Cooperate, helpers, recount. Frames: "The article says ___ ." "First ___, then ___, last ___ ." Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write draws, and you write the words. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 14 reads the second article, A Place to Read, and explains what its photograph shows. Its opening repair is for error N on today's exit (a topic with no key ideas, such as "It is about bags"): cover the text and tell three ideas in order. Any L, M, P or Q miss from today's sort and spelling goes to day 14's foundations opening.
