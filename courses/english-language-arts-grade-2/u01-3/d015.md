# Day 15: Following a school day

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print article 02-c with its map, and the large diagram. Cut the label cards loose and print the cycle 3 written check sheet. Today's six are the fifth group on the record sheet. Their oral card: greet, teeth, reef, peas, kitty, tummy; and "A kitty can nap in a big heap." The day 11 six read the cards in the last part: sweep, stream, beast, baby, belly, creep; and "The happy baby sat by Dee."

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 14's exit (error C), reread the 02-a lunchtime paragraph and show its photograph. Write beside it "It has a classroom" and "The photograph shows a cart with a pot, bowls and a ladle, so the lunch is carried in." Ask which one says what the photograph adds, and have partners say it with the frame "The photograph shows ___ so ___ ." If few missed it, partners hold the picture card and say one thing yesterday's library photograph showed that the words did not.

#### 4 min. Words

Shift: the part of the day when a group of pupils is at school. Two groups stand in turn at the front, one then the other, to act out two shifts. Schedule: a plan that tells what happens and when; read a made-up one aloud, "reading, then math, then art." Diagram: a drawing that explains something with labels; show the diagram of 02-c without reading it (review: in grade 1, day 80, you used a diagram). Each child says one of the words to a partner.

#### 10 min. Read article 02-c, Following a School Day

Say aloud first: "This article is about school days in Brazil. It tells how a national program pays for school meals. The diagram shows three example school days made up for practice; real schools differ." Show the map. Read the whole article (about 259 words) once, about three minutes. When the article says "Look at the diagram," show the large diagram and read its title and caption. Pause after "the full-day pupil has four meals" and at the end, and say "I wonder ..." for one child to add. Read the words to know. Say: "Today the diagram helps us explain how a school day works." Ask nothing about children's own meals.

#### 12 min. Reading a diagram

Show the diagram card and say a diagram is read in steps. Read the title. Read the labels. Read the brackets, which show which part of the day a row covers. Follow the arrows, if there are any, and say the order. Say that this diagram has no arrow, so its bar and its labels give the order: morning on the left, afternoon on the right.

Model the morning shift row, about four minutes. The words say a shift is a part of the day. The diagram shows a bracket under the morning half with two meals inside it: lunch and a snack. Say: "In the morning shift, pupils have lunch and a snack."

Teach arrows on the board, about three minutes. Draw two boxes: lunch on the left, breakfast on the right, and one arrow from breakfast pointing left to lunch.

**Ask.** "Which comes first?" Expect: breakfast, because the arrow starts there and points to lunch (read where the arrow points, not only left to right).

**Ask.** "Why does the diagram explain more than the words?" Expect: it shows the three kinds of school day side by side, with the meals in each; the words give them one at a time.

#### 15 min. Pairs follow a school day

Pairs have the diagram card and its loose label cards. Six minutes: partners take turns tracing a row with a finger, from the title to the bracket, and say "In the ___, pupils have ___ ." Rows: the morning shift, lunch and a snack; the afternoon shift, lunch and a snack; the full day, breakfast, lunch and two snacks. Four minutes: they lift the meal labels off, mix them and put them back under the right row. Five minutes: each pair asks and answers one question of the diagram with a question word; for example, "How many meals does the full-day pupil have?" Expect: four. Walk round and hear your six, about twenty seconds each, on the record sheet.

#### 9 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Ask the planned question: "What does the diagram explain?" Each child turns: draws or says one answer to a partner, then pairs link ("You said ___; I would add ___ ") and clarify ("Which part do you mean?"). Take your six, about thirty seconds each. Expect: how three kinds of school day fit into a day, and which meals each has.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud twice the 02-c paragraph that begins "Some schools run in parts of the day," show the diagram (s01-X15v2), and ask: "Tell me how the diagram shows that one building holds two shifts." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means the one school under the bar and the two brackets. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "Point to the school. What is above it?" Not yet seen means two buildings, or none.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Unscored, to see what each child can do without help. Give each child the check sheet on side 2. Read each sentence twice and say its missing word; children write the word in the gap. 1. "Put the sheet of paper on the desk." The missing word is sheet. 2. "A seed fell from the tree." The missing word is seed. 3. "It is a windy day." The missing word is windy. 4. "The teapot is on the shelf." The missing word is teapot. Give no help with spelling and no hint about the team. Collect the sheets. Mark each word by letter on the record sheet: Q for shet or sead, and for windee (y at the end); M for wundy, where a short vowel stands in place of the team. Write other spellings down as they are.

#### 5 min. Reader

Partners reread The Green Beanbag for expression, a page each. Say: "Read it so your partner enjoys the story."

#### 25 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each, two rounds. Tiles: spell creep, beach, tummy and heap. Sort: sort the ee, ea and y cards and say where the team or the y sits. Reread: partners read The Green Beanbag again. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 3 oral card (group 5 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle); the others work in stations. Round 2, 12 minutes: every group visits a second station. Mark the heard column.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

No new teaching. Day 11's six read to you, two minutes each, 12 minutes: first their grade 1 card (cake, flag, thick, glad, stop, frog; "The frog sat on a big log."), then their cycle 3 card. The others work in pairs: they partner-read a pattern card of ee, ea and y words, then take turns spelling two dictated words you say, deep and meat, on paper or with tiles. For the last three minutes, everyone chooses a word from the wall that was hard for them and reads it to a partner.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the diagram on the board and cut scrap paper into label cards. No wall: draw the sound-spelling-map on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed diagram: draw a bar with morning on the left and afternoon on the right. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The written check and the stations.

- **10 min. The article.** Read 02-c aloud. At "Look at the diagram," show the diagram.
  **Say.** "What does the diagram explain?" Expect: three kinds of school day and the meals each has.
- **10 min. Follow the rows.** Your child traces each row with a finger.
  **Say.** "In the full day, what do pupils have?" Expect: breakfast, lunch and two snacks.
- **10 min. Ee, ea and y.** Write creep, beach and tummy. Your child reads each and says where the long e sits.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X15v2]] Reread the 02-c shift paragraph twice and show the diagram; your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the label cards and keep the three rows, the model and the exit; the written check stays. Longer: a child draws a diagram of a day of reading, then math, then art, with an arrow between each, and says its order.

### Words and supports

Shift, schedule, diagram. Frames: "In the ___, pupils have ___ ." "The diagram shows ___ ." Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write draws, and you write the words. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 16 begins section 1.4: it reads a school article and collects facts and definitions for an informative paragraph. Its opening repair is for error A on today's exit (an answer the diagram contradicts, such as "There are two buildings"): put a finger on the one school and trace each bracket above it. Today's written check goes to day 16's foundations opening: any error Q or M, so the class retrieves ee, ea and y before cycle 4.
