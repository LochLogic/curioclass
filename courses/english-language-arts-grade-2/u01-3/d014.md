# Day 14: A place to read

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print article 02-b, A Place to Read, with its map, one for each pair, and keep its photograph covered by a folded sheet. Print the passage Dee and the Peach for each pair. Today's six are the fourth group on the record sheet. Their oral card: feel, beach, leap, steel, berry, belly; and "Jeff can leap on the beach." Keep article 02-a and its photograph to hand for the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 13's exit (error N), read the exit card again and write beside it "It is about bags" and "Pupils put in questions, the teacher reads one, partners find the answer in a book." Ask the class which one keeps the ideas in order, then cover the card and have partners tell it with first, then, last. Children point to a box of the strip for each idea. If few missed it, partners retrieve the strip and tell yesterday's article in three boxes.

#### 4 min. Words

Review: you met the library as a place on the setting chart on day 4. New today: a library is a place where people borrow books and read. Volunteer: a person who helps without being paid. Act out each: a child borrows a book from a pretend shelf; you offer to hold the door for the class as a volunteer. Children say a sentence with each to a partner.

#### 10 min. Read article 02-b, A Place to Read

Say aloud first: "This article is about reading in Finland. It comes from the European education network, a Finnish government site and a Finnish town's news from April 2025. School rules change, so we say 'in 2025,' as the article does." Show the map. Keep the photograph covered. Read the whole article (about 242 words) once, about three minutes, and read "In 2025" as printed. Pause after "the whole building is quiet and busy at once" and at the end, and say "I wonder ..." for one child to add. Read the words to know. Say: "Today we tell the article again, and then see what its photograph adds." Ask nothing about children's own reading at home.

#### 8 min. Partners recount

The photograph stays covered. Pairs use the recount strip; the article is closed. Each child recounts the article to a partner, a box at a time, with the frame "First ___, then ___, last ___ ," two minutes each, then the partner says one idea they heard. Walk round and hear your six, about twenty seconds each, on the record sheet. Shown is the ideas in order; a topic alone is error N. Say: "This is the same recount you learned yesterday, on a new article." Expect: first, Finland pupils have lessons and breaks and read; then pupils read for ten minutes each day in 2025 in one town; last volunteers listen to them read.

#### 12 min. What a photograph clarifies

Uncover the photograph and read its caption. Hold up the picture card and say its strip: "The photograph shows ___ so ___ ." Say that a photograph is a picture of something real, and that it clarifies when it shows something the words do not say.

Model, about four minutes. The words say a library is a place where people borrow books and read. Then say: "The photograph shows a cushion and a lamp, so this corner is made for comfortable reading." Write "It has a lamp" beside it, and ask which one says what the lamp adds.

**Ask.** "Which one says what the photograph adds?" Expect: the first; "it has a lamp" names a thing and does not say what it adds (error C).

**Ask.** "What does the photograph show about the books?" Expect: they are picture books, set low in a corner where a reader can reach them.

**Ask.** "What do the words not say that the photograph does?" Expect: the cushion and the lamp.

#### 10 min. Pairs explain it

Pairs say the frame once with their own second detail from the photograph: "The photograph shows ___ so ___ ." Then each asks the other a text-dependent question about the article and the partner finds the line: for example, "What do pupils do at about ten o'clock?" Link with the link card: "You said ___; I would add ___ ." Expect: they read for ten minutes. Take three pairs to say their frame to the class. A child may pass.

#### 6 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Ask the planned question: "What does the photograph show that the words do not?" Each child turns: draws or says one answer to a partner, then pairs link and clarify ("Which part do you mean?"). Hear your six on this question, about thirty seconds each. If a comment does not use the last speaker's word, say the last idea first, then add: "You said ___; I would add ___ ."

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud twice the 02-a paragraph that begins "Look at lunchtime," show the 02-a photograph (s01-X14v2), and ask: "Tell me one thing the photograph shows that the words do not say." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means a detail of the photograph that the words do not give. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What do the words say?" Not yet seen means a name for a thing with no detail, or none.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Model the commonest day 13 miss with tiles, five minutes in all. Error M: map the whole team to one sound before blending steam, then creep. Error Q: write "dreem" beside dream on the wall and check which spelling the wall has. Error P: cover the picture on a reader page, read the letters first, then check against the picture. Children spell one word each on tiles.

#### 15 min. Fresh passage

Map the tricky words by and her on the wall and say them plainly; no guessing from the picture or the first letter (error P). Show the passage Dee and the Peach, new to the class. Children read it once to themselves, quietly, then once to a partner, five minutes. Confirm meaning after decoding, three minutes, one ask at a time.

**Ask.** "Who has a sweet peach?" Expect: Dee.

**Ask.** "Where does she eat it?" Expect: by the creek.

**Ask.** "What sits on the leaf?" Expect: a bee.

Dictate, five minutes: "We eat a sweet peach." Say it twice, then once word by word. Children write it and underline the long e in each word; check with the passage. Say "a creek is a small stream of water" if a child asks.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each. Tiles: spell speed, cream, penny and steep. Sort: sort the ee, ea and y cards and add peach, creek, puppy and bee. Reread: partners read Dee and the Peach, then The Green Beanbag. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 3 oral card (group 4 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle); the others work in stations. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Say: "Say one word with ee, ea or y and tell where the team sits." Expect: in the middle of a word, or y at the end.

### Other rooms

No printer: read the article from the screen and draw the photograph on scrap paper. No wall: draw the sound-spelling-map on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the screen. No photograph: draw a cushion and a lamp in a corner. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The partner round and the stations.

- **10 min. The article.** Read 02-b aloud, photograph covered. Your child tells it back with first, then, last.
  **Say.** "What happens first, then, last?" Expect: pupils read each day, for ten minutes, and volunteers listen.
- **10 min. The photograph.** Uncover it.
  **Say.** "What does the photograph show that the words do not?" Expect: a cushion and a lamp.
- **10 min. Dee and the Peach.** Your child reads it, then writes "We eat a sweet peach."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X14v2]] Reread the 02-a lunchtime paragraph twice and show its photograph; your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the pairs part to the frame alone and keep the recount, the model and the exit. Longer: a child draws a second photograph for the article and says what it would add.

### Words and supports

Library, volunteer, photograph. Frames: "The photograph shows ___ so ___ ." "First ___, then ___, last ___ ." Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write draws, and you write the words. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 15 reads the third article, Following a School Day, and uses its diagram. Its opening repair is for error C on today's exit (a photograph named with no detail of what it adds, such as "It has a classroom"): ask what the photograph shows that the words do not, then say it with the frame. Any L, M, P or Q miss from today's passage goes to day 15's foundations reader.
