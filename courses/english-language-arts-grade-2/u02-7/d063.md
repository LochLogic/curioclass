# Day 63: Finishing the draft with a closing

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write Cal's draft so far on the large page, with two closings beside it: "At last his drawing showed the ridge clearly." and "Cal ate a snack on the bus." Hang page 11 of The Mark in the Stone. Mark today's six (group 1 of five in cycle 12) on the unit 2 record sheet. Keep the day 62 check sheets for the opening repair. Key: key-cycle-11-sort-cards.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read aloud twice: "Zane wanted a clean shell. He rinsed it. He dried it. Last he set it on the shelf." Ask three children: "Say the first two actions using First and Next." If many missed day 62's exit (error O), slide a time-word card in front of each action and read it again; otherwise retrieve the draft pages.

#### 5 min. Words

Review: the ending concludes the action (day 36). Closure is the feeling that a story is finished because the end answers the start. Say each once and point to it on the story-map: closure, finish, conclude. A child says one sentence about the end of a story.

#### 8 min. Reread and role-play

Read page 11 of The Mark in the Stone aloud, where Amara tells her friend what the mark is. One child acts Amara on the bus, and you read the words of Amara and Ben. Say aloud: "Amara and Ben are made up." The class says what the ending finishes from the beginning (4 minutes).

#### 8 min. Model: choose the closing that finishes the event

Show Cal's draft so far, ending with "Then he drew the lines." Read the two closings aloud. Ask: "Does the end answer the start?" for each. Children point to the one that does, and say what it answers. Draw an arrow from the right closing to Cal's thought, "Is it a root?" Say: "A closing that ties to the find, with a time word, finishes the event."

#### 18 min. Write

Each child finishes their own draft page with Then and At last, and makes the At last line a closing that ties to their find, using the plan sheet. You move and give no wording. A child who cannot write dictates to you.

If the closing has nothing to do with the find (error P), put the first box beside the last box and ask: "Did the end answer the start?" If the lines have no time words (error O), slide a time-word card in front of each action.

#### 5 min. Illustrate

Each child adds a picture that shows how the event ends.

#### 6 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine from day 1. Partners read their drafts aloud, and the listener points to the time words and then to the closing. Ask: "Does the end answer the start?" Hear the six (about 20 seconds each, 2 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the exit ticket, read the card of item s02-X63 aloud twice, and children write one sentence. Mark the six on the record sheet as shown, shown with help (after "What does the card say?") or not yet seen. Tag error P or O on a miss.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Return the day 62 check. Reread six cycle 11 words and the misses from it (errors Y and AA): map the vowel team that spells the sound in each word, then write the word once. Map the unexpected part of would and once with a star.

#### 15 min. Teach: -tion

Review: an ending can be read as one chunk, as -ed in jumped (day 33). Model mapping nation, section and collection: tion says /shun/, one chunk (6 minutes). Blend by chunk (na tion, sec tion, col lec tion) and say each word. Children say each. Spell nation and section with tiles (4 minutes). Review: said is a mapped word (day 33); star its unexpected part (2 minutes). Never guess a word from its picture.

Check: nation and section spelled with -tion, and said with the unexpected part starred.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six (group 1) sit with you for the cycle 12 oral card, two minutes each; only its first two lines are used today. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 are in three stations of eight: tiles (build nation, section, collection), sort (the twelve cycle 11 cards by spelling) and reread (Blue Fruit Soup with a partner). Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: groups move on.

Check: each child reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which letters say /shun/ at the end of nation?" Expect: tion.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the two closings and the -tion words on the wall and rule a sheet for the exit. Large group: each child in a group answers once, about 20 seconds; the six hear you in turn.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read it aloud.** Your child reads their draft aloud, and you point to each time word.
  **Say.** "Which part finishes the story?" Expect: the last line, which answers what the start asked.
- **10 min. Finish it.** Your child adds a last line with a time word, such as At last.
- **10 min. Words.** Say nation and section. Your child writes each and says which letters say /shun/.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X63]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the illustrate part and keep the writing, the exit and the teaching. Longer: a child writes a second closing for the same find and says which one fits better.

### Words and supports

Closure, finish, conclude, nation, section, collection, said. Frames: "At last ___." "The end answers the start because ___." Pointing is an answer. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

After class read each draft and write one comment on focus (one find) and one on elaboration (an action, a thought and a feeling). Day 64 opens with a repair of today's exit (errors P and O, a closing with no tie to the find or with no time word), then revises a draft after peer and adult feedback. It begins -sion with the cycle 12 reader.
