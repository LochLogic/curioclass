# Section 3.12 opener: recording the stories (days 117 and 118)

## What the section does

Each child tells the planned bee-view story to the recorder in about two minutes and explains how the drawing clarifies it. Twenty children record on day 117 and ten on day 118. Day 118 also compares a formal and an informal story line and gives each child written feedback with a chance to rerecord a short phrase.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The recorder is one kind of tool and stays in the room; a partner recording is offered to any child who prefers it, with each contribution named. The first class is observed by the teacher; none of the stories is scored for the whole course until the answer key is read.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A story has an order,** facts, details a listener can picture, and an ending.
- **A drawing clarifies** when it shows an action or feeling the story names.
- **A formal opening** suits a listener who does not know the story.
- **No home way of talking is wrong.** Each fits a listener.

## The errors, in plain words

- A feeling is missing, an event with no feeling, or a flat statement (AI, Z).
- A drawing that does not show what is said (V).
- A live telling in place of a recording (U).
- A way of talking ranked as bad English (X).
