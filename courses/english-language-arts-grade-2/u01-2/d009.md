# Day 9: Which words change the meaning?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write three line cards a pair (they are listed in the third part) and print the link card, a speech bubble that reads "You said ___ . I would add ___ ." Print the passage "May and Dale" on a card. Write ai and ay words on cards: rain, wait, paid, day, play, stay. Today's six are the fourth group on the record sheet. Their oral card: paid, play, grin, shine, drip, much; and "Dale will wait in the rain."

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many children missed day 8's exit (error A, an action that contradicts the lines), reread the start of chapter 7 with a finger on each action and replace the wrong one with the one the story gives. Otherwise partners point to the two-event row of the story-map (review from day 8) and tell each event and what Mira did.

#### 4 min. Words

Say mean as "intend, or want to say," used in "Do you mean ...?" Say it once in a sentence: "When I say, 'Did you mean no?' I ask what you want to say." Then exchange: the talk between two people. Children say both words and tell a partner one exchange from chapter 7, such as "Not now."

#### 10 min. Reread the exchange in chapter 7

Say aloud: "This story is made up. Mira and Ada are invented." Read the exchange, from "I can draw the hall map" to "Do not forget Pancake," once, about two minutes, in two voices, and show Ada's page and Mira's blank sheet in the picture. Then give each pair these lines to read in parts, one child as Mira and one as Ada:

Mira: Ada, when you said "not now," did you mean no?
Ada: No! I meant after.

Partners read twice and swap parts. Read for the listener: pause at each full stop. Take a moment on the wait signal if two children begin at once (error H).

#### 12 min. Which words change the meaning

Lay out the three line cards: "Not now." "Maybe after I finish this page." "I meant after." Say: "Ada said two things before Mira asked. 'Not now' sounds like no. Some words in the other lines show what Ada meant." Read the three cards aloud, then point to each.

**Ask.** Which words in "Maybe after I finish this page" tell us Ada does not mean no? Expect: "maybe after," or "after I finish."

**Ask.** Which words in "I meant after" settle it? Expect: "meant after."

Pairs put the three cards in the order Ada said them and underline the word that turns "no" into "after". Take answers from three pairs. If a pair says every word changes it, ask: "Which one word do you hear first?" Hear your six, half a minute each, as pairs work.

#### 13 min. Link a comment

Show the link card and say: "You used You said ___; I would add ___ on day 3, and that is review. Now it has a card. A link uses a word the last speaker said. You say it first. Then you add your own idea." Read the frame: "You said ___ ; I would add ___ ." Model with Ada's other line from the exchange, "I missed a whole day of subtraction." Say: "You said you missed a whole day. I would add that you needed time to catch up." Then give one comment that does not link: "I like fish." Ask: "Does it use Ada's words?" Expect: no. A comment that could follow anything is not a link (error I).

Pairs practice for eight minutes. One child reads an Ada line, either "No! I meant after." or "Maybe after I finish this page." The partner links with the card. Then the first child asks "Which part do you mean?", review from day 2, and the partner points to the word. They swap after each turn. Then spend three minutes on whole-class links: two pairs say theirs and the class says which word was used. Hear your six during the practice.

#### 8 min. Make one link card

Each child chooses one of the three line cards and fills the link card on paper or by drawing: a word of the line in the first blank and one added idea in the second. A child who cannot write says it and you write the words. Walk round and read three cards aloud, with the child's permission.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card twice and ask (s01-X9): "Say one comment that links to what Ada said. Start with the words You said." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means "You said" followed by a word of Ada's remark and an added idea. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What word did Ada say that you can use?" Not yet seen means a comment with no word of the remark, or no answer.

#### 3 min. Close

Say: "Tell your partner which word you used to link." Expect: a word of the last speaker, such as missed, after or meant.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Write the word most children missed on day 8's sort or wrong-word hunt. Model it with tiles: say the word, map the whole team on one tile, blend, and spell. If a child reads the team with a short vowel (error M), map the team first. If the team is in the wrong place (error Q), set the word beside its family on the wall: ai in the middle, ay at the end.

#### 15 min. Fresh passage

Show the passage on a card, new to the class: "May and Dale play in the rain. A cat sits on a rail. Dale waits and May stays. The rain stops at last." Children read it cold, a sentence at a time, tapping words as they go and mapping any team before they say it. After each sentence, confirm meaning: "Who is in this sentence?" and "Where is the cat?" Expect: May and Dale, and the cat sits on a rail. Do not let anyone guess from a picture or the first letter (error P).

Then dictation, seven minutes. Write "for" on the board as a tricky word: say it plainly and tap it. Say the sentence "We wait for the train." Children say it back, tap each word and write it on paper or with tiles, checking the team in wait and train against the wall. Say the sentence once more; they touch each word as they reread.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each. Tiles: spell wait, stay, may and paid. Sort: sort the ai and ay cards and say where the team sits, then read each word. Reread: partners read The Rainy Day, a page each, for accuracy first. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 2 oral card; the others work in stations. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Say: "Show your partner an ai word and an ay word and say where each team sits." Expect: ai in the middle, ay at the end.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the lines on torn card and draw the speech bubble. No wall: draw the sound-spelling-map on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper. No printed card: draw a speech bubble on paper and write the two blanks.
**Skip.** The stations and the fresh passage.

- **5 min. The exchange.** Read the lines "Not now." "Maybe after I finish this page." "I meant after."
  **Say.** "Which words tell us Ada does not mean no?" Expect: "maybe after" or "meant after."
- **10 min. Link a comment.** Show the speech bubble. Say the frame "You said ___ ; I would add ___ ." Read Ada's line "I meant after." Your child links to it with a word of the line.
- **10 min. Ai and ay.** Write rain, wait, day and play. Read each.
  **Say.** "Where does ai sit, and where does ay sit?" Expect: ai in the middle, ay at the end.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X9]] Read the card twice. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: link to one line instead of two, shorten the third rotation round and keep the line cards, the model, the card and the exit. Longer: a child makes a second link card for another remark and says which word links.

### Words and supports

Mean, exchange, link, word. Frames: "You said ___ ; I would add ___ ." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write draws, and you write the words. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 10 reads chapter 8. If many children gave error I on today's exit (a comment unrelated to the last remark), its opening repair says the last speaker's word, then adds. Any ai or ay word that today's dictation showed to be missed goes to the tiles station on day 10.
