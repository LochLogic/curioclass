# Day 121: Read a second telling and track what changed

## Teacher page

### Before class

If many children in day 120's exit showed error B (a lesson on the wrong event), run the repair. Write the cycle 20 dictionary page by hand: city, story, cry, lady, pony, bunny, day, play, monkey, each with its plural. Write three partner notes (invented): 1. "Different: the grasshopper sings all summer." 2. "Different: only the second telling has a promise to repay." 3. "Different: only the second telling has a narrator." Mark today's six and the eight for the oral card (children 9 to 16).

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Hold up the day 120 lesson "Never sing" and two events. Ask the pair beside you: which event shows it? Put the sticky dot on the event that holds the lesson up, and say why: the dot goes on the event that shows what the lesson says, not on the one that comes last or shows hunger (5 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Read 02

Say aloud that this is a second telling of the same fable: it follows the French poet La Fontaine as put into English by Elizur Wright, a translation from the 1800s, and Curio Class wrote the words (1 minute). Preview six words: version, request, borrow, repay, promise, narrator (3 minutes). Review: borrow (kindergarten, day 23), promise (grade 1, day 35) and, from grade 1, a narrator is the teller of a story. Read the telling aloud (2 minutes). Pairs read it twice by turns, a sentence each (6 minutes: 169 words). Each child underlines who speaks, one pencil colour for the grasshopper and one for the ant (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Model: what changed

A version is one telling of a story; another telling is another version. On the side-by-side print compare one row: place and who speaks. In the first telling there are two scenes, the field in summer and the ants' nest in winter, and only the animals speak. In the second telling there is one scene, at the ant's door, and a narrator says something about the ant. Run a finger across the print so the class sees the gap on the side that has nothing (6 minutes). Pairs tell each other one thing that is in both tellings, and the class shares two (4 minutes).

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: turn, pair, link, clarify (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Turn and talk on "What does the ant say in each telling?" (4 minutes). Link: "I heard ___ say ___; I would add ___" (3 minutes). Share three in the whole class (3 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Pairs

Read the three partner notes aloud (2 minutes). Say aloud that the notes are invented. Each pair judges each note against the two tellings, finds the words that prove it right or wrong, and fixes any note that is wrong (8 minutes). Note 1 lists something both tellings have as a difference: the pair moves it to a "both" line and says the words. Share each note once (3 minutes). Confer with the six heard (2 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is invented. Read the card twice (1 minute): [[s04-X121]]. Each child marks one of the two pictures (2 minutes). Hear the six, 15 seconds each: [[s04-X121b]] (2 minutes). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Giving the picture of the hand giving a crumb is error D.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

No hand-back is routed to this slot. Pairs read the word slips from day 120 aloud, then each child writes one plural from memory and checks it letter by letter (5 minutes).

#### 10 min. Reread the cycle 20 reader

Pairs read Pennies and Bunnies from page 1, one page each, for accuracy (5 minutes), then read pages 5 to 7 for the first time with a finger under the words, confirming the meaning after decoding. Guessing from a picture or first letter is error Q. Then read the whole once more smoothly (5 minutes, RF.2.4b).

#### 16 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Children 9 to 16 read the oral card to you, two minutes each including the swap (16 minutes). The other twenty-two use tiles, a sort and the reader in two stations of eleven. Mark the heard column.

#### 12 min. Sort

Twelve word slips are sorted into consonant-y and vowel-y: cities, days, stories, plays, ladies, monkeys, ponies, jays, cries, bunnies, ways, pennies (6 minutes). Each child says the letter before the y each time. Each child then writes four on paper: cries, ladies, monkeys, days (6 minutes). Spelling cryes is error R.

#### 12 min. Dictionary

Review: check a spelling in a dictionary entry: find the first letter, find the entry, compare the letters, correct the draft (grade 2, unit 1, day 27). Each pair takes a word slip with one singular word from the cycle 20 dictionary page, writes the plural from memory as a draft, finds the entry, compares letters, corrects if needed and says what changed (8 minutes, L.2.2e). Share four pairs (4 minutes). A plural left unchecked is error S: find the entry word, then say what changed.

#### 5 min. Close

Ask: "What changed when the y became ies?" Expect: the y turned into i and es was added. Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the notes and the plural entries on the board; pairs point to the line. No tiles: paper squares. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No second copy: read both tellings from one page.
**Skip.** The notes, the sort and the dictionary page.

- **10 min. Second telling.** Read the second telling aloud together.
  **Say.** "What does the ant say?" Expect: she asks what the grasshopper did in summer and says now you can dance.
- **10 min. What changed.** Your child tells one thing that is different from the first telling.
  **Say.** "What did the grasshopper ask for?" Expect: to borrow grain and promise to repay.
- **10 min. Plurals.** Your child writes cries, ladies and days and says the letter before the y.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X121]] and [[s04-X121b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the model, two notes and the exit; read the reader once. Longer: a pair finds a third difference in the two tellings.

### Words and supports

Version, request, borrow, repay, promise, narrator. Frames: "In the first telling ___. In the second telling ___." "Only the second telling has ___." Point to the line in the print as an answer.

### Tomorrow

Day 122 compares the two tellings as whole stories. Today's exit tells you who can name what only one version has: if many named what both versions share as a difference (error D), day 122 begins with a five-minute opening repair holding the two request lines side by side.
