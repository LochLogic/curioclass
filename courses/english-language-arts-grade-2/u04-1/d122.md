# Day 122: Compare the two tellings by characters, events and morals

## Teacher page

### Before class

If many children in day 121's exit showed error D (a shared detail called a difference), run the opening repair below. Copy the two request lines onto strips: "May I have a few grains?" and "May I borrow a little grain? I promise to repay you." Highlight nothing yet. Mark today's six and the seven for the oral card (children 17 to 23). Keep the passage By the Pond face down until its part.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Hold the two request lines side by side. Touch the words that only the second line has: borrow and promise to repay (3 minutes). Pairs say which words are in both lines and which are in one line (2 minutes). A difference is something one version has and the other does not.

#### 3 min. Retrieval

Pairs say one thing that was the same and one thing that was different between two characters they met in unit 3 (3 minutes). The class shares one. Practice only.

#### 14 min. Reread both tellings

Pairs lay the side-by-side print between them. They read the first telling aloud, a sentence each (5 minutes), then the second (4 minutes, RF.2.4b), and highlight in one colour every line where the grasshopper asks for help in the first telling and in the other colour the same moment in the second (5 minutes).

#### 12 min. Model: compare whole versions

Use the print. Characters: the same two, a grasshopper and an ant. Events both have: the grasshopper sings all summer and has nothing to eat in winter. Events only one has: the ant's warning and the winter scene at the nest (first telling); the promise to repay, the narrator's remark and "Now you can dance" (second telling) (5 minutes). The request changes: a few grains in one, borrowing with a promise in the other. The ant's answer differs too. Each moral rests on events, so different morals are accepted. For the first: "Getting ready before a hard time helps," because the ants stored grain. For the second: "Plan ahead so you do not have to ask," because the grasshopper sang and stored nothing (4 minutes). Model the frame with two lines you make up, a visitor line and a friend line about the same weekend news: Both tell the news. But the visitor line says what is coming first. Review: formal and informal talk (grade 2, unit 3, day 111) (3 minutes). Say aloud that those lines are invented.

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn, pair, link, clarify on "How does the request change between tellings?" (4 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___" (3 minutes). Share three (3 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 11 min. Pairs

Give each pair a chart on which someone has put "The grasshopper promises to repay" in the box "One thing that is the same" (invented, 1 minute). The pair judges it against the tellings, moves it to the different box, and writes one thing that really is the same (4 minutes). Then they write a moral for each telling in a few words and name the event that holds it up (4 minutes), and say the frame Both ___. But ___. to a partner. Confer with the six heard (2 minutes). Look for one real similarity, one real difference, a moral resting on an event in the chart, and the child's own words.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is invented. Read the card twice (1 minute): [[s04-X122]]. Each child marks one of the two pictures (2 minutes). Hear the six, 15 seconds each: [[s04-X122b]] (2 minutes). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Saying "they are not alike" is error E; giving the picture with the leaf in one scene is also error E.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

No hand-back is routed to this slot. Children write pennies and bunnies from memory, then check them against the reader and say the letter before the y (5 minutes).

#### 15 min. A fresh passage

Hand out By the Pond, not the reader. Say the title yourself. Review: map each word, blend, read it whole. Pairs read it aloud a sentence each (6 minutes). Confirm what it means after decoding: each child tells a partner what Pam does and points to the words that say it (5 minutes). Then the class reads it once together (4 minutes). A word guessed from its first letter is error Q.

#### 14 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Children 17 to 23 read the oral card to you, two minutes each including the swap (14 minutes). The other twenty-three use tiles, a sort and the reader in two stations of eleven and twelve. Mark the heard column. A printed word misread and left is error AE.

#### 14 min. Dictation

Say each word, use it in a sentence, say it again, and children write: pansies, tries, ladies (6 minutes). Dictate in short parts: Pam grins at the ponies on hot days. (4 minutes). Each child then circles the letter before the y in each word and checks the ending against the pattern on the board (4 minutes). Spelling pansyes is error R.

#### 12 min. Stations

The seven heard today sort slips and reread By the Pond with a partner. The other twenty-three reread Pennies and Bunnies in pairs and build three plurals with tiles; walk and hear any child who read the passage with help (12 minutes).

### Other rooms

No printer: write the chart on the board; pairs point to the box and say it. No highlighters: underline in two ways, a line and a wavy line. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No second telling at hand: tell it from memory.
**Skip.** The chart and the highlighting.

- **10 min. Compare.** Tell the two tellings of the fable and say one way they are alike.
  **Say.** "What is the same in both?" Expect: a grasshopper who sings and an ant.
- **10 min. Different.** Your child says what the grasshopper asks for in each.
  **Say.** "How is the request different?" Expect: a few grains; borrowing with a promise to repay.
- **10 min. Spell.** Your child writes pansies, tries and ladies and says the letter before the y.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X122]] and [[s04-X122b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the model, the chart and the exit; read the passage once. Longer: a pair draws the two ants side by side.

### Words and supports

Compare, same, different, version, moral, request. Frames: "Both ___. But ___." "The lesson is ___ because ___." Say it in the home language first; pointing is an answer.
