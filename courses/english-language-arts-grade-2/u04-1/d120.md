# Day 120: Recount the fable and say which events hold up its lesson

## Teacher page

### Before class

If many children in day 119's exit showed error A (the events out of order), run the opening repair below; if few did, give it to a small group. Mark today's six for the exit and the eight for the oral card (children 1 to 8). Set out a story-map strip for each pair.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say aloud that the beaver card is invented. Lay the three beaver pictures from day 119 in the wrong order and in the right order beside each other. Touch each card and say first, next and last aloud with the class, then tell the beaver story with finger pointing: the story says the rain came after the wall was built (5 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Reread 01

Read the winter passage aloud, from "Then the season changed" to the end, while pairs follow with a finger (2 minutes). Pairs read it, one sentence each (5 minutes). Then read it a second time like talking, with the voices of the characters and a pause at each full stop (5 minutes, RF.2.4b). Each pair reads one line they like to the class (3 minutes). Preview the words that gave trouble yesterday: plenty, store, season.

#### 10 min. Model: which events support the lesson?

Say what a moral is: the lesson a fable teaches. Review (grade 1): you called it the central message, and you said an event that shows it. Recount the fable in order, then offer a lesson: "Getting ready before a hard time can help." Test it against two events: the ants gathering grain all summer, and the ants having grain in winter. Both hold it up (5 minutes). Then offer "Run fast" and find no event that holds it up. A lesson is accepted only when an event in the story supports it (5 minutes). Say aloud that you made up "Run fast" to show a lesson with no event.

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn, pair, link, clarify on "Which events support the moral?" Children say "The lesson is ___ because ___." (5 minutes), then link: "I would add ___" (3 minutes). Share two in the whole class (2 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Pairs

Each pair fills a story-map strip with three small drawings or a few words: summer, the season changes, winter (7 minutes). Then give the pair one lesson card, "Never sing," and two events: the grasshopper sings all summer; the ants carry grain. The pair finds and marks which event, if any, holds the lesson up, and says why (5 minutes). Expect: neither; the song did not make the winter hard, and getting no food ready did. Move around and confer with the six heard (3 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is invented. Read the card twice (1 minute): [[s04-X120]]. Each child marks one of the two pictures (2 minutes). Hear the six, 15 seconds each: [[s04-X120b]] (2 minutes). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Marking the picture of Jay eating is error B: the lesson belongs to the event that shows getting ready.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

No hand-back is routed to this slot. Pairs read yesterday's slate words to each other: pony, lady, monkey and their plurals (2 minutes). Each child covers one, writes it from memory and checks it letter by letter (3 minutes).

#### 15 min. First reading of the cycle 20 reader

Say the title of Pennies and Bunnies yourself; children read the pages only. Read page 1 aloud, touching the words (2 minutes). Pairs echo-read pages 2 to 4: one reads a sentence, the other reads it back (7 minutes). Cover each picture while the pair reads; guessing a word from the picture or its first letter is error Q. After decoding, each pair tells what happened in its own words and points to the words that say it (6 minutes). Pages 5 to 7 are read tomorrow.

#### 16 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 2, day 59. Children 1 to 8 read the oral card to you at your table, two minutes each including the swap, so call the next child while the last one returns (16 minutes). Say aloud that the card is for practice of the cycle only. The other twenty-two use tiles, a sort and the reader in two stations of eleven. Mark the heard column. A wrong oral answer that contradicts the passage is error AB.

#### 14 min. Spell

Say each word, use it in a sentence, say it again, and children write it: bunnies, ponies, plays, cities (6 minutes). Dictate two sentences in short parts, saying each part twice: Tess plays with the bunnies. The ponies munch on hot days. (8 minutes).

#### 10 min. Sort and reread

The eight children heard today sort twelve slips by consonant-y or vowel-y (pennies, days, cities, plays, ponies, stories and the six singulars) and reread the reader to a partner (10 minutes). The other twenty-two reread Pennies and Bunnies in pairs.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the strip on the board; pairs point to the box. No tiles: write letters on paper squares. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No picture cards: ask the exit aloud and let your child say it.
**Skip.** The story-map strip and the tiles.

- **10 min. Winter.** Read or tell what happens to the grasshopper in winter.
  **Say.** "What does the ant do all summer? What happens in winter?" Expect: she gathers grain, so she has food; the grasshopper has none.
- **10 min. Lesson.** Your child says what the fable teaches and which event shows it.
  **Say.** "Which event shows it?" Expect: the ants gathering grain.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child reads and spells bunnies and ponies, saying the letter before the y.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X120]] and [[s04-X120b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the model, one pair task and the exit; read the reader once. Longer: a pair defends a second lesson with an event.

### Words and supports

Moral, lesson, event, support (to hold up), recount. Frames: "The lesson is ___ because ___." "The event ___ shows it." Point, draw or say the lesson in the home language first.

### Tomorrow

Day 121 reads a second telling. Today's exit tells you who can name the event that holds the lesson: if many chose the picture of Jay eating (error B), day 121 begins with a five-minute opening repair with the sticky dot. The eight heard on the oral card tell you who needs a small group.
