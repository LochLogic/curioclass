# Day 119: Open the question with the first Ant and Grasshopper telling

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 118's exit told you who can shape a line for a visitor; no repair is routed to today, and the unit 3 records stay with you. Hang the unit question on a strip: How can people help pollinators thrive? Draw the story-map on the board with boxes for who and where, summer, the season changes, the request and the reply. Put each child's three exit cards in an envelope, mixed. Make a slate for each child.

### The day

#### 7 min. Retrieval (practice only, no check evidence)

Review (grade 1): retell a story in order with first, next and last, say what it teaches, and name a feeling word. Pairs take a story they know from grade 1 (for example the magpie bridge tale, or one they choose) and tell it in three events, then say what it teaches and one feeling word (4 minutes). Walk and listen; note one miss for the story-map on day 120. Then hear the four children whose day 118 exit you did not hear, 45 seconds each plus the change-over (3 minutes). This is practice, never a check. Day 118's assessment comes back to the children today as their marked pages; hand each back with one fix to make in pencil, then carry on.

#### 8 min. Hook

Read the first part of the fable aloud, up to the grasshopper's question "Why should I worry?" (1 minute). **Ask.** "What does the ant mean by getting ready?" Expect: getting food ready before winter comes; pairs tell each other (2 minutes), then share two answers (2 minutes). Post the question of the unit and read it together: How can people help pollinators thrive? Each child draws one way to get ready for a hard time on the student page (3 minutes). Nothing from home is asked.

#### 10 min. Where fables come from

A fable is a short animal story that shows something to think about. Say aloud that the fable we read today follows a telling credited to Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece, in a book by Joseph Jacobs (1894), and that Curio Class wrote the words. Point to Greece on the map (2 minutes). Fables are still told today in many languages. Children turn to a partner and tell one story they have heard that had an animal in it, in any language they use (4 minutes); the class shares three (4 minutes). No costume, no accent, and no guess about any people.

#### 12 min. Read 01

Preview the words with the picture of each on the board: gather, store, season, plenty, and prepare (to get ready) (2 minutes). Read the whole fable aloud while pairs follow with their copy (2 minutes). Pairs read it again by turns, a paragraph each (6 minutes). The class rereads the last four paragraphs together (2 minutes). Hunger is never a proof of a bad character: the grasshopper loves music and believes there is plenty.

#### 8 min. Model the story-map

Fill the board map as you think aloud: who and where (a grasshopper, an ant, a field), the summer talk, the ants gathering, the season changing, the request and the reply (4 minutes). Review (grade 1): the story-map of who, where and when, and what happens. The ant says what she thinks; the grasshopper says what she thinks. A recount keeps the key events in order and drops small details. Then read this recount aloud with two events swapped: "The season changed. The ants gathered grain all summer. The grasshopper asked for a few grains." Pairs say which two are in the wrong order and tell it right (4 minutes).

#### 7 min. Talk

Review: turn, pair, link, clarify (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Children turn to a partner on "What does each animal think about winter?", then link: "You said ___; I would add ___" (4 minutes). Share three links in the whole class (3 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is invented for practice. Read the card twice (1 minute): [[s04-X119]]. Each child lays the three cards in the order the story tells, on the desk (2 minutes). Then hear the six, 15 seconds each with "How do you know?": [[s04-X119b]] (2 minutes). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Cards in the wrong order are error A.

#### 3 min. Transitions

Collect the envelopes. Children keep the student page and move to the foundations block.

#### 5 min. Retrieve

Review: plurals with -s and -es, and y as a vowel sound (grade 2, cycles 3 and 4). Children write one plural on a slate for each word you say (2 minutes: bag, box, dish) and read them to a partner (3 minutes).

#### 15 min. Model, map, blend (cycle 20)

New today: when a word ends in a consonant and y, the y changes to i and es is added; when a vowel comes before the y, just add s. Say each word, touch a tile for each sound, blend, then spell with the tiles on the table and the board, class and teacher together. Consonant before the y: city, cities; story, stories; cry, cries (9 minutes). Vowel before the y: day, days; play, plays (4 minutes). Ask children to say the letter before the y each time (2 minutes). Reading a word from its first letter, such as city read as cat, is error Q: cover the picture, touch each tile, blend.

#### 10 min. First-day sample (diagnostic, unscored)

Each child builds three new plurals on a slate: pony, lady, monkey. Say each word once more with a sentence. Walk the groups and mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen for each group (10 minutes). Nothing is scored.

#### 12 min. Map the cycle's irregular words

Map two common words with no guessing from pictures or first letters: they (ey says the long a sound) and have (the e at the end does not make the a long). Say the word, tap the sounds, draw the sound-spelling map with a heart over the surprising part, then read it in a short phrase (6 minutes each).

#### 13 min. Spell

Say each word, use it in a sentence, say it again, and children build it with tiles and then write it: stories, cries, days, plays, cities, city (9 minutes). Dictate: She plays in the city on hot days. (4 minutes). Spelling cityes or dayes is error R: ask, is the letter before the y a consonant or a vowel?

#### 5 min. Close

Ask: "What do you look at before you add the ending?" Expect: the letter before the y. Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No tiles: write the letters on paper squares. No printed cards: draw the three pictures on the board and have children point. The fable is read from one copy and shared by pairs.

### At home

**Swap.** No picture cards: ask the exit aloud and let your child say the order.
**Skip.** The map, the tiles and the partner talk.

- **10 min. The fable.** Read or tell the fable of the ant and the grasshopper.
  **Say.** "What does each animal think about winter?" Expect: the ant says get ready; the grasshopper says there is plenty now.
- **10 min. Get ready.** Your child draws one way to get ready for a hard time on the student page.
- **10 min. Plurals.** Your child says the letter before the y in city, story, cry, day and play, and says each plural.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X119]] and [[s04-X119b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the fable, the story-map model and the exit; trim the sharing. Longer: a child tells the fable to a grown-up in the home language.

### Words and supports

Fable, Aesop, Greece, gather, store, season, plenty, prepare, recount. Frames: "The ant thinks ___ because ___." "The grasshopper thinks ___." Point or draw to answer; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 120 asks what the fable teaches and which event holds it up. Today's exit tells you who can put events in order: if many put them out of order (error A), day 120 begins with a five-minute opening repair. The sample tells you which groups need the plural pattern.
