# Unit 4.6: Opinion writing, one piece from first draft to ending (days 138 to 142)

## What the section does
For five days the class writes one short opinion about a pollinator garden from two reading cards, rereads a model, chooses exact words, revises after a note and edits one convention. The foundations block spends the week on the cycle 23 and 24 spelling patterns.

## How the days run
- Day 138: reread a model opinion and swap flat words for exact ones; cycle 23 written check.
- Days 139 to 141: three sittings on one product (topic and opinion, reasons with a link, an ending), then an editing pass.
- Day 142: a fresh model, a revision after a note, a short exit.

## The ideas at an adult's level
An opinion has five parts: a topic, a statement of what we think, reasons, a link word that joins a reason to the opinion, and an ending. Exact words are near neighbours that differ in strength or manner (a sip is smaller than a gulp). Revision changes an idea; editing fixes a convention.

## The errors in plain words
- Near words treated as the same (a quiet step and a heavy step called one thing).
- A reason with no link word, or with no ending after it.
- A reason that does not help the opinion (it is about the writer).
- Copying the draft out again instead of changing an idea.
