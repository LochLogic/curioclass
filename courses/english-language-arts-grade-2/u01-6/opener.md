# Section 1.6 opener: the school book and the last checks (days 27 to 32)

## What the section does

Days 27 to 29 are the writing of a small school book that answers the question "Explain what schools help children do, using facts and definitions from two articles." Children draft the topic page and a first fact page (day 27), add a second fact page, a meaning for a hard word and a clarifying picture with a caption (day 28), and write the conclusion, after a partner's suggestion, and revise (day 29). Day 30 is a check day: children revise the conclusion from your written feedback, publish the book, and spelling and dictionary tasks are observed. Day 31 is a second check day on three cold passages and two pictures. Day 32 is the last day of the unit: a respond day with four teacher groups and a fresh retry by recorded letter. Throughout, children learn to find an entry on a dictionary page, compare their spelling with it and fix the draft.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes: a 60-minute topic block and a 60-minute foundations block. On days 27 to 29 the topic block is a short repair, words, a model, drafting and a five-minute exit. From day 26 the foundations block is flexible: a short opening, a 24-minute reading conference with six children (Reading Cards 2 to 5 on days 27 to 30), and two short teacher groups chosen from the cycle 5 evidence. Day 30: revision 10, publish 50, spelling and dictionary 15, conferences 24, independent reading 21. Day 31: directions 5, five cold tasks 45, sharing 10, a foundations block of retrieval, two groups and a paired reread. Cards 2 to 5, the spelling card, the dictionary card and the pattern card are reserved: never teach from them or let a child see one before the day.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A book needs a topic that answers its question.** Facts come from two named articles, no outside facts, each with a source.
- **A meaning is the article's meaning.** Tell it in the article's words.
- **A conclusion follows from the facts.** It returns to the topic.
- **A dictionary page is used in four steps.** Find the entry by its first letter, compare the letters with the draft, check, then correct the draft. Copying a peer's spelling is a different act.
- **A picture adds.** Ask what the picture shows that the words do not say.

## The errors, in plain words

- **D, E, F, G:** the four rows of the book: a topic that answers the question; facts from the sources; a meaning for each hard word; a conclusion that follows.
- **R:** a spelling copied without consulting the entry.
- **Q:** a team dropped in spelling.
- **L, M:** a short vowel assigned to a long-vowel word, or a vowel team given a wrong sound.
- **O, P:** rushed reading; a word named from the picture.
- **A, B, C, N:** an answer that contradicts the passage; a trait where a response is wanted; a picture named without what it adds; a retell that loses the key ideas.
- **H to K:** the talk errors, repaired in day 32 round three.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A retry on day 32 is marked shown with help at best and never replaces the first mark. Written feedback on the book comes after class on days 27 to 29 and the book is scored once after class on day 30.
