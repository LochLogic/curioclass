# Day 111: Compare talk for a visitor with talk for a friend

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write the two model lines on the board, covered. Mark today's six, group 4 of five. Cut the three friend lines apart for each pair. If many of day 110's exits showed error R (the word on the wrong job) or error Z (no new word), begin the pairs part by having each pair say one friend line with one word for the thing and one for the action.

### The day

#### 8 min. Two lines, one idea

Today is an application day: the class compares, nothing is taught as new. Say aloud that the lines are invented. Say one idea two ways. To a visitor who has not seen the poster: "Good morning. Let me show you what the fly did on the daisy." To a friend who has: "Look, the fly just sat right there!" Tell the class who each is said to and what each listener needs: the visitor needs to be told what is coming; the friend already knows. Pairs tell each other the words that changed (3 minutes); the class shares (3 minutes). Neither line is bad English: each fits its listener, and the lesson compares, it does not correct (2 minutes).

#### 12 min. Sort the cards

Pairs lay the three friend-line cards in a row. For each, the pair says it again for a visitor who has not seen the poster, and lays a strip of paper under the card with the words that were added (3 minutes a card, 9 minutes). Share one card per pair (3 minutes). Calling the friend line bad is error X: say what it suits and who is listening; do not rank. Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Say it both ways

Each child takes one sentence from the poster and says it for a visitor, then for a friend, to a partner, who says the words that changed (1 minute each way, 2 minutes a child). Swap, then find a new partner and do it again (8 minutes). Five volunteers say theirs to the class (5 minutes). Home ways of speaking are fine for a friend; the class never ranks them. Move around and hear today's six (2 minutes).

#### 8 min. Link

Whole class: "What changed?" (The visitor line said more, and told what was coming.) Review: turn, pair, link, clarify (grade 2, unit 3, day 87). Children turn to a partner, then link: "I heard ___ say..." and ask one question if a line is unclear (8 minutes).

#### 10 min. Independent reading

Each child reads one passage from 01-b to 01-e quietly, a different one from yesterday. Circulate; mark nothing.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is invented. Say the two lines twice (1 minute): [[s03-X111]] and then [[s03-X111b]]. All tell a partner their pick and why (1 minute). Hear the six, 30 seconds each for the two asks (3 minutes). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Choosing the other line is error Z; calling a line bad English is error X.

#### 2 min. Transitions

Children keep the strips with the cards, take the student page and move to the foundations block.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

No hand-back is routed to this slot. Children read the words sorted on 110 to a partner (2 minutes), then cover one, write it from memory and check it letter by letter (3 minutes).

#### 15 min. Fresh passage and dictation

Review: au and aw say one sound (grade 2, unit 3, day 108). Pairs read Gus and the Fawn aloud, one sentence each (4 minutes). Check what it means after decoding: each child tells a partner what happened in their own words, then points to the words that tell it (4 minutes). Dictate two words and one sentence (7 minutes): claw, hawk, then A hawk saw the fawn; the word, a short sentence, the word again.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: today's six read the cycle 19 oral card to you, two minutes each, with 30 seconds of the vowel card and three irregular words in every turn; 24 are at stations. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes. Tiles: claw, hawk, vault, pause. Sort: eight slips by au or aw. Reread: the reader for accuracy. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which letters make the sound in saw?" Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the lines on torn paper. Pointing to the card is an answer. Home ways of speaking are never ranked.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: say the lines aloud.
**Skip.** The sorting of cards and the passage.

- **10 min. Two ways.** Your child says one poster sentence to you as a friend, then as if you had not seen the poster.
  **Say.** "What words did you add?" Expect: words that tell what is coming.
- **10 min. Which fits?** You say "Good morning. Let me tell you about my day." and "Guess what happened!" Your child says who each is said to.
  **Say.** "Is either one wrong?" Expect: no, each fits a listener.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child reads claw and hawk and says which letters make the sound.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X111]] and [[s03-X111b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two friend lines; keep the two ways, the link and the exit. Longer: a child says a second poster sentence both ways.

### Words and supports

Visitor, friend, added, formal (careful talk for a visitor), informal (easy talk for a friend). Frames: "For a visitor I say ___." "A friend already knows ___." A child may say the friend line first. Never mark a home way of speaking as wrong.

### Tomorrow

Day 112 plans the bee-view story and holds the cycle 19 check. Today's exit tells you who can match a line to its listener and say why: choosing the other line is error Z, ranking a line is error X.
