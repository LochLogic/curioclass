# Section 3.10 opener: rehearsing the talk, describing words and the story plan (days 109 to 112)

## What the section does

Children build a three-line chart of a good talk, rehearse in fives with one listener question each, learn how describing words for a thing and for an action make a line clearer, compare a talk for a visitor with talk for a friend, and plan a bee-view story of their own in five boxes. Day 112 includes one planned sentence recorded to show why a recording helps.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The talk work sits in the topic block; the foundations block continues cycle 19 (au and aw) with a written fifteen-minute check on day 112. The recording on 112 is one minute a child at the recording corner, and no recording is scored.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A good talk can be heard,** speaks in whole sentences and uses the source.
- **A question gets one more detail** and ties to what the speaker said.
- **A describing word for a thing** sits near the thing; **a describing word for an action** sits near the action.
- **A formal line and a friendly line** are both fine; each fits a listener.
- **A drawing helps a listener** when it shows what the story says.

## The errors, in plain words

- A question about something else, or a remark where a question is wanted (M, L).
- A word for the action put on the thing (R), or no new word at all (Z).
- One way of talking ranked as bad English (X).
- A drawing chosen for colour, not for what the story says (V).
