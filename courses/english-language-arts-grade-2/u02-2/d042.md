# Day 42: Another try

## Teacher page

### Before class

Sort the days 40 and 41 strips by the letters on the group record. A child with two letters goes to the earlier group (A, B, AD); one with none goes to group 4. Mark today's six (group 5 of five) on the record sheet. Print a fresh strip or six-box sheet for each child who retries. Cut the written check sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return the check and give feedback

Hand each child the strips with the marking (shown, shown with help or not yet seen) and one sentence of feedback that names a line from the strip. Hand back day 41's assessment strips with day 40's. Say: "A mark is where we start today, not where we stop." Name the four groups and where each child goes.

#### 10 min. Teacher group 1: letter A

Groups are about 8 children, a minute each. Everyone else reads The Mark in the Stone pages 4 to 7 or Mary Anning Looks Closely in pairs, a page each. Lay the Beginning card and the Ending card from the large story map beside the strips. Read each aloud. Each child says what that card does: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." Error A is the ending named as the start or the reverse. If a child stalls, ask: "Which card tells what Amara wonders?" Children with the same swap work together on one pair of cards.

Check: the child names each card's job with a detail from the map.

#### 10 min. Teacher group 2: letter B

Others read in pairs again. Read aloud: "How did the mark on the stone look? A: Like a tiny fan. B: Like a leaf." Children turn to page 2, put a finger on the line, say "I know because the text says ___" and cover the wrong choice. Page 3 gives a leaf as a question only. Each child then takes one wrong answer from the strip, reads the question again, finds its line and writes the line's words under it. Error B is a choice or detail the line contradicts. Say "Show me the line" and let the line decide.

#### 10 min. Teacher group 3: letter AD or shown with help

Others read in pairs again. Read aloud: "Why did Dr. Lee write the place and the day on a label?" Say the whole answer aloud first: "Dr. Lee wrote them so scientists know where the fossil was found." Each child says it back, then writes it with a capital and an end mark, and touches each to check. Page 10 holds the line. Error AD is an answer that is only a few words with no sentence. Children who gave an answer in a few words on the strip rewrite it in a full sentence.

#### 10 min. Teacher group 4: neither letter

Others read in pairs again. Each child composes a harder question about page 9 with a question word and asks it of a partner. Then each child says what the page leaves open. Expect: what kind of shell animal it was, which Dr. Lee cannot tell for sure. Ask: "Where does the page say so?" Children find the line, "I can't tell you for sure."

#### 10 min. Fresh retry

Say: "This story is made up for practice." Read the card of Theo and the Cup twice, children following. Each child retries the sheet missed: a child who missed the strip fills a fresh three-box strip and writes two sentences, the beginning and the ending; a child who missed the six-box sheet answers the six questions in a fresh six-box strip. Anyone who showed both may do the strip. Children may look at the card. Key, strip: the beginning has Theo wanting to carry a full cup of water to the plant and water splashing; the ending has the plant watered and the floor dry. Key, six boxes: who, Theo; what, carried a cup of water; where, to the class plant; when, Monday morning; why, the cup was too full and splashed; how, he poured some out and walked slowly holding it with both hands. Walk among children, about 20 seconds each, and mark the group record's retry column: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read Find the Gold aloud twice, showing the pictures; children read it themselves from day 44. Ask (s02-X42v2): "Tell me how the ending solves the opening problem." The six answer, about 20 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means the ring was lost and then found in the last sock. Shown with help means after the prompt "What was lost at the start?" Not yet seen means a detail the story does not give or contradicts (error B). Mark the record sheet.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Unscored, on the completed cycle 7 patterns only. Children use the four numbered lines. Say each sentence twice and the missing word once, a minute a line, and give no help beyond the word wall card for each vowel spelling. The sentences are "The pup found a bone", "Wrap the food in foil", "The king wore a crown" and "Will you join us at the table?" Collect the sheets. Tag wrong spellings as Y: each writes a taught vowel correspondence wrongly, such as fownd, foyl, croun or joyn. Leave the sheets unmarked for children; the marks go on the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Reader

Pairs reread Coins for a Toy, a page each, then again, the second time with the voice rising for the questions and the exclamations. Listen for a stop at each mark.

#### 25 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, two rounds. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes): the grade 1 words, the cycle 6 and cycle 7 words, then the sentence "The boy put the toy in the house." Each says the vowel sound in each word. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build point, loud, cow and oil, then change one letter. Sort: the twelve cycle 7 cards under oi / oy and ou / ow. Reread: pairs read Coins for a Toy. Round 2, 12 minutes: the groups move on one station and the six join the station they have not met.

Check: each child in a station reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

Today only: day 38's six read the oral card words to you, two minutes each (12 minutes), including the cycle 7 words, so that each of the first group is heard on the whole cycle. The others work in pairs with the partner word card: oil, boy, loud, house, cow, how, point, toy. A child reads a word and the partner says the vowel sound and spells it with tiles. No new teaching. Then say round and enjoy once each (3 minutes), unscored; children write them and check the vowel spellings against the sound-spelling-map.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the three-box and six-box strips on folded paper and the written check on four numbered lines. One copy of the card of Theo and the Cup: read it aloud twice. Large group: pair children within a group so each pair speaks for 40 seconds, or hear a rotating six and the others tell a partner.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed story: read a short story you know aloud twice. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The strip, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Back to the story.** Tell a story you both know. Your child says what the beginning introduces and what the ending concludes, and gives one detail for each, then names the line that shows it.
  **Say.** "How does the ending finish what the beginning started?" Expect: the ending finishes the problem or the job the beginning started.
- **10 min. Read.** Show oil, boy, out and how on cards. Your child reads each and says the vowel sound.
- **10 min. Spell.** Say loud, toy and mouse. Your child writes each and checks the vowel spelling.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X42v2]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: hold teacher groups 1 and 2 and the exit, and skip the reader. Longer: group 4 reads Mary Anning Looks Closely and asks a harder question about it.

### Words and supports

Beginning, ending, introduces, concludes, line, question words. Frames: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." "I know because the text says ___." Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 43 begins section 2.3 and repairs today's exit (error B, a detail the story does not give) in a short repair at the opening. Its foundations part also repairs error Y from today's written check, a vowel spelling written wrongly, by comparing the wrong spelling with the word wall.
