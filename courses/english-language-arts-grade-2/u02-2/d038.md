# Day 38: Building the story map

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child, and pages 4, 5 and 7 and the partner story map for each pair. Cut the sort cards. Mark today's six (group 1 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Have day 37's exit marks and the cycle 6 check sheets beside you: error I and error Y are the misses to repair. Set three stations: tiles, sort cards, a reread of The Park Cart. Key: key-sort-cards-cycle-7.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 37's exit (error I, a meaning that loses the small word), say: "This card is made up for practice. Mei tells stories at the library. Mei is the teller." Ask: "Use a small word you know to say what teller means." Children cover the ending with a finger, say tell, then say that a teller is a person who tells. Otherwise partners say the six question words to each other, one word each in turn.

#### 10 min. Reread with a partner

Review: you built a story map in unit 1, and since day 34 it has a beginning box and an ending box. Read page 6 aloud first (about 50 words), showing the picture, so every step is heard. Then pairs read pages 4, 5 and 7 turn about, a sentence each (about 140 words, so each child reads about 70), and put a finger on the sentence that tells what Amara or Dr. Lee does next (4 minutes). Pairs then say the four steps in order with First, Next, Then and At last, one step each in turn (2 minutes). Listen for accuracy before speed. Key: First, Amara looks at the mark carefully. Next, she brushes the stone. Then she carries it to Dr. Lee. At last Dr. Lee compares it with a picture card.

#### 15 min. Build the story map

Pairs have the partner story map: a wide Beginning box, four narrow boxes (First, Next, Then, At last) and a wide Ending box. They fill the Beginning and Ending boxes first (3 minutes each), saying it aloud and then writing or drawing a few words on a card strip. For the Beginning box the prompts are who, where and when, and what starts. Show the pictures for pages 1 to 3, 10 and 11 on the class copy, and leave the large wall map turned to the wall. Then pairs fill the four middle boxes from the reread, one strip a box (6 minutes). While they work, check every pair's Beginning and Ending boxes, about 20 seconds a pair (5 minutes). Key: Beginning: Amara and her class; a fossil park on a cool, bright morning; Amara finds a curved mark in a small stone. Ending: the question is answered, the stone's shape came from a shell, and Amara tells Ben. Pages 8 and 9 are where Dr. Lee explains; pairs may lay them under At last or leave them out. Do not give the words trace or mold a meaning; day 57 returns to them.

**Ask.** "What does your Ending box finish that your Beginning box started?" Expect: at the start Amara wonders what the mark is; at the end she knows it is the shape of a shell. Error A is the ending named as the first part or the reverse.

#### 10 min. Explain the two jobs

Say the frames once and point to each box: "The beginning introduces ___. The ending concludes ___." Review: introduce and conclude, from unit 1, day 17 and day 36. Then the talk routine from unit 1. Turn: each child tells a partner the two sentences, pointing at the map (2 minutes). Pair: two pairs join and each pair says its two sentences; the other pair links ("You said ___; I would add ___") or clarifies ("Which part do you mean?") (3 minutes). Then hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes), and after each one child links or asks. Use the wait signal.

**Ask.** "How do the beginning and the ending work together?" Expect: the beginning starts something that is not finished, and the ending finishes it.

#### 5 min. Compare two maps

Hold up two maps you chose while checking, one with a Beginning box written in words and one drawn. Ask: "What do both Beginning boxes hold?" Expect: Amara, the fossil park on a morning, and the curved mark she finds. If a box holds a middle step, point to the prompts and say which one it answers.

#### 10 min. Write two sentences

Each child writes on the student page two sentences, each with one detail from the map: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." (6 minutes). Partners swap, read aloud and check a capital, an end mark, a noun and a verb in each (2 minutes). Each fixes one thing (2 minutes). A child may draw and tell you, and you write the words and name yourself as the scribe on the page.

Check: each sentence begins with a capital, ends with a mark, and has a noun and a verb.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read Burt and the Bird aloud twice; pairs read it on day 36. Ask (s02-X38v2): "Tell me how the first part and the last part of this story work together." The six answer to you, about 20 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means the first part starts the help (a hurt bird in a box) and the last part finishes it (the bird hops off). Shown with help means that after one prompt: "What is wrong with the bird at the start?" Not yet seen means the ending named as the start, or a middle step (error A). Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Review: in unit 2 so far you read and spelled ar, or, er, ir and ur. Write bern, ster and birn on the board and say each. Children check each against the sound-spelling-map and the word wall, and say which spelling of /er/ belongs in each word (stir, burn, fern). This repairs error Y from the cycle 6 check (2 minutes). Then pairs read six word cards to each other, a word each in turn: car, jar, horn, her, bird, turn (3 minutes).

#### 15 min. Teach: oi, oy, ou, ow

Add four spellings to the sound-spelling-map. Say: "oi and oy both say /oi/, as in oil and boy. ou and ow both say /ou/, as in out and cow." Review: in cycle 5 (unit 1) you read ow as long o in snow, slow and show, and we said the ow in cow was for later. Today is later: ow has two sounds, so read the word, try /o/ first, then /ou/, and keep the one that makes a word that fits. Write "The cow is in the snow." and read it with the class.

Say that oy usually ends a word (boy, toy), that oi usually sits inside it (oil, point), that ou usually sits inside it (out, loud, house), and that ow ends a word (cow, how) or comes before n or l (now, owl).

Model oil: touch under o, draw a bar under oi (two letters, one sound), say /oi/ /l/, blend oil. Build it with tiles, then add b for boil. Do boy the same way (bar under oy, /b/ /oi/), then change the first letter for toy and Roy. Model out (bar under ou, /ou/ /t/), then loud and house (the final e after s is silent), and change h to m for mouse. Last, cow, how and now, changing the first letter, and owl.

Then two mapped words, never guessed from the first letter or the picture. Their: th, eir; star eir, which says /air/ here. People: p, eo, p, le; star eo, which says /ee/ here, and le, which says /ul/. Put both on the word wall. Read don't and doesn't as whole words: the mark in the middle is an apostrophe, and it stands in for a letter that is left out. Today they are read, not spelled, and not scored.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Today only the first two lines are used, the grade 1 words and the cycle 6 words (shelf, class, cloth, lunch, thank, chest; car, park, corn, horn, her, bird, turn, fern), saying the vowel sound in each. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build boil, toy, house and now, then change one letter. Sort: the twelve cycle 7 cards under oi / oy and ou / ow, saying each word aloud. Reread: pairs read The Park Cart twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

Check: each child in a station reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Where does oy come in a word?" Expect: at the end, as in boy and toy. Children point to oy on the sound-spelling-map.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a sheet into the map and write the strips and the twelve sort words on torn paper. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of pages 4, 5 and 7: read them aloud and let pairs find the line on the class copy.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed story: tell the story of Amara and the stone from memory. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The map, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Two jobs.** Tell a story you both know. Your child says what its beginning introduces and what its ending concludes, with the frames "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___."
  **Say.** "How do the beginning and the ending work together?" Expect: the beginning starts something and the ending finishes it.
- **10 min. Read.** Show oil, boy, out and cow on cards. Your child reads each and says the vowel sound.
- **10 min. Spell.** Say boil, toy and house. Your child writes each and checks the vowel spelling on the cards.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X38v2]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: fill only the Beginning and Ending boxes and read two of the four middle steps; keep the exit and the teaching. Longer: a pair writes a new Ending card for a story they know and another pair says which Beginning it finishes.

### Words and supports

Beginning, ending, introduces, concludes, story map, oi, oy, ou, ow, their, people. Frames: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 39 teaches how to answer a question with two choices by going back to the story for the line, reads the cycle 7 reader for the first time and hears the next six on the oral card. Today's exit (error A, the ending named as the start of the search) is repaired at day 39's opening.
