# Section 2.2 opener: the beginning, the ending and a first check (days 38 to 42)

## What the section does

Days 38 and 39 finish the work on *The Mark in the Stone*. Children build a story map with a beginning, four middle steps and an ending, and explain how the ending finishes what the beginning started. They also learn to answer a choice question by going back to the text: find the page, put a finger on the line, and say "I know because the text says ___". Days 40 and 41 are check days: a short invented story is read aloud twice with a printed copy, and children explain what the beginning introduces and the ending concludes, ask a question of their own, and answer six questions. Day 42 is a respond day: you return the check with the marking, teach four small groups by recorded letter, and set a fresh retry on a new story card.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. On days 38 and 39 the topic block holds a repair, the work above and a five-minute exit. On days 40 and 41 the topic block is the check, with no help and no reteaching, and the foundations block continues the cycle 7 work: oi, oy, ou and ow, with the reader *Coins for a Toy*. Day 42 ends with a 15-minute written check and the rotation. You hear the day's rotating six on exit days and every child on the check days.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The beginning introduces; the ending concludes.** Use the frames "The beginning introduces ___" and "The ending concludes ___".
- **A choice answer needs a line.** No answer is passable without the line from the text.
- **A whole sentence beats a fragment.** "She needs the box because it blocks the wind" is a sentence; "Because it blocks the wind" is half of one.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** the ending named as the opening, or the reverse. Lay the beginning and ending cards on the map and say each job.
- **B:** a detail that contradicts the text. Reread the line, cover the choice and find the words that answer the question.
- **AD:** an answer that is a fragment. Say the whole sentence aloud first, then write it with a capital and an end mark.
- **Y, X:** a vowel team misread, or a word guessed from the picture. Hold the oi, oy, ou and ow cards side by side and blend.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Days 40 and 41 are marked after class on the record sheet; the strip and the six-box sheet are collected as evidence. Feedback comes first, and the retry on day 42 comes before any final mark.
