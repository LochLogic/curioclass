# Day 78: How do plants and pollinators help each other?

## Teacher page

### Before class

After day 77, use day 74's group moves and the latest notes to set today's six and the stations. Mark today's six on the unit 3 record sheet. Set the 01-c teacher copy and the cycle 13 oral card by your table. Lay the nine flower-parts cards in an envelope for each pair. Draw a sixth box, empty, on the syllable-types chart. Hang the three picture cards and the question strip, covered. Keys: key-flower-parts-picture-cards-day-78.md, key-sort-cards-cycle-13.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook

Show the flower, the bee on it and the bowl of fruit. Ask: "Who helps a plant make the fruit we eat?" Each child tells a partner a guess (2 minutes). Take three guesses and do not mark any as right (2 minutes). Say: "The article I read next is true. When a practice card is made up, I will say so." Any child may pass.

#### 7 min. Words before reading

Say each word, the class says it back, and you show it once, about 45 seconds a word. New: pollen (a tiny yellow powder, picture card), pollination (moving pollen from a flower to another flower of the same kind), nectar (sweet juice inside a flower), pollinator (an animal that moves pollen), transfer (move from one place to another: slide a pencil across the desk), cling (stick on tight: fingers on a sleeve), carry (hold and take along). Review: connect (join two ideas, as you did with the connection arrow in kindergarten and grade 1) and sequence (the order things happen, from grade 2 unit 2). One word, one sense: land means come down onto, plant means a living thing, visit means a short stay by a creature.

#### 10 min. Read aloud: Pollen on the Move

Read article 01-a straight through (about 330 words, 3 minutes), then again with the pictures, stopping at each numbered step for children to show it with their hands (5 minutes). Say aloud: "Pollination moves pollen. Seeds travelling is a different thing, and science will meet it later." Close with the last paragraph: the bee gets food and the flower gets help (2 minutes). The bee does not mean to help the flower; she comes for food and some pollen rubs off. No bee is brought in; the pictures are the close look.

#### 4 min. Retrieval (grade 1)

Review: the connection arrow, a fact, an arrow and so or because, which you learned in kindergarten and used in grade 1. Lay two invented cards, "It rained" and "Mia wore boots", with an arrow card between them and read: "Mia wore boots, so it rained." Pairs show thumbs for a right link or a wrong link, then fix it by swapping the two cards and reading it again together: "It rained, so Mia wore boots." Unscored. A pair that cannot fix it gets the two-card model on day 79.

#### 15 min. Bridge: plant facts from the text

Model on a flower picture, for 5 minutes, adding four labels from 01-a: petal, anther (makes pollen on a thin stalk), stigma (sticky top at the center) and nectar. Then each pair sorts the nine picture cards into three piles headed "makes or holds pollen", "gets pollen" and "carries pollen" (8 minutes), checking the heading before each card. Three children each show one card from a different pile and say why it goes there (2 minutes, about 40 seconds each). Every fact comes from 01-a.

If many put the bee in "gets pollen": point to the bee's fuzz in the article picture and say "pollen sticks to it and she carries it along."

#### 5 min. The compelling question

Uncover the strip: "How do plants and pollinators help each other?" Read it aloud and say it stays on the wall until day 118. Read the two frames on the student page aloud. Each child draws one thing the bee gets and one thing the flower gets. These first ideas are kept in the folder unscored for day 118.

#### 9 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Ask: "What does the bee get from the flower?" Each child draws or says a first answer, turns, and tells a partner with "The bee gets ___." (1 minute). The partner links with "You said ___; I would add ___" and may ask "Which part do you mean?" (1 minute). Hear the six, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Then ask: "What does the flower get from the bee?" Pairs talk (2 minutes). Expect: nectar or food for the bee; some pollen moved to another flower for the flower. If a child says seeds, say: "That is a real thing; today we follow pollen."

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This is from another true article, about butterflies." Read paragraph 1 of 01-c twice, saying probes means pokes in to look, then ask the one ask. Every child tells a partner; you stand by each of the six for about 10 seconds and mark: shown, shown with help (after "What does it say?") or not yet seen. Then read paragraph 4 of 01-c twice and ask its one ask the same way. [[s03-X78v2]] [[s03-X78bv2]]

#### 5 min. Retrieval (foundations)

Review: closed syllables from grade 1 and the syllable types chart from unit 1. Write sunshine and picnic. Pairs divide and read each, chopping between the two middle consonants (4 minutes). Then tell the class how the oral card works: the day's six read six words and a phrase to you, two minutes each, while the rest work at stations (1 minute).

#### 15 min. Teach: consonant-le

Say: "Words that end in a consonant plus le have one more kind of last syllable." Point to the sixth box and write: consonant-le. Model handle. Map /h/ /a/ /n/, then the last syllable dle (the e is silent), blend han-dle and spell it with tiles (4 minutes). Do giggle (gig-gle) and waddle (wad-dle) the same way; the first syllable is closed and short (4 minutes). Then table: the first syllable is open and says its name, ta-ble (3 minutes). Pairs build little and puddle on tiles and say the last syllable (2 minutes). Star the odd part of the two tricky words, one (o-n-e says /w/ /u/ /n/) and guess (the u is silent), and read them from the letters, never from a picture or the first letter (2 minutes; starring is review from unit 2, day 33).

If many read table with a short a or little with a long i (error T), remap the first syllable: open says its name, closed is short.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 2, day 59: three stations, tiles, sort and reread, ten children each. Round 1 is 13 minutes: the day's six sit at your table for the cycle 13 oral card, two minutes each (12 of your minutes), and the other 24 work at the stations, eight to a station. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes each: all stations fill to ten, and the six take the two stations they have not had. Tiles: build handle, giggle, table and puddle, and say each last syllable. Sort: word cards, handle, giggle, waddle, table in one pile and picnic, sunshine, napkin, rabbit in the other, "ends in consonant plus le" or not. Reread: the phrase cards a little table, a wet puddle, the handle, a giggle; partners read each from the letters, then check the meaning. Oral card today uses grade 1 and unit 1 and 2 words and the cycle words taught above, nothing else. This is group 1 of five; the card marks are reads each word by its syllables, does not guess from the picture, reads the phrase smoothly on the second try. Mark the heard column. If a child guesses a word from the picture (error S), cover the picture, map, blend, then check the meaning.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which letters make the last syllable of handle?" Children tell a partner, then say it together: d, l, e.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the nine cards on torn paper. No flower picture: draw one on the board. Pointing and drawing are answers.

### At home

**Swap.** No picture cards: draw a flower, a bee and a bowl of fruit. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The sorting piles and the stations.

- **10 min. Listen.** Read Pollen on the Move aloud with your child, then ask what the bee gets.
  **Say.** "What does the bee get from the flower?" Expect: nectar, or food.
- **5 min. Fix the link.** Write "It rained" and "Mia wore boots" on two scraps. Read "Mia wore boots, so it rained", then swap the scraps so it reads right.
- **10 min. Words.** Say han-dle, ta-ble and lit-tle in parts. Your child writes each from the parts.
- **5 min. Draw.** One thing the bee gets and one thing the flower gets.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X78v2]] [[s03-X78bv2]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the read-aloud, the question and drawing, the exit, the consonant-le teaching and the oral card; cut the second pile share and the phrase-card station. Longer: a child says what would happen to the second flower if the bee never came.

### Words and supports

Pollen, pollination, nectar, pollinator, transfer, cling, carry; stigma and anther are named on the flower. Frames: "The bee gets ___." "The flower gets ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer, and a child may pass in any talk.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children can join two things with so or because. If many said two statements with no joining word (error B) or ran the link backwards (error C), day 79 opens with the arrow card laid between the two cards and the link read aloud. If many did not know the word pollinator, say it again in day 79's opening. In foundations, children who read table or little with the wrong vowel (error T), or read one or guess from the letters wrongly (error AM), get a five-minute remap on day 79. Day 79 builds the step chain: read a pollination article and explain a chain of three to five steps.
