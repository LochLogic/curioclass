# Exit cards, days 78 to 81

Days 78 to 80 use paragraphs of The Secret World of Pollination (g2-u3-01), read from the teacher copy or printed below; they are true, so say nothing about practice. Day 81's card is invented: say aloud "This card is made up for practice" and print "invented for practice" in small letters at its foot. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading. Answers and wrong answers are in items.csv. Where a day has more than one row, ask them one after another, one ask each.

- **Day 78 (s03-X78v2).** Read aloud twice article 01-c, paragraph 1 (Butterflies visit in the daytime); say first that probes means pokes in to look. Say: "Join what the butterfly does and why, with so or because."
- **Day 78 (s03-X78bv2).** Read aloud twice article 01-c, paragraph 4 (A big difference). Say: "Say the word for an animal like the butterfly that moves pollen between plants."
- **Day 79 (s03-X79v2).** Read aloud twice article 01-c, paragraph 3 (How pollen moves). Say: "Say the steps in order with First, Then, Next, Finally."
- **Day 79 (s03-X79bv2).** Same paragraph, read again. Say: "Why can the last step happen? Say because."
- **Day 79 (s03-X79cv2).** The same paragraph, printed large for every child so each can point to a line (print below). Say: "Point to the sentence that says where some pollen comes off."
- **Day 80 (s03-X80v2).** Give the child the printed paragraph, article 01-d, paragraph 1 (print below). Say: "Read it aloud." Record alone, with help, or not yet seen.
- **Day 80 (s03-X80bv2).** Same paragraph, turned over. Say: "Why do bats visit these flowers at night?"
- **Day 81 (s03-X81).** This card is invented for practice. Show a flower picture with three numbered arrows. Read aloud twice: "A bumblebee reaches into a bell-shaped flower. Pollen sticks to her. Then she flies to a second bell-shaped flower. Pollen touches its sticky top." Say: "Point to the arrow that matches Then she flies to a second bell-shaped flower."
- **Day 81 (s03-X81b).** Same card and picture. Say: "Say what moves along that arrow."

## Print: day 79, from article 01-c

How pollen moves. A butterfly does not look for pollen. It moves pollen by accident. A little pollen sticks to its body while it sips nectar or rests on the flower. Then the butterfly flies to the next flower, and some pollen comes off there.

## Print: day 80, from article 01-d

Some flowers open after dark. Bats that drink nectar feed at night. The flowers they visit are often large, pale or white, and strong smelling. These flowers hold plenty of watery nectar.
