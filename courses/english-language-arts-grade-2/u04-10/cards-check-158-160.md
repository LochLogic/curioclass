# Days 158 to 160 check cards (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Say each time: "These cards are made up for practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of every card. Keep every card face down; collect each one after each child. Day 158 uses set A (children 1 to 15 of the hearing order), day 159 uses set B (children 16 to 30). Print each word card as five large words in a row. Nothing here is read, sorted or spelled in any lesson before day 158.

**Which cards a child reads.** A literary card: the story for an odd place in the order, the poem for an even one. An informational card: the history article when the place divides by three with 1 left over, the science article with 2 left over, the steps when it divides evenly. So across the class every text is read, and the portfolio holds a story or poem and an article or steps for each child.

## Texts, read silently by each child first, then an excerpt aloud

**Story, "Otto and the Slow Clock".** "The clock in Room 9 was slow. Otto noticed it before lunch. His stomach said it was time to eat, but the hands said it was not. Otto frowned. Then he asked his teacher to check. She smiled and set the clock right. "Good eyes, Otto," she said. Otto felt proud as the class lined up."

**Poem, "Recess Bell".** "The bell rings out, the doors swing wide, / We race across the field outside. / We jump and run, we spin and slide, / Until the bell calls us inside."

**History article, "Slates and Chalk Long Ago".** "Long ago, many children wrote on slates. A slate is a flat piece of dark stone in a wooden frame. Children used chalk to write and a cloth to wipe the slate clean. Paper cost a lot then. Today children write with pencils and pens, but some classrooms keep a slate on a shelf."

**Science article, "Where a Puddle Goes".** "After a rain, a puddle sits on the ground. The sun warms the water, and the water turns to vapor. Vapor is water that floats in the air. It is too small to see. Soon the puddle gets smaller, and then it is gone."

**Steps, "How to Make a Paper Fan".** "You need one sheet of paper. Step 1: Fold the paper in thin folds, back and forth, like a stair. Step 2: Press the folds flat. Step 3: Pinch one end tight. Step 4: Let the other end spread open. Now fan yourself."

The excerpt read aloud and reread, and the sentence for the sense check, for each informational card:
- History: the first three sentences; sense check "A slate is a flat piece of dark stone in a wooden frame."
- Science: the first three sentences; sense check "The sun warms the water, and the water turns to vapor."
- Steps: "You need one sheet of paper" and Steps 1 and 2; sense check "Fold the paper in thin folds, back and forth, like a stair."

## Day 158, set A

- Long and short vowels: clamp, drake, plank, flute, prize.
- Additional vowel teams: sprawl, thaw, gauze, jaunt, spawn.
- Two-syllable words with a long vowel: tadpole, inflate, remote, stampede, decade.
- Prefixes: detach, disband, inform, imprint, inland.
- Endings: hurries, fairies, worries, trimmed, skidding.
- Patterns that do not always sound the same: threat, breast, flood, blood, tough.
- Words with an unusual part: island, honest, hour, calf, chef.

**Written station, all thirty children.** Words to dictate, twice each: pledge, budge, glitch, thatch, dredge, crease. Fix-it strip: "A small mouse hid an akorn in the gravle by the fence." (the two wrong spellings are akorn and gravle).

## Day 159, set B

- Long and short vowels: blimp, stake, robe, frisk, drive.
- Additional vowel teams: squawk, bawl, lawful, flawed, awning.
- Two-syllable words with a long vowel: inhale, decline, deny, decay, debate.
- Prefixes: disgust, discount, disobey, inject, inspect.
- Endings: patted, sobbing, spies, gloomy, lumpy.
- Patterns that do not always sound the same: pleasant, treasure, hoof, hood, dough.
- Words with an unusual part: laughter, guy, choir, gauge, sword.

**Narrative prompt card, a new school situation.** "The lights go out in your classroom for a few minutes. Write what happens, with what you do, what you think and feel, and how it ends."
