# Day 98: Study a model paragraph and plan your own

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write the model on a chart; cut six strips and a seventh, "Bats sleep in caves." Cut the butterfly strips: (1) Butterflies visit flowers by day. (2) Flowers with a flat top give it a place to perch. (3) A little pollen comes off at the next flower. (4) So butterflies help flowers too. Choices: To perch is to sit and rest on something. Many flowers are red. A butterfly has two feelers. Mark today's six.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Practice only, no marks. This follows day 97's exit (item s03-X97). If many asked no question (error M), pairs read a slip and point to the starter scrap (What, Where and Why, each written on a scrap) whose question the slip answers, and say that first word aloud. Then each child writes the starter word at the head of the slip. No one answers a question that was not asked.

#### 3 min. Say the plan

Say: "Today we study how a good paragraph is built. It has a topic sentence that says what it is about, facts, a word explained, and an ending. On days 99 to 101 you will write your own from sources you have not seen, so today we only study a model and make a plan." The paragraph is made up for practice from what scientists know.

#### 10 min. Hear and read the model

Read the chart, Bats Help Flowers at Night, aloud twice (2 minutes): "Bats help flowers at night. Some bats drink nectar, a sweet juice inside a flower. Their long noses and tongues reach deep inside. Pollen sticks to the bat while it feeds. Some pollen brushes onto the next flower. So bats help flowers while they eat." Pairs reread it with a finger, one sentence each, twice through (4 minutes, about 45 words). Ask: "Which words did you stumble on?" and map any such word together (2 minutes). Children say in a few words what the paragraph is about to a partner (2 minutes).

#### 12 min. Analyze the model

Pairs lay the seven strips in the order of the paragraph (4 minutes). They cross out the strip that does not belong, "Bats sleep in caves.", and say why to a partner: it does not tell how bats help flowers (2 minutes). Then you set a label beside each strip on the board: topic sentence, definition, fact, fact, fact, ending. Say aloud what the second strip does: it tells what nectar is, so the next sentence about reaching deep inside makes sense (2 minutes). Say that the last strip ends the paragraph and says why the facts matter together. Now pairs lay the butterfly strips and choose which one of the three choice strips fills the gap after strip 1, so the next sentence makes sense (4 minutes). Walk and hear six pairs, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes).

#### 12 min. Shared writing from notes

Show the day 94 hummingbird slips on the board. Model turning them into four parts on the chart as you think aloud: a topic sentence, two facts, a word to explain (bill) and an ending. Write: "Hummingbirds help red flowers. A bill is a bird's beak. A hummingbird pushes its long, thin bill into a flower. Pollen sticks to its face. Some pollen touches the next flower. So hummingbirds help flowers while they feed." Children offer the next word each time and you ask: "Which note goes here?" (6 minutes). Ask the class to count the parts on the chart and say which line is which, together (3 minutes). Children copy none of it. Say that their own paragraphs come from new sources and are not copies of any model (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Ask: "Which fact in the model would you most like to check in a second source, and why?" Children say "I would check ___ because ___" to a partner (2 minutes). The partner links with "You said ___; I think ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" (2 minutes). Hear the six, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Any child may pass. Use the last 2 minutes to say the question and the frames before and thank each speaker after.

#### 8 min. Plan page

Each child fills the plan page, the student page, for the pair of articles the group chose on day 95: a topic idea, two notes, a word to define and an ending idea (4 minutes). Words only, no sentences. The plan stays with the child and is not scored. Then tell the class that the beetle card is made up for practice. Read it aloud twice (1 minute): [[s03-X98]]. Children tell a partner what they would point to, then the six answer aloud, two on each ask (1 minute). Ask [[s03-X98b]] and [[s03-X98c]] the same way (2 minutes). Mark each of the six: shown, shown with help (after "What is a beetle?" or "What is the last thing the card says?") or not yet seen. Tag error AF for a definition read as one more fact, G for no ending named and F for no topic sentence found.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Practice only. This routes the cycle 16 check (item s03-A35). Write the commonest miss from nudge or bulge beside the right word and read it aloud. For a missing ending, such as nuj (error Y), write the dge whole after the short vowel and say it as one sound. For bulj or bulg, write ge after the consonant l. For swapped letters (error AN), say the letters of the ending in order while you point. Children repair the word on scrap paper.

#### 15 min. Teach: -ch, -tch and -er, -est

Retrieve -dge and -ge: children write badge and huge and say which ending each has (2 minutes). Teach -tch after a short vowel: write catch, match, pitch, hatch and itch, map each sound, say the word and underline tch (4 minutes). Teach -ch after a consonant or a long vowel: lunch, bench and teach, and say why there is no t (3 minutes). Review: -er and -est on a known base (grade 1, unit 2, day 61). Write fast, faster and fastest. Say the base word first, then add the ending and say what it adds: -er compares two things and -est names the top of three or more. Do the same for slow, slower, slowest and long, longer, longest (4 minutes). Dictate "I can catch it" and children write it and check catch on the word wall (2 minutes).

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: today's six (group 1 of five in cycle 17) read the oral card at your table, two minutes each including the swap (12 of your minutes); the other 24 are at stations, eight each. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes each: the six join, ten to a station; groups rotate. Tiles: build catch, pitch, lunch and longest. Sort: eight word slips, patch, ditch, fetch, witch, bunch, pinch, beach and reach, by -tch or -ch. Reread: A Package at the Bridge and The Frog on the Bridge. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which ending has the t, in match or in lunch?" Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the model by hand, tear paper into strips and write the plan page on paper. No pairs list: work with one pair at a time while the others reread. A child who cannot write says the plan and you write it, recorded as help. A tag may be a drawn symbol. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The oral card and the stations.

- **5 min. Read.** Read your child the school's model, Bats Help Flowers at Night, twice if you have it.
- **10 min. Plan.** Your child says what a paragraph about a pollinator would be about, one note and one word to explain.
  **Say.** "What is the last sentence for?" Expect: it says why the facts matter together.
- **10 min. Words.** Say catch and lunch. Your child writes each and says which has the t.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X98]] [[s03-X98b]] [[s03-X98c]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the second stations round and the shared writing's counting; keep the strips, the plan page, the exit and the teaching. Longer: a pair writes its own gap strip for the butterfly paragraph.

### Words and supports

Paragraph, topic sentence, fact, definition, ending, strip, plan, model. Frames: "I would check ___ because ___." "You said ___; I think ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may point to the strip instead of saying it.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children can find a definition and say what it is for, and what an ending does. If many read the definition as one more fact (error AF), day 102 corrects it in 3 minutes: point to the definition, say the word it explains and say what the next fact does. Day 99 is the first check: new sources, a topic sentence and facts.
