# Day 96: Find the part of a picture that shows a fact

## Teacher page

### Before class

Keep the reserved photograph page face down until the exit; show nobody. Put the mining bee photograph page and the 01-a diagram page on each pair's desk. On ten word slips write: badge, edge, bridge, dodge, smudge, lodge, huge, large, range, strange. Mark today's six (group 4 of five in cycle 16) on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Practice only, no marks. This follows day 95's slips. Run at most two branches, the two most missed. If many copied a sentence (error D), cover the article and say the fact in four words. If many wrote a true fact that does not answer the question (error E), hold up the question card and ask whether the slip answers it. If a note does not say what its line says (error C), point to the line and compare the note word by word. If a slip has the wrong card or paragraph named (error AE), the child touches the line and reads the tag from it. Children repair one slip on scrap paper.

#### 3 min. Say the plan

Say: "A photograph is a kind of evidence. It shows what was there at one moment. Today we look at pictures with our notes beside them and find the part that shows each fact." Review: a caption tells what a picture shows (grade 2, unit 1, day 22). Nobody has to touch an animal: every picture is on paper.

#### 10 min. Model with the mining bee

Show the mining bee photograph page and read the caption aloud: "A female mining bee collects pollen from willow flowers." Children look for one minute and say one thing they see, to a partner (2 minutes). Then think aloud with one slip, "pollen sticks to the bee" (tag: 01-b): "I look for the part of the picture that shows it. Here is yellow on her body." Put a dot there with a pencil (2 minutes). Then take a second slip, "she flies to the next flower" (tag: 01-b): "I look for a part that shows it. There is one bee on one flower. The picture shows one moment, so there is nothing to mark. This fact stays with its article tag." Say that a photograph shows what is there, and that what happened next needs words (3 minutes). Children say which slip had a part to mark and which did not (3 minutes).

#### 14 min. Pairs check their notes

Each pair takes two of its own slips and hunts for a part to mark on the mining bee page or the 01-a diagram (the diagram has arrows and labels). Where a part shows the fact, the pair puts a dot on it and writes photo, caption or diagram in the corner of the slip. Where nothing shows it, the pair leaves the slip with its article tag and says why aloud: "The picture does not show ___." Nobody writes a new slip yet. Walk and hear six pairs, about 1 minute each (6 minutes with the moves). Mark each: shown, shown with help (after "Where on the picture?") or not yet seen. Tag error C or AE if a dot lands on a part that shows something else.

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Ask: "What does the photograph show that the words do not?" Children say "The picture shows ___ that the words ___" to a partner (2 minutes). The partner links with "You said ___; I think ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" (2 minutes). Hear the six, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Any child may pass. Use the other 2 minutes to say the question and the frames before and to thank each speaker after.

#### 12 min. Fix or add a note

Each child writes one new slip of about four words that the picture or caption shows, and tags it photo, caption or diagram (4 minutes). The child gives the slip to a partner, who puts a finger on the part that shows it (3 minutes). Three children read a slip aloud and say where the part is (3 minutes). A child who cannot write says the slip, and you or a groupmate write it, recorded as help (2 minutes to collect the slips). Slips go on the joint chart sheet.

#### 6 min. Exit

Turn over the reserved photograph page and show it once. Read its caption aloud and show the note. Tell the class the note is made up for practice. Children each say every answer to a partner (1 minute). Then ask the six aloud, one ask at a time, two of the six on each ask (about 20 seconds each, 2 minutes): [[s03-X96]] [[s03-X96b]] [[s03-X96c]]. Put the page away. Note hands for the rest. Mark each of the six: shown, shown with help (after "What does the caption say?") or not yet seen. Tag error C for keeping the note.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Practice only. Write yesterday's commonest miss beside the right word and read it aloud. For badge read as pin from the picture (error S), cover the picture, map each letter, blend, then check the meaning. For huge with a short u (error T), remap the vowel as long before ge. For baj or bridg (error Y or AN), write the dge whole and say its letters in order. For ther (error AM), star the odd part of their and write it twice.

#### 15 min. Fresh passage and dictation

Read the title, The Frog on the Bridge, aloud. Children read the passage in pairs: you read the first sentence and map dodges aloud (map each sound, blend, then say the word); then each partner reads the whole passage once (5 minutes, about 36 words each). Ask: "What did Jack do when the frog jumped?" Children answer to a partner and then the class (3 minutes). That checks the meaning after decoding; nobody guesses from a picture. Then dictate lodge and strange, and the sentence "We hop on their bridge" (7 minutes: two words at 1 minute each, the sentence at 2 minutes, 3 minutes to check against the word wall and the sound-spelling-map and fix with a second color). Collect the papers; they are unscored.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: today's six read the oral card at your table, two minutes each including the swap (12 of your minutes); the other 24 are at stations, eight each. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes each: the six join, ten to a station; groups rotate. Tiles: build smudge, lodge, range and huge. Sort: the ten word slips, first by ending (-dge or -ge), then by the sound before the ending. Reread: A Package at the Bridge and The Frog on the Bridge. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which letters in their do not sound the way you expect?" Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No printer: describe the photograph aloud, draw it on the board, copy the caption by hand and tear paper into slips. No pairs list: work with one pair at a time while the others reread. A child who cannot write says the slip and you write it, recorded as help. A tag may be a drawn symbol. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The oral card and the stations.

- **5 min. Picture.** Show your child any photo with a caption, such as one in a magazine. Your child points to one thing the picture shows.
- **10 min. Slip.** Your child says one fact about a pollinator and finds a part of a picture that shows it.
  **Say.** "Where does the picture show that?" Expect: a finger on the part, or "it does not show that".
- **10 min. Words.** Say lodge and strange. Your child writes each and says a sentence with each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X96]] [[s03-X96b]] [[s03-X96c]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the second stations round and the share of three slips; keep the hunt for parts, the exit and the dictation. Longer: a pair finds a second picture that shows the same fact and says which one shows it better.

### Words and supports

Photograph, caption, diagram, slip, tag, mark, part, evidence, shows. Frames: "The picture shows ___ that the words ___." "The picture does not show ___." "You said ___; I think ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may point to the part of the picture instead of saying it.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children keep a note that a photograph and caption do not support (error C). If many did, day 97 opens with a 5-minute repair: point to what the caption says and what the photograph shows, then change the note. Day 97 turns to talk: a partner asks a question and the child answers from the slips, and the cycle 16 written check closes the foundations block.
