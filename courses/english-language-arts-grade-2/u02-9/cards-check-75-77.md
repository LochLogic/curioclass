# Days 75 to 77 check cards (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Say each time: "These cards are made up for practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of every card. Keep every card face down; collect each one after each child. Day 75 uses set A (children 1 to 15 of the hearing order), day 76 uses set B (children 16 to 30). Print each word card as five large words in a row. Nothing here is read, sorted or spelled in any lesson before day 75.

## Day 75, set A

**Article, "Boots by the Door".** "Our class has a boot spot. It is a mat by the door. On rainy days, each child sets wet boots on the mat. The mat catches the drips, so the floor stays dry. Then we put on dry shoes and walk to our seats. A dry floor is safe to walk on. The boot spot helps all of us." The sentence for the sense check: "The mat catches the drips, so the floor stays dry." The reread: the last three sentences.

**Word cards.**
- Long and short vowels: trot, snip, spine, scrape, flake.
- Vowel teams: waist, scowl, brook, delight, groan.
- Endings: mopped, hugging, stirring, dotted, chased.
- Patterns that do not always sound the same: blind, scold, bread, thread, growl.
- Words with an unusual part: love, shoe, sugar, ocean, pear.

**Written station, all thirty children.** Words to dictate, twice each: sway, praise, loan, moist, shrimp, clasp. Fix-it strip: "The colt ran through the medow. It had a sore cheak." (the two wrong spellings are medow and cheak).

## Day 76, set B

**Article, "A Hat Rack for Our Class".** "Our class needed a place for coats and hats. First we made a list of what we needed. The hooks had to be low enough for us to reach. They had to hold a wet coat, too. Then a helper put up a rack by the window. Now each child hangs a coat on a hook. The room looks neat, and no coat is left on the floor." The sentence for the sense check: "They had to hold a wet coat, too." The reread: the last three sentences.

**Word cards.**
- Long and short vowels: stride, shrug, blaze, stump, pave.
- Vowel teams: stray, seam, greed, throat, stew.
- Endings: napped, skimmed, gripped, freed, bakes.
- Patterns that do not always sound the same: rind, sweat, wealth, dread, frown.
- Words with an unusual part: shove, couple, steak, cousin, ski.

## Day 76 class station, read aloud twice each, then printed for the answers

**Story, "Cora and the Squeaky Door".** "The art room door squeaked. Every time it opened, the class jumped. Cora sat by the door, and the noise made her ears ache. At lunch, Cora asked the helper for oil. The helper rubbed oil on the hinge, and the squeak was gone. Back in the art room, Cora smiled and picked up her brush."

**Article, "Three Bells".** "Our school rings a bell three times each day. The first bell rings at eight and tells us to come inside. The second bell rings at noon and tells us it is time for lunch. The last bell rings at three and tells us school is over. Bells help everyone know the time."

**Message, read aloud only, never printed.** "Dear families, our class will walk to the pond on Friday. Please pack lunch in a paper bag, so it is easy to carry. Please wear boots, because the path is wet. We leave at nine. Thank you, Room 12."

## Day 77 retry cards (new, kept unseen until the retry)

- Reading card: "Moss and Uri walked to the creek. A loud croak came from the reeds. They tiptoed closer and saw a green frog."
- Vowel words: strike, spoil, mouth, howl, sneeze.
- Endings: snapped, jogging, tiptoed, bobbing, dotting.
- Unusual words: bolt, pint, comb, yolk, salt.
- Tile words, said once each: cloak, stoop, croon.
- Short note, read aloud twice: "Remy found the class rabbit under the slide at recess. The rabbit was eating a clover leaf."
