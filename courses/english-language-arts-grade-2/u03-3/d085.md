# Day 85: Words that fit real things, and words that are nearly alike

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the chorus wall strip; keep the step chain on the wall. Set out each pair's cards and strips and each group's eight picture cards. Write the twelve -le cards: ble (marble, humble, noble), dle (noodle, handle, saddle), kle (wrinkle, ankle, tickle), zle (nozzle, dazzle, puzzle). Mark today's six (group 3 of five) on the record sheet. The exit is spoken.

### The day

#### 5 min. Retrieval miss

Practice only, no marks. This follows day 84's retry (s03-X84v2), where the commonest miss (error A) was pollen and seeds mixed up. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read it twice: "A bee lands on a red flower. Pollen sticks to her legs. She flies to a pink flower, and some pollen rubs off." Ask: "Say why the pink flower gets pollen, with so." Partners say it (1 minute), then hear the six, about 10 seconds each. Repair: the bee carries pollen, not seeds, and one step makes the next happen: "Pollen sticks, so she carries it." Say aloud: "Today we find out how words fit real things, and how words that are nearly alike are not the same."

#### 8 min. Hear a poem

Review: the refrain is the line a poem repeats (grade 2, unit 1, day 21). Read 03-a, Land, Cling, Carry, aloud twice, showing the pictures on the second reading (2 minutes). Children only listen; they do not read the stanzas. After the second reading say: "Tarry means to stay and waste time. The poem says do not tarry: take the pollen and go." Partners tell each other one visitor and what it does, in a few words (2 minutes). Then children echo the refrain from the wall strip, two lines at a time, twice (2 minutes). Close with the whole class saying it once, softly (1 minute).

#### 12 min. Words and real uses (L.2.5a)

Four words from the poem and from Pollen on the Move: fuzzy, sticky, cling and tremble. Cling is review: pollen clings to a bee (grade 2, unit 3, day 78). Fuzzy, sticky and tremble are new. For each word, say it, children echo it and act it (fuzzy: stroke the air; sticky: fingers stuck together; cling: hold your own arm tight; tremble: shake your hands), 1 minute a word (4 minutes). Then say one real use and the class echoes it with the action: "A peach is fuzzy, because it has soft hairs." "Tape is sticky, because it holds onto things." "A wet leaf can cling to a boot, because it will not let go." "A leaf can tremble in the wind, because the wind shakes it." You do the saying; children do not make up uses today (2 minutes).

Groups of five sort the eight real-use picture cards under the four word cards, and one child in each group says the word and points to the card as it goes down (4 minutes). Then judge: say "A fuzzy ice cube," and children show thumbs down; say "A fuzzy blanket," thumbs up. One more, then ask: "Which picture card shows a fuzzy thing?" Children point to a card (2 minutes). Nobody is asked to say why today; the reason is what the class hears from you.

#### 12 min. Near words (L.2.5b)

Review: words that are nearly alike but not the same (grade 1, unit 1, day 5, tap, knock and bang). Pairs first lay the near-word cards look, peek and stare in order from least to most and touch each as they say it (3 minutes; the second set, tiny, small and big, stays in the box). Then build the word ladder on the wall with three towel pictures: damp, wet, soaked, from least to most. Say each rung and children touch it and say it back (2 minutes). Hand each pair three scene strips and read each aloud: "A cloth has a few drops of water on it." "A sponge has water you can squeeze out." "A towel fell in the bath, and water drips from all of it." Pairs lay each strip on the rung it fits and say the rung's word (4 minutes). Check one strip aloud with the class.

Then sip and gulp (3 minutes). Show the two picture cards, a small drink and a big fast drink. Say: "A sip is a small drink. A gulp is a big fast one." Read two lines: "Ana took a tiny taste of the cold water." "Ben was so thirsty he drank the whole glass without stopping." Children hold up the sip card or the gulp card for each line. The stroll and dash pair and the cold words are not used today.

#### 8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Ask: "What does the word hover show you about the hummingbird?" Children say "The word hover shows me ___" to a partner (2 minutes). The partner links with "You said ___; I think ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" (2 minutes). Hear the six, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 5 min. Write

Each child picks one of the four words, finds it on the wall, and copies it onto the student page under a quick picture of one card from their group's sort. Write a few words only. Teacher scribes for a child who asks.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "These are made up for practice." No printed card. Ask one thing at a time, and partners whisper each answer before you stand by the six, about 45 seconds a child for all five items, swap included (4 minutes 30 seconds). Items [[s03-X85]] and [[s03-X85b]] (same thing, asked one after another), [[s03-X85c]], [[s03-X85d]] and [[s03-X85e]]. Mark each: shown, shown with help (after "What do you see in your head?") or not yet seen. Tag error P for a word repeated with no place, Q for two near words said to be the same.

#### 5 min. Choral reread

Read the refrain from the wall strip with the poem page open: all together, then one side of the room and the other, then a whisper (5 minutes). No new words.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Write yesterday's commonest miss (error S, T or Y) beside the right word and read it aloud. Use the oral card or The Stable Gate for the word, and point at the letters as you say it.

#### 15 min. Reread and sort

Pairs reread The Stable Gate for accuracy: one reads a page, the other the next, then swap (6 minutes). Say: "If a word stops you, map each letter, blend, then check the meaning." Groups of five sort the twelve -le cards by the letters before le: ble, dle, kle, zle (5 minutes). Dictate noodle and wrinkle, and children write each on the student page (2 minutes). Partners use each word in a spoken sentence (2 minutes).

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: the day's six (group 3 of five in cycle 14) read the oral card at your table, two minutes each (12 of your minutes); the other 24 are at stations, eight each. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes each: stations fill to ten and the six take the two they have not had. Tiles: build noodle and tickle. Sort: today's -le cards. Reread: The Stable Gate. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which letter comes before le in noodle?" Children say it together: d.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the towels and picture cards on torn paper, or use a real damp cloth, wet sponge and soaked towel. One adult with one child: do the sort with the child, then the ladder with real cloths.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The stations and the oral card.

- **5 min. Hear.** Read your child this: "Land, cling, carry. Take the pollen, do not tarry." Your child says it back, then says what tarry means.
- **10 min. Ladder.** Wet a cloth a little, then a lot, and hold each up. Say damp, wet, soaked. Your child points to the one you name.
  **Say.** "Which one has water dripping from all of it?" Expect: soaked.
- **10 min. Read.** Your child reads the four words on the student page with a finger, then you name a thing (a peach, tape, a shivering puppy) and your child points to the word that fits it.
- **5 min. Words.** Your child writes noodle and wrinkle.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X85]] [[s03-X85b]] [[s03-X85c]] [[s03-X85d]] [[s03-X85e]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the choral reread and the sip and gulp cards; keep the sort, the ladder and the exit. Longer: a child draws a fourth towel between wet and soaked and says what it is like.

### Words and supports

Fuzzy, sticky, cling, tremble, tarry, damp, wet, soaked, sip, gulp. Frames: "The word hover shows me ___." "You said ___; I think ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may touch the rung or point to the picture instead of saying it.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children can name a real thing a word fits, tell two near words apart, and give a reason. If many said two near words were the same (error Q), day 86 opens by setting both words on the ladder. Day 86 uses precise verbs and adjectives to describe what a pollinator does.
