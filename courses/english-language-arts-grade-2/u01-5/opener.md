# Section 1.5 opener: a poem, a book of facts and a discussion (days 21 to 26)

## What the section does

Day 21 returns to the poem *Our School Day*: children read it for a listener, with pauses at the line ends, and say how reading well helps the audience understand. Days 22 to 24 start the school book: children choose facts and a picture that fit the question "Explain what schools help children do, using facts and definitions from two articles", compare two details with a photograph, and gather notes (two facts with source tags, one word meaning and one picture idea) on a notes sheet. Day 25 rehearses a discussion that links comments and asks for clarification, and day 26 is a check day: you observe a small-group discussion for 48 minutes and hear six children read in four-minute conferences. Nobody is scored on days 21 to 25.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes: a 60-minute topic block and a 60-minute foundations block. Cycle 5 (oa and ow as long o) runs in the foundations block with the reader *The Slow Boat*; day 25 ends the cycle with a 15-minute written check and a fresh response for the day 21 six. Day 26 has no new teaching: the topic block is the discussion (48 minutes in six groups of five, then a 12-minute return) and the foundations block is retrieval, reading conferences and two flexible groups. Every exit on days 21 to 25 is one question you read aloud, and you hear the day's rotating six. Reading conferences run days 26 to 30, six children a day, so each child is heard once.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Reading for a listener.** Pause at the end of a line or a sentence, keep the voice even, and slow down where the meaning matters.
- **A book needs a question.** A fact belongs in the book when it helps answer the book question and carries its source tag.
- **Both and but.** Name what two details share, then what is different, each with its source.
- **A photograph can add.** Ask what it shows that the words do not say.
- **A link repeats the speaker's idea.** A clarifying question asks which part: "Which part do you mean?"

## The errors, in plain words

- **E:** an ending or a meaning that ignores or contradicts the source. Point to the fact it comes from.
- **O:** lines read so fast they run together. Reread at the phrase boundaries.
- **A:** a detail that contradicts the card. Put the picture beside the line.
- **C:** a photograph named with no word on what it adds. Ask what it shows that the words do not.
- **H, I, J, K:** interrupting, an unrelated comment, accepting an unclear word without asking, a question that ignores the speaker. Rehearse the wait signal, name the last idea, ask which part.
- **N, P:** a purpose that names only the topic; a word named from the picture. Say the key idea; read the letters first.
- **L, M, Q:** a vowel team read with the wrong sound, or dropped in spelling. Map the team; compare with the word wall.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. On day 26 every child gets four cells for talk (H to K) and a reading record; a child may pass a turn and join on the next. Nothing on days 21 to 26 is scored as a number.
