# Day 21: Reading for an audience

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the poem and cut the six stanza cards. Choose today's six from the record sheet (group 1 of five on the oral card). Write oa and ow on the sound-spelling-map, with road under oa and snow under ow. Put day 20's retry results beside you: error E on the retry, error Q on the written check.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 20's retry (error E, an ending that ignores the facts), use 02-a. Read aloud: "In many schools, pupils clean their classroom as a group." Say a made-up ending, "Pupils like to run," and ask where in the sentence it comes from. Children point to the words that give an ending: "Pupils learn to work together." Say: "This is made up for practice." Otherwise retrieve the frame card: children name its five rows to a partner.

#### 4 min. Words

Two words, each with a sentence. An audience is the listeners a reader reads for: "The audience listens to the poem." A refrain is the lines a poem repeats: "We say the refrain together." Review: you joined the two lines that come back after each part of Our School Day on day 2. Children say each word to a partner.

#### 6 min. Reread Our School Day

Give each child the poem. Read the first stanza aloud, then the class reads all five stanzas together, one stanza at a time. After each stanza, the class says the refrain. Point to the refrain lines each time so children see them come back.

#### 10 min. Model expression

Read stanza 3 twice. First rush it, with no pause at the line ends. Second, pause at the end of each line. Ask: "Which reading helped an audience understand?" Expect the second, and a reason such as "I could hear each line." If a child says the first was good because it was quick, say: "Quick can be fun, and the audience can lose the words." (This repairs error O.) Review: on day 10 you marked a slash where a reader pauses. Children mark a slash at the end of each line on their copy of stanza 3, then read it with you. Last, read the refrain and lift your voice on "together." Children copy the lift.

#### 13 min. Group rehearsal

Six groups of five, set by desks. Five groups each take a stanza card (cards 1 to 5), and one group takes the refrain card. In a stanza group, four children take one line each and the fifth is the pace keeper, who raises a hand at every line end so the group pauses. Each child first reads their line to a partner (the pace keeper reads the whole stanza to a partner). Then the group reads the stanza twice. The refrain group works in pairs and a trio, reads both refrain lines, and decides where the voice lifts. You visit each group for about two minutes.

#### 10 min. Perform

Groups perform in the poem's order, stanza 1 to stanza 5. After each stanza the refrain group leads and everyone joins the refrain. The rest are the audience. After every second group, one audience child says one thing the reading helped them hear. A child may pass.

#### 7 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "How did the reading help the audience understand?" Turn: partners tell each other (1 minute). Hear today's six, about 40 seconds each. After a speaker, the next child links with the link card: "You said ___; I would add ___." Or asks: "Which part do you mean?" Wait with the wait signal first. Last two minutes: the class names one thing a reader can do for the audience. Expect: pause at the line ends, keep the voice even.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s01-X21 aloud twice, plainly, and hand each child the printed card. Ask: "Read the stanza aloud so that a listener understands." Today's six read to you, about 30 seconds each. The others read to a partner. Shown means a pause at each line end, the words right and an even, unhurried voice. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "Where would a listener need to rest?" Not yet seen means a rushed reading with no pause at the line ends (error O), or words lost. Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Many children wrote day 20's words by sound (error Q). Write one such spelling, frite, and ask: "Which word on the wall sounds like the end of it?" Children find light and see igh. Mend it to fright. Do the same for fried (pie, ie) and dry (my, y). Children spell tight with tiles from the wall.

#### 15 min. Teach oa and ow

New today: oa and ow as two more spellings of long o. Review: silent-e words such as home and rode say long o too (cycle 1). Say the sound o as in go. Write road on the board, map the whole team, oa under one long o, blend, then read. Do the same for coat, foam, soak, snow, slow, grow and show, with ow in the last four. Children read each word aloud, first all together, then in pairs. Then contrast: write home and rode beside road and coat. All four say the same sound, spelled differently. Say plainly that some words have ow with another sound; we read only the long o words for now. Last, spell two words with tiles: coat and snow. Say each word, children map it, build it and check it against the wall.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each, three rounds. Tiles: build road, coat and snow. Write: copy road, snow, coat and show from the map and underline oa or ow. Reread: partners read A Light Up High, a page each. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 5 oral card (group 1 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle). Use only its first two lines today, since no cycle 5 pattern was taught before today. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: groups swap stations. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Partners tell each other one oa word and one ow word. Read the refrain together, with the lift on "together."

### Other rooms

No printer: write stanza 3 and the exit stanza on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed poem: read from the view. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The groups, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read for a listener.** Read stanza 3 of the poem to your child twice, once fast and once with a pause at each line end.
  **Say.** "Which way helped you understand?" Expect: the slower one with pauses.
- **10 min. Long o.** Write road, coat, snow and show. Your child reads each and underlines oa or ow.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say coat and snow; your child writes each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X21]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the perform part to three stanzas. Longer: a child writes a new refrain line for a classmate's group to read.

### Words and supports

Audience, refrain, pause. Frames: "I understood ___ because ___ ." "You said ___; I would add ___ ." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot read a line may echo it after you. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 22 opens the school book. Its opening repair is for error O on today's exit (a stanza read so fast the lines run together): reread at phrase boundaries. Its foundations opening takes the commonest miss from today's rotation, error L (coat read with a short o) or error M (ow read as the vowel in cow): map the whole team, then blend.
