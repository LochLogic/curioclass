# Day 25: Rehearsing a discussion

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the talk cards, the written check lines (student page side 1) and the word card. Brief two children for the model. Mark today's six (group 5 of five, the last group) and day 21's six for the last part. From your record sheet, choose the two groups with the most H to K letters; you sit with one each round. Have day 24's exit marks beside you: error E is the miss to repair.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 24's exit (error E, a meaning that contradicts the card), reread the source sentence and say the meaning from it. Read aloud from 02-b: "A library is a place where people borrow books and read." Say a made-up meaning, "A library is a place to eat lunch," and ask: "Where does the sentence say that?" Children find that it does not, and say the meaning from the sentence. Say: "This is made up for practice." Otherwise retrieve the notes sheet: partners read one fact and its tag.

#### 4 min. Words

Review three words from days 22 and 23: to gather is to collect facts, to select is to choose the facts that answer the question, and to connect is to join two ideas. Partners say each word in a sentence about their notes.

#### 8 min. Reread

Read aloud stanza 3 of the poem (card 3, the lunchtime stanza) and show the 02-c diagram. Ask: "Which parts of a school day do the poem and the diagram both show?" Children find it, each with a finger on the line or the label. Expect: lunch, which the poem shows with the spoons and the fruit and the diagram shows in each row as the lunch icon. Say each answer back with its source: "The poem says ___ ; the diagram shows ___ ."

#### 10 min. Model

Show the talk card: Wait, Link, Ask, Answer. Say that an Ask can be "Which part do you mean?" or a question about the speaker's idea. The two briefed children and you show a four-turn exchange on the same question. Speaker, child 1: "The poem says we pass the fruit at lunchtime." Link, child 2: "You said we pass the fruit; I would add the diagram has a lunch in every row." Ask, child 1: "How does the diagram show lunch?" Answer, child 2: "It has a lunch icon in every row." After each turn, children say which line of the talk card it was. Run it a second time and swap the two children. Say that each of us may pass.

#### 18 min. Rehearse

Six groups of five, each with a talk card and a tally child. The tally child makes a mark each time a child links or asks, and does not speak for the group in that round. Two rounds of 9 minutes. Each group talks on: "Which parts of a school day do the poem and the diagram both show?" Each child speaks at least once, about a minute and a half each. Between rounds, children numbered 1 and 2 move to the next group and a new tally child is chosen. Sit with one chosen group each round. Watch for interrupting (error H), an unrelated comment (error I), nodding at an unclear remark (error J) and a question that ignores the speaker (error K). If you see one, name the last idea and say: "Say what your partner said first."

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "Which parts of a school day do the poem and the diagram both show?" Whole class. Turn: partners tell each other (1 minute). Hear today's six, about 60 seconds each. After each speaker, the next child links with "You said ___; I would add ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" Use the wait signal.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Say aloud: Lee says, "They carried those to the back before the bell." Ask: "Lee's words are not clear. Say what you would ask Lee." The six answer to you, about 30 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means a question that names what Lee said and asks which things "those" are. Shown with help means that after one prompt: "What would you ask to find out which things?" Not yet seen means nodding or "OK" and asking nothing (error J), or an unrelated comment such as "I like the bell" (error I). Mark the record sheet.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Unscored, to see what each child can do without help. Give each child side 1. Read each sentence twice and say its missing word; children write the word in the gap. 1. "I put jam on my toast." The word is toast. 2. "A frog can croak at night." The word is croak. 3. "Please throw me the ball." The word is throw. 4. "I have a bowl of fruit." The word is bowl. Give no help with spelling. Collect the sheets. Mark each word by letter on the record sheet: Q for tost, croke, thro or bol (spelled by sound, pattern not carried over). Write other spellings down as they are.

#### 5 min. Reader

Partners reread The Slow Boat for expression, a page each. Say: "Read it so your partner enjoys the story, resting at the full stops."

#### 25 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each, two rounds. Tiles: spell foam and grow. Reread: partners read The Slow Boat again. Write: write two oa or ow words from the wall, each in a short sentence. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 5 oral card (group 5 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle). Round 2, 12 minutes: every group visits a second station. Mark the heard column.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

No new teaching. First minute: say oak and low once each to the whole class; children write each and map its team. Then day 21's six read to you, two minutes each, 12 minutes: the grade 1 words (shelf, class, cloth, lunch, thank, chest), then the cycle 5 words (road, coat, foam, soak, snow, slow, grow, show). The others work in pairs: they partner-read the cycle 5 word card (road, coat, foam, soak, snow, slow, grow, show, toad, glow). For the last two minutes, each child chooses a word from the wall that was hard and reads it to a partner.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the talk card's four lines on the board and the four check sentences on the board for you to read. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed talk card: say the four lines to your child. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The groups, the written check and the stations.

- **10 min. Read and talk.** Read stanza 3 of the poem aloud. Ask: "What part of a school day does the poem show?" Your child answers.
  **Say.** "You said ___ ; I would add ___ ." Expect: a link that repeats your idea first.
- **10 min. Take turns.** Your child says one thing the diagram shows. You link, then ask a question about their idea. Your child answers. Swap roles.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say oak and low; your child writes each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X25]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one round of rehearsal and no reader. Longer: a group tries a fresh question on 02-b and keeps its own tally.

### Words and supports

Gather, select, connect. Frames: "You said ___ ; I would add ___ ." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write tells you and you write. A child may pass a turn and join on the next.

### Tomorrow

Day 26 is a check day with no repair slot. Error J on today's exit (accepting an unclear remark) is practiced in day 32's talk group. Error Q on today's written check is repaired at the opening of day 27's foundations; day 26's foundations opens with retrieval only. Children bring their notes sheets, since day 26's fact cards come from them.
