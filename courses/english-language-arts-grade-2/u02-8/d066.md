# Day 66: A fossil letter with commas

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write the model letter large for the wall: "Dear Pip," then "I found a smooth stone by the creek." then "Your pal," then "Rhys". Write the closings your class may choose from on the board: Your pal, Your classmate, Yours truly. Set up your own device and a screen the pairs can see, or the board. Mark today's six (group 4 of five in cycle 12) on the unit 2 record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Hand back the day 65 exit cards (item s02-X65) and repair error AE with fresh cards. Hold up a common-name card and a special-name card, such as a holiday and Halloween, then a state and Ohio. Ask: "One special holiday, place or product?" Children say which card needs a capital and why. Hear three, and use the capital cards from day 65 for the pairs that need them.

#### 5 min. Words

Review: a comma separates the day from the year, as in a date (grade 1, day 59). Today the words are letter, greeting, closing and comma. A letter is a note written to one person. The greeting is how it begins, the closing is how it ends. Say each once and point to it on the model letter.

#### 8 min. Read a model letter

Say: "This letter is made up. Pip and Rhys are made up." Read the model letter aloud twice. Children point to the greeting, then the closing, then each comma, with a partner. Hear four. Ask: "What does Rhys tell Pip?" Expect: a smooth stone by the creek.

#### 8 min. Model: a comma after the greeting and after the closing

Say: "Dear starts a greeting, then the name, then a comma." Write "Dear ___," on the board and let children choose a made-up name. Say: "A closing begins with a capital letter and ends with a comma." Let children choose a closing from the list, and write it. Children choose one sentence for the middle. Read the whole letter aloud and run a finger down the commas.

#### 14 min. Write the letter

Each child writes a short letter to a classmate about their find on the letter frame: a greeting, one sentence, a closing and their name. You move and give no wording. A child who needs it gets the frame with the commas printed in grey. A child who cannot write dictates to you.

If a comma is missing (error AF), walk a finger down the model letter and stop at each comma.

#### 10 min. Shared typing: show why

Say: "This letter is made up for the class." On your own device, type a greeting and one sentence for a class letter while five pairs, one minute each, give a word or an order and their partner says "agree" or "I would change it". The rest tell their partner what they would pick. Children do not type. With no device, write it on the board in the same way.

#### 5 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine from day 1. Ask: "How did you and your partner agree on the order?" Partners turn and talk, then link: "I agree because ___." or clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Hear the six (about 20 seconds each, 2 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Give each child the printed card for items s02-X66a and s02-X66b and read it aloud twice. Three of the six hear item s02-X66a and three hear item s02-X66b; each answers to you in about 20 seconds (2 minutes). The rest tell a partner. Mark shown, shown with help (after "What does the card say?") or not yet seen. Tag error AF on a missing comma and error AE on a lowercase name.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Reread six -tion and -sion cards and the day 65 misses. Children say the ending alone, then the whole word. On a wrong ending (error Y), cover the first chunk and read the ending alone. On a spelling copied without checking (error AC), say: "Say the word, find it, copy it, cover it, write it."

#### 15 min. Transfer to a fresh passage

Pairs read Nell at the Station (made up) once (5 minutes). Ask: "What was Nell's mission?" Expect: to find the right direction. Pairs say what happened at the end (3 minutes). Reread needed, asked, pointed and hopped, saying the ending alone, then the word (3 minutes). Review: the three sounds of -ed (day 53). Dictate "Nell made a quick decision." and children write it (4 minutes). Never guess a word from its picture.

Check: decision spelled with -sion, a capital and a period.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six (group 4) sit with you for the cycle 12 oral card, two minutes each. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 are in three stations of eight: tiles (build mission, direction, section), sort (the twelve cycle 12 cards) and reread (A Choice of Actions with a partner). Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: groups move on.

Check: each child reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which word did you decode, then check?" Expect: a word from Nell at the Station, with its meaning confirmed.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the model letter large, write the class letter on the board while pairs call out words, and copy the exit card by hand. Large group: each child in a group answers once, about 20 seconds; the six hear you in turn.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read a letter.** Read the model letter in your child's folder, or any short letter at home. Your child points to the greeting and the closing.
  **Say.** "What comes after the greeting and the closing?" Expect: a comma.
- **10 min. Write one.** Your child writes a short letter to someone at home: a greeting, one sentence, a closing and a name.
- **10 min. Words.** Say decision and section. Your child writes each and says which letters make the ending sound.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X66a]] and [[s02-X66b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: write the letter to a partner on the board in pairs and skip the shared typing, keeping the model, the exit and the teaching. Longer: a child writes a second letter to another reader and checks each comma.

### Words and supports

Letter, greeting, closing, comma, decision, section. Frames: "Dear ___," "Your ___," "I would change it because ___." Pointing is an answer. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

After class read each letter and mark any missing comma. Day 67 opens with a repair of today's exit (error AF, a greeting or closing with no comma), then edits contractions and words that show who has something, with the editing checklist, the cycle 12 written check and a fresh response.
