# Day 34: How the story begins

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child and pages 2 and 3 for each pair. Hang the large story map with its beginning box at the top. Mark today's six (group 2 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Have day 33's exit marks beside you: error B is the miss to repair. Set three stations: tiles, a partner dictation, the reader reread. The text chart stays on the wall.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 33's exit (error B, the actions given in the wrong order), read aloud an invented card: "Jon finds a flat stone. First he rubs off the mud. Then he shows his dad." Say: "This card is made up for practice." Ask: "What does Jon do second?" Expect: shows his dad. Children hold up two fingers, then say it. Otherwise partners tell each other what Amara does first, next and at last on pages 4 to 7.

#### 10 min. Model rereading pages 1 to 3

Hold up the large story map. Say: "Every story has a beginning that introduces it. Review: you learned introduce and conclude in unit 1 on day 17. A beginning introduces the people, the place and time, and what starts." Read pages 1 to 3 aloud twice. First for the story, with the pictures. Then slowly, stopping at Dr. Lee's words on page 1 and at Amara's three questions on page 3 (about 145 words in all). Between the readings, say one thing you noticed. Say aloud again that Amara, Dr. Lee and the park are made up.

#### 12 min. What the beginning introduces

Fill the beginning box with the class, a finger on each line. Who: Amara, her class and Dr. Lee (page 1). Where and when: a fossil park, on a cool, bright morning (page 1). What starts: Amara finds a curved mark in a small stone and wonders what it is (pages 2 and 3). Children say each entry once and a volunteer points to the page. Review: you used a story map in unit 1, on day 3. Then tell them the beginning does not tell us what the mark is, only that the story has started.

**Ask.** "What does the beginning of this story introduce?" Expect: the people (Amara, Dr. Lee), the place and time (a fossil park, one morning), and what starts (a mark Amara finds). Accept any two with the page named. If a child tells what happens at the end, say "Show me the page": the beginning does not answer the mark.

#### 10 min. Pairs read pages 2 and 3

Pairs share one printed copy of pages 2 and 3 (about 99 words; each child reads about half, turn about by sentence). Read once with a partner, swap and read again, the second time smoothly. Say the check word for each: "Which word did you stop on?" Walk and listen to each pair once (about 20 seconds a pair). Say "Show me the line" to a child who guesses a word. Write a word many stopped on at the board and blend it together.

Check: each pair reads both pages and fixes any word by blending it, then rereads the sentence.

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "Which part of the beginning makes you want to read on?" Turn: each child says one part to a partner (2 minutes). Pair: partners agree on one part and point to its page (2 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Then hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes); after each, one child links or asks. Use the wait signal.

#### 8 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes on the student page: "The beginning introduces ___." with the people, the place or what starts (4 minutes). Partners swap and check a capital, an end mark, a noun and a verb (2 minutes). Each makes one change (2 minutes). A child may draw and tell you; you write it.

Check: the sentence begins with a capital, ends with a mark, and has a noun and a verb.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hold up The Park Cart and read pages 1 to 3 aloud twice, showing the pictures; pairs read the whole reader later today. Ask (s02-X34v2): "Tell me what the beginning of The Park Cart introduces." The six answer to you, about 20 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means who and what starts (Barb and Dad set off up the path with the cart). Shown with help means that after one prompt: "Who is on page 1, and what do they start?" Not yet seen means a later event, such as the jar falling (error A), or no beginning named. Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Review: you built and read r-controlled words yesterday. Write jars, horn, turns, her, birds and curl on the board. Children build them with tiles, one at a time, saying the vowel sound. If many misspell a /er/ word, send them to the word wall card for her, bird or turn. Watch for the letter order in er, ir and ur (a child writes re for er).

#### 15 min. Read: The Park Cart

Give each pair the reader (about 60 words, six pages, one picture a page). You read the title. Review: you map tricky words and star the part that is odd. Map great (star ea, which says /a/); yellow is on the wall from yesterday. Echo read: you read a page, the pair reads it back (6 minutes). Then pairs finish the pages and read once more, smoothly (6 minutes). Confirm meaning after decoding: ask "What does the picture show that the letters did not say?" (3 minutes). Expect: the cart is yellow, or Dad and Barb sort the jars.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Each reads the cycle 6 words (car, park, jar, corn, horn, born, her, bird, turn, fern) and then the sentence "A bird sat on the corn.", saying the vowel sound in each word. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: jars, horn, turns, her, then change one letter. Dictation: one partner says jar, horn and her, the other writes and checks with the word wall. Reread: pairs read The Park Cart a third time and point to one word in each of three vowel spellings. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "What did the picture not tell you that the letters did?" Expect: the words said, like Dad's "Start!", or that the corn was not hurt.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the story map on a long sheet and write the reader on three folded sheets. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of the story: hold up each page.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed story: read the first three pages and tell the rest in your own words. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The reader, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read the beginning.** Read pages 1 to 3 of The Mark in the Stone aloud together.
  **Say.** "Who is in the beginning, where are they, and what starts?" Expect: Amara, her class and Dr. Lee; a fossil park; a mark in a stone.
- **10 min. Say it.** Your child says the beginning in one sentence, then writes it: "The beginning introduces ___."
- **10 min. Spell.** Say jar, horn and her. Your child writes each and checks the vowel against the card.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X34v2]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: read pages 2 and 3 once in pairs and write the sentence with a partner; keep the exit and the reader. Longer: a pair finds a second thing the beginning does not tell and says where the story answers it.

### Words and supports

Introduce, beginning, people, place, time. Frames: "The beginning introduces ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 35 reads the account of Mary Anning for facts, in the order they happened. Error A on today's exit (the middle or end named for the beginning) is repaired at day 35's opening. The story map stays on the wall.
