# Day 36: How the story ends

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child, pages 8 to 11 and the match cards for each pair. Mark today's six (group 4 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Have day 35's exit marks beside you: error B is the miss to repair. Write the three repair strips (see the opening). Set three stations: tiles, a reread of Burt and the Bird, a writing station. Hang the large story map. Key: key-match-cards-day-36.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 35's exit (error B, a question the card does not answer), read three strips from Mary Anning's "Learning to Look": "Mary looked for fossils on the beach below the cliffs." "Her father showed her how to look for them." "He showed her how to clean the ones they found." Ask: "Which strip answers the question Where did Mary look?" Children point to the first and say why. Then ask what question the second answers (Who showed her, or how). Otherwise partners tell each other one fact from the account and its question word.

#### 8 min. Reread pages 8 to 11

Read pages 8 to 11 aloud while children follow with the printed page (about 300 words, about 2 and a half minutes), showing the pictures. Pause on page 9 at "A fossil can show us a lot, but not everything." Then partners reread page 11 turn about, a sentence each (about 100 words; each child reads about half). Say again that the story is made up.

#### 12 min. What the ending concludes

Hold up the large story map and point to the ending box. Say: "An ending concludes the story. It finishes the action the beginning started." Fill the box with the class. The question is answered: Amara tells Ben what is in her bag (page 11). The clues are recorded: Amara draws her stone and Dr. Lee writes a label (page 10). The mark is no longer a mystery (page 11). Children say each entry once with a finger on the page. Review: the time words First, Next, Then and At last for the middle, from day 33 and grade 1.

**Ask.** "How does page 11 answer the question that started the story?" Expect: at the start Amara wonders what the mark is; at the end she says it is the shape of a shell in stone. If a child tells a middle step, say "Show me page 11." Error A is the end named without the start it finishes.

#### 8 min. Words for the unit

Say each word, point to where it was used, and partners say it in a sentence about the story (about 90 seconds a word). Review: introduce and conclude from unit 1, day 17 (a beginning introduces, an ending concludes). Review: sequence from kindergarten (the order things happen). Review: remain from grade 1 (what is left). Read the last line of page 8's second paragraph aloud once, only to show where remained is used, and do not stop on any other word in it.

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "What does the ending tell us that the middle did not?" Turn: each child tells a partner (2 minutes). Pair: partners find the line on page 10 or 11 and read it (3 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Then hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes); after each, one child links or asks. Use the wait signal.

#### 7 min. Match cards

Pairs have the beginning and ending match cards (two beginnings, two endings, two blank). They lay each beginning beside the ending that finishes it and say "The ending finishes the job the beginning started." (4 minutes). Then they write one beginning and one ending of their own on the blank cards and trade (3 minutes). Key: Mira's beginning with Mira's ending; Amara's beginning with Amara's ending. Check one pair in each row.

#### 5 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes on the student page: "The ending concludes ___." (3 minutes). Partners check a capital, an end mark, a noun and a verb (2 minutes).

Check: the sentence begins with a capital, ends with a mark, and has a noun and a verb.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X36 aloud twice. Ask: "Tell me how the ending finishes what the beginning started." The six answer to you, about 20 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means the ending and the start it answers (a missing notch added, so a reader can match it). Shown with help means that after one prompt: "What did Kemi want at the start?" Not yet seen means a middle step or the beginning only (error A). Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Repair the commonest miss from yesterday's sort and spelling. If many wrote a /er/ word with the wrong letters, write curl and fern and say each, then children check against the word wall. Otherwise write her, bird and turn and children say the vowel spelling in each, one at a time.

#### 15 min. Transfer to a fresh passage

Give each pair Burt and the Bird (a fresh passage of about 50 words, pictures none). Pairs read it once together (4 minutes). Confirm meaning after decoding: "What does Burt do for the bird?" Expect: he puts it in a box with corn (3 minutes). Mapped words in the passage are that, start and turns: say each and underline each vowel spelling, never guessing from the first letter (2 minutes). Then say the sentence once and children write it as dictation: "The bird starts to chirp." Say it three times, a word at a time (4 minutes). They check each word against the word wall and fix one (2 minutes). Watch for the /er/ spelling in bird and the ar in starts.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Each reads the cycle 6 words (car, park, jar, corn, horn, born, her, bird, turn, fern), then the sentence "A bird sat on the corn.", saying the vowel sound in each. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: car, born, fern, then change one letter. Reread: pairs read Burt and the Bird twice, the second time smoothly. Writing: each child writes a sentence with two cycle 6 words and a partner reads it aloud. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which words did you check after you decoded them?" Expect: that, start and turns, or any word where the meaning was checked against the picture or the sentence.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the story map on a sheet and write the match cards on torn paper. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of the passage: write it on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed story: read pages 10 and 11 and tell the rest in your own words. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The match cards, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read the ending.** Read pages 10 and 11 of The Mark in the Stone aloud together.
  **Say.** "How does the ending finish what the beginning started?" Expect: Amara wondered about the mark at the start and tells Ben what it is at the end.
- **10 min. Two sentences.** Your child says one sentence for the beginning and one for the ending of a story you both know, then writes the ending sentence: "The ending concludes ___."
- **10 min. Spell.** Say corn, turn and start. Your child writes each and checks it against the word card.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X36]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the blank cards and read page 11 once; keep the exit and the dictation. Longer: a pair writes a new ending for the match cards and another pair reads it and says what start it finishes.

### Words and supports

Conclude, introduce, sequence, remain, ending, beginning. Frames: "The ending concludes ___." "The ending finishes the job the beginning started." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 37 works out fossil words and multiple meanings from the sentence and a small word, and holds the cycle 6 written check. Error A on today's exit (a middle step named for the ending) is repaired at its opening.
