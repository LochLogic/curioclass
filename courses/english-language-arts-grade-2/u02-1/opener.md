# Section 2.1 opener: a mark in the stone (days 33 to 37)

## What the section does

For five days the class reads and rereads *The Mark in the Stone*, an invented story about a girl who finds a fan-shaped mark in a stone at a fossil park, and then reads the first of three short articles about fossils. Children say what a text tells and what it leaves open, explain what the beginning of a story introduces and what the ending finishes, ask who, what, where and when questions of a true account of Mary Anning, and work out a word from the sentence around it and from the small word inside it. The unit's question, "What can fossils tell us about the past?", goes up on day 33 and stays on the wall. Say aloud that Amara and Dr. Lee are made up, that the science in the story is real, and that fossils may not be taken from many places, so we always ask first. Say that fossil time is "very long ago" and give no number.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short opening repair, the day's words, a reading, a model, pair work and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds a repair, new work on r-controlled vowels (ar, or, er, ir, ur), the cycle 6 reader *The Park Cart*, a 37-minute rotation of three stations (tiles, sort, reread) and a close. Day 37 ends with a 15-minute written check. Every exit is one card you read aloud twice and one ask. The child answers aloud; you hear the day's rotating six and the rest tell a partner. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A text tells some things and leaves others open.** The story says a curved mark was pressed into the stone. It does not say what kind of shell. Say "The text tells ___" and "The text leaves open ___".
- **The beginning starts a job and the ending finishes it.** Ask what is wanted or wondered at the start, then find the line at the end that settles it.
- **A true account holds facts.** Mary Anning's account gives dates and deeds. It never says what she felt, because no record does.
- **A word can be worked out two ways.** Read the sentence around it, and look for a small word inside it (record inside recorder).
- **Talk has four steps.** Turn, pair, link, clarify, as in unit 1.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A and B:** the opening and ending swapped, or a detail that the card does not give. Lay the beginning card and the ending card on the story map, or retell with strips in order.
- **F, G, I:** a mold confused with the shape that fills it, a word meaning that does not fit the sentence, and a lost small word. Draw it in three boxes, say the word in its sentence, cover the ending and say the small word.
- **O and P:** events with no order words, or an ending that just stops. Add First, Then and At last, and give the closing line.
- **X, Y, Z, AA, AC:** a word guessed from the picture, an /er/ spelling misread, a dropped ending, an unusual word spelled by sound, a spelling not checked. Cover the picture and blend, hold the three /er/ spellings beside ar and or, underline the ending, star the odd part, and look the word up.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Exits and the cycle 6 check are unscored; mark the exit looks on the unit record sheet, dated, and keep it in your folder.
