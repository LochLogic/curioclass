# Day 33: A mark in the stone

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child and the story with its eleven pictures. Mark today's six (group 1 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Give each pair a folded text chart sheet and the four sequence cards. Put the compelling question on a strip, face down. Set three stations: tiles, the twelve sort cards, the four phrase cards (a car and a jar; corn for her bird; her fern; turn the car). Keys: key-sequence-strip-day-33.md, key-sort-cards-cycle-6.md.

### The day

#### 7 min. Words before reading

Open with the day 32 retry results: tell each child who had a retry one sentence in words, quietly, while the others find their pencils (about a minute; the error letters stay on your sheet). Each of those children speaks first in their pair today.

Then eight words, each said by the class and acted or shown on a picture card, about 20 seconds a word. New today: fossil, what remains of a plant or an animal that lived very long ago, found in rock (a picture card of a shell in a rock); discover, to find something for the first time (lift a cloth off a stone); examine, to look very closely at something, part by part (hold a stone and a magnifying glass). Say that fossil time is only "very long ago", with no number.

Review: you met notice and wonder in unit 1 and in kindergarten, careful in unit 1 on day 22 (look closely so nothing is missed), clue in kindergarten riddles, and evidence in unit 1 and in grade 1 (the line of the text that supports what you say). Say each once, then partners use one in a sentence.

#### 9 min. Read aloud: The Mark in the Stone

Say aloud first: Amara, Dr. Lee and the fossil park are made up, though the science in the story is real. Many places do not let people take fossils, so we always ask first. Read all eleven pages (about 650 words), showing each picture. Pause on page 2 after the mark and let children say what they notice (a curved mark, like a tiny fan). Pause on page 9 at "I can't tell you for sure" and say nothing: the next part uses it. Children listen for what the text tells and what it leaves unsaid.

#### 4 min. Retrieval (grade 1)

Unscored. Review: in grade 1 and in unit 1 you told a story by its beginning, middle and ending. Two minutes: one pair, then the class, tells Mira's story (at the classroom door; Ada, the chart and the card game; Mira holds the door for the newest pupil) and says in one sentence what Mira learned to do. Then four oral prompts, half a minute each, answered together aloud or by pointing. Say "made up for practice" for each. (a) Which is a story: "Eli ran to show Mom his shell", or "A shell has two halves joined by a hinge"? (b) "Bees sip nectar. Bees carry pollen. Bees make wax." What is the topic? Bees. (c) What does a heading tell a reader? What the part is about. (d) "The path was damp, so my shoes got wet." Use the sentence to say what damp means. A little wet. Write on your sheet, by group, any prompt that many missed; no child is marked.

#### 12 min. What the text tells and what it leaves open

Hang the text chart: two columns, The text tells and The text leaves open. Model one of each with a finger on the page. It tells that a curved mark was pressed into the stone (page 2). It leaves open what kind of shell it was (page 9). Say the frame: "The text tells ___; it does not tell ___." Then the talk routine from unit 1. Turn: each child draws or writes one entry for each column on the folded sheet (2 minutes). Pair: partners share and add one to the wall chart each (3 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Then hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes), and after each one child links or asks. Use the wait signal.

**Ask.** "What does the text tell, and what does it leave open?" Expect: it tells something the words state (a curved mark, two matching marks, a shell shape in the rock); it leaves open something they do not say (what kind of shell it was, whether the museum scientist could name it). If a child names a shell animal, say "Show me the page": the text never names one. Error B is a bubble that answers a different question.

#### 5 min. The compelling question

Turn the strip over and read it: "What can fossils tell us about the past?" Say that the past means a time before now, and that we will come back to this question all unit. Each child draws one thing they wonder about fossils in the box on the student page, then says it to a partner with the frame "I wonder ___." (1 minute to draw, 2 to say, 2 to post the drawings under the strip).

#### 10 min. Reread with a strip

Review: the time words First, Next, Then and At last from grade 1. Reread pages 4 to 7, one page at a time. After each page pairs lay the matching card on the strip and say aloud what Amara does, in a whole sentence. Key: First, Amara looks at the mark carefully. Next, she brushes the stone. Then she carries it to Dr. Lee. At last Dr. Lee smiles and compares the stone with a picture card. Spend about 90 seconds a page (6 minutes), then one pair says all four in order and the class says them again (2 minutes), then ask: "Why does At last go last?" Expect: it is the end of the steps.

#### 8 min. Write: two events and an ending

Unscored retrieval. Each child writes, in a few words, two events from the story in order and an ending, on the student page: "First ___. Then ___. At last ___." (4 minutes). Partners swap and check a capital at the start, an end mark, a noun and a verb in each sentence (2 minutes). Each makes one change the partner suggests (2 minutes). Watch for error O (the events with no order words) and error P (an ending that just stops); give the child the closing line from page 11 to copy.

Check: each sentence begins with a capital, ends with a mark, and has a noun and a verb.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X33 aloud twice. Ask: "Tell me what Zara does, in the order the card gives." The six answer to you, about 20 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means the three actions in the card's order (notices, draws, reads). Shown with help means that after one prompt: "What does the card say first?" Not yet seen means an order that is wrong (error B) or fewer than three actions. Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Retrieval (foundations)

Review: you read endings and mapped tricky words in grade 1. Write jumped and said. Children underline the ending in jumped and say it alone, then the whole word. For said, they put a star on the part that does not say what its letters usually say (ai says /e/) and write it from memory once. Watch for error Z (jump for jumped) and error AA (sayed).

#### 15 min. Teach: r-controlled vowels

Review: in grade 1 you met r-controlled vowels, where the letter r changes the vowel sound. Today's cycle has five spellings: ar, or, and er, ir, ur. Add them to the sound-spelling-map. Say: "ar says /ar/ as in car. or says /or/ as in corn. er, ir and ur all say /er/, as in her, bird and turn. That is one sound with three spellings, and we learn which by the word, so the word wall holds her, bird and turn."

Model car: touch under c, then draw a bar under ar (two letters, one sound), say /k/ /ar/, blend car. Build it with tiles, then change c to j for jar. Read the twelve words in order, the class echoing: car, jar, park, corn, horn, born, her, fern, bird, dirt, turn, curl. Then children build jar, corn and turn with tiles as you say each. Last, map two words that do not follow the pattern, never guessed from the picture or the first letter. Yellow: y-e-ll-ow; star ow, which says /o/ here, as in snow. Orange: o-r-a-n-ge; star or, which does not say /or/ here, and ge, which says /j/. Put both on the word wall.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Today only the first line is used, the grade 1 words shelf, class, cloth, lunch, thank, chest, because no cycle 6 pattern has been taught before today. Each child reads the six words and says the vowel sound in each. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: jar, corn and turn, then change one letter (jar to car, corn to horn). Sort: the twelve cards under ar, or and er, ir, ur, saying each word aloud. Reread: the four phrase cards, read twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which spelling of /er/ is in bird?" Expect: ir. Children point to the word wall card.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the twelve words on torn paper and draw the strip on a long sheet. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of the story: show each page on a projector or hold it up; children draw and write on their own sheet.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed story: read pages 4 to 7 and tell the rest in your own words. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The retrieval, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read the story.** Say it is made up. Read The Mark in the Stone aloud together with its pictures.
  **Say.** "What does the story tell? What does it leave open?" Expect: it tells a curved mark was in the stone; it leaves open what kind of shell it was.
- **10 min. First, Next, Then, At last.** Your child lays four paper slips in order for pages 4 to 7, then writes First, Then, At last about the story.
- **10 min. Spell.** Say jar, corn and turn. Your child builds or writes each and checks the spelling of bird.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X33]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the retrieval prompts to the story and the heading, and read pages 1 to 7 only; keep the exit and the spelling. Longer: a pair finds a second thing the text leaves open and says why a reader cannot know it.

### Words and supports

Fossil, discover, examine, notice, wonder, careful, clue, evidence. Frames: "The text tells ___; it does not tell ___." "I wonder ___." "First ___. Then ___. At last ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 34 rereads pages 1 to 3 for what the beginning introduces. Error B on today's exit (the order reversed) is repaired at its opening, and the commonest grade 1 retrieval miss goes there too. The text chart stays on the wall.
