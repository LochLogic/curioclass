# Day 37: Working out fossil words

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child and the article section for each pair. Mark today's six (group 5 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Have day 36's exit marks beside you: error A is the miss to repair. Keep the written check sentences face down at your corner. Set two stations: tiles and sort cards, and a reread of The Park Cart. Hang the story map.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 36's exit (error A, a middle step named for the ending), say Mira's story from unit 1: "Mira stops in the doorway of a new classroom." Ask: "Which line finishes that beginning: 'Ada shows Mira a card game' or 'Mira opens the door for the newest pupil'?" Children point to the second and say why it finishes the job. Otherwise partners tell each other what the ending of The Mark in the Stone concludes.

#### 7 min. Words for the unit

Three words from the story, each said by the class and pointed to on its page, about 2 minutes a word. Preserve: to keep something safe so it lasts (page 8, "Its shape was preserved for you to find"). Patient: able to wait calmly (page 6, Amara waited and tried to be patient). Sediment: small bits of mud, sand or clay that settle in water (page 8, mud at the bottom of a sea). Say each in a sentence about the story, then partners say it in their own. Do not give any other word on page 8 a meaning.

#### 8 min. Read Fossil Clues, article A

Read all of article A aloud (about 240 words, 2 minutes), pointing to each heading. Say: "This is a true article about fossils." Then pairs read the section Signs of What Animals Did (about 55 words, each child reads about half, turn about by sentence), 3 minutes. Ask the class (3 minutes): "What may happen if soft mud turns to rock?" Expect: a footprint may be preserved for a very long time. Do not stop on the word trace or give it a meaning; the article gives its own, and the day 57 reading will return to it.

#### 12 min. Multiple-meaning words

Say: "Some words have more than one meaning. We read the sentence around the word to pick the meaning that fits." Review: the multiple-meaning word fly from grade 1 (a bug, or to move through air). Review: sentence clue, from unit 1, on day 17. Four words from the story, each said in the story's sentence and in a second sentence, 2 minutes a word:
- brush: "Each child got a soft brush" and "I brush my hair at night."
- fan: "It looked like a tiny fan" and "The fan blows air on a hot day."
- mark: "A curved mark was pressed into the stone" and "Use a pencil to mark the page."
- pick: "She picked up a small, flat one" and "Pick a card from the pile."
For each, say which meaning the sentence needs and the clue that shows it. Then pairs choose one word and tell a partner both meanings (4 minutes). Do not use the sentence "a fan-shaped mark" as an example for the clue; it is in the story and stays there. Error G is the meaning that does not fit the sentence. Say the right meaning for fan against "a fan-shaped mark" if a child chooses the air meaning.

#### 10 min. A known root as a clue

Give each pair the sentence clue and small word card (step one: read the sentence around the word; step two: look for a small word inside it). Model careful: "Look at the small word inside." The small word is care. Careful means full of care. Then carefully: the same small word, and ly tells how. Then hunting: the small word is hunt, and ing means it is going on. Children say each with the card, covering the ending with a finger, then adding it back (about 3 minutes a word; one extra minute to fill the card's three lines for hunting). Say that patient has no small word the way careful does, so we check the sentence. Error I is "careful means not caring" or a meaning that loses the small word.

#### 8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "Which clue helped you choose a meaning: the sentence, or a small word?" Turn: each child tells a partner (2 minutes). Pair: partners agree on one word and say its clue (2 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which word do you mean?" Then hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes), and after each, one child links or asks. Use the wait signal.

#### 5 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes one sentence using brush, fan, mark or pick in a meaning the story uses, and underlines the word in the sentence that is the clue (3 minutes). Partners check a capital, an end mark, a noun and a verb (2 minutes).

Check: the sentence begins with a capital, ends with a mark, has a noun and a verb, and the underlined clue fits the meaning.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X37 aloud twice. Ask: "Use a small word you know to say what sorter means here." The six answer to you, about 20 seconds each; the others tell a partner. Shown means the small word and its meaning (sort, so a person who sorts). Shown with help means that after one prompt: "What small word is inside sorter?" Not yet seen means a meaning without the small word, or one that loses it (error I). Mark the record sheet.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Unscored, on the completed cycle 6 patterns only. Children use side 2, the four numbered gaps. Say each sentence twice and the missing word once, a minute a line, and give no help beyond the word wall card for each vowel spelling (4 sentences in about 8 minutes). Collect the sheets. Then say the first two words for market and tiger as unscored practice (closed and open syllables): children write each, then underline the first syllable and say open or closed (5 minutes). Last, call the first word again for anyone who needs it (2 minutes). Tag the marked sheets with letter Y for a vowel spelling written wrongly; the key is on the written check card.

Check: each of the four words is written with its taught vowel spelling and the sentence meaning fits.

#### 5 min. Reader

Pairs have the partner word card: car, jar, horn, her, bird, turn. They read the six to each other, then each spells two words the other says: park and fern (3 minutes). Partners check against the word wall and fix one (2 minutes). Review: reread The Park Cart once, if time allows, for meaning.

#### 25 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, two rounds of 12 minutes. Round 1: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Each reads the cycle 6 words (car, park, jar, corn, horn, born, her, bird, turn, fern), then the sentence "A bird sat on the corn.", saying the vowel sound in each. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in two stations of twelve. Tiles and sort: build jar, corn and turn, then sort the twelve cards. Reread: pairs read The Park Cart twice. Round 2 (12 minutes): the groups swap and the six join the two stations they have not met; use the time to record the heard column.

Check: each child in a station reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

Say the compelling question again: "What can fossils tell us about the past?" Each child writes or draws one thing a fossil shows, using a fact from article A, and one thing the text leaves open from The Mark in the Stone, in two sentences (10 minutes). Partners swap, read each other's sentences aloud and check a capital, an end mark, a noun and a verb (3 minutes). Each fixes one thing (2 minutes). Unscored. A child may draw and tell you; you write.

Check: each sentence begins with a capital, ends with a mark, and has a noun and a verb.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the section on the board and fold a sheet into the two-step card. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of the article: read it aloud and write the section on the board. The written check needs only paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the board or tell the facts in your own words. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The written check, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Read the article.** Read Fossil Clues, article A, aloud together.
  **Say.** "What may happen to a footprint in soft mud?" Expect: if the mud turns to rock, it may last a very long time.
- **10 min. A small word.** Say careful. Your child covers the ending and says the small word inside, then says what the word means.
- **10 min. Spell.** Say her, corn and turn. Your child writes each and checks the vowel spelling.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X37]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: take two of the four multiple-meaning words and one root word; keep the exit and the written check. Longer: a pair finds a second word with more than one meaning in the story and says the clue that picks each.

### Words and supports

Preserve, patient, sediment, brush, fan, mark, pick, careful. Frames: "The sentence says ___, so ___ means ___." "The small word is ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which word do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.
