Teacher note. Two short poems on the pollinators in g2-u3-01. 03-a is a poem of action words: each pollinator has its own verb (the bee lands, the butterfly settles, the hummingbird hovers, the bat swoops and glides), and the pollen clings and is carried. Use it for day 85 and 86: which verbs are nearly alike (land and settle), which are not (hover and swoop), and which pair gives a gentler picture (settle against land; gently). 03-b is a poem of describing words: a dusty visitor is described with adjectives (fuzzy, yellow, small, round, gold, sticky, bright), and the class names the visitor from the clues (a bee). Use it on day 87. Verse has no usable numeric measure; its difficulty is in the exact words, the rhyme and a few domain words (pollen, stigma). Children chant the chorus of 03-a on day 87. The facts in the poems match g2-u3-01.

## 03-a. Land, Cling, Carry

The bee will land with a buzzing drop.
The butterfly will settle, soft, and stop.
The hummingbird will hover, quick and bright.
The bat will swoop, then glide through the night.

Land, cling, carry,
Take the pollen, do not tarry.

A flower will tremble as the bee lands there.
Pollen will cling to her fuzzy hair.
Gently they lift, and the pollen goes,
Carried on fuzz and wings and toes.

Land, cling, carry,
Share the pollen, do not tarry.

Picture lines for an illustrator: Page 1: a bee drops onto a yellow flower, a butterfly rests on a flat orange flower with wings folded, a hummingbird hovers by a red tube flower, a bat swoops toward a pale flower under a moon. Page 2: a close view of a bee's furry back with yellow dust; a flower stem bends slightly. Page 3: the bee lifts off with pollen on its legs, a butterfly's feet showing pollen dust.

## 03-b. A Dusty Visitor

A dusty visitor came to call,
Fuzzy and yellow, round and small.
It dipped in a flower, deep and sweet,
Then buzzed away, so quick, so neat.

Gold on its legs and gold on its back,
It left behind a sprinkle track.

The next flower waited, sticky and bright.
The visitor landed, soft and light,
And dusted the stigma with gold, just right.

Picture lines for an illustrator: Page 1: a fuzzy yellow bumblebee at the front door of a garden gate made of leaves (a play on "came to call"), flowers behind. Page 2: the bee in flight with yellow dots on its legs and back, a trail of yellow specks. Page 3: the bee on a second flower with a sticky pale stigma in the center, dust falling onto it.
