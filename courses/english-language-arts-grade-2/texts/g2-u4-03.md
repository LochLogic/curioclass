## The Hummingbird and the Fire

One dry day, a great fire began in the forest. Flames ran along the ground and up the trees. Smoke rose in a gray cloud.

All the animals ran. The deer ran, and the monkeys ran, and the jaguar ran. They ran until they reached the cool river. There they stood and watched the fire grow. Nobody knew what to do.

Only one small animal did not stand still. It was the hummingbird, no bigger than a thumb.

The hummingbird flew to the river. She dipped her tiny beak in the water and took one drop. Then she zipped over the fire and let the drop fall. Back she flew to the river. Back she went to the fire. Again and again she went.

The jaguar watched her. "Little one," he called, "what are you doing? A drop of water is nothing. You can't put out a fire."

"I know," said the hummingbird. "But I am doing my part."

She took another drop. She flew to the fire. She came back.

The monkeys stopped chattering. The deer lifted their heads. "She is so small," whispered a monkey, "and she is trying."

Then the monkey ran to the river and scooped up water in her hands. The deer filled their mouths and hurried to the fire. The jaguar filled his big paws. The birds gathered water in their beaks.

Drop by drop, and then splash by splash, the water fell. The flames grew smaller. At last the fire went out, and the forest was wet and quiet.

The hummingbird perched on a branch and caught her breath. "See?" she said. "Everyone has a part to do."

## Where this story comes from

This story is told in many places, and nobody has shown where it began. Here is what we found. Wangari Maathai, a Kenyan woman who worked to plant trees, took a hummingbird as her symbol and said, "I will be a hummingbird; I will do the best I can." A film adapted from a folk tale is told in Hatxa Kuĩ, a language of the Amazon forest in Brazil, and it was shared by a language-heritage group at a university in Spain. People tell their own versions in other places too. Curio Class wrote the words you read here, from the shared idea of the tale, not from any one person's telling.
