# Mary Anning Looks Closely

[page 1]
## Growing Up by the Sea

Mary Anning was born in 1799 in Lyme Regis, a town on the coast of England. Tall cliffs rise beside its beach. Those cliffs are full of fossils.

Mary's father, Richard, built furniture. He also hunted for fossils. When Mary was about five, he began taking her along. The family sold fossils to visitors. They did not have much money.

When Mary was eleven, her father died. Mary kept on collecting, and the family kept on selling what she found.

## Learning to Look

Mary looked for fossils on the beach below the cliffs. Her father showed her how to look for them. He showed her how to clean the ones they found. A fossil can be small and hard to see. Mary looked closely at the stones on the beach. She learned this on the beach with her father.

[page 2]
## Big Finds

In 1811, Mary's brother, Joseph, found a strange skull in the cliff. Mary found and dug out the rest of the skeleton. It belonged to a sea reptile that lived very long ago. Scientists later named it an ichthyosaur, which means "fish lizard."

Mary kept looking. In 1823, she found a nearly whole skeleton of another sea reptile, a plesiosaur. In 1828, she found the skeleton of a flying reptile. It was the first of its kind found outside Germany.

Mary studied each fossil with care. She cleaned the bones. She made drawings of them.

## Sharing What She Knew

Mary became a fossil collector and dealer. Visitors came to Lyme Regis to buy fossils, and people who knew fossils wanted her advice. Her fossils and her knowledge helped scientists learn about animals of very long ago.

But the Geological Society of London, a club for scientists, did not let women join. Scientists did not always put Mary's name in their papers.

Mary kept finding fossils for the rest of her life. Today there is a museum in Lyme Regis that tells her story, and people remember her as a careful fossil hunter who looked closely.
