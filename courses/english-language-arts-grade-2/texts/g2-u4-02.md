## The Ant and the Grasshopper

All summer long, a grasshopper sang. She sang in the morning and she sang at night. She did not store one crumb of food.

Then the cold north wind came. The grasshopper was hungry and there was nothing to eat. She walked to the house of her neighbor, the ant, and knocked.

"Please," she said, "I have a request. May I borrow a little grain? It will keep me going until spring. I promise to repay you before the end of summer, with extra on top. I give you my word as an animal."

Now, the ant did not like to lend things. The narrator says this was the smallest of her faults. She looked at the grasshopper.

"What were you doing all summer?" asked the ant.

"I was singing," said the grasshopper. "Night and day, for anyone who came by."

"You were singing?" said the ant. "Well, I am glad to hear it. Now you can dance."

And the ant shut her door.

## Where this fable comes from

This fable was told in French verse by Jean de La Fontaine, who lived in France more than three hundred years ago. He took his ideas from older fables credited to Aesop. This telling follows the English translation by Elizur Wright, and Curio Class wrote the words you read here.
