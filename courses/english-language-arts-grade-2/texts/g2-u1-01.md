# Mira's New Classroom

## Chapter 1. The Doorway

It was the first Monday in January. Mira stood in the hallway of Maple Street School with a backpack strap in each hand. In front of her was a wooden door. A paper snowflake was taped to its window. Behind the door she could hear voices, and a chair scraping, and somebody laughing.

Mira had a new coat, a new pencil case and a new classroom. She did not have one friend in it yet.

She looked at the snowflake. She noticed that one of its points was cut a little crooked. Somebody in there had made that snowflake. Somebody had hung it up. And all those somebodies knew one another's names.

"I wonder what it is like inside," Mira whispered. She wondered who would sit next to her. She wondered if the desks were big or small. Most of all she wondered if she would know what to do.

The school helper who had walked her down the hall was named Mr. Davis. He leaned on the wall and smiled.

"This is Room 6," he said. "Ms. Park is expecting you. You can enter whenever you are ready."

"Thank you," said Mira. She did not move.

Mr. Davis did not hurry her. He looked at the ceiling lights as if they were very interesting.

Mira put her hand on the door. Then she took it away. Then she put it back.

She took a breath. "One," she said, very quietly. "Two. Three."

She pushed. The door swung open, and warm air and noise rushed out to meet her.

She grips her backpack straps with both hands. Mr. Davis leans on the wall a few steps behind her, smiling. Through the glass, blurry shapes of children at desks.]

## Chapter 2. Welcome

The room went quiet. It was not an unkind quiet. It was a curious quiet, the kind that happens when everyone wants to say hello at once and nobody knows who should go first.

A tall teacher with silver glasses walked to the door.

"Good morning, Mira," she said. "I am Ms. Park. Welcome to our classroom. We are glad you are here."

Mira's voice came out very small. "Good morning."

"That is a fine start," said Ms. Park. She turned to the class. "Everyone, this is Mira. She is joining our class today."

A few children waved. A boy at the back gave a thumbs-up so large that his whole arm went up with it.

"Mira, in this classroom, everyone has a partner," said Ms. Park. "A partner is someone you work with and talk with. Ada, would you stand up?"

A girl with a long braid stood up at a desk by the window. She had ink on her thumb and a wide smile.

"Hi," said Ada. "You can sit by me. The radiator is under the window, so it is the warmest desk in the room."

Mira walked across the room. It felt like the longest walk of her life. Her boots squeaked on the floor.

On the desk was a small card. It said MIRA in thick green marker, with a star over the i.

"I made it this morning," said Ada. "In case you came today."

"Thank you," said Mira. She slid into the seat and set her pencil case down very straight.

Mira did not say much more that morning. But she noticed something. Ada had made a name card for her before they had even met.

[picture: The classroom, seen from the door. Ms. Park, tall with silver glasses, holds the door open. Ada stands at a desk beside a tall window with a radiator below it. On the desk is a name card: MIRA in green marker, a star over the i. Children at other desks look up; the boy at the back gives a big thumbs-up. Snow can be seen outside the window.]

## Chapter 3. The Big Blue Chart

On the wall of Room 6 hung a big blue chart. It had every child's name on it, and a job beside each name: Plant Helper, Window Opener, Line Leader, Paper Passer.

Beside the chart hung a smaller sign. It said TURN AND TALK. Under it, in Ms. Park's neat writing: Tell your partner one thing. Listen to one thing. Then stop.

At Maple Street School, everything seemed to have a name Mira did not know yet. There was the Quiet Signal, when Ms. Park raised two fingers and the room went silent. There was Reading Buddies, and Morning Share, and a rule about which pencil sharpener to use.

Everybody else knew all of it already. That was the challenge. It was like walking into a game that had started without her.

At nine fifteen, Ms. Park finished a read-aloud. "Turn and talk," she said. "What did you notice about the main character?"

All around the room, chairs swung toward partners. The room filled with voices.

Mira folded her hands on her desk. She stared at her pencil case.

Ada turned to her. "Do you want to go first?"

"I do not know how," Mira said quietly. "I will just watch."

Ada thought for a moment. "It is easy," she said. "You tell me one thing. I tell you one thing. Then we are done."

"I will just watch," Mira said again.

"Okay," said Ada. "You can watch first."

So Ada talked, and Mira listened, and Mira nodded in the right places. Her hands stayed folded the whole time.

At the end of the day, Mira walked to the bus with her hands in her pockets. She said the words to herself in time with her steps. I will just watch. I will just watch.

[picture: A desk by the window. Ada leans toward Mira and talks, with a hand raised mid-gesture. Mira sits very straight, hands folded tight, eyes on her pencil case. Behind them on the wall hangs the big blue job chart and the TURN AND TALK sign. All around, other pairs lean toward one another, mouths open in talk.]

## Chapter 4. The Long Hall

On Tuesday, Ms. Park asked Ada to show Mira around the school before lunch.

"Come on," said Ada. "I will show you everything."

First came the long hall. It had a shiny floor that squeaked under shoes and ran past door after door. Every door had a name on it. Along one wall stood rows of wooden cubbies, each marked with a name, and each stuffed with boots, hats and rolled-up scarves. Their voices echoed.

"This is the gym," said Ada, pulling a heavy door open. Mira smelled rubber and dust. A line of orange cones stood in the middle of the floor, and a single basketball rolled slowly across it, all by itself.

Next came the lunchroom, with long tables and chairs pushed in tight. Then the library.

The library was Mira's favorite place at once. It had a round blue rug in the middle and shelves as tall as Mr. Davis. A fish tank bubbled in the corner, and one orange fish hung in the water, as if it were also thinking.

"I like the library," Mira said. It was the longest thing she had said all week.

"Me too," said Ada. "That fish is called Pancake."

At the end of the tour, they went out a side door into the yard. Snow lay along the edges of the fence. There was a big oak tree with no leaves, and a red climbing frame, and a painted square on the ground for hopscotch.

"That is the whole school," said Ada. "What do you think?"

Mira looked back at the long hall, the heavy gym door, the round blue rug in the library, and the tall oak tree.

"It is bigger than I thought," she said. "But it is not as scary."

## Chapter 5. The Card Game

On Wednesday, Mira carried her lunch to the end of a long table. She sat down and opened her bag very slowly. She ate one grape. She ate another.

Across the lunchroom, Ada was at the other end of the table, deep in a talk with two girls about a school play. Mira did not want to interrupt.

A boy with a striped hat slid onto the bench across from her.

"Hi. I am Leo," he said. "I am the one who did the thumbs-up on Monday."

Mira almost smiled. "I remember."

"After lunch we play a card game with animal cards," said Leo. "It is easy. You match the animals. Do you want to come play? I invite you."

Mira's stomach did a flip. She wanted to say yes. But the yes was stuck behind a worry. What if she did not know the rules? What if everyone stopped and looked at her?

"No, thank you," she said. "I am fine."

"Okay," said Leo. "If you change your mind, we are by the big window."

He picked up his tray and walked away.

Mira twisted her napkin around her finger. She twisted it the other way. She watched Leo's table fill up with children and cards and laughing. One of the cards fell on the floor, and a girl shouted, "Walrus!" and everyone cheered.

Mira did not go over. She kept on twisting her napkin until it was a tight little rope.

[picture: A lunchroom table. Mira sits alone at the end, her lunch bag twisted in both hands and a napkin wound around one finger. Her face says "no" but her feet, under the bench, point toward the table by the big window, where Leo and other children bend over animal cards, laughing.]

## Chapter 6. Ada's Empty Desk

On Thursday morning, the desk by the window was empty. The name card still said MIRA. The other chair had nobody in it.

"Ada is home with a cold," said Ms. Park. "She will be back soon."

Mira's stomach dropped. Ada was her partner. Ada knew how things worked. Without Ada, who would tell her what to do?

At nine fifteen, Ms. Park said, "Turn and talk. What is your favorite thing about winter?"

Chairs swung round. Voices filled the room. Everybody had somebody.

Mira's hands started to fold. I will just watch, she thought. It was the same old thought, and it was ready.

Then she looked at the name card with the green star. Somebody had made that card before she had even come. Somebody had offered her a warm desk.

Mira hesitated. She breathed in. One. Two. Three.

She stood up. She walked across the room, past the blue chart and the plant shelf, to the table where Leo sat.

"Leo?" Her voice wobbled. "Can I be your partner for turn and talk?"

Leo grinned. "Yes! Sit down. My partner is away too."

Mira sat. For a moment her mind was blank. Then she said, "I like winter because the snow makes everything quiet. And hot chocolate."

"I like sledding," said Leo. "Snow makes everything fast."

They both laughed.

Across the room, Ms. Park looked over her silver glasses and smiled, and said nothing at all.

At lunch, when Leo asked again about the animal cards, Mira said, "Yes. I accept." She learned the rules in two minutes. She won her first round with a walrus.

[picture: Mira halfway across the classroom, walking away from the empty desk with the green-starred name card. Her chin is up, her hands open at her sides. Ahead, Leo waves at his table. Behind her, the empty chair and name card are small in the window light. Ms. Park, in the background, smiles over her silver glasses.]

## Chapter 7. Not Now

On Friday, Ada came back with a red nose and a pocket full of tissues.

Ms. Park was handing out paper. "Our class is making the Maple Street Guide," she said. "It will help anyone who joins us later. Each pair makes one page."

Mira had an idea at once. She knew the long hall now. She could draw a map of it. She grabbed a pencil.

"Ada," she said. "I can draw the hall map. Do you want to work on it with me?"

Ada was bent over a page of subtraction, tapping her pencil on her chin. She did not look up.

"Not now," she said. "Maybe after I finish this page."

Mira felt her face go hot. Not now. It sounded like no. It sounded exactly like no.

She took a step back. She sat down at the desk with the paper in front of her. Her hands began to fold on their own.

I will just watch, she thought.

Then she remembered the chart on the wall by the door. It was called Ask a Question. It said: If you do not understand, say, "Do you mean ...?"

Mira unfolded her hands. She walked back to Ada's desk and spoke in a steady voice.

"Ada, when you said 'not now,' did you mean no?"

Ada looked up, surprised. "No! I meant after. I missed a whole day of subtraction and I have to finish this page first. Then yes. I want to do the guide with you."

Mira let out a breath she had been holding since the word now.

"Okay," she said. "I will start the map. You can add the library when you are done."

"And Pancake the fish," said Ada. "Do not forget Pancake."

[picture: Ada bent over a page of subtraction, pencil tapping her chin, eyes on the paper. Mira stands beside her desk with a blank sheet and a pencil, her shoulders a little lowered. Behind them, on the wall, a poster: ASK A QUESTION, with the words "Do you mean ...?" in big letters.]

## Chapter 8. The Newest Pupil

The snow melted. The oak tree grew small green buds. The paper snowflakes came down from the windows and paper flowers went up in their place.

On the first Monday in March, Mira was hanging up her coat when she heard a sound in the hall. It was the sound of a person standing very still.

She looked out. A boy in a big blue scarf stood outside Room 6, holding both straps of his backpack. He was looking at the paper flowers in the window.

Mira knew that look. She knew it very well.

She did not wait for Ms. Park. She walked to the door and opened it.

"Hi," she said. "I am Mira. Welcome to our classroom."

The boy blinked. "I am Tomas. I do not know what to do."

"That is okay," said Mira. "Nobody does at first. Come in. I will be your partner today."

She took him past the blue chart. She showed him the TURN AND TALK sign, and the Quiet Signal, and the shelf with the plants. She showed him the Maple Street Guide, with its map of the long hall and a drawing of an orange fish named Pancake.

"Did you make this?" asked Tomas.

"Ada and I did," said Mira. "It is for anyone new. Now it is for you."

At nine fifteen, Ms. Park said, "Turn and talk."

Chairs swung round. Tomas looked at Mira.

"You tell me one thing," said Mira. "I tell you one thing. Then we are done."

Tomas smiled for the first time. "You go first."

Mira laughed. She turned her chair toward him.

On a table lies the Maple Street Guide, open to a map of the long hall and a drawing of an orange fish.]
