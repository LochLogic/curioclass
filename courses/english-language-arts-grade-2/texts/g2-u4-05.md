## 05-a Flowers Through the Seasons

Pollinators need flowers all year long. That is why people should plant flowers that bloom in every season.

A pollinator is an animal that carries pollen from one flower to another. Bees, butterflies, moths, bats and hummingbirds are all pollinators. They visit flowers to drink nectar, a sweet liquid, and some also eat pollen. When they move from flower to flower, they help plants make seeds.

Here is the first reason to plant for every season. Bees wake up in early spring. They are hungry, and few flowers are open yet. If early flowers bloom, more bees can eat. Experts say that early spring food leads to more bees later in the year.

Here is the second reason. Flowers that bloom in fall give butterflies and other pollinators food before the cold comes. Without them, the food runs out too soon.

Also, plant the same flower in a clump. A clump of one kind is easier for a pollinator to find than one flower alone.

So a class pot, a school bed or a park with flowers in every season can keep pollinators fed.

## 05-b Room Without Sprays

People should use fewer sprays in places where pollinators live. A spray that kills pests is called a pesticide. Many pesticides do not know the difference between a pest and a pollinator.

There are three reasons to spray less. First, some sprays can kill bees right away. Second, a small amount can hurt a bee in other ways. It might help the bee get sick or lose its way, so it cannot gather food well. Third, the spray can also kill other insects. Songbirds need those insects to eat, so the harm spreads.

Garden experts give some ideas for what to do instead. First, find out what the pest is. Then change what drew the pest to the plant. Also, accept a few holes in the leaves. A few holes usually mean that the garden is healthy, because caterpillars eat leaves and they grow up into butterflies and moths.

If a grown-up must use a spray, they should read the label and never spray flowers that are open. Children should never use a spray.

When people use fewer sprays, they protect the bees, butterflies and birds that visit the flowers.

## 05-c A Place to Live

Every animal needs a place to live. A place where an animal finds what it needs is its habitat. People can help pollinators by giving them a habitat with four things: food, water, shelter and a place to nest.

Pollinators need food. Flowers give them nectar and pollen. Flowers that bloom from spring until fall keep them fed through the year.

They also need water. A shallow dish with some stones in it gives them a safe place to land and sip. The stones keep small insects from drowning. Changing the water every few days keeps mosquitoes from growing in it.

Many bees need nesting places. Some bees nest in hollow stems, and some nest in bare ground that nobody digs up. So people can leave a few dry stems standing and a patch of soil alone.

Pollinators also need shelter. Leaves and dead wood give insects cover from the cold winter. People who rake up every leaf can take away the places insects hide. That is why experts say, "Less is more," when it is time to clean up in fall.

A habitat that gives all four things helps pollinators stay.

## 05-d Planning a Flower Pot

You can grow flowers for pollinators in a pot. First, choose the pot. It needs holes in the bottom so that extra water can drain out. A big pot is better than a small one.

Next, pick the seeds. Choose wildflower seeds from a plant that is native to your area. Native plants suit the weather, the soil and the pollinators nearby. A grown-up can ask a garden shop or a county extension office for help. Pick a mix of flowers only, with no grass, for a pot.

Then add a layer of stones to the bottom, and fill the pot with potting soil. Stop a little below the rim.

After that, scatter the seeds on top. The seeds are tiny, so spread them thinly. Cover them with a thin layer of soil, about as deep as your fingernail is wide. Water the pot gently, so the seeds do not wash away.

Last, put the pot in a sunny, warm spot. Water it regularly so the soil does not dry out, but do not drown it. Then wait. Some wildflowers bloom in the first year, and some wait until the second.

When the flowers open, watch from a little way off. Pollinators may come to feed.
