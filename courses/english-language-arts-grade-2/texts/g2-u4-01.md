## The Ant and the Grasshopper

It was a bright summer day. A grasshopper sat on a green stalk, singing and dancing in the sun.

An ant came by, pulling a heavy grain of corn. She was working hard.

"Come and sing with me!" called the grasshopper. "Stop and chat!"

"I can't stop," said the ant. "I am storing food for the winter. I think you should do the same."

"Winter?" said the grasshopper. "Winter is far away. We have plenty of food right now. Why should I worry?"

The ant did not argue. She kept on pulling. All through the summer, the ants gathered grain and carried it down into their nest. All through the summer, the grasshopper sang.

Then the season changed. The leaves fell. The wind turned cold, and the fields were bare. There was no food left to find. The grasshopper's song grew quiet, and her stomach was empty.

One cold morning she looked over at the ants' nest. The ants were opening their store. They were handing out the grain they had saved, one piece at a time.

The grasshopper crept closer. "Please," she said in a small voice. "May I have a few grains? I have nothing to eat."

The ant looked at her. "In summer we gathered, and you sang," she said. "I am sorry. There is only enough for us."

The grasshopper did not answer. She watched the ants carry the grain inside. Then she wrapped her wings around herself to keep warm.

## Where this fable comes from

Fables like this one have been told for a very long time and are credited to Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece. This telling follows the version printed in Joseph Jacobs' book The Fables of Aesop (1894). Curio Class wrote the words you read here.
