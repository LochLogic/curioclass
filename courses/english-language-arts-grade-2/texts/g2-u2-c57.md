## Text

**Page 1, Keeping a Shape**

Paragraph 1: A shell can leave a hollow shape in sediment. Sediment is material such as mud or sand. If the shell dissolves, the hollow shape may remain. It is a **mold**. Other material can fill that mold and harden into a **cast**. The cast is a copy of the shell's shape. This is one way fossils can form.

Diagram (to draw): a shell buried in sediment, then an empty shell-shaped hollow, then material filling it. Printed beneath it: "The middle picture shows the mold. The last picture shows the cast." No numerical ages.

**Page 2, Recording a Find**

Paragraph 2: People collect fossils to study them. A fossil collector looks closely at a find. The collector writes its location in a notebook. Another bag in the box is unmarked, so nobody can tell where its find was taken. The collector adds a label before putting each find in a bag. These notes help keep the find connected with its place.

**Page 3, Reading a Trace**

Paragraph 3: A fossil can also record an action. A footprint may leave a trace in soft sediment. If that trace is preserved, it can become a fossil. A track tells us where an animal stepped. A track alone may not tell us exactly which animal made it.

**Page 4, Glossary and Index**

Glossary: cast, a copy of a shape made when material fills a mold; location, the place where something is; trace, a sign left by an action.

Index: cast 1; collector 2; footprint 3; mold 1; notebook 2.

Print-menu cards: a shell icon labelled Shapes, page 1; a bag icon labelled Find Records, page 2; a footprint icon labelled Traces, page 3.
