# Section 4.2 opener: tales told in many places (days 123 to 125)

## What the section does

For three days the class reads a tale that is told in many places and an invented tale for reading in parts. Children say who told a tale as the book says it, and nothing more, and tell apart what two animals in a tale think. On day 123 the cycle 20 written check closes the plural work.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short opening repair, the day's reading, a model, talk, pair work and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds a repair, work on the cycle's spelling pattern, a reader, a rotation of stations with the oral card, and a close. Every exit is one card you read aloud twice and one ask on paper or aloud. A child outside the group heard is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Say aloud that every practice card is made up.

## The ideas at an adult's level

- **Say only what the book says.** A tale can be told in many places. If the book says who told it, say that. If nobody knows where it began, say that too. Do not guess an origin.
- **The second tale is made up.** It claims no culture. Say so aloud: "a story we made up".
- **Different characters think different things.** Each animal says what it wants in its own words. A child who gives two animals the same view has not yet told them apart.
- **Voices help the listener.** A different voice for each speaker shows who is talking and what that animal wants.

## The errors in plain words

- Different characters are given the same viewpoint (C).
- The oral-reading answer does not match the passage (AB).
- Phrasing hides the meaning of a sentence (AC).
- A misread word is left after checking (AD).
- A printed word is read incorrectly (AE).

Show the right answer beside the wrong one, say the link together, and let the child try again. Repairs are five minutes at the start of the next day.

## Marking

One scale for every look-for: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record it on the record sheet. Nothing is scored beyond the exits and the oral and written cycle checks; they tell you what to repair.
