# Day 46: The topic and what each paragraph says

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 45's exit marks show who needs the repair. Mark today's six (group 4 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Hang the wall chart with its five boxes empty. Give each pair article B, a chart copy and a pencil. Write the six word cards. Set three stations: tiles, the twelve sort cards, a reread of The Bell on the Post.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read twice: "First, Leo found a smooth stone by the gate. He brushed it clean. He thought, It looks like a bean. He felt glad." Then read two endings. A: "In the end, Leo kept the stone in his pocket to show his class." B: "A bus drove past the school." Ask: "Which ending finishes what Leo did?" Children show one finger for A or two for B, then tell a partner why. Hear two children. Expect: A, because it finishes what Leo did with the stone; B is about something else (error P).

#### 6 min. Words

Review: topic, what a paragraph is about (unit 1, day 16), and fossil and sediment (unit 2, day 37). New today: topic for a whole article, focus, bury and layer. Say each twice and have the class say it back.

- Topic: what a whole article is about, the idea that every paragraph tells about.
- Focus: what one paragraph says. A paragraph has one focus.
- Bury: to cover something so it is hidden. Lay a paper bone under a sheet.
- Layer: one flat covering lying on another. Stack three sheets.

Partners each say one sentence: "Sand can bury ___." "A ___ of mud lay on top." Time in these articles is only "very long ago", never a number.

#### 10 min. Read aloud: article B

Read How a Bone Becomes a Fossil aloud (226 words, about 2 minutes), pointing to each diagram box as you reach it. Say plainly and briefly that in step 1 an animal dies near a river: it is a fact about how fossils start. Then say: "Fossils are protected. We never dig for them or take them, at a park, in a forest or at school. If we find one, we leave it, and we tell a ranger or a teacher." Partners then tell the five steps in order, one box each, using the diagram (4 minutes). Hear two pairs (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Model: topic and paragraph focus

Hang the wall chart: one wide Topic box, and under it four Focus boxes joined to it by arrows. Say: "A focus is what one paragraph says. The topic is what all of them tell together." Reread Buried Fast (47 words). Think aloud: "What is this whole paragraph about? Not only hungry animals, which is one detail. Mud and sand cover the bones." Write in box 1: "Mud covers the bones." Do Minerals Move In the same way and write in box 2: "Minerals fill the bone." Then name the topic from the two boxes, the title and the line "This article tells the steps in order": "How a bone becomes a fossil." Write it in the Topic box.

Check each focus against the topic with the class: "Does mud covering the bones fit inside how a bone becomes a fossil? Yes." Say the rule: a focus fits inside the topic, and the topic is bigger than any one focus. Write "hungry animals" beside box 1 and cross it out: it is one detail, not the focus.

#### 12 min. Pairs do the other two

Pairs read Rock Wears Away and Uncovered With Care turn about (about 75 words, 2 minutes). Then each pair says the focus of each in a few words and writes or draws it in boxes 3 and 4 on their chart copy (6 minutes). Last, they ask of each box, "Does this fit inside how a bone becomes a fossil?" and tick it (2 minutes). Walk among pairs, about 20 seconds each. Expect: "Wind, rain and rivers wear the rock away." and "Scientists uncover the fossil with care." Watch for error C: one detail, such as a hiker or a brush, named as the focus or the topic. Ask: "Is that the whole paragraph, or one sentence in it?"

#### 7 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "How do the paragraph focuses fit the topic?" Turn: each child tells a partner, "The topic is ___; this paragraph is about ___" (1 minute). Pair: two pairs join and each says one focus that fits (2 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes); after each, one child links or asks. Use the wait signal. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes on side 1: "The topic is ___. One focus is ___." in a few words. A child may draw first and say the words for you to scribe.

Check: the topic names the whole article; the focus names one paragraph.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X46 aloud twice. Ask the first question, item s02-X46, of everyone: children tell a partner. Then ask item s02-X46b the same way. The six then answer both to you, about 25 seconds each (2.5 minutes). Shown means a topic wider than both paragraphs and a focus for paragraph 2 taken from the card. Shown with help means the right answers after one prompt: "What do both paragraphs tell about?" Not yet seen means one paragraph's detail given as the topic (error C), or no answer. Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Show six word cards one at a time: gold, fold, kind, bind, host, ghost. Children say the chunk first, then the word. If a vowel is read short, put the chunk on the sound-spelling-map and blend again (error Y). Review: where's, there's and it's are read whole (day 43).

#### 15 min. Transfer to a fresh passage

Review: hello and weird, mapped on day 43; put a finger on the starred part of each. Two words are new today and are mapped before anyone reads. Told: /t/ then old with the long o, so told is read like gold. Behind: be, then hind, with ind saying long i, as in Find the Gold on day 44. Put both on the word wall (3 minutes). Say the title yourself; children do not read it. Read the first sentence aloud and the class echoes it (1 minute). Pairs read the whole passage turn about, a sentence each, and then again smoothly (45 words, 4 minutes). Check meaning after decoding (2 minutes). Ask: "What was tied to the post?" Expect: a gold bell. Ask: "What did Fran tell Kip?" Expect: not to mind the weird post, because it is just for the bell. Dictate: "A gold bell was tied to the post." Say it twice. Children write with tiles or pencil and check each word against the sound-spelling-map (4 minutes).

Check: gold and post have the right chunk.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). All four lines are used. Each says the vowel sound in each word. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build gold, told, kind and post, then change one letter (gold to fold, kind to bind). Sort: the twelve cycle 8 cards under ild, old, ind and ost, a partner naming a word and the child pointing to its pile. Reread: pairs read The Bell on the Post twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

Check: each child in a station reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which word did you confirm after you decoded it?" Expect: any word, and how the sentence told them it fit.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the chart on the board and let pairs draw five boxes on folded paper. One copy of article B: read each paragraph aloud twice and let pairs point to it. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: the parent reads the five steps from the day's page. No cards: write each word on torn paper.
**Skip.** The stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. What is each paragraph about?** Read article B aloud, one paragraph at a time. Your child says what the paragraph tells in a few words.
  **Say.** "What is this paragraph about?" Expect: one idea, such as mud and sand cover the bones.
- **10 min. The topic.** Together look at the four short answers. Your child says what the whole article is about.
  **Say.** "What do all four tell about?" Expect: how a bone becomes a fossil.
- **5 min. Read.** Show gold, kind, host and told on cards. Your child reads each and says the vowel's name.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X46]] [[s02-X46b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs do only one paragraph; keep the model, the sentence and the exit. Longer: a child invents a fifth paragraph that would not fit the topic and a partner says why.

### Words and supports

Topic, focus, bury, layer, sediment. Frames: "The topic is ___." "This paragraph is about ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.
