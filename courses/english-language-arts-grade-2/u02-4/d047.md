# Day 47: Subheadings, bold print and captions

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 46's exit marks show whether error C needs the repair. Mark today's six (group 5 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Write the numbers 1, 2 and 3 on the board. Give each pair article B, three route cards and a pencil. Set three stations: tiles, the twelve sort cards, a reread of Find the Gold. Keep the written check sheets face down until the foundations block. Keys: key-sort-cards-cycle-8.md, key-route-cards-day-47.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read twice: "Title: Hard Parts That Last. Paragraph 1: A bone can turn hard like stone. Paragraph 2: A tooth can stay in rock." Say: "Someone might say the topic is bones. Does bones fit paragraph 2?" Expect: no, a tooth is not a bone. Say: "Read the title and the first lines, name the topic, then check each paragraph fits it." Children tell a partner a topic that fits both. Expect: hard parts of animals that last in rock. Hear two (error C).

#### 10 min. Reread article B

Review: caption, the line under a picture that tells what it shows (unit 1, day 22); subheading and bold print (new in grade 2, taught today). Reread article B aloud (226 words, 2 minutes) while children follow the print with a finger. Name three features and what each is for. A subheading tells what a part is about. Bold print marks a key word the article explains right there. A caption tells what a picture shows. Children point on command: "a subheading", "a bold word", "the caption" (3 minutes), then each pair counts the subheadings, five, and finds the two bold words, layer and minerals (3 minutes).

#### 12 min. Model: the route

Say: "I do not have to read every part to find a fact. I choose the feature that matches my question." Ask: "What does the water carry into the bone?" Walk a finger on the wall copy: the subheading Minerals Move In, because the question is about water and the bone; the bold word minerals; the sentence beside it, "The water carries tiny bits of minerals." Say the route aloud: subheading, bold word, sentence (5 minutes).

Ask: "Which step is last?" Walk: the subheading Uncovered With Care, whose first word is Last; then the caption, "Five steps from living animal to fossil", and box 5 of the diagram. Say what each helps you find: the subheading finds the part, the bold word finds a key word and its meaning, the caption finds what a picture shows (3 minutes). Then show the slow way: start at Five Steps and read every part until the answer. Ask: "Which way took fewer parts?" Expect: the subheading way (2 minutes). Say the limit: a feature helps when it matches the question. A question about a word starts with the bold word; a question about a picture starts with the caption; a question about a part starts with the subheading. Children say each match back to a partner (2 minutes).

#### 12 min. Pairs practise

Pairs take the three route cards in turn: a question and an empty route. One child walks a finger on the print and says the route aloud; the partner checks the answer in the print and says "yes" or "which part do you mean?" Swap roles on each card (9 minutes). Walk among pairs, about 20 seconds each. Hear three pairs say their route (3 minutes). Watch for error E, a pair that reads part after part instead of using a subheading or the caption.

#### 8 min. Mark a printed page

On side 1 of the student page, read the question aloud twice: "What does the sediment keep away from the bones?" Each child circles the subheading that leads to the answer, boxes the bold word in that part, and underlines the caption words that say the diagram shows the steps in order. Then they say the answer to a partner. Expect: circle Buried Fast, box layer, underline "The arrows show the order", answer wind, rain and hungry animals. Walk among children.

Check: the circled subheading leads to the part that holds the answer.

#### 8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "Which feature found the answer fastest?" Turn: each child tells a partner, "The ___ found it fastest because ___" (2 minutes). Pair: two pairs join and compare (2 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Use the wait signal. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X47 aloud twice (40 seconds). Number 1 on the board is the subheading, 2 is the bold print, 3 is the caption. Ask item s02-X47, then s02-X47b, then s02-X47c. After each ask, children tell a partner. The six then answer all three to you, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Shown means the subheading A Track, bold print and the caption, each with a word of reason. Shown with help means the right features after one prompt: "What does the subheading say?" Not yet seen means Shell Shapes or a search of every part (error E). Mark the record sheet.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Give out the sheets, face down, then turn them over. Say: "This is to see what you know. I will not help with the words." Read each sentence twice and say the missing word; children write it in the gap (4 minutes). The four words were not used in any lesson this cycle. Then write the right spellings on the board. Partners swap sheets and tick each word that matches; children circle the chunk in each word (ind, old, ost) and keep the sheet (4 minutes). Collect the sheets (1 minute) and tag misses Y, a taught vowel correspondence written wrongly. The sheet is unscored. Then put the commonest miss beside the right spelling without naming a child, put the chunk on the sound-spelling-map and blend it (4 minutes). Children who finish early reread Find the Gold.

Check: each ticked word has the right chunk.

#### 5 min. Reader

Review: reread with a pause at a full stop and a rising voice at a question mark (unit 2, day 44). Pairs reread Find the Gold, a page each, then again smoothly. On page 4 the voice rises at "Why are you sad?"

#### 25 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, two rounds of 12 minutes and one minute to move. Round 1: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). All four lines are used. Each says the vowel sound in each word. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build bold, kind, post and gold, then change one letter. Sort: the twelve cycle 8 cards under ild, old, ind and ost. Reread: pairs read Find the Gold twice, the second time smoothly. Round 2: the groups move on; the six join one station they have not met.

Check: each child in a station reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

Dictate two words, unscored, and say each twice: behind, then told. Behind was met on days 44 and 46 (be, then hind), told on day 46. Children write with tiles or pencil from memory, then check against the word wall (3 minutes). Then day 43's six (group 1) sit with you and read the cycle 8 words from the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes), while the others partner-read the partner word card (bold, gold, hold, kind, mind, post, fold, ghost) and say the vowel in each. No new teaching.

### Other rooms

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs do two route cards, and the page is marked together; keep the model, the exit and the written check. Longer: a child writes a question for a partner that needs two features to answer.

### Words and supports

Subheading, bold print, caption, route, feature. Frames: "I will use the ___ because ___." "The subheading ___ leads to ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and underlining are answers. A child may pass any turn.

## Student page

### Side 1

**I can find a fact with features.**

**Buried Fast**

First, an animal dies near a river. Mud and sand cover its body. The sediment hides the bones. This helps the bones last, because it keeps away wind, rain and hungry animals. As more sediment piles on top, it makes a **layer** over the bones.

**Minerals Move In**

Next, water seeps down through the layers. The water carries tiny bits of **minerals**. A mineral is a hard material found in the ground. The bits of mineral fill the tiny spaces in the bone. Slowly, the bone turns hard, like stone. The bone keeps its shape.

Caption: Five steps from living animal to fossil. The arrows show the order.
