# Day 48: What the author wants to do

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 47's exit marks show whether error E needs the repair, and the written check sheets show the commonest Y miss. Mark today's six (group 1 of five, cycle 9) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Give each pair the three purpose cards, the three blurbs, articles A and B and a pencil. Set three stations: tiles, the ten cycle 9 sort cards, a reread of Find the Gold. Keys: key-blurbs-day-48.md, key-sort-cards-cycle-9.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Ask: "What might a hiker spot?" about article B, a fresh question. Show the slow way first: start at Five Steps and read part after part. Then walk the quick way on the wall copy: the question names a hiker, so the subheading Rock Wears Away, then the sentence "A hiker might spot a piece of it." Say: "I did not read all five parts." Children tell a partner which feature they used first. Expect: the subheading. Hear two (error E).

#### 8 min. Reread the opening of article B

New word: purpose, the reason an author writes. Review: explain and describe (unit 2, day 43). Read the part called Five Steps aloud (27 words): "A fossil bone does not form in a day. It forms in steps, over a very long time. This article tells the steps in order." Read the title again too. Ask: "What does the author want to tell us?" Pairs say it with the frame "The author wants to ___." Expect: how a bone becomes a fossil, step by step. Hear three pairs.

#### 10 min. Model: three purposes

Lay out the three purpose cards: to answer a question, to explain how, to describe what. Say what each is. To answer a question: the text asks a question and then tells the answer. To explain how: the text tells steps or reasons, in order, for how something happens or is done. To describe what: the text tells what something looks like or is like. Model with article B: "The author wants to explain how. The opening says the article tells the steps in order, and the diagram has five boxes joined by arrows." Point to both. Say: "I can point to words. A purpose needs words to point to." Then ask: "Could the purpose be to make us laugh?" Find no joke anywhere; write it down and cross it out. Say that a purpose with no words to point to is not a purpose the text supports (error D).

#### 10 min. Sort invented blurbs

Say: "These three blurbs are made up." Pairs sort the three cards to a purpose card and say the words that show it (about 56 words, 4 minutes). Walk among pairs. Hear each card from one pair (5 minutes). Expect: A explains how, because the steps go in order; B describes what, because it tells what the fox has and where it lives; C answers a question, because it asks and then tells.

Check: each pair points to words on the card.

#### 10 min. Pairs find the line

Pairs read the title and the first part of article A, What Is a Fossil? (about 35 words, 2 minutes), and decide which purpose it fits. They find the line that shows it and read it aloud (4 minutes). Hear three pairs (4 minutes). Expect: answer a question, because the title asks one and the first line says what a fossil is, or describe what, because it tells what fossils are and where they are found. Accept either when the pair points to a line. A pair that picks explain how has to point to steps in order, and there are none.

#### 8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "How do you know what the author wants to do?" Turn: each child tells a partner, "The author wants to ___; I know because ___" (2 minutes). Pair: two pairs join and compare (2 minutes). Link: "You said ___; I would add ___." Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Use the wait signal. A child may pass.

#### 4 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes on side 1: "The author wants to ___. I know because ___." in a few words, about article B or about one blurb. A child may draw and say the words for you to scribe.

Check: the purpose is named, and a word from the text is the reason.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the first card of item s02-X48 aloud twice and ask it of everyone; children tell a partner. The six answer to you, about 20 seconds each (2 minutes). Read the second card of item s02-X48b twice and ask it the same way; the six answer, about 10 seconds each (1 minute). Shown means explain how for the planting card and describe what for the pebble card. Shown with help means the right answers after one prompt: "What does the card say first?" Not yet seen means a purpose the card does not support (error D). Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Retrieval (foundations)

Review: dropping the silent e before an ending, hope to hoping (grade 1). Write wave and name, and have children say wave plus ing is waving and name plus ing is naming, with the e dropped. Then put the commonest spelling miss from yesterday's check beside the right spelling, without naming a child, and put the chunk on the sound-spelling-map (error Y).

#### 15 min. Teach: doubling before a vowel suffix

New today. Write hop and hope. Say: "Hop has a short o and ends in one consonant. Hope has a silent e and a long o." Add ing to both: hoping drops the e, and the o keeps its name. Hopping doubles the p, and the double p keeps the o short (error AB). Say the rule: a word with one short vowel and one consonant at the end doubles the consonant before an ending that starts with a vowel (3 minutes).

Model rub: r, u, b, a short u and one consonant, so rub plus ing is rubbing, with two b. Build it with tiles, then stop plus ed is stopped and big plus er is bigger. Children say each ending alone, then the whole word (error Z) (4 minutes). Say the limit: doubling needs all three things. Camp ends in two consonants, so it is camping. Rain has two vowels, so it is raining. Say each and show with tiles (3 minutes).

Two more words are mapped, never guessed from the picture or the first letter. Even: ev-en, the first e says its name and the second is a quick sound; star the second e. Finally: fi-nal-ly, the i says its name, the a is a quick sound, and the y says /ee/; star the a. Put both on the word wall (3 minutes). Last, children spell running, sitting and hoping with tiles and check each against the sound-spelling-map (2 minutes).

Check: a double letter where the rule says, and none in hoping.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Today only the first two lines are used, the grade 1 words and the cycle 8 words, each saying the vowel sound in each word. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build rub, hop, stop and big, then add the ending (rubbing, hopping, stopped, bigger) and hope to hoping. Sort: the ten cycle 9 cards under double the last letter, drop the e and just add the ending. Reread: pairs read Find the Gold twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

Check: each child in a station reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Why is it hopping and not hoping?" Expect: hop has a short o and one consonant, so the p doubles; hoping comes from hope, which has a silent e.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the three purposes on paper and read the blurbs aloud twice. One copy of article B: read the opening aloud twice. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: read the three made-up lines aloud. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The stations, the sort and the oral card.

- **10 min. Three reasons to write.** Read your child three made-up lines: "Wash the apples. Cut them in four." "The fox has a long tail and sharp ears." "How many legs does a spider have? Eight." Your child says what each author wants to do: answer a question, explain how or describe what.
  **Say.** "What does the author of the first line want to do?" Expect: explain how, because the steps go in order.
- **10 min. The opening.** Read article B's opening, Five Steps, aloud.
  **Say.** "What does the author want to do?" Expect: explain how, because it says it tells the steps in order.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say rubbing, hopping and bigger. Your child writes each and says why the last letter doubles.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X48]] [[s02-X48b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: sort two blurbs, not three, and skip the article A hunt; keep the model, the exit and the doubling lesson. Longer: a child writes one made-up line for each purpose and a partner names it.

### Words and supports

Purpose, explain, describe, double. Frames: "The author wants to ___." "I know because ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and sorting are answers. A child may pass any turn.
