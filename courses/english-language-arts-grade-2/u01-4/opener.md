# Section 1.4 opener: facts and definitions for a paragraph (days 16 to 20)

## What the section does

Days 16 to 20 turn the articles into writing. On day 16 children ask a new question of an article, then gather a fact, its source tag and the meaning of a hard word on a note slip. On day 17 they learn the parts of a short explaining paragraph (a topic sentence, facts, the meaning of a word and an ending) and put six strips in order. Days 18 and 19 are check days: children read a short new passage about reading partners, ask and answer questions about it, write a paragraph on day 18 and, after you give written feedback, revise and finish it on day 19. Day 20 is a respond day: you give the class one result by letter, teach four small groups and set a fresh retry. The paragraph is about reading partners in the passage, not about the child's own school or home.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes: a 60-minute topic block and a 60-minute foundations block. The topic block holds a short opening repair, one or two words, the reading, a model, partner work, a talk and a five-minute exit (days 16 and 17). Cycle 4 (igh, ie and y as long i) runs in the foundations block with the reader *A Light Up High*; day 20 ends with a 15-minute written check and a fresh response for the day 16 six. On the check days the topic block is the check (day 18: directions 15, questions on the passage 15, begin the paragraph 30; day 19: revise and finish 25, hand in 10, quiet reading 25). Every exit is one question you read aloud, and you hear the day's rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A fact is something the article says.** It is not an opinion. Every fact keeps a source tag: the name of the article it came from.
- **A definition is the article's meaning.** Tell what a word means from the sentence that explains it, not from what you already know.
- **A paragraph has four jobs.** A topic sentence says what is explained; facts match the source; a hard word is explained; an ending follows from the facts. Feedback is written on these four rows only.
- **Say "many" and "some" as the articles do.** No country is ranked or called strange.

## The errors, in plain words

- **D:** a first sentence that explains nothing ("Tables are nice"). Say what the whole paragraph explains, then write that.
- **E:** a meaning or an ending that contradicts or ignores the source. Reread the source sentence and say the meaning from it.
- **F:** a hard word used with no meaning. Find the sentence clue and copy its meaning beside the word.
- **G:** a paragraph that stops after the last fact. Say what the facts show, then write it as the ending.
- **A:** an answer that contradicts the text. Find the line that says it.
- **O, P, L, M, Q:** rushed reading, a word named from the picture, a short vowel read in place of the team, a team dropped in spelling. Read in phrases; cover the picture; map the team; compare with the word wall.
- **H to K:** the talk errors of section 1.2.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet. Days 18 and 19 are scored once, after class, on the four rows; feedback comes first and revision comes before scoring. A child who cannot write may tell the paragraph to you or an approved scribe; a scribe never supplies the words.
