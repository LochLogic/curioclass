# Day 17: The parts of a paragraph

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print a frame card for each child and six strips from 02-b for each pair; keep the four day 16 strips for acting out. Write the frame card large on the board. Print the reader A Light Up High. Today's six are the second group on the record sheet. Their oral card is all three lines: shelf, class, cloth, lunch, thank, chest; tree, feet, green, beach, reach, funny; light, night, high, right, pie, tried, my, fly, why.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 16's exit (error E), read its card again. Put a finger under the sentence that says what a cubby is and say the meaning from that sentence. Then do the same with "A library is a place where people borrow books and read." If few missed it, pairs read their volunteer slip to another pair and point to the sentence the meaning came from.

#### 4 min. Words

Introduce: to start a paragraph by telling its topic. Develop: to add facts that explain the topic. Conclude: to end a paragraph with what the facts show. Four children stand with the four strips from day 16 (topic, fact, fact, closing) in the wrong order; the class says who should move, and the strips go in order: introduce, develop, develop, conclude. Say that here conclude does not mean decide; it means end by saying what the facts show.

#### 5 min. Hook

Read these four facts about 02-a aloud, in this order, with no first sentence and no ending: "Pupils serve the lunch themselves. Pupils clean their classroom as a group. Each pupil has a part to do. Pupils clear up when the meal is over." Ask partners: "Is this a finished paragraph? What does a listener still not know?" Expect: what it is about, and how it ends. Take two answers.

#### 12 min. Model a paragraph

Say: "Today we build one paragraph about 02-a, Learning Together." Fill the large frame card in front of the class, saying why each line is there.

Topic: "Pupils in many Japanese schools help take care of their school." Fact: "Pupils serve the lunch and clear up when the meal is over (Learning Together)." Fact: "Pupils clean their classroom as a group, and each pupil has a part to do (Learning Together)." Meaning: "To cooperate is to work together to get something done." Review from day 13: that is the article's own meaning of cooperate. Ending: "So lunch and cleaning give pupils a way to work together."

**Ask.** "What does the topic sentence tell a reader?" Expect: what the whole paragraph explains.

**Ask.** "Does the ending add a new fact, or tell what the facts show?" Expect: it tells what the facts show.

Read the whole paragraph once through, as a paragraph. Say that each part has a job: introduce, develop with facts, tell a hard word's meaning, conclude. Say "many", as the article does; no school is better than another.

#### 12 min. Pairs order strips

Pairs get six strips from 02-b, A Place to Read, and a mixed pile. Five minutes: slide the strips into a paragraph and read it aloud to each other. Three minutes: one strip is a true fact about school but does not belong; the pair crosses it out and says why in one sentence ("It is about when school starts"). Four minutes: pairs check their order against the next pair's, and say which strip is the topic, which are facts, which tells a meaning and which concludes. Key: strip 1 topic, 2 fact, 3 fact, 4 meaning of library, 5 conclusion, 6 crossed out. Say "in 2025" and "one Finnish town" as the article does. Walk round and hear your six, about twenty seconds each: "Read me your topic strip."

#### 8 min. Each child writes

Each child fills two lines of the frame card for a paragraph about libraries from 02-b: "This paragraph explains ___ ." and "One fact is ___ . (A Place to Read)". The fact comes from the child's day 16 note slip. A child who cannot write tells you the lines and you write them; you never supply the words. Read your six, about thirty seconds each. Look for error D (a first sentence that explains nothing, such as "Libraries are nice") and error E (a fact the article does not say).

#### 9 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Ask the planned question about the strip paragraph: "How does the last sentence follow from the facts?" Each child turns: draws or says an answer to a partner, then pairs link ("You said ___; I would add ___ ") and clarify ("Which part do you mean?"). Take your six, about thirty seconds each. Expect: the facts say pupils read every day, so the last sentence says a little reading every day adds up.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card (s01-X17) aloud twice and ask: "These facts have no first sentence. Say a first sentence that tells what the paragraph explains." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means a sentence that names what the facts explain, such as the job board helping pupils share the table jobs. Shown with help means after one prompt: "What are all three facts about?" Not yet seen means a sentence that explains nothing, such as "Tables are nice" (error D), or none.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Repair the commonest day 16 oral or spelling miss with the sound-spelling-map. For error L or M (light read as "lit", or ie read short) say the team's sound first, then blend. For error P (naming a word from the picture) cover the picture. For error Q (a team dropped in spelling) compare with the word wall. Otherwise each child tells a partner one igh word and one y word.

#### 15 min. First reading

Two minutes: map the high-frequency words a, I, put, said, the and by on the sound-spelling-map and say each plainly; children may not guess them from the picture or the first letter. Read the title aloud: A Light Up High. Eight minutes: cover each page's picture; model page 1 with your finger, then the class reads pages 2 to 7 together, one page at a time. After page 2 ask: "Who has a light, and where will Kay put it?" Expect: Kay has the light and will put it up high. Five minutes: spell bright and dries with tiles, checking the map, then write the sentence "My light is high."

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each, two rounds. Tiles: spell right, why and fly with tiles. Sort: partners sort six word cards (high, my, pie, tried, sky, right) by the letters that say long i. Reread: partners read A Light Up High, a page each. Round 1, 19 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 4 oral card (group 2 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle). The others work in stations. Round 2, 18 minutes: every group visits a second station. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Each child tells a partner the part of a paragraph they wrote today and one word from the reader.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the frame on the board and cut scrap paper into strips. No wall: draw the sound-spelling-map on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed frame card: write the five lines on a sheet. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The strips, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. Hear the parts.** Read these aloud: "Pupils serve the lunch. Pupils clean the room. Each pupil has a part." Ask what is missing.
  **Say.** "What does a listener still not know?" Expect: what it is about, and an ending.
- **10 min. Write.** Your child fills the frame lines on side 1 for a paragraph about libraries.
- **10 min. Spell.** Say bright and dries; your child spells each and checks it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X17]] Read the card twice; your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the pair strips to five and keep the model, the writing and the exit. Longer: a child adds a second fact and a meaning line to the frame card.

### Words and supports

Introduce, develop, conclude, topic, fact. Frames: "This paragraph explains ___ ." "One fact is ___ ." "So ___ ." Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write tells you and you write. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 18 is the first check day: children read a new passage, answer six questions and begin a paragraph, with no teaching. Today's exit error D (a first sentence that explains nothing) is not repaired on day 18 or 19: it waits for day 20's retry. Today's foundations misses, error L, M and P, wait for day 20's groups; day 18 opens with a retrieval on the map and no reteaching.
