# Day 19: Check: finish the paragraph

## Teacher page

### Before class

Return each child's day 18 paragraph with its four written lines, the part of day 18's assessment that was judged after class; the question pages stay marked on the record sheet. Print the passage Ray and the Pie and a note slip for each child. Day 18's error A (the "when" answer) waits for day 20's round four. Today's six are the fourth group on the record sheet. Their oral card is all three lines.

### The day

#### 25 min. Revise and finish

Five minutes: give each child the paragraph and its four-row feedback sheet. Read the four lines aloud to any child who needs them read. Twenty minutes: children revise and finish alone, with the frame card on the desk. Each changes the row the feedback names and finishes the ending. In the revision note box on side 1 they write what they changed, a few words or a drawing. Give no teaching. If a child asks what to write, point to the feedback line and to the frame card line it matches. A child who cannot write tells the paragraph to you and you write it, supplying no words.

Collect the paragraphs at the end of this part. After class, score each finished paragraph once, on the record sheet, one mark for each of four rows (s01-A18): a topic that says what is explained (error D), facts that match 02-d (error E), partner told as the passage tells it (error F), and an ending that follows from the facts (error G). Shown means the row is met. Shown with help means it was met only after the feedback line was read aloud to the child. Not yet seen means it is not met. A paragraph that meets all four reads, for example: "Reading partners work together. A partner works with another person. Pupils choose a book and read in turns. Both follow print. Working together helps them read." One that stops after two facts, such as "Reading partners choose books. A partner works with another person," has no ending (error G). The day 18 paragraph is eligible because it was given written feedback and revised before it was scored.

#### 10 min. Collection

Each child reads the finished paragraph aloud to a partner, two minutes each. Partners say one thing the paragraph explains. Collect any pages not yet handed in, and keep the day 18 question pages with them.

#### 25 min. Quiet reading

Children read, alone or with a partner, 02-d, the reader or the captions and words to know of 02-a and 02-b. Review from day 16: a fact comes with its source tag. Each child then fills a note slip with one fact and its source tag (for 02-d the tag is School Reading Partners). Read with six children, about two minutes each: "Show me where it says it." This is practice only; no mark is kept.

#### 5 min. Opening retrieval (foundations)

Each child finds igh, ie and y on the map and says one word for each. No reteaching.

#### 15 min. Fresh passage

Give each child the passage Ray and the Pie, which no child has seen. Five minutes: children read it silently, then to a partner, one sentence each in turn. Four minutes: confirm the meaning after decoding. Ask: "Who left the pie, and where?" "What did the cat do?" "What did Ray say?" Expect: Ray left it by the light; the cat sat high on the sill and ate it; Ray said "My pie!" If a word was hard, a child says the team's sound and rereads the sentence. Six minutes: read aloud twice "My pie is on the sill." and children write it, then check each word against the map.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Tiles, sort and reread stations, two rounds. Tiles: spell pie, sky and night. Sort: partners sort six word cards (light, cried, high, why, my, pie) by the letters that say long i. Reread: partners read A Light Up High, a page each, for a smooth second reading. Round 1, 19 minutes: your six read the cycle 4 oral card to you, two minutes each (group 4 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle). Round 2, 18 minutes: a second station.

#### 3 min. Close

Each child tells a partner one word from the fresh passage and its team.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the frame card on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed slip: write fact and source on a sheet. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The collection, the stations and the oral card.

- **20 min. The assessment: finish the paragraph.** Read your child's day 18 paragraph aloud. Say one kind, helpful sentence for each row: topic, facts, meaning, ending. Your child revises and finishes it.
  **Say.** "What does your ending tell?" Expect: what the facts show.
- **10 min. Read it aloud.** Your child reads the finished paragraph to you.
- **10 min. Igh, ie and y.** Read Ray and the Pie together; your child spells My pie is on the sill.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the quiet reading to one text. Longer: a child writes a second ending and chooses the better one.

### Words and supports

Revise, partner, topic, fact. Frame: "The passage says ___ ." Pointing is an answer; extra time is allowed. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 20 acts on the checks by letter. Error F (a hard word told with no meaning) and error G (a paragraph that stops after the facts) are practiced in the partner work: find the sentence clue and copy its meaning beside the word; say what the facts show, then write it as the ending. Error A on "when" goes to round four, and children with no letter get a fresh card.
