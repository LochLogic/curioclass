# Day 16: Collecting facts and words

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print article 02-b, A Place to Read, with its map for each pair (the photograph stays out today). Cut two note slips and four paragraph strips for each pair. Today's six are the first group on the record sheet. Their oral card today is only its first two lines: shelf, class, cloth, lunch, thank, chest; tree, feet, green, beach, reach, funny. Write igh, ie and y at the foot of the sound-spelling-map during the foundations teaching, not before.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 15's exit (error A), show the 02-c diagram again. Put a finger on the one school, trace the morning bracket and then the afternoon bracket, then have partners do it once. If few missed it, partners trace one row of that diagram with a finger and say its meals.

#### 5 min. Retrieval

Review: in grade 1 you built a paragraph with a topic, two facts and a closing. Pairs get the four paragraph strips about a reading corner, mixed up. They put the strips in order and name each one: topic, fact, fact, closing. Say that the topic tells what the whole paragraph is about and the closing ends it. This is practice only. Walk round and read one pair's order aloud.

#### 4 min. Words

Review: you used fact and topic in grade 1, and you used define to tell what a tool is. Fact: something the article says. Say "A lesson is usually 45 minutes" and say it is a fact because 02-b says it. Topic: what a whole paragraph is about. Define: to tell what a word means; a definition is that meaning. Each child says one of the three words to a partner in a sentence.

#### 10 min. Reread 02-b, A Place to Read, and ask a new question

Say aloud first: "This article is about reading in Finland. It comes from the European education network, a Finnish government site and a Finnish town's news from April 2025. School rules change, so we say 'in 2025,' as the article does." Show the map. Read the whole article aloud (about 242 words, about three minutes), then the words to know. Review from day 13: a text-dependent question is one the article answers, and a finder points to the line. Today each child asks a new question, one that was not asked on day 14, with a question word from the card, and the partner finds the line and says "The article says ___ ." Two minutes each way. Examples if a child is stuck: "How long do pupils read at about ten o'clock?" "Who listens to third and fourth graders read?" "Where can anyone borrow books at no cost?" Hear your six, about twenty seconds each, on the record sheet.

#### 10 min. Gather a fact

Draw one note slip on the board with four lines: the fact, the source tag (the name of the article it came from), the hard word with what it means, and the sentence where the meaning was found. Review: in grade 1 you named where a fact came from. Model it slowly. The fact: "Anyone can borrow books at a library at no cost." The source tag: A Place to Read. The hard word: library.

**Ask.** "Where does the article tell us what a library is?" Expect: the sentence that begins "A library is a place where people borrow books and read."

Write the meaning in the article's words and copy that sentence on the last line. Say: "We tell a word's meaning from the article's own sentence, not from what we already know."

**Ask.** "Is 'Libraries are boring' a fact for our slip?" Expect: no, the article does not say it.

#### 14 min. Pairs gather

Each pair has two slips. Five minutes: partners choose a different fact from 02-b, say it in a few words, write it and its source tag, and point to the line. Five minutes: on the second slip they write volunteer and what it means, copied from the sentence that tells it. Four minutes: they swap slips with the next pair, who read the tag and find the line. Walk round and hear your six, about twenty seconds each: "Show me where the article says it."

#### 7 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Ask the planned question: "Which fact tells what a library is for?" Each child turns: draws or says an answer to a partner, then pairs link ("You said ___; I would add ___ ") and clarify ("Which part do you mean?"). Take your six, about thirty seconds each. Expect: that people borrow books and read there, and that anyone can borrow at no cost.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card (s01-X16) aloud twice and ask: "Tell me what a cubby is, using the card." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means the meaning the card gives (a small open space with a shelf) and where it came from. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What does the card say a cubby is?" Not yet seen means a meaning the card does not give, such as "a kind of bag" (error E), or none.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Day 15's written check goes here. If many wrote shet, sead or windee (error Q), or a short vowel for the long one (error M), put week, beak and baby on the sound-spelling-map. Map the whole team as one sound, blend, then write each word from the map. Otherwise each child names one ee, ea or y word from the wall and says where its long e sits.

#### 15 min. Teach igh, ie and y

Review: in grade 1 you met y at the end of a short word saying long i, as in my, fly and sky. Today the map gathers three ways to write long i. Write funny and my side by side and read them. In my, y says long i; in funny, y says long e (cycle 3). Two minutes.

igh, four minutes: write light and slide a finger under l, igh, t. Say: "Three sounds. The team igh is one sound, long i; the g and h are silent." Map night, high and right the same way, children saying each sound with you. ie, three minutes: map pie, tried and cried. ie says long i. y, three minutes: map my, fly, try, sky, by and why. A y at the end of a short word with no other vowel says long i. Blend each word again. Three minutes: say night, then pie. Children find the tiles, spell each word and check it against the map. Add igh, ie and y to the map with these words.

Look for error L (reading light as "lit") and error M (long i read as short), and say the team's sound before the word.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each, two rounds. Tiles: spell high, tried and try with tiles, checking the map. Sort: sort the word cards my, fly, sky, by with funny, baby, happy by the sound y says. Reread: partners read the map words aloud and say the long vowel in each. Round 1, 19 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 4 oral card, today only its first two lines (group 1 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle). The others work in stations. Round 2, 18 minutes: every group visits a second station. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Each child says to a partner one word with igh, ie or y and the letters that say long i.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the note slip on the board and cut scrap paper into strips. No wall: draw the sound-spelling-map on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed slip: fold a sheet in four and write the four lines. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The strips, the stations and the oral card.

- **10 min. The article.** Read 02-b aloud, then your child asks you one question the article answers.
  **Say.** "Show me the line that tells it." Expect: your child points to or reads the sentence.
- **10 min. One slip.** Your child fills the slip on side 1 with a fact, its source tag, the word volunteer and what it means.
- **10 min. Igh, ie and y.** Write light, pie and my. Your child reads each and says which letters say long i. Then spell night.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X16]] Read the card twice; your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the retrieval and the second slip; the model, the talk and the exit stay. Longer: a child writes a second fact and finds a second word meaning in 02-b.

### Words and supports

Fact, topic, define, definition, source tag. Frames: "The article says ___ ." "A ___ is ___ ." "This fact comes from ___ ." Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write tells you a slip and you write it. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 17 teaches the paragraph. Its opening repair is for error E on today's exit (a meaning that goes against the card, such as "a kind of bag"): reread the source sentence and say the meaning from it. Today's foundations misses go to day 17's foundations opening: error L, M or P (naming a word from the picture) are repaired with the sound-spelling-map, and error Q with the word wall, before the first reading of A Light Up High.
