# Day 20: Using feedback

## Teacher page

### Before class

Fill the first column of the group record from your notes on days 16 to 19, one letter a child: A, D, E, F, G, H to K, L, M, O or P. Print the written check, the retry cards and the cycle 4 word card. Day 19's assessment was judged after class; have each paragraph ready with its four lines. Day 16's six read in the last part. Key: key-sort-cards-day-18.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Feedback

Give the class one result by letter, kindly and with no names: "Many of us found the facts our ending comes from. Some of us are still finding what a hard word means." Say that today each of us works on the one thing our feedback names, and that nothing is scored today. Hand back the paragraphs and the four-row feedback sheets.

#### 40 min. Four 10-minute teacher groups

Four rounds of ten minutes. In each round one group sits with you, about seven or eight children, so each child gets about a minute and a quarter; the others work as partners (below). Each group repairs one letter.

Round one, O and P (reading): children reread 02-d once fast and once in phrases, marking a slash where a listener would pause. Ask which reading was easier to understand. For P, cover the picture and read the letters first.

Round two, L and M (vowels): on the map, put time, hide and fine beside light, pie and my. All have long i, written in different ways. Children map the whole team to one sound, blend, then read each word.

Round three, H to K (talk): rehearse the wait signal and the link card. Say as a speaker, "It goes on that one." Children ask "Which part do you mean?" and link with "You said ___; I would add ___ ."

Round four, A, E, F and G (the open group, by recorded letter). Error A, from day 18's "when" question: find the line in 02-d that says when and read it. Error E: point to the fact an ending comes from. Error F and error G are practiced by the partners with their paragraphs (below), and you visit them.

The others work as partners, with the frame card and the day 19 feedback. Each partner pair: (1) reread the paragraph and fix the one row the feedback names; for error F, find the sentence clue in 02-d and copy its meaning beside the word; for error G, say what the facts show, then write it as the ending; the score already recorded stays, and the work goes in the child's folder for the book; (2) rehearse reading the paragraph aloud in phrases, two minutes each; (3) sort the cycle 4 sort cards or practice the link card.

#### 15 min. Fresh retry

Unscored. Read the apron card twice to the D, F and G clusters, the 02-b volunteers paragraph twice to the A cluster, or the envelope card to children with no letter. Children first tell a partner, then say the answer to you, about twenty seconds each. Say for each card: "This card is made up for practice."

Letter A (s01-X20av2): "What do children earn when they come?" Letter D (s01-X20d), which repairs day 17's exit (s01-X17): "Say a first sentence that tells what a paragraph about this card explains." Letter F (s01-X20f): "Tell me what a peg is, using the card." Letter G (s01-X20g): "Say an ending that follows from these facts." No letter (s01-X20): read the envelope draft and its printed feedback, then ask: "Say an ending for the draft so that a listener understands."

Shown means the answer the card gives, said so a listener follows it. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What does the card say?" Not yet seen means an answer the card does not support, such as "Money" (error A), "Aprons are fun" (error D), "Pegs are good" (error F), "Aprons are red" (error G), or "I like apples" (error E), or an ending said so fast it is lost (error O).

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Unscored, to see what each child can do without help. Give each child side 1. Read each sentence twice and say its missing word; children write the word in the gap. 1. "The cat got a fright and ran." The word is fright. 2. "Dad fried an egg in the pan." The word is fried. 3. "The wet cloth will dry soon." The word is dry. 4. "The lid is too tight to open." The word is tight. Give no help with spelling. Collect the sheets. Mark each word by letter on the record sheet: Q for frite, frid, dri or tite (spelled by sound, pattern not carried over). Write other spellings down as they are.

#### 5 min. Reader

Partners reread A Light Up High for expression, a page each. Say: "Read it so your partner enjoys the story."

#### 25 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each, two rounds. Tiles: spell high, tried and fly. Sort: sort the cycle 4 sort cards into igh, ie and y. Reread: partners read A Light Up High again. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, for the cycle 4 oral card (group 5 of five; the five groups cover all 30 children across the cycle). Round 2, 12 minutes: every group visits a second station. Mark the heard column.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

No new teaching. Day 16's six read to you, two minutes each, 12 minutes: the grade 1 words (shelf, class, cloth, lunch, thank, chest), then the cycle 4 words (light, night, high, right, pie, tried, my, fly, why). The others work in pairs: they partner-read the cycle 4 word card (light, night, pie, tried, my, fly, sky, high), then take turns spelling two words you say, sight and cry, on paper or with tiles. For the last three minutes, everyone chooses a word from the wall that was hard for them and reads it to a partner.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the frame card and the written check on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: read the card from the view. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The groups, the stations and the written check.

- **10 min. Mend one row.** Read your child's paragraph and its four lines. Your child fixes the one row the lines name.
  **Say.** "What does the last sentence tell the reader?" Expect: what the facts show.
- **10 min. Read it aloud.** Your child reads the paragraph in phrases, twice.
- **10 min. Igh, ie and y.** Write light, pie and my. Your child reads each and says which letters say long i.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Use the card for the letter on your child's feedback: [[s01-X20av2]] A, [[s01-X20d]] D, [[s01-X20f]] F, [[s01-X20g]] G; with no letter, [[s01-X20]].

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the four rounds, the retry and the written check; cut the reader. Longer: a child writes a new ending for a classmate's paragraph.

### Words and supports

Feedback, revise, rehearse, ending, meaning. Frames: "The facts show ___ ." "A ___ is ___ ." Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write tells you and you write. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 21 opens section 1.5. Its opening repair is for error E on today's retry (an ending that ignores the facts, such as "I like apples"): point to the fact an ending comes from. Its foundations opening takes error Q on today's written check (frite, frid, dri, tite): compare with the word wall, igh, ie and final y. Error O (an ending said so fast it is lost) goes to day 21's expression part.
