# Day 134: Compare the most important points of two articles

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print 05-a and 05-c large and one chart a pair; copy the exit cards for days 132 to 136; each chart has one wrong row (see Pairs). Mark the six for the exit's spoken ask and the six for the oral card (children 1 to 6). Say aloud that the exit card is made up.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Hand back day 133's exit (error G). Lay the radish sprout and the wet soil pictures beside the point about watering early. Draw an arrow from the reason to the point it explains and read the pair aloud: the sprout is the reason for planting, not for watering early.

#### 12 min. Read 05-c

Read 05-c aloud (2 minutes, about 250 words). Pairs read it by turns (6 minutes). The class rereads the last paragraph (4 minutes). Words previewed: habitat, shelter, nest, shallow. The water, cleaning and "less is more" lines are taught as the article states them; they were checked against extension and wildlife-agency pages (sources.csv).

#### 8 min. Retrieve

Pairs say the point of 05-a and its reasons from the chart (RI.2.8, practice). Review: you drew these arrows on day 132.

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn, pair, link, clarify on "What is the most important point of each article?" Turn (3 minutes), pair (3 minutes), link with "I heard ___ say ___; I want to add ___" (2 minutes), clarify with "How do you know that is the point and not a detail?" (2 minutes).

#### 20 min. Pairs

Each pair gets a chart for 05-a and 05-c. In the point row of 05-a a small detail has been written: "Bees wake up in early spring". Pairs find it, mark it, and write the most important point instead (8 minutes). They check the 05-c row the same way, using its food, shelter and nest lines and not the water dish (4 minutes). Then each pair tells another pair the point of each article with the frame "The most important point is ___" (6 minutes). Confer with the six heard (about 60 seconds each, 2 minutes inside). A detail in the point row is error F. Review: the compare uses points, not single facts (day 133).

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is made up. Read the card twice (1 minute): [[s04-X134]]. Each child marks one of the two pictures (2 minutes). Hear the six, 15 seconds each: [[s04-X134b]] (1 minute). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Retrieve

Slates: children write ribbon and insect and name the weak part. Review: schwa e and o and the front parts i-m and i-n (grade 2, unit 4, days 129 to 133).

#### 15 min. Model, map, blend (cycle 23)

New today: the ending -ate says two ways. The tiles are on the table. Say pirate: the ending is weak, with the schwa. Say rotate: the ending is strong, with the long a. Model three words each way: pirate, accurate, moderate (schwa); rotate, imitate, pollinate (long a). For each, break it into two parts, map the sounds under the tiles, and blend (9 minutes). Children try two words in pairs, then sort six word slips under the two headings (6 minutes). Teach the pattern before the reader.

#### 15 min. First reading of the cycle 23 reader

Read page 1 of The Garden Gate yourself. Pairs echo-read the rest, a page each (8 minutes). Map the irregular word says: the letters ay say a short e, and there is no guessing from the picture or the first letter (3 minutes). Confirm the meaning: what is the gate in the play (4 minutes).

#### 12 min. Rotation

Children 1 to 6 read the cycle 23 oral card to you, two minutes each including the swap. New material was modelled before each turn. The others use tiles, the sort and the reader in two stations of twelve. Mark the heard column.

#### 13 min. Spell

Build, do not list. Say rotate. Children build it with tiles and write it (2 minutes). Do the same for pirate and imitate (4 minutes). Then pairs complete three printed sentence strips with the missing -ate word from a choice of two, for example "Pete ___ the latch" with rotates or pirates (4 minutes). Map the irregular word says in a sentence strip: the letters ay say a short e (3 minutes). Spelling the long a ending as at is error R.

### Other rooms

No strips or tiles: write the words on the board; card squares for tiles. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No second article: read one short true text twice.
**Skip.** The sort and the slate.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the sheet sent home aloud together.
  **Say.** "What is the most important point?" Expect: the point the details add up to.
- **10 min. Compare.** Ask what the sheet and the article from yesterday both want.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child says pirate and rotate and tells which ending is weak.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X134]] and [[s04-X134b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the retrieve, the wrong-row fix and the exit. Longer: a child writes a detail that is not the point and says why.

### Words and supports

Habitat, shelter, nest, shallow, most important, both. Frames: "The most important point is ___." "Both articles want ___." Say it in the home language first; pointing is an answer.

### Tomorrow

Day 135 opens with a five-minute repair on today's exit: if many marked the brush washing a dish or named the dish washing (error F), name the point the details add up to, cover the details and say it. Day 135 rereads 05-b as an opinion and builds a shared opinion.
