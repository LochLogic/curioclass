# Day 133: Explain why a reason supports a point

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print 05-b large, the twelve strips and one chart a pair; copy the exit cards for days 132 to 136. Mark the six for the exit's spoken ask and the six for the oral card (children 25 to 30). Keep the check words and the dictionary entry unseen.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Hand back day 132's exit (error G). Show the two nest pictures and reread the nests paragraph of 05-c: the point is to leave stems and bare soil, and "stems look nice" is a reason the article never gives. Draw an arrow from the hollow stems and bare ground to the point and read the pair aloud.

#### 10 min. Read 05-b

Read 05-b aloud (2 minutes, about 230 words). Pairs read it by turns (5 minutes). The class rereads the last paragraph together (3 minutes). Words previewed: pesticide, spray, label, caterpillar. Say that children never use a spray; the article tells what grown-ups may do.

#### 12 min. Model

Find the point: "use fewer sprays". Draw an arrow from each of its three reasons: some sprays kill bees right away; a small amount can still hurt a bee; a spray also kills insects that songbirds need. A reason holds up a point when, without it, the point is harder to believe. Now find the most important point of each article. The most important point of 05-a is flowers all year; of 05-b it is fewer sprays. Both want to help pollinators. A small detail, such as "read the label", is not the most important point. We compare points, not single facts, because two articles share an aim even when the facts differ.

#### 8 min. Talk

Turn, pair, link, clarify on "Why does this reason support this point?" Turn (2 minutes), pair (2 minutes), link with "I heard ___ say ___; I want to add ___" (2 minutes), clarify with "Can you say that with because?" (2 minutes).

#### 20 min. Pairs

Pairs sort six given reason strips under the two points they explain, three from 05-a and three from 05-b (8 minutes). Then they fill a two-column chart: the main point of each article, and one thing both want (8 minutes). Confer with the six heard (about 90 seconds each, 4 minutes inside the 20). A reason placed under the wrong point is error G.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read aloud twice the water paragraph of 05-c (1 minute): [[s04-X133v2]]. Each child marks one of the two pictures (2 minutes). Hear the six, 15 seconds each: [[s04-X133bv2]] (1 minute). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Opening repair

No repair is routed to this part. Say the cycle 22 chart aloud with the sound-spelling map: where the weak sound sits in two words, and the front parts i-m and i-n.

#### 15 min. Written check, cycle 22

Hand out the check sheet. Every child writes the words and sentence you say, then uses the printed dictionary entry (5 minutes each): [[s04-A73]], [[s04-A74]], [[s04-A75]]. Say each word, use it in a sentence, say it once more, and wait. Collect the sheets.

#### 18 min. Rotation

Children 25 to 30 read the cycle 22 oral card to you, three minutes each including the swap: [[s04-A66]], [[s04-A67]], [[s04-A68]], [[s04-A69]], [[s04-A70]], [[s04-A71]], [[s04-A72]]. Ask the last only if a word was misread. The others use tiles and the reader in two stations of twelve. Mark the heard column.

#### 9 min. A fresh response

Each child writes or draws a sentence with a cycle word from the reader (pocket, ribbon, wagon, kitten, button, inside or insect), then reads it to a partner (7 minutes). Share two aloud (2 minutes).

#### 13 min. Close

The class reads the cycle 22 reader once more for ease: first with you echoing (4 minutes), then in pairs, a page each (5 minutes), then together, pausing at each full stop (4 minutes).

### Other rooms

No strips: write the six reasons on the board and point. No tiles: card squares. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: read a short true text and find what its author wants.
**Skip.** The written check practice.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the sheet sent home aloud together.
  **Say.** "What does the author want us to do?" Expect: use fewer sprays.
- **10 min. Why.** Ask for one reason and say it with because.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child writes kitten and insect and says which part is weak.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X133v2]] and [[s04-X133bv2]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the model, the sort and the exit. Longer: a child writes one more reason strip for a point and a partner says which point it explains.

### Words and supports

Pesticide, spray, label, caterpillar, most important. Frames: "The most important point is ___." "This reason holds up the point because ___." Say it in the home language first; pointing to a strip is an answer.

### Tomorrow

Day 134 opens with a five-minute repair on today's exit: if many marked the bee on the stone or gave the stones' reason for changing the water (error G), draw an arrow to the point the reason explains. Day 134 reads 05-c and compares it with 05-a.
