# Day 94: Read about a bat and a hummingbird and take notes

## Teacher page

### Before class

Keep the research-cycle chart up. Draw the two-column chart on the board: Bat and Hummingbird, with three rows (when it feeds; how it reaches the nectar; flowers it likes). Write the question on a card: How does each animal reach the nectar? Put the repair card aside. Mark today's six (group 2 of five in cycle 16) on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Practice only, no marks. This follows day 93's exit. The commonest miss was a note accepted that does not match its line (error C). Use a new invented card, said aloud to be made up: the source line is "A daisy has white petals" and the note is "A daisy has red petals." Point to the source line and read it aloud. Then read the note word by word beside it, and ask which word does not fit. Children say it with a partner (3 minutes). If many put the finger on the note instead of the line (error AE), have a child trace the right tag from the line. Children repair one of their own slips on scrap paper (2 minutes).

#### 3 min. Say the plan

Say: "Today we read about a bat and a hummingbird. First you read a little alone. Then I read, and you read again with a partner. We ask: How does each animal reach the nectar?" Say that a nectar bat feeds at night, a hummingbird feeds in the day, and neither one looks for pollen: both move it by accident while they feed. Nobody touches an animal.

#### 14 min. Read with support

Each child rereads the first paragraph of 01-d alone with a finger (about 35 words; first read at the day 80 exit, so this is a second reading); children whisper-read at the same time (1 minute). Listen to the day's six one at a time, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes); the others finish and tell a partner the one thing they learned. Each child puts a pencil dot at the top of the student page: read alone. Read 01-d and 01-e aloud (about 265 words, 3 minutes): the whole articles for the first time, after paragraphs and headings met as the day 80, 89 and 91 exits. Pairs reread 01-e, one paragraph each (about 65 words each), with a finger and a partner, starring words that stop them (4 minutes). Each child makes a mark in a second colour: read with help (2 minutes). Record on the unit 3 sheet, two marks for reading: alone, and with help. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. No score.

#### 14 min. Pairs ask, find and note

Review: the note slip with its tag (grade 2, unit 3, day 88). Pairs write one slip for the bat and one for the hummingbird that answer the question, with tags (01-d, 01-e), and say them aloud. Seat the six in six different pairs. Hear the six pairs, about 1 minute each including the move (7 of the 14 minutes), asking each of the six: "How did you find it?" Do not name a line. Mark: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Tag error D, E, C or AE.

#### 10 min. Organize

Show the chart. Pairs place their two slips in the rows, each in the column of its animal (4 minutes, about 15 seconds a pair). The class reads two rows aloud and says which article each slip came from (3 minutes). A slip in the wrong row, or one that does not answer a row, is moved by its pair (3 minutes). The fourth kind of fact in the articles, how pollen moves, stays for later.

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Ask: "What is one way a bat and a hummingbird are alike, and one way they differ?" Children say "Both ___. But ___" to a partner (2 minutes). The partner links with "You said ___; I think ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" (2 minutes). Hear the six, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Close with the class naming one thing that is the same (2 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 4 min. Share

Each pair names one tag and reads or holds up one slip, about 15 seconds a pair. Say that the bat's flowers open at night and the hummingbird's are in the day; read the figure "more than 500 kinds of flowering plants depend on bats" as the page states it and do not compare it with another count.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Practice only. Write yesterday's commonest miss beside the right word and read it aloud. For badge read as pin from the picture (error S), cover the picture, map each letter, blend, then check the meaning. For huge with a short u or rage with a short a (error T), remap the vowel as long before ge. For baj or ridg (error Y), write the dge whole. For bridge with a hard g (error AM), star the odd part, read it, then write it twice from memory. For bridg or bridgde (error AN), say d, g, e in order, then write.

#### 15 min. Read A Package at the Bridge

First reading after pattern teaching. Read the title aloud. Read page 1 aloud (1 minute). Pairs echo-read pages 2 to 4 with the pictures covered: the teacher reads a line, the pair repeats it (6 minutes). Confirm meaning after decoding: "What is on the edge of the box?" and "What does the note say?" Children answer from the words, not from a picture or a first letter (3 minutes). Spell badge and rage as you dictate them (3 minutes). Map their (review: grade 2, unit 2, day 38) and whole (grade 2, unit 3, day 93), star the odd part and write each twice from memory (2 minutes).

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: today's six (group 2 of five in cycle 16) read the oral card at your table, two minutes each including the swap (12 of your minutes); the other 24 are at stations, eight each. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes each: the six join, ten to a station; groups rotate. Tiles: build edge, bridge, dodge and large. Sort: the oral card words by -dge and -ge. Reread: A Package at the Bridge and the phrase cards. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "What does the picture not tell you that the letters did?" Children tell a partner, then say it together: the words on the note.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the two articles by hand or read them aloud twice, draw the chart on a sheet, and write A Package at the Bridge on a sheet. No pair sets: lay the slips on a table and work with one pair at a time. A child who cannot write says the slip and you write it, recorded as help. A tag may be a drawn symbol. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The oral card and the stations.

- **5 min. Slips.** Your child reads the bat slip and the hummingbird slip aloud and says where each came from.
- **10 min. Ask.** Ask: "How does each animal reach the nectar?" Your child answers in a few words from the slips.
  **Say.** "Where did you find that?" Expect: 01-d or 01-e.
- **10 min. Both and But.** Your child says one way the two animals are alike and one way they are different.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say badge and rage. Your child writes each and checks the ending.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the share and the second round of stations; keep the reading, the slips and the chart. Longer: a child adds a fourth row to the chart, how pollen moves, with a slip for each animal.

### Words and supports

Bat, hummingbird, nectar, night, hover, bill, tongue, tag, alike, differ. Frames: "I found it in ___." "Both ___. But ___." "You said ___; I think ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may point to the line or the row instead of saying it.

### Tomorrow

There is no exit today. The two reading marks, the slips on the chart, the oral card records and the heard column are the evidence. If many missed C or AE, day 95 opens with the same 5-minute repair on a new card; for a foundations miss (S, T or Y), the commonest opens day 95's foundations block. Day 95 puts groups of five to work with two articles each, one question and one slip each.
