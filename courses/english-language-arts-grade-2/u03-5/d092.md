# Day 92: Ask why bees visit flowers and gather the facts

## Teacher page

### Before class

Keep the research-cycle chart up and put the joint chart sheet on the board with the question across the top: Why do bees visit flowers? Keep the step chain and arrow cards (so and because) on the wall beside it. Print 01-b for each pair, and the check sheet, reading card and dictionary entry. Mark today's six (group 5 of five in cycle 15, the last of the cycle) on the record sheet. Key: key-sort-cards-cycle-15.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Practice only, no marks. This follows day 91's draft strips. Run at most two branches, the two most missed. If many copied a sentence (error D), cover the line, say the fact in four words (the anther makes pollen), then write it. If many laid steps with no joining word (error B), lay an arrow card, so or because, between two slips and say the link aloud. If many gave the right fact with the wrong tag (error AE), put a finger on its line and read the tag from it. Children repair one slip on scrap paper.

#### 3 min. Say the plan

Say: "Today the whole class asks one question: Why do bees visit flowers? The answer is in the article 01-b. I give you the whole page and I do not point to the answer. You find it." Review: the research-cycle chart (grade 2, unit 3, day 88) and the joint chart (grade 2, unit 3, day 88). Say that pollination moves pollen from flower to flower. Seeds travelling somewhere else is a different thing, and today's question is about pollen.

#### 8 min. Read 01-b

Read A Bee Visits aloud once (about 170 words, 2 minutes). Say that a bee looks for pollen on purpose, to feed her young; later articles are about animals that move pollen by accident while they feed. Pairs then reread the first two paragraphs with a finger, one paragraph each (about 75 words, 4 minutes), and star any word that stops them (2 minutes). No line is highlighted and nothing is marked on the page for them.

#### 14 min. Pairs ask, find and note

Review: the note slip with its tag (grade 2, unit 3, day 88). Seat the day's six in six different pairs. Pairs read the rest of the article as they need it, find two facts that answer the question and write two slips: a few words in box 1, the tag in box 2 (01-b, or the photograph caption of the mining bee). Say: "The tag names the article, not the animal." Do not name a line. Hear the six pairs, about 1 minute each including the move to the next pair (7 of the 14 minutes), asking each of the six: "How did you find it?" Mark: shown, shown with help (after "Where on the page did you look?") or not yet seen. Tag error D, E, AD or AE.

#### 10 min. Trade and trace

Each pair trades slips with another pair (one group of three pairs passes in a ring). The reader lays each slip on the title of the article it came from and says "It came from ___" (5 minutes). Trade back and swap roles for the second slip (5 minutes). A slip with the wrong tag goes back to its pair with the question "Which article is this line in?"

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Review: the talk routine (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Ask: "Which fact is the most important for the question, and why?" Children say "I think ___ because ___" to a partner (2 minutes). The partner links with "You said ___; I think ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" (2 minutes). Hear the six, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Close with the class naming one fact that came up twice (2 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 8 min. Joint chart

Each pair puts one slip on the joint chart under the question, about 15 seconds a pair (4 minutes). The class sorts them: a slip that answers the question stays; a true slip that does not answer it, such as a bare "bees are fuzzy", moves to the side column "true but not for this question" (error E) (2 minutes). Read two tags aloud and say which article each names (2 minutes). The so and because arrow cards stay beside the chart; a pair may lay one between two slips if one fact explains another.

#### 2 min. The photograph

Show the mining bee photograph and read its caption. Children tell a partner one thing the picture shows that the words did not. Hear three. Pointing is an answer.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Unscored. Give each child the cycle 15 check sheet and say each part twice. Spelling (5 minutes): items [[s03-A29]] and [[s03-A30]]; say each word, its sentence, then the word again. Reading (5 minutes): hand out the printed card and ask [[s03-A31]]; children write a few words (3 minutes). Then ask [[s03-A33]] and [[s03-A34]] one at a time: partners whisper the answer and you stand by the day's six, about 10 seconds a child for each ask, and mark each (2 minutes). Reference (5 minutes): hand out the draft and the dictionary entry and ask [[s03-A32]]; children write the corrected word and mark what they changed. Collect the sheets. Mark: shown, shown with help (after one prompt, recorded as help) or not yet seen. Tag error Y for a missing or changed ending, C for an answer that contradicts the card, J for a copied draft and AL for un- or re- treated as meaningless.

#### 5 min. Reader

Pairs reread The Attic Trunk for expression: one reads a page, the other the next, then they swap. Say: "Read it like talking." Listen to any pair for one page.

#### 25 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78 (the 25-minute form). Round 1 is 13 minutes: today's six (group 5 of five in cycle 15) read the oral card at your table, two minutes each including the swap (12 of your minutes), and the other 24 work at the three stations, eight to a station. Round 2 is 12 minutes: the six join, ten to a station. Every child has two stations. Tiles: build clock, topic and refill. Sort: the eleven cycle 15 cards by ending. Reread: The Attic Trunk and A Picnic on the Deck. Mark the heard column.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

No new teaching. Day 88's six read the cycle 15 words clock, dusk, topic, refill, undo and rerun to you, two minutes each (12 minutes, 13 with the swaps). The other 24 partner-read the cycle 15 sort cards, then spell sock and unpack as you dictate them (unscored, 3 minutes). Mark the heard column.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the question on a sheet, copy the article by hand or read it aloud twice, and write the check card and entry on slips. No pair sets: one article on the table, a pair at a time. A child who cannot write says the slip and you write it, recorded as help. A tag may be a drawn symbol. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The oral card and the stations.

- **5 min. Slips.** Your child reads the two school slips aloud and says where each came from.
- **10 min. Ask.** Ask: "Why do bees visit flowers?" Your child answers in a few words from the slips.
  **Say.** "Where did you find that?" Expect: the article 01-b.
- **10 min. Link.** Your child picks the slip that answers best and says why.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say sock and unpack. Your child writes each, then checks the ending.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the photograph and the second round of stations; keep the question, the slips and the written check. Longer: a child finds a third fact in 01-b and says whether it answers the question or goes to the side column.

### Words and supports

Question, article, source, tag, slip, pollen, nectar, bee. Frames: "I found it in ___." "I think ___ because ___." "You said ___; I think ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may point to the line instead of saying it.

### Tomorrow

There is no exit today. The slips on the joint chart, the check sheets, the oral card records and the heard column are the evidence. Mark the check sheets after class (Y, C, J, AL). Day 93 opens its foundations block with a repair for the commonest miss, and its topic block with a repair for the commonest of D, E and AD. Day 93 asks "Which flowers do butterflies like?" from 01-c, sorts notes against their cards and gives the exit.
