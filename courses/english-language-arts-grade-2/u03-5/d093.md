# Day 93: Gather butterfly facts and sort notes against their cards

## Teacher page

### Before class

Keep the research-cycle chart up. Write the question on the joint chart sheet: Which flowers do butterflies like? Cut three source cards from 01-c (a line, with its tag) and the six practice slips below. Put the exit cards 93 to 95 aside, face down. Mark today's six (group 1 of five in cycle 16, the first of the cycle) on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Practice only, no marks. This follows day 92's slips. Run at most two branches, the two most missed. If many copied a sentence (error D), cover the article, say the fact in four words (the bee mixes pollen with nectar), then write it. If many wrote a true fact that does not answer the question (error E), hold up the question card and ask whether the slip answers it. If you or a faster child named the line (error AD), give a page with no marks and let the child find it. Children repair one slip on scrap paper.

#### 3 min. Say the plan

Say: "Today we ask: Which flowers do butterflies like? The answer is in 01-c. Then we look at whether our notes say what the page says." Say that a butterfly does not look for pollen. It moves pollen by accident while it feeds. A source tag names the article, 01-c, not the animal.

#### 8 min. Read 01-c

Read A Butterfly Visits aloud once (about 150 words, 2 minutes). Children heard three of its paragraphs as the day 78, 79 and 90 exits; today they hear the whole article for the first time. Pairs reread the first two paragraphs with a finger, one each (about 70 words, 4 minutes), and star any word that stops them (2 minutes).

#### 14 min. Pairs ask, find and note

Review: the note slip with its tag (grade 2, unit 3, day 88). Pairs read the rest of 01-c as they need it, find two facts that answer the question and write two slips with tags (01-c). Seat the six in six different pairs. Hear the six pairs, about 1 minute each including the move (7 of the 14 minutes), asking each of the six: "How did you find it?" Mark: shown, shown with help (after "Where on the page did you look?") or not yet seen.

#### 12 min. Check against the source

The slips are sorted against cards, not mended. Lay the three source cards on the board. Hold up the six practice slips one at a time, each under its card: "Butterflies like flat flowers"; "Butterflies like dark blue tall flowers"; "Butterflies move less pollen than bees"; "Butterflies move more pollen than bees"; "Butterflies like red flowers"; "Butterflies like flowers in bunches". Children read the slip and the card and show a thumb up for a slip that says what the card says and a thumb down for one that does not; the slip goes to a pile, "same as the card" or "not the same" (6 minutes). Then pairs trade their own two slips with another pair, read them against 01-c and sort each into the same two piles (4 minutes). A slip in "not the same" goes back to its pair, who put a paper clip on it and keep it; they mend it after the exit, not now (2 minutes). Do not point to a line and do not fix a slip today.

#### 8 min. Exit

Hand out nothing. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Show the bees card, read the source line and the note twice, then ask one thing at a time. Ask [[s03-X93]]: every child tells a partner; hear the six, about 12 seconds each. Ask [[s03-X93b]] the same way. Ask [[s03-X93c]] the same way. Mark each: shown, shown with help (after "Read the source line again") or not yet seen. Tag error C for a note accepted, E for a fix that changes nothing and AE for a note traced to the note, not the source.

#### 10 min. Both and But

Review: the Both and But sheet (grade 2, unit 1, day 23) and describing words (grade 2, unit 3, day 86). Return the paper-clipped slips; each pair fixes its slip to say what 01-c says (2 minutes). Then each pair writes on the student page one fact the bee and the butterfly share and one that differs, each with a tag and one describing word from 01-b or 01-c, such as hairy, long, thin or flat: "Both visit for food. But the butterfly moves less pollen" (4 minutes, while you hear the six pairs, about 40 seconds each). Two pairs read their sentences aloud and the class says which tag fits (2 minutes). Collect the sheets (2 minutes).

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Practice only. This follows day 92's written check. Write the commonest miss beside the right word and read it aloud: for a changed or missing ending (error Y), the wrong spelling beside the right one, with the taught ending starred. For un- or re- treated as meaningless (error AL), cover the prefix and say what is left. Children write the right word once.

#### 15 min. Teach: -dge and -ge

Retrieve ck and k endings from cycle 15 (duck, desk): ck follows a short vowel (2 minutes). Teach -dge after a short vowel in a one-syllable word: model badge, edge, bridge, dodge, judge; say the word, map each sound on the sound-spelling-map, point out that d and g stay together, then blend and spell (5 minutes). Teach -ge after a long vowel or a consonant: model rage, huge, large, plunge. The g says /j/ when e follows (4 minutes). Map their (review: grade 2, unit 2, day 38) and whole: star the odd part, w before ole, and write each from memory twice (2 minutes). Dictate "Dad sat on the edge" (2 minutes); children write on the student page and check each word on the word wall.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: today's six (group 1 of five in cycle 16) read the oral card at your table, two minutes each including the swap (12 of your minutes); the other 24 are at stations, eight each. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes each: the six join, ten to a station; groups rotate. Tiles: build badge, bridge, rage and huge. Sort: the oral card words by -dge and -ge. Reread: The Attic Trunk and the phrase cards. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which ending has the d, in bridge or in rage?" Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the three source cards and the six slips on slips of paper, and draw the bees card on a sheet. No pair sets: lay the cards on a table and work with one pair at a time. A child who cannot write says the slip and you write it, recorded as help. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The oral card and the stations.

- **5 min. Slips.** Your child reads the two school slips and the Both and But sentences aloud.
- **10 min. Words.** Write badge, edge, rage and huge on slips. Your child sorts them by the ending, d g e or g e, and says each word.
  **Say.** "Which ending has the d?" Expect: badge and edge have d g e; rage and huge have g e.
- **10 min. Write.** Say "the bridge is huge". Your child writes it and checks each ending.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X93]] [[s03-X93b]] [[s03-X93c]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the Both and But and the second round of stations; keep the sorting, the exit and the teaching. Longer: a child writes a slip that does not match 01-c on purpose, and a partner puts it in a pile.

### Words and supports

Butterfly, flat, bunch, same, different, source, tag, badge, edge, rage, huge. Frames: "The slip says ___; the card says ___." "Both ___. But ___." "I found it in ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may point to the card instead of saying it.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children can say a note does not match its source, name the word to fix and put a finger on the words that say so. If many accepted the note (error C), day 94 opens with a 5-minute repair: point to the line, read it aloud, then compare the note word by word, on a new card. Mark the Both and But sheets after class. Day 94 reads the bat and hummingbird articles and organizes notes in a two-column chart.
