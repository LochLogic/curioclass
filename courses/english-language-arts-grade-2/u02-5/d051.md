# Day 51: Two words that make one

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 50's exit marks show whether error H needs the repair. Mark today's six (group 4 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Fold the five compound slips so each word shows in two halves. Give each pair article C, the slips and the dictionary card. Set three stations: tiles, the ten day 48 sort cards, a reread of Rob Is Skipping. No screen exists, so the printed card does the dictionary job.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 50's exit (error H), hold up the flip card with zip under an un- flap. Lift the flap, read unzip and say: "Un- means not, so unzip means not zipped." Ask: "Does un- mean again?" Expect: no, that is re-. Children say unzip and rezip with their hands. Hear two. Otherwise pairs retrieve what un- and re- mean from the sort cards.

#### 6 min. Words

Review: compound, a word made of two words joined into one (grade 1); entry, one word and its meaning on a dictionary page (unit 1, day 27); clarify, to ask again so you understand (kindergarten). New: reference, a book or page you look in to check a word or a fact. Say each word and use it: "Backpack is a compound word." "I read the entry for the word." "I clarify by asking, Which part do you mean?" "A dictionary page is a reference." Partners say one sentence back.

#### 8 min. Reread parts of article C

Read the Tracks and Traces part aloud, children following the print (1 minute). Then read the four sentences from Shapes in Stone that begin "Think of a seashell" (1 minute). Pairs point to footprint, riverbed, hillside, seashell and sandstone as you say each (3 minutes), then reread the Tracks and Traces part turn about (3 minutes).

**Ask.** "Which of these words is two words joined?" Expect: all of them. Hear three.

#### 12 min. Model: compound words

Review: a compound word is two words joined into one (grade 1). Today we predict what the new word means, then check. Say the four steps and show them on the board: read each part; say what each part means; predict the whole; check the sentence. Context never replaces reading the letters.

- **footprint** (4 minutes). Point to each part and read it aloud: foot, print. Foot is the body part; print is a mark pressed into something. Predict: a mark made by a foot. Test it on the article: "A footprint left in soft mud or sand is a trace." A mark made by a foot fits.
- **riverbed** (4 minutes). River, bed. A river; and the ground at the bottom, as in a bed. Predict: the ground under a river. Test it: "A riverbed or a hillside may hold many tracks." The ground under a river fits. Look at the dictionary card entry for riverbed: it says the ground a river flows over.

Say the limit (3 minutes): the parts help, but they do not always give the whole meaning. A butterfly is not made of butter. When the prediction does not fit the sentence, try the other meaning of a part or look in a reference. Children say the four steps back with you (1 minute).

#### 10 min. Pairs predict and check

Pairs open one slip at a time: seashell, sandstone, hillside, then raincoat and sunlight. They read each part, say what each part means, predict the whole, close the slip and check. For the first three, check against the article sentence you pointed to. For the last two, say the sentence: "She wore a raincoat to stay dry." "Sunlight came in the window." Walk among pairs, about 20 seconds each. Hear three pairs, one word each (3 minutes). Watch for error J, a prediction that does not fit the sentence.

Check: each of five words has a part meaning, a prediction and a sentence check.

#### 10 min. Print dictionary

Model with the card (4 minutes). Find bark: first letter, find the entry, compare the letters. The entry has two meanings, the rough outside of a tree and a sharp sound a dog makes. Read the example sentence that sits under each meaning. Say aloud: one spelling can have more than one meaning, and the entry lists each one.

Pairs then find wave and fan on the card (2 minutes). For each entry, one child counts the meanings on a finger and reads each aloud with its example sentence; the partner draws a quick sketch or acts out each meaning (4 minutes). Expect: wave has two, a line of moving water and a hand moved to say hello; fan has a blowing thing and a spread shape. Hear three pairs, one entry each. Do not use cast, trace, location, notebook, collector or marked.

Check: for each entry, the pair counts the meanings, reads each aloud and shows each in a sketch or an action.

#### 4 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes: "I think ___ means ___." with one word from the slips, then finishes "I know because ___" with what the parts mean. A child may draw and say the words for you to scribe.

Check: a compound word, a meaning, and a reason from its parts.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X51 aloud twice (30 seconds). Ask item s02-X51. Children tell a partner. Then read the card of item s02-X51b twice (30 seconds) and ask it; children tell a partner. The six then answer both to you, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Shown means for the first card a book of notes about an outdoor place, with both parts named; for the second, the small fence, with a word of reason. Shown with help means after one prompt: "What does each part mean?" or "Say the sentence with each meaning." Not yet seen means a joined meaning that does not fit (error J) or the first meaning chosen without the sentence (error G). Mark the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Show six word cards: running, stopped, even, finally, wrong, won. Children read each. If a mapped word is misread, map the unexpected part with a star: the w in wrong is silent, the o in won says /u/, the y in finally says /ee/ (error AA). If an ending is dropped, underline it, read it alone, then the whole word (error Z).

#### 15 min. Transfer to a fresh passage

Pairs read Digging for a Shell (44 words) turn about, a sentence each, twice, the second time smoothly (6 minutes). No picture: read the letters. Check meaning after decoding (3 minutes).

**Ask.** "How does Tim feel about Jen's find, and how do you know?" Expect: glad, not mad; he is grinning.

Dictate once, then twice: "Jen is hopping and clapping." Children write it and check the capital, the end mark and the doubled letters against the passage (4 minutes). Walk among children and check two spellings each (2 minutes).

Check: two doubled spellings and a sentence.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes), all four lines. Each says the vowel sound in each word and the ending. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build digging, hopping and grinning by doubling, then riding by dropping the e. Sort: the ten day 48 sort cards under three headings. Reread: pairs read Rob Is Skipping twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

Check: each child reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which word did you decode, then check?" Expect: any word, read from its letters first, such as digging, then checked against the sentence.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the five words in two halves on folded paper and the entries for bark, wave and fan on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of article C: show the sentences on the board and let pairs point there.

### At home

**Swap.** No slips: write each word on paper and fold it between the parts. No dictionary card: use any dictionary and look up bark.
**Skip.** The stations, the oral card and the fresh passage.

- **10 min. Two words.** Write sun and light, then sunlight, on folded paper. Open the fold and read each part. Your child says what each part means and predicts the whole.
  **Say.** "What do you think sunlight means, and how can you check?" Expect: light from the sun, checked in a sentence.
- **10 min. A dictionary.** Look up bark in any dictionary. Count the meanings, then read each aloud. Your child sketches or acts out each one.
  **Say.** "How many meanings does bark have, and what are they?" Expect: two, the rough outside of a tree and a sharp sound a dog makes.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say hopping and grinning. Your child writes each and says why the letter doubles.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X51]] [[s02-X51b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs do three slips and the wave entry; keep the model, the exit and the transfer reading. Longer: a child joins two words of their own and says what the new word means.

### Words and supports

Compound, entry, reference, clarify. Frames: "I think ___ means ___." "I know because ___." "Which meaning do you mean?" "You said ___; I would add ___." Pointing and tracing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 52 opens with a repair of today's exit (error J, parts joined to a meaning that does not fit; error G, a meaning chosen without the sentence) on a fresh word. Then children name the topic and each paragraph's focus in article A on a blank chart with no model, and take the cycle 9 written check.
