# Day 52: The topic of a new article

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 51's exit marks show whether error J or error G needs the repair. Mark today's six (group 5 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Cover the wall chart and the day 46 worked example. Give each pair article A, a blank topic chart, the dictionary card and a pencil. Keep the check sheets face down until foundations. Set three stations: tiles, the ten day 48 sort cards, a reread of Rob Is Skipping. Key: key-sort-cards-cycle-9.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 51's exit, repair the commonest miss with a fresh word, saying it is made up for practice. Error J, a joined meaning that does not fit: say "The children built a snowman in the yard." Pairs read snow and man, predict, and check the sentence. Expect: a figure of snow shaped like a man. Error G, a meaning taken without reading the entry: pairs find bold on the dictionary card, count its meanings and read each aloud. Expect: not afraid, and easy to see. Hear two pairs. Otherwise pairs retrieve the five compound slips.

#### 5 min. Read aloud: article A

Review: topic, the whole text; focus, what one paragraph says (day 46). Read What Is a Fossil? aloud once, about 237 words, 2 minutes, children following the print and its four subheadings. Say again that fossils on public land belong to everyone: we never dig for them or take them; if we find one, we leave it, take a picture and tell a ranger or a teacher. Partners tell each other one thing they learned (2 minutes). Hear two.

#### 15 min. Pairs name the topic and each focus, no model

Each pair has article A and a blank chart: a Topic box, and four Focus boxes joined to it by arrows. Say: "Fill the chart with a few words. Today you do it with no example, and the wall chart is covered." Pairs read the title and the four subheadings, then fill in each focus from its part, then the topic last (12 minutes). Move between pairs, about 20 seconds each, and give no answers. If a pair is stuck, say only: "What does the title say?" Mark nothing yet (3 minutes at the end: pairs read their topic to you).

Check: a topic wider than any one focus; each focus smaller than the topic; a word or subheading the pair can point to for each.

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "How do the paragraph focuses fit the topic?" Turn: each child tells a partner (2 minutes). Pair: two pairs join and compare charts: "Your topic is ___; my focus for ___ is ___." (3 minutes). Hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). After each, one child links with "You said ___; I would add ___" or asks "Which part do you mean?" (2 minutes). Use the wait signal. A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Write on your own

Each child writes, with a few words and no model: "The topic is ___." and two focuses, "___ is about ___." One focus is for a subheading of the child's choice, the other for a different one. A child may draw and say the words for you to scribe. Walk among children, about 20 seconds each. Judge three look-fors: the topic and then separate focuses are named; a focus is smaller than the topic and fits it; a word, subheading or line is pointed to as the reason. Mark nothing aloud.

Check: a topic, two focuses and one reason that points to the text.

#### 5 min. Check the writing

Each child checks a capital at the start, an end mark on each sentence and one spelling against the word wall or the beginning dictionary card. Say: "Say the word, find it, copy it, cover it, write it" (error AC if a child skips the check).

Check: a capital, an end mark and one spelling checked.

#### 5 min. Partner fix

Partners swap pages and read each other's focuses against article A. If one does not fit, the partner says: "Your focus fits because ___" or "Your focus is too big because ___" and points to the subheading. The child mends one focus. A child may pass.

Check: each focus read against its subheading and judged fits or too big.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read aloud the first two paragraphs of Scientists at Work, on page 3 of article C, twice (30 seconds). Ask item s02-X52v2. Children tell a partner. Ask item s02-X52bv2 on the same paragraphs. Children tell a partner. The six then answer both to you, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes); mark the record sheet. Shown means a topic wider than either paragraph, such as how scientists study fossils, and the second paragraph's own focus, the questions scientists ask. Shown with help means after one prompt: "What is each paragraph about?" Not yet seen means one detail named as the whole topic (error C), the topic given for the second paragraph, or no answer.

#### 15 min. Written check (foundations)

Give out the sheets, face down, then turn them over. Say: "This is to see what you know. I will not help with the words." Read each sentence twice and say the missing word; children write it in the gap (4 minutes). The four words were not used in any lesson this cycle. Then write the right spellings on the board. Partners swap sheets and tick each word that matches; children circle the doubled letter or the ending in each word and keep the sheet (4 minutes). Collect the sheets (1 minute) and tag misses AB, the doubling left out, and Z, the ending left out; tag any other wrong letter Y. The sheet is unscored. Then put the commonest miss beside the right spelling without naming a child, underline the ending and blend it (4 minutes). Children who finish early reread Rob Is Skipping.

Check: each ticked word has the doubled letter and the ending.

#### 5 min. Reader

Review: reread with a pause at a full stop (unit 2, day 44). Pairs reread Rob Is Skipping, a page each, then again smoothly, with a pause at each full stop and the voice dropping on page 4 at "I was wrong to rush."

#### 25 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, two rounds of 12 minutes and one minute to move. Round 1: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). All four lines are used, so the six read the whole cycle. Each says the vowel sound in each word and the ending. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build skipping, clapping and sitting by doubling, then hiding by dropping the e. Sort: the ten day 48 sort cards under three headings. Reread: pairs read Rob Is Skipping twice, the second time smoothly. Round 2: the groups move on; the six join one station they have not met.

Check: each child reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 15 min. Fresh response

Dictate two words, unscored, and say each twice: making, then stepped. Making drops the e; stepped doubles the p. Children write with tiles or pencil from memory, then check against the word wall (3 minutes). Then day 48's six (group 1) sit with you and read the cycle 9 words from the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes), while the others partner-read the cycle 9 partner word card and say the vowel in each. No new teaching.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the chart on folded paper and write the four check lines on torn paper. One copy of article A: read it aloud twice and let pairs point on the board. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read a short book or article that has a title and sections. No tiles: write letters on scraps.
**Skip.** The stations, the written check and the oral card.

- **10 min. Name the topic.** Read a short article aloud, one section at a time. After each section, ask what that section is about. At the end, ask what the whole article is about. Give no answers; let your child look back.
  **Say.** "What is the whole topic, and what is each part about?" Expect: a topic wider than any one part, then a small focus for each part.
- **10 min. Write it.** Your child writes: "The topic is ___." and one focus, "___ is about ___."
- **5 min. Spell.** Say stopped and hoping. Your child writes each and says why one doubles and one drops the e.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X52v2]] [[s02-X52bv2]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs fill the topic and two focuses only; keep the exit, the written check and the partner fix. Longer: a child names the topic and focuses of a second article.

### Words and supports

Topic, focus, subheading, paragraph. Frames: "The topic is ___." "___ is about ___." "Your focus fits because ___." "Your topic is ___; my focus for ___ is ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Day 53 opens with a repair of today's exit (error C, one paragraph named as the whole topic) on a fresh pair of paragraphs, and its foundations block opens by reading today's check misses (error AB, doubling left out; error Z, ending left out; error Y, any other wrong letter). Then children choose the most useful feature for a question and say what the author of article C wants to explain, and foundations moves to the three sounds of -ed.
