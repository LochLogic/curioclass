# Day 50: A small word and a big word

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 49's exit marks show whether error E needs the repair. Mark today's six (group 3 of five) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Make the two flip cards: cover with an un- flap, play with a re- flap. Give each pair article B, the sort cards and a pencil. Set three stations: tiles, a second sort of the cycle 9 cards, a reread of Rob Is Skipping.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 49's exit (error E), pairs hold page 4 of article C. Say: "A child wants the page for the word track. Which tool tells you the page, and why not read every page?" Walk a finger from the index entry to the page. Expect: the index, track, page 2. Hear two pairs. Otherwise lay one purpose card beside article B and ask a pair for the words that show it.

#### 6 min. Words

Review (days 47 to 49): locate, to find where something is; feature, a part of a text that helps you; purpose, what the author wants to do. New: mineral, a hard material found in the ground, as article B says. Say each word, point to it on the printed article and use it: "A caption is a feature that helps me locate a fact." "The purpose of article B is to explain." "A mineral is a hard material in the ground." Partners say one sentence back.

#### 8 min. Reread Uncovered With Care

Read the last part of article B aloud twice, about 40 words, children following the print. Say the second time slowly. Children point to the word uncover.

**Ask.** "What do the scientists do to the fossil, slowly?" Expect: they take the cover off, or they dig it out with care. Hear three.

#### 12 min. Model: a prefix and a base word

Review: you learned un- and re- in kindergarten. A prefix is a small word part at the front of a word. Today we use it on words we already know. Hold up the flip card, cover with the un- flap down.

Work four words, thinking aloud each time. Step 1: say the word you know. Step 2: lift the flap and say what the prefix means. Step 3: put them together. Step 4: say it in a sentence and check that it fits.

- **uncover** (3 minutes). Cover is the word I know. Un- means not, or the opposite of the action. Uncover is the opposite of cover: take the cover off. Check with the article: "They uncover the fossil slowly." Taking the cover off fits.
- **unlock** (2 minutes). "Mina will unlock the gate." Lock, then the opposite: make it not locked. It fits.
- **replay** (2 minutes). Flip to the re- card. Re- means again. Play, then again: play it again. "We replay the song."
- **rebuild** (2 minutes). Build, then again: build it again. "The wind knocked it down, so we rebuild it."

Say aloud that only uncover is in the article; unlock, replay and rebuild are made-up practice words, and the sentences are invented. Say the limit (3 minutes): un and re start words that are not prefix words, such as uncle and red. The trick works only when what is left is a word you know. Children say the four steps back with you.

#### 12 min. Pairs sort and explain

Read each of the eight sort cards aloud once: unwrap, unfold, redo, refill, unpack, reread, unlock, rebuild. Children do not read the long words; they find the letters un or re at the start and lay the card under that heading (5 minutes). Then each pair says the meaning of four cards with "not" or "again", for example "unpack means not packed" (4 minutes). Hear four pairs, one card each (3 minutes). Watch for error H, a child who says un- means again.

Check: each card under the right prefix, each meaning said with not or again.

#### 7 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "How does the small word help you with the whole word?" Turn: each child tells a partner (2 minutes). Pair: two pairs join and one says what the other said (2 minutes). Say: "You said ___; I would add ___." Hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes); after each, one child links or asks "Which part do you mean?" Use the wait signal. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Write one sentence

Each child picks one word from the cards, writes it and what it means, then finishes "I know because ___" with what un- or re- means. A child may draw and say the words for you to scribe.

Check: a word from the cards, a meaning, and what the prefix means.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X50 aloud twice (30 seconds). Ask item s02-X50. Children tell a partner. The six then answer to you, about 20 seconds each (2 minutes); mark the record sheet. Shown means not finished, with un- named as not. Shown with help means after one prompt: "What does the card say?" Not yet seen means finished again (error H) or no answer.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Show six word cards: rubbing, stopped, bigger, running, sitting, dropping. Children read each. If an ending is dropped or misread, underline the ending, read it alone, then the whole word (error Z). If a doubled letter is missed in a spelling, compare hop and hope: one short vowel and one consonant, so double (error AB).

#### 15 min. Reread, sort and spell

Pairs reread Rob Is Skipping turn about, a page each, the second time smoothly (4 minutes). Then pairs sort the twelve cycle 9 cards under doubled letter and not doubled, saying each word as it is placed (4 minutes). Each child spells waving and tripping with letter tiles, then writes the sentence you say twice: "Rob is waving at Ann." (4 minutes). Last, each child checks waving on the beginning dictionary card: find the first letter, find the entry wave, compare the letters, then drop the e before -ing (3 minutes). Say: "Say the word, find it, copy it, cover it, write it" (error AC if a child skips the check).

Check: two spellings and a sentence, with one spelling checked against the card.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes), all four lines. Each says the vowel sound in each word and the ending. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build tripping, clapping, riding, then hiding by dropping the e. Sort: the ten cycle 9 sort cards from day 48 under three headings. Reread: pairs read Rob Is Skipping twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

Check: each child reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Why is it stopped and not stoped?" Expect: stop has one short vowel and one consonant at the end, so the p doubles before -ed.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a slip of paper over a word for each flip card and write the sort words on torn paper. No tiles: write letters on torn paper. One copy of article B: read the last part aloud and show it on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write the words on torn paper. No tiles: write the letters on scraps.
**Skip.** The stations, the sort and the oral card.

- **10 min. A small word.** Write cover on paper. Fold a flap over it with un- written on the flap. Lift the flap and read uncover. Do the same with play and re-.
  **Say.** "What does un- mean in uncover?" Expect: not, or the opposite, so take the cover off.
- **10 min. Sort.** Read unwrap, unfold, redo, refill, unpack and reread aloud. Your child puts each under un- or re- and says its meaning with not or again.
  **Say.** "How does the small word help you with the whole word?" Expect: it tells part of the meaning.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say stopped and running. Your child writes each and says why the last letter doubles.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X50]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs sort four cards instead of eight and skip the dictionary check; keep the model, the exit and the reread. Longer: a child thinks of one more un- word and one more re- word and says each meaning.

### Words and supports

Prefix, uncover, mineral, locate, feature, purpose. Frames: "Un- means ___." "Re- means ___." "I know because ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and drawing are answers. A child may pass any turn.
