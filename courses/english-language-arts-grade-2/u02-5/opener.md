# Section 2.5 opener: word parts and a new article (days 50 to 53)

## What the section does

Four days work on words and then on an article without a model. On day 50 children use a known prefix and a base word to say what a word means: un- means not and re- means again. On day 51 they use compound parts and the beginning dictionary to check a meaning, in print and on a teacher-held screen. On day 52 they name the topic of a different article, and the focus of each of its four parts, with a blank chart and no worked example. On day 53 they take a question and find the most useful feature to answer it: a subheading, a bold word, a caption, the glossary, the index or the menu icon.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a repair, the day's work and a five-minute exit. The foundations block continues cycle 9 with the reader *Rob Is Skipping*, a transfer passage on day 51, a 15-minute written check on day 52, and the three sounds of -ed on day 53 (/t/, /d/ and /id/). Every exit is one card you read aloud twice and one ask; you hear the day's rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A prefix changes a word the child already knows.** Join the prefix meaning to the base meaning, then check the sentence.
- **A compound is two words that make a third.** Predict, then read the sentence and check that the meaning fits.
- **A dictionary entry may hold more than one meaning.** Choose the one that fits the sentence.
- **Choose the feature that leads straight to the fact.** The best tool depends on the question.

## The errors, in plain words

- **H:** a prefix read as the wrong meaning. Flip the card: un- means not.
- **J:** compound parts joined with a meaning that does not fit. Predict, then check the sentence.
- **C:** one paragraph named as the topic. Compare every paragraph focus before naming what joins them.
- **D and E:** an unsupported purpose, or a useful feature ignored. Point to what the author explains, and walk the route.
- **AB, Z, AC:** a doubled letter dropped, an ending dropped, or a spelling not checked. Compare hop and hope, underline the ending, and look the word up.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice on the shared screen on day 51 is shown, not graded. Exits are unscored; record the exit looks on the unit record sheet.
