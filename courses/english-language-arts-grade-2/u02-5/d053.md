# Day 53: Which feature finds the answer?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 52's exit marks show whether error C needs the repair. Mark today's six (group 1 of five in cycle 10) on the unit 2 record sheet and its heard column. Give each pair article C, the question cards 53, the feature route cards, the purpose cards, the dictionary card and a pencil. List the commonest misses on the day 52 checks (AB, Z, Y). Set three stations: tiles, the ten cycle 9 sort cards, a reread of Rob Is Skipping. Keys: key-route-cards-day-47.md, key-question-cards-day-53.md, key-sort-cards-cycle-9.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many missed day 52's exit (error C), say: "Read the title first." Hold up a made-up pair of lines: "A rock can be sketched. A rock can be photographed." Ask the pair to name the whole topic and say whether each line fits it. Expect: ways to show a rock; each line is one way. Hear two pairs. Otherwise pairs lay one route card beside article C and walk the route with a finger.

#### 8 min. Reread Scientists at Work and Using This Article

Read the Scientists at Work part aloud once and the Using This Article part twice, children following the print (about 4 minutes in all). Say the second reading slowly. Pairs point to the line that names the features, then tell a partner two features it names (4 minutes). Do not use photographs, notebook or location as a question word today. Hear three.

**Ask.** "Which features does the article say help you locate facts?" Expect: bold words, labelled diagrams, subheadings, a glossary and an index.

#### 15 min. Pairs choose the most useful feature

Pairs take the three question cards in turn. For each, they name the most useful feature among the subheading, bold word, caption, glossary, index and menu icon, put a finger on it, and find the answer on the print. One child picks the feature and says why; the partner finds the line and says "yes" or "which part do you mean?" Swap on each card (12 minutes). Walk among pairs, about 20 seconds each. Hear three pairs, one card each (3 minutes). Watch for error E, a pair that reads page after page instead of choosing a tool.

Check: a feature named, the line found, and a word of reason for each card.

#### 12 min. Explain the purpose

Review: purpose, what the author wants to do: answer, explain how or describe what (day 48). Lay the three purpose cards on the board. Pairs reread the Using This Article part and say what the author of article C wants to explain, pointing to the words that show it (6 minutes). Expect: how to find facts, from "features to help you locate facts". Then read the three day 48 blurbs aloud once. Pairs say which blurb has the same purpose as article C's last part and which purpose does not match any (6 minutes). Hear three. Watch for error D, a purpose with no words to point to.

Check: a purpose named, and a line or a word pointed to as the evidence.

#### 8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Question: "Which feature found the answer fastest, and why?" Turn: each child tells a partner (2 minutes). Pair: two pairs join and one says what the other said, with "You said ___; I would add ___" (2 minutes). Hear today's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). After each, one child links or asks "Which part do you mean?" (1 minute). Use the wait signal. A child may pass.

#### 7 min. Write one sentence

Each child writes: "I used the ___." then "I know because ___" on the full-width line, then "The author wants to ___." with a few words each. A child may draw and say the words for you to scribe. Judge three look-fors: a feature is named; a reason points to what the feature does; a purpose is said. Mark nothing aloud.

Check: a feature, a reason and a purpose.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card of item s02-X53 aloud twice (30 seconds). Ask item s02-X53. Children tell a partner. Then read the card of item s02-X53b twice (30 seconds) and ask it; children tell a partner. The six then answer both to you, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes); mark the record sheet. Shown means for the first card that the text explains why photographs help, with the title or caption pointed to; for the second, the subheading What They Eat, with a word of reason. Shown with help means after one prompt: "What does the title say?" or "Which feature has the word eat in it?" Not yet seen means a purpose with no words to show it (error D), or a search of everything for the answer (error E).

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Read the commonest misses from day 52's check, without naming a child. Write the right spellings beside them. If the doubling was left out, compare hop and hope (error AB). If an ending was left out, underline it, read it alone, then the whole word (error Z). Then show three cards: stopped, rubbed, wanted. Children read each; if the ending sound is wrong, reblend it (error Y).

#### 15 min. Teach: the three sounds of -ed

Review from grade 1: -ed is one spelling with three sounds. After some words it says /t/, as in stopped; after others /d/, as in rubbed; and after t or d it says /id/, as in wanted. Say each aloud and have children say the ending alone (3 minutes).

Model wanted. Map w, a, n, t, and -ed, saying /w/ /o/ /n/ /t/ /id/. The a says /o/ here; star it. Blend it; then spell it with tiles and check the sound-spelling-map (error AA, a mapped part guessed from the picture). Then map rubbed, /r/ /u/ /b/ /d/, and stopped, /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/ /t/, saying the doubled letter and then the ending. Children say each ending and spell the word with tiles (6 minutes).

Then two kinds of words to read as wholes, not spelled today. He'd, I'd and you'd: the apostrophe stands in for letters that are left out, as in he would. Read each as a whole word and use it in a spoken sentence: "He'd like a turn." Say the limit: the apostrophe is not a letter. Last, o'clock is a mapped irregular word: the o and the apostrophe stand in for of the; star them, and put o'clock on the word wall (3 minutes). Children spell stopped and wanted with tiles, then write "Rob wanted to skip." from your dictation, twice, and check wanted against the wall (3 minutes).

Check: wanted spelled with -ed and a sentence with a capital and an end mark.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Round 1, 13 minutes: today's six sit with you for the oral card, two minutes each (12 minutes). Only the first two lines are used, the grade 1 words and the cycle 9 patterns. Each says the vowel sound in each word and the ending. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and the heard column. The other 24 children are in three stations of eight. Tiles: build rubbed, stopped and wanted, then running by doubling. Sort: the ten cycle 9 sort cards under three headings. Reread: pairs read Rob Is Skipping twice, the second time smoothly. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each: the groups move on and the six join the two stations they have not met.

Check: each child reads or builds each word and a partner says if it matches the card.

#### 3 min. Close

**Ask.** "Which sound does -ed make in wanted?" Expect: /id/, because the word ends in t before it.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the three questions on the board and draw page 4 with its glossary and index. One copy of article C: show the page on the board and let pairs point there. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: use any short book with a glossary or an index. No tiles: write letters on scraps.
**Skip.** The stations, the oral card and the sort.

- **10 min. Which tool?** Read your child three questions about a book that has a glossary or an index, such as what a word means, what page a topic is on, and what a picture shows. Your child names the best feature for each.
  **Say.** "Which feature would you use first, and why?" Expect: the glossary for a meaning, the index for a page, the caption for a picture.
- **10 min. The author.** Read a short made-up line aloud, such as "Pour the milk. Add the oats. Stir." Your child says what the author wants to do and the words that show it.
- **5 min. Spell.** Say stopped and wanted. Your child writes each and says which sound the -ed makes.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X53]] [[s02-X53b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs do two question cards and name the purpose only; keep the exit, the -ed lesson and the oral card. Longer: a child writes a question for a partner and the partner names the feature to use.

### Words and supports

Feature, locate, purpose, glossary, index, subheading, caption. Frames: "I used the ___ because ___." "The author wants to ___." "You said ___; I would add ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing and tracing are answers. A child may pass any turn.

### Tomorrow

Then children use the three sounds of -ed in a first reader and name groups of animals.
