# Day 2: Who welcomes Mira, and where?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the question words card on the wall. Write six questions on the board (see the partner part). Cut eight card halves for each pair. Choose today's six: the second group on the record sheet. Write their oral card on a slip: game, rope, mile, be, plant, wish; and "Pete can make a game at home." Have the story-map sheet, the talk routine card, the exit passage list and the record sheet to hand.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many children missed day 1's exit (error A, the order reversed), reread the paragraph that begins "I wonder what it is like inside" and write the three things Mira wonders on the board in the wrong order beside the right one. Say the right order together with a finger for each step: first, next, last. Otherwise partners say the four talk steps to each other (turn, pair, link, clarify) and show the wait signal.

#### 4 min. Words

Say each word, the class says it, then each goes in a sentence you say and the class echoes. Welcome: "We welcome a visitor." Partner: "My partner listens to me." Review: you worked with a partner in grade 1. Classroom: "Our classroom has a window." Children say one sentence with a word to a partner.

#### 12 min. Read chapter 2, Welcome

Show the chapter picture. Say aloud: "This story is made up. Mira, Ada and Ms. Park are invented." Read the chapter once, about three minutes, pausing after "Welcome to our classroom", after "Ada, would you stand up?" and after the name card. At each pause say "I notice ..." and let a child add. Then partners say one thing they remember.

#### 8 min. Model questions

Review: in kindergarten you learned that each question word asks for a kind of answer. Point to the question words card on the wall: who, what, where, when, why, how. Today you use the six together as a set. Show two questions on chart paper and answer each with a finger on the line.

Model one: "Who stood up when Ms. Park asked?" Say: "Who asks for a person, so I look for a name. The words say: Ada, would you stand up? A girl with a long braid stood up. It was Ada." Model two: "Where is Ada's desk?" Say: "Where asks for a place. The words say: a desk by the window."

**Ask.** Which question word asks for a person? Expect: who.

**Ask.** Which question word asks for a place? Expect: where.

Then say a fuzzy question: "Who made it?" Wait. Children say "Which part do you mean?" You answer "The name card." Say: "That question was not clear until you asked which part." Class answer: Ada.

#### 10 min. Ask a partner

The board holds six questions: Who made the name card? Where is the radiator? Who gave a big thumbs-up? Where do Mira's boots squeak? Who has silver glasses? Where does Ada say Mira can sit? Each child picks one, asks it, and the partner answers with "The words say ..." and a finger on the line. Then swap. If an answer is unclear, the asker says "Which part do you mean?" (error J if a child just says yes). Pairs work together at once. Hear your six, half a minute each, during the swap. Expected answers: Ada; under the window; a boy at the back; on the floor; Ms. Park; by Ada, at the desk by the window.

#### 8 min. The poem

Read Our School Day once through, two minutes. Say: "In this poem pages turn means the pages flip over." Read it again; the class joins the two lines that come back after each part. Pairs answer: "When does the poem say the school day begins?" Expect: with the morning bell.

#### 8 min. Words and picture

Review: in grade 1 you told what a picture shows that the words do not. Pairs point to the chapter 2 picture for each of three sentences you read: Ada's desk is by the window; the card says MIRA; Ms. Park holds the door. Then each pair says one thing the picture shows that the words do not. Expect: the snow outside the window, or that children look up. Accept any detail in the picture that the chapter's words leave out. Frame: "The words say ___; the picture shows ___."

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud twice the two sentences that begin "On the desk was a small card," and ask (s01-X2v2): "Where is the star on the card? Use the words of the story." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest say it to a partner. Shown means over the i, in the story's words. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What do the words say?" Not yet seen means the wrong place or none.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Show day 1's misses with counters. Say cape slowly; pairs push one counter for each sound: three. Then lay tiles: four letters. Say: "The e takes a tile but makes no sound; it tells the vowel to say its name." Compare cap (three sounds, three letters). If a child read cape short or wrote it with no e (error L), have them blend cap, then add the e and blend again.

#### 15 min. Reader

Pairs lay out the eight halves. Review: you learned closed and silent-e syllables yesterday. Read each half: nap, kin, in, vite, cup, cake, sun, shine. In nap, kin, in, cup and sun a letter closes the vowel, so it is short; in vite, cake and shine the e at the end is silent and the vowel says its name. Build the four words together, one at a time: nap and kin, in and vite, cup and cake, sun and shine. Each pair blends and writes each word as a whole.

Say the tricky words before reading: said, you, are, they, the, to, a, I. Map each: say its sounds and mark the tricky part (the ai in said, the ou in you). Do not let anyone guess from the picture or the first letter.

Model the first reading of A Note for Sam, a page at a time, then the class reads it in chorus.

**Ask.** Who is the note for? Expect: Pete.

**Ask.** What does the note ask? Expect: it asks him to bake cupcakes with Sam.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each. Tiles: spell cupcake, sunshine, invite and napkin from their halves. Sort: sort the eight halves into short (closed) and long (silent-e). Reread: partners read A Note for Sam, a page each. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each (the oral card: six words and one sentence, patterns taught on day 1 and grade 1 only); the others work in stations. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Say: "Tell your partner one word you read today by its letters, not by a picture." Expect: napkin, sunshine, invite or any word from the halves.

### Other rooms

No wall: write the question words on a sheet and set it where all can see. No printer: copy the eight halves on card. No projector: copy the two questions on chart paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No wall card: write the six question words on paper. No halves: cut a sheet into eight pieces.
**Skip.** The poem and the stations.

- **5 min. The question words.** Write who and where. Say who asks for a person and where asks for a place.
- **10 min. Welcome.** Read chapter 2 aloud. Say this story is made up.
  **Say.** "Where is Ada's desk?" Expect: by the window.
- **10 min. Words from halves.** Put nap with kin, and sun with shine. Read each.
  **Say.** "What does the e do in shine?" Expect: it is silent and the vowel says its name.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X2v2]] Reread those chapter 2 lines twice. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the poem to one reading and shorten the third rotation round; keep the questions, the picture part and the exit. Longer: pairs ask one why or how question from chapter 2 and point to the line that answers it.

### Words and supports

Welcome, partner, classroom, question words. Frames: "The words say ___." "Which part do you mean?" "The words say ___; the picture shows ___." Pointing is an answer; a child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 3 reads chapter 3 and adds the challenge and response boxes to the story-map. If many children gave error A on today's exit (the wrong person at the place), its opening repair rereads the two lines aloud with a finger on who and where.
