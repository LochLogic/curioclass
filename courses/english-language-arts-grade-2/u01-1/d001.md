# Day 1: The school question and the doorway

## Teacher page

### Before class

Tape the unit question to the wall: "How are schools around the world alike and different?" Choose today's six: the first group on the record sheet. Write the oral card for them on a slip: chop, fresh, thank, stand, drum, grin; and "Beth can sing with Ben." Lay out tiles, counters, the long and short sound cards, the story-map sheet and the exit passage list.

### The day

#### 8 min. Open the school question

Whole class, then pairs. Read the question on the wall aloud. Say: "Today we start a story about a school. First, what does a school have?" Each child draws one thing a school has (a room, a thing, or a helper's job) and writes one label word under it. Never ask about a child's home, family or a move. Pairs show each other and say "A school has ___." Tape the drawings under the question. A child who cannot write the label draws and tells you the word; you write it.

#### 5 min. Words before reading

Say each word, the class says it, then act it out once. Enter: step through the classroom doorway. Notice: look closely at something and point. Wonder: tilt your head and say "I wonder ...". Review: you used notice and wonder in kindergarten; enter is the new word. Children say a sentence with each to a partner.

#### 12 min. Read chapter 1, The Doorway

Show the chapter picture. Say aloud: "This story is made up. Mira, Mr. Davis, Ms. Park and Maple Street School are invented." Read the chapter once, about three minutes, with three pauses of about a minute each: after the crooked snowflake (say "I notice the snowflake is cut a little crooked. I wonder who made it"), after "One," she said, "Two. Three." (say "I wonder what she will do") and after the door swings open (partners whisper one thing they noticed). Then look at the picture for two minutes: "What do you notice?" Accept any detail a child points to.

#### 8 min. Retrieval

Review: in grade 1 you answered who and what questions about a text, named the characters, and said what a picture shows that the words do not. Reread the two Mr. Davis paragraphs aloud. Pairs, in turn, do four things, a minute each: name the character the paragraph is about; answer one of three cards you read aloud (What is the helper's name? Where does he lean? Who is expecting Mira?); say one thing the picture adds (he stands a few steps back); link to the partner's answer with "I agree because ...". In the last three minutes hear your six, half a minute each, while the other pairs repeat the link step. Unscored notes.

#### 12 min. What the words show about entering

Give each pair an event-response card. Review: in kindergarten you met event and response (day 50). Say: "An event is what happens in a story. A response is what a character says or does about it. A child's spoken reply is an answer; a character's is a response." Model on the board: draw a door in the Event box; in the Response box write what Mira does: hand on, hand off, hand on, "One, two, three," she pushes. Frame: "The event is a new door, so Mira ___."

**Ask.** What does Mira do first? Expect: she puts her hand on the door and takes it away.

**Ask.** What does Mira do next? Expect: she puts it back, says "One, two, three" and pushes.

If a child says "Mira is shy", say: "That tells what she is like. What does she do?" (error B). Pairs fill their cards, then one partner tells what Mira does first and the other what she does next. Walk and listen.

#### 10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify

Say: "In this routine, turn means face your partner. Last year take a turn meant have your go. Today the routine has four steps." Write them on the board. Turn: write or draw one detail, then face your partner. Pair: share it, one at a time. Link: "I agree because ..." or "I would add ...". Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" Review: you used I agree because, I would add and Which part? on a turn card in grade 1. Rehearse the wait signal: two fingers up, hands down, quiet; do it twice. Run it once on "What does Mira do before she enters?" a minute and a half a step. Walk to hear your six, half a minute each, during pair and link. If someone talks over a partner (error H), rehearse the signal again.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud twice the paragraph that begins "I wonder what it is like inside," then ask (s01-X1v2): "Tell me what Mira wonders, in the order the story gives." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means three things she wonders, in the story's order. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What does the story say next?" Not yet seen means a wrong order or no answer.

#### 10 min. Retrieval (foundations)

Review: in grade 1 you learned short and long vowel sounds and the silent e. Pairs use counters. Say "shop"; pairs move three counters, one for each sound. Say: "Change the first sound to /ch/." They read chop and write it. Then show cap and cape on the board. Pairs read each word and write each word; take no answer yet about why they differ. Note, unscored, who reads cape with a short vowel and who writes it without the e (error L).

#### 5 min. Sound sort

Each child holds a short card and a long card. Say each word once and pause: nest, bone, we, jump, tide, no, stop, tune. Children hold up the card that matches the vowel sound they hear. Then ask which words were long. Expect: bone, we, tide, no, tune. Note who held the wrong card for we or no.

#### 10 min. Words rule

Pairs get the word sort. Say: "We read words from the letters first, then check against a picture." Read the three columns with the class: closed (jump, stop, chin), silent-e (bone, smile, mule), open (go, she, no). Review: you learned these three kinds of syllable at the end of grade 1.

**Ask.** What do the letters after the vowel do? Expect: in jump, stop and chin a letter closes the vowel in and it is short; in bone, smile and mule the e at the end is silent and the vowel says its name; in go, she and no nothing follows, so the vowel says its name.

Model smile: map /s/ /m/ /ī/ /l/, say the e is silent, blend the word, then spell it with five tiles. Pairs spell go and bone with tiles.

#### 32 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each: tiles (spell jump, bone, she, stop, smile, mule), sort (sort the nine words of the word sort again, then say the rule), reread (partners read the six phrase cards: a big tub, she can jump, go home, stop and sit, a cute mule, no pup). Round 1, 12 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each, reading the oral card (six words and one sentence, grade 1 patterns only); the others work in stations. Rounds 2 and 3, 10 minutes each. Mark who was heard in the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "What helps me read a word with an e at the end?" Expect: the e is silent and the vowel says its name. Partners say it to each other.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the word sort on the board for the class to read together. No projector: draw the door on the board. No sound cards: stand up for long, crouch for short.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper. No crayons: use a pencil. No chapter: tell it from the story file.
**Skip.** The sound sort and the stations.

- **5 min. The question.** Read: "How are schools around the world alike and different?" Your child draws one thing a school has and writes one word.
- **10 min. The doorway.** Read chapter 1 aloud. Say this story is made up.
  **Say.** "What does Mira do before she goes in?" Expect: hand on the door, off, on; she says "One, two, three"; she pushes.
- **10 min. The e at the end.** Spell jump, bone and go with tiles.
  **Say.** "What does the e at the end do?" Expect: it is silent and the vowel says its name.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1v2]] Reread that chapter 1 paragraph twice. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut round 3 of the rotation (the six are still heard) and shorten the first retrieval to five minutes; keep the event-response card, the four steps and the exit. Longer: a child names a second thing Mira does at the door and what it shows she feels.

### Words and supports

Enter, notice, wonder, event, response. Frames: "The event is ___, so Mira ___." "I agree because ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write draws and you write the words. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 2 reads chapter 2 and asks who and where. If many children gave error A on today's exit (the order reversed), its opening repair shows the wrong order beside the right one and says it together.
