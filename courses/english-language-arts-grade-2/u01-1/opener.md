# Section 1.1 opener: Mira's first week (days 1 to 6)

## What the section does

For six days the class reads the first six chapters of *Mira's New Classroom*, an invented story about a girl who joins a class in the middle of the year. Children ask and answer who, what, where, when, why and how questions, tell how Mira responds to each challenge, and use the words and the pictures together to tell where the story happens. They also learn the talk routine (turn, pair, link, clarify) and review the long and short vowel sounds. The compelling question for the unit, "How are schools around the world alike and different?", stays on the wall. Never ask a child about their own move, home or family.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short opening repair, a word or two before reading, a read-aloud of one chapter, a model, partner work and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds a repair, work on the day's vowel patterns, a reader or a fresh passage, a 37-minute rotation of three stations (tiles, sort, reread) and a close. Cycle 1 covers closed, open and silent-e syllables, and on day 5 it ends with a 15-minute written check. Every exit is one question you read aloud; the child answers aloud, points or draws. You hear the day's rotating six, and the rest tell a partner. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A question has a place in the text.** Who and where questions point to a name or a place. Why and how questions point to a sentence, or to the words and the picture together. Say "The words say ..." and point to the line.
- **A response is something a character does or says.** A trait such as "shy" tells how a character is; a response tells what she does about an event. Put the event beside the action: "The challenge is ___, so Mira ___."
- **A picture can add what the words leave out.** The words give the setting; the picture shows more of it. Ask which source gave which fact.
- **Talk has four steps.** Turn (write or draw a detail), pair (share it), link ("I agree because" or "I would add"), clarify ("Which part do you mean?"). The two-finger signal means hands down and listen.
- **Everyone may pass.** Public talk is never a test of one child.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** a detail that the card or chapter does not give, or the wrong person or place. Reread the answering line with a finger and replace the detail.
- **B:** a trait where a response is wanted ("she is shy"). Lay the event beside two actions and let the child pick.
- **H, I, J, K:** talking over a partner, a comment that does not link, accepting an unclear answer, a question that ignores the speaker. Rehearse the signal, say the last idea first, ask "Which part do you mean?".
- **L, P, Q:** a long vowel read as a short one, a word named from its picture, and a spelling by sound that drops the pattern. Blend a matched pair, cover the picture, and compare with the wall words.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet, dated, and keep the sheet in your folder. Exits and the written check are unscored.
