# Day 4: Where is the story? The words and the picture

## Teacher page

### Before class

Give each pair a setting chart and an event-response card. Write on a slip: "Mira says, I will just watch." Write two action cards: "Ada says, You can watch first." and "Ada is nice." Today's six are the fourth group on the record sheet. Their oral card: stone, wave, flat, me, crush, spend; and "We can hike to the lake." Have the exit passage list ready.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

If many children missed day 3's exit (error B, a trait with no action), lay the slip beside the two action cards. Ask: "Which card tells what Ada does about it?" The class picks the first and says why: it is what she says. Otherwise partners say what a challenge is and what a response is, each with one example from chapter 3.

#### 12 min. Read chapter 4, The Long Hall

Show the picture. Say aloud: "This story is made up. Mira, Ada and Ms. Park are invented." Read the chapter once, about four minutes, pausing after "First came the long hall", after "That fish is called Pancake" and at the end. At each pause say "I picture ..." and let a child add one thing. Reread the paragraph about the long hall while the class looks at the top picture.

#### 10 min. Setting from two sources

Say: "The setting is where and when a story happens. You used where on day 2. Today we get it from two places: the words and the picture." Show the setting chart: the left column The words say, the right column The picture shows. Model on the long hall with a finger on each source. The words say a shiny floor that squeaks and rows of wooden cubbies. The picture shows that Mira now looks up and not at her feet. Say the frame: "The words say a shiny floor; the picture shows Mira looking up."

**Ask.** What do the words say about the hall? Expect: a shiny floor that squeaks, doors with names, cubbies full of boots, hats and scarves.

**Ask.** What does the picture show that the words do not say? Expect: Mira looks up, no longer at her feet.

If a child names a word from the picture alone, say: "Cover the picture and read the words first, then check the picture" (error P).

#### 13 min. Fill the chart

Pairs fill the setting chart for two places in chapter 4, the schoolyard and the library. They write or draw one fact in each column and point to the line or the part of the picture that gave it. They say the frame to each other. Run the four steps: turn (write or draw), pair, link, clarify. Hear your six, half a minute each. Expect for the yard: the words say snow along the fence, a bare oak tree, a red climbing frame; the picture shows the hopscotch square. Expect for the library: the words say a round blue rug, tall shelves, a fish tank; the picture shows one orange fish in a small box. Accept any fact a child can point to. If a partner says "yes" to an unclear answer (error J), ask "Which part do you mean?"

#### 12 min. My own question

Show the question words card and the tally on chart paper: who, what, where, when, why, how. Say: "Who and where were used on day 2. Pick a word nobody has used yet." Each child writes one question about chapter 4 with that word, four minutes: "What is in the yard?", "When does Ada show Mira around?", "How does Mira feel at the end?" Then partners swap and answer by pointing to a line or the picture, four minutes. Back to the writer: "Is that the answer you wanted?" Two minutes. Make a tally mark for each word chosen. Hear your six during the swap. A child who cannot write draws and tells you the question; you write it.

Check: The answer matches the question word and points to a line or a part of the picture.

#### 3 min. Vary the words

Count the marks together. Say: "Today we used ___ the most and ___ the least." Choose why or how for tomorrow and circle it on the card.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud twice the first two paragraphs of chapter 3, show the chapter 3 picture and ask (s01-X4v2): "Tell me one thing the picture shows about the room that the words do not say." Your six answer aloud, about twenty seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means a detail only the picture holds. Shown with help means right after one prompt: "What do you see that the words do not say?" Not yet seen means a detail not in the picture, or a repeat of the words.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Write not and note on the board with the e covered, then shown. Blend not, then uncover the e and blend note. Say: "The e takes a place and makes no sound. It tells the vowel to say its name." Pairs push counters for each sound and lay tiles for each letter: three sounds, three letters; three sounds, four letters. If a child read note short or wrote it with no e (error L), have them blend both again.

#### 15 min. Fresh passage

Say the new words once: Jess, Pete, hike, hill, gate, bell, tone. Map each: say the sounds and mark the pattern. Pairs read Jess and Pete on their card, a sentence each, about eight minutes. If a child names a word from the first letter or from what would make sense (error P), say "Read the letters first, then check."

Then confirm meaning, three minutes. Ask: "Who spots the bell?" Expect: Pete. Ask: "What does Jess do to the bell?" Expect: she taps it. Dictate "We can sit and rest." twice, four minutes. Children write it; read the sentence aloud and check each word against the patterns.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Three stations, ten children each. Tiles: spell hike, gate, me, spot, tone, bell. Sort: sort the words of the passage into short (closed) and long (silent-e). Reread: partners read A Note for Sam, a page each, then the passage. Round 1, 13 minutes: your six sit with you, two minutes each (the oral card: six words and one sentence; new material modeled first); the others work in stations. Rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Say: "Tell your partner one place in the story and one fact from the words about it." Expect: the library, a round blue rug; the yard, a bare oak tree; any fact children can point to.

### Other rooms

No wall card: write the six question words on the board. No printer: draw the two columns on a folded sheet. No tiles: write letters on torn paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on torn paper. No chart: fold a sheet in two and head the sides Words and Picture.
**Skip.** The passage and the stations.

- **5 min. Two sources.** Say a story gets its setting from the words and from the picture.
- **10 min. The Long Hall.** Read chapter 4 aloud. Say this story is made up.
  **Say.** "What is in the yard?" Expect: snow, a bare oak tree, a red climbing frame.
- **10 min. My question.** Your child writes one question about the chapter with when or how, and you answer it by pointing.
  **Say.** "Where did you find the answer?" Expect: a line or the picture.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X4v2]] Reread the first two paragraphs of chapter 3 twice and show its picture. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the second chart place and the third rotation round; keep the model, the own question and the exit. Longer: a child draws a place from chapter 4 and labels one fact from the words.

### Words and supports

Setting, picture, question words. Frames: "The words say ___; the picture shows ___." "Which part do you mean?" Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write draws and you write the words. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 5 reads chapter 5 and answers why and how questions. If many children gave error A on today's exit (a detail that is not in the picture, such as a fish tank), its opening repair rereads the two paragraphs with the chapter 3 picture and replaces the detail.
