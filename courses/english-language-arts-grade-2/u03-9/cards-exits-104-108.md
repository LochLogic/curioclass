# Exit cards, days 104 to 108

Say aloud each time: "This card is made up for practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of every card. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading. Read each card twice, then ask the one question. Answers and wrong answers are in items.csv. Where a day has more than one row, ask them one after another, one ask each.

- **Day 104 (s03-X104).** This card is invented for practice. Read aloud: "A moth rests on a pale flower at night. Pollen sticks to its body. Then it flies to a pink flower." Say: "Tell this visit as the moth, with I, in two sentences that keep the order."
- **Day 105 (s03-X105).** This card is invented for practice. Show the sources. Source A: "A butterfly sips nectar with a long tongue." Source B: "Pollen sticks to its body when it lands." Show two poster lines: "Butterflies sleep at night." "Pollen sticks to its body." Say: "Point to the line the sources support."
- **Day 105 (s03-X105b).** Same lines. Show two labels: "night" and "body". Say: "Point to the label that goes with that line."
- **Day 106 (s03-X106).** This card is invented for practice. Show the plan words: "moth" and "pollen sticks to its body". Say: "Say one whole sentence a listener could understand, using the plan words."
- **Day 107 (s03-X107).** This card is invented for practice. Show the line: "A dusty bug rests on the plant." Say: "Read this line to me so a listener can follow."
- **Day 108 (s03-X108).** This card is invented for practice. Show the poster line: "The bee takes dust." Show Source A: "Pollen is the yellow powder on a flower." Show Source B: "Hairs on a bee carry it to the next flower." Say: "Say the line again with one word from Source A and one word from Source B."
