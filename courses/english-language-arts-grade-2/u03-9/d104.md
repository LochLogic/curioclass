# Day 104: Hear the bee-view story and tell a visit with detail

## Teacher page

### Before class

Open the day 103 exit marks: note who joined the sticking to the landing in one sentence and who did not; seat a child who did beside one who did not. Mark today's six, group 2 of five, children 7 to 12. Clear a wall space for the class story line. Keep the exit card out of sight until the exit; it is new.

### The day

#### 4 min. Retrieval (e1-describe-present)

Review: telling a find in order with a drawing (grade 1). Pairs tell a find the class met in grade 1, two or three complete sentences in order, and draw it in a corner of the strip (2 minutes each way; both pairs talk at once). Note one miss, such as sentences in a jumble.

#### 8 min. Read aloud: One Bee, Two Flowers (02)

Ask first: "Listen for what the bee thinks." Read the whole story (about 5 minutes); children join the line "One bee. Two flowers." on the last page. Then ask: "Which parts did the bee only think or say?" Children raise a hand to answer; accept the thoughts and sayings on pages 2, 4, 6, 9 and 10 (3 minutes). Say the story is made up and the bee is a character. A child may pass.

#### 12 min. Check against the articles

Review: telling a stated fact from an imagined idea (grade 2, unit 2, day 43). Read aloud the lines of 01-b that say a bee drinks nectar and gathers pollen with its hairy legs, and the lines of 01-a that say pollen rubs onto the sticky stigma and seeds can grow (2 minutes). Model one strip together: "A bumblebee drinks nectar from the flower." Say where the article says it and put the strip under "true of real bees" (1 minute). Pairs sort the other five strips under "true of real bees" and "imagined", a finger on the article line for each true one (6 minutes). Share: two pairs give one strip and the proof; the class links ("I add...") (3 minutes). The true strips are the nectar, the pollen on the stigma and the seeds. The imagined strips are the bee's spoken words, liking the girl and the taste of a sunny day.

#### 10 min. Pairs order the picture cards

Say the cards are invented for practice: a fly and a daisy, not the bee's story. Each pair has three shuffled cards (a fly on a white daisy; dust on the fly's body; the fly on a yellow flower with dust falling off). One child lays them in the order they must go (3 minutes). The partner points to one card and asks, "How do you know this card is not first?" and the child answers from the picture (2 minutes). Swap with the cards shuffled (3 minutes). Then each child says one thing they can see on one card, in a whole sentence (2 minutes). They do not retell the visit; the telling comes at the exit. All pairs work at once; circulate.

#### 8 min. Talk routine

Review: turn, pair, link, clarify (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Ask: "What does the bee notice with its body?" One minute to look at the story pictures and think. Pairs talk (3 minutes, 90 seconds each). Two pairs share; the class links with "I add..." (3 minutes). One child asks "Which part do you mean?" about a vague answer (1 minute). Expect fur, back legs, sun on the wings, the sticky stigma, the breeze. Any child may pass.

#### 10 min. Sentence strips

Model with the class (2 minutes): write "The bee landed." beside "I landed on a soft yellow flower." Ask what was added; children say what you can see, feel, smell or taste. Each child takes a spread of the story from their seat number (spreads 3 to 8), draws what happens and writes or says one sentence with a detail from it (6 minutes). Each tapes the strip on the wall line in spread order (2 minutes). A scribe writes for a child who asks.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say the card is invented. Read the card twice and ask the one question (1 minute): [[s03-X104]]. All tell a partner their two sentences (1 minute). Hear the six, 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Tag sentences in a jumble error B, a piece that stops error O, a telling with no detail error Z.

#### 3 min. Transitions

Children change blocks and take the student page.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Nothing is routed here, so this is a warm-up, not a repair. Write the five spelling words of day 103 on the board. Children read them (2 minutes), cover them and write two from memory (3 minutes).

#### 15 min. Reread and spell

Pairs echo-read the cycle 18 reader for accuracy, a sentence each, then say what the label is for (5 minutes). Review: -ible and -able mean able to be (grade 2, unit 3.8, day 103). New words are modeled first (3 minutes): breakable, movable, lovable; say the word, break it at the ending, check the entry when unsure. Spell five (5 minutes): breakable, movable, lovable, reversible, enjoyable; the word, a short sentence, the word again. Dictate one sentence (2 minutes): "The lid is movable."

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: the six read the cycle 18 oral card to you, two minutes each; 24 are at stations. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes. Tiles: breakable, movable, lovable, enjoyable. Sort: ten slips by -able or -ible. Reread: the reader for expression. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which end of the word says able to be?" Children tell a partner, then say it together: both -able and -ible.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the strips and cards by hand. A child who cannot write dictates to a scribe; pointing to a card is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw three pictures of a visit. No tiles: letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The words.

- **10 min. Real or made up?** Read aloud: "A bee drinks nectar." "A bee likes a girl." Your child says which one a real bee does.
  **Say.** "How could we check?" Expect: look in a book or article.
- **10 min. Add a detail.** Say "The bee landed." Your child adds what the bee could feel or see.
  **Say.** "What did you add?" Expect: a soft petal, a yellow flower.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child writes breakable and movable and underlines the ending.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X104]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the talk routine; keep the sort, the cards, the strips and the exit. Longer: a child adds a second strip to the wall line.

### Words and supports

Imagined, real, detail, strip, spread. Frames: "I can see ___." "The article says ___, so it is real." A child may say the sentence first and then write it.

### Tomorrow

Day 105 builds a pollinator poster from two of the articles: a fact, a label and a drawing. Today's exit tells you who keeps the order and who adds a detail. The cycle 18 reader comes back for sorting.
