# Day 105: Choose facts and labelled drawings for a pollinator poster

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put the four articles on the tables and the planning sheets in a pile. Set the shared keyboard with a queue of 15 pairs, each with its day 102 typed sentence and written feedback. Mark today's six, group 3 of five, children 13 to 18. Read day 104's exit marks: a child who added no detail (error Z) works beside you at the first table.

### The day

#### 8 min. Launch

Today is an application day: the class makes, nothing new is taught. Read aloud the "how pollen moves" paragraph of each of 01-b, 01-c, 01-d and 01-e (about 2 minutes). Ask: "Which two visitors will you put on your poster?" Each child picks two and says to a partner one fact from each that shows how the visitor and the flower help each other (4 minutes). Each writes the two article letters at the top of the planning sheet (2 minutes). A child may take 01-a instead of one visitor.

#### 30 min. Stations

Poster building by hand, a fact, a label and a drawing for each of the two visitors; nothing is traced. A planning sheet holds one fact, its article letter, one label and one drawing, so a child fills two. Model one on the board with a visitor no one chose, the hummingbird: fact "Its face is dusted with pollen", article e, label "bill", a bill drawn by hand (3 minutes). Children work with the article sheet at the table and a finger on the line they take (about 25 minutes). A child who finishes writes a question a listener might ask at the foot of the sheet.

At the shared keyboard, the pairs take turns from the queue (review from day 102, grade 2, unit 3.8): each pair retypes its revised sentence from its written feedback. A turn is 80 seconds typing and 10 seconds to change over, 90 seconds in all; 15 pairs take 22 minutes 30 seconds, and the next pair stands ready, so about 7 minutes of the station are slack for a pair that needs a second try. Each child gives the poster about 28 minutes.

#### 12 min. Share at tables

Review: turn, pair, link, clarify (grade 2, unit 1, day 1). Pairs show the poster, one child at a time, 3 minutes each way: the owner reads a fact and says its article letter; the partner asks one clarifying question, such as "Which part do you mean?" (6 minutes). Swap partners and do it again, one fact only (6 minutes). Any child may pass the question.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say the card is invented. Show the board card with the two sources and the two poster lines, and read it aloud twice (1 minute): [[s03-X105]]. Then ask the second ask: [[s03-X105b]]. All children point with a finger to the board card on your signal for each ask, and you watch the fingers (1 minute). Hear the six one at a time at the card, 30 seconds for both asks (3 minutes). Tag the line neither source gives error C.

#### 5 min. Transitions

Children change blocks, take the student page and put the poster in the folder.

#### 5 min. Opening repair (foundations)

Nothing is routed here, so this is a warm-up, not a repair. Children read the day 104 spelling words from the board (2 minutes), then say what -able means (1 minute) and write one of the words from memory (2 minutes).

#### 15 min. Reread, sort and use the pattern

Pairs reread the cycle 18 reader for accuracy and expression (4 minutes). Groups of five sort the ten cards into -ible and -able, saying each ending's word out loud as it lands (6 minutes). Review: -ible and -able mean able to be (grade 2, unit 3.8, day 103). Each child says one word in a short sentence to a partner, then writes the sentence under the sorted cards (5 minutes). Hear one sentence from each group.

#### 37 min. Rotation

Standard rotation, review from unit 3, day 78. Round 1 is 13 minutes: the six read the cycle 18 oral card to you, two minutes each; 24 are at stations. Rounds 2 and 3 are 12 minutes. Tiles: visible, flexible, readable, fixable. Sort: slips by -able or -ible. Reread: the reader for accuracy. Mark the heard column.

#### 3 min. Close

Ask: "Which of -ible or -able did you hear last?" Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the planning sheet by hand. A child who cannot write dictates the fact and label to a scribe; pointing to the line in the article is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: use the fact your child tells you. No tiles: letters on torn paper.
**Skip.** The words.

- **10 min. Choose a fact.** Your child tells you one thing a bee, a butterfly, a bat or a hummingbird does at a flower.
  **Say.** "How does the flower get helped?" Expect: pollen moves to the next flower.
- **10 min. Label and draw.** Your child draws it and writes one label word.
  **Say.** "Which word names the part you drew?" Expect: the label.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child reads readable and visible and says what the ending means.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X105]] and [[s03-X105b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one visitor, one fact; keep the share and the exit. Longer: a child writes a question a listener might ask.

### Words and supports

Poster, fact, label, article, source. Frames: "My fact is ___." "It came from article ___." A child may say the fact first and then write it.

### Tomorrow

Day 106 plans three spoken sentences from the poster. Today's exit tells you who points to a line a source really gives. The cycle 18 reader returns for a fresh passage.
