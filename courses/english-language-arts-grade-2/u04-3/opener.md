# Section 4.3 opener: beats, rhymes and repeated lines (days 126 to 128)

## What the section does

For three days the class reads a poem and a song that sound like the things they describe. Children clap the beat, find the repeated lines, hear the rhyming words and the words that start with the same sound, and say what each adds. On day 128 the cycle 21 written check closes the prefix work, and a short comparison of two versions is rehearsed.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short opening repair, the day's reading, a model, talk, pair work and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds a repair, work on the cycle's spelling pattern, a reader, a rotation of stations with the oral card, and a close. Every exit is one card you read aloud twice and one ask on paper or aloud. A child outside the group heard is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Say aloud that every practice card is made up.

## The ideas at an adult's level

- **A beat is steady.** The beat is the pulse you can clap. A repeated line is a line said again.
- **A rhyme is words that end with the same sound.** Alliteration is words that start with the same sound.
- **Naming is not enough.** A child who says "it repeats" has named the device. The child must also say what it adds, to the beat or the meaning.
- **The poem and the song are made for our class.** Say that the practice lines are made up.

## The errors in plain words

- A sound device is named with no effect said (O).
- A word is guessed from its first letter instead of read (Q).
- A taught spelling pattern is not used in a new word (R).
- A spelling is not checked with the dictionary (S).

Show the right answer beside the wrong one, say the link together, and let the child try again. Repairs are five minutes at the start of the next day.

## Marking

One scale for every look-for: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record it on the record sheet. Nothing is scored beyond the exits and the oral and written cycle checks; they tell you what to repair.
