# Day 126: Hear how a steady beat and a repeated line carry a poem

## Teacher page

### Before class

Mark today's six for the exit and the six for the oral card (children 13 to 18). Print 06-a and 06-b in two colours; hang the exit cards for days 126 to 128 with the picture cards. Write twelve word slips: ahead, alone, along, around, asleep, agree, decode, decrease, defend, disagree, distrust, distract. Say aloud today that the poem is made for our class and every practice card is made up.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Day 125's exit ticket, s04-X125: Snail says, Let us wait for the rain. Beetle says, Let us move before the rain. Read each line aloud and ask "Who wants what?" (2 minutes). Then read the two lines again in two different voices so the class hears two wants (3 minutes). Marking the waiting snail for Beetle is error C: read the line, then say who wants what. Any child may pass.

#### 12 min. Read 06-a

Say the words bloom, petals, stem and visit and show each in the poem (1 minute). Read Room for Wings aloud once (1 minute). Review: you clapped a beat in grade 1, day 5. A beat is a steady pulse you can clap, like a heartbeat. Clap the first stanza together on the stressed words (3 minutes). Chant the whole poem four times, the class joining the repeated line each time (4 minutes). Pairs read the stanzas by turns, one stanza each (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Model

Clap the line "Room for wings, room for wings": four claps, on room, wings, room, wings. The beat stays the same in every line (2 minutes). Pairs find every line that comes back in the poem and mark it in the second colour (3 minutes). The two lines "Room for wings, room for wings, Room for all the flying things" come back after every stanza. Now say what they do: they ask again and again for room, and the steady beat sounds like wings going. Describe means say what the sound adds, not only name it (2 minutes). Give a made-up answer: "It says room for wings a lot." Pairs say whether it tells what the line adds, and fix it so it does (3 minutes). The fix names the effect: asking again and again, with a beat like wings.

#### 8 min. Talk

Turn, pair, link, clarify on "What does the repeated line ask for?" (3 minutes). Link: "I heard ___ say ___; I would add ___" (3 minutes). Share three in the whole class (2 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 20 min. Independent: recount

Pairs take 01 or 02. One partner recounts the telling in order with the story-map strip (4 minutes); the other does the same (4 minutes). Then both name the event that shows the lesson they found in unit 4.1 (4 minutes). Confer with the six heard for the remaining 8 minutes, about 80 seconds each. Review: you made a story-map strip and found a lesson that an event holds up (grade 2, unit 4, days 119 to 122).

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is invented. Read the card twice (1 minute): [[s04-X126]]. Each child marks one of the two pictures (2 minutes). Hear the six, 15 seconds each: [[s04-X126b]] (2 minutes). Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Saying "it repeats" names the line but leaves out what it adds (error O).

#### 5 min. Opening repair

No repair is routed to this part. Use it to retrieve: children write alone and agree on slates and tell a partner the part that is not stressed. Review: schwa a (grade 2, unit 4, day 124).

#### 12 min. Reread the cycle 21 reader

Pairs read A Moth in the Garden aloud for accuracy, one page each (6 minutes). Then read the whole once more smoothly, pausing at each full stop so it sounds like talking (6 minutes, RF.2.4b). After each reading ask what Kit wants for the moth. Reading a word from its first letter is error Q: cover the picture, touch each tile, blend.

#### 12 min. Rotation

Standard rotation. Children 13 to 18 read the cycle 21 oral card to you, two minutes each including the swap (12 minutes). The other twenty-four use tiles, the sort and the reader in two stations of twelve. Mark the heard column. A printed word misread is error AE.

#### 15 min. Sort

Pairs sort the twelve word slips into two columns, schwa a and de- or dis- (8 minutes). Check by saying each word and where the weak uh sound sits. Review: prefix de- means undo or take away and dis- means not (grade 2, unit 4, day 124). Then each child spells four words on slate or paper, said in a sentence and said again: alone, agree, defend, distract (7 minutes). Spelling alone as elone is error R: compare the new word with the schwa a on the chart.

#### 11 min. Use the pattern in sentences

Each child writes one sentence with a word from the sort, in the box on the student page (7 minutes), then reads it to a partner (4 minutes). Check one word against the dictionary page if unsure. A spelling left unchecked is error S.

#### 5 min. Close

Ask: "Which part of agree is not stressed?" Children tell a partner, then say it together.

### Other rooms

No colour printing: underline the repeated line and circle the rhyming words. No tiles: paper squares. No sets of slips: write the twelve words on the board and children point. Pointing is an answer.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on paper squares. No printed poem: copy the first four lines by hand.
**Skip.** The sort and the dictionary page.

- **10 min. Clap.** Read the first four lines of Room for Wings and clap the beat together on each stressed word.
  **Say.** "Which line comes back after every verse?" Expect: Room for wings, room for wings.
- **10 min. What it adds.** Say the repeated line three times, slowly and steadily.
  **Say.** "What does the repeated line ask for?" Expect: Room for wings.
- **10 min. Words.** Your child writes alone and agree and says the part that is not stressed.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X126]] and [[s04-X126b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reading, the model, one recount by each pair and the exit. Longer: a pair adds a second repeated line to the poem and says what it adds.

### Words and supports

Beat, repeat, bloom, petals, stem, visit, adds. Frames: "The repeated line asks for ___." "The beat sounds like ___." Say it in the home language first; clapping or pointing is an answer.

### Tomorrow

Day 127 reads the song, 06-b, and begins by repairing today's exit if needed. Today's exit tells you who can say what a repeated line adds: if many said only "it repeats" (error O), day 127 opens with a five-minute repair, asking what the repeated line makes you feel or see. The six heard on the oral card tell you who needs a group.
