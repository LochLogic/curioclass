# Exit cards, days 147 to 149

Rows that open "This card is invented for practice" are made up: say so aloud and print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of the card. The other rows reread a passage of the unit's own texts, named in the row. Read the card or passage twice, then ask the one question. Answers and wrong answers are in items.csv; they are not printed here. Each day has two rows: the first is marked on paper; the second is spoken and is heard from the rotating six.

- **Day 147 (s04-X147).** This card is invented for practice. Show the strip, printed large and read aloud once: "dear class we will meet at Oak Hill park your helpers". Say: "Write the strip again as three lines, the greeting, the message and the closing, with capital letters and commas."
- **Day 147 (s04-X147b).** Same strip. Say: "Say why Park needs a capital letter in Oak Hill Park."
- **Day 148 (s04-X148).** This card is invented for practice. Read aloud twice: "An invitation: a big picture of a flower at the top, then the words Come to our show, then at the bottom in tiny letters Room 7, Tuesday, 1:15." Show two layouts. Layout 1 is as read. Layout 2 puts the room line in large print under the title. Say: "Mark the layout where a guest finds the room fastest."
- **Day 148 (s04-X148b).** Same card. Say: "Say why that layout helps a guest."
- **Day 149 (s04-X149v2).** Show the card with the grasshopper's and the ant's summer lines from the first telling. Read both aloud twice. Say: "Underline the line said by the one who wants to play."
- **Day 149 (s04-X149bv2).** Same card. Say: "Read both lines so a listener hears who wants to play and who wants to work."
