# Day 147: Write a class invitation with commas and capital letters

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put yesterday's edited cards and envelopes in the folder (scored on day 154); carry day 146's exit marks to the record sheet. Open the shared device on a text editor. Write eight name cards a pair: Mill Street, Bridge Road, Willow Lane, Cedar School, Maya, Sam, Rosa, Ben. Mark the six for the exit ask (children 13 to 18) and the six for the oral card (19 to 24). Say aloud that the exit card is made up.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Point to the letter-part card: greeting, body, closing. Review: you learned the parts of a letter in unit 2 (day 66), a comma after the greeting and after the closing, and a capital for a name (days 65 to 67). Pairs say one part each and what follows it: the greeting and the closing each take a comma.

#### 10 min. The invitation

Show the printed invitation model for layout only; ask what a guest looks at first (2 minutes). Then show a finished strip, invented for practice: "Dear friends," / "Please come to Bridge Road on Friday." / "With thanks,". Review: capitals and commas from unit 2. Children tick each capital and each comma already there and say what each one marks (4 minutes, L.2.2a, L.2.2b). Not taught as new. Pairs then sort the eight name cards into place names and person names and say the rule for the place names: every word of the name begins with a capital (4 minutes). Cedar School is a place, so both words are capitals.

#### 30 min. Write and type

First agree one place and one day for the class show and write them on the board with their capitals (for example Mill Street Library, Thursday). Each child writes the invitation by hand on the student page: a greeting, one sentence naming the place and day the class chose, and a closing (W.2.5, L.2.2a, L.2.2b). Fifteen pairs take the shared device in turn, 2 minutes each: one child types the greeting, the other types the place line (W.2.6). You stay at the device for all 30 minutes and ask each pair for one choice each child made, such as a word or a place; the pair writes it on the contribution card. Pairs not at the device keep writing, then check each place name against the street and park name card, copying Mill Street or Bridge Road if they need the pattern. Error AA: a child who only watches the typing says "nice" and names no choice; hand that child the keyboard for ten seconds and ask what to type next.

#### 10 min. Pairs check

Pairs check each other's invitation against the editing checklist, one look at a time: a capital for every word of a place name, a comma after the greeting, a comma after the closing, one spelling checked in the printed dictionary (4 minutes each way, 2 minutes to swap). The partner may point but not change the page. Hear the two not reached on day 146's exit, 45 seconds each with the swap, at your table. Walk and tap a line that misses a mark; ask what the checklist box asks.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is made up. Show the strip, printed large, and read it once: [[s04-X147]]. Children write the three lines on their exit paper while you hear the six at your table, 45 seconds each, one at a time (4 minutes 30 seconds): [[s04-X147b]]. Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The strip stays on the board until the six are done.

#### 5 min. Opening repair

No repair is routed here. Pairs warm up on slates: build vertical and typical with tiles, tap the two parts and say which ending each takes. Review: -cal and -cle both say the same sound at the end, from day 144.

#### 15 min. A fresh passage

Read the title Kit and Bo and nothing else. Children read the passage in pairs, finger under the words (5 minutes, RF.2.4a). It is not the reader. Then confirm the meaning (RF.2.4c, 5 minutes): **Ask.** "What does Bo do all day?" Expect: sings and chirps. **Ask.** "What does Kit read about?" Expect: birds of tropical lands. **Ask.** "What is a miracle for Bo?" Expect: a sunny day. A child who guesses a word from its first letter reads it again, tile by tile (error Q). Then the class rereads smoothly, one sentence each by turns (5 minutes).

#### 12 min. Rotation

Children 19 to 24 read the cycle 25 oral card to you, two minutes each including the swap. Model one word on the board before the first turn: particle, tapped in three beats and blended. The others use tiles and the cycle 25 reader at stations. Mark the heard column.

#### 15 min. Spell with tiles and cloze strips

This is not dictation; dictation is the day 148 check. Pairs build vertical, typical, particle and vehicle on tiles, tap the parts, blend every letter and check the ending against the pattern (6 minutes; Review: tile builds from day 144). Then each pair takes the cut copy of Kit and Bo with six words covered: vocal, comical, tropical, logical, article and miracle. Each child chooses a word from the bank, spells it in the gap and reads the sentence to confirm it fits (9 minutes). Error R: a pattern not carried over; point to the ending and ask which ending the sentence needs.

#### 8 min. Dictionary

Each pair picks one cycle word from article, vehicle, particle or tentacle, finds it in the printed beginning dictionary and checks the spelling, then says what they compared (L.2.2e). Review: finding an entry from unit 1 (day 27). If a word has no entry, pick another. A spelling left unchecked is error S.

#### 5 min. Close

Each child reads one sentence from Kit and Bo to the partner, 45 seconds each way with the swap. The class says the two endings together.

### Other rooms

No device: pairs write the invitation by hand, rule the layout with a ruler and say aloud what they would type; the typing goal is then left incomplete and noted on the record sheet. No printed model: draw the layout of three lines on the board. Read the exit strip aloud twice and let the answer be written on folded paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No device: write by hand. No name cards: write the names on scraps.
**Skip.** The device, the oral card and the cloze strips.

- **10 min. Capitals.** Read the invitation strip on the page together.
  **Say.** "Which words begin with a capital in a place name?" Expect: every word of the name.
- **15 min. Write.** Your child writes a greeting, a sentence about where and when, and a closing, and ticks each look on the page.
- **5 min. Spell.** Your child builds the -cal and -cle words on the page.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X147]] and [[s04-X147b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the invitation, the pairs check and the exit. Longer: add a second sentence to the invitation naming something to see.

### Words and supports

Invitation, greeting, closing, guest, capital, comma. Frames: "Please come to ___." "I chose ___ because ___." Read the cards aloud, enlarge the print, allow a scribe for the body of the invitation and a partner to type; the checks stay with the child.

### Tomorrow

Day 148 opens with the contribution cards (error AA): each pair says one choice it made today. The invitation drafts come back for peer feedback and layout; if many missed a capital or a comma on today's exit, each pair rereads its greeting and closing against the letter-part card first. Today's exit marks go to the record sheet.
