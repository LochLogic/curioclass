# Day 149: Reread the invitation and rehearse a tale in two voices

## Teacher page

### Before class

Carry day 148's exit marks to the record sheet. Make six groups of five and let each group choose one tale from texts 01 to 04 (the two ant versions, the hummingbird, the lizard). Mark the six for the spoken exit ask (children 7 to 12) and the six for the oral card (children 1 to 6). Say aloud that the exit card is made up for practice.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

No new teaching. Hold up the two-voice card and name its two marks, the arrow and the wave. Review: in unit 4 you learned that a character wants something (day 124), and in unit 1 you read an exchange in two voices (day 9). Each group says what one character in its tale wants and which mark fits that character.

#### 10 min. Reread the invitation

Pairs reread their printed invitation aloud, finger under the words, first for accuracy and then smoothly (RF.2.4b, 8 minutes). Then each child tells the partner where a guest finds the room, 45 seconds each way with the swap (2 minutes at the end; if many chose the small room line or said a partner typed it on day 148's exit, spend the whole 2 minutes here).

#### 25 min. Rehearse in parts

Six groups of five rehearse the chosen tale. Each child takes one character or the narrator; where a tale has fewer than five parts, two children share the narrator by turns. Show the voice with speed, loudness or pitch, never with an accent or a costume, and never copy how real people speak. Listen to each group for 4 minutes in turn (6 x 4 = 24 minutes inside the 25), 48 seconds a child with the change-over counted, so no group is cut short. While you wait, the other groups rehearse alone: a partner ticks on the two-voice card the mark that fits each speaker, and the group claps the beat of one line. Mark nothing as a score; it is rehearsal. Error C: two characters read in one voice; ask what each wants before the voice is used again.

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn, pair, link, clarify on **Ask.** "How did the voice show what the character wanted?" Expect: a voice made to fit the character, with a reason from the tale. Turn: each child speaks 45 seconds in a pair, then moves to a new pair for a second round (4 minutes). Link: "I heard ___ say ___; I want to add ___" (2 minutes). Clarify: "Which part do you mean?" (1 minute). Three pairs share to the class, about 45 seconds each (3 minutes). Any child may pass. Review: turn, pair, link, clarify from unit 1.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say aloud that the card is made up. Read the card twice and show it: [[s04-X149]]. Call the first of the six to your table at once. The class underlines one line at their seats, then pairs read the two printed lines to each other, 45 seconds each way, while you hear the six, about 45 seconds each: [[s04-X149b]]. Mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Close

Three children each name one voice from today that helped a listener, about 45 seconds each. The class says thank you to the groups.

#### 5 min. Retrieve

On slates, build tropical and article with tiles and say which ending each takes. Review: -cal and -cle from day 144.

#### 15 min. Model, map, blend: cycle 26

New pattern. The ending -ous says the same sound as the ending -us (the s is not a plural). Write enormous on the board. Tap its three beats, map the sounds to the letters on the sound-spelling map, blend the whole word and check it in a sentence (3 minutes). Do the same with marvelous, dangerous, circus, bonus, chorus and octopus (6 minutes). Pairs then build each word on tiles, tap the parts and blend every letter (4 minutes), and sort the seven words into -ous and -us (2 minutes). A child who guesses from the first letter reads the word again, tile by tile (error Q).

#### 15 min. First reading: The Joyous Bus

Read the title and the first page aloud. Pairs echo-read the other six pages, finger under the words (8 minutes, RF.2.4a). Ask after the pages: **Ask.** "Where do the children go?" Expect: to a park. **Ask.** "What do they find?" Expect: crocus in bloom. Confirm each answer from the print (4 minutes, RF.2.4c). The class rereads pages 1 to 3 together (3 minutes).

#### 12 min. Rotation

Children 1 to 6 read the cycle 26 oral card to you, two minutes each including the swap; this first sitting is diagnostic. Model one word on the board before the first turn: marvelous, tapped and blended. The others use tiles and the reader at stations. Mark the heard column.

#### 13 min. Spell with cloze strips and fixes

This is not dictation; the cycle check is later. Write four cloze strips on the board, each with a gap for an -ous or -us word from today (for example "The elephant is ___." and "The ___ rides a bike."), and children write the word on slates (5 minutes). Then fix four wrong spellings: enormus, bonas, marvelus, chorous (4 minutes). Map the irregular words they, are and says: circle the letters that do not say what you expect, with no guessing from pictures or first letters (4 minutes).

### Other rooms

No two-voice card: draw an arrow and a wave on card. One tale in big print for the room; groups read parts from a copy. Read the exit card aloud twice; the answer is a line drawn on a folded sheet.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on scraps. No group: read all the parts yourselves in different voices.
**Skip.** The oral card and the rehearsal groups.

- **10 min. Reread.** Your child reads the printed invitation to you with a finger under the words.
- **15 min. Voices.** Your child reads a short tale to you, one character in a voice that fits.
  **Say.** "What does this character want?" Expect: the thing the character is after, such as food or help.
- **5 min. Words.** Your child builds enormous and bonus with scraps.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X149]] and [[s04-X149b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the rehearsal, the exit and the first reading. Longer: a child reads the narrator and one character.

### Words and supports

Voice, character, narrator, rehearse, enormous, bonus. Frames: "This character wants ___." "My voice is ___ because ___." Voices may be shown by a gesture card. Enlarge print, read cards aloud, let a child say a line in the home language first.

### Tomorrow

Day 150 runs the first sitting on poem P for children 1 to 15 and assembles the packet. Today's exit marks go to the record sheet; a child who read both lines in the same voice reads them again in the reread of the steps on day 150. The cycle 26 reader is read again for accuracy.
