# Day 28: Rival empires and independent nations

## Teacher page

### Before class

Use unit 3 nation-notes.md as a teacher reference; copy the reached teaching notes below instead. The student page embeds unit 3 Ohio base schematic and unit 3 private Ohio claim schematic on separate sides. Cut today's unit 3 section 1 fresh repair slips. From unit 3 section 1 private exits use only the exact stem; today's covered source card below replaces its old card. Read teacher-only key-u3.md and generated keys. Keep the claim schematic hidden.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Hand back `s03-X27a`, `s03-X27b`, `s03-P27`, `s03-F27`, `s03-T27`.

Return day 27's exits, question sort, petition clues and roof-log response. If many tickets show error U or error V, choose one: other readers versus only the asker, or dates versus interpretive ideas. Model five minutes without a full question/use answer. Then reveal the invented reading-rule inquiry, [[s03-F28]], for five independent minutes. Others use written cues; retain unresolved gaps.

#### 10 min. Rival claims

Keep yesterday's promise: Britain and France competed for land and trade during the French and Indian War, 1754 to 1763. Locate only river and region on the physical schematic, without claim overlays. Review homeland, land a nation's people have long known as home; alliance, an agreement between groups to help one another. Both empires had Native allies; the war's name does not mean France fought all Native nations.

#### 12 min. Nation evidence

Read separate notes, never one generic Native voice. Oneida Nation describes fur-trade rivalry and differing alliances. The reached park account describes Lenape refuge in Ohio and efforts to preserve trade and land. The Delaware Tribe of Indians locates Lenape history's beginning in the Delaware Valley. This government differs from Delaware Nation. Review sovereignty: a nation's right to govern itself. These are living nations.

**Ask.** [[s03-P28]] Expect trade in the Oneida note; refuge or land in the park note. The homeland-location note leaves motives unknown. Model underlining one stated concern. Fifteen pairs speak simultaneously, 45 seconds each way; all annotate. Location cannot prove allegiance. No one acts a nation. These printed notes provide the fallback when a live page cannot open.

#### 8 min. Exit ticket

Reveal the new claim schematic and today's covered source card with [[s03-X28]]. Lines show claims, not ownership or surveyed borders. Read labels neutrally without interpreting overlap. Children explain alone on one half slip. Collect all work. After class: 30 times 30 seconds for exits plus 30 times 15 seconds for annotations/retry, 22.5 minutes. Return everything day 29, Return and repair. Review the component scale; support separately.

### Other rooms

### At home

**Swap.** No printer or projector: copy today's named panels and point to paper. Use scrap paper. Keep held cards hidden.

**Skip.** Class grouping and public sharing.

- **5 min. Repair.** Read yesterday's cue, then [[s03-F28]] alone.
- **10 min. Rival claims.** Point to the river and region on the physical schematic.
- **12 min. Nation evidence.** Read today's separate notes and do [[s03-P28]].
  **Say.** "Point to words naming a concern." Expect trade, refuge or land in its own note.
- **8 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal the claim schematic and today's source card with [[s03-X28]]. Your child answers alone; do not explain the overlap.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce sharing, keeping repair and independent evidence. Longer: identify evidence still needed for one answer.

### Words and supports

Review homeland, alliance, sovereignty and map key, using earlier meanings. A map label is not an allegiance. Offer tactile outlines. Everyone may pass in public talk. Offer neutral reading, large print and transcribed dictation. No personal disclosures or reenactment.

### Tomorrow

Day 29 returns every day 28 paper. If many tickets show error H or error T, contrast separate nation evidence with a blanket allegiance claim. One five-minute model and five-minute fresh map-limit response precede war debts, new taxes and the Proclamation.
