# Skilled work and routes to freedom

Teacher only. Label every fictional person and record made up before reading.
No child represents a group or reports personal or family history.
No reenactments, auctions, simulations, graphic images, slurs, or sacred activities.
Offer a pause and the same neutral factual summary without requiring an explanation.
Read neutrally or transcribe dictated answers onto paper, preserving the historical decisions.
Do not diagnose an error from blank work.

All thirty students submit paper. Fifteen pairs talk simultaneously, 45 seconds each way.
Hear at most six rotating public observations, each 45 seconds, within five minutes.
Public talk is optional. Record names heard; collected paper reaches everyone.
Five minutes explaining one priority plus five on fresh paper uses the protected repair slot.
Teach together for more than ten shared errors; otherwise meet one adult group.
Others annotate different evidence with written cues. Keep unresolved needs recorded.
One scale: shown; shown with help; not yet seen. Record access support separately.

Artisan means a skilled craftsperson. Hired out means an enslaver sends someone to work for another.
Skill, receiving money, and freedom are different evidence claims.
Smith describes negotiated earnings, never proof that everyone controlled wages.
The 1795 payroll names the enslaver signing for wages, not a proven worker share.
The exit carpenter and permitted share are made up; keep that case out of teaching.

Manumission means an enslaver legally grants freedom. Indenture means bound service for a term.
Explain the endpoint independently, even though unit 1 introduced the word.
A petition requests action. It is not a court ruling or grant.
Recall Freeman and Hall only as reminders. Follow Walker's court procedure and Flora's deed separately.
No four-document sort or selected free subset appears in practice.
Emancipation means release from slavery. Gradual emancipation delays some people's freedom.
Pennsylvania's 1780 act required post-act children to serve until 28.
Massachusetts courts made slavery unenforceable by 1783, without a gradual state statute.
Read each institution separately. Free-life records show rights, not guaranteed equal treatment.
