# Day 32: Petitions, organizing and pressure

## Teacher page

### Before class

Prepare unit 3 action-cards.md as separate named-action panels. Cut today's unit 3 section 2 fresh repair slips and unit 3 section 2 private exits. Use today's covered Exit box and one-item half slip instead of the old two-item master. Read teacher-only key-u3.md and generated keys. Keep the two fresh unnamed notes hidden. Bring day 31 papers and the timeline.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Hand back `s03-X31`, `s03-P31`, `s03-F31`.

Return day 31's explanation, annotations and retry. If many tickets show error G, error I or error P, choose one: a pre-independence institution, consent rather than no taxes, or restricted eligibility. Model five minutes from one teaching fragment, not the held records. Reveal the invented enrolled-craft case and [[s03-F32]] for five independent minutes. More than ten sharing a gap: teach together; otherwise one group, others use cues. Retain other needs.

#### 10 min. Methods

Keep yesterday's promise: mark the backward return to 1765. Review resistance from day 20, now extending opposition to imperial authority. Review petition: a written request to officials. The Stamp Act Congress coordinated colonial delegates and petitions against the Act. Sons of Liberty organized opposition, including coercive pressure on stamp officials. Samuel Adams used writing and correspondence to organize opposition, without commanding every protest.

Teach boycott: organized refusal to buy as protest. Intimidation means pressure through threats. No child performs threats or takes a historical role. Point ahead to 1772 communication committees, exchanging news and coordinating responses. This preview is later than today's 1765 focus.

#### 12 min. Action annotations

Show named actions separately. Model the Congress petition as a written request. **Ask.** [[s03-P32]] Children classify one isolated action at a time. Expect petition for Congress's request, communication for an organizer's letter, intimidation for a threat. Fifteen pairs speak simultaneously, 45 seconds each way; everyone writes. Keep separate entries, without a two-group chart or comparison paragraph. If many call every action peaceful, revisit the meaning of a threat without acting it. A common goal does not make all methods alike.

#### 8 min. Exit ticket

Uncover fresh notes N and O with [[s03-X32a]] in the student page's Exit box. Give only s03-X32b on the half slip. All thirty explain roles and one method difference independently. Accept neutral reading and transcribed dictation. Collect all work. After class: 30 times 35 seconds for exits plus 30 times 15 seconds for annotations/retry, 25 minutes. Return everything day 33, Return and repair. Review the component scale; support separately. Political agreement is never scored.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce sharing, keeping repair and independent evidence. Longer: identify evidence still needed for one answer.

### Words and supports

Review resistance and petition. Boycott: organized refusal to buy. Intimidation: pressure through threats. Correspondence: exchanged letters. Everyone may pass in public talk. Offer neutral reading, large print and transcribed dictation. No personal disclosures or reenactment.
