# Day 2: Disease, dates, and land rights

## Teacher page

### Before class

Separate teacher pages and key-exits.md. Teacher-only exit look-fors appear below. Keep exits covered. The source block is on the student page; print no separate sources sheet. Prepare only needed timeline and source-log panels. Prepare the question board and unit record sheet. Sort yesterday's papers by one priority need.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Return day 1's written tasks and exit tickets together. If many day 1 tickets showed error P, explain a map user's information need for five minutes; use [[s01-R2-1]] for a five-minute fresh response. For error H, distinguish a question from a conclusion using this same library case. Handle one priority; others receive written cues. A date or nation retrieval gap may replace that priority. Review day 1's nation definition and invented date labels.

#### 10 min. Explain sources

Day 1's board leads to disease and land practices. Read Separate records on sources-2. Review source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose (grade 4). **Ask.** [[s01-K2-1]] Wait before [[steps:s01-K2-1]]. Explain epidemic: disease spreading through a community. Deaths harmed communities and relationships; losses do not mean everyone disappeared. Review event years as distinct from the date of a modern account (grade 4).

Review timeline: equal gaps for equal years and ten-year marks (grade 4). Mark the epidemic interval. Review Plymouth's 1620 settlement on Wampanoag land (grade 4), using the Mashpee timeline's separate entry. A modern tribal history is not an eyewitness transcript.

#### 9 min. Separate annotations

Pairs complete [[s01-P2]], one 45-second turn each, then individual paper. Independently complete [[s01-P2-2]]. Point to the invented community field and private garden: different rights have different decision makers. This made-up town represents no Native nation. Never generalize it to every historical community. Do not combine disease and rights annotations into a practiced paragraph.

#### 5 min. Revisit the question

Six rotating children share one annotation each, at most 45 seconds each. Everyone marks the disease topic's evidence day on their retained sort. If many deny shared-use authority, point to the diagram's rule maker; save individual correction for day 3. All thirty submit paper even when someone passes publicly.

#### 6 min. Exit ticket

First reveal [[s01-X2]] now. Children work alone; read neutrally or take dictation without explaining. Collect all paper for day 3. After class, mark at 30 seconds each, fifteen minutes total; allow ten additional minutes for annotations. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen per component; record support separately.

Teacher only, mark each component: [[steps:s01-X2]]

### Other rooms

Hand-copy source blocks with their makers before class. Draw equal timeline intervals. Paper replaces projection; no live page or visitor is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the source blocks and questions. No timeline: draw equal ten-year marks.

**Skip.** Public sharing and class return routines.

- **10 min. Records.** Read Separate records. Use [[s01-K2-1]], then reveal its steps.
  **Say.** "Which years belong to the event?" Expect: 1616 to 1620.
- **10 min. Annotations.** Take the partner turn for [[s01-P2]], then let your child answer [[s01-P2-2]].
  **Say.** "Who sets the field rules?" Expect: the community.
- **9 min. Connect.** Place the epidemic interval and settlement on equal-year marks. Keep their makers separate.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** First reveal [[s01-X2]]. Read neutrally if needed. Your child answers alone; collect before feedback.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing; retain explanation, individual work, and exit. Longer: revisit the existing source annotation with support withdrawn.

### Words and supports

Epidemic means disease spreading through a community. Private rights concern a particular holder; community rights involve shared authority. Neutral reading, movable date labels, and dictation preserve the decisions.
