# Day 1: Questions about land decisions

## Teacher page

### Before class

Separate teacher pages and key-exits.md. Teacher-only exit look-fors appear below. Keep exits covered. The source block is on the student page; print no separate sources sheet. Prepare only needed timeline and source-log panels. Prepare the question board and unit record sheet. Send family.md privately. Use X1a alone on the half-slip; X1b is the covered Exit box.

### The day

#### 5 min. Retrieve

This is our first course day. Review: voyage, a trip by ship; colony, a settlement far from the home country, which claims it (grade 3). Review: nation, a people with their own government (grade 4). All answer [[s01-T1]], [[s01-T2]], and [[s01-T3]] privately. The dated history is invented. Collect without diagnosing blanks.

#### 8 min. Hook and explain

Read Homeland and claim on sources-1. Review source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose (grade 4). Review the source log, recording those fields (grade 4). **Ask.** [[s01-K1-1]] Wait before revealing [[steps:s01-K1-1]]. Children complete [[s01-P1-2]]. Explain ownership as rights claimed over land. Review the kindergarten class-agreement sense of charter; teach its historical sense, an official grant. The king's claim does not establish everyone's consent. Named Native homelands were already governed.

Review scale: lay equal strips end to end along a bar. Use the invented route on timeline.md for [[s01-P1-3]], never the unscaled homeland sketch. Expect 75 miles.

#### 10 min. Topic connections

Review big question: a question too large to answer at once (grade 4). Post Whose land was it, and who decided? Review supporting question: a smaller question that helps answer the big one (grade 4). Explain that investigating authority matters because decisions affect access to land. Review evidence: what we saw or read that shows it (grade 1). Pairs use [[s01-P1]]. Supply the investigation schedule from its key. Each partner has one 45-second turn; both record their own sort. No finished questions are supplied.

#### 7 min. Question board

Review the question board routine: group topics and questions by evidence day. Everyone completes [[s01-P1-4]]. Collect and group historical questions by topic and evidence day. Hear six rotating volunteers, one 45-second observation each, 4.5 minutes total. All may pass; record names. Distinguish a question from a conclusion. If more than ten need help, point to missing information without composing their questions. Keep the board.

#### 10 min. Exit ticket

Uncover [[s01-X1a]] and the separate Exit box [[s01-X1b]] only now. Children answer both alone. This town is made up. Offer neutral reading or dictation. Collect all paper for day 2. After class, allow fifteen minutes: thirty children times 30 seconds for both exit components; another fifteen minutes for practice. Record shown, shown with help, or not yet seen, with support separate.

Teacher only, mark each component: [[steps:s01-X1a]] [[steps:s01-X1b]]

### Other rooms

Hand-copy source blocks with their makers before class. Draw equal timeline intervals. Paper replaces projection; no live page or visitor is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the short texts, route bars, and questions. No board: use paper.

**Skip.** Public sharing and the class retrieval collection.

- **8 min. Sources.** Read Homeland and claim together. Use [[s01-P1-2]].
  **Say.** "Who made each source?" Expect: Aquinnah; Charles II.
- **7 min. Scale.** Use the invented route for [[s01-P1-3]].
  **Say.** "What does each bar stand for?" Expect: 25 miles.
- **10 min. Topics.** Take the partner turn in [[s01-P1]]. Supply its schedule, then let your child add [[s01-P1-4]].
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal [[s01-X1a]] and [[s01-X1b]]. Your child works alone. Collect before discussing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing; retain explanation, individual work, and exit. Longer: revisit the existing source annotation with support withdrawn.

### Words and supports

Review nation and supporting question with their definitions above. Offer neutral reading, bilingual labels, or dictation. Historical charter means official grant; ownership means rights claimed over land.

### Tomorrow

Day 2 returns every paper in Return and repair: error P excludes affected users; error H replaces inquiry with an unsupported conclusion. Choose one five-minute explanation and five-minute fresh response. Then investigate disease and different land practices.
