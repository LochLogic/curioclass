# Day 20: Actions against denied freedom

## Teacher page

### Before class

Use unit 2 rights fragments and unit 2 action cards as teacher references. Show only action teaching panels, covering the exit-source credit. Print student pages and unit 2 private exit 20 half slips. Cut R20 alone from unit 2 section 2 fresh checks. Read teacher-only ../u02-1/key-u2.md and generated keys. Keep historical exit summaries sealed until the exit.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Return all day 19 paper, including the Exit box. If many tickets showed error D, explain that sale threatened an existing family bond. For error Q, identify skill separately from rights; for error O, separate freedom from equality. Error B needs forced versus chosen work. Spend five minutes on one priority using separate teaching fragments. Give unseen [[s02-R20]] for five minutes alone; collect and retain other gaps.

#### 10 min. Explain resistance

Teach resistance: action against denied freedom, including escape, requests and revolt. Review petition: a written request to officials. Distinguish leaving captivity from requesting action and an armed uprising. Use no acting or graphic account. Revolts challenged enslavement and faced violent suppression.

Read the Fort Mose summary and point to its locator. People escaped Carolina plantations for Spanish Florida. In 1738 the Spanish governor established their fortified town near St. Augustine. Spanish freedom required service and religious conversion, not universal equality. No religious activity accompanies this account. Discuss one action at a time; never model shared-action inference across two accounts.

#### 10 min. Action annotations

Pairs use [[s02-P20-1]] and [[s02-P20-2]], adding separate action symbols beside the locator. Review legend, the map key. Footpath means escape; paper means petition; flag means revolt. **Ask.** Read the matching question before explaining symbols. Expect: escape, petition, revolt.

Fifteen pairs take 45 seconds each way. Within five minutes hear at most six optional 45-second observations; record names and collect everyone's paper. Review perspective: two sides of one event, without introducing held accounts. Supply source maker, date, audience and purpose prompts. Never request personal or family history.

#### 10 min. Exit ticket

First reveal [[s02-X20a]] and [[s02-X20b]]. Identify them as original summaries of Stono records. The official account dates from 1739; George Cato's inherited family testimony was recorded about 1937, not eyewitness reporting. Read neutrally without adding a shared-action answer. Children write alone for eight minutes, reading included; collect in two. After class, thirty times 60 seconds gives thirty minutes to mark all responses. Use the same shown scale, support separately. Return everything on day 21.

### Other rooms

No projector: point to teacher-held teaching panels. No printer: hand-copy the student text and separate held checks, preserving covered exit work. Dictated responses are transcribed onto paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the teaching locator and symbols. Keep exit summaries separate and hidden.

**Skip.** Public observations and class grouping.

- **10 min. Return.** Explain one need from yesterday. Give [[s02-R20]] alone afterward.
- **10 min. Resistance.** Read the Fort Mose summary. Point to the locator; explain conditions attached to Spanish freedom.
- **10 min. Actions.** Use [[s02-P20-1]] and [[s02-P20-2]].
  **Say.** "Which word names a written request to officials?" Expect: petition.
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** First reveal [[s02-X20a]] and [[s02-X20b]]. Explain that inherited testimony differs from eyewitness reporting. Read neutrally if needed. Your child answers alone. Collect before discussing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing, preserving individual work, repair and exit time. Longer: name one detail the source cannot establish, without previewing another case.

### Words and supports

Review petition, legend, perspective and forced labor in earlier words. Escape means leaving captivity; revolt means an armed uprising. Permit passing, a pause, enlarged print or transcribed dictation. Never ask a child to represent an enslaved person or enslaver.

### Tomorrow

Day 21 returns all day 20 responses in Return and repair. Error E denies resistance; error L allows only revolt; error B misses coercion. Use a separate escape record for five-minute teaching, then unseen R21 for five minutes. Retain other gaps. Day 21 studies skilled artisans, payment control and unchanged legal status.
