# Day 19: Family bonds and denied control

## Teacher page

### Before class

Use unit 2 rights fragments and unit 2 scope cards as teacher references. Print student pages with Side 2 sealed and unit 2 private exit 19 half slips. Cut R19 alone from unit 2 section 2 fresh checks. Read teacher-only ../u02-1/key-u2.md and generated keys. Keep held cases hidden until their parts; never distribute the full fresh-check bank.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Return all day 18 paper. If many tickets showed error C, locate northern presence; for error K, explain why a proportion needs ownership counts and a household total. For error S, read Washington's record alone. Five minutes addresses one priority; retain other gaps. Give unseen [[s02-R19]] for five minutes of independent paper; collect.

#### 10 min. Explain constraints

Review forced labor: work people could not refuse. Read separate Smith and Vassall fragments. Review Smith's freedom story as a reminder; introduce these family details separately. Smith and Meg married while enslaved; sale separated him from his wife and child. People had family bonds despite legal threats. Use [[s02-P19-1]].

Read the 1781 request's recipient, Massachusetts lawmakers. Review petition: a written request to officials. Use [[s02-P19-3]]. Anthony and Cuba Vassall requested support after slavery; the park history records paid work and home ownership alongside continued racial discrimination.

Education access varied; some enslaved people learned to read, while enslavers could block learning. Never describe a universal colonial ban. Separately summarize Virginia's 1705 escape punishment and Smith's account of violence, without graphic details. Teach legal status: a person's position under law. Skills do not establish freedom.

#### 12 min. Rights fragments

Model one control at a time: identify the family bond before the threat from sale. Pairs use [[s02-P19-2]] for vocabulary, never underline four restrictions or compare two named biographies. **Ask.** What relationship does the marriage fragment record? Expect: spouses. Ask before the annotation.

Review source log; supply maker, date, audience and purpose independently. The petition addresses lawmakers to request support. Fifteen pairs take 45 seconds each way; then each writes separate notes. Collect everyone's paper. Keep rights categories separate until the independent exit.

#### 8 min. Exit ticket

First reveal Side 2 and [[s02-X19a]], four minutes. Give half slip [[s02-X19b]] for three minutes, neutral reading included. Keep Ruth's record visible; collect both in one minute. After class, thirty times 75 seconds gives about 38 minutes to mark all responses. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen; support separately. Return everything on day 20.

### Other rooms

No projector: point to teacher-held teaching panels. No printer: hand-copy the student text and separate held checks, preserving covered exit work. Dictated responses are transcribed onto paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy separate teaching fragments, the library check and covered exit record.

- **10 min. Return.** Explain one need from yesterday. Give [[s02-R19]] alone afterward.

  **Say.** "What relationship does the marriage record show?" Expect: spouses, despite enslavement.
- **12 min. Fragments.** Use [[s02-P19-2]]. Point separately to each kind of control.
  **Say.** "What does restriction mean?" Expect: a limit on what someone can do.
- **8 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal [[s02-X19a]] on Side 2, then [[s02-X19b]]. Keep Ruth's record visible. Your child answers alone. Collect before discussing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing, preserving individual work, repair and exit time. Longer: name one detail the source cannot establish, without previewing another case.

### Words and supports

Restriction: a limit on what someone can do. Sale treats a person as property; people are never objects. Review petition and forced labor. Allow neutral reading, dictation, a pause and a factual summary. No personal-family comparisons or role play.

### Tomorrow

Day 20 returns every day 19 response. If many show error D, separate threatened family separation from absent bonds. Error Q confuses denied rights with absent skill; error O confuses freedom with equality. Error B treats slavery as a freely chosen job. Select one priority, then fresh R20. Next study actions against denied freedom.
