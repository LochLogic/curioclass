# Legality, lives, and resistance

Teacher only. Label every fictional person and record made up before reading.
No child represents a group or reports personal or family history.
No reenactments, auctions, simulations, graphic images, slurs, or sacred activities.
Offer a pause and the same neutral factual summary without requiring an explanation.
Read neutrally or transcribe dictated answers onto paper, preserving the historical decisions.
Do not diagnose an error from blank work.

All thirty students submit paper. Fifteen pairs talk simultaneously, 45 seconds each way.
Hear at most six rotating public observations, each 45 seconds, within five minutes.
Public talk is optional. Record names heard; collected paper reaches everyone.
Five minutes explaining one priority plus five on fresh paper uses the protected repair slot.
Teach together for more than ten shared errors; otherwise meet one adult group.
Others annotate different evidence with written cues. Keep unresolved needs recorded.
One scale: shown; shown with help; not yet seen. Record access support separately.

Presence in a record is not a household proportion.
Georgia permitted slavery in 1751; all thirteen colonies then permitted it.
Not every household enslaved someone. Ownership differed by place.
Read Washington and Jefferson records separately; reserve city percentages for day 25.
Washington's list includes personally owned people and people held through other arrangements.

People formed families despite threats of sale and separation.
Freedom brought greater control of work and family life while discrimination persisted.
Education access varied. Do not claim a universal colonial ban or backdate later laws.
Practice uses separate rights fragments, never paired biographies or restriction underlining.
Resistance includes petitions, escape, and revolt. Fort Mose supplies southern escape context.
Spanish freedom required service and religious conversion. Conduct no religious activity.
Revolt challenged bondage and faced violent suppression; use no graphic account or acting.
Reserve both Stono extracts until E20; Cato's inherited testimony is not eyewitness evidence.

E17 returns on 18; E18 on 19; E19 on 20; E20 on 21.
Each receiving Return and repair holds ten minutes for one priority only.
S codes denial that early leaders enslaved people; check the named records.
C/K concern scope; D/Q/O concern families, skill and equality; E/L/B concern resistance/coercion.
