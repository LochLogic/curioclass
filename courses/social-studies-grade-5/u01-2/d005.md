# Day 5: A different war and its sources

## Teacher page

### Before class

Separate teacher pages and key-exits.md. Teacher-only exit look-fors appear below. Keep exits covered. The source block is on the student page; print no separate sources sheet. Prepare only needed timeline and source-log panels. Prepare the question board and unit record sheet. Sort yesterday's papers by one priority need.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Return day 4's written tasks and exit tickets together. If many day 4 tickets showed error C, use five minutes to rebuild named alliance links, then five for [[s01-R5-1]]. For error B, instead point to living Wampanoag communities on the retained sources-1 sheet, then use [[s01-R5-2]]. Review nation: a people with their own government. Choose one route; others get written cues. Neither blank work nor an omitted detail proves an error.

#### 10 min. Explain the second war

Yesterday's Pequot War work leads to a different conflict. Read A different conflict on sources-5. Place King Philip's War beginning in 1675 on the carried timeline, well after 1636 to 1638. Metacom was a Wampanoag leader whom the English called King Philip. The museum chronology describes alliances intended to protect homelands as colonization increased pressure on access to land. Land loss resulted from people's actions; it was not inevitable.

#### 9 min. Source fragments

Review source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose. Partners separately label the museum chronology and Easton's report. One 45-second turn each; both keep their own annotations. Easton reports a meeting intended to prevent war; he is an English official, not a Native author. His recording mediates others' words. Complete [[s01-P5]] and [[s01-P5-2]] separately. Keep the reserved grievances and land-recovery account hidden.

#### 5 min. Reasoning connection

Review evidence, information used to support a claim; reasoning, how a detail supports a claim (grade 4). **Ask.** [[s01-K5-1]] Wait before [[steps:s01-K5-1]]. Everyone adds this single arrow. Hear six rotating 45-second observations. If many choose an unrelated detail, return to the stated purpose. Collect all papers; do not complete a pressure-and-consequence chart or paragraph.

#### 6 min. Exit ticket

Review displacement: having to leave a place because it cannot be used safely (grade 4). Extend it to forced removal during war. Review enslavement: forcing people into bondage, never voluntary work. Reveal [[s01-X5]] only now; children answer alone, with neutral reading or dictation available. Collect for day 6. After class, allow fifteen minutes for exits, thirty times 30 seconds; ten more for annotations. Record shown, shown with help, or not yet seen, support separately.

Teacher only, mark each component: [[steps:s01-X5]]

### Other rooms

Hand-copy source blocks with their makers before class. Draw equal timeline intervals. Paper replaces projection; no live page or visitor is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy A different conflict and the questions. No timeline: draw equal ten-year marks.

**Skip.** Public sharing and class return routines.

- **10 min. Different dates.** Read the chronology fragment. Use [[s01-P5-2]].
  **Say.** "Where does 1675 belong?" Expect: between 1670 and 1680.
- **10 min. Makers.** Take the partner turn for [[s01-P5]].
  **Say.** "Whose report is this?" Expect: John Easton's, an English official's.
- **9 min. Detail.** Use [[s01-K5-1]], then reveal its steps. Explain displacement and enslavement using the class definitions.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** First reveal [[s01-X5]]. Your child works alone. Read without explaining if needed; collect before feedback.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing; retain explanation, individual work, and exit. Longer: revisit the existing source annotation with support withdrawn.

### Words and supports

Review displacement and enslavement using the definitions above. Review consequence: an effect of an action or event. Offer neutral reading, a tactile timeline, or dictation. No child shares personal experiences.
