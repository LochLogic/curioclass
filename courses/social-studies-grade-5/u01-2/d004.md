# Day 4: Named sources about the Pequot War

## Teacher page

### Before class

Separate teacher pages and key-exits.md. Teacher-only exit look-fors appear below. Keep exits covered. The source block is on the student page; print no separate sources sheet. Prepare only needed timeline and source-log panels. Prepare the question board and unit record sheet. Sort yesterday's papers by one priority need.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Return day 3's written tasks and exit tickets together. If many day 3 tickets showed error H, spend five minutes separating a record's subject from an unsupported inference. Give five minutes for fresh [[s01-R4-1]]. For error A, first review shared-use authority from day 2 instead. Claiming consent beyond the record is an unsupported inference, not automatically error A. Other gaps receive written cues.

#### 10 min. Context and chronology

Yesterday's agreement limits lead to conflict. Read Named participants on sources-4. Review context: where, when and for whom a source was made (grade 4). Place the Pequot War, 1636 to 1638, on the carried line. Keep the particular attack and survival accounts withheld until the exit. Explain that English forces had particular Mohegan and Narragansett allies, not all nations acting identically.

#### 9 min. Source identities

Review source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose. **Ask.** [[s01-K4-1]] Wait before [[steps:s01-K4-1]]. Pairs annotate [[s01-P4]], one 45-second turn each, then individual writing. The Mashantucket Pequot Tribal Nation publishes a modern public history, not an eyewitness transcript from the seventeenth century. Review perspective: two sides of one event; here identify particular makers rather than forcing everyone into two sides.

#### 5 min. Evidence arrows

Demonstrate one arrow from the modern-history label to its maker field. Everyone completes [[s01-P4-2]]. Hear six rotating observations, 45 seconds each. Collect every child's annotation. If many flatten alliances, point to one named link. Keep source identity separate from agreement with the account.

#### 6 min. Exit ticket

Teach massacre, killing many people, as the ticket's vocabulary. Reveal [[s01-X4]] only now. Underhill was an English participant publishing in 1638 about earlier events. Explain no graphic detail. Children independently connect the two new summaries; provide neutral reading or dictation. Collect all paper for day 5. Then place the 1637 Mystic attack on the timeline. After class, thirty times 30 seconds gives fifteen minutes for exits; allow ten more for annotations. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen per component; support stays separate.

Teacher only, mark each component: [[steps:s01-X4]]

### Other rooms

Hand-copy source blocks with their makers before class. Draw equal timeline intervals. Paper replaces projection; no live page or visitor is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy Named participants and the questions. No timeline: draw equal ten-year marks.

**Skip.** Public sharing and class return routines.

- **10 min. Dates.** Read Named participants. Use [[s01-K4-1]], then reveal its steps.
  **Say.** "Are these war years or website years?" Expect: war years.
- **10 min. Identities.** Take the partner turn for [[s01-P4]].
  **Say.** "Who publishes this history?" Expect: Mashantucket Pequot Tribal Nation.
- **9 min. Connection.** Your child completes [[s01-P4-2]]. Keep this specific alliance separate from other nations.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** Explain massacre means killing many people. First reveal [[s01-X4]]. Your child answers alone. Collect before feedback.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing; retain explanation, individual work, and exit. Longer: revisit the existing source annotation with support withdrawn.

### Words and supports

Massacre means killing many people. Review context and perspective using the definitions above. Offer a tactile timeline, neutral reading, or dictated labels; never reenact violence.
