# Day 6: Living nations and their governments

## Teacher page

### Before class

Separate teacher pages and key-exits.md. Teacher-only exit look-fors appear below. Keep exits covered. The source block is on the student page; print no separate sources sheet. Prepare only needed timeline and source-log panels. Prepare the question board and unit record sheet. Sort yesterday's papers by one priority need.

### The day

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Return day 5's written tasks and exit tickets together. If many day 5 tickets showed error M, spend five minutes separating the wars on the equal-year line, then five on fresh [[s01-R6-1]]. For error H, instead trace a relevant purpose detail, then use [[s01-R6-2]]. For error C, point to each source's named nation before the relevance retry. Choose one priority; other gaps get written cues.

#### 10 min. Present government

Yesterday's consequences lead to living governments. Read Government records on sources-6. Review nation: a people with their own government (grade 4). Name Aquinnah, Mashpee Wampanoag, Narragansett, and Mashantucket Pequot separately. These dated public summaries describe different institutions. A council record identifies a representative body; a court record describes access to justice. A visitor or live page is unnecessary.

**Ask.** [[s01-K6-1]] Wait before [[steps:s01-K6-1]]. Show the maker label first, then its particular function. Keep environmental activities and an all-past correction out of practice.

#### 9 min. Government records

Review source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose. Fifteen pairs complete [[s01-P6-2]], one 45-second turn each. Every child then completes [[s01-P6]] independently, choosing a different record from the Aquinnah model. A heading establishes the listed institution; do not invent an unstated function. If many treat governments as identical, point to the separate nation labels. Offer neutral reading or dictated labels.

#### 5 min. Question board

Hear six rotating observations, one 45-second turn each. All submit government annotations. Add the records' reading date, November 24, 2026, to the board, outside the historical event line. Review event and account dates: these current records are not seventeenth-century accounts. Keep the chronology for later colonial institutions.

#### 6 min. Exit ticket

Reveal [[s01-X6]] and the covered Exit box [[s01-X6b]] only now. Gloss environmental programs as organized work concerning the environment; capacity as ability to do work. Children explain alone using the new activities and government records. Aquinnah states a purpose, not a measured result. Collect all paper for day 7. After class, thirty times 30 seconds gives fifteen minutes for exits, plus ten for annotations. Record shown, shown with help, or not yet seen per component, with support separate.

Teacher only, mark each component: [[steps:s01-X6]] [[steps:s01-X6b]]

### Other rooms

Hand-copy source blocks with their makers before class. Draw equal timeline intervals. Paper replaces projection; no live page or visitor is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the government summaries and questions. No board: use paper.

**Skip.** Public sharing and class return routines.

- **10 min. Government.** Read the summaries. Use [[s01-K6-1]], then reveal its steps.
  **Say.** "What does Aquinnah's council do?" Expect: represents members in tribal affairs.
- **10 min. Records.** Take the partner turn for [[s01-P6-2]].
  **Say.** "Which record names a court?" Expect: Mashpee Wampanoag's.
- **9 min. Annotation.** Your child completes [[s01-P6]] alone using another record. Keep its maker and function together.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** Explain capacity means ability. First reveal [[s01-X6]] and [[s01-X6b]]. Your child answers alone; collect before discussing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing; retain explanation, individual work, and exit. Longer: revisit the existing source annotation with support withdrawn.

### Words and supports

Review nation with its definition above. Review representative: a person others choose to decide for them. Review court: institution that interprets law and resolves disputes. Use named records without sacred material. Offer neutral reading, movable headings, or dictation.
