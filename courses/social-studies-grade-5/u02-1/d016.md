# Day 16: How Virginia laws strengthened slavery

## Teacher page

### Before class

Use unit 2 law slips and unit 2 route and harbor instruction as teacher references. Prepare student pages, unit 2 private exit 16 half slips and selected unit 2 section 1 fresh checks. Read teacher-only key-u2.md and generated keys. Return the unit 2 readiness master from day 14, never administer it again. Keep fresh checks covered.

### The day

#### 5 min. Importance return

Return all day 15 paper. If many tickets showed irrelevant questions or error R, explain one priority for three minutes: relevant questions lead to information readers need. Use supplied inquiry topics, without rehearsing yesterday's display case. All independently answer [[s02-R16]] for two minutes; collect. Retain other gaps.

#### 10 min. Route and harbor instruction

Return day 14's readiness. Teach the simplified route network as new: exact earlier grade 3 instruction remains planned only. Model the completed route and Copper Dock for five minutes. People were forced; objects are goods. The made-up harbor's shelter allows stopping; its dock connects boats with repairers. Guide [[s02-P16-4]] for three minutes. Give unseen [[s02-R16h]] for two. Teach together for more than ten shared gaps; otherwise meet one group while others sequence event slips.

#### 10 min. Explain law clauses

Read each law separately, with maker, date and subject. Teach hereditary: passing from parent to child. Virginia lawmakers tied status to the mother in 1662; baptism did not change bondage under the 1667 law. Discuss baptism only as historical law, without religious activity. These racist laws strengthened lifelong slavery; they did not make it just.

Review timeline: equal gaps for equal years and ten-year marks. Model locating one event, then use [[s02-P16-2]]. Atlantic forced transport predates the 1619 Virginia arrival. Do not correct an invented label or combine laws in a family scenario.

#### 8 min. Clause annotations

Pairs annotate separate clauses with [[s02-P16-1]] and [[s02-P16-3]]. **Ask.** Whose status does the first clause follow? Expect: the mother's. Ask before showing that annotation. Review source log prompts: maker, date, audience, purpose. Lawmakers addressed colonial authorities to enforce status rules. Fifteen pairs take 45 seconds each way; remaining time is individual writing. Collect all paper.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Reveal [[s02-X16a]] and [[s02-X16b]] only now. Allow six minutes alone, neutral reading included, then one to collect. After class, thirty times 60 seconds gives thirty minutes to mark all responses. Use the same shown scale, recording support separately; blank work needs another opportunity. Return on day 17.

### Other rooms

No projector: point to teacher-held teaching panels. No printer: hand-copy the student text and separate held checks, preserving covered exit work. Dictated responses are transcribed onto paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy separate clauses, the Copper Dock diagram and fresh slips.

**Skip.** Class grouping and public observations.

- **5 min. Return.** Give feedback on yesterday's work. Use [[s02-R16]] alone afterward.
- **10 min. Routes.** Explain the completed route and made-up harbor. Use [[s02-P16-4]], then unseen [[s02-R16h]].
  **Say.** "Which feature lets repairers reach boats?" Expect: the dock.
- **10 min. Laws.** Read each clause separately. Use [[s02-P16-2]] on the timeline.
- **8 min. Annotate.** Use [[s02-P16-1]].
  **Say.** "Whose status does this clause follow?" Expect: the mother's.
- **7 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal [[s02-X16a]] and [[s02-X16b]]. Your child answers alone. Collect before discussing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public sharing, preserving individual work, repair and exit time. Longer: name one detail the source cannot establish, without previewing another case.

### Words and supports

Hereditary: passing from parent to child. Baptism: a religious ceremony, discussed only as law context. Review timeline and source log. Offer printed summaries, enlarged type or transcribed dictation. No reenactments or graphic originals.

### Tomorrow

Day 17 returns every day 16 response. If many give error A, separate Virginia's arrival date from earlier Atlantic enslavement. For error I, return to the maternal clause. Retain other gaps. Then interpret the completed Atlantic network before a fresh independent construction.
