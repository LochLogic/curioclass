# Teacher-only neutral context, day 25
Read one panel at a time within its question allocation, without supplying the answer.
Read A and B first, within five minutes. The source sheet credits them.
Q1 four minutes; Q2 four; Q3 four; Q4 three; Q5 two; Q6 three; Q7 three; Q8 six; Q9 one.

**M1.** Virginia passed a maternal-status law in 1662 and a baptism-status law in 1667.
The Virginia arrival in 1619 was not the beginning of Atlantic enslavement.
Students retrieve each law's effect from teaching; do not supply that explanation now.

**M2.** Georgia's prohibition lasted from 1735 through 1750.
In 1703, 42 percent of New York City households enslaved someone.
Charleston's proportion was higher. These are not all-colony counts.
Washington's 1799 list and Jefferson's 1774 Farm Book record people they enslaved.

**M3.** Three arrows connect Europe, West Africa, and the Americas; route labels are withheld.
**M4.** Read the made-up Mara record neutrally.

**M5.** A 1770 notice seeks two enslaved men skilled in making clothes and offers wages.
Separate modern context: The wages went to enslavers who hired them out.
The notice is by Thomas Fell. Do not explain its legal effect for students.

**M6.** Four new made-up documents:
An official confirms a fixed service term ended yesterday.
A signed legal grant releases its recipient immediately.
A court judgment declares a claimant free.
Lawmakers receive an unanswered request for release.
These are not Ada, Ben, Celia, or David's papers.

**M7.** Compare Pennsylvania's 1780 act with Massachusetts court decisions by 1783.
Students retrieve institutional effects from teaching; do not supply them now.

**M8.** A free Black household record shows legally free children, a home purchase, and paid work.
It also describes racial exclusion. This original composite is not one quoted historical document.
Its facts rest on the Vassall free-life history.
