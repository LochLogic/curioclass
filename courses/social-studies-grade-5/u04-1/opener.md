# Section 4.1: Promises and protected rights

## Purpose and teaching

Days 44 to 46, forty minutes daily. Teach natural rights as inherent human rights and legal rights as protection established in law. Equality is an argument about human standing, not evidence that all people gained equal participation. Rule of law constrains officials. Review consent as people agreeing to how decisions are made. Separate a petition's request from an outcome. Use Hall only as a reviewed hook, never as fresh exit evidence.

## Continuity and access

Review timeline: equal gaps for equal years and ten-year marks. Review perspective: two sides of one event.
Review map-scale: source and response calibration match. Modern boundaries orient geography, not eighteenth-century borders.
Review corroboration: two tellers who each saw it agree; a copy adds nothing.
Modern summaries do not become independent eyewitnesses merely by agreeing.
Earlier grade 5 drafts supply directions, not assumed learning. Reteach explicitly when evidence is absent.
Allow public passing, neutral reading, large print, pointing and private dictation without content cues.
Never ask about home, money, bodies, religion or immigration status. No historical role-play or sacred material.
Name continuing Native nations specifically. Enslaved people were forced and denied freedom.
For sensitive passages, offer an explicitly made-up public-rule comparison with the same evidence skill.
Record historical content as not yet seen when the alternative does not sample it.
