# Day 140: Midwest communities and movement evidence

## Teacher page
### Before class
Read opener. Prepare cards-140, question-board, evidence-sheet, locator, repair-cards and record. Keep teacher-key and today's generated key private. Conceal future exits in exit-masters. Attach one cue to each day 139 paper.

### The day
#### 10 min. Return and repair
Return day 139's exit and written tasks: `s07-P139` and `s07-F139`. If many show error I, compare repair-cards' Scope line for five minutes. Otherwise meet up to six; others follow private cues. All attempt fresh [[s07-F140]] for five minutes, then see [[steps:s07-F140]]. This checks a note's coverage without completing an account of a region. Keep retry for day 141.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review region: a group of states that sit together and share something, grade 3 day 11. Add Midwest to locator. Review culture and contribution from days 139 and 138. Read MN-T: Menominee leaders organized research and education. Review sourcing from grade 3 day 95. **Ask.** [[s07-P140]] Begin with MN-T, then show its annotation look-fors only. Event date: 1993; publication date: unknown. Native nations did not immigrate into their own homelands. A movement reason may be unstated.

#### 14 min. Regional evidence
Read all four cards: Menominee education, Black women's voting organization, Swedish sawmill recruitment and Hmong adult education. Explain recruited: invited to take particular work. Review immigrant, a person coming from another country to live, from day 92. The supplied Swedish fragment supports a work reason; the other fragments give no movement reason. Do not invent a shared motive.

Fifteen simultaneous pairs complete [[s07-P140]] on every fragment. Each partner takes one minute; independent annotations follow. Use evidence-sheet's columns and the movement column on today's page. Mark “not stated” where appropriate. These are labels and evidence words, not group-contribution sentences or an expanded regional account. No held civic caption appears.

#### 4 min. Record and board
Add source labels to question-board's community question. Keep differing community labels on locator in Regional Voices; four cards cannot represent everyone. Collect all annotations and retries for tomorrow. Give private cues without an oral round.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
First reveal [[s07-X140]] on half slips. Children answer alone, with neutral reading or exact dictation. “Advised” means gave advice; explain only that word if needed. After class allow 30 times 30 seconds, 15 minutes, for exit and practice. Record components only as shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Return every paper on day 141.

### Other rooms
Copy current fragments and tasks on plain paper. Keep the exit separate. No live page, visitor or device is needed.

### At home
**Swap.** Copy current cards and tasks onto plain paper without a printer.
**Skip.** Class meetings and roster sampling.
- **10 min. Feedback.** Return yesterday's work. Your child tries [[s07-F140]]. Read its key privately afterward.
- **8 min. First source.** Read MN-T; begin [[s07-P140]].
  **Say.** “What event does 1993 date?” Expect the institute's organization.
- **14 min. Other sources.** Read the remaining cards-140. Complete the same annotations, including movement evidence.
- **4 min. Limits.** Recheck the difference between a stated reason and “not stated.”
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s07-X140]] alone, without hints. Save all papers for tomorrow's feedback.

### Shorter or longer
Trim repeated talk, preserving every card, movement annotations and the exit. Longer: check another annotation against its source.

### Words and supports
Review sourcing, region, culture, contribution and immigrant in their learned meanings. Read aloud, enlarge print or scribe exactly; public passing is welcome.

### Tomorrow
Day 141 studies Southwest communities. Return day 140's exit, retry and annotations. If many show error A, only European immigrants shaping the region, use the ten-minute Return and repair to identify missing community sources. Others use private cues.
