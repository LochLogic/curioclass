# Day 141: Southwest peoples, dates and evidence

## Teacher page
### Before class
Read opener. Prepare cards-141, question-board, evidence-sheet, locator, repair-cards and record. Conceal other exit-masters. Use today's generated item key for exit look-fors; teacher-key supplies annotation guidance. Return day 140 papers with private cues. Reserve the army context below until tickets are collected.

### The day
#### 10 min. Return and repair
Return day 140's exit, retry and annotations. If many show error A, use repair-cards' Evidence line for five minutes: one record leaves other voices unknown. Otherwise meet up to six while others follow private cues. Everyone attempts fresh [[s07-F141]] for five minutes, then sees [[steps:s07-F141]]. Keep retry for day 142.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review region, culture and contribution from days 138 to 140. Add Southwest to locator. Read MES only: the Mescalero Apache Tribe includes Mescalero, Lipan and Chiricahua people today, in ancestral homelands. Teach this specific account as new. Read no sacred sections. Review sourcing from grade 3 day 95. **Ask.** [[s07-P141]] Model selecting the named people and “today” after attempts. A present account is not a claim that nothing changed.

#### 14 min. Regional evidence
Read the other five fragments. Mescalero Apache archaeologists study history with students. Nixon's medical practice shows African American civilian life. Distinguish the two CAM fragments: Spanish government building, 1610; Mexican village, 1843. Keep their identity labels with their dates. Vietnamese support groups, media and stores show later community institutions. These examples never describe every person's work or justify conquest.

Fifteen simultaneous pairs annotate every fragment using [[s07-P141]] and evidence-sheet's columns. Each partner takes one minute; independent marks follow. Match labels, evidence words and dates; order the two CAM dates. No complete occupational descriptions, military road example or final contribution is practiced.

#### 4 min. Date and scope
Check CAM date order and identities on the same annotations; do not equate Spanish with Mexican. Add labels beside question-board's community question. Hear at most four 45-second label observations, three minutes total. Record speakers; public passing is welcome. Collect all annotations and retain locator in Regional Voices.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
First reveal [[s07-X141]] on half slips. Give three minutes alone, with neutral reading or exact dictation. Collect, then explain: these army operations included warfare against Mescalero and Lipan Apache peoples. Building something does not excuse taking another people's land. No reenactment or personal disclosure follows.

After class allow 30 times 30 seconds, 15 minutes, for exit and practice. Record components as shown, shown with help, not yet seen only. Require road construction and limited scope, not unstated army-post connections. Return every paper on day 142.

### Other rooms

  **Say.** “Who does MES name today?” Expect Mescalero, Lipan and Chiricahua people.
- **14 min. Other sources.** Read the remaining cards-141; finish the same annotations together, then independently.
- **4 min. Dates.** Check the two CAM dates and their identity labels.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s07-X141]] alone. Collect it before reading the teacher page's brief army context. Keep all papers for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer
Trim sampled talk, preserving every fragment and the exit. Longer: check an existing identity/date annotation.

### Words and supports
Review sourcing, contribution, culture, region, forced and chosen in their learned meanings. Read aloud, enlarge print or scribe exactly. Identities come from records, never assumptions about classmates.

If many show error I, universal occupations, or error L, Spanish always meaning Mexican, combine source-scope and dated-label repair in its ten-minute Return and repair. Give remaining pupils written cues.
