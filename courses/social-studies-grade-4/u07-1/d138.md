# Day 138: Northeast community evidence

## Teacher page
### Before class
Read opener. Prepare cards-138, town-pictures and repair-cards; conceal other exits in exit-masters. Use today's generated item key for the exit; teacher-key supplies annotation guidance. Prepare record, evidence-sheet, locator and question-board. Mark day 137's work privately, with one next move each.

### The day
#### 10 min. Return and repair
Return day 137's exit and written tasks: `s07-RET137`, `s07-P137`, `s07-P137-questions` and `s07-P137-evidence`. If many show error U or dated-evidence gaps, compare the two town-pictures for five minutes: evidence of a change versus a preference. Otherwise meet up to six; others follow written cues. Use repair-cards' Evidence line for a reminder. Everyone attempts fresh [[s07-F138]] for five minutes. Then reveal [[steps:s07-F138]]. Keep retries for day 139; no extra oral round.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review region: a group of states that sit together and share something, grade 3 day 11. Put Northeast on locator; communities overlap classroom regions. Review sourcing: who made it, when and why, grade 3 day 95. **Ask.** [[s07-P138]] using the first MW-T fragment. Model locating 1665 and 1685 only after attempts. These are event dates; the public webpage is undated. Its maker is the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe today. Review grade 3 day 100's Plymouth settlement reminder in the second MW-T fragment; same maker, different event.

#### 14 min. Regional evidence
Read all five fragments, including Black Bostonians, Irish laborers and Somali Bantu public-service work. Review contribution, something a nation shares or makes that continues today, grade 3 day 83. Extend it to particular community institutions. Abolition means ending slavery. Review forced and chosen, grade 3 day 146: enslaved people were forced to work, without freedom to refuse. Nothing here makes everyone's work chosen.

Fifteen pairs annotate all fragments through [[s07-P138]]; use evidence-sheet's columns. Each partner takes one minute concurrently, then works independently. Record source labels, dates and evidence words, not finished group-contribution sentences. Support one group while others annotate. No future construction caption appears.

#### 4 min. Scope marks
Everyone checks dates and limits on the same annotations. Mark a particular institution rather than every person's occupation. Check today's evidence against question-board's community question. Collect records for day 139; retain locator in Regional Voices.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
First reveal [[s07-X138]] on half slips. Children answer alone; neutral reading and exact dictation preserve the paper path. After class allow 30 times 30 seconds, 15 minutes, to check exit and practice. On record use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen per component. A valid construction detail and lack of freedom meet the exit; no unstated fact is required.

### Other rooms
Copy current fragments and tasks on plain paper. Keep the exit separate and hidden. No live source or device is required.

**Skip.** Class meetings and roster sampling.

  **Say.** “Which dates belong to the deed?” Expect 1665 and 1685, from the first fragment.
- **14 min. Other voices.** Read the remaining fragments. Complete the same annotations together, then independently.
- **4 min. Limits.** Check which people each fragment actually names.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s07-X138]] alone, with no hints. Save all papers for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer
Trim repeated discussion, retaining all five fragments and the exit. Longer: check a second annotation's evidence words.

### Words and supports
Review contribution, sourcing, region, forced and chosen as above. Read fragments aloud; use enlarged text or exact dictation. Public passing is welcome.

### Tomorrow
Day 139 studies Southeast communities. Return day 138's exit, repair and annotations. If many show error B, all work chosen, compare choice and force. Address error I, universal occupations, or error J, nations only in the past, within the same ten-minute block and written cues.
