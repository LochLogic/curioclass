# Day 82: Claims and homelands

## Teacher page
### Before class
Prepare cards-082.md and practice-082.md master; set out record.md, question-board.md, source-log.md, growth-map.md, teaching-locator.png and timeline.md. Return day 81 papers. Keep teacher-key.md, current generated keys and exit-082-sources.md adult-only. Hold exit-082.md and exit-082-stimulus.md covered until Exit ticket.

### The day
#### 5 min. Return and repair
Return day 81's exit and question-board papers. Hand back every assigned written task, including practice and repair papers, with private feedback. For error A use the made-up admission/road notices and [[s05-F82-1]]. Error E uses [[s05-F82-2]]; a scale gap uses [[s05-F82-3]]. More than a third sharing one gap: one class move; otherwise a small group. Each child receives one priority. Other needs get written reminders in the same slot.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review homeland: land a nation's people have known as home for a very long time. Read the river card aloud. Cayuse, Umatilla and Walla Walla peoples have river homelands and continue today. Their account describes fishing, trading and travel. Locate the river region without drawing an exact homeland boundary.
Review purchase: payment for another government's claim. Teach Louisiana's political transfer separately from its resident account. The Osage Nation describes later treaties ceding its land. Explain cede: give up claimed rights or control. Land was taken from Native nations; it was not empty.
Review treaty from day 81. Oregon's boundary agreement settled British and United States claims. Review consent: people agreeing to how decisions are made. Outside governments' agreement alone does not establish Native agreement. Do not display the exit's paired captions or chronology task.

#### 17 min. Homeland work
First five minutes finish the assigned repair, with the worked notice covered for the response. Preserve twelve minutes for homeland work. Children answer [[s05-P82-1]] from the river card. With the Louisiana maker label, answer [[s05-P82-2]] only about authorship. Then underline the Osage land detail for [[s05-P82-3]]. Treat the cards separately, without a combined dates/consent response.
Fifteen parallel pairs each take 45 seconds per child, 90 seconds total, pointing to one supporting detail. Review source log: maker, date, detail and question. Keep Growth Map annotations and the timeline in folders. No child reports personal history or performs a historical role.

#### 5 min. Question board
Review the board's consent question. Six new volunteers read one annotation, 45 seconds each, 4.5 minutes. Passing is allowed. Record names and collect every child's papers; day 83 returns written feedback to all.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket
Cover practice. Reveal the two new captions and the separate equal-year line with the half slip. Children answer [[s05-X82-1]] and [[s05-X82-2]] independently. Accept approximate positions that preserve the ten-year scale: three years beyond 1800; six beyond 1840. The captions cannot prove either agreement or opposition by Native nations. Neutral reading and exact dictation are access. After class allow thirty minutes for thirty exits, plus fifteen for practice and board papers. Record shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: reduce pair repetition. Keep both resident accounts and exit. Longer: identify a missing agreement record.

### Words and supports
Review homeland, treaty, purchase, consent and source log at use. Define cede. Read context aloud; offer home-language rehearsal and a neutral scribe. Public passing is allowed.
