# Day 142: Western communities and unequal rights

## Teacher page
### Before class
Read opener and cards-142. Use Side 2 instead of duplicate cards; evidence-sheet is the column master. Prepare question-board, locator, repair-cards and record. Return day 141 papers with one private cue each. Keep exit-masters and teacher-key hidden. The generated key corrects the older exit key: require only information printed on the slip.

### The day
#### 10 min. Return and repair
Return day 141's exit, annotations and retry. If many show error I, everyone having one occupation, or error L, interchangeable Spanish/Mexican labels, compare repair-cards' Scope line for five minutes. Keep date and identity together. Otherwise meet up to six while others follow private cues. Everyone tries fresh [[s07-F142]] for five minutes; reveal [[steps:s07-F142]] afterward. Say these exhibit tags are made up.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review region, culture and contribution from days 138 to 141. Review grade 2's Ellis Island and Angel Island labels, reminders only. Read GR and CH-T on Side 2. The Gold Rush drew people from many places. Review Chinese farm and railroad work from days 92 and 134. CH-T adds this Western community record, including town-building. Do not assign one occupation to everyone.

Read EX-T. Exclusion denied targeted people entry or rights. Naturalization is becoming a citizen through a legal process. The law restricted Chinese labor immigration; exceptions included diplomatic officials with required credentials. It also barred Chinese naturalization. Rights restrictions did not measure people's contributions.

#### 14 min. Source investigation
Read the other Side 2 fragments. MT returns day 137's living Makah community. PICO identifies one Afro-Latino governor, not all Black Western experiences. PR-T and PM-T distinguish Spanish and later Mexican control. Review sourcing: who made it, when and why, from grade 3 day 95. These are present institutional summaries for public learning, not eyewitness accounts. Event dates differ from summary dates.

**Ask.** [[s07-P142]] Start with PR-T; after attempts, model marking its label and 1776. Fifteen concurrent pairs annotate all seven fragments, using evidence-sheet columns. Each partner takes one minute within this block; independent marks follow. Unknown source dates stay unknown. Native homelands are not immigrant destinations.

#### 4 min. Record and board
Add West source labels to locator and question-board. Hear at most four 45-second evidence-location observations, three minutes total; record speakers. Passing is welcome. Collect every annotation and retry. Keep locator in Regional Voices.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
First reveal [[s07-X142]] on half slips. Allow three minutes alone; read neutrally or scribe exact words. Collect everyone's paper. After class, allow 30 times 30 seconds, 15 minutes, for exit and annotation cues. Record only shown, shown with help, not yet seen by component. Return all work on day 143.

### Side 2
**Western evidence**
Original summaries for learners, 2026. Event dates appear below.

**MT, Makah Tribe.** Makah people maintain community life in their homeland today. Some artists sell their work.

**PICO, National Park Service.** Pio Pico, an Afro-Latino Californian, served as governor under Mexican rule.

**PR-T, National Park Service.** Spanish colonists established San Francisco's military garrison in 1776.

**PM-T, National Park Service.** After Mexican independence, California passed into Mexican control.

**GR, California State Archives.** After 1848, gold seekers came from China, Australia and many other places.

**CH-T, California State Parks.** Chinese immigrants sought gold and helped build railroads, farms and towns.

**EX-T, National Archives.** The 1882 law restricted Chinese labor immigration and barred naturalization. Some groups had exceptions.
