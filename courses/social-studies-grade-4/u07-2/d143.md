# Day 143: Japanese American work and denied freedom

## Teacher page
### Before class
Prepare Side 2 from cards-143, evidence-sheet columns, continuity-change headings, locator, question-board, repair-cards and record. Keep teacher-key and exit-masters private; use the generated item key for look-fors. Return day 142 work with one cue per child. Do not show future cards or photographs.

### The day
#### 10 min. Return and repair
Return day 142 papers: `s07-P142`, `s07-F142` and `s07-X142`. If many show error A, miners alone, error I, everyone alike, or error L, interchangeable identities, compare repair-cards' Scope line for five minutes. Otherwise meet up to six while others follow cues. Everyone attempts fresh [[s07-F143]] for five minutes, then sees [[steps:s07-F143]]. These labels are made up.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review culture, contribution and sourcing from days 138 to 142. Read Side 2's JA-T gardening summary, an account from a Japanese American museum. Japanese immigrants did agricultural work; restrictions on landholding pushed many toward urban gardening. These are varied livelihoods, not everyone's occupation.

Read IN-T separately. Incarceration means confinement that denies freedom. Government forced Japanese Americans, including citizens, into confinement in 1942. This denied freedom; it was not their choice. Review forced and chosen from grade 3 day 146. Read JA-T's 1988 apology summary. Present institutional summaries describe earlier events; they are not firsthand testimony. No victim role play or personal histories.

#### 14 min. Evidence and connections
Read FI-T. Its Filipino community history is distinct from the Japanese and Chinese records. Day 142's Chinese law record is a reminder only. **Ask.** [[s07-P143]] After attempts, model locating FI-T's label and 1935. Pairs mark each current fragment's label, date and evidence; each partner takes one minute concurrently. Independent marks follow.

Review grade 3 day 147's continuity-change record: then, now, what stayed, with sources. Continuity means something stays; change means something differs. Use separate tokens in [[s07-K143-1]], then reveal [[steps:s07-K143-1]]. Mark headings only, without a completed historical explanation. A later record never proves when an activity began.

Ideas include instructions; goods are usable things, reviewed from day 124. People, goods and ideas can move. **Ask.** [[s07-K143-2]] Reveal its steps afterward. Shared sketches show a social connection; land alone is insufficient.

#### 4 min. Record and board
Add source labels to locator and question-board. Hear at most four 45-second label observations, three minutes total; record speakers. Public passing is welcome. Collect every marked fragment and retry; retain continuity-change headings in Regional Voices. Return one day 142 cue to everyone, through their papers.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
First reveal [[s07-X143]] on half slips. Give three minutes independently, with neutral reading or exact dictation. Collect all papers. After class, allow 30 times 30 seconds, 15 minutes, for exit and practice cues. Use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen by component. Return every paper on day 144.

### Words and supports

### Side 2
**Community evidence**
Original summaries for learners, 2026. Historical dates appear below.

**JA-T, Japanese American National Museum.** After California's 1920 land restrictions, many Japanese immigrants left agricultural work for urban gardening.

**IN-T, National Archives.** In 1942, government forced Japanese Americans into confinement. Citizens were among those denied freedom.

**JA-T, Japanese American National Museum.** In 1988, federal law provided an apology for wartime incarceration.

**FI-T, Filipino Cultural Heritage Society of Washington.** A Seattle Filipino organization traces its beginnings to 1935. It now provides youth and senior services.
