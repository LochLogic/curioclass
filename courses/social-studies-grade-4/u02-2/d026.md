# Day 26: Resources and industries

## Teacher page

### Before class
Read opener.md, teacher-only key-exits.md and the generated key. Prepare industry-sources.md, topic-notes.md (s02-u02-2-topic-notes), source-log.md, question-board.md and record.md. The student page incorporates practice-26.md, industry summaries and note fields; print no duplicates. Bring day 25 papers and each child's earlier source log. Keep exit-26.md hidden until the exit.

### The day
#### 5 min. Return and repair
Return s02-X25, s02-P25, s02-P25-R and s02-P25-1. If many showed error D, unsupported map conclusions, use [[s02-P26-R]]. Otherwise meet one group while others write. This map record is made up. Review legend: the map key. A key supports its named feature, not unrelated weather. Extend the same priority during Research work.

#### 8 min. Industry sectors
Review industry in grade 3's words: all the work that gets or makes one kind of thing, such as fishing. Do not reuse that earlier case. Review natural resource, something from nature people use. Read industry-sources.md's dated country summaries. Explain sawmill as a place cutting wood into lumber; agriculture as growing crops and raising animals. Children keep the country selected earlier. Ask no one about household work or money. No port case or resident-job limit is rehearsed with an industry explanation.

#### 17 min. Research work
First five minutes extend day 25's priority; others begin [[s02-P26]]. Children independently explain how wood supplies lumber production or water supports growing crops, citing their source. Review the source log and six-topic headings. Each child audits existing people, languages, government, cities, land and industries notes. Check dated language scope, locator, landmark, resource and one analyzed relationship. Mark missing source kinds in the audit table; map and atlas can overlap, but name the map and collection actually used. Mark gaps privately for day 27. Collect all notes and repairs for written feedback.

#### 5 min. Explanation criteria
Review order, detail and accuracy from day 22. These equipment fragments are made up. Complete [[s02-P26-1]] and [[s02-P26-2]], separately. Discuss the single recorded supply and the heading's organizing job after responses. Do not combine them into a complete strength-and-repair critique. Retain individual versions for later revision; add one supported industry finding to the question board.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket
Reveal exit-26.md, exactly [[s02-X26]]. Its text supplies cargo, goods being moved, and the service context. Explain service only as work done for others, without interpreting the case. Everyone writes independently; neutral reading or dictation is available. Collect all. Mark afterward: 30 times 45 seconds, 22.5 minutes, for exits; 30 times 90 seconds, 45 minutes, for practice and folder audits. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen per component; record access separately.

### Other rooms
Copy the source summaries and blank note fields on paper. Retain earlier paper notes and draw the audit table. Reveal the separately copied exit only at its slot.

**Swap.** No copies: copy the source summary and fold paper for note headings.

- **8 min. Sources.** Read your country's industry summary.

- **5 min. Criteria.** Complete [[s02-P26-1]] and [[s02-P26-2]] separately.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal [[s02-X26]]. Your child answers alone; discuss afterward.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: omit board reporting, preserving the mechanism, audit and criteria. Longer: verify an existing resource/work note against its source.

### Words and supports
Review industry, natural resource and source log. Read summaries aloud, enlarge text or take dictation. Judge the supported relationship separately from handwriting. No public reporting is required.

Day 27 drafts a short report from these notes. Return s02-X26 and every day 26 practice paper. Error I, everyone's job inferred, or error D, an unsupported connection, enters Return and repair and the first five Draft work minutes. Carry each child's marked topic/source gaps.
