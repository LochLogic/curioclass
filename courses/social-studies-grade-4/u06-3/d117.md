# Day 117: Dated lands and forced movement

## Teacher page
### Before class
Print cards-117, student pages, practice-117 and its seven source layers. Trace practice-117-response separately for physical and climate annotations. Hold exit sources. Trace the exit response outline onto half slips, retaining its size. Keep keys separate; mark from generated keys. Prepare day 116 returns. Use printed secular summaries, not live photographs or a visitor. Offer private supported reading of the same history.

### The day
#### 5 min. Return and repair
Return day 116's exit tickets and written tasks: s06-X116, s06-P116-1, s06-P116-2, s06-R116, s06-P116, s06-P116-3. Review Return and repair. If many showed error T, use [[s06-R117]]. Error E matches a practice mark to its key; error R locates one practice feature. Further needs use the first five Layer work minutes.

#### 7 min. Historical context
Teach this historical context explicitly. Read cards-117's community account first, then historical context, naming the Mescalero Apache Tribe, museum and state-history sources. The army forced Mescalero Apache people to Bosque Redondo in 1863; they left in 1865. The Navajo Long Walk includes forced removals in 1864. Navajo people resisted removal and negotiated return in 1868. Their nations govern today. No marching enactment or graphic details. Review homeland and present lands from grade 3. Define reservation as land set aside for a nation; confinement means being held without freedom to leave.

#### 18 min. Layer work
Review climate: what a place is usually like over many years. Independently use [[s06-F117]] and [[s06-P117]] on separate physical and climate outlines. Use [[s06-P117-1]] for each Mescalero locator separately, labeling time and relationship. Locators are not legal borders; arrows connect general areas, never exact travel tracks. Review Coronado expedition context from day 72; supply the 1540 summary afresh. Use [[s06-P117-2]] with separate exploration and settlement sheets. Hear six different children, 45 seconds each, within work. Record names; all submit paper. If many conflate layers, reread one date and relationship before revision.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket
Cover practice. Reveal the unseen Navajo locators now. Read captions without interpreting them. Children complete [[s06-X117a]] on the traced half-slip outline and [[s06-X117b]] in the student Exit box. Match the arrow's pattern to the held key. Neutral dictation is available. Collect both pieces.

#### 3 min. Close
The Mescalero Apache Tribe and Navajo Nation are living communities. Allow private discussion and public passing. Tomorrow uses Western feature categories.

### Other rooms
No printer: trace separate outlines at matching size. Printed attributed summaries replace unavailable pages, photographs or visitors. No sacred material is shown.

- **7 min. Exit ticket.** Cover practice. Reveal held Navajo sources. Your child completes [[s06-X117a]] and [[s06-X117b]] independently.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: omit public observations. Longer: compare one source label with its own dated annotation.

### Words and supports
Review homeland, present lands and climate. Forced removal means movement against people's choice. Use neutral reading and exact dictation. Never request personal or family histories.
