# Day 112: What institutions do

## Teacher page
### Before class
Print practice-112, student pages and only the institution portion of cards-112. Use today's sourced voting note for historical limits. Prepare day 111 returns, record and source-index. Draw equal decade marks from 1870 through 1900 with yearly subdivisions. Keep exit-112-stimulus and exit-112 covered. Keep teacher-key and generated item keys separate; use the generated keys' fuller acceptance rules.

### The day
#### 5 min. Return and repair
Return day 111's exit tickets and every practice response. Review Return and repair. If many showed error C, assign [[s06-R112-C]]; for arithmetic, [[s06-R112-arithmetic]]; for error N, [[s06-R112-N]]. Each child does one fresh paper retry. Complete other repairs in the first five Institution work minutes.

#### 10 min. Teaching first
Review grade 3's constitution: a basic plan that sets up a government, says what it may and may not do, and may list rights. Review colony: a settlement far from the home country, which claims it. Review consent: people agreeing to how decisions are made, by vote or by choosing representatives; not liking every law. Representation means someone acts for others in government. Read institution cards separately.
**Ask.** [[s06-K112-constitution]]
Then reveal its explanation.
**Ask.** [[s06-K112-congress]]
Then explain Congress's national lawmaking work. The Declaration complained about taxation without consent. States have voting congressional members; British colonies lacked that voice in Britain's Parliament. Colonial assemblies existed: self-government did not begin with statehood. Ratification means formal agreement to a constitution, distinct from declaring independence. Teach the printed voting note separately.

#### 17 min. Institution work
Children match isolated functions with [[s06-P112-1]] on practice-112. Read the two historical claims; use [[s06-P112-2]] and [[s06-P112-limit]] on side 2. Review territory from day 81: land governed within a larger country. Territories can have local government and constitutions. A delegate can work on committees but cannot vote on final House legislation; territories have no senators. These limits do not mean no government. Hear six single source observations, 45 seconds each, while others write. Record names; public passing is allowed. Everyone submits paper. Do not rehearse a petition response or combined benefits-and-limits paragraph.

#### 6 min. Exit ticket
Cover teaching examples. Reveal the made-up petition in exit-112-stimulus. Children answer [[s06-X112a]] and [[s06-X112b]] on half-slip exit-112, without an option bank. Neutral reading or exact dictation is available. Collect all paper for after-class marking and return on day 113.

#### 2 min. Close
Collect paper. Retain the statehood question for tomorrow's public records.

### Other rooms
No printer: copy isolated cards and response spaces; keep the petition separate. No device or visitor is required.

### At home
**Swap.** No cards: copy the institution lines separately.
**Skip.** Six public observations.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return day 111 paper. Complete one assigned retry.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Use [[s06-K112-constitution]] and [[s06-K112-congress]], then explain their answers.
  **Say.** "What work does Congress do?" Expect: makes national laws.
- **14 min. Institution work.** Use [[s06-P112-1]] on practice-112. Read the voting note and answer [[s06-P112-2]] and [[s06-P112-limit]]. Explain the delegate's voting limit separately.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** Cover examples. Reveal exit-112-stimulus. Your child answers [[s06-X112a]] and [[s06-X112b]] alone.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: omit public observations. Longer: use [[s06-C112-1]] to distinguish committee work and final House voting.

### Words and supports
Review constitution, colony, consent and territory. Read one card at a time. Representation: acting for others. Ratification: formal agreement to a constitution. A neutral scribe records the child's ideas.
