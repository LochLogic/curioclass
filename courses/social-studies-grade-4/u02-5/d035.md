# Day 35: Write from notes and name both makers

## Teacher page

### Before class
Read opener.md, teacher-only key-exits.md and current generated answers. Prepare practice-35.md and importance-record.md within the page. Return day 34 papers. Keep blank claim-notes.md headings, established topic-notes.md (s02-u02-2-topic-notes), source-log.md, goods-map.md and selected trade-sources.md. Bring question-board.md and record.md. Seal exit-35.md with clothing-map.svg; remove completed paragraph models.

### The day
#### 5 min. Return and repair
Return s02-X34, s02-P34 and s02-P34-R. If many day 34 exits showed error G, preference as proof, or error N, source titles only, use [[s02-P35-R]] with all; otherwise one group. This reading room is made up. Underlining a supplied link avoids modeling the withheld argument. Others read feedback on their notes.

#### 5 min. Organized notes
Review day 14 claim, evidence and reasoning, and day 34's claim sheet headings. A claim answers the question; evidence is a relevant source detail; reasoning explains its support. Remove filled prose frames. Children select their notes and two distinct makers; optional blank prompts remain. The article and its goods diagram count as one underlying source.

#### 20 min. Claim work
First five minutes extend the day 34 repair. Others start [[s02-P35]] alone. Write a bounded shared-purchasing connection, using the foreign affairs article and national goods table, with established notes if useful. These records show exchanges, not every worker's dependence or benefit. Use only notes linked to checked source rows; otherwise use the two selected records. Give no complete paragraph model. Partners each get 45 seconds simultaneously to read their claim and point to its details. Return to individual writing; collect all paragraphs. If many overclaim, indicate the named record's scope before revision.

#### 5 min. Source audit
First three minutes: reveal [[s02-E9]] on the importance record, independently, with no model of another user's need. Collect it. Last two minutes: children underline their paragraph's two source-linked details and circle the reasoning link. Teacher updates the question board's unit answer. This audit adds no second judgment of the paragraph.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket
Give the unseen half slip, exactly [[s02-X35]], with its diagram. Children respond alone; supply neutral reading or transcription only. Collect all. After class allow 22.5 minutes for thirty exits, 30 minutes for paragraphs/repair, and 15 minutes for E9, thirty at 30 seconds. Use record.md's shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keep source support and claim reasoning in separate cells, without points. Return every paper day 36.

### Other rooms
Paper extracts replace screens. Hand-copy blank prompts and the sealed exit diagram if needed.

### At home
**Swap.** No copies: copy selected records and blank headings. Hide independent tasks.
**Skip.** Class collection and public board update.
- **5 min. Return.** Return day 34 work; use [[s02-P35-R]] for one detail link.
- **5 min. Notes.** Choose notes from two distinct makers.
- **15 min. Claim.** Your child writes [[s02-P35]] independently. **Say.** "Where is each source's detail?" Expect two actual details linked to the claim, not titles alone.
- **5 min. Source audit.** Give [[s02-E9]] without suggesting a user. Then point to the paragraph's existing links.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s02-X35]] alone; keep all papers for feedback.

### Shorter or longer
Shorter: reduce optional sharing, not independent evidence or audit. Longer: improve the paragraph's existing scope limit.

### Words and supports
Review source-sourcing, claim, evidence and reasoning. Optional blank prompts: Claim; source detail; other source detail; reason. Allow dictation without supplying ideas; no personal disclosures.

### Tomorrow
Day 36 returns s02-X35, s02-P35, s02-P35-R and s02-E9. Error G, preference, error N, titles only, or error F, everyone gains, gets five opening minutes and five Evidence work minutes. Match components without redrafting paragraphs.
