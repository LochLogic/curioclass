# Day 40 teacher-only answer key

Keep this sheet separate from every student packet.
Use one scale per component: shown; shown with help; not yet seen.
Access support is separate from content help. No points or totals.

Record columns: facts/opinions; map evidence; source limit; access/help; private next step.

## C1-E40-1

Sort sentences 1 to 4 into facts and opinions.

Facts: 1 and 2. Opinions: 3 and 4. Facts concern locations that evidence can check; opinions express preferences.

Teaching sequence: Look for: Facts: 1 and 2. Opinions: 3 and 4. Facts concern locations that evidence can check; opinions express preferences.

Samples, first shows the component, second needs work: Facts 1, 2; opinions 3, 4. | All four are facts because they are printed.

Wrong: All four are facts because they are printed. Error L. A printed claim still needs relevant evidence; separate preferences.

## C1-E40-2

Use Source B to judge sentence 1. Explain your evidence.

Supported: the thick Yukon River line runs through the outline labeled Alaska. A location detail, not merely the source title, supports sentence 1.

Teaching sequence: Look for: Supported: the thick Yukon River line runs through the outline labeled Alaska. A location detail, not merely the source title, supports sentence 1.

Samples, first shows the component, second needs work: The river line crosses the area labeled Alaska. | The map has a title, so the sentence is true.

Wrong: The leaflet says it, so the map agrees. Error D. Read the separate map detail instead of repeating the first source.

## C1-E40-3

Explain one limit of using Source B to trust the whole leaflet.

The map supports the location claim, not preference claims or advice for every visitor. Agreement on location does not make opinions factual or establish all leaflet claims.

Teaching sequence: Look for: The map supports the location claim, not preference claims or advice for every visitor. Agreement on location does not make opinions factual or establish all leaflet claims.

Samples, first shows the component, second needs work: It shows where the river is, not what everyone likes. | The map proves every visitor should choose Alaska.

Wrong: The map proves every visitor should choose Alaska. Error C. Location evidence cannot establish a preference.

Wrong: One opinion makes all the leaflet false. Error K. Judge each claim separately.
