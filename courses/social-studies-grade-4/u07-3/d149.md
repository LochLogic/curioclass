# Day 149: Johnstown: built systems and warning evidence

## Teacher page
### Before class
Prepare cards-149 as Side 2, evidence-sheet columns, locator, question-board, relationship-strips and record. Read opener; keep teacher-key and exit-masters separate. Use generated current item keys; these take precedence over teacher-key. Return day 148 papers with private location cues. Keep repair-cards' Evidence line ready. Keep the half-slip hidden until its turn.

### The day
#### 10 min. Return and repair
Return day 148's two judged items, annotations and retry. Review Return and repair. If many show error T, rules make rain, or error P, quiet appearance means safe access, compare [[s07-K149-R]] and the clearance notice for five minutes. Otherwise meet up to six. Everyone tries fresh [[s07-F149]] for five minutes before its key. Carry priority error S or error O through private cues.

#### 8 min. Teaching first
Review flood and grade 3 day 93's cause-effect relationship. Introduce technological catastrophe: disaster involving built-system failure. Read JOF aloud. Draw static-inventories' reservoir, dam and downstream valley, labeled not to scale. **Ask.** [[s07-K149]] Then show [[steps:s07-K149]]. Locate failure and warning evidence separately. The summary supplies neither rainfall intensity nor dam changes. Do not infer legal blame. Introduce displacement: having to leave because a place cannot be used safely; this summary alone does not establish who moved.

#### 14 min. Investigation
Review sourcing: who made it, when and why. The public agency summarizes historical accounts for public readers; our short summary is for learners. Alone for six minutes, children attempt [[s07-P149]] in evidence-sheet columns. Separate natural-event words from built-system words. Fifteen concurrent pairs take one minute per partner to locate evidence. Spend six further minutes marking records silent about residence. Compare invented windstorm and industrial-fire inventories individually. They are not Johnstown witnesses. No single-cause critique or paired settlement diagram is modeled. Retain uncompleted relationship-strips.

#### 4 min. Record and board
Collect every classification and retry. Review question-board; add a label about what the account leaves unknown. Hear at most four 45-second source-location observations, three minutes total. Record names; everyone may pass public talk. Road or workshop closure alone does not prove relocation.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket
First reveal [[s07-X149]]. Give three minutes alone, with neutral reading or exact dictation; collect all papers in the last minute. After class, allow 30 children times 40 seconds, 20 minutes, for components and private cues. Review record's sole scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Return all work on day 150.

### Other rooms
Copy current records and tasks on plain paper. Keep the exit hidden. Use separate labels without a projector. No visitor, live event or personal story is needed.

### At home
**Swap.** Copy current records and tasks without a printer; point instead of projecting.
**Skip.** The class board and sampled public observations.
- **10 min. Repair.** Return day 148 papers. Compare [[s07-K149-R]], then attempt [[s07-F149]] before its key.
- **8 min. Read.** Read Side 2 together. Try [[s07-K149]], then reveal its steps.
- **14 min. Investigate.** Your child tries [[s07-P149]]. Be the location partner after independent marks.
  **Say.** "Which object is built?" Expect: the South Fork Dam.
- **4 min. Record.** Check one label; keep papers.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s07-X149]] independently before reading the key. Keep responses for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer
Trim repeated sharing, preserving every record, the fresh retry and independent exit. Longer: refine one source-limit annotation.

### Words and supports
Technological and displacement use the meanings above. Review sourcing, cause-effect and flood. Read neutrally, enlarge labels or scribe exactly; omit frightening details and personal disaster accounts.

### Tomorrow
Day 150 investigates residence and receiving-place evidence. Return day 149 work. If many show error C, excluding built-system causes, compare fresh natural/built labels and give a classification retry in its ten-minute Return and repair. Do not rehearse a missing-cause paragraph.

**JOF: Johnstown public history**
On May 31, 1889, the South Fork Dam failed.
Warnings had been sent downstream.
After failure, those communications became impossible.
Source: National Park Service, public FAQ, updated January 19, 2026.
This summary is for learners.
It does not describe each person's later home.
