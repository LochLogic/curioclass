# Count across 100

Count loose squares once before making tens. Ten full tens make 100. Add one single at a time and point between the new total and its numeral through 110. To add a ten to a starting count in the nineties, join one ten-strip and record the new total. Keep single units visible in the ten-strips.

Paper squares replace counters; folded or drawn strips replace bundled cubes. Keep long rows on a table and use a folded paper view when a joint needs a close look. Put shared counts away on day 120. Children keep their own drawings and blank mats.

Error F appends the ones to 100. Build the groups and write the matching numeral in its places. Error G counts a piece twice. Move or mark once as the count advances. For error K, join the ten as one group of ten ones. Repair a gap or overlap with equal units before recounting.
