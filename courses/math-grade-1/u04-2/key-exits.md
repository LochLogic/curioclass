# Teacher key for the section exits

Teacher only. Confirm the observed reason before assigning any error letter. Record what the paper shows separately from what you heard.

### s04-X74

Four groups of ten and three loose squares; 43 squares in all.

Shape: Organize fixed marks to distinguish tens from loose ones.

- B: 3 tens, 4 ones. Reverses the quantities by place.
- A: 7 ones. Counts four groups as four single ones.

### s04-X75

20; the 2 names two tens.

Shape: Give the value of the left digit of a bare numeral in its linked callout.

- A: 2 ones. Draws two tens but counts the rods as two ones.
- B: 2 ones from the ones place. Treats the left digit as the ones digit.

### s04-X75b

6; the 6 names six ones.

Shape: Give the value of the right digit of a bare numeral in its own linked callout.

- B: 60. Treats the right digit as the tens digit.

### s04-X76

82; eight tens and two ones.

Shape: Recover a missing total from a grouped display presented in two tower clusters.

- B: 28. Reverses the places.
- A: 10. Counts the towers and singles all as ones.

### s04-X77

50 + 9 or 9 + 50; the addends account for the whole 59.

Shape: Generate an expanded expression from a supplied drawing.

- A: 5 + 9. Counts each rod as one one.
- B: 90 + 5. Swaps the counts of tens and ones.

### s04-X78a

74; link 7 to the seven tens and 4 to the four squares.

Shape: Select a numeral for a newly pictured amount and trace the units.

- B: 47. Reverses the places.
- A: 11. Counts sticks as singles.

### s04-X78b

61; show six tens and one single on the mat.

Shape: Select a numeral from spoken units with an empty mat.

- B: 16. Reverses the places.
- A: 7. Treats six tens as six ones.
