# Section 2.2 opener

## Parts and wholes

Use one boundary around the whole, then bring the labels to the groups. Moving a piece from one part to another keeps the whole fixed. Include an empty part. For a missing part, cover one group while the whole is still known; then connect the picture to both operations.

## The mathematics

In a part-whole story the groups already belong to one collection. Finding a missing part can mean subtracting the known part or finding what can join it to make the whole. A numeral goes next to the group it names. A sketch may replace the frame after the relationship is clear.

## Errors to listen for

D: keep the whole outline and cover the known group. G: assign each piece exactly once. H: use one new count per piece.

Use the correct example first, then the folded-paper puppet's invented error. Return feedback privately. Record one rotating group of six at 45 seconds each in Synthesis and collect paper marks from everyone. A child may pass in public talk. Read every stem aloud and offer large dark outlines or a scribe who makes only the marks the child directs. Mark evidence as shown, shown with help, or not yet seen.

## Supplies

Imagined animal cards, Two labeled part mats, cover and split cards, ticket pockets, separate retained booklet pages, blank paper and folded covers. Draw the animals and tear large patterned scrap pieces when supplies are unavailable. Keep all three day 25 pages with their day 26 revisions.

Use adult-cut paper pieces larger than a small-parts tube. Keep them on trays and collect them after use. Every task can be drawn on paper. Keep the teacher keys and assessment pieces closed until their named parts.
