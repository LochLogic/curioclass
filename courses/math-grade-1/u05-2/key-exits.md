## s05-X100

What does 37 plus 6 look like after a trade?

**Answer.** 43. Thirteen ones make one new ten with three ones left; now four tens and three ones.

The drawing connects a group of ten singles to one strip and accounts for the three loose ones.

Shown: Circles ten of the thirteen singles, links the circle to a new strip and shows three loose ones.. Not yet seen: Crosses out ten and shows only three old tens and three ones (A).

- 33 (A). Drops the ten instead of making a strip.
- 16 (D). Counts the three starting tens as three singles alongside thirteen loose ones.

## s05-X101

How does this move help you show 39 plus 9?

**Answer.** 48. 39 + 1 = 40, then 40 + 8 = 48.

Nine old ones and one moving one form the fourth ten. The remaining eight added ones are still present.

Shown: Uses the moved single to complete a new ten, then shows eight leftover ones.. Not yet seen: Drops the newly filled ten and shows 38 (A).

- 38 (A). Loses the newly filled ten.
- 58 (B). Keeps the ten singles beside their replacement strip.

## s05-X102a

Will 45 plus 4 need a new ten?

**Answer.** No. There are 9 ones; 45 + 4 = 49.

The initial Yes or No remains visible; the picture supports the prediction with nine loose ones.

Shown: Marks No and indicates a group of nine loose ones.. Not yet seen: Marks Yes because four old strips count as four more loose ones (D).

- Yes, 13 ones (D). Reads four old ten-strips as four more singles.

## s05-X102b

Will 45 plus 5 need a new ten?

**Answer.** Yes. There are exactly 10 ones to trade; 45 + 5 = 50.

The initial mark stays visible. Look for exactly ten ones matched to one new strip. A correct prediction with a flawed representation gets descriptive feedback.

Shown: Marks Yes and circles all ten ones as one group.. Not yet seen: Marks No and removes ten singles without replacing them, showing 40 (A).

- No, 40 (A). Crosses out ten ones without preserving them in one strip.
- Yes, 60 (B). The prediction is correct; only the corroborating work shows duplicated quantity.

## s05-X103

How did you put 72 and 9 together?

**Answer.** 81. Eleven ones become one new ten and one loose one. Also 72 + 8 = 80, then 80 + 1 = 81.

Look for a true method with the changed ten and a single remaining one.

Shown: Joins two old and nine new singles, then links ten of them to an eighth ten-strip.. Not yet seen: Draws seven strips and one single after discarding the traded group (A).

- 71 (A). Discards the traded ten.
- 91 (B). Keeps both the traded ten and its ten loose ones.
