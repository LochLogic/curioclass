# Making a new ten

Put loose ones into two ten-frames. Trade exactly ten singles for one marked ten-stick. Let children see all ten before they move them. A drawing can circle the group and trace it to a strip. The strip replaces those same pieces. In a written method, the added ones can split between the ones that fill the ten and the ones that stay loose. Review make ten with pieces before moving to the larger amounts.

Withdraw the shared frames after day 100. Keep a personal drawing and a strip to show what happened. Have pairs try and check the three cases with different starts, then bring the familiar roles back for the rounds. Grouped or ungrouped, the amount stays the same.

For A, restore the missing strip from the circled singles. For B, move that same group once. For D, lay ten singles beside a marked strip. Keep the two same-start prediction panels covered until their exit.

Supply paper, pencils, adult-precut marked strips and large paper squares if there are no connecting cubes. A folded sheet can stand in for a mat. Handwrite cards or draw the groups when there is no printer. Keep loose pieces on trays and out of mouths; an adult counts them back in. Read all stems aloud. Use enlarged or raised cells and accept child-directed marks. Offer a private turn to a child who passes. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen.
