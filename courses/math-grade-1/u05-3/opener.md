# Two ways to add two-digit numbers

Make both addends visible with separate sticks and singles. In a like-unit method, collect tens together and ones together. Either order keeps every piece. In adding on, keep a whole starting group while adding the other group's tens and ones in parts. Compare how the same parts contribute in each order. Mark the new ten once. At 100, show ten tens and zero loose ones. Take down shared worked drawings for Two methods and leave personal marks available.

Have partners alternate explaining and checking a move. Ask what stays true when the order changes. One partner can point to the pieces while answering an objection. For A or B, match the circled group to one new strip. For C, handle whole strips. For E, read the amounts on both sides of each equals sign and write separate true steps.

Supply paper, pencils, adult-precut marked strips and large paper squares if there are no connecting cubes. A folded sheet can stand in for a mat. Handwrite cards or draw the groups when there is no printer. Keep loose pieces on trays and out of mouths; an adult counts them back in. Read all stems aloud. Use enlarged or raised cells and accept child-directed marks. Offer a private turn to a child who passes. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen.
