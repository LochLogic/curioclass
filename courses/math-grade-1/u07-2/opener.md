# Draw from features and reuse a whole design

Review side, corner, straight, open, closed, square and rectangle. Trace all the joins of a triangle aloud. A triangle has a complete boundary of three straight sides. A rectangle has four straight sides and four square corners. A square has those same features, plus equal sides. A turn can change how a figure looks while all the corners stay the same. For Triangle detective, test one supplied edge or join. A folded paper corner fits a square corner.

For Reusable motif, trace the first whole and move its tracing as a single piece. Add a spare piece at a point of contact. In a sketch, show the outside and enough of the original joins to let the child recognize the first whole. Set aside shared worked builds before opening private records. Use the started outline, paper strips, corner guide and motif cards for practice. The R1 strip kit and R2 solid and flat stock are prepared and stored separately. Adult precuts paper, checks removable tabs and assembles clean boxes or folded solids where needed. Offer plain folded paper as a corner guide.

Repair error A by testing the same geometric feature after a change in appearance. Close a gap for error B. Test a bowed edge with a straight strip for error C. For error J check all four corners. For error D move a tracing intact. On days 135 and 136, one personal explanation in each assigned minute serves both records. Keep scores and feedback private until both groups have completed the same tasks. Day 135 flat practice stays descriptive; every child makes the fresh flat record on day 136. Return R1/R2 on 137 for a dated revision.
