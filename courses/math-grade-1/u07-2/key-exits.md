# Section 7.2 exit key

Keep this key with the teacher. Use shown, shown with help, and not yet seen. Leave an unheard response open. Record language separately when a picture already shows the mathematics.

**s07-X132a.** Draw a closed shape with four straight sides. Any simple closed quadrilateral. Allow intended straight joins and minor motor imprecision. Describe a deliberate gap without a letter. Error A: Draws a large circle because size makes it fit.

**s07-X132b.** Mark the sides. One mark on each of the four sides. Check that each edge has a single visible mark. Record number or location slips descriptively.

**s07-X132c.** Dot the corners. A dot at each of four corners. Check the joins of the child’s outline. An adult may transfer the child’s exact indicated locations.

**s07-X133a.** Draw a skinny triangle. A narrow closed outline with three straight sides. Accept any intentional skinny triangle. Error B: Leaves a corner open and accepts the figure as a triangle.

**s07-X133b.** Draw a turned triangle. A closed three-straight-sided triangle turned from the first. Accept any perceptibly different orientation. Error C: Draws a bowed side and treats it as straight.

**s07-X133c.** Mark what both triangles need. Marks the closed joins and the three straight sides of both drawings. Read the paired paper marks or record an exact pointing response on that paper.

**s07-X133d.** Show something that can change. Indicates a different turn, size or color. Accept a small sketch or a one-word label. Both remain triangles. Error A: Crosses out the turned drawing because it cannot be a triangle.

**s07-X134a.** Mark every rectangle. Marks the first four drawings and omits the slanted fifth. Each chosen drawing has four straight sides and four square corners. Error A: Leaves out the recolored rectangle because of its color. Error J: Marks the slanted figure because it has four sides.

**s07-X134b.** Why is the turned one still a rectangle? Four straight sides meet at four square corners. Accept marking the four square corners or indicating how a paper corner fits them. Error A: Says it stays a rectangle because it is still the same color.

**s07-X135a.** Join these two pieces into a circle. The matching half-circles touch along their full straight edges. The outer edge makes one complete circle. Use a paper mark showing the seam of the child’s build.

**s07-X135b.** Join your whole circle to this rectangle. An intact circle touches the rectangle in the larger design. Both halves remain joined. A tracing or paper tabs keep the full circle boundary visible. Error D: Splits the half-circles across the rectangle.

**s07-X135c.** Trace what makes this whole a circle. Traces the entire round outer boundary of the embedded circle. Ignore the straight internal seam. Accept neutral support to trace where the child indicates. Error A: Uses the circle’s color as the deciding feature.

**s07-X136a.** Add one piece to this flat whole. Adds one touching piece to the band-and-panel square. Its two unequal rectangles and off-center seam remain unchanged. Error D: Separates the two rectangles to place the new piece between them.

**s07-X136b.** Add one piece to this solid whole. Adds one touching solid to the original two joined cubes. The cubes still share their original face. Error D: Separates the cubes to add the third solid.

**s07-X136c.** Mark a feature that makes it a square. Marks four equal straight sides or four square corners on the original square. A necessary feature is sufficient for this item. Do not use the added piece’s outline. Error A: Marks the fill and gives color as the deciding feature.
