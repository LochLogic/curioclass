# Choose a pair, reach ten, or count up

## The mathematics

A pair making ten helps with many sums, and a known double can be a shorter path on some cases. Moving a piece within the whole preserves the total. In subtraction, partition what leaves before crossing it out. Counting up from the removed amount to the start measures the same gap as subtracting.

## In class

Use individual frames from day 58. On day 63 use separate surfaces to compare the two supplied methods. Hold the smaller count; the first mark is the next number. A child can point to the stopping amount. Pair practice uses paper marks and no timer.

## First repairs and supplies

For C, account for moved and remaining parts. For D, trace each term. For G, split the removed amount and cross each part once. For A, start marking at the next number. Use drawn frames, folded scraps, and outlined shapes.

Read the prompts aloud and offer large dark pictures or a neutral scribe. Hear one rotating group of six in Synthesis or Close. Six 45-second turns leave 30 seconds to switch. Children may pass and respond privately later. Use shown, shown with help, and not yet seen. Adult-cut large pieces stay on trays and are collected after practice. Offer individual drawings whenever the printer or a shared device is unavailable. Open each reserved check only in its named part.
