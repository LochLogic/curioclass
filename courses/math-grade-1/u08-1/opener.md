# Section 8.1 opener: use a known fact

Use dated response notes from days 149 and 150 to choose a familiar fact deck. If the notes are missing, use the mixed two-group deck. Send home the unit family note on day 151. The first familiar fact marks bring out the unit question. Keep all three fluency days untimed.

Review Kindergarten's Partner turns and paired frames. One child handles or counts, the other watches, then they switch. Keep ten visible ones in a full frame. Review equals as the same amount on both sides. A number sentence uses numbers and signs. A fact is a true relationship. The sum is the total. A double has equal groups. A partner of ten is the part that makes ten with the part you have.

To count on, hold the known start and give each added piece the next count. To subtract by using addition, rebuild the starting quantity from the removed and remaining groups. Switching the addends leaves the same total. Regrouping changes the order of putting pieces together while preserving every piece. Related addition checks subtraction; switching the subtraction quantities changes the question.

For error A, cover the fixed start and touch only the new pieces when counting. For error B, move pieces into ten and then point to what remains. Keep the original with a private retry. Use the named ten-minute repair inside Practice. Keep frame-supported strategy work apart from the retained mental-fluency record.

Print the fact decks, double ten-frame, partner game recording sheet, half slips, family note and private return slips. Keep keys with the teacher. Read prompts aloud, accept pointed or tactile marks, and record only the speech you hear. With scrap paper, draw frames and use large paper squares instead of counters. An adult cuts any cards before class. Every child marks paper.
