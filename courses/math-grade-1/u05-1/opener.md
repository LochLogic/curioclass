# Adding ones or tens without an exchange

Use separate groups of tens and ones. A ten-stick represents ten equal singles. Review ten ones and further ones with every single visible. Open and restore a marked strip before using the grouped drawing. Use **equation** for a number sentence with an equals sign. Read each side as an amount and point to the same quantity. A paper chart connects moving one cell to adding one and moving down one row to adding ten. Build the chart move with pieces first.

In Like-unit pairs, sort tens with tens and ones with ones. Record separate true equalities next to the pieces. On the movement cards, one child tries while the other checks. Retry one move and switch in all fifteen pairs. Remove the shared towers on the fluency day and keep personal sketches.

For C, put whole strips on the mat beside the unchanged singles. For D, match each cell of a marked strip to a single. For E, restore separate true equations. Rebuild bundles with the readiness group on day 97. Keep exit tickets folded until their time.

Supply paper, pencils, adult-precut marked strips and large paper squares if there are no connecting cubes. A folded sheet can stand in for a mat. Handwrite cards or draw the groups when there is no printer. Keep loose pieces on trays and out of mouths; an adult counts them back in. Read all stems aloud. Use enlarged or raised cells and accept child-directed marks. Offer a private turn to a child who passes. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen.
