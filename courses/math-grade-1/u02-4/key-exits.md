# Daily exit key

Teacher copy. Read each exact stem from its learner piece. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen. Record a letter only when the picture or the child's words show that reasoning.

## E33a

Which label fits Lee's star picture?

**Answer.** Take away; the seven departing stars are the clue.

Lee takes seven of twenty paper stars away. Read the independent label bank. Accept marks by the group that leaves. Describe unsupported choices.

Shape: Classify an independent action with a reusable label bank.

## E33b

Which label fits this mat of stars?

**Answer.** Parts; the seven marked red stars form a known part.

The mat holds twenty stars, seven red and the rest blue. No change of count occurs; the pattern key distinguishes parts.

Shape: Classify a fixed whole using the same bank independently.

## E33c

Which label fits the pins and tags?

**Answer.** Comparing. Mark the 1 unmatched pin.

Nineteen pins and eighteen tags are side by side. Read all three labels. Use the student-page Exit box.

Shape: Classify a static comparison in the fresh mixed set.

- **E.** Rings all 19 pins. Uses the full larger group as the clue.

## E34

Which part of the picture does the box name?

**Answer.** The arriving group, 16 if counted.

Inez puts more paper lanterns on a ledge with four to make twenty. In 4 + □ = 20, mark the arrivals panel. Identifying the group is enough.

Shape: Locate the numeral box's role on a finished equation and new picture.

- **C.** Marks the final group of 20. Uses all the lanterns as the change.

## E35

What addition finds the birds that left?

**Answer.** 10 + 6 = 16 or equivalent. Accept 10 + □ = 16 when 6 departures are marked.

Rae moves some of sixteen paper birds off a mat. Ten stay. The printed situation is 16 - □ = 10. Link the missing group to the child-generated solution relation.

Shape: Generate a solution equation from the removal situation equation.

- **C.** 10 departures. Copies the end as the change.

- **H.** 7 departures. Includes ten as a new step.

## E36

What story fits this equation?

**Answer.** One actor starts with 11, adds 9 and ends with 20.

Read □ + 9 = 20. Accept a drawing like Bo placing nine more cushions by eleven. This supported encounter and its retry are unscored; describe the paper evidence and help.

Shape: Invent an entire story from a bare equation with a missing start.

- **D.** 29 in the starting group. Adds the known total and the added part.
