# Section 8.3 exit key

Teacher copy. Read the cues aloud. Use shown, shown with help, and not yet seen where the response is judged. Keep unheard speech open. Attribute an error only when the child's marks or words show it.

## Day 158

**s08-X158.** How would you fix the loose squares to show where this new stick came from? Cross out ten loose squares in picture B and link them to its new stick. Eight tens and nine ones still make 89. A circle, crossing marks or a drawn link can identify the ten loose ones that became the new stick. A correct numeral by itself leaves the reason open. Error F: 719. Joins the seven and nineteen as adjacent digits.

## Day 159

**s08-X159.** How would you fix the picture to match both clues? Six ten-sticks and zero loose squares. One route trades two rows for sticks and takes the three extra loose squares off the mat. Accept any marked revision with exactly six sticks and no singles. The task changes a model to meet clues. Save the independent mark before the reason cue. Error G: Six sticks and three loose squares. Checks the stick count alone.

**s08-X159b.** How does your picture fit both clues? Marks all six sticks and indicates no squares remain loose. The picture shows 60. Check the two clues separately. Accept counted stick marks and a point to or mark in the empty loose area. Keep any neutral dictation beside the marks. Error G: Checks six sticks only. Treats one marked clue as sufficient.

## Day 160

**s08-X160.** Make a two-clue riddle for this picture. For example: nine tens and five ones. Accept any two clues that together identify only 95 among two-digit numbers. Accept drawings, marks or neutral dictation. Give private descriptive feedback during the handoff. Keep the first response for feedback only. Error G: Nine tens and four ones. Checks the tens alone.
