# Section 8.3 opener: make every clue fit

Review Kindergarten's Partner turns, ten ones, further ones and zero. Ten loose ones can become one ten-stick with the same quantity. A digit records the number of tens or the remaining loose ones in the usual grouping. A picture can show another grouping of the same quantity. Zero ones means that no loose singles remain. A clue is a hint the number must fit. Check each clue separately.

For error E, unfold a stick into ten equal squares and fold it back. For error F, connect the circled ten loose ones to the new stick before reading the two digits. For error G, mark the result of checking each clue. Give a fresh private retry. Use descriptive feedback if a wrong numeral alone leaves the reasoning unclear.

Use a chart for practice and then cover it. Keep product pages and exits sealed until their turns. R1 and R2 each start with an independent picture mark. Allow up to six 45-second reason turns in each product block while the others draw or dictate. Give written feedback after class and keep the original beside the next day's revision. Day 160 closes the record from that retained work while the class works on familiar paper puzzles. Use the last ten minutes for paired repairs, a fresh unscored response and the Grade 2 handoff.

Print the chart, exchange practice sheet, clue cards, product and revision sheets, private numeral cards, quiet puzzles, response cards and half slips. Large ten-square paper strips or pencil marks substitute for connecting cubes. An adult cuts paper before class. A paper fold covers a practice answer. Read all cues aloud and offer tactile marks, pointing or a neutral scribe. Keep the record and keys with the teacher.
