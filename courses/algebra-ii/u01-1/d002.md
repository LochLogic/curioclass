# Day 2: Folding a strip

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut one folding strip for each pair from a full sheet of paper, at least 25 cm by 4 cm, and print the folding table. Return day 1's exit ticket and start-of-year check with the error letters marked. Open the answer key and the record sheet.

### The day

#### 10 min. Warm-up and repair

Five minutes of warm-up for everyone, alone: [[s01-P2-1]] and [[s01-P2-2]].

**Ask.** In q(3) = 11, which number went in and which came out? Expect: 3 went in and 11 came out. Reading q(3) as q times 3 is error G; trace the 3 through a labeled input and output table. If someone gets v(0) = 0, show 2^2, 2^1, 2^0, each half the one before.

Then five minutes of repair from day 1: teach any item more than a third of the class missed to everyone, with one board example for each error; otherwise work with a small group while the rest begin folding. The retries are on Side 2: item 6 for day 1's exit, items 7 and 8 for check items 2 and 3; the warm-up retries check item 1. Each student who missed one does it alone in the first six minutes of the pairs part, while a partner folds.

For error C on day 1's exit, count three changes from chain 1 to chain 4 on the board, once. For error D, write "each chain has 4 more than the one before" and ask which chain it means. For error B in the check, set a doubling table beside an adding table and compare differences and ratios.

#### 12 min. Worked example: folding a strip

Fold a strip in half again and again, opening it after each fold to count the sections; each was one layer. Students try [[s01-K2-1]] before you model it.

**Ask.** After 0 folds, how many layers are there? Expect: 1, the strip as it is. This is why 2^0 is 1.

[[steps:s01-K2-1]]

Build the table of n and L(n) on the board. Point to L(3) = 8 and say the position is the input, the number of folds, and the term is the output.

**Ask.** What changes by the same factor each time? Expect: the layers double, so each term is 2 times the term before it.

Then change the start: 4 squares at stage 0, doubling, gives 4, 8, 16, 32, so the rule is 4(2)^n. Write the frame "term = start x factor^position" and point to the 4 and the 2.

#### 15 min. Pairs: tables and notation

Pairs fold their own strip four times and fill the folding table: folds n and layers L(n). The fifth row is filled by doubling, because a strip cannot be folded that far. Then they write L(n) = ____ and say aloud, in L(3), which number is the position and which is the term. Layers should read 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32.

Then each pair takes the pattern card on the folding table and does [[s01-P2-6]].

**Ask.** In P(4) = 48, what is the position? Expect: 4, the stage. Counting three steps to reach stage 4 is error C; count steps from stage 0 on a line.

Answers of 24 or 11 on the pattern card show error C or B; send them back to the table. An answer of 12 multiplies the start by the stage; ask for the table.

#### 8 min. Talk

Students sort four statements by position and term: [[s01-P2-7]].

**Ask.** In w(0) = 9, which number is the term, and what does the 0 mean here? Expect: 9 is the term and 0 is the position, the starting input.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X2]], alone, on paper. Collect. After class, record it against the key and mark the error letters on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

A projector is not needed. Without a printer, draw the folding table on the board and have pairs copy it.

### At home

**Swap.** No scrap paper: fold a page of any kind. No printer: copy the table onto paper.

**Skip.** The retry, the pair work and the sort.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Do [[s01-P2-1]] and [[s01-P2-2]] alone.
- **10 min. Fold.** Fold a strip of paper in half, again and again, up to four folds, and count the layers.
  **Say.** "How many layers after each fold?" Expect: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 for a fifth fold.
- **8 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K2-1]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "In L(3) = 8, which number is the position?" Expect: 3 is the position, 8 is the term.
- **7 min. Pattern.** Do [[s01-P2-6]] together.
- **5 min. Sort.** [[s01-P2-7]], parts (a) and (b).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X2]], alone. Keep it for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip part (d) of the sort. Longer: ask how many folds give more than 1,000 layers.

### Words and supports

Position: the input, the number of folds or the stage. Term: the output, the number that comes out. Layer: one thickness of paper. Frames: "term = start x factor^position." "In L(3), the position is __ and the term is __." "The term at position __ is __." "Each term is __ times the term before." Accept a drawing of the folds beside the table.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who adds when the pattern multiplies (error B: 27), who counts one step too few (error C: 300), who drops the start (125) and who multiplies the start by the position (36). The last two have no letter. Day 3 opens by putting an adding table beside a multiplying table. Day 3 then compares a fixed increase with a fixed ratio in tables and dot graphs.
