# Day 3: Adding or multiplying

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut the sort cards, six to an envelope for each pair, and set out graph paper and rulers. Return day 2's exit ticket with the error letters marked. Print the student page and the paper exit ticket. Open the answer key and the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Alone: [[s01-P3-7]]. It is practice only.

**Ask.** Is q(3) + 1 the same as q(4)? Expect: no. Adding 1 after the output is a different change from adding 1 to the input.

If many made error B on day 2's exit (27, from adding 5 three times to 12), the two tables of the next part are the repair: set an adding table beside a multiplying table and ask for both the differences and the ratios of each. For error C (300), count the steps from stage 0 on a line. For 36 or 125, write the table 12, 60, 300, 1500 and point to the start and the factor.

#### 10 min. Worked example: two tables

Put both tables of the next item side by side on the board. Students try before you model:

[[s01-K3-1]]

**Ask.** What stays the same in table X, and what stays the same in table Y? Expect: X goes up by the same amount each step; Y is multiplied by the same factor each step.

[[steps:s01-K3-1]]

Say that you may compare only over equal steps in the input: here each step is 1. Point out that table Y is the rule v(n) = 6(2)^n from the warm-up on day 2.

#### 10 min. Plot both by hand

Students plot (n, term) for X and Y on graph paper: [[s01-P3-8]]. Dots only, no line drawn. The plots stay in the folder. The tool is a coordinate graph, light, for plotting; graph paper and a ruler do the same job and give the same evidence. Ten minutes, for showing why: the graph of X stays in a straight line and the graph of Y bends upward.

**Ask.** Why are the graphs dots and not lines? Expect: the inputs are the whole numbers 0, 1, 2, 3; nothing is defined between them.

Confer: the dots of X are on a straight line; Y's are not. Watch for a line drawn through the dots; ask what the line would claim at n = 1.5.

#### 15 min. Pairs: sort six cards

Pairs sort cards A to F, which are four tables and two dot graphs (cards C and F). The items are [[s01-P3-1]], [[s01-P3-2]], [[s01-P3-3]], [[s01-P3-4]], [[s01-P3-5]] and [[s01-P3-6]]. For each card they decide whether the pattern adds or multiplies, prove it with a calculation, and write the term at n = 4. They read dots as (n, term).

Watch for a card where the last change is used on a multiplying pattern (error B). Cards B, C and E multiply; A, D and F add. A fraction ratio on cards C and E is not a mistake.

#### 5 min. Talk

**Ask.** How do you know it is a difference and not a ratio? Expect: equal differences between neighbors mean a difference; equal ratios mean a ratio. A student who says "it goes up" has not decided.

Students do [[s01-F3-1]] alone and compare with a neighbor. The student there used the last change on a multiplying pattern.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X3]], alone, on paper. Collect. After class, record it against the key and mark error letters.

### Other rooms

No device is needed. Without a printer, copy the six cards onto the board. Without graph paper, rule a grid on plain paper. With one projector, show the tables only after pairs have written their answers on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the tables from the page onto paper. No graph paper: rule a grid on plain paper.

**Skip.** The pair sort of six cards, beyond cards A, B and C.

- **4 min. Warm-up.** Do [[s01-P3-7]] alone.
- **8 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K3-1]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "Is the same amount added, or the same factor used?" Expect: X adds 4; Y multiplies by 2.
- **8 min. Plot.** Plot [[s01-P3-8]] on grid paper, dots only.
- **8 min. Cards.** Do cards A, B and C from the sort cards sheet.
  **Say.** "How do you know it adds and does not multiply?" Expect: the differences between neighbors are equal.
- **4 min. Fix.** Do [[s01-F3-1]] together.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X3]], alone. Keep it for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: sort only cards B, C, D and F. Longer: ask for a table that adds and a different table that multiplies, both starting at 5.

### Words and supports

Difference: how much a term changes from one position to the next, found by subtracting. Ratio: how many times as large a term is as the one before, found by dividing. Frames: "Each term is __ more than the term before." "Each term is __ times the term before." Accept a calculation shown without a sentence.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who subtracts the last change of 20 from table P, which multiplies by 1/3 (error B: P(3) = -10). If many made error B, day 4's warm-up puts a table with equal differences beside one with equal ratios. Day 4 then builds tables from a start and a rule and checks plotted points.
