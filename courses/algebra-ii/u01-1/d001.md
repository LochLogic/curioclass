# Day 1: A smaller puzzle helps with the next one

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put three disks and a larger fourth in an envelope for each pair, and one disk puzzle sheet for each pair. Print the student page and the paper exit ticket. Open the answer key and the record sheet. Send the family note home today.

### The day

#### 8 min. Hook

Pairs stack the disks in space 1 of the disk puzzle sheet, largest at the bottom. State the rules: move one disk at a time, and never put a larger disk on a smaller one. The goal is the whole stack in space 3. Pairs try 1 disk, then 2, then 3, and write the fewest moves they find in the table on the sheet.

Expect 1, 3 and 7. Tell no story about the puzzle, and do not hint at how to build the 3-disk answer yet.

#### 12 min. Worked example: the smaller puzzle helps

Put three disks in space 1 on the board. Students try [[s01-K1-1]] first, then you model it.

**Ask.** Before the biggest disk can move, where must the other two be? Expect: both in space 2, because space 3 must be empty and the big disk cannot sit on a smaller one.

[[steps:s01-K1-1]]

Build the table of disks and moves on the board: 1, 3, 7. Students copy it on Side 1. Say the rule in words: each count is twice the one before it, plus one.

**Ask.** Where does the plus one come from? Expect: the single move of the biggest disk. If a pair answers "times 2" only, ask them to replay the 3 disks and point at the move they have not counted.

Say that each term comes from the term before it, so a first term must be known. Name no formula for the count; that comes later.

#### 10 min. Pairs: four disks

Pairs add the fourth disk. They predict the moves for 4 disks from the table before touching a disk: [[s01-P1-1]]. They write the prediction in the "4, predicted" row and name the earlier row they used. Then they move the four disks and write the count in "4, checked by moving".

Watch for 14, from doubling 7 and leaving out the biggest disk; send those pairs back to replay the three steps with 3 disks as one block. Expect 15. Pairs finish with [[s01-F1-1]], alone, in one minute, then compare with a neighbor. It repairs the same 14, for 5 disks.

#### 5 min. Talk

**Ask.** What did you need before you could find the next one? Expect: the one before it, and a first one to start from. Write one sentence from the class on the board, such as "To find a count I need the count before it and where it starts."

#### 10 min. Start-of-year check

Hand out Side 2. Students do [[s01-R1]], [[s01-R2]] and [[s01-R3]] alone and silent. They look back at Algebra I; this is practice and no score. Collect the page. After class, mark each item right, partly or not yet on the record sheet, with error G on item 1 and error B or C on items 2 and 3. Day 2 repairs these.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X1]], alone, on paper. Collect. After class, record the three look-fors on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

No device is needed. Without a printer, draw the three spaces and the table on the board and have pairs copy them. Torn scrap paper makes the disks. With one projector, show the table only after pairs have predicted on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No paper disks: tear a page into three sizes, or stack coins of three sizes. No printer: copy the table onto scrap paper.

**Skip.** Pair work, the class talk and the neighbor check.

- **8 min. Try it.** Use three disks and three spaces. Move 1, then 2, then 3 disks.
  **Say.** "Move one disk at a time; a big one never sits on a small one. How few moves can you do?" Expect: 1, 3 and 7.
- **8 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K1-1]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "Where must the two small disks wait?" Expect: in the other space, so the big disk can move.
- **7 min. Four disks.** Predict [[s01-P1-1]], then move the disks to check.
- **5 min. Talk.** Fix [[s01-F1-1]] together.
  **Say.** "What did you need before the next one?" Expect: the one before it, and a first one.
- **7 min. Check.** Do [[s01-R1]], [[s01-R2]] and [[s01-R3]] alone.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]], alone. Keep it for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the neighbor comparison of the fix-it. Longer: ask how 6 disks could be predicted without moving a disk, and what that needs.

### Words and supports

Term: one number in a list that follows a rule. Move count: the fewest moves for a given number of disks. Frame: "To move 3 disks I first move __ disks, then the __ disk, then __ disks again." Say "the count before it" and let students point at the table. A student may demonstrate with the disks and dictate the sentence.

### Tomorrow

Return [[s01-X1]] in day 2's repair. Error C is 44, from counting four changes from chain 1 instead of three. Error D is a rule with no first chain. If many wrote either, day 2 shows both on the board with chains of another size. Day 2 then folds a strip and tracks repeated multiplication, using the rule that each term comes from the one before.
