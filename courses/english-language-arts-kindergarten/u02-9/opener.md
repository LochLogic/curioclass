# Section 2.9 opener: drafting a weather story and learning how it sounds

## What the section does

Days 70 to 73 turn each child's plan into a draft. Day 70 drafts the character and setting; day 71 the three events in order, with plural nouns said aloud; day 72 the ending and the reaction, and a partner question; day 73 the peer question and one added detail. Alongside, the teacher hears each child in a short oral battery: plurals, question words and where-words (G1), the meaning of a word from a card (M1, M2), a spoken sentence expanded (C1), and the sounds of the cycle (P1). Two fifteens split each task across days, so no one is observed twice.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block ends with a five-minute exit, a card read twice or a spoken ask. The oral battery runs in the conference minutes: two minutes for one child at a time, fifteen children a day. Day 73 uses its five spare conference minutes for the exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A draft is a first try.** It has a who and where, three events, and a reaction. It is not final.
- **More than one takes a mark.** bell, bells; dress, dresses: the ending s or es says more than one.
- **A question word asks for one kind of information.** Who, what, where, when, why, how.
- **A word part carries a meaning.** re- again, un- the opposite, -ed done, -ing happening now.
- **A partner question helps a draft grow.** The writer adds a detail that answers it.

## The errors, in plain words

- **G, H:** an object named as a character, or the wrong setting.
- **AH, X, Y:** events out of order, or a plural said without its s or es.
- **AG, AW:** a reaction left out, or a question that does not learn a missing detail.
- **AI:** a draft left unchanged after a good question.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child. Weather stories keep safe endings.

## Supplies

The arc chart and the writing-process poster from section 2.8, each child's draft and plan cards, real classroom objects, counters, the exit cards, the record sheets and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Use real objects from the classroom, one and then more than one. The cards are read aloud, so no printing is needed.
