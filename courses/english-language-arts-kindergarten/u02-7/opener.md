# Section 2.7 opener: one long story, heard in five parts

## What the section does

Days 59 to 63 read one long story, Yusuf and the Parcel, in five parts, one part a day. Each day the class adds to one wall map with movable cards: who, where, and what happens. Day 60 adds where words (in, by, on) and a sentence grown with where. Day 61 joins the events in order with first, next, last. Day 62 reads feelings from what a character does and says. Day 63 is a check: each child answers three spoken asks about the whole story, with the map turned to the wall. Day 64 is the response: a targeted reteach, then each child retells The Picnic Plan.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. On days 59 to 62 the topic block ends with a five-minute exit, a card read twice, answered by pointing, sorting or speaking. Day 63 has no exit; its three asks are the check. Day 64 has no new exit; its retell is the record.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The story is one story.** The map grows across the days; the character, the place now and the change are all on one wall.
- **A character acts.** The parcel is not a character; the person who carries it is.
- **A place word tells where.** In, by and on say where something is.
- **A retell is in order.** First, next, last, with every important event.
- **A feeling is shown, not only named.** A face that falls, a lean on an arm.

## The errors, in plain words

- **G, H, I:** an object named as a character, the wrong place, a minor thing named instead of the event. Match the person who acts to the who box; reread the place sentence; match an action sentence to its picture.
- **J, AV:** events out of order, or one event told alone. Order three cards while rereading first, next, last.
- **A:** an answer the card does not give. Reread the exact sentence and match its detail.
- **Z, AA, AC, AB:** where answered with when, a place word reversed, a feeling word given only its other meaning, a question that is not about the word.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child. The story keeps weather safe: nothing outdoors is acted, no disaster images, and the ending is warm.

## Supplies

The wall map and its picture cards, paper maps, the feeling chart, the parcel, bag and bakery pictures, chart paper, crayons, the exit cards, the S1 roster sheet and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the map on a sheet of scrap paper and put drawn cards on a table. Use a closed window or a printed scene for the weather.
