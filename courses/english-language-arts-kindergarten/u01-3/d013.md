# Day 13: Toy Riddles, and walk, march and tiptoe

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the student page lines aloud; children do not read them. Cover the answer pages of Toy Riddles (pages 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13) with sticky notes. Clear a floor space and a seat-side space for each child. Read the cycle 1 notes from day 12 and choose the one slip most children made (error N, O, P or AA, or a print hard to read). Today's six are the thirteenth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. If many circled on and on (error AE), or pointed to the smooth paper for rough (error V), in day 12's exit, spend 3 minutes on the one more children made. For error AE, act open, then close with the open and closed book pair, and ask which one reverses it. For error V, feel the sandpaper and the smooth paper beside the word rough. Write each word large on a word card as you teach it with your body: riddle (a question with clues), clue (a hint), walk, march, tiptoe (hands at the sides, face to the class).

#### 10 min. Toy Riddles

Whole class on the rug. Review: poem: a text made to say together (Toy Clues, which you said on day 12). Say: "A riddle gives clues. The clues help us guess the toy." Read the riddle on page 2 aloud (about 20 seconds), with the answer page covered, and show the picture.

**Ask.** Which clues tell what the toy is? Expect: fuzzy, soft, brown, two round ears, waits for a hug. Reread the clues; children say the toy. Uncover page 3: "I am a teddy bear!" Then read the riddle on page 6, covered.

**Ask.** Which clues tell what the toy is? Expect: bumpy, snaps, small parts, click together. Uncover page 7: building bricks. Children point back to the clue words as you reread each line, and say which clue helped most.

#### 8 min. Model walk, march and tiptoe

Whole class standing beside the seat. Say: "I walk to the cart." Walk in place with ordinary steps. Say: "I march with firm steps." March in place, knees high, steady and even. Say: "I tiptoe quietly past the bear." Tiptoe in place, light and quiet. The class acts each in place beside the seat and says the word. Then ask them what is different: firm and even against light and quiet. The puppet marches when it should tiptoe past a sleeping toy (error W, marching and tiptoeing done the same way); the class says which word fits, and shows it. A child who cannot stand shows firm and light with the hands on the desk.

#### 12 min. Act and guess

Pairs. Review: turn and talk: ask, answer, swap, say one thing back; a child may pass. One child acts walk, march or tiptoe beside the seat; the partner says which one it is; then swap. The partner says "You are marching" or "You are tiptoeing." Each child acts 3 times (about 5 minutes in all), then pairs choose one and show it to another pair (3 minutes). Hear three pairs show a movement, about a minute each. Keep paths clear and stop for crowding. A child may pass and name the movements the partner acts.

#### 10 min. Riddle talk

Whole class, then pairs. Read the riddle on page 10 aloud, covered: "What is hard and wooden and stacks up high? Build a tall tower and reach for the sky."

**Ask.** Which toy fits, and which clue tells you? Each pair tells each other one answer: the toy and the clue that tells it, such as "blocks" and "hard", "wooden" or "stacks up high" (3 minutes). Hear three pairs, a minute each; each pair points to the clue words as you reread. Uncover page 11. Then the day's six each ask one question about a clue, such as "What does fuzzy mean?", and you answer with a feel or an action (3 minutes).

#### 11 min. Draw

Alone. Each child draws a puppet that marches or tiptoes on the student page and tells a partner which one it is and how it moves (3 minutes). For the first 8 minutes the day's six come to the table in turn, a minute each, to say how their puppet moves while you write a label they ask for.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 13 card (the band and the cat). Read aloud once: "The toy band marches into the hall. The toy cat tiptoes past the dog." Children circle the picture that shows how the cat moves ([[s01-X13]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each, in a clear space: "Show me how the band moves." Expect firm, even marching steps, in place or with fingers. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: the cat marching or the band tiptoeing (error W, the two actions done the same way).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Chant for a and t: "a, a, /a/, apple." "t, t, /t/, top." (Review: letter a: name "ay", sound /a/, keyword apple. Review: letter t: name "tee", sound /t/, keyword top.)

#### 10 min. Foundations response

Whole class. Review: repair then fresh retry: wrong example beside a right one, then a new try. The puppet shows the one slip most children made on day 12's check, and you show the right way (4 minutes). This replaces the usual prompt.

- Error N: the puppet says "ay" for /a/. Say the name, then the sound with the letter and the keyword apple.
- Error O: the puppet writes t when it hears /a/. Hear the sound, choose the letter card first, then write.
- Error P: the puppet prints only A when lowercase is asked. Model the lowercase path (curve round, close it, straight down); each child writes it once in the air.
- Error AA: the puppet calls the letter a "t". Hold the two cards side by side and name each.

Then a fresh retry on a new strip (6 minutes), not scored: say /a/, each child holds up the a card and prints lowercase a in box 1; say /t/, holds up the t card and prints lowercase t in box 2; say /a/ and prints capital A in box 3. Look for: starts at the top, the curve closes, the cross goes left to right, says the sound.

#### 4 min. Mapped words

Whole class. Review: mapped words I and my, read as whole words. Write "I march with my toy." on chart paper and say it, finger under each word, left to right. Children point to I and to my. The other words are read to them; nobody sounds out march or toy.

#### 3 min. Closing

Whole class. Chant for a and t.

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): the retry of the one slip they made on day 12, then say whether "mat" rhymes with "cat" and clap the beats of a toy name (puzzle, teddy, dinosaur: 2, 2, 3). Table notes for the day's six go on the record sheet. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger a and t in the sand tray; draw and label; name-card match; big-book browse.

### Other rooms

One child: read two riddles, with the answers covered, and act walk, march and tiptoe together. Then do the exit card and the retry. One projector: show the riddle pages and the answers on one screen; children point to the clue words and act beside the seat.

### At home

**Swap.** No large book: say the riddles aloud. No clear floor: act with fingers walking on a table. No strip: print on a scrap.

**Skip.** The paired acting, the table retry, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. A riddle.** Read or say the teddy bear riddle, with the answer hidden.
  **Say.** "Which clues tell what the toy is?" Expect: fuzzy, soft, brown, two ears.
- **5 min. Three ways.** Act walk, march and tiptoe in place. Say the word each time.
  **Say.** "How is marching different from tiptoeing?" Expect: marching is firm, tiptoeing is light and quiet.
- **5 min. Guess.** You act one; your child says which.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws a puppet marching or tiptoeing.
- **5 min. The letters.** Say /a/ then /t/; your child picks the letter and prints it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X13]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles the cat, then shows you how the band moves.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the second riddle and the share of the pairs; keep the model, one round of act and guess, and the exit. Longer: a child who is secure acts a fourth movement, such as hop, and says how it is different from the others.

### Words and supports

Words: riddle, clue, walk, march, tiptoe. Frames: "The clue is ___." "I am ___." "You are marching." A home language, signs and acting count as answers; say the English back once. A child who cannot move shows firm and light with the hands.

### Tomorrow

If many circled the cat marching or showed the band tiptoeing (error W) in day 13's exit, day 14's first 4 minutes show two contrasting motions and the child says which is which. Day 14 reads A Toy Can Turn, adds a new detail to the topic chart and meets the letters h and p.
