# Day 9: The information book Push, Pull, Roll, and a topic box

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the student page lines aloud; children do not read them. Put Push, Pull, Roll together with a front cover, title page, seven pages and back cover, closed. Hang the topic chart, a large empty topic box. Set the ball, the cart or block and the toy on a string on the rug. Cut three picture cards per pair. Today's six are the ninth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. If many circled Tal holding the drum away (error B) or said "I cannot do it" (error E) in day 8's retry, the puppet shows that one again and you show the right way (2 minutes). Otherwise, if many missed the book parts in day 2's exit, repair error I ("The illustrator writes the words") by matching the author and illustrator labels to the credit lines, or error AC (an inside page named as the back cover) by closing the book and finding its outside back. Then gesture the three words of the book with your hands, the class copying: push (hands away), pull (hands toward you), roll (hands turning over each other).

#### 4 min. The information book

Whole class. Hold up the closed book. Review: front cover, back cover, title page: the parts of a book (you met them in the story). Point to each and the class points with you. Review: author makes the words and illustrator makes the pictures. Open the title page: "Words by the Curio Class writing team. Pictures by the Curio Class art team." Hold the pencil label beside the first line and the brush label beside the second. Say: "This is an information book. It tells facts about something. The story about the two friends was a different kind of book." Say: "A poem, a story and an information book are three kinds of text. We say a poem together. We hear a story. We learn facts from an information book."

#### 8 min. First hearing, whole

Whole class on the rug. Read Push, Pull, Roll (205 words, seven pages) once, holding up each picture for a moment. Say each push, pull and roll with the toys as it comes: push the cart, pull the toy on the string, roll the ball. Do not stop to ask questions; the book is heard whole first.

#### 6 min. Talk

Partners, then whole class. Review: turn and talk: ask, answer, swap, say one thing back; a child may pass.

**Ask.** What is the whole book about? Expect: how toys move, or "toys move". Partners tell each other (2 minutes). Hear three pairs, about a minute each (3 minutes). A child who says "a cart" or "a ball" gives one detail, not the whole topic (error K): say "A cart is one thing in the book. What is the whole book about?"

#### 12 min. Model the topic box

Whole class at the chart. Say: "A topic is what the whole book is about. A detail is one fact under it." Write "Toys move" in the large topic box, finger under each word, and the class says it with you. Say: "If I wrote only 'push' here, it would be one page. It would not be the whole book." Then draw two detail boxes under the topic, joined by lines.

Reread page 2 aloud and point to the picture. Say: "A push moves a toy away from you." Write "push" in the first box with a small drawing of the cart going away, and the class says the sentence with you. Reread page 3. Say: "A pull brings a toy toward you." Write "pull" in the second box with a drawing of the toy on its string coming toward you, and the class says the sentence with you.

**Ask.** Which box tells what the whole book is about, the top box or a lower box? Expect: the top box. If a child points to a lower box, point to the words "Toys move" and say "this one covers every page". Say: "A detail alone is not the topic."

If someone gives a detail from home ("my bike goes fast"), say "That is about a toy at home. We use what the book tells" (error A), and reread the sentence.

#### 12 min. Pairs choose details

Pairs at the desk. Each pair has the small topic box and the three picture cards (push, pull, roll from pages 2 to 4). Say: "Pick two cards. Put them under the topic. Say the sentence each one shows." Pairs work for 6 minutes. Reread page 4 aloud once to the class ("Some toys roll. A ball rolls. A wheel rolls.") for any pair that needs it. Then hear three pairs, a minute each (3 minutes): the pair points to the card, then says "A ___ moves a toy ___." The class says "I heard ___" after each. Spend the last 3 minutes with the chart: put the roll card on it beside push and pull. A pair that puts a card above the topic box (error K) hears the sentence again; ask where a detail goes.

#### 9 min. Draw one detail

Alone, then pairs. Each child draws the topic box at the top of the student page, writes nothing, and draws one detail below it, such as a push, a pull or a roll (6 minutes). The class copies "Toys move" from the chart if they want a label; you write it for any child who asks. Then each child tells a partner which fact the drawing shows (3 minutes). A child may point to the chart card instead.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 9 card (stacking toys). Read aloud once: "This page tells about toys that stack. Rings stack on a pole. Cones stack in a pile." Children circle the picture that tells what the whole page is about ([[s01-X9]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each: "Tell me one detail the page gives." Expect "Rings stack on a pole" or "Cones stack in a pile". Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: one ring alone circled, or "Toys" given as a detail (error K, a detail taken for the whole topic or the reverse); an idea the page never said (error A).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Sing the ABC song along the alphabet strip, the pointer under each letter left to right. Chant "t, t, /t/, top." and "a, a, /a/, apple." (Review: letter t: name "tee", sound /t/, keyword top. Review: letter a: name "ay", sound /a/, keyword apple.) Point to the word I on the class message and the class says it (Review: mapped words I and my, read as whole words).

#### 8 min. Keyword and printing a

Whole class. Hold up the a card: apple, and the sound /a/ with the mouth wide as if biting an apple (Review: letter a: skywrite and print lowercase a and capital A, the shapes from day 7). Say: "Lowercase first. Start near the top on the right, curve back round and close the circle, then go straight down." Skywrite it, then each child prints lowercase a on handwriting paper, then capital A (slant down left, slant down right, a line across), saying /a/ each time. A child who prints only A when lowercase is asked (error P) writes the lowercase path once more from memory. Look for: the curve closes, the line goes down, says the sound and not the name.

#### 6 min. Sounds both ways

Whole class. Show how a letter and its sound go in both directions. First, say a sound and find the letter: say /t/ and the class points to the t card; say /a/ and they point to a. Then see the letter and say the sound: hold up the a card and the class says /a/; the t card, /t/. Add the entry to the sound-spelling map: point to the card, say the keyword, say the sound. If a child says "ay" for the sound (error N), say the name, then say the sound with the letter and the keyword. The keyword picture reminds; it never decides a word.

#### 3 min. Closing

Whole class. Chant "a, a, /a/, apple."

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): say the name and the sound of a and of t, find each on the map, say whether "hat" rhymes with "mat" and whether "top" does, and clap the beats of a toy name (doll, puppet, dinosaur: 1, 2, 3). Table notes for the day's six only go on the record sheet. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger a in the sand tray, say /a/ each time; draw and label; name-card match; big-book browse.

### Other rooms

One child: read Push, Pull, Roll, ask the same question, draw the topic box and two details together, and do the exit card. Print a and say each sound. One projector: show the covers, the pictures and the chart on one screen; children place cards on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No large book: tell the pages aloud. No chart: draw a box on a sheet of paper. No sand tray: write a with a finger on a table.

**Skip.** The reopen, the pairs, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. The book.** Read or tell Push, Pull, Roll, showing each picture.
  **Say.** "What is the whole book about?" Expect: how toys move.
- **5 min. Topic and details.** Write the topic in a box with two detail boxes below: a push and a pull.
  **Say.** "What is one fact the book tells?" Expect: a push moves a toy away, or a pull brings a toy toward you.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws the topic box and one detail below it.
- **5 min. Tell it.** Your child says which fact the drawing shows.
- **5 min. The letter a.** Say /a/ like biting an apple. Your child prints a and A.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X9]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles a picture, then tells you one fact the page gives.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the second pair work and the share; keep the model, the one drawing and the exit. Longer: a child who is secure adds a second detail box to the drawing.

### Words and supports

Words: information book, topic, detail, push, pull, roll. Frames: "This book is about ___." "A ___ moves a toy ___." "I heard ___." A home language, signs and pointing count as answers; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

If many circled one ring alone or gave "Toys" as a detail (error K) in day 9's exit, day 10's first 4 minutes put two details under the topic picture. Day 10 rereads the first four pages of Attributes of Toys for color and size words.
