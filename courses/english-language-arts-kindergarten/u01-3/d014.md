# Day 14: A Toy Can Turn, a new detail, and the letters h and p

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the student page lines aloud; children do not read them. Have the day 9 chart ready (topic "Toys move", with push, pull and roll). Set the top, the cart and the toy car on the rug. Cut three picture cards per pair. Hang the h and p cards, face down, beside the map. Today's six are the fourteenth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. If many circled the cat marching or showed the band tiptoeing (error W) in day 13's exit, the puppet marches when it should tiptoe, you show two contrasting motions, and a child says which is which (3 minutes). Write each word large on a word card as you teach four words with things: turn (turn the cart around), spin (spin the top), circle (draw one in the air), label (Review: label: a word written beside a part of a drawing, as on day 7).

#### 8 min. First hearing, whole

Whole class on the rug. Read A Toy Can Turn (159 words, eight pages) once, holding up each picture. On page 6, point to each label as you read it: wheel, handle, box. Say: "A label tells a name." Do not stop for questions; the book is heard whole first.

#### 8 min. Compare

Whole class at the chart. Review: topic and detail, from the day 9 chart: topic "Toys move"; details push, pull and roll. Point to each as you say it.

**Ask.** Which detail is new beside Push, Pull, Roll? Expect: a top spins, or a toy turns. Say: "This book is about toys that turn. Turning is one more way a toy moves, so it goes under 'Toys move'." Add a new detail box and write "turn", with a drawing of a top and curved lines. Say the sentence with the class: "A top spins in one place." Say: "A detail alone is not the topic. The topic is what the whole page covers." (Review: A detail is one fact under the topic.)

#### 12 min. Reread pages 2 to 5

Whole class, then pairs. Read pages 2 to 5 aloud, pointing under the words, holding up each picture (about 2 minutes). On page 4, point to the curved arrows around each wheel and then to the one straight arrow.

**Ask.** How does its picture explain the movement sentence? Expect: the curved arrows show the wheel turning round and round, and the straight arrow shows the cart moving forward. Reread page 4: "A rolling wheel spins and moves forward at the same time." Pairs talk for 2 minutes. Then on page 3 ask which picture shows the top leaning over, and children point. Hear three pairs, a minute each; each points to the arrow or the picture and says "This shows ___."

#### 12 min. Pairs add a detail

Pairs at the desk. Each pair has the small topic box and three detail cards: a top, a wheel and a steered car. Say: "Choose one card that tells how a toy turns. Put it under the topic. Say its sentence." Each pair works for 6 minutes. A pair that puts a card above the topic (error K) hears the sentence again, and you ask where a detail goes. Reread page 5 for any pair with the steered car: "Steer a toy car to the right, and it turns to the right." Hear three pairs, a minute each (3 minutes); the class says "I heard ___." The last 3 minutes, each pair walks to the chart and pastes its card under the topic. Say the picture and the words back to a pair that confuses a picture with its action (error B, a picture of a different action from the words).

#### 11 min. Draw

Alone. Each child draws the topic box on the student page and adds a new detail below it (7 minutes): a top, a wheel or a steered car. The new detail must be a fact from today's book, not a copy of push, pull or roll. The day's six come to the table, 60 seconds each, to say one sentence that you write under the drawing, and they say it back with you. The others tell a partner what the drawing shows (4 minutes). Judge the drawings from the six with three look-fors: (1) the picture goes under the named topic; (2) the detail is new beside day 9's; (3) the sentence matches the picture. Shown: a top picture with "A top spins in one place". Shown with help: the detail placed after a prompt. Not yet seen: the detail named as the whole topic (error K).

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 14 card (turning toys). Read aloud once: "This page is about toys that turn. A paper spinner turns when you blow on it. A toy merry-go-round turns when you push it." Children circle the picture that shows what the whole page is about ([[s01-X14]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each, with two pictures, a child blowing a spinner and a hand pushing a merry-go-round: "Point to the picture that shows turns when you blow on it." Expect the blowing picture. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: the spinner alone circled (error K, one detail taken for the whole topic); the merry-go-round picture pointed to (error B, a picture of a different action from the words).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Sing the ABC song along the alphabet strip, the pointer under each letter left to right.

#### 10 min. Letters h and p

Whole class. Hold up the h card and say its name "aitch" and the sound /h/ at the start of hat, a breath on the back of the hand; the class copies. Then the p card: the name "pee" and the sound /p/ at the start of parrot, a puff of air, the lips popping. The keyword pictures are hat and parrot; they remind, they never decide a word. Hang each card on the sound-spelling map beside t and a, saying keyword, sound, letter. Chant "h, h, /h/, hat; p, p, /p/, parrot." Hold up the h and p cards side by side and name each, because the two shapes look alike. A child who gives the name for the sound (error N) hears the sound with the letter and the keyword.

#### 4 min. Chant

Whole class. Letter-sound chant for h, p, a and t: say the name, the sound and the keyword for each, pointing along the map from left to right.

#### 3 min. Closing

Whole class. Write "Look at me." on chart paper and point under each word, left to right. The class says it. Point to me and the class says it: me is a word we remember whole, and we do not sound it out. The other words are read to them.

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): say the name and the sound of h and of p, point to each on the map, and say the keyword. Table notes for the day's six only go on the record sheet. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger h and p in the sand tray, saying the sound each time; draw and label; name-card match; big-book browse.

### Other rooms

One child: read A Toy Can Turn, ask the same question, put two details under a topic box, and do the exit card. Show h and p with a breath and a puff. One projector: show the book, the chart and the map on one screen; children place cards on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No large book: tell pages 2 to 5 aloud. No chart: draw a topic box on paper. No sand tray: write with a finger on a table.

**Skip.** The first hearing, the pairs, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. The book.** Read or tell pages 2 to 5, showing each picture.
  **Say.** "How does the picture show the wheel turning?" Expect: the curved arrows go round, the straight arrow goes forward.
- **5 min. The chart.** Draw the topic "Toys move" with boxes for push and pull. Add a top spins.
  **Say.** "What is new in this book?" Expect: a top spins, or a toy turns.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws the topic box and a new detail below it.
- **5 min. Tell it.** Say one sentence about the drawing; write it under.
- **5 min. h and p.** Say /h/ with a breath and /p/ with a puff. Your child says each sound.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X14]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles one picture, then points to the picture that shows blowing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the compare part to the one question and the pasting; keep the reread, one new detail drawing and the exit. Longer: a child who is secure adds a second new detail under the topic and says its sentence.

### Words and supports

Words: turn, spin, circle, label, wheel, handle. Frames: "A ___ turns." "This shows ___." "I heard ___." A home language, signs and pointing count as answers; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

If many circled the spinner alone (error K) or pointed to the merry-go-round picture (error B) in day 14's exit, day 15's first 4 minutes repeat the more common of those: topic above, details below, or the picture matched to the reread sentence. Day 15 begins the next section with Push, Pull, Roll again and an opinion about a classroom toy.
