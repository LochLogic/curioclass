# Day 10: Attributes of Toys: color words and size words

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the student page lines aloud; children do not read them. Set a bead and a large ball on the rug. Print the poem Ask and Listen large with a green dot at each line start. Make the size cards: four toys (ball, teddy, block, cart), each as a tiny drawing and as a gigantic one that fills the card. Today's six are the tenth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. If many circled one ring alone or gave "Toys" as a detail (error K) in day 9's exit, show the day 9 chart: the puppet puts one detail in the top box, you move it down and put two details beneath the topic "Toys move" (3 minutes). Write each word large on a word card and put it on the pocket chart as you teach four words with real things: color (hold up crayons), size (hold up the bead, then the ball), tiny (the bead), gigantic (the ball, arms wide).

#### 12 min. Reread Attributes of Toys, pages 1 to 4

Whole class. Say: "This is a new book. It tells how toys look." Read pages 1 to 4 aloud once without stopping (about 1 minute). Then go page by page, holding up the picture: reread the page, point under the words, and ask the two questions below, hearing two or three answers each. The words are read to children; they point back to the picture and the words you point under.

**Ask.** What color is it? Expect on page 1 red, blue, yellow or green bricks; on page 2 a light yellow ball, a dark blue cart, a bright striped scarf.

**Ask.** Is it tiny or gigantic? Expect on page 3 the dollhouse pieces are tiny; on page 4 the teddy bear is gigantic, as tall as the door. If a child says "big" for the bear, say "It is bigger than big. It is gigantic," and reread page 4.

#### 8 min. Model far-apart words

Whole class. Hold the bead and the large ball. Act each: tiny, with fingers pinched close and small hands; gigantic, with arms thrown wide. Say: "The bead is tiny. The ball is gigantic." The class acts it and says the sentence. Say: "Tiny and gigantic are far apart." Then show a medium object, such as a block, and the class acts "big" with arms half wide. Say: "Big is bigger than tiny, but gigantic is farther. We use gigantic for the very biggest." If a child calls "huge" or "big" the far-apart partner of tiny (error U), act tiny beside gigantic again and ask which pair is farther apart.

#### 12 min. Pairs describe

Pairs at the desk. Each pair has two size cards of one toy: a tiny card and a gigantic card. Say: "Color each card with a crayon. Then tell your partner a sentence for each." The frame is "The tiny ___ is ___." and "The gigantic ___ is ___." (for example, "The tiny ball is red."). Coloring takes 5 minutes, the pairs' sentences 4 minutes, with the turn card passed in turn. Hear three pairs, a minute each (3 minutes), the class saying "I heard ___." A child may point to the card instead of saying it.

#### 12 min. Draw

Alone. Each child draws one tiny toy and one gigantic toy from the room on the student page, colors them, and tells a partner. For the first 8 minutes the day's six come to the table in turn, a minute each, to say their two sentences while you write a label for the toy they name; the others draw and then tell a partner (4 minutes). Children do not write words today.

#### 7 min. Talk

Pairs, then whole class. Review: turn and talk: ask, answer, swap, say one thing back; a child may pass. Review: point back to the words and the picture that tell.

**Ask.** Which words helped you choose the tiny card and the gigantic card? Expect: "tiny", "gigantic", "as tall as the door". Pairs talk for 2 minutes. Reread page 3 and page 4 and hear three pairs, a minute each; each points to the picture or to the line you point under.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 10 card (two drums). Read aloud once: "The tiny drum is white. The gigantic drum is black." Children circle the two drums that fit these words ([[s01-X10]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each: "Which word tells you which drum is the big one?" Expect "gigantic". Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: the tiny black and gigantic white drums circled, or "black" given for the big one (error V, a describing word attached to the wrong object).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Chant "a, a, /a/, apple." and "t, t, /t/, top." (Review: letter a: name "ay", sound /a/, keyword apple. Review: letter t: name "tee", sound /t/, keyword top.) Point to I in the poem as it hangs and the class says it (Review: mapped words I and my, read as whole words).

#### 10 min. Poem letter search

Whole class. Review: poem: a text made to say together (Ask and Listen, which you said on days 7 and 8). Read the poem in large print with the pointer under each line from the green dot; the class joins "We listen well." Say: "Now we look for letters." Call children to the poem one at a time to find an a: they point, the class says the name "ay" and the sound /a/. Do the same for t. Then find capital T in "Then" and capital A in the title. Each child is called at most once, about 6 children in all, and everyone says the name and sound together. Say: "We find a letter by its shape, not by the picture." A child who says "t" for the letter a (error AA) holds the a and t cards side by side and names each.

#### 4 min. Printing

Whole class. Skywrite a and t, then each child prints a and t once on handwriting paper, saying the sound. Look for: the curve of a closes, the t starts at the top and crosses left to right.

#### 3 min. Closing

Whole class. Chant for a and t.

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): find an a and a t in a line of the poem printed on a strip, say the sound of each, and say whether "wait" rhymes with "late". Table notes for the day's six only go on the record sheet. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger a and t in the sand tray; draw and label; name-card match; big-book browse.

### Other rooms

One child: read pages 1 to 4, ask the two questions, act tiny and gigantic with two objects, and do the exit card. Find a and t in the poem together. One projector: show the pictures and the poem on one screen; children circle on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No large book: tell pages 1 to 4 aloud. No poem: write three lines large by hand. No sand tray: write with a finger on a table.

**Skip.** The reopen, the pairs, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. The words.** Show a bead and a large ball, or a crumb and a pot. Name tiny and gigantic.
  **Say.** "Which is tiny? Which is gigantic?" Expect: the bead is tiny, the ball is gigantic.
- **5 min. Read.** Read or tell pages 3 and 4. Point under the words.
  **Say.** "Which toy is gigantic?" Expect: the teddy bear.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws one tiny toy and one gigantic toy and colors them.
- **5 min. Tell it.** Your child says a sentence for each: "The tiny ___ is ___."
- **5 min. Letters.** Find a and t in a line of print and say each sound. Your child prints both.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X10]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles two drums, then names the word for the big drum.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the second pairs work and one page of questions; keep the model, the drawing and the exit. Longer: a child who is secure draws a third toy that is neither tiny nor gigantic and says "big".

### Words and supports

Words: color, size, tiny, gigantic, dark, light, bright. Frames: "The tiny ___ is ___." "The gigantic ___ is ___." "I heard ___." A home language, signs and pointing count as answers; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

If many circled the swapped drums or said "black" for the big drum (error V) in day 10's exit, day 11's first 4 minutes put the real object beside the words of the text. Day 11 rereads pages 5 to 9 of Attributes of Toys and sorts real toys by one attribute.
