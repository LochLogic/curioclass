# Section 1.3 opener: toys that move, and words that describe them

## What the section does

Days 9 to 14 give the class its first information reading. The children hear what a whole page is about and one detail under it, reread a page of describing words (size, touch and opposites), hear toy riddles and act out walking, marching and tiptoeing, and meet a second page about toys that turn. Every day ends with a five-minute exit.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short reread, the new reading or word work, a draw or act, and the exit. The foundations block holds a chant, a letter or sound, printing, and five 8-minute rotations of six children. Day 12 is a fluency day that models a new idea briefly. Day 14 adds the letters h and p to the sound-spelling map.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A topic is what the whole text is about; a detail is one fact under it.** A detail alone is not the topic. Put the topic picture above and two details below.
- **Describing words tell how a thing looks, feels or sounds.** Size words and touch words need a real object beside them.
- **Opposites are two words that mean far apart.** A stronger word is not an opposite: "huge" is not the opposite of "tiny".
- **Verbs can differ in how they move.** Marching is firm and even; tiptoeing is light and quiet. Children act each one in place beside the seat.
- **A riddle gives clues.** Each clue narrows the toy; children say which clue helped.
- **Letters h and p arrive on day 14.** Each has a keyword picture and a mouth shape; the picture reminds, it never decides a word.

## The errors, in plain words

- **K:** a child names one detail as the topic. Put two details under the topic picture.
- **T, U, V:** the rule for a sort is lost, a stronger word is called the opposite, or a word is used for an object it does not fit. Hold the real object beside the word.
- **W, AE:** two movements are done the same way, or an action is repeated when its opposite is asked. Show both side by side.
- **I, AC, AA:** see sections 1.1 and 1.2.

A puppet voices wrong examples. Movement games keep a clear path; stop for crowding.

## Supplies

Chart paper for the topic chart, real objects for size and touch, a tray with rough and smooth things, picture-pair cards of opposites, riddle strips, the word cards, the h and p cards with their keyword pictures, the exit cards and the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Make the topic chart on the back of a poster. Gather objects from the room for size and touch. Fold paper into pair cards. Say the riddles from memory and act the movements with your hands on the table.
