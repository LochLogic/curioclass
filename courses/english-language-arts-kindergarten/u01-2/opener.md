# Section 1.2 opener: a class message and a plan for playing

## What the section does

Days 6 to 8 turn the sharing story into the class's first writing. On day 6 the children dictate a class message about how friends play together. On day 7 each child draws a plan for playing in our room and says what the drawing tells a reader. Day 8 responds to the check on day 5: you reteach the two most common slips and then try a fresh retry card that is not scored.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short reread of the sharing story, the writing and a five-minute exit (days 6 and 7). The foundations block holds a chant, the letter work for the day, and five 8-minute rotations of six children. Day 8 swaps the exit for the reteach and retry.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **You write while the children dictate.** The child supplies the ideas and you supply the letters. Say each word as you write it, finger under it, left to right.
- **A message tells a reader something.** A good class message names the friends or the toy and one thing they do. A drawing plan names the place and one action.
- **A label alone is a name, not a message.** "Ball" names the topic; "It rolls" adds a fact.
- **The sound /a/ is shown with a mouth shape and a keyword.** The apple on the card is a reminder of the sound, never a way to guess a word.
- **Rhyme and beats run every day.** Clap the beats of a toy name; find two words that end the same way.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, M, X:** a picture or sentence that has nothing to do with the friends, or that names the topic and gives no fact, or says only "I like it". Put the label beside the fact and say both.
- **B, E:** a picture of a different moment, or a statement where a question for help is needed. A puppet shows the slip; the class shows the repair.
- **AA:** the letter a is called t. Hold the two cards side by side and name each.

A puppet voices every wrong example. The friends in the message are invented, not children in the room.

## Supplies

Chart paper, crayons, thick pencils, torn paper for the plan, the letter a card with its keyword picture, the sound-spelling map, the exit cards, the retry card and the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Use torn paper for the plan and a drawn chart for the message. Use two real toys. Copy the poem large by hand and draw the apple on a scrap card.
