# Day 7: A drawn plan for playing, and the letter a

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the poem Ask and Listen large, eight lines, with a green dot at the start of each line. Set two or three real classroom toys on the rug, and a turn card in each pair's basket. Have the letter a card (apple, A and a, and the mouth picture) ready; it joins the map at 8 minutes of the foundations block. Today's six are the seventh six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Repair

Whole class. If many circled the child drinking alone (error A) or gave a name or a feeling with no fact (error M) in day 6's exit, spend these minutes on the one more children made.

- Error A: the puppet circles the child drinking alone. Reread the card sentence ("Two friends stack cups. One holds the pile steady while the other adds a cup."), point to the matching picture, and each pair says one fact the picture shows.
- Error M: the puppet says "cups". Write "cups" on the chart beside a fact: "They take turns." Say both, and the class says one fact about another toy of your choice.

If neither was common, reread the class message from the wall, finger under each word.

#### 8 min. Shared reading: Ask and Listen

Whole class. Review: poem: a text made to say together, read once, then with the class joining the repeated line. Say: "Listen for what we do when a friend talks." Read the poem (gk-u1-08 p02) with the pointer under each line, left to right, from the green dot. Read it twice more, the class joining the repeated line "We listen well." Point to the word I in "I ask, you tell." and the class says it (Review: mapped words I and my, read as whole words). The other words are read to them; children do not sound them out.

**Ask.** Which line tells us to wait? Expect: "Then it's your turn, wait and see." Reread it; children find it in the large print by its place on the page and point. If a child names a line about asking, reread that line and the line after it, and match the word wait.

#### 8 min. Model a plan

Whole class at the chart. Say: "A plan is a drawing that names one toy and one thing to do with it." Draw the block corner (a shelf, a rug, a tower of blocks, two children). Think aloud: "My toy is blocks. My action is stack." Then ask for help in the way the class learned: "Can you write blocks for me?" and write "blocks" beside the tower, saying each sound as you go (the label names the toy). Say one sentence about the plan: "We stack blocks." Write it under the drawing, finger under each word, and the class says it with you. Say: "The drawing tells a reader what we play with and what we do."

#### 10 min. Choose and plan

Pairs. Show the real toys and name the shelf's toys (for example blocks, balls, a cart, a puzzle, play dough). Each child picks one classroom toy and one thing to do with a partner; no toys from home. The frame is "I plan ___." and "One ___, one ___." Pairs tell each other, two exchanges, each holding the turn card in turn (about 4 minutes). Hear three pairs, a minute each, the class saying "I heard ___" after each (3 minutes). A child who says "I like it" (error X) gets: "Which toy? What do you do with it?"

#### 20 min. Draw the plan

Alone, with the table. Each child draws the plan on the student page: the toy and a person doing the action. For the first 9 minutes call today's six to the table one at a time (about 90 seconds each): they name the toy, you write the label they ask for, they say one sentence and you write it under the drawing, and they say it back with you. The other 24 say their sentence to a partner, who says it back; they label by writing the first letter they hear when it is t or a, and leave a line for any other. Children do not write words today.

Judge the drawings from the six with three look-fors: (1) names one toy; (2) tells one thing a person does with it; (3) the drawing and the sentence agree. Shown: a drawing of blocks and "One stacks, one hands blocks." Shown with help: the toy named after a prompt. Not yet seen: "I like it" (error X) or a label with no action (error M). The drawings go in the portfolio.

#### 5 min. Share

Whole class. Six children say their plan in one sentence (about 30 seconds each); after each one the class says "I heard ___." A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 7 card (the toy shop). Read aloud: "Three children open a toy shop. One sorts the toys on a shelf." Children draw a plan picture of one thing a child does in this shop, in the box on the card ([[s01-X7]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each: "What will a reader learn from your drawing?" Expect that they name the shop and one action, such as "One child sorts the toys." Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: a toy drawn alone, with no child or shop (error A, a picture that shows no part of the shop); "I like it" (error X, no shop named).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Chant "t, t, /t/, top." (Review: letter t: name "tee", sound /t/, keyword top.) Review: name game: the word we heard, the word we see; one word, left to right. Hold up two cards you have written large, "blocks" and "stack"; the class says each word as you point, left to right, then says both: "blocks, stack."

#### 9 min. Showing the sound a

Whole class. Say /a/ with a wide mouth and the jaw dropped, as if taking a bite of an apple, and everyone does it with you (2 minutes). Show the mouth picture on the letter a card. Say: "This letter's name is ay. Its sound is /a/, the sound at the start of apple." If a child gives "ay" for the sound (error N), say the name, then isolate the sound with the letter and the keyword. No letter story is told. The apple on the card is a reminder of the sound; it is never used to guess a word.

Hang the a card on the sound-spelling map beside t, with its keyword apple. Then hold up t and a side by side and name each (error AA, one letter called the other's name). Last, say a sound, /a/ or /t/, and the class points left for t or right for a (2 minutes). A child who points to the wrong card hears the sound again before the card is chosen (error O).

#### 5 min. Lines for a

Whole class. For lowercase a: start near the top on the right, curve back round to the left and close the circle, then go straight down. For capital A: slant down to the left, slant down to the right, then a line across. Skywrite each, then draw it on a partner's back (2 minutes), then each child prints a and A once on handwriting paper, saying /a/ (3 minutes). Look for: the curve closes, the straight line goes down, says /a/ and not "ay". A child who prints only A when lowercase is asked (error P) writes the lowercase path once more from memory.

#### 3 min. Closing

Chant "a, a, /a/, apple."

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): say the name and the sound of a, pick the a card from the pair (t and a), say whether "bat" rhymes with "hat" and whether "ball" does, and clap the beats of a toy name (doll, wagon, dinosaur: 1, 2, 3). Table notes go on the record sheet for today's six only. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger a in the sand tray, say /a/ each time; draw and label; name-card match (match name cards to the chart by pointing); big-book browse (turn pages, finger under the words).

### Other rooms

One child: read the poem twice with your finger under each line, ask the same question, draw a plan together and write the label and one sentence, then show the letter a and its sound. One projector: show the poem, the map and the exit card on one screen; children circle on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No large poem: copy it big by hand. No apple card: draw one apple. No sand tray: write a with a finger on a table.

**Skip.** The repair, the share, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. The poem.** Read Ask and Listen, finger under each line.
  **Say.** "Which line tells us to wait?" Expect: "Then it's your turn, wait and see."
- **5 min. A plan.** Draw one toy and one action together. Your child picks one toy you have to hand.
  **Say.** "What toy, and what do you do with it?" Expect: blocks, stack.
- **5 min. The sentence.** Your child says one sentence about the drawing. Write it under, finger under each word.
- **5 min. The sound a.** Open wide and say /a/ like biting an apple. Name: ay. Sound: /a/.
- **5 min. Print a and A.** Your child prints each once, saying /a/.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X7]] Read the card aloud. Your child draws one thing a child does in the shop and tells what a reader will learn.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the share and the name-card station; keep the model, the drawing with one label and sentence, the sound a, and the exit. Longer: a child who is sure adds a second action to the dictated sentence.

### Words and supports

Words: plan, toy, action, label, ask, listen. Frames: "I plan ___." "One ___, one ___." A home language, signs and pointing count as answers; say the English back once. A tactile letter a (cut from sandpaper) helps children who trace with a finger.

### Tomorrow

If many circled the toy alone on the floor (error A) in day 7's exit, day 8's first 7 minutes reteach it from the puppet (the two most common slips of days 1 to 7 are chosen from the record sheet). Day 8 is the response day: a reteach, a fresh card that is not scored, and a reread of pages 5 to 9 of the story.
