# Day 16: How a toy moves, and my favorite toy

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the student page lines aloud; children do not read them. Set the class wagon, a ball, a top, a cart and the blocks on a table where each child can reach one. Keep movement paths clear. Hang the opinion chart and strips from day 15. Today's six are the sixteenth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. If many said "The drum is loud" or "The puzzle has pieces" (error L) in day 15's exit, spend 3 minutes: the puppet says "This is a drum," then "My favorite classroom toy is the drum"; the class says which tells and which prefers, and each child adds a preference to a named toy. Then write each word large on a word card as you teach three words with things: rock (rock the wagon handle back and forth), move (slide a block along the table) and fact (a true thing you can see, such as "The ball is round").

#### 8 min. Reread selected pages

Whole class on the rug. Review: Push, Pull, Roll and A Toy Can Turn, which you heard on days 9 and 14. Read page 4 of Push, Pull, Roll aloud, pointing under the words: a ball rolls. Then read page 2 of A Toy Can Turn aloud: a top spins.

**Ask.** How does the toy move, and which sentence says so? Expect: the ball rolls ("A ball rolls"), and the top spins round and round ("The top spins round and round on its point"). Children point to the picture and then under the sentence you reread.

#### 8 min. Model information

Whole class at the chart. Review: topic and detail: a topic is what the whole text is about; a detail is one fact under it (day 9). Hold up the class wagon and push it along the floor. Draw it in a box on chart paper and write the word "wagon" beside it. Review: label: a word written beside a part of a drawing (day 7). Say: "The wagon is the topic. Here is a fact: the wagon rolls when you pull it." Write the fact under the box, finger under each word, left to right, and the class says it with you.

Say: "A name and one fact teach a reader something." Show the puppet's drawing: only the word "wagon" and no fact. The puppet says, "It is a wagon." The class says what is missing (error M, the toy is named with no fact about how it moves). Add the fact together.

#### 14 min. Draw and dictate a fact

Alone, then pairs. Each child takes one real classroom toy from the table, looks at how it moves, and draws it in the first box on the student page (6 minutes). Then each child tells a partner how it moves, and the partner says it back. Review: dictate a sentence to a partner who says it back (day 6). Walk the room and hear the toy and the fact. Take dictation from the day's six, a minute each: write the fact under the drawing, finger under each word, left to right; the child says it back with you. A child who draws only a happy face and no toy (error X) gets the toy back in hand: "How does it move?"

#### 14 min. Draft the opinion

Alone, then pairs. Say: "Now you say what you like best." Each child draws a classroom toy in the second box and holds a sentence strip: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." Children say the sentence to a partner with the turn card and a finger under each word (8 minutes). Write the toy name on the strip for any child who asks; take the day's six first, a minute each, and write what they say. Keep the page: it is the piece you revise on day 17.

Children say the sentence aloud to you or a partner, and the teacher hears it later, in day 17's groups. Where a child said only a toy name (error L), the puppet says the preference sentence beside it and the child adds it.

#### 7 min. Share

Whole class. Six children each show a drawing and say the sentence, with a finger under each word as you model once. The class says "I heard ___" after each. A child may pass or show it to you at the table.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 16 card (the rocking horse). Read aloud once: "The class rocking horse rocks back and forth. It stops by the door." Children circle the drawing that teaches a reader about this toy ([[s01-X16]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each: "What will you tell a reader about the horse?" Expect "It rocks" or "It rocks back and forth." Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: the happy face with no motion circled (error X, a drawing that does not teach about the toy); "It is a horse" (error M, the toy is named with no fact).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Letter chant for h, p, a and t: "h, h, /h/, hat; p, p, /p/, parrot; a, a, /a/, apple; t, t, /t/, top." (Review: letters h, p, a and t, with their keywords.)

#### 10 min. Gestures and a poem

Whole class at the large poem My Choice. Say the poem once, pointing under each word, the class joining the last two lines. Review: the mouth for /h/ is a breath on the back of the hand; for /p/ it is a puff, the lips popping (day 14). Hold the h and p cards beside the poem. Then touch a word, say it, and the class makes the gesture for its first sound: hall, have, the, top, proud. Say: "We look for the letter. We do not read the word." Children say the name and the sound; a child who says "aitch" for the sound (error N) hears the name, then the sound with the letter and the keyword hat.

#### 4 min. Rhymes

Whole class. Say: "Words that rhyme end the same." Say ball and hall from the poem, stretching the ending: "baaaall, haaaall." The class says them. Then ask for another word that rhymes, such as wall, tall or call. The puppet says "ball" and "bell" and says they rhyme because they start alike (error Q, rhyming by the first sound). The class stretches the endings and says whether they match: "ball" ends like "hall"; "bell" does not.

#### 3 min. Closing

Whole class. Chant "h, p, /h/, /p/."

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): make the gesture for /h/ and for /p/, say the name and the sound for each letter, and say a word that rhymes with ball. Table notes for the day's six go on the record sheet. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger h and p in the sand tray; draw and label; name-card match; big-book browse.

### Other rooms

One child: draw and say how one real toy moves, then the opinion sentence, with you as the partner. Do the exit card, make the gestures for /h/ and /p/, and say ball and hall. One projector: show the two pages and the poem on one screen; children point and say the sentence.

### At home

**Swap.** No class toys: use any one of your child's own safe toys from a shelf. No chart: use paper. No sand tray: write with a finger on a table.

**Skip.** The pairs, the sharing, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. How it moves.** Roll a ball or spin a top.
  **Say.** "How does the toy move?" Expect: it rolls, or it spins.
- **5 min. Draw a fact.** Your child draws the toy and tells you a fact: "It rolls."
- **5 min. My favorite.** Your child draws a toy they like best.
  **Say.** "Which toy do you like best?" Expect: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___."
- **5 min. Rhyme.** Say ball and hall. Ask for one more that rhymes.
- **5 min. h and p.** Make the breath for /h/ and the puff for /p/. Your child does each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X16]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles the drawing that teaches, then says a fact.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the share and the second half of the pair talk; keep the model, one drawing with its fact, the opinion draft and the exit. Longer: a child who is secure adds a second fact to the first drawing.

### Words and supports

Words: rock, move, fact, rhyme, toy. Frames: "The ___ rolls." "It ___." "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." A home language, signs and pointing count as answers; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

If many circled the happy face (error X) or said "It is a horse" (error M) in day 16's exit, day 17 does not repair it: it is a check day, so note the more common on the record sheet and day 19 takes it up. Day 17 returns the opinion drafts for each child to revise, hears small groups talk about toy choices, and reads a riddle.
