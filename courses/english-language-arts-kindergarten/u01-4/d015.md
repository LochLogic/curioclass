# Day 15: My favorite classroom toy

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the student page lines aloud; children do not read them. Hang the opinion chart and strips (three boxes: ball, jump rope, blocks) with a green dot at the left edge of the empty strip under it. Put a toy cart, the puppet and the toy picture cards on the rug; use classroom toys only. Hang the h and p cards on the map. Today's six are the fifteenth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 4 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. If many circled the top alone (error K) or pointed to the spinning top (error B) in day 14's exit, use 3 minutes on the more common: for error K, put two different details under the day 9 topic box; for error B, lay the picture beside the reread sentence. Then write each word large on a word card as you teach three words with things: favorite (hold up the toy you like best), choose (pick one of two toys), prefer (like one more than the other). Say: "Because is a word you may add. You do not have to."

#### 8 min. Reread Push, Pull, Roll, page 7

Whole class on the rug. Review: Push, Pull, Roll, which you heard on day 9. Show the page 7 picture and read the page aloud, pointing under the words: the shelf of classroom toys.

**Ask.** Which ones do you push? Which ones roll? Expect: children point to the cart, the ball or the wagon and say what it does. Each child may point or pass. Say: "Look at the toys on our shelf. Today we say which toy we like best."

#### 10 min. Model the opinion

Whole class at the chart. Review: statement: a statement tells (day 1). The puppet holds the cart and says, "This is a cart." Say: "That tells what the toy is."

Then the puppet says, "My favorite classroom toy is the cart." Say: "That tells what the puppet likes best. It is an opinion: it says what you prefer."

**Ask.** Which sentence tells what the toy is, and which tells what the puppet prefers? Expect: "This is a cart" tells what it is; "My favorite classroom toy is the cart" tells what it prefers. The class says each sentence back (error L, a toy named with no preference).

Draw a box on chart paper. Draw a cart in it. Write the opinion under the box, the finger starting at the green dot and moving under each word, left to right, the class saying each word as you touch it (error H, a finger sliding right to left). Optional: draw a small arrow from the cart to a small wheel and say, "The puppet may say why: it has wheels." A reason is welcome. It is never needed.

#### 10 min. Compose together

Whole class at the opinion chart. Point to the ball, the jump rope and the blocks and ask the class to choose one for all of us. Children show a hand for one toy at a time; each child votes once, and a child may pass. Count aloud and say: "Every toy stays on the chart. A child may like a different toy."

Write the winner on the strip under the chart: "Our favorite classroom toy is the ___." Finger under each word from the green dot, left to right, the class saying it with you. Then one child points under each word while the class says it again. A child who prefers another toy says so aloud: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." The teacher answers, "Thank you for saying what you prefer."

#### 14 min. Pairs say it

Pairs on the rug. Review: turn and talk: ask, answer, swap, say one thing back; a child may pass (day 1). Each child takes one toy picture card, and one child in each pair holds the turn card. The child with the turn card says, "My favorite classroom toy is the ___," then asks, "What is your favorite?" The partner answers with the same sentence, and they swap. That is two exchanges. Each pair works for 6 minutes.

Hear three pairs, a minute each (3 minutes), by walking the circle. The class says "I heard ___." A pair that says only "A ball" and "A drum" (error L) hears the puppet say the two sentences, and each child adds "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." The last 5 minutes, pairs swap cards and say it again to a new partner. A child who passes may point to a card.

#### 9 min. Draw

Alone. On the student page each child draws the toy chosen in the opinion box and holds a sentence strip beside it. Walk the room: write the toy name on the strip for any child who asks, finger under each word, left to right. A child who cannot draw may point to a picture card. A child who wants to may add an arrow and draw a reason. Keep the drawings for day 16.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 15 card (the shelf). Read aloud once: "The class shelf has a drum and a puzzle. The drum is loud. The puzzle has pieces." Children circle the toy they choose for their opinion box ([[s01-X15]], part a: 2 minutes). Hear today's six, 30 seconds each: "Say your sentence about that toy." Expect "My favorite classroom toy is the ___" naming the circled toy. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Signs: "The drum is loud" or "The puzzle has pieces" (error L, the toy is named and no preference is said).

#### 3 min. Opening

Whole class. Sing the ABC song along the alphabet strip, the pointer under each letter left to right. Then the letter chant for h, p, a and t: "h, h, /h/, hat; p, p, /p/, parrot; a, a, /a/, apple; t, t, /t/, top." (Review: letter h: name aitch, sound /h/, keyword hat; letter p: name pee, sound /p/, keyword parrot; letter a; letter t.)

#### 10 min. Poem letter search

Whole class at the large poem Our Play. Review: poem: a text made to say together (Our Play, day 1). Say the poem once with the class, pointing under each word. Then hold up the h card and the p card side by side and say each name (error AA, one letter called by the other's name). Say: "The name is aitch. The sound is /h/ as in hat." Then: "The name is pee. The sound is /p/ as in parrot." A child who gives the name for the sound (error N) hears the name, then the sound with the letter and the keyword.

Say: "We look for the letter. We do not read the word." Cover the pictures with a sheet. Children come up, one at a time, and touch a lowercase h in line 2 (There's), line 6 (Share, the) and line 7 (then); they say its name and its sound. Then they touch a lowercase p in line 1 (play), line 3 (push, pull) and line 8 (play). Nobody says "it says hat" because of the picture (error S, a word named from its picture); lift the sheet only afterward. The poem has no capital H or P, so show them only on the cards: "This is the capital H, and this is the capital P." Eight children each take one turn; the rest point from the rug.

#### 4 min. Printing

Alone at the desk, on handwriting paper. Skywrite lowercase h (the tall line down, back up the line, a hump over and down), then lowercase p (the line down below the line, back up, a round bump). Each child prints one h and one p, saying the sound as they print (L.K.1a). Look for: starts at the top, the line is straight, the hump or bump closes. A child who prints only capital H when lowercase is asked (error P) sees the lowercase path again: show it once more and each child prints it once.

#### 3 min. Closing

Whole class. Write "Come and play with me." on chart paper and point under each word, left to right. The class says it. Point to me: the class says it. (Review: mapped word me, which we remember whole and do not sound out, day 14.) The other words are read to them.

#### 40 min. Rotations

Review: foundations rotations: table for the day's six, four stations, five 8-minute turns. Five groups of six. At the table each child gets a minute (6 minutes, 2 for the move): say the name and the sound of h and of p, find each in the poem line on the table, and say the keyword. Table notes for the day's six go on the record sheet. Stations (six each, no teacher): finger h and p in the sand tray, saying the sound each time; draw and label; name-card match; big-book browse.

### Other rooms

One child: use the puppet or a soft toy for the two sentences, let the child choose between two real toys, and say the sentence with you. Do the exit card and show h and p with a breath and a puff. One projector: show the chart and the poem on one screen; children point and say the sentence.

### At home

**Swap.** No puppet: use a soft toy. No chart: draw three boxes on paper. No sand tray: write with a finger on a table.

**Skip.** The vote, the pairs, the rotations and the stations.

- **5 min. Two sentences.** Hold a toy. Say "This is a ___." Then say "My favorite is the ___."
  **Say.** "Which one says what I like best?" Expect: "My favorite is the ___."
- **5 min. Your turn.** Your child chooses a toy and says the opinion.
  **Say.** "What is your favorite toy?" Expect: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___."
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws the toy in a box. Write its name under it, left to right.
- **5 min. h and p.** Say /h/ with a breath and /p/ with a puff. Your child says each sound.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints h and p once.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X15]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles one toy, then says the sentence.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the vote and the second round of pairs; keep the puppet's two sentences, one drawing, the exit and the poem letter search. Longer: a child who is secure adds a drawn reason arrow and says "because" with it.

### Words and supports

Words: favorite, choose, prefer, opinion, toy. Frame: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." "What is your favorite?" A home language, signs and pointing count as answers; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

If many said "The drum is loud" or "The puzzle has pieces" (error L) in day 15's exit, day 16's first 4 minutes use the puppet's two sentences again and each child adds a preference to a named toy. Day 16 rereads two pages, draws and dictates a fact about how a classroom toy moves, and drafts the opinion to keep.
