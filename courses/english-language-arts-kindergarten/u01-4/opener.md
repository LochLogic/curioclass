# Section 1.4 opener: choosing a toy and saying why

## What the section does

Days 15 to 19 teach the class to name a classroom toy and say a preference: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." On day 15 you model it and the class composes one together. On day 16 each child draws and dictates information about how a classroom toy moves. Days 17 and 18 are check days: you hear small groups talk about their toy choices, and after class you score the day 16 piece, revised on day 17. Day 19 responds with a model, a fresh choice and a short retry.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds a short reread, the modeling, the drawing and the exit (days 15 and 16). The foundations block holds a chant, the letters h and p, the word I, and five 8-minute rotations of six children. On the check days (17 and 18) nothing new is taught and nothing is reteached; children revise their drawings or draw while you work with small groups.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A statement tells; an opinion says what you prefer.** "This is a top" names a toy. "My favorite classroom toy is the top" gives a preference. Both are good sentences; only the second is an opinion.
- **A reason is welcome but never required.** An arrow or a drawn reason is a bonus.
- **Scoring uses two marks.** The topic (a named toy) and the preference (favorite or prefer) are scored separately. Spelling is not scored.
- **A toy teaches a reader something.** An information drawing shows how a toy moves and names it, with one fact.
- **A "no" to a toy is respected.** A child may choose any toy on the shelf or pass.
- **Feel the beats.** Tap a toy name on your hand: two beats are not two sounds.

## The errors, in plain words

- **L:** a toy is named with no preference. A puppet says the two sentences; the class tells which is which.
- **M, X:** the piece names a toy and gives no fact, or names no toy. Add one fact the picture shows.
- **K, W, Q, R:** see earlier sections; a rhyme shares its ending, not its first sound; a beat is not a sound.

A puppet voices every wrong example. Never ask a child about toys at home.

## Supplies

An opinion chart with a toy picture for each choice, sentence strips, the exit cards, the retry card, toy picture cards, riddle strips, crayons, the h and p cards, the sound-spelling map and the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the chart on scrap paper. Tear a strip of paper for each child's sentence. Use two real toys from the room and say the riddles from memory.
