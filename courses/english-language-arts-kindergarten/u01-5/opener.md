# Section 1.5 opener: different ways to play, and who makes a book

## What the section does

Days 20 to 25 return to one picture story, Mira Makes a Road, and to the information book Push, Pull, Roll. Children hear the story whole, then go back to a page to find the words and the picture that go together. By the end they can tell a story, a poem and an information book apart, find a book's front cover, back cover and title page, and say who made the words and who made the pictures. Nobody reads a text. Everything is heard, pointed to, marked, drawn or said.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block opens with a five-minute repair of the day before (days 21 to 25), then a few words first, a hearing or a return, a partner turn, a drawing and the exit. The foundations block holds oral rhyme and syllables, the letters h and p (day 20) and then n and c (days 21 to 25), printing, mapped words and five 5-minute rotations of six children.

- Days 20 to 23 teach. Day 23 hears a second story; see the note on that text below.
- Days 24 and 25 practise. Nothing new is taught; children reread, ask for help and sort book kinds.
- Day 20 and day 26 hold the cycle checks inside the rotation. The response comes in the first ten minutes of the next foundations block.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Words and pictures work together.** A picture shows one moment of the words. The right picture is the one for that moment, not just any picture of the characters.
- **An answer points back.** "Where did you hear that?" is answered with a page, a picture or a word the child says again.
- **A question and a statement are different.** A question asks for help, for facts or for what a word means. "I know it" does not.
- **A story, a poem and an information book are different kinds.** A story has characters and a turn. A poem has short lines that go together. An information book tells facts about a topic.
- **Two people make a book.** The author makes the words. The illustrator makes the pictures.
- **Fast and slow are opposites; marching and tiptoeing are different ways to walk.** Act each with fingers first; the whole class moves only in a clear space.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** the child gives an idea that is not in the words heard. Reread the words and point.
- **B:** the child marks the picture of a different moment. Lay the pictures beside the reread sentence.
- **E:** a statement is offered when a question is needed. Model one question; children echo it.
- **I, J, AC:** author and illustrator reversed; a poem called an information book; an inside page called the back cover.
- **N, O, P:** the letter name given for a sound; the wrong letter written for a sound; only the capital printed.
- **Q, R, S, AF, AG:** see earlier sections; a rhyme shares its ending; a beat is not a sound; letters, not the picture, are used to find a word.

A puppet voices every wrong example. Never ask a child about toys at home.

## One text to watch

Day 23 plans to read a second story. That text is not in the course folder yet, so no item here depends on it. Until it is written and checked, hear Mira Makes a Road again, or Push, Pull, Roll, and keep the same questions about what changed.

## Supplies

The exit cards, Mira page cards, book-kind cards, the n and c letter cards, play-picture cards, the class book credit page, thick pencils and crayons, the sound-spelling map and the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the page cards on scrap paper. Use real classroom objects for fast and slow. Tear paper into labels for the credit page.
