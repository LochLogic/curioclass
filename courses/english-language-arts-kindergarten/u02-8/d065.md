# Day 65: A weather story has a shape: plan one together

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the narrative-arc chart: three boxes in a row, the middle one holding three small boxes. Make the nine choice cards (three people, three places, three weather changes: wind, snow, hot sun) and the exit sheet. Mark Rain and Snow page 1. Read day 64's R1 marks and pick the more common of errors J and AV. Keep the exit card to yourself. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Whole class. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Lay three mixed pictures for "First Dara fills a jug. Next she carries it to the garden. Last she waters the beans." Repair the more common of day 64's R1 errors, one only. Error J: Pip puts the watering first. **Ask.** "Did the watering come first?" Expect: no; first is the filling. Error AV: Pip tells only the watering. **Ask.** "What did Pip leave out?" Expect: the filling and the carrying. Children say the three events with first, next, last. Pip says the wrong examples, never a child.

#### 5 min. Words

Whole class. Imagine: to see a picture in your mind that is not in front of you; hands over eyes, then open. Narrative: a story that someone made up, with a beginning, a middle and an end; sweep one hand left to right. Review: plan (u01-2, day 7), a drawing that says what you will make. Today the class plans a narrative.

#### 15 min. Hear 02a, The Picnic Plan, whole (314 words)

Whole class, then pairs. Review: The Picnic Plan (days 49 to 53). Show the six pictures, then read the whole story straight through (about 3 minutes). Give 12 minutes to the pictures, two minutes a page: partners take turns, each telling one thing the picture shows. **Ask.** page 1: "Who is in the story, and where are they?" Expect: Tomas and Grandma Rosa, at home and then on the way to the park. **Ask.** page 3: "What changes?" Expect: the sky turns gray and rain starts. **Ask.** page 6: "How does Tomas feel at the end?" Expect: happy; he says "This is the best picnic." You listen to six pairs; a child may pass or point.

#### 15 min. Model the narrative-arc

Whole class. Review: the story-map (day 49), who, where and what happens. Say: "A story that someone made up has a shape. We call it a narrative-arc." Draw its boxes as you talk. First box: Tomas and Grandma Rosa, on the path to the park. Middle box, three events with first, next, last: they walk to the park; rain starts and they go home; they have the picnic on the living room floor. Last box: how the character feels at the end, Tomas smiles and says "This is the best picnic." **Ask.** "What is the same as the story-map, and what is new?" Expect: the same, who, where and what happens; new, how Tomas feels at the end. Then read Rain and Snow page 1 aloud, "Rain and snow both fall from clouds," and find the gray cloud on page 3 of the story: the writer used a true weather fact for the rain.

#### 10 min. Joint plan

Whole class. First teaching, unscored. Say: "Let us imagine a different story." Hold up the three people cards, then the three place cards, then the three weather cards, one box at a time. Children show 1, 2 or 3 fingers for their pick; count and say the winner (about a minute a box; a child may pass). Fill the class arc: who, where, the weather change. Each child then draws the class plan on side 1 of the student page, one picture a box, for 5 minutes. Keep the plan safe and gentle; the wind or snow can come and go.

#### 5 min. Class weather journal

Whole class. Review: the class weather journal (day 41). Say today's weather from the window or a printed scene. Write one line and read it. Children draw it in their journal.

#### 5 min. Exit E65

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card twice with its picture (40 seconds). Each child marks one picture in each row of the exit sheet (who, where, what happens) while you ask [[s02-X65]] (about 1 minute). Then ask the day's six "Which words told you?", about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Signs: a bicycle for who (error G); the park for where (error H); the sun alone for what happens (error I).

#### 5 min. Foundations: rhyme and syllables

Whole class, oral (cycle 9, lesson 50). Rhyme: say bug, rug and mug; children give another (hug). Clap sun (one), puddle (two) and umbrella (three).

#### 10 min. Foundations: q, u and x

Whole class. Review: letters q, u and x (day 59). Hold up each card; children give its name, sound and keyword: q, quail, /kw/; u, umbrella, /u/; x, fox, /ks/. Skywrite each, then print one line of Qq, Uu and Xx on handwriting paper, saying the sound as it is written.

#### 10 min. Foundations: oral three-sound work

Whole class, with counters. Blend /j/ /u/ /g/ and children say jug. Segment cub into three. Isolate the first sound of gum, the middle sound of dig, the last sound of cup. Add /s/ before it to make sit. Never use a word that ends in /l/, /r/ or /x/.

#### 5 min. Foundations: mapped words a and my

Whole class. Review: a (day 21) and my (day 5), read whole. Write "A cat sat on my mat." and point under each word; children read it. Map the part to remember in a and in my. Then reread "My cat sat on a mat."

#### 10 min. Foundations: shared print

Whole class, then pairs. Review: reread verse 7, Rainbow, with the pointer, then the journal strip. Slide the movable windows over one word at a time; tap the spaces. Pairs take turns pointing while the partner reads it back. Find q, u and x.

#### 20 min. Foundations: rotation and cycle 9 check

Four groups of five, five minutes a group, about a minute a child. Take the twenty children the cycle 9 record shows least heard: each names q, u and x, gives each sound, and writes the letter for one sound you say. Mark the record. The others work in pairs with letter cards, handwriting paper and a rereading strip. Anyone marked shown with help gets a fresh retry in the first 10 minutes of day 66.

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Hear the story, fill the three boxes with the child, then invent a person, a place and a weather change together. One projector: show the six pictures one at a time and the arc chart; the cards and votes stay on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No chart: fold a sheet into three boxes. No cards: draw three small pictures. No story: use any short story with a weather change.

**Skip.** The votes, the pair talk and the rotation.

- **10 min. A story.** Read a short story with a weather change, twice if you can.
  **Say.** "Who is in it, where are they, and what happens?" Expect: the person, the place and the weather change.
- **10 min. Plan.** Your child imagines a person, a place and a weather change and draws each in a box on side 1 of the student page.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints q, u and x on side 2.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X65]] Read twice: "On the park path, bright sun shines on Ravi and his uncle. They sit on a bench in the shade. Ravi feels cool." Your child tells who, where and what happens, in that order.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reading, the arc and the exit; cut the journal. Longer: a secure child plans a second weather change for the same person and place.

### Words and supports

Words: imagine, narrative, plan, narrative-arc. Frames: "My story is about ___." "It happens at ___." "Then ___." Dictation, pointing and a communication aid all count. A pair may talk away from the group.

### Tomorrow

Day 66 rereads The Picnic Plan for the details that name its people and draws an imaginary weather character with clear detail. Its first 5 minutes repair the more common of today's errors G, H and I, and the first 10 minutes of its foundations answer the cycle 9 check. Today's marks go on the record sheet.
