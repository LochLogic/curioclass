# Section 2.8 opener: planning a weather story

## What the section does

Days 65 to 69 teach children to plan a weather story of their own with a narrative-arc: a character and a setting, three events in order, and a reaction. The class reads The Picnic Plan again for the arc, invents a class character, place and weather event together, and then each child draws and describes their own character, setting and events. On day 69 children add a reaction and a partner asks a question that learns one more detail. The teacher shows a shared offline text editor for five minutes on day 69; a paper copy sits beside it.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block ends with a five-minute exit, a card read twice or a spoken ask. Days 67 and 68 also hold the T1 and H1 spoken records, which are heard from fifteen children a day. Every day starts with five minutes that repair one thing.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A story plan has three parts.** A character and a setting; three events in order; a reaction.
- **A character is a person who acts.** A good detail makes the picture exact: the red boots, not just a girl.
- **A setting says where and when.** Adding where or when makes a sentence clearer.
- **Events go in order.** First, next, last, with the weather change in one of them.
- **A reaction shows a feeling.** The story ends with what the character does or says about what happened.
- **A partner question learns a missing detail.** It asks about something the writer left out; it does not ask about dinner.

## The errors, in plain words

- **G, H, I:** an object named as a character, the wrong setting, a minor object named as an event.
- **D, A:** a drawing that does not match the details given, or a detail that the card does not say.
- **AE, AF:** a fragment, or a short sentence said again with nothing added.
- **AH, AG:** events out of order, or no reaction.
- **AW:** a question that does not learn a missing detail.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child. Weather stories keep safe endings; no disaster images and no retelling of real events from a child's life.

## Supplies

Chart paper for the class arc, paper arcs for each child, the arc cards, the writing-process poster, crayons, the exit cards, the T1 and H1 record sheets and the unit record sheet; one shared device and a paper copy for the day 69 demonstration.

## A room with nothing

Draw the arc boxes on scrap paper. The editor demonstration becomes the teacher writing a dictated sentence on chart paper.
