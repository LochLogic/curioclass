# Day 69: How a character feels, and a question that helps a writer

## Teacher page

### Before class

Pick the more common of day 68's errors I and AH from its exit marks. Draw the repair card: "First Lia puts on her coat. Next she opens the door. Last she walks out." Hang the writing-process poster. Return the day 68 formation pages with written feedback. Open a text editor on one shared screen, or lay out chart paper. Until the T1 and H1 record sheet (u02-8/t1-h1-record) exists, mark on the unit record sheet. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Whole class. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the repair card twice. Repair the more common of day 68's errors, one only. Error I: Pip says the coat alone is what happens. **Ask.** "Is the coat something that happens?" Expect: no; Lia putting it on is. Error AH: Pip puts walking out first. **Ask.** "Did walking out come first?" Expect: no; first is the coat. Children say the three events with first, next, last. Pip says the wrong examples, never a child.

#### 5 min. Words

Whole class. Reaction: what a character does or says to show how they feel about what happened; put a hand on your heart and smile. Question: Review: a question asks for help or for more (u01-1, day 1). A useful question asks about something the writer left out.

#### 10 min. Hear 02a, page 6

Whole class. Read page 6 of The Picnic Plan again with its picture. **Ask.** "Which words show how Tomas feels?" Expect: he smiled; his shoulders lifted; he said, "This is the best picnic." **Ask.** "What would a partner ask to learn one more detail?" Expect: a question about something the page does not say, such as what the toy duck looks like. Children turn to a partner and say it.

#### 10 min. Model

Whole class. Look at the class arc. Fill its last box: Pia smiles and says, "I am warm now." Say why: the reader learns how she feels. Hang the writing-process poster and point to plan, ask, add. Read the class sentence "Pia waits in the market." Pip asks two questions. **Ask.** "Which question helps the writer: 'What does Pia wait for?' or 'What is for dinner?'" Expect: the first, because it asks about something left out. Error AW is the dinner question helping; Pip asks it, never a child. The class answers the first question and you add the new words beside the sentence.

#### 10 min. Add a reaction

Pairs. Each child adds a reaction to their paper arc, drawing how the character feels or what they say (about 4 minutes). Then partners take turns: the writer tells their story in one sentence, the partner asks one question, and the writer says a new detail and draws it (about 3 minutes each way). The partner says the new detail back. You take the dictation of six. First teaching, unscored. Frames: "My character feels ___." "I want to know ___."

#### 5 min. Shared editor demonstration

Whole class. Ask one child to dictate a sentence from their arc. Type it on the shared screen in large print while the class watches, saying each word as it appears, then print a copy and hang it beside the screen. Say: "A typed piece begins with the child's own words." If no device works, write it on chart paper and say so. This is a demonstration only; children do not type today.

#### 5 min. Exit E69

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card twice with its picture (40 seconds). Ask [[s02-X69a]]; children mark one of three pictures on the exit sheet (30 seconds). Then ask [[s02-X69b]] to the day's six, about 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Signs: the hill alone for the reaction, a reaction left out (error AG); a question that does not learn a missing detail (error AW).

#### 10 min. T1 oral revision

Pairs. Each child reads their T1 feedback comment from day 67 or 68 aloud with their partner (one exchange each, about 2 minutes), then tries the part again with the partner: asking a detail question, asking for help or taking a second turn. You listen to six pairs. Unscored. Repairs: a topic and its details named, a turn waited for, a request for help.

#### 5 min. Foundations: rhyme and syllables

Whole class, oral (cycle 10, lesson 54). Rhyme: say hen, ten and men; children give another (pen). Clap rain (one), sweater (two) and umbrella (three).

#### 10 min. Foundations: H1 formation revision

Children revise their day 68 page after your written feedback, printing again only the forms you marked, with no tracing. Unscored until day 75. Anyone with no marks prints a line of b, o and w.

#### 10 min. Foundations: H1 verse-prompt retry

Small groups of up to five, by the prompts missed on days 67 and 68, about 2 minutes a group; the others handwrite. Use a fresh line of verse 8 for each retry. Right-to-left tracking: model the one actual line with the pointer. A space counted in a word: frame the words. A word not matched to its letters: point under you and are in line 4. Sounds counted as beats: tap the beats of puddle. A shared first sound called a rhyme: say coat and throat, then coat and cup. Every sound split: join the vowel and the ending on one card. A word said from the picture: cover the picture and map the sounds. Unscored until day 75.

#### 5 min. Foundations: mapped words you and are

Whole class. Review: you and are, read whole (day 66). Point under "You sat on a mat." and "Are you my cat?"; children read them. Name the part to remember in each word.

#### 10 min. Foundations: oral three-sound work

Whole class, with counters. Blend /t/ /e/ /n/ and children say ten. Segment jug into three. Isolate the first sound of kid, the middle sound of pig and the last sound of hen. Change the last sound of ten to /d/ to make den. Never use a word that ends in /l/, /r/ or /x/.

#### 10 min. Foundations: shared print

Whole class, then pairs. Review: reread verse 8, Dressed for Weather, with the pointer, then the journal strip. Tap the spaces. Find you and are in line 4. Pairs take turns pointing under each word while the partner says it back.

#### 10 min. Foundations: rotation

A rotating six, 1 minute each (6 minutes): name b, o and w, give each sound, write one letter; mark the cycle 10 record. The others print b, o and w on handwriting paper.

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Hear page 6, add a reaction to the arc, ask and answer one question, and type or write one sentence together. One projector: show the arc, the poster and the editor on one screen; the drawing stays on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No poster: draw plan, ask, add as three small pictures. No device: write the sentence on paper.

**Skip.** T1, H1, the editor and the rotation.

- **10 min. How they feel.** Look at the end of a short story.
  **Say.** "How does the person feel at the end, and what tells you?" Expect: a feeling, and a smile or a word that shows it.
- **10 min. Ask.** Your child tells a one-sentence story. Ask one question that learns something left out, then let your child add the answer to the drawing on side 1.
  **Say.** "What did you leave out that I would like to know?" Expect: a new detail.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints b, o and w on side 2.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X69a]] [[s02-X69b]] Read twice: "Rain stops. Lena sees the sun and cheers." Show three pictures: Lena cheering, Lena looking at the ground, the sun alone. Your child points to how Lena reacts, then asks the writer one question to learn more.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reaction, the question and the exit; cut the editor demonstration. Longer: a secure child asks two questions and adds both answers.

### Words and supports

Words: reaction, question, plan, ask, add. Frames: "My character feels ___." "I want to know ___." Dictation, pointing and a communication aid all count. A child may pass in any talk.
