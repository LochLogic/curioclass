# Section 3.3 opener: who needs trees

## What the section does

Days 89 to 94 ask who needs trees. The class hears a chant about a tree as a living thing, then a page about who depends on a tree, and records an animal and its need in three boxes. It then hears an oak and maple profile, makes notes that answer the class question, and turns two notes into information about an animal and a tree. Foundations hold short a, then short i, letters H to T and the words is and it.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit or, on check days, the observation groups. The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the day's words and the rotation. A child outside the six heard for the exit is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read alone; the teacher reads every card, direction and text aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A topic with a detail.** A named tree and one fact about it is one example of a topic with one detail.
- **An animal needs something from a tree.** The three boxes hold the animal, what it needs and the tree part that gives it.
- **A note answers the question and names its source.** The picture, the sentence it came from and the text are kept together.
- **Two notes can become one sentence.** Join them with because or so.

## The errors, in plain words

- **X, B, D:** a minor detail chosen as the topic, a picture the words do not support, or a fact the text does not give. Reread the sentence and trace it.
- **E, AI:** a note that does not answer the question, or a fact credited to the wrong text. Restate the question; match the fact to its sentence.
- **C, A:** a topic left unnamed, or two notes left unjoined. Label first; add an arrow and a joining word.
- **Y:** no question asked when a word like it is unclear. Model the question and reread the line.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion.

## Supplies

Texts 03, 06 and 05a in large print, the three-box chart, the picture note card with a source sticker, short i sentence cards, the readers, letter tiles, counters and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw each card on scrap paper and say it is made up for practice. Cut scrap paper into cards. Use buttons for counters, a torn strip for the talking token and a leaf or a drawn tree for the tree. Write the sound-spelling map as a strip by hand.
