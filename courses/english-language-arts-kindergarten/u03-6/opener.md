# Section 3.6 opener: a second question about one tree

## What the section does

Days 102 to 106 open a second shared inquiry, this time about one tree: what it needs and who needs it. Children hear short tree readings, ask a question, find a line, make a picture note with a source label and an arrow, and share it. Day 106 ends the section with small-group notes. Foundations bring in short o and the words or and for.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk, the drawing and a five-minute exit, after which six children, a rotating group named on the record sheet, are heard saying why. The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the day's words and the rotation. A child outside the heard group is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read alone; the teacher reads every card, direction and text aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A question has to fit the card.** A card about a tree can answer some questions and not others; we reread to see which.
- **A tree and a living thing that uses it go together.** We draw an arrow and say so.
- **A page starts with the tree's name.** Then the fact.
- **A fact comes from a card.** We point to the line and name the card it came from.

## The errors, in plain words

- **E:** a question or fact the card cannot answer. Restate the question and choose the one answering line.
- **A, B, D:** facts not joined, a picture that does not show the words, or something the card never says. Reread the line and point.
- **C, AI:** the tree unnamed, or a fact given to the wrong card. Label first; match the fact to its line.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion. Animals shown are only eating or resting; nothing is hunted or hurt.

## Supplies

The exit cards, the P1 two-page print card, picture-note cards with source stickers, chart paper and a marker, notebooks, crayons, letter and sound cards, counters, the cycle 16 reader and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw each card on scrap paper and say it is made up for practice. Cut scrap paper into note cards. Use buttons for counters, a torn strip for the talking token and a leaf or a drawn tree for the tree. Write the sound-spelling map as a strip by hand.
