# Section 3.1 opener: what makes a tree alive

## What the section does

Days 80 to 84 open the unit question: what do trees need, and who needs trees? The class hears short information pages about living and nonliving things, learns that a fact and another fact can be joined by an arrow and a joining word, makes a first labeled note, and hears a story of one tree across years. Foundations hold the new sounds sh, th and ch, spoken rhyme and onset-rime work, letter names, and the words to and do.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit or, on check days, the observation groups. The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the day's words and the rotation. A child outside the six heard for the exit is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read alone; the teacher reads every card, direction and text aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A page has a topic and details.** The topic is what every sentence is about. A detail is one thing the page says.
- **Two facts can be joined.** A fact, an arrow and a joining word (because, so) show how one fact leads to the other.
- **Alive is not the same as moving.** A living thing needs things and grows. Moving alone does not tell us.
- **A note has a drawing, a label and one fact from the reading.** The teacher writes the label and the dictated line.
- **Time words show that years passed.** In the story, a season is not the same as years.

## The errors, in plain words

- **X, E:** a detail named as the topic, or a wonder that has nothing to do with trees. Cover the page and ask what every sentence is about.
- **A, B, D:** two facts side by side, a picture the words do not support, or a reason the reading does not give. Put an arrow card between the facts and point to the sentence.
- **P, Q:** moving taken as proof of life, or roots said to eat soil. Compare the toy and the tree; show that water goes up and food is made in the leaf.
- **C, Z, W:** a topic left unnamed, events out of order, or a tree thought dead when it loses its leaves. Name it first, place first and later, reread the bud page.
- **T, O, AC, AK, AD, S:** letter and sound slips in the rotation; see the foundations notes in each day.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion.

## Supplies

The unit 3 text set, the exit cards, four research-cycle wall pictures, picture sort cards, the labeled note page, a toy truck, a leaf or a drawn tree, chart paper, a talking token, crayons, glue sticks, the sound-spelling map and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw each card on scrap paper and say it is made up for practice. Cut scrap paper into cards. Use buttons for counters, a torn strip for the talking token and a leaf or a drawn tree for the tree. Write the sound-spelling map as a strip by hand.
