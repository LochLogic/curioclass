# Day 81: How two facts go together

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read day 80's exit marks and pick the repair below that most children needed. Draw the moth and bath picture cards. Write the two chart fact cards and an arrow card on paper. Make three sets of pair fact cards from the story, five pairs to a set. Cover the exit card. Read aloud to children: the student page says "I can show how two facts go together" and "Print A to G, big and small."

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

Whole class. Read the class question on the chart: "What does a tree need?" Do the one or two repairs most children needed, five minutes at most. Wrong examples are said by the puppet, never a child. Branches, from day 80's exit:

- **If many marked a detail as the topic (error X) on day 80's exit.** Cover all but the picture of a page you know and ask what every sentence is about. Place the details under the topic on the chart.
- **If many asked about something that is not a tree (error E).** Say the class question again. Hold up two question cards about a tree and let each child point to the one they would ask.
- **If someone said a moving thing is alive (error P).** Put the toy and the tree side by side. Name two things only the tree does: it grows, and it needs things. Moving does not tell us.

#### 15 min. Read What's Alive and What's Not? (02), then 01a pages 3 and 4

Whole class. Say aloud: "The child in this story is made up." Preview wonder (a head tilt with a finger on the chin), grow (a hand rising), explain (open hands) and plant (a finger rising from a fist) as they come. Read the story whole, one picture at a time (221 words, about two minutes, with pauses). Then reread A Tree and a Toy pages 3 and 4, the old photo and the tree now. Review: observer, a person who looks closely (unit 2).

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, then whole class. Talking token, one turn each, pass allowed. **Ask.** "What does the observer wonder?" Expect: whether the ants are alive. Pairs talk one minute; hear six children, 30 seconds each. **Ask.** "Why does moving not settle the question?" Expect: the truck moves and is not alive; the tree stays still and is alive. Pairs talk one minute; hear six more.

#### 5 min. Retrieval

Pairs. Review: match a picture to the sentence it shows (unit 1). Each pair gets one picture of the story and picks, from three sentences you read aloud, the one it shows. Listen to three pairs, 40 seconds each. Sign: a sentence the picture does not show (error B).

#### 10 min. Model the connection arrow

Whole class. Put two fact cards on the chart: "In the old photo the tree was thin and small." "Now the tree is taller than the slide." Read each. Draw an arrow card between them and say how they go together: "The photo shows the tree grew." Name the model: a fact, an arrow, the joining words. Say the joining words you used, "shows" and "so". Then point to the arrow and read the two facts and the joining words together; children say it with you.

#### 10 min. Partners try

Pairs. Each pair gets two fact cards chosen to join from the story, from three prepared pairs:

- "A tree takes in water and sunlight." and "The tree grows taller."
- "Ants eat and breathe." and "Ants are alive."
- "A truck moves only when pushed." and "The truck is not alive."

Each pair places an arrow between the cards, says the link to a partner, and draws the two facts and the arrow in the notebook. Listen to six pairs, about 40 seconds each. Sign: two facts side by side with no link (error A).

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Whole class, then six. Say aloud: "This card is made up for practice." Uncover E81 and read it twice with its picture. Ask [[s03-X81a]]: children hold up one, two or three fingers against their chest for the picture in that place. Mark the day's six only (shown, shown with help, not yet seen), then ask them [[s03-X81b]], 30 seconds each, while the class draws. Signs: an arrow to something the words do not say (error B); two facts side by side (error A).

#### 8 min. Say it

Whole class, oral. Blend by picture: /sh/ /ip/, /sh/ /ell/, /sh/ /op/; children say the whole word each time. Review: onset and rime, the first sound and the rest (unit 1, day 24).

#### 12 min. New sound: th

Whole class. Show th on the sound-spelling map beside sh. Two letters, one sound, with your tongue between your teeth; never /t/ then /h/. Read verse 2 of Two Letters, One Sound twice; children say /th/ with you. Show thumb, moth and bath: thumb starts with /th/; moth and bath end with it. Children say each word, stick out the tongue for the sound, trace th in the air and print it on handwriting paper.

#### 8 min. Letters A to G, printing

Individuals. From dictation, each child prints the capital and small letter for A to G on a new strip. Say each letter name and wait. Collect the strips for written feedback. Sign: a letter that cannot be read (error AK).

#### 7 min. Words to and do

Whole class. Reread the word cards to and do; say the word, tap the sounds, name the part to remember (o says /oo/). Review: the, a and in are read whole. Find to and do in a verse line.

#### 12 min. Tree Words, verse 1

Whole class. Reread chorally with the actions. Find the first line and point under each word, left to right. Clap the two-syllable word, acorn.

#### 13 min. Rotation

Table and stations. Hear six different children, two minutes each: "Which rhymes with sun: run or hat?" then "Say one more." Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Sign: picks a rhyme but cannot make another (error AD). The others sort picture cards in partners by /sh/ or /th/. One minute to write who was heard.

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Read the story, place two fact cards with an arrow and say the link, then print th. One projector: show the story pictures on one screen; the child still places and draws on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No picture cards: draw three on paper. No story: use a book about a plant or animal.

**Skip.** The pairs, the rotation and the verse.

- **10 min. Two facts.** Write "A seed gets rain." and "The seed sprouts." on two cards. Put an arrow between them.
  **Say.** "How do the two facts go together?" Expect: the seed gets rain, so it sprouts.
- **10 min. Print.** Your child prints A to G, big and small.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Your child says /th/ and a word that starts with it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Ask [[s03-X81a]] and [[s03-X81b]] about a card you draw. Read twice: "A duck has webbed feet. Its feet push the water." Draw feet pushing water with an arrow, a duck beside a hat with an arrow, and bread falling with an arrow. Ask which picture shows how the two facts go together.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the Read, the model and the exit; one pair set instead of three. Longer: a secure child finds a third fact that joins the same two.

### Words and supports

Words: wonder, grow, explain, plant. Frame: "Fact one. Fact two. So ___." Pointing, dictation, a home language and a communication aid all count. A child may pass in any talk. Read each exit prompt twice, with no added hint.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children can say how one fact leads to the next. If many joined facts with an arrow to something the words do not say (error B) or said two facts side by side (error A), day 82 opens with five minutes on the repair: put an arrow card between two facts and say the joining word. Day 82 reads What a Tree Needs and adds the word because. Day 82 also hands back the A to G strips with written feedback.
