# Day 80: What does a tree need?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 79 is the course response and is not written yet; the last written day is day 76, whose Tomorrow hands on the day 77 to 79 records. Read them for a child who could not name a topic or blend three sounds. Hang the four wall pictures with the class on "ask". Mark E80's page. Read aloud to children: the student page says "I can draw the tree or the toy" and "Print s and h side by side."

### The day

#### 5 min. Words

Whole class. Show a picture card and a gesture for each word, and children say it once: living (a hand lifts like a shoot growing), compare (two hands side by side), notice (a hand over the eyes, looking), question (a finger curls up like a question mark). Review: compare, notice and question are words from units 1 and 2; living is new. Nothing is marked.

#### 15 min. Read A Tree and a Toy (01a)

Whole class on the rug. Say aloud: "Leila is made up. This page is for finding out." Read pages 1 to 6 with the pictures (266 words, about three minutes of reading), pausing to show each picture. Children listen for the four words. On page 6 stop and read its questions twice: "What does a tree have that a truck does not?" "What does a tree need?" Roll the toy truck on the floor at page 1 and point to the real or drawn tree at page 2.

#### 5 min. Retrieval

Pairs. Review: topic and details (unit 1, day 9). Show a Weather Changes page children know (unit 2, texts/gk-u2-01.md, selection a, page 3, rainy playground). Each pair names the topic and points to two details; the topic-and-details frame (a box with detail boxes below) is on the chart if needed. Listen to three pairs, 40 seconds each, about 2 minutes. Note any child who needs the frame; that child draws first in today's Draw part.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, then whole class. Review: turn and talk, and the talking token, one turn each, pass allowed (unit 1, day 1). Model first. Ask a key-detail question aloud: "What does Leila touch?" Expect: a leaf and the rough trunk. Ask a clarifying question: "Do you mean the toy or the tree?" Review: clarify means ask again so you understand (unit 2, day 43). Then **Ask.** "What grows while the toy moves?" Expect: the tree grows; it got taller and its trunk got wider. Pairs talk one minute, then hear six children, 30 seconds each. **Ask.** "Which sentences leave us a question about trees?" Expect: "How can I tell if a tree is alive?" and "What does a tree need?" Pairs talk one minute; hear six more. A child may pass. Someone might say "A truck is alive because it moves"; the puppet says it, never a child (error P): answer, "A truck moves when pushed. A tree grows but stays put. Moving does not tell us."

#### 10 min. Bridge: the class question

Whole class. Say what the page gave, in your words and with no science answer: the toy stays the same, the tree changed, Leila has a question. Write the class question on the chart: "What does a tree need?" Read it aloud, then children say it with you. Show the four wall pictures: ask, find, note, share. Say: "Today is ask." A child points to the picture that is ours today. Children do not read the words under the pictures; you do.

#### 10 min. Draw

Individuals. In the first notebook page each child draws the tree or the toy. You copy the class question across the top. A child who needed the frame draws a topic box first. Take dictation from the day's six first, 30 seconds each; then from any child who asks, as time allows. Drawing and dictation need no spelling.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Whole class, then six. Read page 2 of A Tree and a Toy aloud again, its picture covered; the lesson worked other pages. Show strip E80 and ask [[s03-X80av2]]: children hold up one, two or three fingers against their chest for the picture in that place, left to right, so neighbors cannot copy. Note the class pattern; mark the day's six only (shown, shown with help, not yet seen). Then, 30 seconds each, ask the six [[s03-X80bv2]] while the class draws quietly. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". Signs: a detail named as the topic (error X); a wonder with nothing to do with trees (error E).

#### 8 min. Say it

Whole class, oral. Review: rhyme, words that end the same (unit 1, day 16). Show cat and hat, and sun. Say: "Which rhymes with cat?" Children point. Then: "Keep the end, change the start: cat, hat, bat." Pairs say one more rhyme for cat. Nothing is marked; the six are heard in the rotation.

#### 12 min. New sound: sh

Whole class. Say: two letters can stand close and make one sound. Show the letters s and h on the sound-spelling map (Review: the map, unit 1). Say /s/, then /h/, then slide them into /sh/ with a finger to your lips. This is a digraph: two letters, one sound, never /s/ then /h/. Read verse 1 of Two Letters, One Sound twice; children say the refrain "/sh/". Show ship, shell and shoe picture cards; children say each word and its first sound. Add sh to the map. Children trace it in the air with a finger, then print s and h side by side on handwriting paper. Printed words are never read from the cards.

#### 8 min. Letters A to G

Pairs. Review: letter names, both cases (units 1 and 2). Each pair has shuffled upper and lowercase cards A to G and an answer card; they name each card, then pair its capital with its small letter. Hear six pairs, about a minute each; write who was heard on the record sheet. Signs: B named but not b (error AC); a letter formed so it cannot be read (error AK, seen on day 81's printing).

#### 7 min. Words to and do

Whole class. Say the word, tap its sounds, then mark the regular sound and the part to remember: in to and do, the o says /oo/. Hold up each word card; children say it whole. Review: the, a and in (unit 1) are read whole. Ask each child to find the word on their own card.

#### 12 min. Tree Words, verse 1

Whole class. Show verse 1 in large print. Read it aloud twice. Children join the actions: curl like a seed, wiggle fingers for roots, stretch tall. Review: a finger under each word, left to right (unit 1, day 6). Children clap the two-syllable word, acorn (a-corn), then point to the first line. Find to and do in the verse.

#### 13 min. Rotation

Table and stations. Hear six children, two minutes each (12 minutes): "Which rhymes with hat: ten or bat?" then "Say one more." Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Sign: picks a rhyme but cannot make another (error AD). A child who could not blend three sounds on days 77 to 79 sits in this table's group today. The others sort picture cards in partners by /sh/ or not. One minute to write who was heard.

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Read A Tree and a Toy aloud, ask the two questions, draw the tree together and print s and h. One projector: show the read-aloud pictures on one screen; the child still draws on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No toy truck: any toy that rolls. No picture cards: draw three on paper. No story: describe one tree you know.

**Skip.** The pairs, the stations and the verse.

- **10 min. Tree and toy.** Roll a toy, then look at a tree or a picture of one.
  **Say.** "What grows while the toy moves?" Expect: the tree grows; it gets taller.
- **10 min. Ask.** Your child draws the tree or the toy and says one question about trees you write at the top.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints s and h side by side and says /sh/.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X80av2]] [[s03-X80bv2]] Read page 2 of A Tree and a Toy aloud. Your child points to what the page is all about, then asks a question about its tree.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the Read, the Talk and the exit; cut the Draw to three minutes and print only sh. Longer: a secure child asks a second question about the tree.

### Words and supports

Words: living, compare, notice, question. Frames: "The tree grows. The toy ___." "I want to find out ___." Pointing, dictation, a home language and a communication aid all count. A child may pass in any talk. Read each exit prompt twice, with no added hint.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether the class can name what a page is about. If many marked a detail (error X) or asked a question with nothing to do with trees (error E), or someone said moving shows life (error P), day 81 opens with five minutes on the repair: cover all but the picture and ask what every sentence is about; offer two tree question cards; or set the toy and the tree side by side. Day 81 reads What's Alive and What's Not? and models the first connection arrow.
