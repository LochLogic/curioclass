# Day 41: The topic of a page, and the class journal

## Teacher page

### Before class

Lay the topic card and the two detail cards face down. Print the journal page and keep scene card 2 beside it. Draw the exit card C41 and its three pictures and keep them out of sight. Hang the word card on near the verse. Today's six are the next two unheard from each foundations group.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up and repair

Whole class. Repair day 40's exit for the more common of error AF and error AE. For error AF: Pip says "The snow falls." again; the class asks "Where?" and adds the card "on the roofs": "The snow falls on the roofs." For error AE: Pip says "At night." alone; the class says who and what first ("The snow falls"), then adds the card. Then three children each make one fresh sentence longer: "The rain falls." and "The wind blows."

#### 10 min. Reread 01c

Whole class, then partners. Before reading, ask: "What is this page about?" Say: "Listen for the one thing the whole page is about." Review: you put details under a topic in kindergarten (day 9). A topic is what the whole page is about. A detail is one fact under it. Read Rain and Snow whole, pages 1 to 4, showing each picture (about 2 minutes). Then **Ask.** "What is the whole page about?" Expect: rain and snow. Partners tell each other first (3 minutes). A child who says "puddles" or "a mitten" gives a detail; say "that is a detail; the topic is bigger." The reading is not stopped for questions.

#### 8 min. Retell the details

Pairs, then whole class. Turn over the topic card, "rain and snow", and put it on top. Review: this is the topic chart from day 9, with the topic on top and the details under it. Each pair retells two details from the pictures and the page (4 minutes): drops of water; flakes of ice. As they tell, say the matching words from the page: "Rain falls as drops of water." "Snowflakes are made of ice." Put the two detail cards under the topic card. Two pairs share (2 minutes). Say again: the topic goes on top, the details go under it.

#### 5 min. The class picture journal

Whole class. Show an empty journal page. Point to the big box for the picture and the two lines under it where you will write what the children say. Say: "A weather observer records what they see. Our journal records what we see." Review: record was a word on day 39. The class names today's topic. Look through the window for 1 minute. If the sky shows nothing to draw, say "This scene is made up for practice" and show scene card 2, rain falling on a roof and a puddle, with drops on the window. Nobody goes outside and nobody is asked what they wear.

#### 17 min. Joint entry

Whole class. The class makes the first entry together. First, the topic (3 minutes): children choose it, for example "rain" or "gray clouds". Draw it large in the picture box as they tell you. Second, two details (6 minutes): children say two true details from the window or the scene (drops fall on the roof; a puddle gathers). Draw each inside the box. Third, one sentence (4 minutes): the children say it and you write it on the first line, saying each word as you write, for example "Drops fall on the roof." Fourth (4 minutes): five volunteers each add one letter they know for a sound they hear in the sentence, on the second line. Write the date in the corner. The page goes on the wall.

#### 10 min. Own practice

Alone. Each child folds a half sheet in two (or uses the two boxes on the student page): the topic picture on the top half, one detail picture on the lower half, and one word or letter for the topic. Walk the room (30 children at about 20 seconds each is 10 minutes). Say: "What is your page about?" This is practice only; the sheet stays with the child and is not marked.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Six children at the heard table, 40 seconds each (6 times 40 is 4 minutes). Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read card C41 twice, showing its three pictures. Ask item a, then item b ([[s02-X41b]]). A child may point to the picture. Signs: a detail given as the topic (error B); a change the card does not say (error A). Mark the two items apart: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The others add a letter they know to their practice page.

#### 5 min. Foundations opening

Whole class. Read page 1 and page 2 of the verse Snow twice with the pointer, tracking each line. Find the first word of the verse and name its first letter in both cases: Snow starts with S and s (review: s, day 35).

#### 15 min. Foundations work time

Whole class. Meet two new letters (8 minutes). Show the cards g and i, each in both cases. For g: the name is gee, the sound is /g/, the keyword is goat; say /g/ with the back of the tongue. For i: the name is eye, the sound is /i/ as in igloo, with a small smile; the letter name and the sound are different. Add the cards to the sound-spelling map. Chant the twelve letters with keywords (3 minutes): a, t, h, p, n, c, m, r, s, v, g, i.

Map a new word, on (4 minutes). Say the line "Snow falls on the grass" and point to the word on in the print. Say: the n says /n/, as in nest, and the o is the part to remember here. Read on whole by sight. Children find on twice in the verse and touch it each time.

#### 2 min. Foundations closing

Whole class. Say: "Today I practised ___." Three children finish the sentence; the rest say it with a thumb.

#### 38 min. Foundations rotations

Three groups of ten, three 12-minute turns and 2 minutes to move (3 times 12 plus 2 is 38). Teacher table, writing place, sound and verse place. At the table each child has about one minute. Table: middle sounds, say sat and sit and then the middle sound; sort spoken words by middle sound, an /a/ word or an /i/ word (sat, hat, ham; sit, hit, him), stretching each word slowly. Error O: both said with /s/ in the middle; stretch s-a-t and s-i-t and listen to the middle. Writing place: trace g and i in the air, then on paper with the arrow path. Sound and verse place: say a word, then push the middle counter; find g and i in the verse (g in grass, go, dig; i in is, I, mitten, dig). The day's six (two from each group) are heard at the table and marked in the heard column: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Read the page, put the topic card on top, make one journal page together and do the exit card. One projector: show each page and the journal page one at a time; draw the entry on paper while the children tell it.

### At home

**Swap.** No topic cards: write "rain and snow" on a paper. No picture cards: say the words. No counters: use buttons.

**Skip.** The pairs, the rotations and the class journal wall.

- **5 min. Listen.** Read Rain and Snow aloud from the lesson text.
  **Say.** "What is the whole page about?" Expect: rain and snow.
- **5 min. Details.** Write "rain and snow" on top. Your child says two things the page tells and you write them below.
- **10 min. Journal page.** Look out a window or draw an invented scene. Your child draws the topic big, then two details, and says one sentence. You write it.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Say /g/ and /i/ (goat, igloo); your child finds g and i in a book.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X41a]] [[s02-X41b]] Read: "Ice forms on a pond on a cold night. The sun melts the ice by noon." Ask the two questions.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the retell short and put the details under the topic once; keep the entry, the exit and the letters. Longer: a child who is secure draws a third detail and says its sentence.

### Words and supports

Words: topic, detail, journal, record. Frames: "The page is about ___." "One detail is ___." "I see ___." A pointed picture, a gesture or a home-language word names the topic if a picture shows it; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

Day 42 rereads page 4 and holds a short science talk. Its first 5 minutes repair the more common of error B (a detail given as the topic) and error A (an idea not on the card): the topic card on top, detail cards under it, and one fresh page.
