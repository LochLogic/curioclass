# Section 2.2 opener: rain, snow and sentences that grow

## What the section does

Days 39 to 42 read the page about rain and snow, then use it to practice three things: saying more than one (drops, dishes), making a sentence longer by adding where or when, and telling what changed in the weather from a page and a picture. On day 41 the class starts a picture journal, one weather picture a day. Day 42 is a short science talk in which each child says something, asks something and answers.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. Every day begins with a five-minute warm-up that repairs one thing from the day before. The topic block ends with a five-minute exit heard from the day's six children. The foundations block holds the verse, the new letters (g and i begin on day 41) and the rotations.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **More than one.** Many words add /s/ (drops). Some add the beat /es/ (dishes). We say it, clap it, then say it again with the picture.
- **A sentence can grow.** "The boy waits." grows to "The boy waits under the roof." by adding where, then when. Say who and what first.
- **Topic and detail again.** A page about ice is about ice; the pond and the sun are details. The topic goes on top.
- **A talk has exchanges.** Say a thing, ask a question, answer, ask one more. One speaks while the other listens.

## The errors, in plain words

- **X, Y:** the plural ending is left off. Contrast one and two with real objects.
- **AF, AE:** the sentence is said again with nothing added, or only a place is said. Say who and what first, then add where.
- **A, B:** an idea not on the card, or a detail given as the topic. Reread the exact words and put the topic on top.
- **AR, AS:** two voices at once, or the talk stops after one turn. Pass the token; answer and ask one more.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child. Rain and snow are pictures and words; no one is asked about their own weather or clothes.

## Supplies

A puppet, the page on rain and snow in large print with its pictures, object cards (drop, dish, cup, box), word and picture cards for a pocket chart, topic and detail cards, a token, paper and crayons, the journal pages, g and i cards, the exit cards and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Fold a sheet of paper into a pocket for the cards. Use real dishes and cups. Tear a strip of paper for the token. Staple six sheets for the journal.
