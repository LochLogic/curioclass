# Section 2.10 opener: describe, publish and share the weather story

## What the section does

Days 74 to 76 finish the unit. On day 74 each child describes a familiar weather scene to the teacher, with a drawing that helps, in a group of five, while the others edit their caption: the first word, the capital I, the end mark. Day 75 publishes the class piece: each child chooses wording or order, or types with an adult, on a shared offline text editor with a paper copy beside it. Day 75 also closes the end-mark record. On day 76 the class shares the finished stories, the teacher reteaches the one most-missed idea and a fresh retry follows, and the class rereads the last part of Yusuf and the Parcel.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. Day 74 and day 75 are check days: the records they collect are the O1 and E1 look-fors, the D1 publication, the N1 narrative and the P1 sound record, and no exit follows. Day 76 is a response day: no record is collected, and the fresh retry is for support only.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Describing means naming, adding and showing.** A child says what is on the page, adds a detail, and points to the drawing that shows it.
- **Editing is looking for three things.** A capital first letter, a capital I, an end mark that fits.
- **A story is published when someone else can read it.** Typing or choosing the words is part of it; paper keeps the composition but does not complete typing.
- **A retry is practice.** Nothing is marked.

## The errors, in plain words

- **AJ, AK, AL:** a lowercase first word, a lowercase I, a wrong name for an end mark. Circle the first word and change its first letter; find I in a model strip; match a heard telling to its period.
- **AI, AF:** a draft left unchanged after a question, or a short sentence said again.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child. A child who prefers not to speak points to the drawing; the teacher reads the dictation.

## Supplies

The finished drafts and drawings, written feedback, the O1 group list, the three E1 strip sets, a shared device for typing and a paper copy, chart paper, the record sheets, the unit record sheet and the fresh retry card.

## A room with nothing

Use chart paper for the typed piece and hand-drawn strips for E1.
