# Place pictures for the five-place chart

Five places, six small pictures for each, hand-drawn on card stock or paper squares and added to the class chart one place at a time (three places on day 49, two more on day 50). Plain black line drawings on white; people are shown by shape, with no faces to name.

The six pictures for every place: the sky; the ground; what people wear; what people do; a word picture for the weather (rain, dry, wind, dark and cold, fog); and a place-name picture (a small map pin).

- **Mawsynram, India:** heavy rain on green hills; umbrellas and rain hats.
- **The Atacama Desert, Chile:** sand and rock, a clear sky, no plants nearby.
- **Mount Washington, New Hampshire:** a bare mountaintop and a tall tower; people in thick coats leaning into the wind.
- **Utqiagvik, Alaska:** a dark sky at midday, snow, people in thick coats and hats.
- **San Francisco, California:** fog over water and hills; people in light jackets. Do not draw a bridge or a garden here; those are word cards only.

## How to use them

The class sorts each place's six pictures onto the chart and says one sentence about the weather there. The weather is words and pictures; no numbers. Use the places for comparing ("It rains in one place and is dry in another"), not for guessing which is nicest.

## Key (teacher)

A good chart row has a place, its weather and one thing people do there. A place pictured with weather it does not have is a miss to reread from the page (A).
