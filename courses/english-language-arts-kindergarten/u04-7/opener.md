# Section 4.7 opener: paint, revise and compare

## What the section does

Days 146 to 151 finish the appreciation card and compare. On day 146 each child adds a thin watercolor wash to the pencil sketch and keeps the words readable. Day 147 rereads the planting text against the reasons text, and each child revises the card after a partner's question. Day 148 hears each child on the added detail (R1), day 149 practises feedback and choosing words for a shared audio version, day 150 compares the two familiar stories (C3, with T2) and day 151 compares two unseen information cards (C4). Foundations teach long o and u by ear (cycle 24), start long e and r-controlled sounds heard only (cycle 25) and map you, said and were, then she, her and like.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit (days 146, 147 and 149) or the observation windows (days 148, 150 and 151). The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the reader, the response to the cycle check and the rotation. A child outside the six heard is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read the cards; the teacher reads every card aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Paint serves the words.** A thin wash goes around the words; the words stay dry and readable.
- **A revision answers the partner's question.** The detail asked about is added, and the child can say which sentence it came from.
- **Two texts on one topic can differ.** The same tree may be described with different details.
- **Characters in two stories can be compared by what they do.** What they wear is not an experience.
- **A long vowel is heard first, then linked to its letter.** No word is decoded by a long vowel yet.

## The errors, in plain words

- **K, L:** painting or a sketch that has nothing to do with the source, or a whispered explanation.
- **M:** a questioned detail left unchanged.
- **E, A:** two texts taken to say the same thing, or characters compared by their clothes.
- **Z, AA, AB, U, AF:** a story part with no character, place or event, a picture that shows a different event, or watching the reread.
- **AO, AP, AQ, O, Y:** a vowel letter that does not match its sound, a mapped word mis-read, changed letters named with no sound, a picture guess, or a bug left in the cup.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion; there is no yard, only the class pot or the school grounds. Painted-over words have no register letter: blot with a dry towel and write the words again.

## Supplies

Heavy paper, watercolor, brushes, water cups, palettes and cloths (watercolor labeled "Conforms to ASTM D-4236" with no cautionary label, in small amounts, closely supervised; keep food away, wipe spills, wash hands), the two stories and two information texts in print, the exit cards, the sample card, the partner-question strip, the observation sheet, the picture cards for day 150, the unseen cards for day 151, the classroom device (light, days 147 and 148), cards o, u and e, letter tiles, counters, whiteboards and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Use colored pencil in place of paint with the same rule: the words stay readable. Write the caption on a paper strip with six children each adding a word. Make the cards from scrap paper and say each invented card is made up for practice.
