# Section 4.2 opener: compare two stories and check the comparison

## What the section does

Days 125 to 128 use the two familiar stories again. Day 125 builds a two-column comparison and rehearses discussion exchanges. Days 126 and 127 are check days: groups of five compare two pages the class has never worked through, and the teacher records. Day 128 hands the checks back and repairs what they showed. Foundations continue cycle 20 (the an and am families).

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. On check days the topic block is a short familiar reread, thirty minutes of group observation, and a drawing page for everyone; the other children do familiar work at the tables. A child outside the group heard is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read the stories; the teacher reads every page aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A comparison names what is the same and what is different about what each character experiences.**
- **A follow-up question uses the answer just heard.**
- **A turn card marks whose turn it is.**
- **Print is read left to right; a space marks a word; capital and small letters are one letter.**

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** clothes or hair compared instead of what each character does. Repair on day 128 with the event cards.
- **H, I:** talking over a partner, or a talk that stops after one turn. Repair with the turn card and the follow-up card.
- **S, T:** capital and small letters taken as different letters, or two words read as one. Repair in the foundations block.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion.

## Supplies

The two stories in print, picture cards of the rain and noise moments, the chart from day 123, a turn card, follow-up question cards, the cycle-20 reader, letter tiles, counters and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Read from the printed manuscripts. Draw the cards on scrap paper and say they are made up for practice. Cut a strip of paper for the turn card.
