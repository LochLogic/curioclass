# Section 4.6 opener: an appreciation card

## What the section does

Days 140 to 145 begin with the true account of Wangari Maathai reread for what people did together. Each child then makes an appreciation card: a subject, a preference and a drawing, a first draft with written feedback, a revision after a partner's question, and a pencil sketch that adds one detail from reading. Day 142 reads the day-141 card, day 143 listens for turns in talk and for questions and answers about two cards, and day 144 shows the teacher-held recorder. Foundations finish cycle 22, print and write the letters of the alphabet, and teach long a and i by ear (cycle 23).

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit (days 140, 141, 144 and 145) or observation windows (days 142 and 143). The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the reader and the rotation. A child outside the six heard is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read the cards; the teacher reads every card aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **An appreciation card names its subject and a preference.** "I like it" names neither; "I like reading beneath this tree" names both.
- **A drawing adds information.** It shows a detail the page names, not a picture that has nothing to do with the subject.
- **A partner's question is answered beside the draft, and then the detail is added.**
- **Taking turns in talk means waiting, answering the detail and asking a follow-up.**
- **A long vowel is heard first.** The letters a and i are linked to the sounds they name, in spoken words only; no word is read by its long vowel yet.

## The errors, in plain words

- **F, G:** a subject with no preference, or a liking with no subject.
- **J, K:** a drawing or a description with no detail, or a drawing with nothing to do with the subject.
- **L:** speech too soft for the listener to repeat.
- **M:** a questioned detail left unchanged.
- **N:** watching the recorder and choosing nothing.
- **H, I, X, Y, W:** talking over a partner, stopping after one answer, asking no question, an answer the card contradicts, or an uncertainty left unasked.
- **Q:** taking a long-vowel letter name for a spelling pattern.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion; there is no yard, only the class pot or the school grounds. The account names no violence.

## Supplies

The true account and the reasons text in print, the card sheet, the partner-question strip, the exit cards, the two unseen cards for day 143, six step pictures, the teacher-held recorder (shown on day 144 only), cards a and i, letter tiles, counters, whiteboards and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Read from print. Make the cards from scrap paper and say each invented card is made up for practice. With no recorder, write two lines on cards and have three children choose which goes first.
