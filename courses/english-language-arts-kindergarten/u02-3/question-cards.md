# The six question picture cards

Six cards, one for each question word, hand-drawn on card stock. Each card has a large word-picture and the question word in large letters for the teacher to point to; children do not read it. They hear the word and learn what kind of answer it asks for.

- **who:** a person's face. An answer names a person or people.
- **what:** a hand holding a small thing. An answer names a thing or an action.
- **where:** a pin on a small map. An answer names a place.
- **when:** a sun and a moon on a strip. An answer names a time.
- **why:** two boxes joined by an arrow. An answer gives a reason, often with "because".
- **how:** a hand turning a handle. An answer tells the way something is done.

## How to use them

Day 43: the teacher asks about a picture page and children point to the card whose kind of answer fits, then answer. Later days: pairs draw a card and ask a question with that word to a partner. Wrong examples are said by a puppet: the puppet answers a when question with a person; then picks the when card and answers with a time.

## Key (teacher)

A good use: the child points to the matching card, or asks a question starting with the word, and gives the kind of answer it asks for (Z when the kind is mixed up).
