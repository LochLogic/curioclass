# Section 2.1 opener: weather words, questions and a first description

## What the section does

Days 35 to 38 open the unit's question: how does the weather change what people wear and do? The class hears short information pages about weather, asks and answers questions about a detail, learns that a page is mostly about one topic with details under it, and says a sentence about today's weather using a naming word and an action word. Day 36 and day 38 also look through the real window, or at a drawn scene card the teacher says is invented for practice.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds a verse, new letters (v and s) and the rotations. The exit has a spoken part heard from the day's six children and, on some days, a picture to point to. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A page is mostly about one thing.** The topic goes on top and the details go under it. A detail is one thing the page says; the topic is what the whole page is about.
- **We ask about a detail and point to where it is.** The three request cards are: Tell me about a detail. What does ___ mean? Can you say that again?
- **A sentence has a naming word and an action word.** Snow falls. A naming word alone is a good start, not yet a sentence.
- **Weather is words and pictures, never numbers.** No thermometer readings. Warmer and cooler are felt with a hand.
- **Say again when something is not clear.** Asking "What thing?" is a skill, not a failure.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, B:** a child gives an idea that is not on the card, or names one detail as the topic. Reread the card and put the topic picture on top of the detail pictures.
- **AE, F, I, AB:** a fragment, an unclear sentence, the thing instead of the change, or a question not about the word. Recast with who or what and what it did.
- **AR, AS, AT:** talking over a partner, stopping after one turn, or not asking for repair. A puppet shows each, then the right way. Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child.
- **V, W, Q, R, L:** letter and sound slips; see the foundations notes in each day.

No child is asked about clothes or weather at home. Every picture is of a scene in our room or on a card.

## Supplies

A puppet, a window or a drawn scene card, the weather pages in large print with their pictures, topic and detail cards, a passing token, crayons, thick pencils, handwriting paper, v and s letter cards, the exit cards and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the scene on scrap paper and say it is made up for practice. Fold a paper bag into a puppet. Tear a strip of paper for the token. Write the three request cards by hand with a small picture on each.
