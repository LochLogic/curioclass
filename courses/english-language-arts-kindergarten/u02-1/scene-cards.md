# Scene cards: weather pictures for the window

Four hand-drawn weather scenes the teacher uses on days 36 and 38 when the window shows nothing new. Say aloud each time: "This scene is made up for practice." Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines. Each scene has a naming thing and an action in it, so a child can say "Wind turns the vane." No people's names; no thermometers or numbers.

- **Scene 1, a windy day:** a weather vane turning on a roof; a leaf blowing across a path.
- **Scene 2, a rainy day:** rain falling on a roof and a puddle; a window with drops on it.
- **Scene 3, a sunny day:** a bright sun over a hill; a bird on a branch in the light.
- **Scene 4, a cloudy day:** a gray sky with thick clouds over a field.

## How to use them

Day 36: scene 1 or scene 2 if the window shows nothing new; children say one sentence with a naming word and an action word ("Wind turns the vane."). Day 38: show scene 1 with the vane turning and then the same roof with the vane still, and say what changed: the wind stopped. Use scenes only if the window gives nothing; the real window comes first. No outdoor activity in a storm.

## Key (teacher)

A good sentence names a thing and what it does: "Rain taps the roof." "A leaf blows." A naming word alone ("Rain") is a good start, not yet a sentence (AE).
