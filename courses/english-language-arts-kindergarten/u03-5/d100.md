# Day 100: Share the animal piece and check it against its source

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 99's assessment (the contribution, the source information and the earlier writing) onto the record sheet first. Put each piece on its owner's desk. Make one comparison card for each pair. Choose six children for T3 whose pieces name The Oak or Who Depends on a Tree?, and six for isolation. Cover E100. Turn the check card face down. Read aloud: "I can check my fact against its source" and "Write the letters I say."

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and words

Whole class. Review: source, detail, information and check (days 92 to 99). Say each with a gesture. Show The Oak, the squirrel page of Who Depends on a Tree? and the three cards C2a, C2b and C2c, and say they are the places the pieces came from. Today children share and check; the check cards are not used.

#### 10 min. Reread two sources

Whole class. Reread The Oak, page 3, up to "So acorns are food for animals," and the squirrel page of Who Depends on a Tree?, about a minute each. **Ask.** "Which statement in the animal piece matches each source?" Children hold up their piece when their fact is on the page you read, and point to the sentence on the picture copy. Expect: a squirrel eats acorns; a squirrel finds nuts and seeds.

#### 5 min. Model the comparison

Whole class. Review: partner questions (day 85). On chart paper write the statement "A jay eats acorns." Point to the sentence on The Oak, page 3, and read it. Say: "The page says the same. This is a match." Draw a check. Then write "A squirrel eats acorns. A squirrel has a bushy tail." Point to the page: the first sentence is there and the second is not. Say: "This one adds a fact. The page does not say tail." Cross the second sentence. Say aloud that these statements are made up for the model; they are not children's. Use The Oak, not the three cards.

#### 20 min. Share in pairs (SL.K.1, SL.K.3)

Pairs. Review: continue a talk with the token (day 85). Partners trade pieces and the comparison card. Four minutes a turn, two turns: the child holding the other's piece asks "Which card?" and "What does the card say?", the owner points to the sentence and answers, and both draw a check or a cross beside the statement and say match or adds. Then the asker asks one question about a missing detail, such as "What does it eat?" Each child keeps the talk going with the token for three turns; any child may pass. Walk past pairs. For [[s03-T3]], stand beside the three pairs you chose, about three minutes a pair, six children in all. Say: "Ask your partner which source supports the animal piece." The partner points to the sentence and names the source; then reverse. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Signs: "I like squirrels" for a source (error E); the wrong card named (error AI); calling out while a partner speaks (error G); stopping after one comment (error H); saying nothing when a detail is missing (error Y). A wrong example is said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child.

#### 10 min. Add the missing detail

Individuals. Each child adds one detail to the piece in answer to the partner's question, drawn, dictated or written, with a source sticker. Take dictation from children who ask, about 20 seconds each; label for the others as you walk. Look for: (1) the detail comes from a card or a text; (2) the child shows the sentence; (3) the piece still names the animal. "Deer; also, it eats the pear that falls; card C2a" meets; "deer; deer are brown" does not (error D). Unscored; a drawing or dictation is a full answer. Signs: the piece left unchanged after the question (error AA).

#### 5 min. Add to the chart

Whole class. Each child presses the piece, with its source sticker, onto "Who needs trees?" while walking past in a line, about eight seconds each. Say each animal as it goes up. Nothing is scored.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Whole class, then six. Say aloud: "This card is made up for practice." Uncover E100 and read it twice with its pictures. Ask [[s03-X100a]]: children hold up one, two or three fingers against their chest. Mark the day's six only (shown, shown with help, not yet seen), then ask them [[s03-X100b]], 30 seconds each, while the class finishes its drawings. Signs: both sentences checked or both crossed (error B); "It matches" (error D).

#### 5 min. Say it

Whole class, oral. Review: three sounds of a word with counters (days 47 and 70). Say rug, then children push a counter for the first, the middle and the last sound as they say /r/ /u/ /g/. Do fin and jam the same way. Sign: dropping the middle sound (error V).

#### 8 min. Short u, again

Pairs. Review: short u against a and i (days 96 to 99). Each pair builds hat, hit, hut, cut and cup from tiles h, a, i, u, t, c, p and sorts them into three piles by the middle sound: a, i, u. Say each word and the middle sound together. Walk past six pairs. Signs: naming the changed letter as the first one when the middle changed (error L); i or a for the vowel in hut (error U).

#### 10 min. Gus in a Tub, reader 3

Small groups of six. Review: Gus in a Tub (days 96 to 99). Each group rereads it with the picture covered and says what happened and what was learned about Gus. Sit with two groups for a few pages each. Signs: guessing the word from the picture (error R). No reteaching today.

#### 7 min. Spell and change

Individuals. This is the written half of the cycle 15 check; nothing is taught. Review: counters for spelling (day 95) and change one letter (day 96). Dictate gum and dug; each child writes a letter for each sound on side 2. Then dictate rub, and each child writes rub, changes one letter to make rib, and says the sound that changed (/u/ to /i/). Collect the sheets. Mark no one aloud. Signs: u written as a (error U); the changed letter named as r (error L).

#### 30 min. Rotation and cycle 15 check

Table and floor. The cycle 15 check reads cold: it is a new card, never practised. Call children to your table one at a time, about 35 seconds each: the child reads the check card, "That fun chap has his big jug.", and says who has what (error K if the words are read but not understood); then reads the cards was and his without sounding out, for [[s03-P5]]. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Hear the six chosen for isolation for two minutes each, in the same time as their card: ask "Tell me the first sound, the middle sound and the last sound of tug." Write who was heard on the record sheet. Twenty-four children at 35 seconds and six at two minutes fill about 26 of the 30 minutes. The rest reread Gus in a Tub and chain words with tiles in pairs at the floor stations. No reteaching today; repair waits for day 101. Signs: cannot read was or his without remapping (error S); guessing a word from the picture (error R); /t/ /g/ for tug (error V).

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Read one source sentence, say a statement and ask the child to point and say match or adds, then ask the T3 question. Build hat, hit and hut on tiles and read the check card at the end. One projector: show the source sentence and the statement side by side; the child still draws on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No tiles: write letters on paper squares. No counters: use buttons.

**Skip.** The pairs, the chart and the stations.

- **10 min. Spell.** Say gum and dug; your child pushes a button for each sound and writes a letter for each.
- **5 min. Words.** Your child reads was and his on cards.
- **10 min. Change.** Spell rub on squares; change one letter to make rib and name the sound that changed.
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Ask [[s03-X100a]] and [[s03-X100b]] about a card you draw. Read twice: "Source: A bee lands on a flower of the cherry tree. Draft: A bee lands on a flower of the cherry tree. The bee also sleeps in the tree." Draw the source and draft side by side with a check beside the first sentence and a cross beside the second, both sentences checked, and both crossed.
  **Say.** "What does the draft add?" Expect: the bee also sleeps in the tree, and the source does not say it.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the comparison, the added detail, the exit and the spelling; cut the chart and the sort. Longer: a secure child compares a second statement with its source and says match or adds.

### Words and supports

Words: match, adds, source, detail, question. A match says the same as the source; adds says something the source does not. Frames: "The source says ___. The draft says ___." and "Which card did you use?" Home-language rehearsal with a partner first, then English. The cycle 15 words are read aloud twice; a child may point, mark or answer privately, and a scribe records without adding ideas. Use an agreed access method rather than a default "not yet seen". A child may pass in any talk.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you whether children can pick the picture that checks a draft against its source and say what the draft adds. If many marked both sentences checked or both crossed (error B), or said it matches (error D), day 101 opens with five minutes on the comparison card. Day 101 hands back each child's marks from days 97 to 99 and today's, repairs them in groups, gathers one fresh note and repairs the cycle 15 errors; copy today's records onto the record sheet first.
