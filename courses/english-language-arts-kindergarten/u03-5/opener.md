# Section 3.5 opener: share, compare and gather again

## What the section does

Days 100 and 101 share the animal piece and gather one more note. Day 100 compares a draft statement with its source and each pair asks and answers about the source. Day 101 returns the check evidence, repairs it and gathers one fresh note from a new question and source. Foundations finish short u and the cycle check.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit or, on check days, the observation groups. The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the day's words and the rotation. A child outside the six heard for the exit is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read alone; the teacher reads every card, direction and text aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A statement can match its source or not.** A check beside a matching statement and a cross beside the part the source does not say.
- **We ask where a fact came from.** The partner points to the sentence and names the text.
- **A fresh note is not another conference.** It is one note, one question, one source.

## The errors, in plain words

- **B, D:** a comparison that does not follow the sentences, or an added statement the source does not give. Read the sentence and tick or cross.
- **E, AI:** a fact that does not answer the question, or the wrong source named. Restate the question; match fact to sentence.
- **C, F:** the animal unnamed or no contribution. Repair on day 101.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion.

## Supplies

The comparison card, the T3 prompt card, the animal pieces, the three unused source cards, text 05a and 06 in large print, the reader Gus in a Tub and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw each card on scrap paper and say it is made up for practice. Cut scrap paper into cards. Use buttons for counters, a torn strip for the talking token and a leaf or a drawn tree for the tree. Write the sound-spelling map as a strip by hand.
