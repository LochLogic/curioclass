# Section 1.1 opener: listening, talking and the sharing story

## What the section does

Days 1 to 5 open the year's first question: what makes a toy fun, and how do we share it? The class hears a shared poem, learns four talk rules, asks and answers questions about a picture story, and meets the front cover, back cover and title page of a book. Day 5 is a check day: you watch small groups of five talk about a short story you read once. Nothing is taught on the check day.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds a chant, letters, sounds, printing and five 8-minute rotations of six children. The exit on days 1 to 4 has two parts: every child marks a picture card (circle the dots or the picture), and you hear the day's six children answer one spoken question. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A question asks; a statement tells.** "Can you help me?" gets help; "This is stuck" only reports. A statement is never wrong, only not yet a question.
- **We listen with the whole body.** The four rules are look, listen, wait and take a turn. You model them first with a puppet; pairs then use a turn card.
- **Pictures and words go together.** Point back to the words and the picture that tell. A picture shows one moment; the words tell which.
- **A book has parts.** Front cover, back cover and title page; the author makes the words and the illustrator makes the pictures.
- **The word we hear is the word we see.** A name is one word made of letters. A keyword picture reminds us of a sound; it is never used to guess a word.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, B:** a child tells an idea that is not in the story, or picks a picture of a different moment. Reread the sentence and lay the two pictures beside it.
- **C, D, E:** a child talks over a partner, stops after one answer, or says a statement where a question is needed. A puppet shows each, then the right way.
- **I, AC:** the author and illustrator are swapped, or an inside page is called the back cover. Close the book and look at its outside.
- **G, H, N, O, P, S:** letter and print slips; see the foundations notes in each day.

Wrong examples are voiced by a puppet, never by a child. No child is asked about toys at home; every toy is one in our room.

## Supplies

A puppet, two large classroom toys with no loose parts, chart paper, crayons, thick pencils, handwriting paper, name cards, the poem and the story in large print, paper booklets of the story, the exit cards, the sound-spelling-map frame and a record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the poem on scrap paper and copy it by hand. Fold a paper bag into a puppet. Tear a strip of paper for the turn card. Use two real toys from the room, and write the names on torn paper.
