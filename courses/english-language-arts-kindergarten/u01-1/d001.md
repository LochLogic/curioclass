# Day 1: Come and play, and the four talk rules

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make the talk-rules poster as four rule cards (look, listen, wait, take a turn), each with one picture, to hang beside the chart. Put the cart and the top where you can reach them. Copy the exit card for the paint jar. Today's six are the first six names on the record sheet; look at them early.

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook and first words

Whole class on the rug, the cart and the top in the middle. Say: "These are toys. A toy is for play." Name each toy together. Teach two gestures: "toy" (two hands lift an invisible toy) and "turn" (one hand passes the toy to the other hand: my turn, your turn). Children copy each gesture as you say the word.

#### 12 min. Poem Our Play, beside the toys

Show the large-print poem: "This is a poem. It was made for us to say together." Read it once, sliding a finger under each line, left to right, top to bottom. Read it twice more; the class joins the repeated line, "Come and play, come and play". Hold up the cart at "roll it" and the top at "spin it".

**Ask.** What does the poem invite us to do? Expect: come and play.

**Ask.** Which words tell whose turn comes next? Expect: "You take a turn, then I take mine." Reread that line with the finger under it. Pointing counts as an answer.

#### 10 min. Model listening and taking turns

Whole class. You and the puppet show it wrong, then right. The puppet speaks one line at a time. Wrong: you say "I like the cart" while the puppet looks away, then the puppet talks over you once. Right: the puppet looks at you, waits, and says its line only when you finish.

**Ask.** What did the puppet forget when it talked while I talked? Expect: to wait, or to listen.

Post the four rules in order and have children do each move once: look (point to eyes), listen (cup an ear), wait (hands in lap), take a turn (pass a hand across). The puppet voices every wrong example; no child is shown as the mistake (error C). Hang the poster; social studies uses the same one from school day 3.

#### 8 min. Ask for help

Hold the cart and say: "I cannot find the place." Then say: "Where does the cart go, please?" A question asks; a statement tells. The first is not wrong, it only tells.

**Ask.** Which one asks for help? Expect: the second.

Class says the question back together. Each child then turns to a neighbor and asks one question about a toy on the rug: "What is it called?" "Does it spin?" "Where does it go?" A child may pass. About 15 pairs, 30 seconds each way; walk around and listen without correcting. Model a statement given to a question as "not yet a question": say "Tell me as a question" and wait.

#### 10 min. Turn and talk

Show with the puppet first (1 minute): it asks "Which toy would you like to play with here?", you answer, then you say one thing back ("You would like the top."). Pairs of neighbors do two exchanges: ask, answer, swap, say one thing back. A child with no partner joins a pair. Listen to three pairs, one minute each. Only toys in our room, never home toys. Note nothing as a mistake today.

#### 10 min. Draw a toy from our room

Each child draws one toy from our room on the student page. Write the toy's word on request; the others get a label at the stations. Say: "Draw the toy you would like to play with here."

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hand out the day 1 card (the paint jar). Read aloud: "Lu cannot get the lid off the paint jar. Dan sits at the same table." Read the three sentences Lu could say, one at a time. Children circle the dots for the one that asks Dan for help ([[s01-X1]], part a: 2 minutes). Then hear today's six, 30 seconds each (3 minutes): "Dan tells Lu how to twist the lid. What does Lu do while Dan talks?" Expect: listens, waits or looks at Dan. A child may point or act. Mark each as shown, shown with help or not yet seen. A child outside the six is not yet heard, never missed.

Signs: one dot (a statement, error E); three dots (an idea that is not about help, error A); "I say my idea too" (error C).

#### 5 min. Word game: What's the word?

Show a ball, then the cart, then act roll, then act push. The class says the one word each time, then claps its beats (ball, cart, roll and push are one beat each). Say: "A word is a sound we say that means something."

#### 8 min. Name game: the word we heard, the word we see

Name cards face down in a pocket chart. Up to six volunteers say their own name aloud. Turn the card: "We heard it. Now we see it. It looks the same every time." Slide a finger under the name, left to right. Start with your own card. A child may decline. Names stay in this room.

#### 4 min. Words on paper

On chart paper, write three words the class said in the word game: ball, cart, roll, left to right with a wide gap between each. Slide a finger under each as the class says it. Children do not read them; they see that a word we say can be written, and that a space sits between words.

#### 3 min. Closing

**Ask.** Where can you find a written word in our room? Expect: a name card, the poem, the chart, a label. Point to each.

#### 40 min. Rotations

Five groups of six, five turns of 8 minutes, a move between each. At the table (6 children, 1 minute each, 2 minutes for the move) each child gets one toy word to say and clap: ball (1 beat), cart (1), top (1), wagon (2), puzzle (2), blocks (1). Then say "play, today" together; ask each child "Does ___ sound like play?" with day (yes), ball (no), say (yes), cart (no), one a child. Table notes go on the record sheet for today's six only.

Stations (children do each for 8 minutes): draw and label (draw a toy, an adult labels on request); name-card match (match a name card to the same name card); big-book browse (look at the class big book, finger under the words, left to right); sand tray (draw straight lines and curved lines with a finger).

### Other rooms

One child: read the poem aloud and ask the two questions. No projector: the poem copied on paper. Everything else is on the rug.

### At home

**Swap.** No puppet: a sock or a spoon. No large-print poem: you read it aloud.

**Skip.** The name cards, the stations, the class talk.

- **5 min. The poem.** Say twice: "Come and play, come and play, there's a toy for us today." Show two toys.
  **Say.** "What does the poem invite us to do?" Expect: come and play.
- **5 min. Listen.** You and a toy take turns speaking. Show the four rules: look, listen, wait, take a turn.
- **5 min. Ask for help.** Say "I cannot find the place," then "Where does it go, please?"
  **Say.** "Which one asks for help?" Expect: the second.
- **5 min. Turn and talk.** Ask "Which toy would you like to play with?" Then say one thing back.
- **5 min. Draw a toy.** Your child draws one toy from the page.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]] Read the card aloud. Your child circles the dots and tells what Lu does while Dan talks.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the name game to your own and one other name; keep the poem, the four rules, the ask for help and the exit. Longer: a child who is sure asks a second question about the same toy.

### Words and supports

Words: toy, turn, ask, poem. Frames: "I ask ___." "My turn." "Your turn." A home language, signs, pointing or a drawing all count; say the English back once. A communication aid or a pointed card is an answer. A child who needs to move may stand at the rug edge.

### Tomorrow

If many circled one dot (error E) or said "I say my idea too" (error C), day 2 opens with a five-minute repair: the puppet says a statement beside a question, or talks over you beside waiting, and children try it. Then we hear the storybook One Hoop, Two Friends once, and meet its front cover, back cover and title page.
