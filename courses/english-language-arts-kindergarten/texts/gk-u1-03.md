# Push, Pull, Roll

[page 1]
Toys move in many ways. A toy sits still. Then a push or a pull makes it go.
Picture: A wide view of a classroom floor with a small toy cart, a rubber ball, a toy duck on a string, and a wooden block. Four children of different backgrounds look at them.

[page 2]
A push moves a toy away from you. Push a cart, and it goes ahead. Push a block along the floor, and it slides.
Picture: A boy with glasses pushes a toy cart across the floor, his hands on the back of it. The cart moves away from him.

[page 3]
A pull brings a toy toward you. Pull the string, and a toy duck on wheels comes along behind you. Pull a wagon, and it follows.
Picture: A girl in a wheelchair pulls a toy duck on a string toward her. The duck has small wheels.

[page 4]
Some toys roll. A ball rolls. A wheel rolls. A cart has wheels, so it rolls when you push it. A box with no wheels slides and rubs along the floor. A cart with wheels rolls along, and it is easier to push.
Picture: Two toys side by side on the floor. A cart on four round wheels with a child's hand pushing it, and a flat box with no wheels that a second child has to shove.

[page 5]
A push and a pull are different. A push sends a toy away. A pull brings a toy along. But a push and a pull are the same in one way. Both make a toy move.
Picture: Two small pictures side by side. In the first, a child's hands push a cart away. In the second, a child's hands pull a wagon toward her. A curved arrow joins the two pictures.

[page 6]
A push or a pull can do more. It can make a toy go. It can make a toy stop. It can make a toy go faster or slower. It can make a toy turn a new way.
Picture: A toy car rolls along a classroom rug, then stops, then turns, shown in three steps with a child's hand at each step.

[page 7]
Look at the toys in your room. Which ones do you push? Which ones do you pull? Which ones roll?
Picture: A shelf of classroom toys, including blocks, balls, a cart and a wagon, with a child pointing at one of them.
