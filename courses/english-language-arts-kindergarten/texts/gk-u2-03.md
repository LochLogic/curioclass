## 1: Section 1: The Parcel (212 words)

[page 1]
Picture: Late afternoon on a small-town street outside a post office with a wooden door. Yusuf, a boy of about six in a green hat and scarf, holds a brown paper parcel tied with string. His grandfather Baba, in a gray coat, holds the door. The sky is pale gray.

Yusuf and his grandfather Baba came out of the post office with a parcel. The parcel was square, wrapped in brown paper and tied with string. It was from Aunt Salma, who lived far away. Mama had been waiting for it all week.

"May I carry it?" asked Yusuf.

"You may," said Baba. "Hold it with both hands."

Yusuf held the parcel against his coat. It felt like something soft was inside.

[page 2]
Picture: The two walk past shop windows. The sky is gray and low; the air looks hazy. A single flake drifts past a lamp post.

The sky had turned gray while they were inside. The air felt cooler on Yusuf's cheeks.

"Look," said Yusuf. "A snowflake."

One flake drifted down. Then another. Baba looked at the sky.

"We have three streets to go," said Baba. "Let us walk fast, and stay close."

They walked past the shoe shop and the bakery and the library. The flakes fell a little faster. Yusuf caught one on his mitten. It was tiny and white and then it was gone.

"Snow comes from clouds when the air is cold," said Baba. "Today the air is cold."

Yusuf pulled his hat down over his ears. "Mama will be so happy when she sees the parcel," he said.

"She will," said Baba. "We will get it home."

At the corner, the wind gave a little push. The flakes began to swirl.

## 2: Section 2: The Snow Thickens (279 words)

[page 1]
Picture: The street from the corner. The snow now falls thick; the lamp post is a blur. Baba holds Yusuf's free hand and the parcel is tucked under Yusuf's other arm. A bakery window glows ahead.

The wind pushed harder. The snow came thick and fast. It blew sideways across the street. Yusuf could not see the end of the block. He could hardly see the lamp post.

Baba stopped. He held Yusuf's hand tight.

"Yusuf," said Baba, "the snow is too thick now. I cannot see the street clearly. We will not walk in this."

"But we are almost home," said Yusuf. His voice was small.

"We are not almost home," said Baba gently. "We are three streets from home. And we are not going to walk three streets when we cannot see. Look. The bakery is right there, with its lights on. We will wait inside."

Yusuf felt his stomach tighten. He did not like it when the world turned white. But Baba's hand was warm, and the bakery was close.

[page 2]
Picture: Inside the bakery, a warm room with a counter, a small table by the window and a loaf-filled basket. Mrs. Chen, a round-faced woman with a white apron, holds the door. Snow on their coats.

They stepped into the bakery. A little bell rang. The air smelled of bread. Mrs. Chen stood behind the counter in her white apron.

"Come in, come in!" said Mrs. Chen. "Shake the snow off your coats. You will wait with me until it eases."

Baba took out his phone and called Mama. "We are safe," he said. "We are in the bakery. We will wait for the snow to ease. We will be home as soon as we can."

Yusuf looked out the window. The snow was a thick white wall. He could not see the lamp post at all.

"Baba, how long?" he asked.

"I do not know," said Baba. "We will watch the window, and we will decide when it is safe."

Yusuf set the parcel on the little table. He sat down, and he looked at the snow.

## 3: Section 3: Waiting (228 words)

[page 1]
Picture: The bakery: a table by the window; Yusuf sits with a cup of warm milk and the parcel; the paper corner is damp. Mrs. Chen hands him a clear plastic bag. Baba watches the window.

Mrs. Chen brought Yusuf a cup of warm milk. "For waiting," she said.

Yusuf held the cup in both hands. Then he saw that the corner of the parcel was damp. The snow on his coat had melted onto the brown paper.

"Oh no!" said Yusuf. "The paper is wet."

Mrs. Chen came with a clear plastic bag. "Let us keep it dry," she said. Yusuf slid the parcel into the bag. He pressed the top shut, and he tucked the bag under his arm.

"Now it is safe," said Yusuf.

"Good thinking," said Baba.

Outside, the snow kept falling. The wind rattled the door. Two more people came in from the street, stamping their boots. They sat down by the counter. Mrs. Chen gave them warm bread.

"We should all wait," said one of them. "Nobody can see the street."

Yusuf looked at the white window. He had wanted to run home. Now he saw that many people were waiting. He was not the only one. The waiting felt a little easier.

[page 2]
Picture: Yusuf at the window with Baba. The snow outside is still falling but the flakes look smaller. A figure of a plow truck with an orange light is a faint smudge far down the street.

Yusuf asked Mrs. Chen, "Why is it so hard to see?"

"The wind is pushing the snow sideways," said Mrs. Chen. "The air is full of flakes. When the wind slows, the air will clear."

Yusuf watched. The flakes still flew sideways. Then, a little later, they fell straight down. The wind had grown quiet.

## 4: Section 4: The Quiet (264 words)

[page 1]
Picture: Yusuf, Baba and Mrs. Chen at the window. The snow now falls straight and soft. The street is white; the lamp post is visible again. Yusuf looks eager. Baba shakes his head gently.

The bakery was quiet. The wind had stopped. Snow fell straight down in big soft flakes. Yusuf could see the lamp post again, and the shop across the street.

"Baba," said Yusuf, "I can see the street. May we go now?"

Baba looked out the window. "The wind has stopped," he said. "That is good. Wind pushes snowflakes sideways and hides the street, and now we can see. But look at the street. The snow is deep, and it is still falling. The plow has not come."

Yusuf's face fell. He felt a hot, wriggling feeling in his chest. He wanted to go home. He did not want to wait anymore.

"I know," said Baba. "Waiting is hard. You are worried and tired. It is all right to feel that way."

"I am worried the parcel will not get home," said Yusuf in a small voice.

"The parcel is safe in the bag," said Baba. "And you are safe with me. We will go when it is safe to go."

Yusuf leaned against Baba's arm. His chest slowly felt less tight.

[page 2]
Picture: A snowplow truck with a flashing orange light moves slowly along the street, pushing snow into a heap by the curb. Yusuf's face at the window is lit orange. Mrs. Chen smiles.

Then they heard a rumble. Yusuf pressed his nose to the window.

A big truck rolled slowly down the street. It had a bright orange light on top. It pushed a wide blade of snow to the side. Behind it, the street was clear and dark.

"The plow!" cried Yusuf.

"There it is," said Baba. "We will wait a little more, until the snow thins and the street is clear."

The big flakes began to turn small. Soon only a few drifted down.

## 5: Section 5: Home (309 words)

[page 1]
Picture: Outside the bakery: the snowfall has nearly stopped; the street is plowed; the sky is a soft pink-gray. Baba and Yusuf wave goodbye to Mrs. Chen at the door. Yusuf has the bagged parcel against his chest.

At last, the snow was only a few flakes. The street was plowed. The wind was gone.

"Now," said Baba, "it is time."

They put on their hats and scarves. Yusuf held the parcel, still in its bag. He said, "Thank you, Mrs. Chen, for the warm milk."

"Come again," said Mrs. Chen. "Safe home."

The bell rang as they stepped outside. The air was cold and still. The snow was soft and deep along the sides of the road, but the street was clear.

[page 2]
Picture: Yusuf and Baba walking hand in hand along the plowed street under a soft pink sky; windows glow in warm yellow along both sides. A lit window with Mama waiting.

They walked past the library. They walked past the shoe shop. They walked slowly, hand in hand, with the lights of the windows glowing yellow on the snow.

At the last corner, Yusuf saw a window with a lamp lit in it. A face looked out. It was Mama.

She opened the door before they reached it. "You are home!" said Mama. "I was worried. Come in, come in."

"We waited at the bakery," said Yusuf. "The snow was too thick to walk in. Baba said we would not walk when we could not see."

"That was wise," said Mama, and she hugged them both.

[page 3]
Picture: The family kitchen: Mama, Baba and Yusuf at a table. The parcel is open: a pair of blue mittens with a red stripe. Yusuf wears one and grins. Snow falls lightly outside the window.

Yusuf took the parcel out of the bag. It was dry. He gave it to Mama.

Mama untied the string. She opened the brown paper. Inside was a pair of blue mittens with a red stripe, from Aunt Salma. And a note.

"They are for you, Yusuf," said Mama. "Aunt Salma knit them for you."

Yusuf put them on. They fit. They were warm.

"I carried them all the way home," said Yusuf. Then he thought. "No, we waited, and then we carried them home."

Baba laughed. "Yes. Waiting was a part of getting home."

Yusuf sat by the window in his new mittens. Outside, the last few flakes drifted down, and he watched them with a calm heart.
