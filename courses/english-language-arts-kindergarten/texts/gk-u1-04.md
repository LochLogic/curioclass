# A Toy Can Turn

[page 1]
Some toys move in circles. They turn.
Picture: A child holds a toy top in one hand and a small cart under the other arm, looking at both.

[page 2]
A top spins. You twist its stem with your fingers and let go. The top spins round and round on its point.
Picture: A boy with dark skin twists the stem of a red top between his finger and thumb. A circle of curved lines shows the top spinning.

[page 3]
A top that spins fast stands up straight. When the top slows down, it leans over. Then it falls down.
Picture: Three pictures in a row. A top spins upright, then leans, then lies on its side on the table.

[page 4]
A wheel spins, too. When a cart rolls forward, each wheel spins round and round. A rolling wheel spins and moves forward at the same time.
Picture: A cart rolls across the floor. Curved arrows around each wheel show it turning, and one straight arrow shows the cart moving forward.

[page 5]
A whole toy can turn, too. Steer a toy car to the right, and it turns to the right. Steer it to the left, and it turns to the left. It changes the way it is going.
Picture: A toy car on a mat, with a girl's hand guiding it around a curve. A curved arrow shows the car's path.

[page 6]
A label tells you a name. Here is a cart with labels.
Picture: A toy cart drawn large. A line from each part points to a label: wheel, handle, box.

[page 7]
A top spins in one place. A cart rolls from place to place. They are different. But they are the same in one way. Each one has something that turns.
Picture: A top and a cart side by side on a table. Two children, one on each side, look at them and smile.

[page 8]
What turns in your room?
Picture: A bright classroom. A child looks up at a round clock on the wall and another child tips a toy top onto a table.
