# The Tree Beside the Path

[page 1]
Noor walked to the park on the same path every day. Beside the path stood a small tree. It came up to Noor's shoulder.
Noor was five.

Picture: A path of packed earth between a row of apartment buildings and a park. A girl with brown skin and a yellow coat, Noor, walks it. A small, thin tree stands beside the path.

[page 2]
In spring, the tree's buds opened. Out came small green leaves, as soft as new paper.
"Hello, tree," said Noor.

Picture: Close view of a twig with open buds and tiny green leaves. Noor leans in to look.

[page 3]
In summer, the leaves were wide and dark green. They made a little shade. Noor sat in it and drew pictures.

Picture: Noor sits cross-legged in the small patch of shade with a sketchbook.

[page 4]
In fall, the days grew shorter. The leaves turned yellow and red. Then the wind pulled them down, one by one.
Noor picked up the leaves and crunched them in her hands.

Picture: Yellow and red leaves drift down around the tree. Noor holds up a handful of them.

[page 5]
In winter, the branches were bare. Snow sat on the twigs.
"Is the tree gone?" asked Noor.
She looked close. On every twig sat a tiny bud, shut tight.
The tree was not gone. The buds were waiting.

Picture: A snowy morning. The bare tree stands beside the path. Noor, in a red hat, looks at a twig close up. The buds are small bumps on it.

[page 6]
Next spring, the buds opened again. The little leaves came back.
But this year, something else had changed. Noor reached her arms around the trunk, and her hands touched, just barely. And the top of the tree came up past her head.
Noor was six.
The tree had grown.

Picture: Noor hugs the trunk with her arms, fingertips just touching. The tree's top is above her head. New green leaves are on the twigs.

[page 7]
Summer came. Fall came. Winter came. Each year the leaves came and went. And each year the tree grew a little more.

Picture: Four small drawings in a row, one for each season, showing the same tree. In each drawing the tree is a little taller.

[page 8]
When Noor was seven, she reached her arms around the trunk. Her hands did not touch. The trunk was too wide.
When Noor was eight, she looked up and could not see the top of the tree unless she tipped her head far back.

Picture: Noor, older, stands with her arms spread out against the trunk. Her hands do not meet. She looks up through the branches.

[page 9]
Now Noor was nine. The tree was taller than the buildings' first-floor windows. The summer shade was so big that three children could sit in it. Noor sat in the shade with her friend and drew.
It was the same spot, the same path, and the same tree. But the tree was not the same size. And Noor was not the same size either.

Picture: A tall, full tree. Noor, nine, sits with a friend in the big shade. Both have sketchbooks.

[page 10]
One day in spring, a small boy stopped on the path. His name was Sam. He looked up at the tall tree.
"How big this tree is!" he said.
"It was small once," said Noor. "When I was five, it only came up to my shoulder."
Sam looked at Noor. He looked at the tree. "It grew," he said.
"Yes," said Noor. "And the buds are opening right now. Look."

Picture: Sam, small, with a backpack, looks up. Noor points to a twig with opening buds. The tall tree fills the page.

[page 11]
Noor smiled. She had seen the tree in spring, summer, fall and winter. She had seen it all through the years.
"Hello, tree," she said.
And the tree stood beside the path.

Picture: Noor's hand rests on the wide trunk. Leaves move in a light wind. The path goes on toward the park.
