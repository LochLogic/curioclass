# Tree Care Words

- text_id: gk-u4-tcw
- author: Curio Class
- year: 2026
- kind: literary (rhymed verse with tree-care facts)
- form: seven short shared verses, each two pages of two lines, teacher-read and tracked in shared print; a set of seven in one file
- word count: 255 in all (verse by verse below; the words read aloud, not the headings or picture notes)
- use: foundations practice beside each cycle reader: echo reading with the finger on the line, the line that gives the order, rhyme, syllables, onset and rime, the cycle's mapped words in the line, and one source sentence written together; not an independent reader; the teacher reads the rich language
- read on: verse 1 (cycle 19, day 119); verse 2 (cycle 20, day 124); verse 3 (cycle 21, day 130); verse 4 (cycle 22, day 135); verse 23 (cycle 23, day 141); verse 24 (cycle 24, day 146); verse 25 (cycle 25, day 151); and then by echo on the days of each cycle
- numbering: the section plans call the first four verses 1 to 4 and the last three verse 23, verse 24 and verse 25 (their cycle numbers); this file keeps those names. Verses 1 to 4 are Tree Care Words, cycle 19 to cycle 22, and the section 4.4 plan's cycle-22 verse is verse 4.
- what each verse carries: an ordered action (First, Then, Last) so a child can point to the line that gives the order; two rhyme pairs; one two-syllable word that is really in the verse; an onset-rime pair; the cycle's mapped words in the lines; one true tree-care detail; words beyond the mapped and taught patterns are heard, never decoded; children are not asked to guess them
- facts: tree-care details rest on Iowa State University Extension, Care of Newly-Planted Trees, https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/care-newly-planted-trees (checked: 2026-11-19): water when the soil is dry down a little and wet the whole root zone; mulch kept back from the trunk; many new trees need no stake, and a stake uses a wide flexible strap; check whether the root ball moves when the tree sways; watch the leaves for poor growth. Colorado State University Extension (Mulching Trees and Shrubs) and Utah State University Extension (Post-planting Tree Care) say the same about mulch and watering (surfaced in the search of 2026-11-19; not opened). No number of inches, gallons or years is given in the verses.

## Verse 1. A Drink for the Tree (day 119, cycle 19: of, have, from)

[page 1]
First, I touch the soil, so dry and brown.
Then I pour from my can, and the water goes down.

Picture: A child kneels at a young tree and presses a finger into pale, dry soil. Then she tips a watering can; water falls onto the soil.

[page 2]
Last, I wait for the last of it to sink.
Down in the dark, the roots have a drink.

Picture: A cutaway of the ground beside the tree: water soaks down and thin roots reach toward it. The child sits back and watches.

Hooks: order words: First (page 1 line 1), Then (page 1 line 2), Last (page 2 line 1); rhyme pairs brown/down, sink/drink; two-syllable word: water (wa-ter, 2 syllables); onset-rime: down (d + own) and brown (br + own); mapped words: of, have, from; tree-care detail: touch the soil to see whether it is dry; water slowly so it soaks down to the roots.

## Verse 2. A Blanket of Mulch (day 124, cycle 20: there, they, where)

[page 1]
First, spread the mulch there on the ground,
a soft, brown blanket, all around.

Picture: A child and a grown-up spread wood chips in a wide ring around a young tree, using their hands and a small rake.

[page 2]
Then, where the trunk meets the soil, keep it away.
The bark needs air, or so they say.

Picture: Close on the base of the trunk. A bare ring of soil shows between the trunk and the edge of the mulch; the child points at the gap.

Hooks: order words: First (page 1 line 1), Then (page 2 line 1); rhyme pairs ground/around, away/say; two-syllable word: blanket (blan-ket, 2 syllables); onset-rime: say (s + ay) and away; ground (gr + ound) and around; mapped words: there, they, where; tree-care detail: spread mulch in a ring but keep it back from the trunk so the bark is not covered.

## Verse 3. A Soft Strap (day 130, cycle 21: we, all, one)

[page 1]
First, we tie one wide, soft strap in place.
It holds the tree, but gives all its branches space.

Picture: Two children and a grown-up fasten a wide cloth strap loosely around a thin young tree and a stake.

[page 2]
Then we watch the top sway and bend,
while the roots hold firm, like a friend.

Picture: The wind blows. The top of the young tree leans while the base stays still. The children watch with their hands on their knees.

Hooks: order words: First (page 1 line 1), Then (page 2 line 1); rhyme pairs place/space, bend/friend; two-syllable word: branches (bran-ches, 2 syllables); onset-rime: bend (b + end) and friend (fr + end); mapped words: we, all, one; tree-care detail: a loose, wide strap can support a young tree while it can still sway; many young trees need no stake at all.

## Verse 4. Visiting the Tree (day 135, cycle 22: but, not, no)

[page 1]
First, we visit the tree with our class.
We look at each leaf as we pass.

Picture: A line of children walks along a path toward a young tree. Each child looks up at the leaves.

[page 2]
Then we feel the soil, but not too much.
No tugging, no poking, just a gentle touch.

Picture: One child presses a finger into the soil near the tree's base while the others wait their turn with open hands.

Hooks: order words: First (page 1 line 1), Then (page 2 line 1); rhyme pairs class/pass (both end in the doubled final ss), much/touch; two-syllable word: visit (vis-it, 2 syllables); onset-rime: class (cl + ass) and pass (p + ass); mapped words: but, not, no; tree-care detail: look at the leaves and feel the soil near the roots; water when the soil is dry.

## Verse 23. Rain on the Tree (day 141, cycle 23: what, when, then)

[page 1]
First, when the sky turns gray, we wait and see.
What will the rain bring? A drink for the tree.

Picture: Children stand under a covered porch and look up at a gray sky. A young tree stands in the yard.

[page 2]
Then the drizzle ends, and the sky turns blue.
We touch the soil, and the roots are drinking too.

Picture: The sky clears. A child kneels at the tree and touches dark, wet soil.

Hooks: order words: First (page 1 line 1), Then (page 2 line 1); rhyme pairs see/tree, blue/too; two-syllable word: drizzle (driz-zle, 2 syllables); onset-rime: see (s + ee) and tree (tr + ee); blue (bl + ue) and too (t + oo); mapped words: what, when, then; tree-care detail: rain can water a young tree, so check the soil before adding water.

## Verse 24. Taking Turns (day 146, cycle 24: you, said, were)

[page 1]
We said, "Let us take turns with the can."
First you pour; then I will. That is the plan.

Picture: Two children stand beside a young tree. One holds a watering can; the other waits with open hands.

[page 2]
Then you said, "Look! The soil is dark and wet."
The thirsty roots had a drink; they were all set.

Picture: Both children crouch over the dark, wet soil and smile at each other.

Hooks: order words: First (page 1 line 2), Then (page 1 line 2 and page 2 line 1); rhyme pairs can/plan, wet/set; two-syllable word: thirsty (thirs-ty, 2 syllables); onset-rime: can (c + an) and plan (pl + an); wet (w + et) and set (s + et); mapped words: you, said, were; tree-care detail: water until the soil around the roots is wet; dark, wet soil shows the water has soaked in.

## Verse 25. A Visit to Her Tree (day 151, cycle 25: she, her, like)

[page 1]
She kneels by her tree and looks at its bark.
She likes to visit it from morning to dark.

Picture: A girl kneels beside a young tree and looks closely at its bark. The sky behind her is pale pink.

[page 2]
Then she waters it slowly, a little, then more.
Last, she sits by her tree, like she did before.

Picture: The girl pours water slowly from a can. In the last picture she sits with her back to the trunk.

Hooks: order words: Then (page 2 line 1), Last (page 2 line 2); rhyme pairs bark/dark, more/before; two-syllable word: visit (vis-it, 2 syllables); onset-rime: bark (b + ark) and dark (d + ark); mapped words: she, her, like; tree-care detail: water slowly and visit the tree often so you can notice changes.
