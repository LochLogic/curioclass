# Mira Makes a Road

[page 1]
It was play time in Mira's class. Some children were playing zoo. Jonah roared like a lion. Kiko flapped his arms like a bird. Everyone was laughing.
Picture: A bright classroom. At the left, a group of children of different skin tones and hair play zoo, one roaring on hands and knees and one flapping his arms. At the right, a girl with short dark hair walks toward a shelf of wooden blocks.

[page 2]
Mira did not go to the zoo. Mira went to the block shelf. She picked up a long, flat block and put it down on the rug. Then she picked up another and set it next to the first, end to end.
"I am making a road," she said.
Picture: Mira kneels on the rug and sets one long flat block next to another. A small basket of toy animals and a few little wooden cars sit beside her.

[page 3]
The road grew. It went past the reading chair. It went around the leg of the table. It went across the rug, block after block after block.
Mira hummed a tune with no words. Her tongue poked out of the corner of her mouth, the way it did when she was thinking hard.
Picture: A long road of blocks winds across the rug past a small chair and a table leg. Mira lies on her stomach at the end of it, humming, tongue poking out.

[page 4]
"Mira!" called Kiko. "Come and play zoo! We need a giraffe!"
Mira looked up. "Thank you for asking," she said. "I am making a road right now."
"A road? By yourself?" asked Jonah.
"Yes," said Mira. "I like making it."
Jonah shrugged. "Okay," he said. And back they went to the zoo.
Picture: Kiko and Jonah stand at the edge of the rug, calling to Mira. Mira looks up from her road and holds out one open hand, friendly and sure.

[page 5]
Mira heard the roaring and the laughing. Her hands stopped. She held a block in the air and watched the zoo for a little while.
Then she looked down at her road. There was a gap in it, right in the middle. The gap was waiting.
Mira picked a block. She slid it into the gap. It fit just right. She began to hum again.
Picture: Mira holds a block in the air, looking across the room at the zoo game. A gap shows in the middle of her road in the foreground.

[page 6]
Next came a corner. A long block was too long for the corner. A short block was too short. Mira tried two short blocks side by side. "Yes!" she whispered. The road bent around the corner like a curl.
Then she laid a blue scarf across the rug. "A river," she said. She stacked two blocks on each side and set a flat block on top. "And a bridge."
Picture: A road of blocks bends around a corner. A blue scarf lies across it like a river, with a little block bridge over the scarf.

[page 7]
At last the road was finished. It went all the way across the rug. Mira sat back and folded her hands in her lap. She smiled at her road.
"That is just right," she said. Mira felt content.
Picture: Mira sits with folded hands and a calm smile, looking at the whole long road with its curved corner and its bridge.

[page 8]
Kiko came by, holding a toy lion. His mouth fell open. "Wow," he said. "Your road is so long."
"You can walk on it," said Mira. "I invite you."
Kiko set the lion at the start. Walk, walk, walk went the lion. At the corner it began to march. March, march, march! Then it came to the bridge.
"The river is full of sleepy fish," said Mira. "The lion must tiptoe."
So Kiko made the lion tiptoe, tiptoe, tiptoe all the way across.
Picture: Mira and Kiko kneel on either side of the road. Kiko holds the toy lion on the bridge, lifted on its toes. Mira points at the scarf river.

[page 9]
After a while, Kiko went back to the zoo. Then Jonah came to visit with a toy zebra, and the zebra walked the road, too.
Mira stayed by her road. She picked up one more block and began a new bit at the end. She hummed her tune.
It was a very good play time.
Picture: Jonah sets a toy zebra on the road while Mira lays a new block at the far end. In the background the zoo game goes on and everyone is smiling.
