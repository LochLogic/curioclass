## gk-u2-02-a: The Picnic Plan (314 words)

[page 1]
Picture: A park by a pond, a bright morning. Tomas, a boy with curly dark hair, and Grandma Rosa, in a straw hat, load a wicker basket and a colorful blanket into a wagon.

On Saturday morning, the sky was cloudless and blue. Tomas jumped out of bed. "Today is our picnic!" he said.

Grandma Rosa was already in the kitchen. She packed the basket. She packed sandwiches. She packed grapes and plates and cups. She had precut the apples, and she tucked them in too. Tomas packed the colorful blanket.

[page 2]
Picture: Tomas pulls the wagon along a path toward a park. Grandma Rosa walks beside him. Small white clouds are in the sky.

They walked to the park by the pond. Tomas pulled the wagon. Grandma Rosa carried the jug of lemonade.

"Where shall we sit?" asked Grandma Rosa.

"Under the big tree, by the pond," said Tomas. "I can watch the ducks."

They were still walking when the sky began to change.

[page 3]
Picture: The sky is gray. A first drop lands on Tomas's nose. Grandma Rosa holds out a hand. The pond is dotted with rings.

A gray cloud slid over the sun. The wind began to blow. A drop landed on Tomas's nose. Then another drop landed on his hand.

"Oh no," said Tomas. "It is raining."

"Quick," said Grandma Rosa. "Let us go home." Tomas's shoulders dropped. He had wanted to eat by the pond.

[page 4]
Picture: They hurry home, Grandma holding the jug above the basket and Tomas pulling the wagon. Rain drops pelt the path. A neighbor waves from a doorway.

They hurried back with the wagon. Rain tapped on the path. Rain tapped on the hat. Rain tapped on the lemonade jug. Tomas was wet, and he was sad. His picnic was gone.

[page 5]
Picture: In the living room: the wet coats hang on pegs. The colorful blanket lies on the floor with plates, cups and sandwiches. Rain streaks the window.

Inside, Grandma Rosa had an idea. "We can still have a picnic," she said. "We can have it on the living room floor."

Tomas stopped and thought. "Can we spread the blanket?"

"We can," said Grandma Rosa.

Tomas unpacked the basket. He spread the blanket on the floor. He put out the plates, the cups and the grapes. Grandma Rosa refilled the lemonade.

[page 6]
Picture: Tomas and Grandma Rosa sit on the blanket eating sandwiches, laughing, with rain on the window behind them. A toy duck sits on the blanket.

They ate their sandwiches on the soft blanket. They listened to the rain. Rain pattered on the window, and the sound was cozy. Tomas found his toy duck and set it on the blanket.

"Now the duck can have a picnic too," he said.

Tomas smiled. His shoulders lifted. "This is the best picnic," he said.

Grandma Rosa smiled too.

## gk-u2-02-b: Priya at the Window (313 words)

[page 1]
Picture: A rainy afternoon in a kitchen. Priya, a girl with a long braid, kneels on a chair at a big window with a notebook and a crayon. Her older brother Dev reads on a couch.

It was a rainy afternoon, and Priya could not go outside. She did not feel sad. She had a job. She was the weather observer for the day.

Priya knelt on a chair by the kitchen window. She held her weather notebook and a crayon.

[page 2]
Picture: Close-up of the window: many drops sliding down the glass, a gray sky, a tree bending, a small puddle on the path.

Priya looked. Rain fell in fat drops. The drops slid down the glass. The sky was gray. The tree bent a little in the wind.

Priya listened. Tap, tap, tap! Rain drummed on the roof.

She wrote in her notebook: "Rain falls." Then she drew a picture of the drops.

[page 3]
Picture: Priya and Dev at the window. Priya points at the sky. Dev squints out. The drops on the glass are smaller and farther apart.

After a while, Priya noticed something. "Dev, look!" she said. "The rain is changing."

Dev looked up from his book. "How do you know?" he asked.

"The drops are smaller," said Priya. "They are not as many. The tapping is soft now."

"You are right," said Dev. "What will you write?"

Priya thought. "Rain falls on the green leaves," she said. She wrote it down. Then she added a few more words: "The drops are small.

[page 4]
Picture: The window: the rain has stopped; a patch of blue sky; the sun shining on the wet leaves, which sparkle. Priya grins, Dev stands beside her.

Then the tapping stopped. Priya held her breath.

"Is the rain gone?" she asked.

The gray clouds were thinning. A patch of blue sky showed. The sun peeked out, and the wet leaves began to sparkle.

"The rain stopped!" cried Priya. "The sun came out!

[page 5]
Picture: Priya writing in her notebook at the window, a picture of a bright sun and wet leaves beside the words. Dev gives a thumbs up.

Priya wrote a new line in her notebook: "The rain stops. The sun shines."

She looked at her page. She drew a sun and some sparkling leaves. Then she made a mark at the end of each sentence. A period marks a telling sentence. A question mark ends a question. An exclamation mark shows a strong feeling. Priya used all three that day.

"Can we go out now?" she asked.

"Put on your boots!" said Dev. "The puddles are waiting."

Priya grinned. She had watched the whole change from inside, and she had written down every bit.
