# One Hoop, Two Friends

[page 1]
Noor and Teo ran out to the grass behind the school. It was a bright morning, and their class had one toy for outside play. It was a big red hoop. Just one.
Picture: Two children, a girl with a black braid and a boy with brown curls, run toward a grassy field beside a school. A basket holding one big red hoop sits on the grass.

[page 2]
Noor got to the basket first. She took out the hoop and stood it up on its edge. She gave it a push. Roll, roll, roll! The hoop rolled across the grass, and Noor ran after it.
"Look at it go!" she called.
Picture: Noor, laughing, runs behind the red hoop as it rolls away across the grass. Teo stands near the basket.

[page 3]
Teo watched the hoop roll. He wanted to push it, too. He walked up to Noor. "Can I have a turn?" he asked.
"In a minute," said Noor. Then she pushed the hoop again. Roll, roll, roll!
Picture: Teo, his hand half raised, speaks to Noor, who is already bending to catch the hoop and is looking at it, not at him.

[page 4]
Teo waited. He watched the hoop roll away, and he watched Noor run after it. His arms hung down by his sides. He scuffed his shoe in the grass.
Noor pushed the hoop again. And again. And again.
"It is a long minute," Teo said. But Noor was too far away to hear.
Picture: Teo stands alone in the middle of the page with his arms hanging down and his shoe scuffing the grass. Far behind him, small, Noor chases the hoop.

[page 5]
At last the hoop fell flat on the grass, right by Teo's feet. Noor ran up, out of breath. She picked up the hoop and held it close to her chest.
Then she looked at Teo. Teo was looking at the ground. His mouth was a small, straight line.
Noor stopped smiling. Her tummy felt tight and funny. She looked at the hoop. She looked at Teo again.
Picture: Close view of Noor hugging the hoop to her chest and looking at Teo. Teo looks down at the ground, his mouth a straight line.

[page 6]
"Teo," she said slowly, "did you want a turn?"
Teo looked up. "I asked you," he said. "You said in a minute. It was a long minute."
"I was having so much fun, I forgot," said Noor. "I did not see you waiting."
"I wanted to push it, too," said Teo.
Picture: The two children face each other. Teo has lifted his head. Noor holds the hoop in front of her with both hands, her eyebrows tilted with worry.

[page 7]
Noor thought for a moment. "We have only one hoop," she said. "How can we both have it?"
"We can take turns," said Teo.
"I know!" said Noor. "I will push it ten times. Then you push it ten times. We can count together."
"And then it is my turn?" asked Teo.
"Then it is your turn," said Noor.
Picture: Noor and Teo stand side by side, each with a finger raised. Small number marks one to ten float in the air above them.

[page 8]
Noor stood the hoop on its edge. "One!" they counted, and she gave it a push. Roll! "Two!" Roll! All the way to "Ten!"
Then Noor held out the hoop. "Now it is your turn," she said.
Teo's whole face lit up. He took the hoop and gave it a big push. It rolled across the grass. Then it began to wobble. It turned and turned in a wide circle and fell flat.
"Look at it go!" called Teo. And this time Noor said it with him.
Picture: Teo, grinning, pushes the hoop with both hands. Noor stands beside him with her arms out, cheering. The hoop curls away in a wide circle behind them.

[page 9]
They played all morning. Noor's turn, Teo's turn. Noor's turn, Teo's turn. Each one counted ten, and each one ran after the hoop, and each one waited, too.
When the bell rang, Noor put the hoop back in the basket. "Tomorrow, you go first," she said.
Teo grinned. "And you go next," he said.
Picture: The two friends walk side by side toward the school door, with the basket swinging between them. The red hoop rests on top.
