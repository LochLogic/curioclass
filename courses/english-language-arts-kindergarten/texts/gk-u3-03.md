# A Tree Is a Living Thing

[page 1]
A tree is a living thing! A tree is a living thing!
Picture: A big tree standing alone in a grassy field under the sun, with a child's hand touching its trunk.

[page 2]
Roots take in water and minerals from the ground. Roots take in water and minerals from the ground. (Minerals are tiny bits in the soil that a tree can use to grow.)
Picture: A cutaway picture of the ground: the roots of a tree spread out under the soil, with small blue drops and tiny specks going in.

[page 3]
The trunk is strong, and it carries the water up. The trunk is strong, and it carries the water up.
Picture: The trunk of the tree with arrows going up from the roots toward the branches.

[page 4]
Leaves use sunlight, air and water to make food for the tree. Leaves use sunlight, air and water to make food for the tree.
Picture: Close view of green leaves in sunlight with three arrows pointing in: sun, air, water.

[page 5]
It grows and grows, a little more each year, and reaches up toward the sun. It grows and grows, a little more each year, and reaches up toward the sun.
Picture: Three pictures in a row of the same tree: small, taller, tallest, with the sun above.

[page 6]
A tree is a living thing! A tree is a living thing!
Picture: The tree again with a child, a bird and a squirrel near it; the child's arms are lifted.

## Sources

- Texas A&M Forest Service, How Trees Grow: https://texastreeid.tfs.tamu.edu/content/howTreesGrow/ (checked: 2026-11-19). Fact used: Roots absorb water and nutrients from the soil; the trunk's sapwood carries water and nutrients up to the leaves; leaves combine sunlight, water and air (carbon dioxide, taken in through tiny pores) to make food.

All other content is invented for the page and states no further real-world fact.
