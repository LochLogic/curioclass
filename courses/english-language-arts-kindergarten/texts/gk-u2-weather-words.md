# Weather Words

- text_id: gk-u2-ww
- author: Curio Class
- year: 2026
- kind: literary (rhymed verse with weather facts)
- form: nine short shared verses, each two pages of two lines, teacher-read and tracked in shared print
- word count: 296 in all (verse by verse below; the words read aloud, not the headings, picture notes or hooks)
- use: foundations practice with the weekly read-aloud; children track lines, name letters, rhyme, count syllables and read the cycle's mapped words; not an independent reader
- read on: days 35 (verse 1), 36 (2), 41 (3), 47 (4), 51 (5), 56 (6), 61 (7), 66 (8), 71 (9)
- what each verse carries: an opening word with a clear first letter, one rhyming pair at the line ends (two in most), one two-syllable object, three-sound words, an onset-rime pair actually in the verse, the cycle's mapped words in the line, and one true weather detail

Every verse follows the nine prompts of the foundation sequence (section U6). Three-sound words listed avoid /l/, /r/ and /x/ endings. Mapped words listed are those the source cycles map by that day: he, we (cycle 5, days 35 to 40); on, to (cycle 6); and, of (cycle 7); up, do (cycle 8); a, my (cycle 9); you, are (cycle 10); see, does (cycle 11); plus the, I and in from earlier cycles. Words beyond these are heard, never decoded; children are not asked to guess them.

## Verse 1. Hot Sun (day 35, cycle 5: v s)

[page 1]
The sun is up. The sun is hot.
We sit in the shade, in the cool, cool spot.

Picture: A sunny playground. Three children of different skin tones sit in the shade of a big tree. Their friend Sam stands at the edge of the shade.

[page 2]
Sam's sunhat is big and red.
He puts it on his head.

Picture: Sam, a boy with brown skin and curly hair, puts a floppy red sunhat on his head.

Hooks: rhyme pairs hot/spot, red/head; two-syllable object sunhat (sun-hat); three-sound words sun, hot, sit, red, hat; onset-rime hot to spot (add /s/ to the front of the rime /ot/), red/head; mapped he, we; weather detail the sun is hot, shade is cooler.

## Verse 2. Rain (day 36, cycle 5: v s)

[page 1]
Clouds are gray, and the wind goes by.
Rain falls down from the sky.

Picture: A gray sky over rooftops. Rain falls from a cloud. Treetops lean in the wind.

[page 2]
Sam hops in the puddles. He gets wet.
We splash in our boots. We are not dry yet.

Picture: Sam and two friends in rain boots jump in a street puddle, water flying up.

Hooks: rhyme pairs by/sky, wet/yet; two-syllable object puddle (pud-dle); three-sound words wet, hop, Sam; onset-rime wet to yet (change /w/ to /y/; /et/ stays); mapped he, we; weather detail rain falls from clouds.

## Verse 3. Snow (day 41, cycle 6: g i)

[page 1]
Snow falls on the grass and falls on the street.
The air is cold, so we stamp our feet.

Picture: A snowy street. A girl in a puffy coat stamps her boots. A father pulls a wagon.

[page 2]
I pull a mitten on, left and right.
We go to dig. The snow is white.

Picture: A child pulls a red mitten onto one hand. Behind, children dig in a pile of snow with plastic shovels.

Hooks: rhyme pairs street/feet, right/white; two-syllable object mitten (mit-ten); three-sound words dig (pull ends in /l/, so it is not used for isolation); onset-rime right to white (change /r/ to /w/; /ite/ stays); mapped on, to; weather detail snow falls when the air is cold.

## Verse 4. Wind (day 47, cycle 7: d f l)

[page 1]
Wind is air that moves along.
It tugs a flag and hums a song.

Picture: A windy park. Leaves swirl. A flag flaps on a pole.

[page 2]
A ribbon of red and a kite of green
flap in the wind. What a lovely scene!

Picture: Two children hold a green kite string while a red ribbon tied to the kite flutters. Their dad stands behind them, laughing.

Hooks: rhyme pairs along/song, green/scene; two-syllable object ribbon (rib-bon); three-sound words red, tug; onset-rime flag to flap (change the last sound /g/ to /p/; /fla/ stays); mapped and, of; weather detail wind is moving air that pushes things.

## Verse 5. Fog (day 51, cycle 7: d f l)

[page 1]
Fog is a cloud that sits on the ground.
Dad drives slow, and there is no sound.

Picture: A foggy road at dawn. A car with its lights on creeps along. Gray fog hides the trees.

[page 2]
The fog is a blanket of gray.
It hid the hills and the town away.

Picture: A town and low hills almost hidden inside soft gray fog.

Hooks: rhyme pairs ground/sound, gray/away; two-syllable object blanket (blan-ket); three-sound words Dad, hid, fog; onset-rime fog to dog not in text, so use hid/hills (h-id, h-ills); mapped and, of; weather detail fog is a cloud near the ground and it hides things.

## Verse 6. Thunder (day 56, cycle 8: k y)

[page 1]
A flash in the sky, then a rumble and boom.
The thunder is loud, so we stay in the room.

Picture: A stormy sky with one white flash. Rain on a window. Children inside.

[page 2]
What do we do? Kim and I look up high
from the window as the storm goes by.

Picture: Kim, a girl with black braids, and her friend look out of a window at the dark clouds. Their grown-up sits close.

Hooks: rhyme pairs boom/room, high/by; two-syllable object window (win-dow); three-sound words Kim, room (r-oo-m, heard only; /m/ ends it), boom; onset-rime boom to room (change /b/ to /r/; /oom/ stays); mapped do, up, we; weather detail lightning flashes then thunder booms; stay inside when you hear thunder.

## Verse 7. Rainbow (day 61, cycle 9: q u x)

[page 1]
The rain is done. The sun comes through.
Quick, look up! There is red and blue.

Picture: A wet street at the end of a shower. Sun breaks through clouds. A child points up.

[page 2]
A rainbow is sun shining through the drops.
My rainbow fades when the sunlight stops.

Picture: A rainbow over a neighborhood, with a boy and his big sister looking up.

Hooks: rhyme pairs through/blue, drops/stops; two-syllable objects rainbow (rain-bow), sunlight (sun-light); three-sound words sun, red, and quick as a four-sound heard word to leave alone; onset-rime drops/stops (dr-ops, st-ops); mapped a, my, and earlier on, to; weather detail a rainbow shows when the sun shines through raindrops.

## Verse 8. Dressed for Weather (day 66, cycle 10: b o w)

[page 1]
On a cold day, a sweater and a coat,
and a scarf that wraps up your neck and throat.

Picture: A child in a green sweater, blue coat and yellow scarf stands in cold air with puffs of breath showing.

[page 2]
Rain boots on! Go splash and hop.
You are dry from toe to top.

Picture: A child in bright rain boots jumps into a puddle. The rain falls.

Hooks: rhyme pairs coat/throat, hop/top; two-syllable object sweater (sweat-er); three-sound words hop, top, dry is not (d-r-y); onset-rime hop to top (change /h/ to /t/; /op/ stays); mapped you, are, on, to; weather detail you dress for the weather: coat for cold, boots for rain.

## Verse 9. Look Out and See (day 71, cycle 11: j e z)

[page 1]
Jed looks out the window. What does he see?
Sun and a cloud, and a bending tree.

Picture: Jed, a boy with a wheelchair and a big smile, looks out a window at a sunny sky with one cloud and a tree bending in the wind.

[page 2]
The weather does change from day to day.
Zip up your jacket, or run out to play!

Picture: Three small windows in a row: a sunny day, a rainy day, a windy day. A child zips a jacket in front of the last one.

Hooks: rhyme pairs see/tree, day/play; two-syllable objects window (win-dow), jacket (jack-et); three-sound words Jed, zip, sun; onset-rime day/play (d-ay, pl-ay); mapped see, does, he, to, and; weather detail weather changes from day to day.

Teacher notes: the verses are shared print, not decodable text. Mapped words are the only words children read by mapping; every other word is heard. "Hot sun" is a safety touch (shade) with no medical claim. Verse 9 pictures a child in a wheelchair at a window, and verse 6 a grown-up beside children; no verse mentions a frightening storm event. Weather words children can use later: cloud, wind, rain, snow, fog, thunder, rainbow.
