# What a Tree Needs (Living Things, part b)

[page 1]
Is a tree a living thing? Yes, it is.
Living things need some things to stay alive. A tree needs water. A tree needs air. A tree needs light from the sun. A tree needs soil to stand in.

Picture: A chart with four boxes, each with a drawing: a raindrop (water), a puff of wind (air), a sun (light), a handful of soil.

[page 2]
First, the tree needs water.
Rain falls on the ground. The rain sinks into the soil.
A tree has roots under the ground. The roots spread out in the soil. The roots take in the water. Roots also take in minerals, which are tiny bits that come from the soil.
Roots do one more job. They hold the tree in place.
The tree needs water, so its roots take water from the soil.

Picture: A cutaway of a tree. Rain falls on the grass. Blue arrows go from the soil into the roots, then up the trunk.

[page 3]
Next, the water goes up.
It goes up the trunk and into the leaves.
The leaves take in air. The leaves catch light from the sun. Then the leaves use the water, the air and the light to make food for the tree.
A tree does not eat like we do. A tree makes its own food in its leaves. That is why a tree needs air and light. Without them, the leaves could not make food.
Soil is not the tree's food.

Picture: A close view of a green leaf in the sun. Arrows labeled "air" and "light" point into the leaf. An arrow labeled "water" comes up from below.

[page 4]
Then the tree uses its food to grow.
After one year, a tree is a little bit taller. After many years, a tree is much taller. Its trunk grows wider, too. Every spring, new leaves grow.
A tree grows slowly. We cannot watch it grow in one day. But we can look at old photos, and we can see how much it grew.

Picture: Three small drawings in a row of the same tree: a young thin tree, a taller tree, and a big tree with a wide trunk. They are labeled "one year," "five years," and "many years."

[page 5]
A tree also makes seeds. A seed can grow into a new tree.
So a tree is alive because of what it needs and what it does. It needs water, air and light. It makes its own food. It grows. It makes seeds.
Now think about a toy truck. A truck does not need water. A truck does not need air or light. A truck does not make food. A truck does not grow. It does not make new trucks. A truck is not living. A rock is not living, either.

Picture: On the left, a tree with a seed on the ground below it. On the right, a toy truck and a rock sit side by side. A checkmark is on the tree and a cross is on the truck and rock.

[page 6]
A truck can move when we push it, but it is not living. A tree stays in one place, but it is living. Moving is not the way to tell.
Now we can join two facts. Roots take in water. So the leaves can use the water to make food. So the water that roots take in helps the whole tree grow.
A tree needs water, air, light and soil. But who needs the tree? That is our next question.

Picture: A tree with arrows linking roots, leaves and trunk. Two children point at the tree and talk.
