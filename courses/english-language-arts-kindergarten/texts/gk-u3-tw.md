# Tree Words

- text_id: gk-u3-tw
- author: Curio Class
- year: 2026
- kind: literary (rhymed verse with tree facts)
- form: seven short shared verses, each two pages of two lines, teacher-read and tracked in shared print; actions for children to join
- word count: 214 in all (verse by verse below; the words read aloud, not the headings, picture notes or hooks)
- use: foundations practice with the weekly read-aloud; children join the actions, find the first line in print, track words, line and page, find the one rhyming pair, clap the one two-syllable word, and read the cycle's mapped words; never an independent reader and never scored as decoding
- read on: days 80 (verse 1), 85 (2), 91 (3), 96 (4), 102 (5), 107 (6), 112 (7); each is reread until the next first hearing; on assessment dates the adult reads without introducing a new decoding pattern
- what each verse carries: an opening word with a clear first letter, exactly one rhyming pair at line ends, exactly one two-syllable object (all other words are one syllable), the cycle's mapped words, an action sequence, and one true tree fact
- kept out on purpose: pen, pin, bed, sun and the F1 passage words (Ben, hut, pot, mud, red, cup, hid); also the exit and card nouns net, bug, Jed, Sam, bag

Mapped words listed are those the cycle maps by that day (to, do; at, has; is, it; was, his; or, for; be, by; are, with, will), plus the, a, in, I, you and see from earlier cycles. Every other word is heard, never decoded; children are not asked to guess them.

## Verse 1. Seed to Tree (day 80)

[page 1]
Curl up small like a seed in the ground.
Do roots take in rain? Yes, deep they go.

[page 2]
Reach to the light and stand up tall.
An acorn drops, so plump and small.

Picture: Page 1: a child curled small beside a patch of soil with rain falling and thin roots going down. Page 2: an oak tree with an acorn dropping from a branch.

Actions: crouch like a seed; wiggle fingers downward for roots; rise and stretch tall; drop one hand like an acorn.

Hooks: rhyming pair tall / small; two-syllable object acorn (a-corn): the nut that grows on an oak tree; mapped words to, do; opening word Curl; fact: roots take in water from wet soil; a tree reaches for light; oaks make acorns.

## Verse 2. Branches (day 85)

[page 1]
Hold your arm out, strong and wide.
A branch grows out on each side.

[page 2]
At the top, a robin has a nest.
Sway, sway, sway in the wind.

Picture: Page 1: a tree trunk with a branch growing out on the left and on the right, a child's arm held out in the same way. Page 2: a robin sitting in a nest in the fork near the top of the tree, branches swaying.

Actions: hold one arm out like a branch, then the other; sway side to side.

Hooks: rhyming pair wide / side; two-syllable object robin (rob-in): the brown bird with an orange chest that builds its nest in a tree; mapped words at, has; opening word Hold; fact: a tree has branches; robins build nests in trees, often on a branch or in a fork.

## Verse 3. Leaf Fall (day 91)

[page 1]
Wave your hands like leaves in the air.
Is it fall? Yes, it is.

[page 2]
A maple leaf let go of its twig.
It floats down soft, so gold and big.

Picture: Page 1: a child waving both hands among falling leaves. Page 2: a close picture of one gold maple leaf drifting down from a bare twig.

Actions: wave hands overhead; flutter fingers downward like a falling leaf.

Hooks: rhyming pair twig / big; two-syllable object maple (ma-ple): the tree whose leaves turn gold in fall; mapped words is, it; opening word Wave; fact: maple leaves change color in fall and then fall from the tree.

## Verse 4. The Squirrel (day 96)

[page 1]
A squirrel was up in a big oak tree.
His tail went flick, flick, flick.

[page 2]
Up the trunk with a nut, run, run, run!
Hop on the limb. It is fun.

Picture: Page 1: a gray squirrel high in an oak tree, tail curled in a flick. Page 2: the squirrel running up the trunk with a nut, then standing on a limb.

Actions: flick a hand behind you for a tail; run two fingers up your arm; hop in place.

Hooks: rhyming pair run / fun; two-syllable object squirrel (squir-rel): the small gray animal that climbs trees and carries nuts; mapped words was, his; opening word A; fact: squirrels climb trees and eat and carry acorns and other nuts.

## Verse 5. Apple Tree (day 102)

[page 1]
Stand like an apple tree, still and tall.
The fruit is food for a bird or a deer.

[page 2]
Hop, hop, hop, hop!
Reach up high to the top.

Picture: Page 1: an apple tree with fruit, a bird on a branch and a deer under it. Page 2: children hopping in a ring and reaching up toward the top of the tree.

Actions: stand still with arms out as branches; hop four times; reach high.

Hooks: rhyming pair hop / top; two-syllable object apple (ap-ple): the fruit that grows on an apple tree; mapped words or, for; opening word Stand; fact: apple trees grow fruit; birds and deer eat apples.

## Verse 6. Shade (day 107)

[page 1]
Sit by a tree on a hot, hot day.
See the shadow it makes on the ground.

[page 2]
Sit and be still. Take a rest.
A tree in the shade is the best.

Picture: Page 1: a child sitting by a tree trunk, the tree's dark shadow stretching across the grass. Page 2: the child resting in the cool shade with eyes closed.

Actions: sit; make a shadow shape with both arms; sit still and breathe slowly.

Hooks: rhyming pair rest / best; two-syllable object shadow (shad-ow): the dark shape a tree makes on the ground; mapped words be, by; opening word Sit; fact: shade under a tree is cooler than full sun.

## Verse 7. Grow (day 112)

[page 1]
You are a seed. Curl up tight.
A sapling will grow with rain and light.

[page 2]
Now you are tall. Reach up, up, up!
A bird will rest with you. Chirp, chirp, chirp!

Picture: Page 1: a seed in soil, then a thin sapling pushing up toward light and rain. Page 2: a tall tree with a bird perched on a branch.

Actions: curl up; rise slowly; reach up; chirp together.

Hooks: rhyming pair tight / light; two-syllable object sapling (sap-ling): a young tree; mapped words are, with, will; opening word You; fact: a young tree needs water and light to grow; birds rest in trees.
