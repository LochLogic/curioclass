[page 1]
This page is about an oak.
An oak is a big tree. It has a thick trunk and wide branches. Its leaves are green in summer. An oak can stand for hundreds of years.

Picture: A wide old oak in a meadow, with a thick trunk and spreading branches. Two children of different heights stand beside the trunk, one in a wheelchair, looking up.

[page 2]
An oak grows acorns.
An acorn is a nut with a little cap. It is the oak's seed. In the fall, acorns drop to the ground. An acorn that lands in good soil can grow into a new oak.

Picture: A close view of an acorn on a twig, then three acorns on the grass with their caps beside them. In the corner, a tiny oak sprout pokes up from the soil.

[page 3]
Animals eat acorns. A squirrel eats acorns. It holds an acorn in its paws and nibbles. A jay eats acorns from the oak, too. So acorns are food for animals. A food that an animal needs is a resource.

Picture: A gray squirrel sits on a branch holding an acorn. Below it, a blue jay stands on a limb with an acorn in its beak.

[page 4]
An oak needs water, too. Its roots take water from the soil. The roots hold the tree in place.

Picture: A cutaway of an oak, with roots spreading in brown soil. Blue arrows go from the soil into the roots.

[page 5]
Birds rest on its branches. Some birds build nests in the oak. The branches and leaves give shelter. Shelter keeps a bird and its nest safe from wind and sun.

An oak is a habitat. A habitat is a place where animals live and find what they need. The oak gives food and shelter.

Picture: A songbird sits on a nest in a fork of the oak's branches. Leaves shade the nest. A squirrel peeks out from a hole in the trunk.

[page 6]
People use oak, too. Oak wood is hard and strong. People make chairs and tables from it.

So an oak gives acorns to squirrels and jays, shelter to birds, and wood to people. One tree is a resource for many.

Picture: A wooden chair and a table in a bright room. A girl and her grandfather sit at the table.
