[page 1]
Today a school is planting a young tree. Adults and children will plant it together on the school grounds. Planting has steps, and they go in order.
The tree grew at a nursery. A nursery is a place where young trees are grown. This tree comes in a big pot.

Picture: A young tree in a large black pot stands on a school lawn. A group of adults and children of many ages gather around it. A school building is behind them.

[page 2]
First, choose a good place. An adult checks the spot. Does it get sunlight? Is there room for the tree to grow tall?
A tree planted in a good place can grow well.

Picture: An adult in a vest and a child stand on the lawn. The adult points at the open sky and the empty space. The child looks up.

[page 3]
Next, an adult digs the hole. The hole is wider than the tree's roots. Because the hole is wide, the roots have room to spread out.
Children stay back and watch while the adult digs.

Picture: An adult digs with a shovel. A pile of soil sits beside the hole. A group of children stands a few steps away, watching.

[page 4]
Then the adult lifts the young tree out of its pot and sets it in the hole. The bottom of the trunk is not buried. The tree stands straight.

Picture: Two adults lower a young tree into the hole. The empty pot lies on its side on the grass. A child holds up a thumb.

[page 5]
After that, everyone fills the hole with soil, a little at a time. Children pat the soil gently with their hands. This presses the soil around the roots so the tree stays steady.

Picture: Three children kneel around the tree and pat the soil with their hands. An adult holds the trunk steady.

[page 6]
Next, children carry water in watering cans. They pour it slowly around the tree. The water soaks down to the roots. It also settles the soil.

Picture: Children carry watering cans and pour water around the base of the young tree. The soil is dark where the water has soaked in.

[page 7]
Then an adult spreads mulch in a circle around the tree. Mulch is a layer of wood chips. It helps the soil stay damp. The adult keeps the mulch back from the trunk, so the bark can stay dry.

Picture: An adult spreads pale wood chips with a rake in a ring around the tree. A gap shows between the chips and the trunk.

[page 8]
Last, people keep watering the new tree again and again during the first weeks, until the roots grow strong.
Some classes plant a small tree in a big pot, with an adult's help and the same steps in the same order.

Picture: Children and an adult water the young tree. Behind them, a second class waters a small tree in a big pot at the classroom door.
