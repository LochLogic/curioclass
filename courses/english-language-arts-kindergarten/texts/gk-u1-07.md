# The Magic Bow

[page 1]
Once upon a time, a little old woman had a magic bow. Anyone who held it could learn something amazing that they had never learned before.
Picture: A little old woman sits in a doorway with a long curved bow resting across her knees. No arrow is shown.

[page 2]
A child could hold the bow and think hard about a wish. One wished to climb a tree. One wished to play a drum. One wished to read every word in the world. And each one learned it!
Picture: Three children in a row: one standing at the foot of a tree, one with a drum, one with an open book. The bow rests on a stool in the middle.

[page 3]
People came from far and near to borrow the bow. The woman was happy to share it with anyone who asked kindly.
Picture: A line of people walking up a path to the woman's house. The bow lies on her lap.

[page 4]
But one day, someone took the bow. Nobody asked. The bow was gone.
Picture: The empty stool in the doorway, with a dent in the cushion where the bow had rested.

[page 5]
The people were sad. "Now no one will ever learn anything amazing again," they said.
Picture: A group of people with their heads down outside the woman's house.

[page 6]
But the old woman knew better. "It was never the bow," she said. "You learned because you tried. You learned because you believed in yourself."
Picture: The woman speaks gently to the group, her hands open. A child looks up at her.

[page 7]
Then she gave them a little poem to help them remember.
Choose the thing you want to learn.
Believe in you, and take your turn.
Try and try, and try some more.
You can learn it. That is what trying is for.
Picture: The woman and the people sit together in a circle, listening; one child counts the lines on fingers.

[page 8]
The people went home. They kept on trying, and they asked each other for help. And they learned amazing things.
Picture: A child in a classroom stacking blocks, with the bow resting beside the child on the floor and a friend pointing to help.
