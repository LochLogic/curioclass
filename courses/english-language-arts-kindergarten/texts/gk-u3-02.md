# What's Alive and What's Not?

[page 1]
A child sits by a tree with a toy truck and wonders about living things. A line of tiny ants walks by.
Picture: A child sits on the grass under a tree with a small toy truck. A line of tiny ants crosses the ground near the child's shoes.

[page 2]
The child pushes the truck. It rolls along the ground. The ants move too, but nobody pushes them. The child wonders, "Are the ants alive?"
Picture: The child's hand on the truck; the ants walking past. A thought bubble with a question mark.

[page 3]
The child thinks, "I am alive. I eat and drink. I breathe air. I grow and change." Ants eat and breathe, too. Ants grow from tiny eggs into grown-up ants. The ants are alive!
Picture: The child pointing to the child's own chest, then a close-up of an ant on a leaf.

[page 4]
What about the swing and the bench? They do not eat or drink. They do not breathe. They do not grow. The swing and the bench are not alive. The truck is not alive either. It moves only when someone pushes it.
Picture: A playground swing and a bench next to the tree; the child looks at them.

[page 5]
What about the tree? The tree does not run or hop. It stays in one place. But a tree takes in water and air, and it uses sunlight to make food. It grows taller. The tree is alive!
Picture: The child looks up at the tree; arrows show water going in at the roots and sunlight on the leaves.

[page 6]
The grass and the flowers are plants. Plants take in water and air, and they grow. Plants are alive, too.
Picture: Grass and a few flowers near the tree; the child touches a flower.

[page 7]
Now the child can explain. Living things need food, water and air. They grow and change. Moving is not the way to tell. A truck moves, and it is not alive. A tree stays still, and it is alive.
Picture: The child, the truck, the ants and the tree together, with the child smiling.
