## gk-u2-01-a: Weather Changes (329 words)

[page 1]
Picture: Two children at a window. On the left, a sunny yard with kids in sun hats. On the right, the same yard in rain, kids in boots and hoods.

Weather is what the air outside is doing today. Look up at the sky. Look at the trees. Is the sky bright and clear? Are clouds gathering? Is wind pushing the leaves?

Weather changes. It can change from one day to the next. Sometimes it changes in one afternoon.

[page 2]
Picture: A sunny playground: a girl in a sun hat swings, a boy with a kite runs, a grandfather sits on a bench in the shade.

On a sunny day, children play outside. They run. They swing. They wear sun hats because the sun is bright. They sit in the shade when they feel hot.

[page 3]
Picture: The same playground in rain: puddles, a boy in boots stomps, a girl holds an umbrella, others wave from a doorway.

On a rainy day, the playground looks different. Rain falls in drops. Puddles gather on the ground. Children wear boots and open umbrellas. Some children play inside. They choose a different game because of the rain.

Weather changes what people wear. Weather changes what people do.

[page 4]
Picture: A weather observer, a woman with a notebook, stands on a porch and looks up at the sky while a boy beside her points.

Some people watch the weather as their job. They are called weather observers. A weather observer looks at the sky. A weather observer listens for wind. A weather observer feels the air and writes down what happens.

You can be a weather observer too. Look. Listen. Feel. Then tell what you notice.

[page 5]
Picture: A map of the world with five pins; a child holds a magnifying glass over one.

Weather is not the same in every place. Let us visit three places and see.

[page 6]
Picture: A village on green hills in heavy rain. People walk with umbrellas and rain hats. Water runs down the road.

This is Mawsynram, in India. It is one of the rainiest places on Earth. A rainy season comes every year, and then rain falls and falls. People there plan for the rain. They carry rain gear when they go out.

[page 7]
Picture: A wide dry desert under a bright sky: sand, rocks, far mountains, no clouds, no plants nearby.

This is the Atacama Desert, in Chile. It is one of the driest places on Earth. In some parts, rain does not fall for years. The ground is dry. The sky is often clear.

[page 8]
Picture: A bare mountaintop station with a tall tower. A flag stands straight out in the wind. Two people in thick coats lean into the wind near a door.

This is Mount Washington, in New Hampshire, in the United States. It is very windy at the top. Weather observers live there. They stay in a weather station and watch the weather day and night. They go outside in thick coats to check their tools.

Rain. Dry air. Wind. Each place has its own weather, and people in each place get ready for it.

## gk-u2-01-b: Warmer and Cooler (199 words)

[page 1]
Picture: A child in a sunny yard in a light shirt, then the same child at dusk putting on a sweater. A sun sinks behind the roofs.

Weather can be warm. Weather can be cool. How warm or cool the air is has a name. It is called temperature.

Temperature tells how warm or how cool something is. We can feel it with our skin.

[page 2]
Picture: Noon: a girl with her sleeves rolled up carries a water bottle across a bright park.

At noon, the sun is high and bright. Sunlight warms the air. The air feels warmer. People wear light shirts. They drink water. They look for shade.

[page 3]
Picture: Dusk: the same girl with a sweater and a scarf walks home. Long shadows. The sun low on the hills.

When the sun goes down, the sunlight goes away. The air feels cooler. Cooler means less warm. People pull on sweaters. They wrap scarves around their necks. They go inside where it is snug.

The air is warmer in the afternoon. The air is cooler at night. The weather has changed, and so have the clothes.

[page 4]
Picture: A very snowy town street at midday, dim light, a pale sun low over rooftops. People in big coats, hats and boots.

Some places have cool air for a very long time. This is Utqiagvik, in Alaska, in the United States. In deep winter, the sun stays below the edge of the land for many weeks. There is very little sunlight, and the air is very cold. People wear thick coats, hats, mittens and boots. Then the sun comes back, a little at a time, and the days grow brighter.

Warmer air. Cooler air. The sun makes a difference.

## gk-u2-01-c: Rain and Snow (244 words)

[page 1]
Picture: A gray sky over a town. Two children at a window, one holding a notebook, one pointing out.

Rain and snow both fall from clouds. Clouds hold tiny drops of water and tiny bits of ice. When the drops and bits grow heavy, they fall.

[page 2]
Picture: Rain falling on a street. Drops bounce in puddles. A girl in a yellow raincoat holds out her hand. Hats hang on pegs; two bowls on a porch collect rain.

Rain falls as drops of water. Drops tap the roof. Drops splash in the puddles. Drops fill the dishes on the porch. One drop is small. Many drops make a puddle. Rain can wet your hat and your boots.

When the rain stops, the sun can dry the ground. A puddle can shrink. Then more rain comes and refills it.

[page 3]
Picture: Snow falling on a quiet street. Flakes drift past a lamp. A boy catches a flake on a mitten. Two hats and two scarves on hooks by a door.

Snow falls as flakes. Snowflakes are made of ice. The flakes land softly on the roofs, the trees and the ground. Many flakes pile up and cover the ground in white. Snow is cold to touch.

When the air near the ground is cold, snow can fall. When the air is not cold enough, the flakes melt on the way down and become rain.

[page 4]
Picture: A windy snowy street: flakes fly sideways, a flag stands out straight, two people lean on a porch rail.

Wind can push snowflakes sideways. Then the air looks full of white. It is hard to see far.

Rain is drops of water. Snow is flakes of ice. Both are wet. Both fall from clouds. Only snow is made of ice.

A weather observer named Ben looks out the window. "Snow is coming," says Ben. His partner asks, "How do you know?" Ben says, "The clouds are gray. The air feels cold. Ice is in the clouds when the air is cold." Ben has noticed three signs, and the signs support his words.

## gk-u2-01-d: Clouds (248 words)

[page 1]
Picture: A wide sky with many kinds of clouds: puffy white, flat gray, thin and wispy. Children lie on a hill and look up.

Look up. What do you see? Clouds!

A cloud is made of tiny drops of water, tiny bits of ice, or both. The drops and bits are so small that they float in the air.

[page 2]
Picture: A pond with a little steam rising in morning light. A simple picture: water rising as invisible air, then small drops gathering into a cloud above.

Where do clouds come from? Water is all around us. Some water is in the air, and we cannot see it. When this water in the air gets cool, it changes back into tiny drops. Many tiny drops together make a cloud.

[page 3]
Picture: Three small pictures: a tall puffy cloud, a flat gray layer, a thin wispy streak.

Clouds are not all the same. Some clouds are tall and puffy. Some are flat and spread across the sky. Some are thin and wispy. Clouds can be white. They can be gray too.

[page 4]
Picture: A dark gray sky, rain falling from the cloud over a school and a hill. Two children with umbrellas.

Rain and snow come from clouds. When the drops or ice in a cloud grow heavy, they fall. That is why people look at the clouds. A cloud can tell us weather may be coming.

[page 5]
Picture: A long red suspension bridge half hidden in thick fog; a ferry boat; a small cloud-like fog bank low over the water; two cyclists with scarves.

There is a cloud that sits low near the ground. It is called fog. In San Francisco, in California, fog often comes in from the ocean in summer. The ocean air is cool. Fog forms in the cool air. Then it drifts in through the gap by the big bridge.

Who watches the clouds? Weather observers do. Where do they look? They look up at the sky. When do they look? Every day. Why do they look? To learn what the weather may do. How do they look? With their eyes, and with their notebooks.

## gk-u2-01-e: Rainbows (247 words)

[page 1]
Picture: A girl on a hill in light rain with the sun low behind her. A rainbow arches over a faraway town.

Sometimes sun shines and rain falls at the same time. Look around. You may see something special. A rainbow!

[page 2]
Picture: A diagram-like picture: sun at the left behind the girl, her shadow, raindrops in front, the rainbow in the air in front of her.

A rainbow needs two things. It needs sunshine. It needs raindrops in the air. Sunlight looks white, but it has many colors hidden inside it. Each raindrop is round and clear. A clear drop lets light pass through. The drop bends the light, and the colors spread apart. Together, millions of drops make a rainbow.

[page 3]
Picture: A close picture of one raindrop on a leaf with colored light inside, beside the arc of a rainbow with red on the outer edge and blue on the inner edge.

To see a rainbow, stand with the sun behind you. The rain must be in front of you. If the sun is too high in the sky, you will not see a rainbow. A rainbow is an arch, and its colors always come in the same order. Red is on the outside. Blue is on the inside. In between are orange, yellow and green.

[page 4]
Picture: A scene: a girl stands on the hill, the sun behind her. A boy sits on the fence beside her. A dog lies under the tree. A small boat floats under the bridge. A bag sits on the bench. Rain falls over the town in front of them and a rainbow arches above it.

Here is a rainbow scene. A girl stands on the hill. The sun is behind her. A boy sits on the fence beside her. A dog rests under the tree. A bag is on the bench. A small boat floats under the bridge. In front of them, rain falls over the town, and a rainbow stretches above the roofs.

After a rain, the sky may turn clear. The word clear can mean "with no clouds." The sky is clear. The word clear can also mean "easy to see through." A drop of rain is clear. Two meanings, one word.

Rain, sun, and a bow of color in the sky.
