# Attributes of Toys

[page 1]
Toys come in many colors. Look at the building bricks. What colors do you see?
Picture: A small pile of plastic building bricks in red, blue, yellow and green on a classroom table.

[page 2]
Some colors are light and some are dark. Some are bright. Some toys have a pattern, like dots, stripes or checks.
Picture: Three classroom toys side by side: a light yellow ball, a dark blue cart, and a doll's bright striped scarf.

[page 3]
Toys come in many sizes. Some toys are large and some are small. A very small toy is tiny. Look at the dollhouse. It has many tiny pieces inside.
Picture: A classroom toy dollhouse with a tiny table, tiny chairs and a tiny bed inside.

[page 4]
A very large toy is gigantic. Look at the teddy bear. It is as tall as the door. It is a gigantic toy!
Picture: A very large teddy bear sitting next to a classroom door, its head level with the top of the door; a child stands beside it, small by comparison.

[page 5]
Toys come in many shapes. Look at the blocks. What shapes do you see?
Picture: A heap of wooden blocks: cubes, long rectangles, triangles and round cylinders.

[page 6]
Some toys are made of many shapes. Look at the truck. Can you find circles and rectangles? How many shapes can you find?
Picture: A simple wooden toy truck: round wheels, a rectangle body, a square cab.

[page 7]
Toys feel different, too. Texture is how something feels. A toy can feel smooth or rough. It can feel soft or hard. It can feel squishy or bumpy.
Picture: Four toys in a row, each with a plain label word beside it: a smooth wooden bead, a rough rope toy, a soft stuffed rabbit, a bumpy brick.

[page 8]
How do you think play dough feels? How do you think building bricks feel?
Picture: A child's hands pressing a ball of play dough, with two building bricks beside it.

[page 9]
We can describe toys in many ways. Look closely at color, size, shape and texture, and you will be a toy expert!
Picture: A child holding up one toy and looking closely at it, with a row of other classroom toys on the shelf behind.
