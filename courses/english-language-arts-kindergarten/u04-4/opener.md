# Section 4.4 opener: a planting opinion and a true account

## What the section does

Days 133 to 136 start with the child's own opinion about where to plant a tree, with the class pot or the school grounds as the only choices. Then the class hears the true account of Wangari Maathai, who led women in Kenya to grow and plant trees, names its people and place, returns to the organizing and planting pages for stated reasons, and connects actions to results. Foundations finish cycle 21 on day 134 (check) and start cycle 22 on day 135.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit. The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the reader and the rotation. A child outside the six heard is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read the account; the teacher reads every page aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **An opinion names a choice and a preference.** A reason the writer gave is labeled "the writer says"; a child's own reason is said as their own.
- **A real person and a real place are named plainly.** The account says what women did together: they grew seedlings in nurseries and planted them side by side.
- **A question asks about a detail or what a word means.**
- **An action and its result are joined in the right order.** The seedlings are grown, then planted.
- **No people or culture is ranked above another.** The account only says what happened.

## The errors, in plain words

- **F, C:** a choice with no preference, or a liking given as the writer's reason.
- **W, X:** no question about a word or a detail.
- **AC, AD, AE:** a detail named as the topic, no details retold, or an action and result reversed.
- **V:** a claim the account does not make, such as an event it never tells.
- **AL to AO:** foundations slips from the day 134 check.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion; there is no yard, only the class pot or the school grounds. The account names no violence.

## Supplies

The true account in print and its original map and work scene, the community text, purpose cards, the box and arrows mat, statement cards, a time strip, the cycle-21 and cycle-22 readers, letter tiles and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Read from print. Draw the map by hand on chart paper, plainly, with Kenya and Nairobi marked. Make the cards from scrap paper and say each invented card is made up for practice.
