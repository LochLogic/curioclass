# Capital and mark strips

Strips for day 56, printed large by the teacher on sentence strips, one sentence a strip, read aloud by the teacher. Children do not read the words; they find the first letter and the mark at the end, and they copy the gestures.

## The model strips

- Rain falls.
- The rain stops. The sun shines.
- Can we go out now?
- The sun came out!

Beside each, a copy with the first letter lowercase and no end mark ("rain falls") so the class can see what is missing. A puppet says the wrong copy; the teacher shows the right one.

## The three marks

Three large mark cards, one mark each, with a gesture:

- A period: pat the air. It tells.
- A question mark: raise a hand. It asks.
- An exclamation mark: arms up. A strong feeling.

## The capital I

One strip with the word I large and in a different color wherever it stands alone: "Then I see the sun."

## How to use them

The teacher reads each strip, children point to the first letter and then to the mark, and say what the mark tells. Children copy the gesture for each mark. No strip used here is an exit card.

## Key (teacher)

A sentence starts with a capital and ends with a mark that fits what it says: a telling sentence takes a period, an asking sentence takes a question mark, a sentence of strong feeling takes an exclamation mark. The pronoun I is always a capital.
