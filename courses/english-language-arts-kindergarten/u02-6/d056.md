# Day 56: A capital to start, a mark to end

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the model strips large on sentence strips and keep the three mark cards face down; show them one at a time. Make Pip's wrong strips: "rain falls" and "Then i see the sun". Take out the children's journals from day 55. Make the day's six: the next six unheard. Keep the exit cards to yourself. Read the student page aloud when it is handed out.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Whole class. If many children missed day 55's exit, repair the more common of errors AE and AF. For error AE (a fragment), Pip says "Rain roof." Ask who or what and what happens, and say it back whole: "Rain taps the roof." For error AF (the same short sentence said again), add where or when and say the longer sentence back. For error A (the wrong picture first), lay two pictures, a calm sky and a stormy sky, and say "first" and "later" for the order a story tells them.

#### 5 min. Words

Whole class. Capital: a big letter that starts a sentence; reach your hands up high. Punctuation: the little signs, the marks at the end; tap the end of a line. Children say each word with the gesture. Review: the big and the little form of a letter, as in T and t (day 54).

#### 10 min. Hear Priya at the Window again, pages 3 to 5

Whole class. Read pages 3 to 5 aloud with the pictures (4 minutes). Page 5 names the three marks in the story's own words; read it slowly. Then talk (6 minutes).
**Ask.** "What does the mark at the end tell a reader?" Expect: what kind of sentence it is: telling, asking or strong feeling. Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then take answers from the whole group; hear six.

#### 15 min. Model

Whole class. Show Priya's lines printed large: "Rain falls." and "The rain stops. The sun shines." Point to the first letter of each sentence and say it is a capital. Run a fix-it with Pip (7 minutes). Show Pip's strip "rain falls". **Ask.** "What is missing at the start?" Expect: the big letter. Circle the first letter with the marker, write the capital R above it, add a period, and read it with the pointer. Show Pip's strip "Then i see the sun". The word I, the one that means me, is always a capital: here it is in a different color on your own strip. **Ask.** "Which letter should be big?" Expect: the i. Fix it. Then the marks (8 minutes), one at a time, each at the end of a strip. Period, on "Rain falls.": it tells; pat the air. Question mark, on "Can we go out now?": it asks; raise a hand. Exclamation mark, on "The sun came out!": strong feeling; arms up. Children copy each gesture and say the name. Then say sentences aloud and children make the gesture of the mark that would end each: "The wind is soft." "Is it raining?" "Look at that cloud!" Never line the three mark cards up in a row.

#### 15 min. Expand and mark

Whole class, then alone. Take yesterday's sentence, "The sky is gray over the school." Write it as a class (3 minutes), with a capital and a period, and read it. Each child tells a partner an expanded sentence about their own journal drawing (2 minutes). Take dictation at the table from the day's six, 1 minute each (6 minutes); the others print the first letter of their sentence as a capital, if it is a letter they know. Then walk among the others and write each child's sentence beside the drawing, with the capital and mark, about 10 seconds each (4 minutes). A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Journal read-back

Whole class. Read each child's line aloud while that child circles the capital and the end mark on the student page strip, about 10 seconds each. Read the day's six first. If time runs out, read the rest in tomorrow's Journal part.

#### 5 min. Exit E56

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read each card twice. All children circle on the exit sheet: ask [[s02-X56a]], then [[s02-X56b]], then [[s02-X56c]], then [[s02-X56d]]: read "A seal naps.", "A seal naps on the rock." and "A seal.", each beside its picture, and ask which sentence says where the seal naps. To the day's six, 30 seconds each, add: "Say the name of each mark." Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Signs: the lowercase strip circled (error AJ); the strip with the small i circled (error AK); the period circled for the question mark, or a wrong name (error AL); the short sentence with no where circled (error AF); the fragment circled (error AE).

#### 5 min. Foundations: rhyme and syllables

Whole class, oral (cycle 8, lesson 43: k and y). Rhyme: say key, see and me; children give another word that rhymes with key. Syllables: clap the beats of kitchen (two), kangaroo (three), yellow (two) and yesterday (three).

#### 10 min. Foundations: k and y

Whole class. Say the names, sounds and keywords: k, kangaroo, /k/; y, yak, /y/. Print k and y, little and big, in lines. Say the path as children write. Then sound to letter: say /k/ and /y/ in a new order, and children write each letter without a model.

#### 10 min. Foundations: oral three-sound work

Whole class, with counters. Add a sound: say us and add /b/ at the front: bus. Say in and add /p/ at the front: pin. Substitute a sound: say tub and change /t/ to /r/: rub; say ten and change /t/ to /h/: hen. Never use a word that ends in /l/, /r/ or /x/.

#### 10 min. Foundations: mapped words up and do

Whole class. As day 55: map each word, touch each letter and star the part to remember. Review: up and we, and do (day 54). Read the three words in the strip, then in Thunder in the next part.

#### 10 min. Foundations: shared print

Whole class. First, reread the strips from the model with the pointer (3 minutes), finding the first letter and the mark of each. Then the new verse Thunder (7 minutes). Read it once, then children track with the pointer. Find do, we and up. Find the capital I in "Kim and I". Find the question mark after "do" and the periods. Find k in Kim.

#### 15 min. Foundations: rotation

Teacher table and stations. The teacher hears the next six, 2 minutes each (12 minutes): names, sounds and writing for k and y. The others work in pairs: handwriting and letter cards. The last 3 minutes are for moving.

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner. Fix Pip's two strips, make the three gestures, and write one sentence. One projector: show the strips on one screen; the circling stays on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No sentence strips: write the sentences large on paper.

**Skip.** The verse, the pair talk and the rotation.

- **10 min. Capital and mark.** Write "Rain falls." large. Show where the capital is and where the mark is. Make a gesture for each mark: pat the air, raise a hand, arms up.
  **Say.** "What does the mark at the end tell a reader?" Expect: a period tells, a question mark asks, an exclamation mark shows strong feeling.
- **10 min. A sentence.** Your child tells a longer sentence about their drawing. Write it with a capital and a mark and read it aloud while they circle both.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints k and y.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X56a]] [[s02-X56b]] [[s02-X56c]] [[s02-X56d]] Write "the puddle is big." and "The puddle is big."; ask which starts the right way. Write "Soon i run." and "Soon I run."; ask which I is right. Draw the three marks on cards; ask for the question mark. Say "A seal naps." and "A seal naps on the rock."; ask which says where.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the model, the expand-and-mark part and the exit; leave the verse. Longer: a secure child finds every capital and every mark in the verse Thunder.

### Words and supports

Words: capital, punctuation, period, question mark, exclamation mark. Frames: "My sentence starts with a capital." "The mark at the end tells ___." Dictation, pointing and a communication aid all count. A pair may talk away from the group.

### Tomorrow

Day 57 opens with 5 minutes for the more common of errors AJ, AK and AL on today's exit: circle the first word and change its first letter, find I in a model strip and fix it, or match a heard telling to its period and a heard ask to its question mark. Then the class hears The Magic Bow again and retells it with cards. Today's heard column and the exit marks go on the record sheet.
