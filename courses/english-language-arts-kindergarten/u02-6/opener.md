# Section 2.6 opener: a second weather story and the marks of a sentence

## What the section does

Days 54 to 58 read a second weather story, Priya at the Window, and map it in the same three boxes as The Picnic Plan: who, where, and what happens. Children begin a picture journal of the weather they see, expand one sentence with where or when, and learn the capital letter and the three end marks. Day 57 rereads The Magic Bow and retells it with cards. Day 58 turns weather facts into a drawn and spoken report and adds the word parts re- and pre-.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block ends with a five-minute exit, a card read twice by the teacher, answered by pointing, sorting, drawing or speaking. Every child marks pictures on paper; six children, a different six each day, are asked for their reasons. Every day starts with five minutes that repair one thing from the day before.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A story has the same three boxes every time.** A new story fits the old boxes: who acts, where it happens, and what happens.
- **A sentence grows by adding where or when.** "Rain falls." becomes "Rain falls on the green leaves."
- **A sentence starts with a capital and ends with a mark.** The mark tells the reader what kind of sentence it is: a period tells, a question mark asks, an exclamation mark shows strong feeling. The pronoun I is always a capital.
- **A retell keeps the important events in order.** First, then, last, with the middle not skipped.
- **A fact is true to the text or the window.** The drawing shows the same fact the child says.
- **A word part changes a meaning.** re- means again, pre- means before, -s means more than one, -er means a person who.

## The errors, in plain words

- **G, H, I:** an object named as a character, a place the card does not give, a minor thing named instead of the event. Match the person who acts to the who box, and an action sentence to its picture.
- **AE, AF:** a fragment, or the same short sentence said again. Say who and what happened, then add where or when.
- **AJ, AK, AL:** a lowercase first word, a lowercase I, a wrong name for a mark. Circle the first word and change its first letter; find I in a model strip; match a heard telling to its period and a heard ask to its question mark.
- **J, AV:** events out of order, or only one event told. Order the cards while rereading first, then, last.
- **D, E, AD:** a drawing of something other than the fact, a voice too quiet to hear, a prefix ignored.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet, never by a child. Weather is shown by pictures or the closed window; no child is asked about weather at home or what the family wears.

## Supplies

The three-box map and picture cards, the retell cards for The Magic Bow, the capital-and-mark strips, the report page, the sort cards and word-part cards, the class journal page, chart paper, crayons, the exit cards and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw three boxes on scrap paper and sketch the cards on index cards. Print the strips by hand in large letters. A closed window or one printed scene stands in for the weather.
