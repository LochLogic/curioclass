# Day 30: A new toy portrait

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the record sheet for day 29's items and for day 28's, and choose the one of errors C, D and E seen most. The next seven names after day 29's eight are the battery. Put a fresh portrait sheet at each place. Keep a clear path between the tables for the gallery. Today's six are the thirtieth six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

Whole class. Say: "These talks are made up for practice." Pip shows the most common of errors C, D and E from day 29's items and day 28's. For error D (stopping after one answer), Pip answers "It rolls" and stops; you ask "Tell me more" and then a follow-up that grows from the answer: "What makes it roll?" Children echo the follow-up. For error C (talking over), Pip speaks over you; take one counted breath together before answering. For error E (a statement offered when a question is needed), sort two sentences into questions and statements by voice. If only a few erred, take them as a small group during the new edition.

#### 12 min. Hear 03 and 04

Whole class on the rug. Review: Push, Pull, Roll (days 9 to 28) and A Toy Can Turn (days 14 to 29). Read both whole, holding up each picture (about 3.5 minutes plus pictures). Then ask two questions, each answered by pointing back to a page. Partners point first.

**Ask.** "Which fact matches this labelled picture?" Hold up the cart with its labels on page 6 of A Toy Can Turn. Expect: a label tells you a name; the cart has a wheel, a handle and a box.

**Ask.** "Which second detail would help its reader?" Expect: a wheel rolls (page 4 of Push, Pull, Roll), or a top spins (page 2 of A Toy Can Turn). Any real second fact from either book is right; a child may point to the page and not say it.

#### 18 min. New edition

Alone, at the table. Say: "Today you make a new portrait. It is not scored." Each child asks the child beside them one question with a turn card, "Which toy do you pick?", and listens (2 minutes). Then each child draws that classmate's pick in the frame of a fresh portrait sheet, labels it using the letters a, t, h, p, n and c (the first sound is enough), and dictates two facts, one from Push, Pull, Roll or A Toy Can Turn and one from the interview, which you write (16 minutes). Model once with the frame on the chart: "Our classmate prefers the ___. Its ___ ___." Hear the day's six, three pairs, 2 minutes a pair, while the others work alone. The scored portrait is day 27's as revised on day 29; you score it after class ([[s01-P1]], 30 teacher minutes), topic and information separately: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Signs: "I like it" with no named toy (error X); a named toy with no fact (error M).

#### 10 min. Gallery

Whole class. Portraits stand on the tables. Children walk the clear path to visit two tables and say "I saw ___" to the maker. Walking paths stay clear; walk, do not run. A child may point and not say.

#### 10 min. Turn and talk

Pairs, then whole class. Review: turn and talk: ask, answer, swap, say one thing back; a child may pass (day 1). Ask each pair: "Which question helped you most? Which fact did you add?" Each child speaks for 4 minutes, the partner says "Tell me more" once (8 minutes). Then three children tell the class one thing they heard, "I heard ___" (2 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Whole class, then six children. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card twice: "The toy fox sits in its box. Its tail is soft." Each child circles the labelled drawing that teaches a fact about the toy ([[s01-X30]]; 2 minutes, not scored). Then each of the day's six hears one spoken question, 30 seconds each: "Say a word that rhymes with tail." Signs: the drawing labelled "I like it" (error X); a word that only starts like tail, such as tub (error Q). Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 10 min. Oral work

Whole class, then six. Review: rhyme: words that end the same, ball and hall (day 16). Say two words and the class says if they end the same: swing and ring (yes); boat and coat (yes); boat and book (no). A child who says boat and book rhyme because they start alike (error Q) hears the endings stretched: boat, coat (5 minutes). Then each of the day's six hears one spoken word, 30 seconds each, and says a word that ends the same: swing, ring or sing; kite, light or bite (3 minutes). Review: tap the beats of a toy name, the whole word first, not its sounds (day 18): marbles (two), dinosaur (three) (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters m and r

Whole class at the sound-spelling map. Review: letter m: name em, sound /m/, keyword moon; letter r: name ar, sound /r/, keyword rake (day 28). Chant "m, m, /m/, moon; r, r, /r/, rake" (2 minutes). Say /m/ and each child holds up a card, then /r/ (3 minutes). Then write four toy words on chart paper: marbles, robot, drum, ring. Children come up one at a time to touch an m or an r in a word and name it (5 minutes). Naming only: you read the words, and the children name the letters.

#### 28 min. Common battery, seven

Teacher table, 4 minutes a child, with the prompts and keys of day 28's items: book kinds and makers, track, sequence and spaces, letter names, formation, sound writing, consonant sounds, rhyme, syllables and mapped words. A component not reached stays blank. The other twenty-three rotate through four stations, 7 minutes each: letter cards, printing, oral picture games, pointing in shared print.

#### 7 min. Formation and mapped words

Whole class. Print M m R r on handwriting paper, saying the sound as you form each (4 minutes). A child who prints only the capital when small is asked (error P) sees the lowercase path written once. Review: mapped words the and a (day 21) and in (day 28). Show the large poem Toy Clues, point under "in" and "the" in "A toy in the yard", and the class reads each as a whole word (3 minutes).

#### 5 min. Check and feedback

Whole class. Say /m/ and then /r/. Each child chooses the letter card first, then prints the letter on the card. This is practice, not scored. Collect the cards. Say: "Tomorrow we hear a new information book."

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner; the child asks and answers, makes a new portrait of your pick with two facts, and hears the battery in order. One projector: show one book page at a time; mark on printed cards.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw the letters on paper. No books: say a fact aloud.

**Skip.** The gallery, the battery and the stations.

- **5 min. Ask.** Your child asks you one question about a toy and one follow-up.
  **Say.** "Which toy do you pick?" Expect: a toy from the room.
- **10 min. Portrait.** Your child draws your pick, says one label and tells you two facts for you to write.
- **5 min. Rhyme.** Say swing and ring; your child says if they end the same.
- **5 min. Letters.** Say /m/ and /r/; your child prints each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X30]] Say "The toy fox sits in its box. Its tail is soft." Your child circles the drawing that teaches a fact and says a word that ends like tail.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the gallery and the second turn and talk; keep the portrait, the exit and the letters. Longer: a child who is secure adds a third fact and says where it came from.

### Words and supports

Words: portrait, interview, label, fact. Frames: "Our classmate prefers the ___." "Its ___ ___." "I saw ___." A home language, pointing or a drawn toy counts as an answer; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

Day 31 opens with a five-minute repair of error M, a portrait that names its toy and gives no fact: add one fact the picture shows to the named topic. Day 30's items, the revised portrait and the fox card, are scored after class, and day 31 hears a new information book.
