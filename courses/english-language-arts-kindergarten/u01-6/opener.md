# Section 1.6 opener: asking a partner and making an information portrait

## What the section does

Days 26 to 30 teach children to interview a partner about a classroom toy and to draw a labelled portrait of that partner's pick with one true fact. The class hears the information books again, asks for help, for facts and for what a word means, and answers in return. Days 28 and 29 are check days: you hear small groups of five interview one another, and you hear each child for about four minutes on a battery of early reading and sound tasks. Day 30 scores the portrait after class.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block opens with a five-minute repair of the day before, then a few words first, a hearing, a partner turn, a drawing and an exit. The foundations block holds oral rhyme and syllables, the letters n and c (day 26) and then m and r (days 28 to 30), printing, mapped words and rotations.

- Day 26 models the interview with a puppet, Pip. Day 27 rehearses and drafts the portrait.
- Days 28 and 29 are check days; nothing new is taught. Interviews run in groups while the other children draw at tables.
- Day 30 lets every child make a fresh, unscored edition of a portrait. The scored portrait is the day 27 draft as revised on day 29.
- The battery is the same for every child. A component you do not reach stays blank; try it another day.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **An interview has two jobs.** Ask, then answer. A good follow-up question grows out of the answer.
- **Three questions.** One asks for help, one asks for facts, one asks what a word means. "What do you mean by that word?" is a question, not a challenge.
- **A statement is not a question.** "I like it too" says something. "What does it do?" asks.
- **A portrait names its topic and teaches one fact.** The topic is the toy. The fact comes from an information book, the interview or the picture. "I like it" tells a feeling, not a fact.
- **A label is for the reader.** The label names the picture; the dictated sentence adds the fact.
- **Early reading skills are heard, not decoded.** Children name letters, point under words, count word parts and blend sounds. No child reads a passage.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, B, E:** an idea not in the words, a picture of a different moment, a statement offered when a question is needed.
- **C, D:** the child talks over a partner, or stops after one answer. Model a follow-up.
- **K, M, X:** the topic is a detail, or a fact is missing, or the label says only a feeling. Name the toy, then add a fact.
- **N, O, P:** see section 1.5. A letter name given for a sound; the wrong letter written; only the capital printed.

A puppet voices every wrong example. Never ask a child about toys at home.

## Supplies

The exit cards, Pip, the portrait sheet, the topic chart, the battery cards, the m and r cards, the n and c cards, the class book, thick pencils and crayons, the sound-spelling map and the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the portrait boxes on scrap paper. Make Pip from a sock. Use real shelf toys for the interview. Say the battery prompts from the record sheet.
