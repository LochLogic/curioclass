# Section 1.7 opener: explaining with details and describing with words

## What the section does

Days 31 to 34 close the unit. Children hear information texts and explain a topic with details, counting the parts of spoken words. They use describing words and opposites, join the shared reading of poems and the class book, and show what they learned about letters m and r and about blending and splitting spoken words. Day 33 closes the opinion record and gives make-up hearings. Day 34 responds to what the unit showed and gives one fresh unscored try.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block opens with a five-minute repair, then a few words first, a hearing or a return, a partner turn and a drawing. On days 31 and 32 you hear small groups for a minute or two each while the other children draw or make pages for the class book. The foundations block holds oral rhyme and syllables, the letters m and r, printing and mapped words, and the final battery and cycle checks.

- Day 31 hears a new information booklet and works with detail cards; the battery for the last seven children runs in the foundations block.
- Day 32 holds the word-relationship check and the cycle 4 check, which tests the day 28 components fresh; the oral blending check has no print.
- Day 33 has no exit. It holds the make-up hearings, the response to the cycle 4 check, and a share of the earlier opinion drawing. Nothing new is scored.
- Day 34 is a response day. Small groups repair their own target, then try one fresh card. Nothing is scored.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A detail explains a topic.** The topic is what a page is about. A detail tells one more fact about it. Pictures add details the words do not say.
- **Parts of a spoken word are beats.** Clap them; they are not the letters or the sounds.
- **Describing words make an object clear.** A describing word fits an object only if the object really is that way. Check each word against the real thing.
- **Opposites are the far sides of one idea.** Different ways to move are not opposites of each other.
- **Blending puts spoken parts together; splitting takes a first sound apart.** The rest of a word stays whole.
- **A fresh card is a new chance, not a test of a taught card.** Day 34 is unscored.

## The errors, in plain words

- **B, R:** a picture of a different moment from the words; letters or sounds counted as beats.
- **K, M, X:** a detail named as the topic; no fact; a feeling instead of a fact.
- **V, W, U:** a describing word used for an object that does not fit; two contrasting movements done the same way; a stronger word given as an opposite.
- **AA, AF, AG:** a taught letter given another letter's name; spoken parts left apart; the rest of a word split further.

A puppet voices every wrong example. Never ask a child about toys at home.

## Supplies

The exit cards, detail cards, word-relationship cards, the C4 and C5 strips, the class book Our Classroom Toys, thick pencils and crayons, the sound-spelling map and the record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw the cards on scrap paper. Use finger movements for the verbs. Use chalk or a finger in a tray for letters. Say the oral words from the strip.
