# Day 33: Close the record

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the record sheet for day 31 and day 32 and list up to fifteen names with the most blank spoken items for the make-up hearings. Put the five toys on a mat and the hidden toy behind a screen. Set out the toy-choice drawings from the portfolio. Mark the children missed on each part of the cycle 4 check in four small groups. Nothing today is a new score.

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

Whole class. Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Take the more common of error W and error V from day 32's strips. For error W, Pip acts a march and a tiptoe the same way; you act the two side by side, firm steps and light quiet steps, and each child says which is which with a finger march. For error V, Pip says the strip with two steaming bowls shows cold; hold the two strips side by side and the class points to the one with the ice. If only a few erred, take them with the make-up hearings.

#### 3 min. Words first

Whole class. Review: riddle and clue, a riddle gives clues and each clue narrows the toy (day 13). Hide a top behind the screen and give three clues, one at a time: "It is a toy." "It spins on its point." "You twist its stem." After each clue the class holds up fingers for how many toys it could still be, then guesses. Review: guess, then check by looking.

#### 10 min. Shared reading

Whole class. Review: the poem Read Together (day 32). Point under each word of the large poem, left to right, top to bottom, with the spaces shown, and read it; the class joins in, twice (6 minutes). Then show page 1 of the class big book: "We have blocks. Blocks stack up." Slide a finger under each word and the class reads it back (2 minutes). Ask "Where do I start? Where do I go next?" Expect: at the green dot, then left to right (day 6). Pairs read the poem once more with small copies (2 minutes).

#### 12 min. Hear Toy Riddles

Whole class, then pairs. Review: Toy Riddles (days 13 and 17). Read the riddles on pages 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 only, one at a time, with the answer pages covered. After each, each pair points to the toy it thinks the clues name on the mat: the teddy bear, the ball, the building bricks, the play dough, the blocks (about 2 minutes a riddle with the answer page opened and the class saying it).  A child who points to a toy that does not fit the clues, error A, hears the first clue again and points to the page.

#### 12 min. Share the opinion drawing

Pairs. Review: an opinion says what you prefer, "My favorite classroom toy is the ___" (day 15). Each child takes the toy-choice drawing from the portfolio folder. Partner A says the sentence and the partner asks "Tell me more" and A says one more thing; then they swap (2 exchanges each, 2 minutes each way, 4 minutes). Then each child may add a label to the drawing with taught letters, the first sound is enough (6 minutes). You circulate and listen. Nothing is scored; the scored piece stays as it is on the record.

#### 15 min. Make-up observations

Teacher table, 1 minute a child for up to fifteen children with the most blank spoken items from day 31 and day 32. For a missed T item, read Folio B once, then ask only that item (s01-T1 to s01-T4). For a missed V item, use the card for it (s01-V1 to s01-V5); a missed V6 or V7 is made up with the poem or Attributes of Toys page (s01-V6, s01-V7). Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen. An item not reached stays blank and is not guessed; a child who is absent keeps blanks. The other children add a page to the class book Our Classroom Toys: they draw a classroom toy and label it with a letter they know.

#### 3 min. Close the record

Teacher table, while the class draws. Read the record for each child's opinion piece, the day 17 or 18 toy choice (s01-O4): it names a toy and a preference. If one part is missing, write "not yet seen" and leave it. No new opinion performance is collected and nothing is scored twice.

#### 10 min. Response and fresh retry

Four small groups by the part of the cycle 4 check missed on day 32, 2 minutes with you and the rest in pairs. For each group, repair with a contrasting example, then a fresh case of the same part. The groups: a letter called by another name (error AA): hold M beside N and R beside P; fresh retry, name R and P on a new card. A name given for a sound (error N): say the name, then the sound; fresh retry, a new card of m, r and t. A different letter written, or only the capital (errors O and P): hear it, choose the card, print; fresh retry, a new dictated sound. A word guessed from a picture (error S): cover the picture and point under in; fresh retry, "The doll is in the bed", with in found in the print. Each child retries only the part that was the child's own. Unscored.

#### 10 min. Oral work

Whole class. Review: rhyme (day 16): hen and ten (yes); ten and tub (no, the words start alike, error Q) (3 minutes). Review: tap the beats (day 18): pencil (two), elephant (three), bus (one) (3 minutes). Review: onset and rime, swept together (day 24): d, ig sweeps to dig; b, ell sweeps to bell (4 minutes, each child sweeps once with a partner).

#### 10 min. Reread

Whole class. Review: the poems Many Ways (day 25) and Read Together (day 32). Point under the large poem and read the lines already taught: "We walk, walk, walk. We march, march, march." and the first four lines of Read Together. No new pattern. Show a space between two words with a finger and ask the class to point to the first word of a line.

#### 25 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, five turns of 5 minutes (5 times 5 is 25). Teacher table: the day's targets, a letter dictated and printed, or a rhyme said; hear each child about 50 seconds. Four stations: letter cards with the map, printing M m R r, oral picture games, pointing in the class big book. The teacher records on the sheet.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Whole class, then six children. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read it twice: "Mo drew a toy globe. Mo said, The globe is blue. Mo said, I like the globe best." Ask each of the day's six, 30 seconds each, "Which sentence tells what Mo prefers?" ([[s01-X33]]). Signs: the fact about the toy given (error L); the toy named and no preference (error L). Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen. The other children print one capital and one small letter from memory on a card, saying its sound; collect the cards, not scored. Say: "Tomorrow we try one more time."

### Other rooms

One child: you are the partner; read the poem with a finger under each word, play the riddle with a hidden toy, and ask about the toy-choice drawing. One projector: show one page at a time; children mark on printed cards.

### At home

**Swap.** No toy: draw one. No poem: say any two lines you know.

**Skip.** The make-up hearings, the groups and the rotation.

- **5 min. Riddle.** Hide a toy behind your back and give three clues; your child guesses.
  **Say.** "It is a toy. It rolls. It is round." Expect: a guess that fits each clue.
- **5 min. Drawing.** Your child draws a favorite toy and says why they like it.
  **Say.** "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." Expect: a named toy.
- **5 min. Rhyme.** Say hen and ten; your child says if they end the same.
- **5 min. Letters.** Say /m/ and /r/; your child prints each.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Say d, then ig; your child says the whole word, dig.
- **5 min. Beats.** Say pencil and elephant; your child claps each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X33]] Say "Mo drew a toy globe. Mo said, The globe is blue. Mo said, I like the globe best." Your child says which sentence tells what Mo prefers.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the class big book page and one riddle; keep the opinion share, the make-up and the response. Longer: a child who is secure makes up a riddle with two clues for a classroom toy.

### Words and supports

Words: riddle, clue, guess, opinion, label. Frames: "I think it is the ___." "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." "It is a toy. It ___." A pointed card or a home-language word is an answer; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

Day 34 gives one response to the unit's evidence: the portrait, the interview and the opinion, each by its own target, with one fresh unscored card, then a sound repair for blending and splitting spoken words. Today's make-up marks stay on the record sheet.
