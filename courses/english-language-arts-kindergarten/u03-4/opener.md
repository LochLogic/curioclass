# Section 3.4 opener: people, animals and trees

## What the section does

Days 95 to 99 connect a person and a tree, then turn to the first animal piece. Day 95 hears a chant about what people get from trees. Day 96 offers three unused source cards and the children plan an animal piece with its evidence. Days 97, 98 and 99 are check days: ten children are heard each day on their contribution, source card and piece. Foundations hold short i and then short u.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and a five-minute exit or, on check days, the observation groups. The foundations block holds sounds, letters, the day's words and the rotation. A child outside the six heard for the exit is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time. Children do not read alone; the teacher reads every card, direction and text aloud.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A tree gives a person something.** The arrow runs from the tree part to the person.
- **An animal piece names the animal, gives one fact and shows the card.** The card is the source.
- **Feedback is a next step.** The slip says one thing to add; the child revises before the check.
- **A check day is a look, not a lesson.** Groups of five are heard; the rest revise and rest.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, B, D:** a list instead of a link, a picture that leaves out the link, or a fact not on the card. Trace the arrow and point to the sentence.
- **C, D, E, F:** the animal unnamed, an unsupported fact, an answer to a different question, or no contribution. Repair on day 101 in the response groups.
- **AI:** a right fact with the wrong card. Match the fact to its card.

Wrong examples are said by a puppet or as "someone might say", never by a child. No picture or question asks about a child's home, family, money, body or religion.

## Supplies

Texts 04 and 06 in large print, the three unused source cards, card C1 (page 3 of A Tree Is a Living Thing), the feedback slips, the reader Gus in a Tub, the three-box chart and the unit record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Draw each card on scrap paper and say it is made up for practice. Cut scrap paper into cards. Use buttons for counters, a torn strip for the talking token and a leaf or a drawn tree for the tree. Write the sound-spelling map as a strip by hand.
