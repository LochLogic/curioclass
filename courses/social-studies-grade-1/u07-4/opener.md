# Section 7.4 opener: looking back and showing what we know

## What the section does

Three days, 78 to 80, of 30 minutes. Day 78: the look back. The class draws and counts the baseline place again, the same way, and compares it with day 70, the staff account and the action log. The class says what changed and what remains, and does not say its action caused every change. Each child writes, draws or dictates a claim, a reason and a closing that answer the compelling question. Day 79: the observation. A fresh packet is read aloud and every child answers six paper parts; five groups of six are heard, one ask each. Day 80: the response. Small groups re-teach what the observation showed, then a brief fresh try on changed cases.

## How the days run

Day 78: observe and log (3), warm-up and repair (3), return and revise (5), model a look back (3), our look back (5), claim, reason, closing (5), pair check (2), exit (4). Day 79: hear the packet (5), the six items (3), paper parts and spoken groups (20), hand in (2). Day 80: return and repair (5), reteach (10), fresh try (10), review (5).

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Compare two dated records taken the same way.** The same place, drawn and counted the same way, with the dates visible. If a count cannot be repeated, compare the drawings and the staff account, and say so aloud.
- **A dated record shows change; it does not show cause.** One neat picture cannot show lasting change. Limited or no improvement is an acceptable conclusion.
- **A claim, a reason and a closing:** "I think ___ because ___ so ___." Completed claim examples are covered before children write; the blank frame and picture supports stay.
- **Feedback comes before a score.** The day 76 judgments come back with a written note and are revised on day 78; the revised sheet is what counts for item 2. The day 78 local claim is practice and never scored; item 6 is scored only on the day 79 packet.
- **Day 79 asks nothing about any child or family.** The three made-up citizenship cases are the only ones, and no person in the packet has a status stated or implied. If a child volunteers family information, do not question or record it.
- **How day 79 runs.** Each child does the six paper parts alone at their own pace, about three minutes each (items 4 and 6, four). Meanwhile five groups of six are called to the teacher for three minutes, about thirty seconds a child, one ask each. A child not heard is heard in the rotation on day 80. Record paper evidence and spoken evidence apart; mark shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- **How day 80 uses the written moves.** Item 1: show a made-up resident who is not a citizen belonging. Item 2: link a quality to a deed. Item 3: lay the then-and-now pictures. Item 4: sort plan and record, and a problem at each scale. Item 5: one small, safe part. Item 6: claim, reason from the source, closing. Teach a shared error first if more than a third missed it. Never rehearse the day 79 cases.
- **Facts in the packet rest on checked pages** (sources 79A and 79B): the packet is held out and printed only on day 79.

## The errors, in plain words

- **E:** our action solved everything forever. Lay the baseline beside today's drawing and name one change and one thing that remains.
- **K:** one tidy picture shows it is fixed. Ask for the date on each picture and put both in order.
- **B:** "because I like it" is a reason. Match a claim to a detail card, then say "because" and the detail.

## Care

Nobody is rated. A child may pass in every talk. Read every prompt aloud without hints; accept a point, a token, a drawing, dictation or familiar-language speech. Judge meaning and evidence, not drawing skill or handwriting.

## Supplies for the section

The day 70 baseline sheet and the action log, the day 78 look-back columns and picture checklist, the model look-back pictures, the blank claim frame (unit 6), the held-out day 79 packet and picture cards, the move cards, the fresh-try cards, the exit cards and the unit 7 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn sheets, the teacher reading the printed sources aloud, and a tally of marks on one page.
