# Day 79 packet (withheld)

Keep this packet closed and out of sight until day 79. None of it is taught before then. Cover every completed teaching example and every model in the room on day 79; only blank frames stay up. Read each item aloud once, without hints; every source is read aloud. Say aloud: "The people in the first three cards are made up. The sources A and B are about real places. The comparison cards are made up." Print "invented for practice" on the made-up cards. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Accept a point, a token, a drawing, dictation or familiar-language speech. Judge meaning and evidence, not drawing skill. Five groups of six are heard for three minutes a group during the paper minutes; a child not heard is heard on day 80.

## Source A: a book wagon (read aloud; credited to Kathryn Miller, NIST, March 20, 2024, in our words)

In Washington County, Maryland, farms were spread far apart. In 1905 Mary Titcomb planned a book wagon for the library. Joshua Thomas drove it along the roads, with stops at homes along the route. People could borrow books near home. Later the library had a new wagon with a motor.

## Source B: a park invitation (read aloud; credited to the National Park Service, in our words)

At Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore in Michigan, people can volunteer to help clean up. A group picks a site in the park. It meets at a visitor center to collect the supplies. It fills out a form to get started. The page invites people to sign up.

## Three comparison cards (made up, each with a picture)

1. **Near:** In a nearby town, wrappers blow across a playground. Staff add a covered bin.
2. **Region:** Across a region, litter washes from several towns into one river. Town workers arrange collections.
3. **Far:** At a school in another country, books are too far away for many children. A traveling library brings books.

## The six items (one picture and one word label each)

1. **Nola, Paz and Ren (made up).** Read aloud: "Nola was born in Colorado. Paz became a citizen as an adult, by following the steps the law sets. Ren lives here, goes to a public school and is not a citizen." Paper: match each person to "born here", "became one later" or "not a citizen", and circle whether Ren belongs, yes or no. Spoken, one ask: "Does Ren belong? Why?" or "Say a right Ren has." or "Say a responsibility Ren has."
2. **A quality (source A).** Paper: circle the action that shows a quality: "planned a book wagon" or "the farms were far apart". Spoken, one ask: "Name a quality and one detail from A."
3. **Long ago and later (source A).** Paper: place the wagon picture and the motor picture in order. Spoken, one ask: "What did the library use long ago, and what later?" Or: "What stayed the same?"
4. **Problems near and far (source B and the three cards).** Paper: on a strip of near, region and far, circle each problem. Spoken, one ask: "Name a problem near and what people do." Or: "Does B tell us the park is already clean?"
5. **Ways to help.** Paper: draw one safe, child-sized way to help at one card. Spoken, one ask: "Name one safe helping step."
6. **A claim (source A).** Paper, three boxes with a word label (claim, reason, closing): "Should the people in A keep bringing books to neighborhoods?" Dictated claims are taken at the child's spoken turn.
