# Repair cards, unit 2 (days 15, 16 and 17)

New cards for the return and repair parts. Say first: "These cards are made up." Print "invented for practice" in small letters on each invented card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading. None of these cards is used in an exit or in the day 18 packet.

## Day 15: after day 14 (errors C, I and L)

- **C, six cards to sort into the kind boxes** (say each one's kind): a pretend photograph of a market street with a horse cart; a letter, "Dear Room 4, thank you for the cards. From Pat."; a drawn tin cup; a drawn map of a village green; a drawing of an old mill; a recording of a made-up shopkeeper telling about her shop.
- **I, six cards to sort into real and invented by the label, then say what each can show:** the Johnston photograph (real, credited); a page from our own class calendar (real, dated); and four cards printed "invented for practice": a letter, a map, a drawing of an object and a recording.
- **L, one new picture to look at:** a drawing of a street with a horse cart, a shop with a window and two people at the shop door. Name three things we can see. Then name one thing we cannot tell, such as the people's names or what they were thinking.

## Day 16: after day 15 (errors D and J)

- **D, four new questions to match to the kind that fits** (the frame "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___"): What did the town square look like long ago? (a photograph); How did children get to school in the town long ago? (a story people tell); Where was the old mill? (a map); What did the school board write to the teachers? (a letter).
- **J, two new cards with captions read aloud, then "whose school is this?":** a drawing of a school door with the caption "Elm Street School, 1920"; a drawing of a schoolyard with the caption "our school, today". The first is not our school; the second is.

## Day 17: after day 16 (errors E and K)

- **E and K, one new then-and-now pair:** a hand-turned pencil sharpener on a desk, then a plug-in pencil sharpener on a desk. Fill the chart: what changed (how it is turned), what stayed (it sharpens a pencil to a point), what we do not know (when the first one was made; whether every school had one). Add the unknown column and say "this is one pair, not every school."
