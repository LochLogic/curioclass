# Day 15: Which clue helps?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Post the big question, What was our school like long ago?, and three smaller ones: What was in the children's room? How did children get to school? Where were the doors and rooms? Table: fifth six.

### The day

#### 3 min. Calendar corner

Date today's square on the class calendar. Group B revise a sentence from your picture feedback, group C say a fresh one, about 12 seconds each. The rest draw their square.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Use day 14's exit and the day 15 repair cards, for the common errors. Error C (all printed cards called photographs): sort six new cards into the boxes, saying each kind. Error I (a made-up card taken for real): sort six cards by label, real or invented for practice, and say what each can show. Error L (a feeling the caption never gives): name three things we see in a new picture and one we cannot tell.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

Review: in unit 1 you broke a big question into smaller ones. Ours is too big for one source. For each smaller question say "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___." A photograph could help with the room, because it shows desks and a board. The Johnston print is another school: read its caption; it shows one old classroom, not ours. A story people tell could help with getting to school, because it tells about the ride. A map or a building could help with doors and rooms. The made-up helper picks a photograph for every question because it likes photographs (error D); the kind has to fit the question. **Ask.** Which kind could help us find out how children got to school? Why? Expect: a story people tell, because it tells about the ride.

#### 12 min. Supported work

Pairs lay six question cards (cards-questions-sources) by a kind card that could help, saying the frame. Replace the playground question with "Where were the old coat hooks?" For the question no kind on the table can answer, the pair says "we would look for ___", such as a record book. The fifth six come to the table, about 70 seconds each, to mark exit card 15 and answer aloud (7 minutes).

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

Everyone circles the more helpful source, exit card 15. [[s02-X15]] [[s02-X15b]] Marks: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

The materials line names stand-ins.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: write each question on scrap paper.

**Skip.** The pairs, the side table and the sorting.

- **5 min. Big question.** Say "What was our school like long ago?" Add three smaller questions: the room, getting to school, doors and rooms.
- **15 min. Match.** Draw six kind cards. Read each smaller question; your child lays it by a kind and says "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___."
  **Say.** "Which kind could help? Why?" Expect: a photograph for the room, because it shows desks.
- **5 min. No match.** Ask "How many children were in the school long ago?" Your child says what they would look for.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X15]] [[s02-X15b]] Draw two sources for one question. Your child circles the more helpful one and says why.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: four cards. Longer: a second kind.

### Words and supports

Frame: "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___." A point or home-language word counts.
