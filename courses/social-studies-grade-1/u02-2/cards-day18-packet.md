# Day 18 observation packet

Kept closed until day 18. Paper parts and the spoken parts of items 1 to 5. The teacher reads each prompt aloud. Every card but the photograph is printed "invented for practice" in small letters, and the teacher says "these are made up" before each one. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading. Do not confuse the story date with today's real date. Blank models and the picture supports stay on the wall; completed examples are withdrawn. Mark each part shown, shown with help or not yet seen, recording the child's words or placements.

## The photograph (real, not seen before)

Frances Benjamin Johnston, Library of Congress, about 1899. The teacher prints it large with this caption under it and reads the caption aloud: "Grade school children posed in classroom, with teacher standing in back of room, Washington, D.C." Keep the date as about 1899. Do not say who the children are or what they feel. For a child who cannot see the print, read this neutral description: "Children sit at wooden desks in rows and face the camera. A person stands at the back of the room. A flag hangs by a doorway. The chalkboard has maps drawn on it. Tall windows are on one wall."

## Item 1: the October page

An October month page, large, seven columns headed by weekday names, the squares numbered 1 to 31 and empty. Three small pictures to place, each a drawing of a leaf. The teacher reads: "A class drew leaves on October 6, on October 13 and on October 20."

## Item 2: yesterday, today and tomorrow

Three picture cards in a row, headed "yesterday," "today" and "tomorrow": a drawing of children at a table drawing leaves, a drawing of leaf pictures taped to a wall, and a dotted drawing of leaf pictures with labels. Under the row: "The story's date is October 21." The teacher says what each card shows, without a tense: "Children draw leaves. Children display the leaves. Children label the leaves." The child tells what the children did yesterday, do today and will do tomorrow. Then the paper part: today's real calendar square, with a box to draw one classroom experience.

## Item 3: the year strip

A blank year strip. Three cards, read in this order: a drawing of music notes, "December 8"; a drawing of a row of chairs, "first assembly, September 15"; a drawing of two books swapping hands, "book swap, April 11". Under them: "All in one year." Spoken, a new case: a field trip on March 3 and a spelling bee on October 20, "Which came first?"

## Item 4: the photograph and a letter

The Johnston photograph above, and one practice card in a letter's shape: "Dear Teacher, our class used a bell to begin lessons. From Lee." Two rows of circles for the paper part: for each source, a camera picture and an envelope picture, and a pair of circles for the letter, labeled "pretend" and "real."

## Item 5: the front sign and the lunch recording

These are described sources, not records that exist. Two ringed pictures: a drawing of a school front sign beside a dated photograph frame, and a recording icon beside a drawing of a lunch tray. The teacher reads the question. Then a blank box to draw a reason.
