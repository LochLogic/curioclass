# Section 2.2 opener: kinds of sources, choosing one, and then and now

## What the section does

Six days, 14 to 19, of 30 minutes. Day 14 teaches six kinds of source about the past (photograph, letter, object, map, building, story people tell) and four questions to ask of each: what kind, who made it (known or not), old or new, what could it help us find out. Day 15 shows how to choose a kind that fits a question, using the unit's big question, What was our school like long ago?, and its smaller questions. Day 16 revises kindergarten's then-and-now chart with a third column, what we do not know. Day 17 is the spoken claim: "I think we can find out ___ with a ___, because ___." Day 18 is the observation: five items, the owner of items 4 and 5 being this section, items 1 to 3 being section 2.1's. Day 19 responds to what day 18 showed.

## How the days run

Day 14: calendar corner (3), return and repair (4), retrieval (4), teaching (7), supported work (10), exit (2). Day 15: calendar corner (3), return and repair (5), teaching (8), supported work (12), exit (2). Day 16: calendar corner (3), return and repair (4), model (7), supported work (14), exit (2). Day 17: calendar corner (3), return and repair (4), reopen (5), supported work (16), exit (2). Day 18: orient (5), item 1 (5), item 2 (6), item 3 (4), item 4 (5), item 5 (5). Day 19: review the evidence (5), move groups (10), fresh try (10), self-check (5). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A source is something from the past that survives and helps us find out.** It is a clue, not the whole story.
- **Look first, name only what we see, then read the caption.** The caption tells the place, the date and the maker. A photograph shows what was in front of the camera.
- **One source answers some questions, not all.** A photograph of another school shows another school. A person's story tells about things a photograph cannot, such as how children got to school.
- **A labeled invention is not a record.** A card that says invented for practice helps us practice; it does not show a real past event.
- **Then and now: some things changed and some stayed.** The chart has a column for what we do not know, so we do not claim more than our sources show. One school is not every school.
- **A reason names a detail.** "Because I like it" tells a feeling. "Because it shows the desks" ties the source to the question.

## The errors, in plain words

- **C: everything printed is a photograph.** Sort a map, a letter and a photograph by kind into boxes.
- **I: a made-up card taken for a real record.** Sort cards into real and invented by their label and say what each can show.
- **L: a feeling or a fact about a pictured person that the caption never gives.** Name three things we can see and one thing we cannot tell.
- **D: one favorite source for every question.** Match the kind to the question.
- **J: another school's photograph taken for ours.** Read the caption aloud and ask "whose school is this?"
- **E: nothing stayed.** Find one thing that is the same in a then-and-now pair.
- **K: all schools changed like this.** Add the unknown column; this is one school.
- **M: an opinion with no detail.** Add a detail from the picture to the claim.

A wrong answer is always voiced by the made-up helper or "someone might say", never by a named child.

## Care with photographs of people

Frances Benjamin Johnston's photographs of Washington, D.C. classrooms, about 1899, are real children in a real place and time. Look first, name only what we see, and never guess who a child is, how they felt or what their family was like. If a child asks why old schools differed, say only that some public schools kept children apart by race, that this was unfair, and that families went to court to change it. Do not simulate being kept out and do not ask a child about their own family. A photograph never shows what a child's life was like.

## Our own school first

If the school office, the district's website or the town's own records hold a dated photograph or record of this school or town, print it for day 16 beside the Johnston photograph, credited. Otherwise day 16 compares Johnston's classroom with today's drawing of our room and names what a photograph of our own school would add; the class's answer to the big question is then the kinds of source it would look for.

## A room with nothing

A chart drawn on the board, cards of scrap paper with a drawing on each, and the Johnston photograph described aloud from its caption.
