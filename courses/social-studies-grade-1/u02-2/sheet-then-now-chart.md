# Then-and-now chart (days 16 and 17)

A large chart on the wall, a revision of kindergarten's chart. Plain black line, thick lines, a row for each pair. Say first: "This is one school and one place, not every school."

## Layout

Three columns, each with a small picture heading: **then** (a closed door behind), **now** (a hand pointing down), **what we do not know** (a question mark). A row at the bottom, **what stayed**. Movable detail cards (a small drawing and a label read aloud) tape on and off. The teacher says "I think ___, because ___" with the named source.

## Row 1, getting to school (the teacher's model, day 16)

- **Then:** our two-sentence summary (the Webster bus summary), read aloud.
- **Now:** a drawing of a school bus today.
- **Changed:** how the bus moved. **Stayed:** children rode together. **Not known:** how long the ride took.
- The claim: "I think children rode together to school in 1928 as they do now, because our summary says children rode a horse-drawn bus."

## Rows 2 and 3, the room and dress (pairs, day 16)

Beside the Johnston photograph (credited) place today's drawing of our room. If the school office or the town holds a dated photograph of our own school, print it beside the Johnston photograph; otherwise name what a photograph of our own school would add. Pairs name only what they see and what they cannot tell, and fill:

"In the photograph ___, but today ___; both ___."

Detail cards to choose from (what the print shows; the teacher adds any the class sees): desks in rows, a chalkboard with writing, framed pictures on the wall, light-colored dresses, dark jackets with collars. Never say who the children are or how they felt.

## What the chart is for

Day 16 models one row; pairs fill two. Day 17 keeps the chart on the wall for the spoken claim.
