# Question and kind cards (days 15 and 16)

Say first: "These questions are for our class to think about. The cards are made up." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading; one small picture clue on each question card. The teacher reads each card aloud.

## The big question and its smaller questions (day 15, the teacher's model)

The big question stays posted: **What was our school like long ago?** It is too big for one source, so it needs smaller questions:

1. What was in the children's room?
2. How did children get to school?
3. Where were the doors and rooms?

For each, model the frame "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___." A photograph helps with the room, a story people tell helps with getting to school, and a map or a building helps with the doors and rooms. The Johnston photograph is another school: it shows one old classroom, not ours. The made-up helper picks a photograph for every question because it likes photographs (error D); the class repairs it: the kind has to fit the question.

## The six kind cards (day 15, pairs)

The same words and pictures as the six boxes of day 14: photograph, letter, object, map, building, story people tell.

## The six question cards (day 15, pairs)

Pairs lay each question card beside a kind card that could help and say the frame.

1. What did the children's desks look like?
2. How did the school helper keep the rooms warm?
3. Where was the old playground?
4. What did the school leader write to the teachers about a new roof?
5. What did the old school building look like from the street?
6. How many children were in the school long ago?

Five of the six match a kind on the table. The sixth has no kind on the table that can answer it: the pair says "we would look for ___" and names a kind of source that is not on the table, such as a record book the school kept.
