# Section 7.1 opener: who belongs, and what needs attention

## What the section does

Three days, 69 to 71, of 30 minutes. Unit 7 asks: how can our class help our community? Day 69: who is a citizen, with three invented people at a library, and the idea that everyone who lives in a community belongs, with rights and responsibilities. Day 70: the class chooses a real, safe school problem, learns to notice before it guesses, draws and counts the place for a dated baseline, and writes questions that ask for a fact. Day 71: what adults are already doing about it, from a dated note, and what the note shows and does not show.

## How the days run

Day 69: reopen (3), hook (6), teaching (8), pairs sort (5), belonging talk (3), exit (5). Day 70: warm-up and repair (5), notice then guess (7), choose our problem (5), baseline (4), supporting questions (5), exit (4). Day 71: warm-up and repair (4), shows and does not show (6), our staff note (8), who already helps (5), what is unknown (3), exit (4). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A citizen is a person the country's law counts as its own member.** A person born in the United States is a citizen from birth. The Fourteenth Amendment says all persons born or naturalized in the United States, and subject to its jurisdiction, are citizens (source 69B). A person born elsewhere may become a citizen later by applying and following the steps the law sets (source 69A). To children: "follows the steps the law sets, as an adult."
- **Two routes are not every route,** and being born abroad does not prove a person is not a citizen. The cards show two routes and one resident who is not a citizen, and the teacher says so. No name stands for a route; shuffle the cards every time.
- **Belonging does not depend on citizenship,** and citizenship is a legal tie, never a prize for helping. A resident who is not a citizen can help, goes to the public school and has rights and responsibilities. Every child may learn at a public school (Plyler v. Doe, decided 1982; source 69C). Adult background only; it is not taught as a dispute. The unit plan, field 10, names a further court opinion for adults; re-read it before use. It is not taught.
- **Never ask a child, or let a child say aloud, a family's status.** If a child volunteers it, do not question or record it. Say "everyone here belongs" and go back to the made-up cards.
- **"Right" has two meanings in this course** (the right side, a right a person has). Say which one every time and never put both on one card.
- **Noticing is not guessing.** "I see drooping leaves" is noticing. "Someone forgot" is guessing, and it blames a person. A problem is a place or a thing, never a child.
- **A note shows some things and not others.** A dated note that adults are checking a leak shows work underway. It does not show the leak is mended.
- **Unit 4's word "citizen"** meant a member under a town's rules. Here it is the legal tie. If a child says "citizens vote," say: adults vote in elections; our class votes are open to every child in the class.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** only citizens belong. Show a made-up resident who is not a citizen, helping and belonging.
- **N:** only citizens have rights. Name two rights on picture cards and put all three people beside them.
- **F:** helping earns citizenship. Sort "steps the law sets" from "ways to help".
- **M:** a "favorite" question is a problem question. Sort "a fact we can look for" from "what someone likes".
- **J:** a note that adults are checking shows the work is done. Say what it shows and does not show.
- **C:** nobody helped until we noticed. Add one more adult to the helper web.

A wrong idea is voiced by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child.

## Care

Nobody is asked about their family, home, money, religion, body or where they were born. Families are many kinds. Children may pass in every talk. All cards say "invented for practice" in small letters and the teacher says aloud that every person on them is made up.

## Supplies for the section

Three mats, the library hook cards, the right and responsibility cards, six sort cards, the notice-and-guess cards, three problem cards, blocks and cups, the baseline sheet, the music-room note and its two-sided card, statement cards, the helper web, the exit cards, the repair cards and the unit 7 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Three sheets of paper as mats, hand-drawn cards on the floor, a block and a cup for each child, a sheet to draw and count on.
