# Section 8.1 opener: earning, goods and services, and prices

## What the section does

Four days, 81 to 84, of 30 minutes. Day 81: a hook (a sign painter with 9 tokens and two things that cost more than that together), then employed, income, wages and salary, and people who earn by selling what they make. Day 82: how jobs in a bread chain differ, why people specialize, and how each earns. Day 83: goods and services, sorted. Day 84: price tags on a made-up class store flyer, found and compared. The compelling question, "How should people choose what to buy and what to save?", goes up on day 81 and stays up for the whole unit.

## How the days run

Day 81: reopen with the hook and kindergarten's work (5), teaching first (10), supported work (13), exit (2). Day 82: reopen (4), model the chain (9), supported work (15), exit (2). Day 83: reopen (5), teaching first (9), supported work (14), exit (2). Day 84: reopen (4), teaching first (9), supported work (15), exit (2).

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Employed** here means a person has a job and someone pays them for it. **Income** is money received for work. **Wages** are pay counted by the hour. A **salary** is an agreed amount paid in regular parts, such as each month (source 81B). A person who sells what they make or grow earns income without an employer. Government surveys count people who run their own business as employed too (source 81A); grade 1 keeps the simple meaning and says only that such a person earns by selling.
- **Unpaid work is work.** Caring at home and volunteering are work with no pay. Never rank paid above unpaid, and never ask a child about their own home.
- **A good is a thing you can hold; a service is something one person does for another.** What a service uses, such as scissors, is still a good.
- **Why people specialize:** each learns one part well, so the work goes faster and better. It is not a ranking of jobs.
- **A price tag sits by one offer.** The number on the tag is the price; the size of the picture is not.
- Every flyer, price, shop and person in this section is made up, and the teacher says so. Nothing on a flyer is a fact about a real shop. This unit follows earnings; income comes in other ways, said aloud once.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** unpaid work is not work. Put a paid and an unpaid worker side by side and ask "is it work? is it paid?".
- **H:** a salary arrives in one lump. Lay a month strip with one part on each month.
- **I and J:** every job is the same work; only hourly pay counts as income. Point to each worker's skill, then to how each is paid.
- **B:** a haircut is a good because you pay. Hold an object, then act out the service.
- **K and L:** a big picture costs more; one tag is the price of everything. Follow each tag by its line to one offer.

## Care

Nobody is rated, and no one is asked what their family does or earns. A child may pass in every talk. Read every card aloud; accept a point, a token, a drawing, dictation or familiar-language speech. Judge economic ideas, not drawing skill or handwriting.
