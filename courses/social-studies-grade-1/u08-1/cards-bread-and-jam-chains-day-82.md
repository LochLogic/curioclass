# The bread chain and the jam chain (day 82)

Say aloud: "These people, shops and things are made up. These cards are practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Tokens are paper pieces, each worth one dollar; no real money.

## The bread chain (model, 9 minutes)

Three movable cards on a strip, left to right.

1. **The baker** bakes loaves with an oven. **Read aloud:** "The baker is paid wages. The skill is baking. The tool is an oven."
2. **The carrier** takes the loaves on a hand cart to the stall. **Read aloud:** "The carrier is paid wages. The skill is carrying safely. The tool is a hand cart."
3. **The seller** sells the loaves at the stall. **Read aloud:** "The seller is paid a salary each month. The skill is selling and counting. The tool is a scale."

**Say:** each learns one part well, so the work goes faster and better. That is called specializing. It does not say one job is better than another. A fourth card: **an unpaid cook at home** feeding people. **Read aloud:** "This cook also feeds people. That work counts, with no pay."

## The jam chain (practice, 7 minutes, each table)

Three movable cards, read aloud: someone who grows fruit and sells it to the jam maker; a jam maker who cooks jam in a pot and is paid wages by the hour; a shop seller who is paid a salary each month. Each table sets the cards in order and says each worker's skill, tool and how they earn.

## Pip

Pip says: "Everyone in the bread chain does the same work." Right: the baker bakes, the carrier carries, the seller sells. Pip says: "Only people paid by the hour earn income." Right: wages, a salary and selling what you make all bring income. Pip is making a mistake on purpose.
