# Day 48: A capital is a city

## Teacher page

### Before class

Fill in the state card from your state's usa.gov page: a star on the capital, a dot on one other city. If the class lives in Colorado or Washington, use the other and a third state, never Oregon or Texas. Say: "These are real maps." Teacher keys: key-sheet-mini-map.md, key-map-cedar-park-and-signs.md, key-maps-locator-and-state-cards.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Unmarked, spoken. If day 47's exit showed error C (the page right), rotate the Cedar Park map and trace the indicator to the north sign, 3 minutes. Return the mini map sheets; the word is south. Review: in unit 4 a government is the leaders and workers who make a community's rules. **Ask.** "Point to one leader on the card." Expect: any leader pictured.

#### 11 min. Teaching first

Review: in kindergarten a city is a large town and a state is part of our country. On the locator, a capital is the city where a government meets; the nation's government meets in Washington, D.C. As in kindergarten, Pip makes a mistake on purpose, pointing at the circled building: "This is the capital." **Ask.** "Is the capital the city or the building?" Expect: the city. Show Denver on the Colorado card and Olympia on the Washington card: the state's government meets there. Then our card: the caption, not the size, says where the government meets; a capital is often not the biggest city. Use the indicator, as on day 47: "The capital is ___ of the dot." **Ask.** "Why is this city the capital?" Expect: the state's government meets there.

#### 10 min. Supported work

Pairs put a token on the capital of the state outline card and say "___ is the capital because the state's government meets there." Then a token on Washington, D.C. You hear the first six, a minute each. **Ask.** "What government meets in Washington, D.C.?" Expect: the nation's government.

#### 2 min. Share

One pair says its sentence. Add a board card: "Why is this city the capital?"

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

All mark exit card 48. [[s05-X48]] [[s05-X48b]] Read aloud: "Circle the capital." Then: "Portland has more people." The first six come to you, 20 seconds each: "Why is Salem the capital?" Look for: the government meets there, not size (error G), not a building (error L).

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No state card: draw your state's outline and star its capital.

**Skip.** The pairs and the heard six.

- **5 min. City or building.** Draw a city with one building circled. Put a star on the city.
  **Say.** "Is the capital the city or the building?" Expect: the city.
- **15 min. Our capital.** Find your state's capital on its usa.gov page. Star it on a drawn outline and put a token on it.
  **Say.** "Why is this city the capital?" Expect: the state's government meets there.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X48]] [[s05-X48b]] Show the card and read it. Your child circles.
  **Say.** "Why is Salem the capital?" Expect: the government meets there, not because it is bigger.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the Washington card. Longer: direction words.

### Words and supports

Read the student page aloud; the word to copy is capital. Words: capital, city, government. A child may pass or point.
