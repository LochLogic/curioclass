# Exit cards, days 48 to 51

Each exit has a paper card every child marks and a spoken question heard from the day's rotating six. Say aloud first: "These cards are made up," except where a card is labeled real. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each invented card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading. Keep these closed until the day. No card reuses the locator, the state cards, the Harbor Map, Cattail Crossing, Heron Valley or a repair card.

## Day 48 exit card: Oregon (real places)

**Picture:** an outline of Oregon, a real state, labeled real. A star labeled Salem and a dot labeled Portland. A key: a star is the capital, a dot is another city. A caption read aloud: "Portland has more people."

**Mark:** Circle the capital, the star or the dot. **Spoken:** "Why is Salem the capital?"

If the class lives in Oregon, use Texas: an outline of Texas, a star labeled Austin, a dot labeled Houston, the caption "Houston has more people."

## Day 49 exit card: Pine Dock

**Picture:** a map of Pine Dock from above. A lake across the page. A dock on the near shore beside a mill, and a row of benches beside the mill. A key: a short line is a dock, a square is a mill, a row of dots is benches. Beside the map, a record card read aloud: "Boats bring grain to the dock. The mill grinds it." Two word cards: "the record tells who lived here before the mill" and "the record does not say."

**Mark (every child, one ask):** Place a token on one of the two word cards. The teacher reads both aloud. **Spoken (the second six, a separate ask):** "Why did the mill grow here? Point to the clue."

## Day 50 exit card: linked outlines and a fresh world view

**Picture:** three linked outlines in a row: our town (a dot), our state and our country, each labeled. Below them, a fresh flat world view with the continents and oceans named.

**First mark:** Circle our state on the country outline. **Second mark:** Circle our continent on the world view. **Spoken, one ask chosen from the record sheet:** "What kind of place is the part named South America?" or "What kind of place is the blue part named Indian?"

## Day 51 exit card: Fern River and Elm Lake

**Picture:** a landform map. A mountain at the top drawn as brown rings. Fern River, drawn as a blue band starting below the mountain with arrows pointing down, running into a blue oval labeled Elm Lake. A key: a blue band with arrows is a river, a blue oval is a lake, brown rings are a mountain.

**Mark:** Circle the lake. **Spoken, one ask chosen from the record sheet:** "How might the lake help people?" or "What is the river doing on the map?"
