# Exit cards, days 48 to 51

Each exit has a paper card every child marks and a spoken question heard from the day's rotating six. Say aloud first: "These cards are made up," except where a card is labelled real. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each invented card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading. Keep these closed until the day. No card reuses the locator, the state cards, the Harbor Map, Cattail Crossing, Heron Valley or a repair card.

## Day 48 exit card: Oregon (real places)

**Picture:** an outline of Oregon, a real state, labelled real. A star labelled Salem and a dot labelled Portland. A key: a star is the capital, a dot is another city. A caption read aloud: "Portland has more people."

**Mark:** Circle the capital, the star or the dot. **Spoken:** "Why is Salem the capital?"

If the class lives in Oregon, use Texas: an outline of Texas, a star labelled Austin, a dot labelled Houston, the caption "Houston has more people."

## Day 49 exit card: Ash

**Picture:** a map of Ash from above. A river across the page. A ferry landing beside a row of shops on the near bank, and a row of benches beside the shops. A key: a short line is a landing, a square is a shop, a row of dots is benches. Beside the map, a record card read aloud: "Customers ride the ferry to the shops."

**First mark:** Circle the map clue that matches the record, the ferry landing or the benches. **Second mark:** Place a token on one of two word cards: the record tells who lived here before the shops, or the record does not say. The teacher reads both aloud. **Spoken:** "Why did shops grow here?"

## Day 50 exit card: linked outlines and a fresh world view

**Picture:** three linked outlines in a row: our town (a dot), our state and our country, each labelled. Below them, a fresh flat world view with the continents and oceans named.

**First mark:** Circle our state on the country outline. **Second mark:** Circle our continent on the world view. **Spoken, one ask chosen from the record sheet:** "What kind of place is the part named South America?" or "What kind of place is the blue part named Indian?"

## Day 51 exit card: Fern River and Elm Lake

**Picture:** a landform map. A mountain at the top drawn as brown rings. Fern River, drawn as a blue band starting below the mountain with arrows pointing down, running into a blue oval labelled Elm Lake. A key: a blue band with arrows is a river, a blue oval is a lake, brown rings are a mountain.

**Mark:** Circle the lake. **Spoken, one ask chosen from the record sheet:** "How might the lake help people?" or "What is the river doing on the map?"
