# Day 32 cases: two ways to decide

Say first: "These cards are made up." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. One person acts in each picture. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each card. The teacher reads every card aloud. Safety and a child's turn are never put to a vote; say so once today.

## Hook case 1: the reading corner (three pictures)

Picture 1: Ana holds a cushion and points to the window. Read: "Ana wants cushions by the window."
Picture 2: Ben holds a book and points to a shelf in the same corner. Read: "Ben wants the shelf in that corner."
Picture 3: the corner from above, with cushions at the window, the shelf by the wall, and room left over. Read: "The corner has room for both."

## Hook case 2: two storybooks (three pictures)

Picture 1: a group on a rug and one book cover with a red fox. Read: "Some of the group want the book about Red Fox."
Picture 2: the same group and a book cover with a blue whale. Read: "Some of the group want the book about Blue Whale."
Picture 3: a short bar for rug time and only one book open. Read: "There is time for one book today."

## Retrieval case: scenery and costumes (three pictures)

Picture 1: a group painting a tall tree for a play. Read: "One group wants to paint scenery."
Picture 2: a group holding cloth and scissors. Read: "Another group wants to make costumes."
Picture 3: the room with a paint table and a cloth table, and space for both. Read: "There is time and space for both."
Ask: "How could talking help everyone agree? Give a reason." The teacher listens for three parts, kept apart: listening, a workable agreement, a reason.

## The two ways, with puppets (day 32 teaching)

Puppet Pip and puppet Dot speak one line at a time. For the reading corner, Pip says, "I want cushions by the window, because the light is good." Dot says, "I want the shelf there, because the books are close." Then both say, "We can do both." For the storybooks, the class votes with blocks in a closed cup, or passes, and the teacher counts aloud five and three. The choice with more votes wins and the other reason stays on Our Decision Record.

## Six sort cards (two hoops: talk can find a way for both; only one can happen, so vote if still apart)

Talk can find a way for both:
1. Two children want the paint table and the clay table, and the room has space for both.
2. One child wants to sing and one wants to clap during music, and they can do both together.
3. Two children both want the box of crayons, and the box holds plenty.

Only one can happen, so vote if still apart:
4. The class can visit one place on outing day, the pond or the market.
5. The one fish tank has room for one class pet, a snail or a goldfish.
6. The class door can be painted one color, green or orange.

## For the teacher

The hoop headings give the answers; the cards are for teaching. A wrong answer is voiced by a puppet or "someone might say", never by a named child. None of these cases is used in an exit card.
