# Section 4.1 opener: ways to decide, votes and our first election

## What the section does

Three days, 32 to 34, of 30 minutes. Unit 4 asks: what is the fairest way for a group to decide? Day 32 retrieves kindergarten's class decision and teaches two ways: talk until both ways fit, and vote when only one can happen and the group is still apart. Day 33 teaches the election, a vote that chooses a person for a job, with equality (every vote counts the same), fairness (the same rules for all) and accepting a fair result. Day 34 is the class's own secret-ballot election for a made-up garden captain.

## How the days run

Day 32: hook (5), retrieval and repair (5), teaching (9), sort six cases (6), exit (5). Day 33: warm-up and repair (4), teaching (8), count the ballots (6), supported work (7), exit (5). Day 34: warm-up and repair (3), two candidates (5), rules and ballots (4), count and read (6), look back and record (7), exit (5). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Talk first, vote when still apart.** Many differences can be settled by a change both sides accept. A vote is for when only one thing can happen.
- **An election chooses a person for a job.** A vote on a matter chooses a thing or a plan. Both are votes; only the first is an election.
- **Equality and fairness.** Each vote counts the same, whatever a person's age or friends. The same rules hold for all, including the winner.
- **The secret ballot.** People mark in private so no one is pressured; two counters count every ballot in front of everyone; the tally is public. A private mark is not a secret count.
- **A fair result can be disagreed with.** A child can say "I disagree," accept the count and ask the counters to check.
- **Reading a tally.** Line up the marks under each name; the longer row wins. Two choices only. Math unit 1 already taught comparing totals, so nothing new is taught here.
- **Every case is invented.** The candidates, clubs and children are made up and said to be made up. Safety and a child's turn are never put to a vote.

## The errors, in plain words

- **Q and R:** the loudest picks; talking never works. Show the equal-turns card; replay a case where talk found both.
- **A and I:** every decision is an election; extra votes. Sort person or thing; line up one mark each.
- **B and J:** private means uncounted; no one may disagree. Re-run a covered box; two puppets say the pair of sentences.
- **T and C:** the name I like wins; winning permits any order. Line up marks; the job card still holds the captain.

A wrong answer is voiced by a puppet or "someone might say", never by a named child.

## Care

No child is asked how they voted or how anyone at home votes; a child may pass. Candidates are never classmates, so no child loses a vote. An adult can mark a ballot as the child directs, privately. The teacher never announces a child's choice.

## Supplies for the section

The day 32 cases and sort cards, Our Decision Record, the kite club cards, the garden election cards, ballots and tally sheets, folders, two covered boxes, a closed cup and blocks, two puppets, the printed exit cards, the repair cards and the unit 4 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Scrap slips, a paper bag, a hand-drawn tally, two socks as puppets.
