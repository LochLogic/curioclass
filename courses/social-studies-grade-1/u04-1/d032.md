# Day 32: Two fair ways to decide

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 31 left no repair time; its misses stay on the record sheet. Keep exit card 32 closed. The first six are heard today. Say: "These cards are made up."

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook

Show the reading corner (cushions or shelf, room for both) and the storybooks (Red Fox or Blue Whale, time for one). Children point to the case talking could settle. Read aloud: "What is the fairest way for a group to decide?" Say once: safety and a child's turn are never voted on.

#### 5 min. Retrieval and repair

Review: you learned agree, vote and Our Decision Record in kindergarten: listen, ask why, look for a change both accept, vote only if still apart. Show scenery and costumes. Partners answer for a minute: "How could talking help? Give a reason." Hear three pairs. Mark listening, agreement and reason apart (shown, shown with help, not yet seen). If many miss a part, model it in teaching; if not, repair during the sort. Error Q: the loudest group picks. Error R: vote at once.

#### 9 min. Teaching first

Pip and Dot speak in turn. Talk: Pip wants cushions, Dot the shelf; hear each reason, find a change both accept: do both. Vote, only when still apart and only one can happen: each child drops a block in the closed cup for Red Fox or Blue Whale, or passes. Count aloud; more votes wins, the other reason stays on the record. Mark both ways on Our Decision Record. **Ask.** "When is talking enough? When do we need a vote?" Expect: when a change fits both; when only one can happen.

#### 6 min. Sort six cases

Fifteen pairs place six cards in two hoops, "talk can find a way for both" and "only one can happen, so vote if still apart": paint or clay table; sing or clap; crayon box; outing place; class pet; door color. The class checks by pointing.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Everyone marks exit card 32. [[s04-X32]] Read aloud: "Circle how the group decides." The first six come to you, half a minute each: "Why is that a fair way for them to decide?" Look for: everyone heard; each vote counts the same, or a vote is for when still apart. The rest draw one way to choose.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No puppets or hoops: use two spoons and two plates. Blocks can be coins or buttons.

**Skip.** The sort and the six.

- **5 min. Two cases.** Tell a made-up case: two children want a cushion and a shelf in one corner, and the corner has room for both. Ask which way helps.
- **15 min. Talk or vote.** Tell a second made-up case: a family of animals may hear one story, fox or whale. Your child drops a button in a cup for each.
  **Say.** "When is talking enough? When do we need a vote?" Expect: when a change fits both; when only one can happen.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X32]] Show the card with three ways to decide. Your child circles one.
  **Say.** "Why is that fair?" Expect: everyone is heard and each vote counts the same.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: four sort cards and the exit. Longer: a new talk card.

### Words and supports

Words with pictures: vote, agree, fair. Frame: "I want ___ because ___." Pointing and home words count. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 33 teaches the election. Its warm-up repairs this exit: error Q, two puppets, loud then equal turns; error R, replay scenery and costumes.
