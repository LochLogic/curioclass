# Day 37: belonging, voting and a term on the calendar

Say first: "This town and this pond-watch group are made up." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. One person acts on each card. Print "made up" in small letters on each card. The teacher reads every card aloud.

## The town picture: everyone belongs

A picture of many kinds of people in a town square: a child with a book bag, a grown-up with a stroller, an older neighbor, a worker with a cart. Read: "Everyone who lives here belongs. Everyone has rights, such as learning at school and being heard." Then, said once and calmly: "A citizen is a member of a town, city or nation under its rules. Citizens who are old enough and follow the voting rules choose the leaders. Children cannot vote in government elections yet. Neighbors who cannot vote still belong. A class may vote, as we did."

No child's or family's status is asked, and no child reports on home. A child may pass.

## The term strip (a calendar strip with three cards)

The class calendar strip with three cards laid along it for the made-up pond-watch group. Card 1: election day, with a ballot box. Card 2: end of term, with a full-stop mark on the calendar. Card 3: voters choose again, with a ballot box. Read: "The pond-watch captain, Wes, was chosen on election day for a term. A term is a set time a leader serves. When the term ends, voters choose again. They may pick Wes again or someone new. To choose the same leader again is to re-elect."

## For the teacher

Terms are lengths of time set before the election; the length varies and is not taught here. Belonging is not the same as being able to vote. None of these cases is used in an exit card: not a mayor whose term is ending.
