# Day 37: the Navajo Nation chooses its leaders today

Read aloud, then ask the class for one thing they heard. Do not act it out, and show no pictures of people. Show only a plain name card with the words Navajo Nation, in text.

## The read-aloud (about 50 words)

"The Navajo Nation has its own government today. The people of the Navajo Nation elect a President, a Vice President and delegates to the Navajo Nation Council. The delegates are elected from their districts. Other nations have their own governments, chosen in their own ways."

## For the teacher

This is one nation's way and is not every nation's way. Name only the offices the nation's pages name; give no term length and no numbers. Use the present tense: the nation and its government are alive today. The nation's name is its own name. Credit once, aloud: "This is our own telling of what the Navajo Nation's pages say." The sensitivity read comes before this is taught.

If a child says Native nations are only from long ago, say once: "The Navajo Nation chooses its leaders today." Do not ask any child about their own family or nation.
