# Day 36: the town card and the shared jar

Say first: "This town is made up." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. One person acts on each card. Print "made up" in small letters on each card. The teacher reads every card aloud.

## The town card (read aloud)

A picture of a made-up town with a school, a mended road, a park with a bench and a fire station, and workers at each. Read: "The town government makes rules, such as: the park closes at dusk. Town workers carry out the rules. The government provides a school, a mended road, a park bench and a fire station."

Government is many leaders and workers sharing the tasks, not one person. A park bench is a good (a thing). Mending the road is a service (work done). Words with pictures: government, good, service.

## The shared jar (a drawing)

A big jar. Many grown-ups drop coins in. An arrow points from the jar to the school, the road workers, the park and the fire station. Read: "Many people put money in together. This is called taxes. The government uses it for what everyone uses." No sums, no amounts, and no child says what any family pays.

## Six sort cards

Provided by the government: a park bench; road mending; a fire station.
Not provided by the government: a toy shop; a bakery; a neighbor's swing.
Children sort the six, then name each provided card a good or a service: the bench is a good, the road mending is a service, the fire station is a building and the firefighters' work is a service.

## For the teacher

The jar is told simply: taxes are money many people pay together to pay for things everyone uses. A business such as the toy shop or the bakery, and a family's swing, are not government.
