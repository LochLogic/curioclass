# Section 7.2 opener: hearing views, choosing and acting

## What the section does

Three days, 72 to 74, of 30 minutes. Day 72: more than one view. Two adults who see the class's problem differently are heard, and each view is written with its reason and what we saw. A view that differs is not a bad view. Day 73: the class lists ways to help, filters them, and chooses one by secret-ballot vote. Day 74: the class takes the first safe step through the school, with an adult, and writes the first line of the action log.

## How the days run

Day 72: reopen (4), return and repair (5), teaching with Pim and Dov (6), two real views (6), fill the columns (3), both helped (3), exit (3). Day 73: reopen (2), warm-up and repair (3), ideas and a filter (6), a vote not an election (3), cast the ballots (5), count (4), record (2), exit (5). Day 74: warm-up and repair (3), plan the steps (6), carry out the first step (14), action log (2), exit (5). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A view is what one person thinks, with a reason and something they saw.** Two views can sit side by side. Neither is ranked and both stay on the wall after the vote, with the proposal that did not win, for the return on day 95.
- **The two views are real adults' words,** with the date they were said. Use a role card and no face. Where only one adult view exists, say so aloud and read a second view labeled "possible view, invented for practice".
- **A vote picks a plan, not a leader.** An election picks a leader (unit 4). Every child in the class may put in one slip; adults do not vote on the class plan.
- **A secret ballot is private and still counted:** a folded slip, a box, counters who read the marks aloud, and no name on any slip. A tie: a runoff between the top two; if still tied, try both in turn.
- **Safety, rights and access are never put to a vote.** Every proposal is checked before the ballot: inside the school, an adult can help, nothing unknown to touch (no unknown waste, road, water or sharp thing), no money, no leaving school. Children act only with an adult nearby. A child who finds something unknown stops and tells an adult.
- **We start with a small part that is ours,** and say which parts adults do. We never say our class will fix everything.
- **Offer seated, drawing and recording roles** on day 74.

## The errors, in plain words

- **D:** a view that differs from mine is bad. Put each reason beside its view and say it back.
- **I:** the side that is winning puts in the slips. Rehearse one slip each.
- **P:** our class can fix every problem. Sort ideas into "we can" and "adults do".
- **H:** a child may take any step alone. Sort safe and unsafe step cards, with the stop-and-tell rule.

A wrong idea is voiced by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child.

## Care

Nobody is asked to say which view is theirs or whom they voted for. The class's real problem is the class's own choice, and the course takes no side on it. No ballot is shown to anyone.

## Supplies for the section

The Pim and Dov cards, the two-column sheet, role cards, slips and a box with a slot, tally sheets, proposal cards, the idea filter card, step cards, the safe-step check card, the action log, Our Decision Record (kindergarten chart, reused), the exit cards, the repair cards and the unit 7 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Scrap-paper slips, a hat or a box, a crayon, hand-drawn cards, a page for the action log.
