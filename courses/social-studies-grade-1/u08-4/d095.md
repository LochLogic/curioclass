# Day 95: Show what you know about people and choices

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 94 said today is part two of the check: a letter read aloud, our Decision Record and a town problem. It teaches nothing. Keep the packet closed until now. Bring Our Decision Record from unit 7 and the proposal not chosen; check what it says before class. Open the check 2 record sheet, items 8 to 13, and number three groups of six from children not yet heard on a spoken part. Never ask a child about their own home or languages.

### The day

#### 3 min. Directions

Say: "Today I find out what you can do with people and choices. I will not help you." Say: "Cottonwood and Tamsin Bay are made up. Our record is ours." Read each prompt aloud once, again if asked. Pointing, drawing and dictation count.

#### 10 min. The Cottonwood letter

Read the letter once. Paper: circle the detail that shows these neighbors are united [[sc2-E8]] [[sc2-E8b]]; then draw, say or dictate a claim with one piece of evidence [[sc2-E9]]. Group 1 comes for three minutes, one ask a child chosen by the record sheet from [[sc2-E8]] [[sc2-E8b]] or [[sc2-E9]]. Take dictation for the others, a minute each, from up to six who ask.

#### 8 min. Our Decision Record

Read our record aloud. Pairs hold the picture copy. Paper: point to what our class chose and to the proposal it did not choose [[sc2-E10]]. Group 2 comes for three minutes, one ask a child from [[sc2-E10]] or [[sc2-E11]]: what we gave up, or why we had to choose.

#### 6 min. Tamsin Bay

Read the fountain case aloud. Children never touch or fix a fountain. Group 3 comes for three minutes, one ask a child from [[sc2-E12]] or [[sc2-E13]]. The others draw one way people in the town could work on it.

#### 3 min. Hand in

Collect every sheet. Mark each part shown, shown with help or not yet seen, about a minute a child after class. A claim is shown when its evidence is in the letter. Three groups hear 18 children; the others are marked on paper only, and their spoken parts stay not yet seen for day 96.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No packet: draw the letter's ballot box and festival lanterns on scraps.

**Skip.** The record sheet and the groups.

- **25 min. The assessment.** Say the letter is made up. Read: bakers, bus drivers and boat builders live in Cottonwood; neighbors speak different languages and keep different festivals; all vote with one kind of ballot box; all follow the same laws. Your child says how the neighbors are united and different, with one detail. Say a family has time for one of two Saturday jobs; your child says what is given up by choosing. Read: a park has one fountain and a long line. Your child says one way a town could help and one safe way a class could, with a grown-up. Give no help.
  **Say.** "How are they united? What do you give up? How could a class help?" Expect: the same laws; the other job; a note to the town workers with a grown-up.
- **10 min. Respond.** Your child points to one part to try again.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: items 8, 10 and 12 first; the rest wait for day 96. Longer: nothing is added.

### Words and supports

Read every prompt aloud. Pointing, drawing, dictation, a scribe, a private hearing and home-language words count. Frame: "I think ___, because ___."

### Tomorrow

Day 96 re-teaches from day 94's items and day 95's items, in groups by letter, then each child tries a fresh case.
