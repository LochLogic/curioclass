# Day 95 packet: check 2, day two (closed until day 95)

Nothing here is taught before day 95. Read each card aloud once, again when asked. Say first: "Cottonwood and Tamsin Bay are made up." Print "invented for practice" on each. Pointing, drawing and dictation count.

## The Cottonwood letter (items 8 and 9)

Read aloud, once: "Cottonwood is a made-up town in the United States. Our town has bakers, bus drivers and boat builders. We speak different languages at home and keep different festivals. Every spring the grown citizens of Cottonwood mark a ballot for mayor and drop it in the same kind of box. Everyone in town follows the same laws, such as stopping at the red light." Two pictures: a ballot box with a slip, and two festival lanterns. Nobody is asked about their own home or language.

## Our Decision Record (items 10 and 11)

The class's own record from unit 7: the problem, the proposals, what the class chose, and the proposal it did not choose. A picture copy for each pair; the teacher reads it aloud.

## Tamsin Bay (items 12 and 13)

Read aloud: "In Tamsin Bay, a made-up town, the park has one drinking fountain, and the line gets long on hot days." One picture: a line of people at a fountain. Children never touch or fix the fountain. A safe class step is small and goes with a grown-up: a note, a poster, a count.

## How the day runs

Three groups of six are heard, about three minutes each, one ask a child chosen by the record sheet. A child not heard stays not yet seen and is heard on day 96. Mark each part shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Either side of a united-and-different claim meets when its evidence is in the letter. The class's own record sets what counts as given up.
