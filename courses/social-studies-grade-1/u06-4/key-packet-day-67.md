# Teacher key, the day 67 packet

Teacher only. Keep apart from packet-day-67.md; never hand to children.

## Key

### Sources A and B: two streets (items 1 and 2)

**Teacher key for A and B.** A has snow, coats, cleared paths, Japanese writing and a school lunch; B has rain, a roof over a walkway, English and Tamil. Sapporo is farther north than Singapore. Weather shapes a choice such as a coat or a roof and does not decide a language. B's caption does not tell about school lunch, so that is unknown; not telling is not the same as having none. One picture shows one street at one time.

### Item 3: four fresh symbol views (E6 to E9)

**Teacher key.** Flag: the country and its states, the stars or the stripes. Seal: the eagle stands for the country; the olive branch suggests peace; the arrows, the power to defend. White House: the president's work. Statue: the torch stands for liberty and welcome; a hope, not proof of equal treatment. Accept a supported alternative.

### Item 4: a new seal view and phrase cards (E10, E11)

**Teacher key.** Out of many, one means many states join as one country, and people may be different. The Pledge describes loyalty and freedom and fair treatment for everyone. Saying it is a choice. No one is asked to say it.

### Item 5: two hummed passages (E12, E13)

**Teacher key.** The first tune and the grain phrase belong to "America the Beautiful"; the song admires the fields and the land. The second tune and the flag phrase belong to "The Star-Spangled Banner"; the flag still flying after the night expresses hope. Sources 65A to 65D. "The flag" or "the landscape" alone is not enough: the child links each phrase to a detail on the card. For a child who cannot hear a tune: title and printed words; record the tune as inaccessible, not incorrect. No one is asked to hum back.

### Item 6: Willow Town, a Constitution line and a seal view (E14 to E16)

**Teacher key.** United: they live under the Constitution and use public roads. Different: English, Spanish and Cherokee; born here or moved here; different beliefs. The seal's words show many joined as one. A country can share government without everyone living alike. Fiction illustrates an idea; it is not evidence about a real town. No one is asked about their own beliefs.
