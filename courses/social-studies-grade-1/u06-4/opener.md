# Section 6.4 opener: what holds a country together

## What the section does

Three days, 66 to 68, of 30 minutes. Unit 6 asks: what holds a country of so many different people together? Day 66: each child answers with a claim, one piece of evidence, a reason and a closing, with no model on the wall. Day 67: the unit check, an observation on a fresh packet; nothing new is taught. Day 68: respond. Each child gets their own sheet back, groups reteach the component most missed, and every child tries again on a new card.

## How the days run

Day 66: return and repair (5), reopen (3), step back (4), independent work (12), exit (3), look back (3). Day 67: orientation (5), then item 6 (5), item 3 (5), item 1 (4), item 2 (3), item 4 (4) and item 5 (4); five groups of six are heard in two minutes each. Day 68: return evidence (5), reteach by component (10), fresh attempt with the exit (10), compare work (5). Every child marks the exit; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six on days 66 and 68.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **What holds a country together** has several good answers: shared government (the Constitution and laws), shared history, shared ideals (liberty and justice for all) and symbols that people read in different ways. Each answer needs a detail from a source.
- **Unity and diversity need separate evidence.** One detail does not show both. Shared roads, a courthouse or the Constitution show what is shared; languages, festivals and beliefs show what differs.
- **The towns in the packet and the day 68 exit are made up** and the teacher says so. A made-up record illustrates an idea and is not evidence about any real town.
- **Day 67 is scored from the fresh packet only.** Practice on days 62 to 66 is not scored. Nobody is asked to say the Pledge, stand or sing. A child who cannot hear a tune matches title and printed words, and the tune match is recorded as inaccessible, not as a miss.
- **The packet shows nothing taught.** Every view, caption, phrase and record in it is first seen on day 67 and is kept closed until then.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, B, I** (items 1 and 2): one picture shows everyone; weather decides language; writing is a natural feature.
- **D, K** (item 3): naming is meaning; the statue proves equal welcome.
- **C, E** (item 4): belonging needs sameness or saying the words.
- **D, E** (item 5): recognizing a tune is understanding; everyone must sing.
- **C, G, M** (item 6): belonging needs one language; a liking is evidence; one detail shows both sides.

## Care

No child is asked about home, family, language, belief, money or where their family comes from. A child not heard stays "not yet seen". Day 68 returns each child's own sheet and names no other child.

## Supplies for the section

Three source cards for day 66, the blank claim frame (sheet-claim-frame-unit6.md), the day 67 packet (closed until the day), the record sheet with six rows for each child, the exit cards, the written move cards and the day 68 fresh-try cards, tokens, crayons and the unit 6 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn source cards, a copied Constitution line, scrap-paper tokens, humming.
