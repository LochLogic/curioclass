# Day 67 packet (withheld)

Keep this packet closed and out of sight until day 67. None of it is taught before then. Cover every completed teaching example and every model in the room on day 67; only blank frames stay up. Read each item aloud; every source is read aloud. Say aloud: "Willow Town is made up. The Sapporo and Singapore streets are drawings of places that are real. The symbols are real." Print "invented for practice" on the Willow Town record. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Accept a point, a token, a drawing, dictation or familiar-language speech. Judge meaning and evidence, not drawing skill. Five groups of six are heard for two minutes a group in the working minutes of items 1, 2 and 4; items 3 and 6 are heard from the rotation.

## Sources A and B: two streets (items 1 and 2)

**Source A, Sapporo (drawing):** a street with apartment buildings, snow piled beside them, a cleared path, people in warm coats, and a street sign in Japanese writing. Caption, read aloud: "Snow lies beside these apartment buildings. People clear paths and wear warm coats. Japanese appears on this street sign. The city of Sapporo says its city schools serve a school lunch." Sources 67A (the city's own school lunch page, in our words) and 56A.

**Source B, Singapore (drawing):** a street with apartments, a roof over a walkway, rain falling, people in light clothes, and a street sign with two lines of lettering, one in English and one in Tamil. Caption, read aloud: "Singapore is warm and rainy. A roof covers a walkway beside apartments. The sign uses English and Tamil. Singapore's government says people keep their own mother languages and use English together. This picture does not tell us what school lunches are like there." Sources 56C (the weather account) and 56D (the multicultural account), in our words.

**A globe view** with a key (a dot with a name is a city), a direction indicator labelled N pointing to the top of the page, and the dots Sapporo, Singapore and Nairobi. Tokens.

**Item 1 (E1 to E3):** circle what shows the weather on each picture (snow on A, rain on B); point to the lunch words in the caption of A; circle yes or no for "Could everyone in this place live exactly like this picture?" Spoken (one, chosen by the record sheet): see items.csv.

**Item 2 (E4, E5):** place a token on Sapporo and on Singapore; circle one cultural feature (made or learned) and one environmental feature (land, water, weather) in each picture. Spoken: which is farther north; how the weather might affect daily life.

## Item 3: four fresh symbol views (E6 to E9)

- **The flag over a school yard.** A flag with fifty small stars and thirteen stripes, flying over a school yard. Caption: "A flag flies over a school yard. It has fifty small stars and thirteen stripes." Detail: the stars, one for each state, or the thirteen stripes. (62B, 62E)
- **The Great Seal carved in a stone floor.** An eagle holds an olive branch in one foot and arrows in the other; a banner says E pluribus unum. Caption: "The Great Seal is carved in a round stone set in a floor." Detail: the olive branch, peace. (62A)
- **The White House at dusk.** The building with its lights on. Caption: "The White House at dusk with its lights on. The president's home and offices are inside." Detail: the president's work. (62C)
- **The Statue of Liberty with a ferry.** The statue with a ferry in front and a plaque on the base with the poem line: "I lift my lamp beside the golden door." Caption: "The Statue of Liberty with a ferry in front. The plaque has a line from the poem." Detail: the torch and the poem's welcome. (63A, 63B)

Mark: circle the part of each picture that shows what it stands for. Spoken (one symbol per child): "What does it stand for, and which detail shows it?" For the statue: "Does the statue show everyone was welcomed the same?"

## Item 4: a new seal view and phrase cards (E10, E11)

A new seal view with the banner E pluribus unum. A phrase card "Out of many, one". The whole Pledge read aloud once as a text (64A), and a phrase card "with liberty and justice for all". Meaning pictures: freedom and fair treatment; a flag; a song.

## Item 5: two hummed passages (E12, E13)

The adult hums a short passage of each tune, one after the other, for the whole class. Title cards: "America the Beautiful"; "The Star-Spangled Banner". Phrase cards: "amber waves of grain" with a picture of fields; "our flag was still there" with a picture of a flag flying in the morning. Tokens.

## Item 6: Willow Town, a Constitution line and a seal view (E14 to E16)

**Source C, the Willow Town record (invented, read aloud):** "In Willow Town, neighbors speak English, Spanish and Cherokee. Some were born here; others moved here. Some follow a religion; others do not. They use public roads and live under the United States Constitution. They mark national events in different ways." **A fresh Constitution line, read aloud (59A):** "The executive Power shall be vested in a President of the United States of America." **A new seal view** with its banner.

**Mark:** circle the detail that shows these neighbors are united; circle the detail that shows they are different; place a claim, a detail, a reason and a closing. **Spoken (one):** see items.csv.
