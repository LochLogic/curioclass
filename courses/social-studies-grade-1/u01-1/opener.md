# Section 1.1 opener: big questions and small ones

## What the section does

Two days, 1 and 2, of 30 minutes. On day 1 the class sees two invented picture panels of a group making a paper mural, hears the unit's big question (What makes a group work well?), and learns that a big question is too large to answer at once, so we ask smaller ones. On day 2 the class learns four ways to ask (history, geography, civics, economics) and, for each of the unit's three smaller questions, one fact to find out and one important word. Nothing from these two days is scored. The scored versions come on day 9 (items E1 and E2).

## How the days run

Day 1: a six-minute hook with the panels, a five-minute wonder wall, a seven-minute worked case (the class block corner), a six-minute look at the three supporting questions, and a six-minute exit. Day 2: a five-minute reopen, eight minutes on the four ways to ask, seven minutes sorting four new mural questions, six minutes on facts and words, and a four-minute exit. Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six (day 1 the first six on the record sheet, day 2 the second six).

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A question is not a statement.** "Where does the snail live?" asks something. "Snails are slow" tells something. Children said "I wonder" in kindergarten; here we check that what they say is a question.
- **A big question needs smaller ones.** "How can the block corner work well for everyone?" cannot be answered in one breath. "Who uses the blocks?" can be found out. Smaller questions are called supporting questions because they help answer the big one.
- **A subject is a way of asking.** History asks what it was like before. Geography asks where. Civics asks how a group works together. Economics asks what we have and what we choose. One question can fit two subjects; accept either when the child gives a reason. Say "four ways to ask", never "four kinds of children".
- **Each smaller question needs a fact and a word.** We name them aloud only. The words (belong, rule, duty, role, goal) are taught on days 3, 4 and 6.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A: a statement is taken for a question.** "Snails are slow" is something we tell. Read three cards aloud, a question or a statement each. Children stand for a question and sit for a statement. Then turn one statement into a question together.
- **F: a subject label with no idea behind it.** A child circles the map and says "the slide" but cannot say what it would tell us about before. Show a before card and an after card for a door and ask which way of asking looks at before.

A wrong answer is always voiced by "someone might say", never by a named child. No child reports on home or family.

## Supplies for the section

Chart paper and tape for the question board and the wonder wall, a marker, the two mural panels, the block corner cards, three supporting question cards, the four subject cards (a large set, and a small set for each child), four mural question cards, the fact and word cards, and the printed exit cards. The record sheet has a row for each child and names the rotating six. Everything pictured is invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## A room with nothing

Two sheets of scrap paper stand for the panels. Chart paper is the board. Four hand motions and four drawn symbols stand for the subject cards. A projector is not needed: if there is one, show the pictures on one screen and let children mark on paper, never on the screen.
