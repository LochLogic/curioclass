# Block corner cards (day 1)

The worked case for a big question and smaller ones. It is ours and invented, and it is a made-up class. Say so aloud: "This is a pretend class and a pretend block corner."

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no printed words except the question on each card.

## The picture

**Picture:** A corner of a classroom with a low open shelf of wooden blocks, a rug, and a tall block tower in the middle. Three children are around the tower: one reaches for a block from the shelf, one holds the base steady, one waits with a block in hand. A bin sits empty by the shelf with a picture of blocks on its front.

## The big question card

**Card text:** How can the block corner work well for everyone?

Hold it high with arms wide. Say that it is too big to answer at once, so we ask smaller questions that help. This is a big question.

## Three smaller question cards

Each card has one question in large letters and a tiny picture clue.

1. **Who uses the blocks?** Clue: three small faces in a row.
2. **Where do the blocks go when we finish?** Clue: a block with an arrow to the bin.
3. **How do we take turns on a big build?** Clue: two hands, one holding a block out, one waiting.

Hold each one low with small hands. These are supporting questions: smaller questions that help us answer the big one.

## Partners

Partners tell each other one more smaller question for the block corner. Fifteen pairs talk at once; the teacher listens to three pairs. Any question that helps meets the aim; a statement is not yet a question (error A) and is answered with "Can you ask that as a question?". The teacher writes two of the questions heard on cards for the question board.
