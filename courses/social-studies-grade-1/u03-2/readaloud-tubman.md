# Harriet Tubman read-aloud (day 22; used again on day 28)

Read aloud by the teacher twice, with the four drawings. Say first: "This is our own short telling of a page from the National Park Service. It is about real people." Print the credit once in large type under the first drawing: "Our telling of a National Park Service page."

## The four sentences

1. Long ago in our country, some people were owned by other people and forced to work, which was called slavery, and it was wrong.
2. Harriet Tubman was held in slavery, and in 1849 she escaped.
3. Then she went back about thirteen times, and she helped more than seventy people escape to freedom, including her brothers.
4. She had helpers, such as Jacob Jackson, a free Black farmer who helped her in 1854.

## The four drawings

Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no faces shown, one person in each. Nothing shows hardship being done to a person.

1. An empty field with a fence and a signpost, and a small calendar card that reads "long ago."
2. A woman walking alone along a road toward a distant sign that reads "freedom" (a star), her back to the viewer.
3. A woman leading a short line of people, seen from behind, along a path under stars. A calendar card reads "about 13 trips."
4. A man standing at a farm gate with a hand raised in welcome, beside a farm cart. A card reads "helper."

## Source questions, asked every day

What kind of source is this? (Our telling of a government page.) Who made it? (Us, from the National Park Service page.) Old or new? (New. The events are old.) What can it help us find out? (What Tubman did and who helped her.) Say once: "This is a record of real people."

## For the teacher

Say the hard fact once, in plain words, and move to what people did. Do not act out slavery, escape or travel. Do not ask a child about their own family or history. Tubman had helpers; she did not act alone (D). The page gives no years for her later trips, so the class says "about thirteen trips" and does not claim a year. The class thinks of courage as doing something hard to help others; the evidence is the action, going back to help others reach freedom. Other qualities that the actions support also meet. The 1849 card goes on the leader strip.
