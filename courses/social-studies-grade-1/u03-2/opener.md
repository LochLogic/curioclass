# Section 3.2 opener: leaders from history and what they did

## What the section does

Five days, 22 to 26, of 30 minutes. The class meets four real leaders from short read-alouds that the teacher made from pages by public sources: Harriet Tubman (day 22), Abraham Lincoln (23), Dolores Huerta (24) and Wilma Mankiller (25). Each day the class asks what the leader did, who else acted and what changed, names one quality shown by an action, and adds a card to the timeline strip and a row to the action chart. Day 26 compares two leaders and joins one quality to an action from each.

## How the days run

Days 22 to 25: reopen and repair (5), words with pictures (3), read-aloud with sourcing (7), action chart (4), questions and hearing the six (7), exit (4). Day 26: reopen (5), revisit the board (4), compare in pairs and hear the six (10), share (7), exit (4). Every child marks the exit; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Study actions.** A quality is a way a person is, shown by what they do. The action is the evidence. Being famous is not a quality.
- **Leaders had helpers.** Each row has "who else acted." No one in these stories acted alone.
- **Each source is checked.** Kind of source, who made it, old or new, what it helps us find out. The read-alouds are the class's own words from public pages, credited aloud. Lincoln's paper is old and real; the class hears one sentence from it.
- **Limits.** Lincoln's proclamation did not free everyone or end slavery everywhere. Huerta died on October 28, 2026, and is spoken of in the past tense. The Cherokee Nation governs itself today.
- **The same quality, different jobs.** Two leaders with different jobs can show the same quality.

## The errors, in plain words

- **D: the leader did it alone.** Name what each helper did on the chart.
- **B: fame is a quality.** Cover the name, read one action, name the quality.
- **E: a liking question.** Sort questions into asks what someone did and asks what we like.
- **O: the record does not say.** Sort "the record says" from "we guess."
- **N: the same quality needs the same job.** Place a new pair of jobs under one quality card.
- **K** is on the day 23 exit and **L** is watched in talk only; one sentence beside the card.

## Care

Slavery is named plainly once, as wrong, and then the class turns to what people did. No one acts out slavery, escape, work in fields or any hardship. No child is asked about their own family, money or a parent's work. If a child names Cesar Chavez, say one sentence: he started the farm workers' union with Huerta, and this class studies her work. Wrong answers are voiced by "someone might say," never by a named child.

## Supplies for the section

Four read-alouds with drawings, the old paper sentence and the split card, the action chart, the strip and its cards, pair question cards, six quality cards and the two-column strip, the repair cards, the printed exit cards, the question board and the unit 3 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Stick figures on scrap paper, told aloud; a string and clothes pins for the strip; the chart drawn on butcher paper.
