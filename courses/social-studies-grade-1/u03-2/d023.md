# Day 23: Abraham Lincoln and a hard decision

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the print of the old paper's sentence. 

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

If many missed day 22's exit, cover the name on the bakery cards, read one action and say the quality it shows (error B); then lay the bridge leader card and helper cards on the chart and say what each did (error D).

#### 3 min. Words with pictures

Review: in kindergarten you heard about President Lincoln on Presidents' Day, and that he helped end slavery. Today's paper was one step. **President**, the leader of the country. **proclamation**, a paper that tells the people something important. Review: responsibility, a job you take on that others count on.

#### 7 min. Read-aloud with sourcing

Say: "This is a very old paper from President Lincoln's government. It is a real record." Read its one sentence aloud once, slowly, with its credit, then the plain words from the page. Read our telling twice with the drawings; say plainly that slavery was wrong.

Review the four source questions.

**Ask.** What kind of source is the old paper? Who made it? Old or new? What does it help us find out? Expect: a government paper; Lincoln's government; old; what Lincoln decided.

#### 5 min. Action chart

Fill row two: did (issued the proclamation), others (many people), changed (and what did not). Place the split card's pictures. If a child says the paper freed everyone, say once: "It said people who were enslaved in some places were free; slavery did not end everywhere that day" (error K). Add the 1863 card. Quality: taking responsibility for a hard decision.

#### 7 min. Questions

Twelve pairs take two question cards, one about Lincoln and one about the others who worked for freedom. Write their questions. Walk the room and hear a few pairs read theirs aloud.

#### 3 min. Exit

Read card 23 aloud; say it is made up. [[s03-X23]] Each child circles one answer alone (2 minutes). Look for: the east pool opened, not all three.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No print: write one word, "free," large.

**Skip.** The partner questions.

- **5 min. Tell.** Say in your own words: Abraham Lincoln was President. In 1863 he issued a paper saying people who were enslaved, owned and forced to work, in the states fighting the country were free. It did not free everyone, and many other people worked for freedom.
- **15 min. Chart.** Your child draws what he did, who else worked and what changed.
  **Say.** "What did the paper say? What did it not do?" Expect: some were free; slavery did not end everywhere that day.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X23]] Tell a made-up story: a town paper says the east pool is open, and the town has three pools. Your child circles what the paper did: opened the east pool, or opened all three pools.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the split card. Longer: a question about the others.

### Words and supports

Frames: "He decided ___." "I think he shows ___ because ___." Pointing counts.
