# Day 23: Abraham Lincoln and a hard decision

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the print of the old paper's sentence. The six: fourth six on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

If many missed day 22's exit, cover the name on the bakery cards, read one action and say the quality it shows (error B); then lay the bridge leader card and helper cards on the chart and say what each did (error D).

#### 3 min. Words with pictures

Review: in kindergarten you heard about President Lincoln on Presidents' Day. **President**, the leader of the country. **proclamation**, a paper that tells the people something important. Review: responsibility, a job you take on that others count on.

#### 7 min. Read-aloud with sourcing

Say: "This is a very old paper from President Lincoln's government. It is a real record." Read its one sentence aloud once, slowly, with its credit, then the plain words from the page. Read our telling twice with the drawings; say plainly that slavery was wrong.

Review the four source questions.

**Ask.** What kind of source is the old paper? Who made it? Old or new? What does it help us find out? Expect: a government paper; Lincoln's government; old; what Lincoln decided.

#### 4 min. Action chart

Fill row two: did (issued the proclamation), others (many people), changed (and what did not). Place the split card's pictures. If a child says the paper freed everyone, say once: "It said people held as slaves in some places were free; slavery did not end everywhere that day" (error K, talk only). Add the 1861 to 1865 card, 1863 pinned under it. Quality: taking responsibility for a hard decision.

#### 7 min. Questions, and hearing the six

Twelve pairs take two question cards, one about Lincoln and one about the others who worked for freedom. Write their questions. The six come to your table, a minute each, to mark exit card 23 and answer the spoken ask.

#### 4 min. Exit

The twelve pairs mark exit card 23. [[s03-X23]] Read aloud: "Circle who opened the school." Spoken to the six: "Ask one question about the builders or the families." Look for: all three together, and a question about what they did.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No print: write one word, "free," large.

**Skip.** The partner questions.

- **5 min. Tell.** Say in your own words: Abraham Lincoln was President from 1861 to 1865. In 1863 he issued a paper saying people held as slaves in the states fighting the country were free. It did not free everyone, and many other people worked for freedom.
- **15 min. Chart.** Your child draws what he did, who else worked and what changed.
  **Say.** "What did the paper say? What did it not do?" Expect: some were free; slavery did not end everywhere that day.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X23]] Tell a made-up story: a leader asks for a school, and builders and families work a year to open it. Your child circles who opened it.
  **Say.** "Ask one question about the builders or the families." Expect: a question about what they did.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the split card. Longer: a question about the others.

### Words and supports

Frames: "He decided ___." "I think he shows ___ because ___." Pointing counts.
