# Repair cards, section 3.2 (days 23 to 26)

New made-up cards for the reopen parts. They never appear in an exit or end item. Say first: "These cards are made up." Each is used for five minutes at most, and only when many children missed the error the day before.

## Day 23 repair for B: action, not fame

A made-up bakery. Card 1: a baker whose name is on a big sign, standing with arms folded. Card 2: a baker with no sign who carries a tray of warm rolls to a neighbor who is sick. Ask: "Cover the name. Read one action. What quality does it show?" Children name caring from the tray, not from the sign.

## Day 23 repair for D: who else acted

A made-up bridge. Lay the leader card and the helper cards on the chart: the leader holds up a plan, one helper carries boards, one helper hammers. Name what each did. "The leader led, and helpers gave a hand."

## Day 24 repair for D: leader and helpers together

A made-up lost-mitten box. A leader sets out a box; three children bring mittens; two children sort them by color. Children say who did what and place the cards on the chart.

## Day 24 repair for E: asks what someone did, or asks what we like

Six question cards read aloud, sorted into two boxes.
- Asks what someone did: "What did the helpers carry?" "Who held the ladder?" "How did the group plan the work?"
- Asks what we like: "Was the paint nice?" "Is the fence tall?" "Do you like the flag?"

## Day 25 repair for D: not the leader alone

Two pictures of a made-up courtyard day. Left: a leader chooses a place for a wall mural alone. Right: a leader asks four helpers where they would like the mural. Children point to the picture that shows listening and say what the helpers did.

## Day 25 repair for E

The same six-card sort as day 24, with new cards: "What did the helpers choose?" "Who drew the plan?" "Was the courtyard big?" "Do you like ducks?"

## Day 26 repair for D

A made-up choir. A leader holds up a song card while singers raise hands for each line. Children name what the singers did.

## Day 26 repair for O: the record says, or we guess

Read a made-up line aloud: "Many people came to the sing-along. The record does not say if all liked it." Children sort two cards: "The record says: many people came." "We guess: all liked it." The teacher names the difference once.

## Watched in talk only: K and L

K: if a child says Lincoln's paper freed everyone, say once, beside the split card: "It said people held as slaves in some places were free; slavery did not end everywhere that day." L: if a child says the Cherokee Nation was only long ago, say once: "The Cherokee Nation governs itself today." No score.

## For the teacher

Wrong answers are voiced by "someone might say," never by a named child. Do not reuse a repair case as an exit case.
