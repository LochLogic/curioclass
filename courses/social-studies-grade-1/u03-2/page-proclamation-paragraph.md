# The old paper: one sentence from the 1863 proclamation (day 23)

Show a large print of this sentence with its credit. The teacher reads it aloud once, slowly. Children do not read it. Say first: "This is a very old paper written by President Lincoln's government. It is a real record."

## The sentence, read aloud

"I do order and declare that all persons held as slaves within said designated States, and parts of States, are, and henceforward shall be free."

Credit printed under it: "Abraham Lincoln, Emancipation Proclamation, January 1, 1863. National Archives."

## In plain words, said right after

"Designated states means the states the paper names, the ones fighting against the country. The paper said that people who were enslaved, owned and forced to work, in those places were free. It did not name every place. It did not end slavery everywhere."

## Words with pictures

- **President**: the leader of the country (review from kindergarten).
- **proclamation**: a paper that tells the people something important.
- **responsibility**: a job you take on that others count on (review).

## For the teacher

The old paper is a federal document of 1863 and is credited to the National Archives. The same text says Lincoln issued it as a war measure. Use only the one sentence above and the plain words. The split card (cards-said-did-not-say.md) holds the limits. Say plainly, once, that slavery was wrong, and return to what people did.
