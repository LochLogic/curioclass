# Wilma Mankiller read-aloud (day 25)

Read aloud by the teacher twice, with the four drawings. Say first: "This is our own short telling of pages by the Cherokee Nation and the Oklahoma Historical Society. The Cherokee Nation is a nation today." Print the credit once in large type: "Our telling of pages by the Cherokee Nation and the Oklahoma Historical Society."

## The four sentences

1. The Cherokee Nation is a nation that governs itself today, with its own government and its own laws.
2. From 1985 to 1995, Wilma Mankiller was its Principal Chief, the first woman to hold that job.
3. In 1989, young Cherokee citizens and Chief Mankiller worked together, and the Cherokee Nation Tribal Youth Council began that year.
4. While she led, the Cherokee Nation opened three rural health centers.

## The four drawings

Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, one person in each.

1. A building with a flag beside a card that reads "the Cherokee Nation today."
2. A woman at a long table with a title card, "Principal Chief," and the dates 1985 and 1995 on a small calendar card.
3. A young person and a woman holding a paper together between them, beside a card that reads "1989."
4. A small building with a cross sign (a health center), drawn alone, with a card that reads "three centers."

## Words with pictures

- **nation**: a people with their own government; named by its own name, the Cherokee Nation.
- **Principal Chief**: the leader of the Cherokee Nation's government.
- **citizen**: a person who belongs to a nation.

## Source questions

What kind of source is this? (Our telling of the nation's own pages and a state history page.) Who made it? (Us, from the nation's pages.) Old or new? (The pages are new; the events are from 1985 to 1995; the nation is here today.) What can it help us find out? (What Mankiller did and who worked with her.)

## For the teacher

The nation is named by its own name, Cherokee Nation, and spoken in the present tense: it governs itself today, and its youth council continues today. Mankiller has died, so she is spoken of in the past tense. Do not say the Cherokee were "a people of long ago" (L). Credit the nation's own pages by name. No images are copied. The 1985 to 1995 card goes on the leader strip. The quality the class draws from the action is working together with her nation.
