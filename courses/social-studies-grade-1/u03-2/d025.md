# Day 25: Wilma Mankiller and the Cherokee Nation

## Teacher page

### Before class

Say "Cherokee Nation" by its own name. The six: first six on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

If many missed day 24's exit, set the leader card and helper card beside the Huerta row and say what each did (error D); sort six new questions into asks what someone did and asks what we like (error E).

#### 3 min. Words with pictures

Review: in kindergarten you met the Wampanoag, a nation named by its own name and here today. **nation**, a people with their own government. **Principal Chief**, the leader of the Cherokee Nation's government. **citizen**, a person who belongs to a nation.

#### 7 min. Read-aloud with sourcing

Say: "This is our own telling of pages by the Cherokee Nation and the Oklahoma Historical Society. The Cherokee Nation is a nation today." Read it twice with the drawings, the nation in the present tense. Mankiller has died; the nation is here now.

**Ask.** What kind of source? Who made it? Old or new? What can it help us find out? Expect: our telling of the nation's pages; us; new, and the nation is here today; what she did.

#### 4 min. Action chart

Fill row four: did (Principal Chief; worked with young citizens in 1989), others (young citizens), changed (a youth council began; three rural health centers opened). Add the 1985 to 1995 card. Quality: working together with her nation. Say once: the telling says they worked together, not that everyone agreed; put that on the board as a question (error O). The nation is not only long ago (error L).

#### 7 min. Questions, and hearing the six

Twelve pairs take two question cards, one about Mankiller and one about the nation's young citizens. Write their questions. The six come to your table, a minute each, to mark exit card 25 and answer the spoken ask.

#### 4 min. Exit

The twelve pairs mark exit card 25. [[s03-X25]] Read aloud: "Circle the picture that shows listening." Spoken to the six: "Ask one question about what the members chose." Look for: the picture where the leader invites members, and a question about what members chose, not "everyone agreed."

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No chart: draw three boxes.

**Skip.** The partner questions.

- **5 min. Tell.** Say in your own words: the Cherokee Nation governs itself today. From 1985 to 1995 Wilma Mankiller was its Principal Chief. In 1989 she and young citizens worked together, and a youth council began.
- **15 min. Chart.** Your child draws what she did, who worked with her and what changed.
  **Say.** "What did she do? Who worked with her?" Expect: Principal Chief; young citizens; a youth council began.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X25]] Tell a made-up story: in one picture a club leader invites members to choose where to plant, in the other the leader chooses alone. Your child circles listening.
  **Say.** "Ask one question about what the members chose." Expect: a question about their choice.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one chart row. Longer: a question about the health centers.

### Words and supports

Frames: "She did ___." "The nation is ___ today." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Day 26 compares two leaders. If many circled the leader choosing alone (error D), it lays fresh leader and helper cards; if many said everyone agreed (error O), it sorts "the record says" from "we guess"; the nation is said in the present tense once more (error L).
