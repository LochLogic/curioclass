# Section 3.1 opener: what a leader is

## What the section does

Two days, 20 and 21, of 30 minutes. Unit 3 asks: what makes a good leader? Day 20 shows two made-up pictures (a principal who listens and organizes, a captain who shouts), retrieves kindergarten's fair, caring and in-charge characters, and teaches that a leader is a member of the group who takes on extra responsibility through three actions: encourage, organize and guide. Day 21 teaches children to ask what a leader did, what the group did and what changed, and the class writes the unit's three supporting questions on the board.

## How the days run

Day 20: hook (4), retrieval (5), words with pictures (3), worked example (6), sort and hear the six (8), exit (4). Day 21: reopen and repair (5), words (3), model questioning (7), co-write the supporting questions (4), pairs and hear the six (7), exit (4). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six, and writes the question a pair asks in words beside its drawing.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A leader is a member first.** The leader sits inside the group ring, does the group's work and also takes on more. "Extra" is the key word, not "above."
- **Three actions in children's words.** Encourage: help others feel they can. Organize: sort out who does what. Guide: show the way or help the group choose.
- **A badge or a shout is not the job.** A vest helps us see a person. The job is the actions: asking, organizing, guiding.
- **Ask what someone did.** A good question asks what a person or group did or what changed. "Was it pretty?" asks what we like and does not investigate a change.
- **Every case is invented.** The principal, the captain, the crossing guard, the neighbors and the clubs are made up and said to be made up. No general fact about what principals or crossing guards do everywhere is claimed.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A: the leader is outside or above the group.** Lay the leader card inside the team ring and name one shared task.
- **F: a title or badge alone is the responsibility.** Match a role to the action it does and why.
- **G: a leader may order anything.** A duty is the group's job; a shout is not the job. Sort a duty card beside a shout card.
- **E: a liking question.** Sort questions into asks what someone did and asks what we like.

A wrong answer is voiced by the made-up helper or "someone might say," never by a named child.

## Care

No child reports on who is in charge at home or in their family. No one acts out Captain Bo. Families are many kinds; the cards show only the school team, the neighbors and the clubs. Kindergarten's characters are recalled as a prior routine; a miss is met inside the hook and retrieval in five minutes at most.

## Supplies for the section

The hook pictures, the play leader story, the leader and action cards with the team ring, the Muddy Path record, the pair record cards, the repair cards, the printed exit cards, the three action words for the wall, the question board and the unit 3 record sheet. All pictured people are invented.

## A room with nothing

Stick figures on scrap paper, told aloud; a circle drawn on the floor for the group ring; the question board on a sheet of paper.
