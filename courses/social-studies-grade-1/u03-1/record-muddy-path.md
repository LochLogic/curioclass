# The Muddy Path (day 21 model)

A made-up picture record in four pictures. Say first: "This record is made up." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. One person acts in each picture. The teacher reads each picture aloud and covers faces when asking "What did she do? What shows that?"

## Picture 1: the problem

A path between two houses is a patch of thick mud with deep footprints. A neighbor stands at one end holding a bucket, looking down at wet shoes.

**Teacher reads:** "The path between the houses is muddy. Neighbors cannot walk on it."

## Picture 2: the leader organizes

Ms. Okoye stands at a table of stacked flat stones. She hands a job card (a stone, a bucket, a rake) to a neighbor and points along the path.

**Teacher reads:** "Ms. Okoye is a neighbor and she is the leader. She gives out jobs to her neighbors: who will carry stones, who will bring buckets."

## Picture 3: the group lays the stones

A neighbor kneels on the path and presses a flat stone into the mud. Other neighbors are drawn behind in a line, each carrying a stone.

**Teacher reads:** "The neighbors lay stones over the mud. Many people do the work."

## Picture 4: what changed

A child walks across a dry stone path with dry shoes.

**Teacher reads:** "Now the path is dry, and people can walk on it."

## The three model questions

Ask each from one picture and cover the faces:

1. "What did the leader do?" (Picture 2: she gave out jobs.)
2. "What did the group do?" (Picture 3: they laid the stones.)
3. "What changed?" (Picture 4: the muddy path became a dry path.)

A wrong question beside a right one, said aloud: "Was the path pretty?" asks what we like. "What did she do to make the path?" asks what someone did.

## For the teacher

Ms. Okoye is a member of the group (a neighbor) who takes on the extra job of organizing. Do not use this record again in an exit or end item. Question words go on the board: who, what, how.
