# Leader and action cards (days 20, 21 and 26)

Say first: "These cards are made up." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. One person acts on each card. Print "made up" in small letters on each card. The teacher reads every card aloud. Cards for pairs go in an envelope, one set for each pair; the teacher keeps one large set.

## The words, with pictures (day 20)

- **leader**: a person in a group, drawn inside a ring of people, with a star above.
- **member**: one person in a ring of people, with a dot above.
- **responsibility**: a person holding a box with a hand open to others behind, "a job you take on that others count on."
- **encourage**: a hand patting a back and a thumb up. "Help others feel they can."
- **organize**: a list with three small pictures and arrows to three people. "Sort out who does what."
- **guide**: a person pointing the way, with a dotted path. "Show the way or help the group choose."
- **in charge** (review from kindergarten): a person with an open hand beside a question mark. "It is someone's job to decide for the group."

The three action words (encourage, organize, guide) go on a wall strip with their pictures and stay through day 31.

## Two daily-life cards

- **Ms. Reyes, a principal (made up).** She stands inside a ring of four people in school clothes. Her hand points to a list on the wall. Label: member of the school team; organizes.
- **Mr. Tran, a crossing guard on the school crossing team (made up).** He stands at a painted crossing in a safety vest, a hand up toward a line of children, beside two other crossing team members with hands up. Label: member of the school crossing team; guides children across, encourages the shy ones, organizes the line. The badge is on the vest and has no label.

## Six action cards

One acting person on each. Pairs sort them under the three words.

1. A team captain cheers a tired player who is bent over, hands on knees. (encourage)
2. A helper at a picnic table hands out job cards: napkins, cups, plates. (organize)
3. A hiker holds up a map and points along a trail to a group behind. (guide)
4. A swimming coach crouches at the pool edge and says "You can do it" to a nervous swimmer. (encourage)
5. A class helper writes who will carry which box for a sale, with a name beside each box. (organize)
6. A museum helper walks ahead of a small group and points through a door to the next room. (guide)

The teacher's key is on the back of each card in words, not shown to children until after the sort.

## The team ring

A large circle drawn on paper or on the floor with chalk or tape, labeled "the team." Pairs lay the leader card inside the ring. Day 26 uses the ring again for the chart.

## For the teacher

Say once that a leader is a member first: inside the ring, doing the group's work, and also taking on more. The badge or vest helps us see the person; the actions show the leader (F). Facts about what principals or crossing guards do in general are not claimed: only what the made-up cards show.
