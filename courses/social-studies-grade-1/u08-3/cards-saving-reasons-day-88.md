# Saving reasons: boots, a playground gift and a cracked pipe (day 88)

Say aloud: "These people, shops and things are made up. These cards are practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Tokens are paper pieces, each worth one dollar; no real money.

## Three reasons (teaching, 10 minutes)

1. **Winter boots.** Imo saves a little each week because the cold comes at a known time. **Read aloud:** "Imo knows winter is coming and saves for boots." A calendar strip with a snowflake.
2. **A playground gift.** A class saves tokens to give to a town fund that builds a swing. **Read aloud:** "The class saves a gift. The fund wants it by spring." Gifts can have deadlines.
3. **A cracked pipe.** A water pipe cracks and nobody knew it would, so saved money pays the repair. **Read aloud:** "Nobody planned the crack. The pipe cannot wait."

**Compare two:** which can wait? The boots can wait a little; the pipe cannot. The mat: if I spend now I give up saving; if I save I give up the thing today. Words with pictures: save, saving reason, emergency.

## Six cards for each table (supported work, 4 minutes), three hoops

Sort into later purchase, gift, emergency: a bus fare for a class trip next spring; a gift of books to a school; a burst water tap; a new skipping rope for summer; tokens for a town tree-planting fund; a sudden vet visit for a class guinea pig.

## Pip

Pip says: "Saving is only for buying something for yourself." Right: people save for a later purchase, a gift to a cause, or an emergency. Pip says: "Everything you save for has a day you know." Right: boots have a known winter, a cracked pipe has no known day. Pip is making a mistake on purpose.
