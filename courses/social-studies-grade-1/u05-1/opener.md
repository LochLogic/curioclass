# Section 5.1 opener: what a map shows, keys, globes and directions

## What the section does

Four days, 44 to 47, of 30 minutes. Unit 5 asks: why did people build towns and cities where they did? This section builds the tools for answering it. Day 44: a map stands for a real place, drawn from above and made smaller, and it helps people find places. Day 45: the key says what each symbol stands for, and the same shape can mean different things on two maps. Day 46: a globe and a map show land and water, and a paper relief shows what people made and what was already there. Day 47: north, south, east and west, found with the indicator on the map, never "the top of the page".

## How the days run

Day 44: hook and retrieval (6), teaching (9), supported work (10), share (3), exit (2). Day 45: reopen (5), teaching (10), supported work (10), share (3), exit (2). Day 46: retrieval (5), step back (5), supported work (10), pairs sort (8), exit (2). Day 47: retrieval (5), teaching (11), supported work (8), written task and share (4), exit (2). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A map is a view from straight above, made small, that leaves things out.** It stands for a place and is not the place. A side drawing of one thing is not a map of the room.
- **A symbol stands for a place; the key is the maker's agreement.** A shape means what this map's key says, on this map only.
- **A globe and a map are models of the Earth.** A globe view can have a city named on it, and it shows land and water.
- **People made some features and others were already there.** Fields and houses were made; a hill and a river were there.
- **Cardinal directions belong to the Earth.** A compass needle points toward north. A map shows north with an indicator that may point anywhere on the page. Four directions only.
- **Every place is invented unless a card says it is real.** Maple Park, Linden, Meadow Valley and Cedar Park are made up and printed "invented for practice".

## The errors, in plain words

- **A and N:** the map is the place; a side drawing is a map. Hold a tray of objects beside its drawing from above and say "stands for".
- **B:** a shape means the same on every map. Trace symbol to key and back on two maps.
- **S, R and I:** the globe is only water; fields are natural; a globe has no cities. Find land, water and a labelled city on the views; sort made by people and was already there.
- **C:** north is the top of the paper. Turn the map and the indicator turns with it.

A wrong answer is voiced by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child.

## Care

No child's address, home or street is drawn or asked. The address cards on day 47 are an invented school address. A child who cannot point uses a partner's finger or eye gaze.
