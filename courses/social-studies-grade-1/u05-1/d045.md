# Day 45: The key tells what a symbol stands for

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print a photograph of each school place beside its symbol. Say: "Maple Park and Linden are made up." Teacher keys: key-maps-three-with-keys.md, key-pictures-tray-and-room.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen

If many missed day 44's exit, error A: touch each tray object, then its mark, saying "stands for"; two minutes. Error N: draw the cup and block from the side, then above; children choose which shows where each sits; one minute. Then set a new arrangement of the same three objects; children point to its drawing; two minutes.

#### 10 min. Teaching first

Review: in kindergarten you learned that a symbol is a small picture that stands for a real place, and the key is the box that says what each one stands for. Show three maps, each with its boxed key. The school map: a drawn map, made by us, this year, to find places at school; each symbol is shown beside a photograph of the place. Maple Park and Linden are made up. Trace from a symbol to the key and back. **Ask.** "What does the square stand for on Maple Park?" Expect: a picnic table, because the key says so. **Ask.** "What does the square stand for on Linden?" Expect: a school. **Ask.** "Is a square always a picnic table?" Expect: no, read each map's key. Model the frame: "The map shows the slide. I can find it because the key says a triangle is a slide."

#### 10 min. Supported work

Pairs (15) place the six cards on Maple Park, trace each to the key, and say "I found ___ because the key says ___." You hear the second six, a minute each. **Ask.** "Show me the stream. How do you know?" Expect: the wavy line, because the key says so.

#### 3 min. Share

One pair per map tells one find. Add a board card: "How can a key help us?"

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

All mark exit card 45. [[s05-X45]] Read aloud: "Circle what a circle means, a tree or a pond." The second six come to you, 20 seconds each: "How can you tell what grows in Sunny Plot?" Look for: the key says what the shapes mean (error B, "a circle is a pond because it is round").

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed maps: draw a map of a toy table with a key.

**Skip.** The pairs and the heard six.

- **5 min. Map and key.** Draw three toys from above. Box a key: a square is a block, a triangle is a hat.
- **15 min. Read it.** Your child traces from each symbol to the key and back.
  **Say.** "What does the triangle stand for? How do you know?" Expect: a hat, because the key says so.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X45]] Show the card. Your child circles the meaning.
  **Say.** "How can you tell what grows here?" Expect: the key says what the shapes mean.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: Maple Park only. Longer: a key for three symbols the child invents.

### Words and supports

Frame: "The map shows ___. I can find it because the key says ___." A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 46 opens with retrieval. If many read the shape without its key (error B), trace one shape on two keys: a triangle is a slide on Maple Park and a fire station on Linden.
