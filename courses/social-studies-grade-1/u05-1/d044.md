# Day 44: A map stands for a place

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 43 hands nothing on. Say: "These pictures are made up." Teacher key: key-pictures-tray-and-room.md.

### The day

#### 6 min. Hook and retrieval

Hang the Harbor Map: drawn by us this week to show a harbor, a calm place by the water where ships stop. Ask the board's big question, "Why did people build towns and cities where they did?", and leave it unanswered. Review: in kindergarten you learned a map is a flat drawing seen from above. With the playground picture, unmarked, three children, by pointing:
**Ask.** "What is between the trees?" Expect: the path.
**Ask.** "What is next to the slide?" Expect: the bench.
**Ask.** "Show what the bench picture stands for." Expect: the real bench, or saying it. A miss is repaired next.

#### 9 min. Teaching first

View the tray from the side, then from above. **Ask.** "Which picture shows where each thing sits?" Expect: from above; from the side the cup hides the block. A map stands for a place, drawn from above and made smaller (new: made smaller; the drawing is a small copy). Touch each object, then its mark: "The circle stands for the cup" (review). Classroom drawing, made by the teacher: find the door and the rug. Model one claim (review of the claim frame): "I think this map helps find the door, because the door mark is by the rug mark."

#### 10 min. Supported work

Pairs (15) set the objects on the tray outline, then match each to its mark, saying one position word (review): "The cup is next to the book." You hear the first six, a minute each. **Ask.** "Point to the mark for the book. Where does it sit?" Expect: the book mark, with a position word.

#### 3 min. Share

Two pairs show one match. Add a card to the question board: "What does a map show?"

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

All mark exit card 44. [[s05-X44]] [[s05-X44b]] Read aloud: "Circle the drawing that is a map of the reading room." The first six come to you, 20 seconds each: "How could the map help someone find a shelf?" Look for: the room made small, marks standing for shelves (error N, side view; error A, "you can sit on it").

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed pictures: draw the tray on scrap paper.

**Skip.** The pairs and the heard six.

- **5 min. The tray.** Put a cup, a block and a book on a mat. Look from the side, then stand over it.
- **15 min. Draw it.** Your child draws the three things from above, then touches each thing and its mark.
  **Say.** "Which picture shows where each thing sits? What does the circle stand for?" Expect: the one from above; the cup.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X44]] [[s05-X44b]] Show the card. Your child circles the map.
  **Say.** "How could the map help someone find a shelf?" Expect: it shows the room small, and the marks stand for the shelves.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two prompts. Longer: a new arrangement.

### Words and supports

Words: map, from above, stands for, made smaller. A child may pass.

### Tomorrow

Day 45 teaches the key. Its Reopen acts on this exit: if many circled the side view (error N), draw the cup and block both ways and choose; if many said you can sit on the map (error A), touch each object and its mark, saying "stands for".
