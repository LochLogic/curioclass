# Day 47 pieces: Cedar Park, the wall signs and the address cards

Say aloud first: "Cedar Park is made up." Print "invented for practice" on the map. Thick lines, large print.

## Cedar Park map (invented)

**Picture:** a park drawn from above. Its direction indicator is a large arrow labelled N pointing to the right edge of the page. A boxed key: a diamond is a swing set, a blue oval is a pond, a rectangle is a bench, a triangle is a slide, a thick short line is the gate. On the map, working in page terms: the pond at the center; the swing set to the left of the pond; the bench to the right of the pond; the slide above the pond; the gate below the pond.

For pairs: copy the map on paper with tokens; put a token on the place east of the pond (the gate) and say the direction of the swing set from the bench.

## Four wall signs

Four large paper signs, one letter each in big print: N, S, E, W. Set with a compass held away from steel, tables and screens, N toward where the needle points. If the compass will not settle, use the school's printed site plan and its north arrow, and say aloud which was used. Everyone stands and points to each sign together; no child is singled out.

## The school grounds map indicator

The day 45 school map with a direction indicator added. Turn the map on a table; the indicator turns with it, and a finger traced along its arrow always points to the N sign.

## Address cards (retrieval, unmarked)

Four nested cards on a ribbon, an invented school address, with a label under each: the street card "18 Birch Street", the town card "Milltown", the state card "Colorado", the country card "United States". They are read aloud, and children point to the street, then the town, the state, the country. No child's address is asked or drawn.
