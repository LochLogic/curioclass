# The Harbor Map

A schematic map of New York Harbor, drawn by us. It is approximate and simple. It is used on day 44 (the compelling question hangs beside it), day 49 (with the Lenape layer laid over it) and day 53 (the model closing). Print "ours, approximate" in small letters at the bottom.

## What the picture shows

Plain line drawing from above, thick lines. The sea along the bottom edge, drawn as blue waves. A long, narrow island above the water, with its pointed southern tip toward the sea. A wide stretch of calm water just off the island's southern tip, with small ship shapes on it. Along the island's shoreline at the tip, a row of short docks sticking into the water, with small square buildings behind them. A river shape on each side of the island. A key box: waves mean water, a small triangle on the water means a ship, a short line sticking into the water means a dock, a square means a building. A direction indicator with an arrow and the letter N, pointing up the page on this map. Title: Harbor Map. Under the title: "schematic, approximate".

## What to say about the source

Name the map's kind, maker, date and purpose before using it: a drawn map; made by us (the teacher or the class's team); this year; to show a harbor. It is useful for seeing where ships and docks sit by the water. It is not useful for counting ships or measuring distance, because it is small and leaves things out.

## A room with nothing

Draw a curved coast, a few small boats, a row of short lines for docks and a square for each building. Write the key beside it.
