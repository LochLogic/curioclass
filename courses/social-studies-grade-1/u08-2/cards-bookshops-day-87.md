# Two bookshops, and Shop C and Shop D (day 87)

Say aloud: "These people, shops and things are made up. These cards are practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Tokens are paper pieces, each worth one dollar; no real money. Each card was made by the shop. A card cannot prove what a real shop does. Nobody has to choose either shop.

## Shop A and Shop B (teaching, 10 minutes)

**Shop A card:** "Our workers have a steady schedule and take turns choosing days off. Our book costs 9." **Shop B card:** "We recycle our paper bags and take back old books to give them new readers. Our book costs 8."

**Source questions, aloud:** Who made each card? The shop. When? Now. What for? To get buyers. What a shop says about itself is not proof.

**Model:** "A buyer might choose A because ...; another might choose B because ..." Also: "I think a buyer might pick B, because the card says it costs 8."

## Shop C and Shop D (supported work, 4 and 6 minutes)

**Shop C card:** "Our workers have a safe, clean break room. Our book costs 9." **Shop D card:** "We turn old cloth into new bags. Our book costs 9." Pairs say "a buyer might choose C because ...; another D because ...". Then each child draws two buyers and their reasons on **My Two Shops Page**.

## Pip

Pip says: "A person who buys from the other shop is bad." Right: one buyer cares about how workers are treated, another about the lower price; both are fine. Pip is making a mistake on purpose.
