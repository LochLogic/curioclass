# Class rule strips (day 5)

These are about our own real class, not an invented group. Say so aloud each time you hold one up beside an invented card: "This one is real. It is ours."

## The strips

Six strips from the class's real rules and duties, written on the first weeks of school and dated. If the class has fewer than six, add some from those weeks. Each strip has one rule or duty and a small picture the teacher draws. For example (replace with the class's own):

1. Walk in the hall. Picture: feet walking.
2. Use quiet voices at the tables. Picture: a finger at the lips.
3. Push in your chair. Picture: a chair pushed up to a table.
4. Hang your coat on your hook. Picture: a row of coat hooks.
5. Line leader holds the door. Picture: a hand on a door.
6. Share the markers. Picture: a box open to two hands.

Make six matching sets, one for each table, and a wall set a little larger.

## The three purpose mats and the fourth

Each table has mats: stay safe (shield), be fair (scales), help us finish together (two hands on one flag), and a "not sure" mat (a question mark). Children place a strip and say: "The rule is ___. It helps because ___."

## The class chart

Chart paper with the three purposes as headings. Each table puts one strip under a heading; the teacher writes the class answer beneath: "Groups need rules and duties so ___." Keep the chart up to day 8.

## For the teacher

Five children at about thirty seconds each is two and a half minutes a table. Stand near one table all the way through; that table's children are the day's fifth six. A strip that fits two mats is welcome with a reason. Never use a strip that records one child's behavior; the strips are about rules and duties, never about people.
