# Section 1.2 opener: belonging, rules and duties

## What the section does

Three days, 3 to 5, of 30 minutes. Day 3 teaches group, member and belong with two invented groups and five invented families, then sorts eight picture cards onto what belonging gives: help, friendship, shared work. Day 4 teaches a rule and the reason for it, and a duty, with a swim team. Day 5 turns to the class's own real rules and duties and sorts them by the purpose each serves. Day 5 answers the second supporting question: Why do groups need rules and duties? The scored versions come on day 9.

## How the days run

Day 3: seven-minute return and repair with retrieval, seven on the words, five on families, six on the sort, five for the exit. Day 4: six, seven, six, six, five. Day 5: four, six, ten, five, five. Each exit is marked by every child; the spoken why is heard from one rotating six (the third six on day 3, the fourth on day 4, the fifth on day 5).

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A group is people together for something.** Matching shirts do not make a group; doing something together does. A family is a group too.
- **Belonging gives something.** Help, friendship, shared work. Membership never needs members to look alike.
- **A rule says how we act; a duty is a job we are expected to do.** Both serve what the group is for.
- **A reason is not a command.** "Because the coach said" gives no reason. "So no one jumps onto a swimmer" does.

## Families on day 3

All five family cards are invented. Say aloud: "Families come in many kinds. These are made up. No one needs to tell about their own." Cards show a parent and a child, two fathers and a child, a grandfather and two grandchildren, a foster parent and a child in foster care, and an adoptive family. Say each plainly and move on; never ask what any of these words mean for anyone in the room, and never rank one kind above another. A child who offers something about their own family is thanked and the lesson goes on.

## The errors, in plain words

- **B: a command, not a reason.** Show two cards for one rule, one with a reason, one with only the coach. Children point to the card that says what the rule is for.
- **D: matching things make the group.** Show two invented groups with different ages and hats doing one thing together. Ask what makes each a group.
- **F: a place name with no before** (carried from day 2).
- **I: choosing a rule that works against the group's purpose.** Choose between two rules by which one helps the group do what it is for.

## Supplies

The group cards, five family cards, eight belonging cards and three mats, the pool deck picture and rule and purpose cards, class rule strips, six matching strip sets for the table groups, the printed exit cards.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn family cards on scrap paper. A sheet of paper with three mats drawn on it. A child's drawing can serve as a card.
