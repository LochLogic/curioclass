# Five family cards (day 3)

The teacher holds these. Say before the first one, and again at the end: "Families come in many kinds. These are made up. No one needs to tell about their own." No child is asked to say anything about their own family, and no card is better than another. They are cards about groups, not questions about a child.

Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no printed words. Each card shows the people doing one thing together.

## Card 1: fixing a bike

**Picture:** One parent and a child kneel beside a bicycle. The child holds a wrench; the parent holds the wheel steady.

**Teacher says:** "A parent and a child fix a bike. What does belonging give here? Help."

## Card 2: folding laundry

**Picture:** Two fathers and a child stand at a table folding a pile of towels. The child holds up one towel by its corners.

**Teacher says:** "Two fathers and a child fold laundry. What does belonging give here? Shared work."

## Card 3: reading on the porch

**Picture:** A grandfather sits on a step with two grandchildren, one on each side, sharing a picture book. A pot of flowers stands beside them.

**Teacher says:** "A grandfather and two grandchildren read together. What does belonging give here? Friendship."

## Card 4: setting the table

**Picture:** A foster parent and a child in foster care set a table together, one with plates and one with spoons.

**Teacher says:** "A foster parent and a child in foster care set the table. What does belonging give here? Help and shared work." Say the words plainly, once, and go on.

## Card 5: planting a tree

**Picture:** An adoptive family, two children and two grown-ups, kneel around a small tree and pat the soil. One child holds a watering can.

**Teacher says:** "An adoptive family plants a tree. What does belonging give here? Shared work and friendship." Say the words plainly, once, and go on.

## For the teacher

Read each card and ask what belonging gives: help, friendship or shared work. Any answer tied to what the picture shows meets. If a child tells about their own family, thank the child and say, "That is a family too," and move on. Do not ask follow-up questions about home.
