# Song cards, day 65

Say aloud first: "Singing is a choice. You may listen or hum." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Lyrics are public domain. No score is printed in this course: the adult hums each tune. If an adult does not know a tune, the class listens to the words read aloud instead and the tune match is recorded as not yet seen, not as a miss.

## The two taught songs

1. **"America the Beautiful."** Katharine Lee Bates wrote the words after a trip in 1893 (65A). Taught phrase: "purple mountain majesties." Plain words: the mountains she saw. The song admires the land. Source 65A and 65C.
2. **"The Star-Spangled Banner."** Francis Scott Key wrote it in 1814 and a law made it the national anthem in 1931 (65B). Taught phrase: "land of the free and the home of the brave." Plain words: the country's people being free and brave. Sources 65B and 65D.

## Cards

For each song: a **title card** (the song's name in large print, with a small picture clue: mountains; a flag), a **phrase card** (the taught phrase), a **detail box** to draw in (the mountain; the people) and a **token** (used on the exit, not in pairs' work). The adult hums each tune twice with the title card shown.

## The move

Say the words, find the detail (the mountain; the people), say what the song expresses. Pairs draw the detail each taught phrase tells about, the mountain and the people, in two boxes, and say "The song says ___, so it expresses ___." They do not match tune, title and meaning picture: the exit does that. About 4 minutes. Pip says error E ("Everyone must sing it to belong") and is corrected.

## Hum and listen

The adult hums both tunes once more with the title card shown. Joining by humming is a choice.

## Not used here

The exit phrases and the end packet's phrases are kept closed. Only the two taught phrases are shown today.
