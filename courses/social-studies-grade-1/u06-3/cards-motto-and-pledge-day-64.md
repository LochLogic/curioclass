# Motto and Pledge cards, day 64

Say aloud first: "Saying the Pledge is a choice. You may listen." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print. Print "invented for practice" on the quilt and library cards.

## The motto

The Great Seal's banner, held up: E PLURIBUS UNUM. "It is Latin for Out of many, one." Record: the motto joins the thirteen states into one nation (62A). People also say: many people, one country. Two bins, as on day 62.

An invented quilt card for the picture: many patches, each a different shape and color, stitched into one quilt. Caption: "Many patches, one quilt." Invented for practice. A hand-drawn library card for the second: Fox Town library, invented, with a sign "Everyone may borrow a book." Teaching ask: "Out of many, one. What are the many here? What is the one?"

## The Pledge, read once as a text

The teacher reads aloud, once, without standing: "I pledge allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America, and to the Republic for which it stands, one Nation under God, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all." Source 64A (4 U.S.C. 4). Plain meanings: a promise of loyalty; one country together; liberty and justice for all, freedom and fair treatment for everyone. "Under God" is part of the text; people believe different things, and no one is asked to agree.

## The choice line

"A court said in 1943 that students cannot be forced to say it." Source 64B (West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette). Said again at the end of the day. Pip says errors C ("Out of many, one means everyone must be the same") and E ("You must say the Pledge to belong") and is corrected.

## Pairs' phrase and meaning cards

Phrase cards: "Out of many, one"; "liberty and justice for all." Meaning cards: many joined as one; freedom and fair treatment. Each pair places each phrase with its meaning and a detail (the seal's banner; the Pledge line) and says "Out of many, one means ___." Turns: about 4 minutes.
