# Symbol cards, day 62

Say aloud first: "These cards are practice. Saying or singing is a choice." Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, large print. Print "invented for practice" on any made-up card. Colors are not explained.

## The record, in two bins

Bin one, "the record says": what the thing is. Bin two, "people say": a meaning people give it. Say both for each symbol, in one breath.

## Nine cards for pairs

Three symbol cards and six statement cards, read aloud. Pairs do not match symbols to meaning pictures: they sort statements into the two bins and point to the part of the symbol that the record tells about.

1. **The flag at a town library.** A flag on a pole beside a library door. Record: thirteen stripes for the first states, a star for each state, fifty stars today (62B, 62E).
2. **The Great Seal, large.** A round seal: an eagle with an olive branch in one foot and arrows in the other, thirteen stars above its head, a banner. Record: adopted in 1782 (62A). Olive branch, peace; arrows, the power to defend.
3. **The White House, front view.** A large building with columns and a flag. Record: the home and workplace of the president (62C).
4. **Statement, record:** "Thirteen stripes for the first thirteen states."
5. **Statement, record:** "The seal was chosen in 1782."
6. **Statement, record:** "The home and office of the president."
7. **Statement, people say:** "It shows strength."
8. **Statement, people say:** "Some people say it looks grand."
9. **Statement, record:** "An olive branch, for peace."

Each pair sorts the statements into the two bins and points to the part of each symbol that the record tells about: the stars; the olive branch; the building's front door and flag. Turns: about 3 minutes for three symbols.

## A short line

"The bald eagle has been the national bird by law since December 23, 2024." (62D) A bird card beside the seal. The seal's eagle is called the American Eagle on the State Department's page.

## Pip's error D

Pip: "It is a bird, so the eagle is just a bird." Corrected: "A symbol stands for something. The eagle on the seal stands for the country."

## Retrieval (kindergarten's symbols)

A flag on a classroom wall and an eagle on a coin. "What can each stand for? Is the bird itself the country?" Answer: each can stand for the country; a symbol stands for something else. A miss goes to a small group with a building's sign beside the building.
