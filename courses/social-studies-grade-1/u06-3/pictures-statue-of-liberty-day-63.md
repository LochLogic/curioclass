# The Statue of Liberty, day 63

Say aloud first: "The statue is real. Lena is made up." Print "invented for practice" on the Lena cards. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, large print.

## The picture

The statue seen from the front, standing on her base. A torch held up in her right hand and a crown. A caption: "The Statue of Liberty. A gift from France, dedicated in 1886." Source 63A. Words with pictures: liberty (freedom), welcome.

## The torch and the poem (teaching lines only)

"The torch is held up for liberty, which is freedom." (63A: the torch represents enlightenment and freedom.) The poem on the base was written by Emma Lazarus in 1883 (63B). Teaching lines, in plain words after each: "Give me your tired, your poor, your huddled masses yearning to breathe free." Plain words: it welcomes tired people who want to be free. The other lines of the poem are held out for the exit and the end packet.

## Lena, one scene (invented)

A made-up girl, Lena, stands on a ship deck and sees the statue far ahead. Caption: "Lena sees the statue from a ship." Said aloud: "Lena is made up." Perspective: the same statue can mean welcome to one person and something else to another. No other actor in the scene.

## The limit, said plainly

"The statue is a hope of welcome. Not everyone was treated the same. In 1882 a law kept Chinese workers from coming for ten years." Source 63C. Two cards: "a hope" and "not equal for all." Pip says error K ("Everybody who came by ship got the same welcome") and is corrected.

## The timeline: three dated cards (not scored)

"1782, the Great Seal." "1886, the Statue of Liberty." "2024, the bald eagle becomes the national bird by law." Dates supplied, read aloud. Pairs place them left to right and say first, next, last.

## Pairs' drawing

Each pair draws the statue's torch with a one-word label, then says "The statue shows ___. This supports ___ because ___." The teacher holds the limit cards and each pair says one limit ("a hope" / "not equal for all"). No card is matched to a meaning picture: the exit circles what the welcome words are, so the pairs' work is drawing and saying. Turns: about 3 minutes.

## Not used here

The exit view and the end packet's view of the statue are kept closed. The other lines of the poem are not read today.
