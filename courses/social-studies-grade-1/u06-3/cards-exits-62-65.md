# Exit cards, days 62 to 65

Each exit has a paper card every child marks and a spoken question heard from the day's rotating six. Say aloud first: "These cards are practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each made-up card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. Keep these closed until the day. No card reuses a teaching card, a repair card, a day 67 packet view or the held-out phrases of the end packet.

## Day 62 exit card: three symbols

**Pictures:** a flag flying above a post office; an eagle inside a round seal stamped on an official paper from the United States government; the White House seen from its lawn side, captioned "The White House, where visitors take pictures." Five small meaning pictures with a circle each: a country; a president's work; a bird; any house; a decoration.

**Mark:** Match each of the three pictures to what it stands for; two pictures may share one meaning. **Spoken (one, teacher points to a picture):** "What does this one stand for?" Or: "Show me the part of the picture that helped you decide."

## Day 63 exit card: the statue at sunset

**Picture:** the Statue of Liberty seen from the ground below her base at sunset, her torch held up. A line on its base, read aloud: "Send these, the homeless, tempest-tost to me." The puppet says, "The welcome words prove everyone who came was treated the same." Three picture choices: a hope of welcome; proof everyone was treated the same; a rule on a sign.

**Mark:** Circle what the welcome words are. **Spoken:** "What does the torch stand for?" Or: "Does the statue show everyone was treated the same?"

## Day 64 exit card: Ari and Bea

**Pictures:** two scenes, one neighbor in each. Ari stands quietly while the Pledge is said. Bea says it aloud. Read aloud: "Ari and Bea believe different things. They are neighbors in the United States." Three picture choices: both neighbors; only the one who says the words; only people who believe the same.

**Mark:** Circle who belongs to the country. **Spoken:** "What does out of many, one tell us about Ari and Bea?" Or: "What do liberty and justice for all mean for Ari?"

## Day 65 exit card: two songs

**Cards:** two lyric cards, "spacious skies" and "broad stripes and bright stars", each with a words-only card, and two title cards (the songs' names). The adult hums each tune once, not in the order of the cards. Four meaning pictures: a wide sky over land; a flag with stripes and stars; a crowd cheering; a bird. Tokens.

**Mark:** Put each tune's token on its title; put each words card on its title; put each meaning picture with its words. **Spoken:** "What does this song say about our country?" Or: "Which words tell you?"
