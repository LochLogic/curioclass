# Repair cards and fresh-try cards, section 6.3

Each repair card puts a wrong idea beside a right one. The wrong idea is spoken by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child. Say aloud: "Pip is making a mistake on purpose." Print "invented for practice" on every invented card. Used only when the exit showed the error; at most five minutes, a small group when few missed it. Nobody is asked to say, stand or sing. No card reuses an exit or end picture.

## D: naming explains the meaning (days 63, 66)

**Pip:** "It is a bird, so the eagle is just a bird." **Right:** "The eagle on the seal stands for the country. The olive branch is the detail for peace." **Retry:** a fresh symbol, a small flag with stars on a town hall in Hollis Bend, invented: "What does it stand for? Which part told you?" For a song, use the taught phrases "purple mountain majesties" and "land of the free and the home of the brave" with the same move: say the words, find the detail, say what it expresses.

## K: the statue proves equal welcome (day 64)

**Pip:** "The poem proves everyone was welcomed the same." **Right:** "It is a hope of welcome. Not everyone was treated the same." **Retry:** two cards, "a hope" and "not equal for all"; sort three statements read aloud: "The torch stands for liberty"; "A law in 1882 kept Chinese workers from coming for ten years"; "The poem welcomes tired people who want to be free."

## C and E: belonging needs sameness or saying the words (day 65)

**Pip:** "Out of many, one means everyone must be the same," and "You must say the Pledge to belong." **Right:** "Many different people, one country. Saying the Pledge is a choice." **Retry:** a fresh case, Wren Hollow, invented: two neighbors with different beliefs both help build the town playground, and neither is asked to say any words. "Who belongs? What shows it?"
