# Day 63: A hope of welcome

## Teacher page

### Before class

Lay out the statue picture and the cards. Keep exit card 63 closed. Say: "The statue is real. Lena is made up."

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 62's exit, error D, 5 minutes: show a small flag with stars on the town hall of Hollis Bend, made up. **Ask.** "What is it? What does it stand for? Which part told you?" Expect: a flag; the country; the stars. Retry with a second new card.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

Show the Statue of Liberty: a gift from France, dedicated in 1886. Review: liberty is freedom, from the Pledge in kindergarten. The torch is held up for liberty. A poem is on its base; read one line: "Give me your tired, your poor, your huddled masses yearning to breathe free." It welcomes tired people who want to be free. Many newcomers who came by ship saw the statue as a sign of welcome. Show Lena, made up, on a ship deck, alone in the scene, seeing the statue. Review: people can see one thing in different ways. Say the limit plainly: the statue is a hope of welcome, and not everyone was treated the same. In 1882 a law kept Chinese workers from coming for ten years. Place two cards, "a hope" and "not equal for all". Review: Pip makes a mistake on purpose. Pip says error K: "Everybody who came by ship got the same welcome." Correct: "The statue shows a hope. Not everyone was treated the same."

#### 4 min. Timeline

Review: a timeline is read left to right. Pairs lay three dated cards, dates read aloud: the Great Seal, 1782; the Statue, 1886; the bald eagle made the national bird by law, 2024. Each pair says "first, next, last". Not scored.

#### 8 min. Supported work

Each pair draws the torch with a label, then says "The statue shows ___. This supports ___ because ___." Hold the limit cards: each pair says one limit. About 3 minutes. Hear the fourth six at the exit cards, 40 seconds each: 4 minutes.

#### 3 min. Exit

Everyone marks exit card 63. [[s06-X63]] [[s06-X63b]] Read the card aloud.

#### 2 min. Look back

**Ask.** "What hope does the statue show? What does it not show?" Expect: welcome; that everyone was treated the same. "Tomorrow: words on the seal and the Pledge."

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No dated cards: write 1782, 1886 and 2024 on three scraps.

**Skip.** Pip, the pairs and the heard six.

- **10 min. Show.** Show the statue and read the poem line aloud. Say: "A hope of welcome. Not everyone was treated the same."
  **Say.** "What does the torch stand for? What does the statue not show?" Expect: liberty; that everyone was treated the same.
- **15 min. Draw.** Your child draws the torch and says: "The statue shows ___. This supports ___ because ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X63]] [[s06-X63b]] Show the card and read it. Your child circles.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the timeline. Longer: a second limit card.

### Words and supports

Read the student page aloud. Words: statue, liberty, welcome, torch. Frame: "The statue shows ___. This supports ___ because ___."

### Tomorrow

Day 64 repairs error K (the statue taken to prove equal welcome): 5 minutes. Then the motto and the Pledge.
