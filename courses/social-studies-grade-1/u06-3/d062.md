# Day 62: What a symbol stands for

## Teacher page

### Before class

Lay out the symbol cards and bins. Keep exit card 62 closed. Say: "Saying or singing is a choice. You may listen."

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 61's exit, error G, 3 minutes: two new cards, a liking and a detail; point to the one the source shows. Error M, 2 minutes: Briar Fields, made up, neighbors speak many languages and share one fire station. **Ask.** "What shows united? What shows different?" Expect: the fire station; the languages.

#### 3 min. Retrieval

Review from kindergarten: a symbol is a picture or thing that stands for our country. Show a flag on a classroom wall and an eagle on a coin. **Ask.** "What can each stand for? Is the bird itself the country?" Expect: each can stand for the country; no. A miss joins a small group.

#### 7 min. Teaching first

Review: two bins from kindergarten, "the record says" and "people say it means". Flag, review: the record says thirteen stripes for the first thirteen states, a star for each state, fifty today. Great Seal, review: the record says it was chosen in 1782; the eagle holds an olive branch, for peace, and arrows, for the power to defend. People say: "it shows strength." A law made the bald eagle our national bird in December 2024. White House: the record says it is the home and office of the president, so it stands for the president's work. Review: Pip makes a mistake on purpose. Pip says error D: "An eagle is just a bird. The White House is any white house." Correct: "A symbol stands for something."

#### 9 min. Supported work

Pairs have the three symbols and six statement cards, read aloud. Each pair sorts the statements into the two bins and points to the part of each symbol the record tells about: the stars, the olive branch, the door. About 3 minutes. Hear the third six at the exit cards, 40 seconds each: 4 minutes. Run the small group for 3 minutes.

#### 3 min. Exit

Everyone marks exit card 62. [[s06-X62]] Read the card aloud.

#### 3 min. Look back

**Ask.** "Which bin is 'fifty stars today' in?" Expect: the record says. The class says one symbol and what it stands for. "Tomorrow: the Statue of Liberty."

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw a flag, an eagle and a house on scrap paper.

**Skip.** Pip, the pairs and the heard six.

- **10 min. Show.** Show a flag. Say: "The record says a star for each state. A symbol stands for something."
  **Say.** "What can a flag stand for? What part tells you?" Expect: the country; the stars.
- **15 min. Sort.** Your child draws the flag and an eagle and says what each stands for and one detail.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X62]] Show the card and read it. Your child matches.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two symbols. Longer: the olive branch.

### Words and supports

Read the student page aloud. Words: symbol, flag, eagle, seal, president. Frame: "It stands for ___. The detail is ___."
