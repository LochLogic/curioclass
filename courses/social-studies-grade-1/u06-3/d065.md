# Day 65: Two songs

## Teacher page

### Before class

Lay out the song cards. Keep exit card 65 closed. Practice both tunes aloud, or plan to read the words aloud instead. Say: "Singing is a choice. You may listen or hum."

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 64's exit, error C, 3 minutes: Wren Hollow, made up, two neighbors with different beliefs both help build the town playground. **Ask.** "Who belongs? What shows it?" Expect: both; they both help. Error E, 2 minutes: a child listening and a child humming are both inside one circle; the class says "belongs". Retry with a new scene. Else a small group.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

Hum each tune twice, the title card shown. Katharine Lee Bates wrote the words of "America the Beautiful" after a trip in 1893. Take the phrase "purple mountain majesties": the mountains she saw. The song admires the land. Review from kindergarten: an anthem is the song a law chose for the country. Francis Scott Key wrote "The Star-Spangled Banner" in 1814, and a law made it the anthem in 1931. Take the phrase "land of the free and the home of the brave": the country's people, free and brave. The move for each phrase: say the words, find the detail (the mountain; the people), say what the song expresses. Review: Pip makes a mistake on purpose. Pip says error E: "Everyone must sing it to belong." Correct: "Singing is a choice. You may listen."

#### 10 min. Supported work

Pairs have a title card and a phrase card for each song, and two boxes. Each pair draws the detail each phrase tells about, the mountain and the people, then says "The song says ___, so it expresses ___." About 4 minutes. Nobody is asked to sing. Hear the second six at the exit cards, 40 seconds each: 4 minutes.

#### 3 min. Exit

The adult hums each tune once. Everyone marks exit card 65. [[s06-X65]] Say: "These are practice cards." Read the card aloud.

#### 4 min. Hum and listen

Hum both tunes once more, the title card shown. Joining by humming is a choice. "Tomorrow: what holds a country of so many people together?"

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in. If no adult knows a tune, read the words aloud; record the tune part as not yet seen, never a miss.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write each title and phrase on a scrap.

**Skip.** Pip, the pairs and the heard six.

- **10 min. Show.** Hum a tune, or read the words aloud, with the title shown. Nobody has to sing.
  **Say.** "What does this song say about our country? Which words tell you?" Expect: it admires the land, or the people free and brave; the phrase.
- **15 min. Draw.** Your child draws the detail for each phrase and says: "The song says ___, so it expresses ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X65]] Show the card, hum, and read the card. Your child places tokens.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one song. Longer: a second phrase.

### Words and supports

Read the student page aloud. Words: song, tune, anthem. Frame: "The song says ___, so it expresses ___."

### Tomorrow

Day 66 repairs error D (knowing a tune taken as knowing what it means): 5 minutes. Then each child answers the compelling question with a claim and a detail.
