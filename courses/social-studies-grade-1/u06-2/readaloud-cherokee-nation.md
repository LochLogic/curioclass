# The Cherokee Nation, in our words, day 60

Say aloud first: "These are the Cherokee Nation's own pages, told in our words. The Cherokee Nation is a nation today." Named by its own name, in the present tense. Credit: the Cherokee Nation's own pages (sources 60A, 60B, 60C). No picture of a person, no costume, no ceremony. A calm map card only: the Cherokee Nation's reservation in eastern Oklahoma, a gray outline with its name, drawn from Natural Earth outlines (source 50E) with the words "present-day eastern Oklahoma."

## Read aloud, five short parts

1. **A nation today.** "The Cherokee Nation is a government. It has its own constitution and its own laws. It has three branches, so that no one branch does everything. The Cherokee Nation governs itself today." (60A)
2. **Citizens.** "People who are citizens of the Cherokee Nation belong to it, and the nation keeps a list of its citizens." (60A) No child is asked whether they are a citizen of anything.
3. **Language.** "The Cherokee Nation works to keep the Cherokee language and culture alive. It has a language department." (60A)
4. **Perspective.** "For the people who live it, this makes sense. We learn about it from the nation's own account." (6.1's sentence, returned)
5. **A hard part, two sentences, once.** "In 1838 the United States sent soldiers to force the Cherokee people away from their homelands. It was not a choice." (60B) Do not describe the march or the camps. Stop and say: "Native nations were here first, and live here now." Review: the Wampanoag in Massachusetts.

## Pip's error F

Pip: "Native people lived long ago and are gone." Correct with part 1: "The Cherokee Nation governs itself today."

## Teacher key

The ten-square strip and this page serve different asks: the strip counts neighbors; the page tells what a living nation does and where it has been. Never say a number of Cherokee people, never describe anyone's belief. Beliefs are described as "what some people think is true about important things", and never practiced.
