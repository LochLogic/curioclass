# Rowan Town and Tansy Town, day 61

Say aloud first: "Rowan Town and Tansy Town are made up. These are practice cards." Print "invented for practice" on each. One neighbor or group at a time. Plain black line drawing on white.

## Model: Rowan Town (the teacher models)

**Picture:** a street crossing with a sign, "Look both ways." **Caption, read aloud:** "Neighbors in Rowan Town sing different songs. All follow the same crossing rule." **Frame, modeled:** "The source shows everyone follows the same crossing rule. This supports united because they share a rule." A second detail for diversity: "They sing different songs. This supports different because the songs are not the same." Words with pictures: evidence (a magnifying glass over a detail), claim. Pip says errors G ("I like Rowan Town, so it is united") and M ("The crossing rule shows they are different too") and is corrected: a liking is not evidence; each claim has its own detail.

## Pairs: Tansy Town

**Picture:** a market drawing with two stalls selling different foods and a board, "Keep the lane clear." No caption is read. Each pair fills the claim frame twice by pointing to the card, once "united" (the same lane rule) and once "different" (different foods), and says it aloud. The pair swaps and listens.

## The co-written answer, day 61

The class answers "How can Americans be united and different?" in two sentences, each with a detail from 59 or 60 (the Constitution, a language, the nation's account). The teacher writes them on the question board.
