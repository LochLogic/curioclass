# Day 60: How we are different

## Teacher page

### Before class

Lay strips and cards. Keep exit card 60 closed. Say: "Holly Run and Dell Run are made up. The Cherokee Nation is real." Ask no child about home or beliefs. Teacher key: key-sheet-strip-our-town.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

If many missed error C on day 59 (s06-X59b): Hazel Town again, then made-up Thorn Cove: one neighbor plays a fiddle, one picks apples; both follow a market law. **Ask.** "What is the same for both?" Expect: the law. If few, a small group.

#### 9 min. Teaching first

Diverse means different from one another, in languages, birthplaces, beliefs and traditions. A belief is what people think is true about important things; we describe beliefs, never practise them. Read the Cherokee Nation's own account, in our words: a government with its own constitution and laws, governing itself today; it works to keep the Cherokee language alive and has a language department. Review: it makes sense to the people who live it. Review: Pip makes a mistake on purpose. Pip says error F: "Native people lived long ago and are gone." Correct with the account: "Native nations were here first, and live here now." Review: the Wampanoag in Massachusetts, from kindergarten. Say once, plainly: "In 1838 the United States sent soldiers to force the Cherokee people away from their homelands. It was not a choice." Show no picture. Show the Holly Run strip: three of ten shaded, neighbors who speak another language at home. Count: "some"; "many" is more than three. Show the blank town strip sheet: a real count comes from a published page, never the class; we have none yet. "A strip counts. It cannot tell what any neighbor believes."

#### 10 min. Supported work

Pairs count the Dell Run strip, five of ten born in another town: "some" or "many"? Pip says error L: "The strip tells what each neighbor believes." Pairs sort five cards, a language, a birthplace, a belief, a tradition and the language department, into "a number can show it" and "needs an account": about 4 minutes. Hear the first six at the exit cards, 40 seconds each: 4 minutes.

#### 3 min. Exit

Everyone marks exit card 60. [[s06-X60]] Say: "These are practice pictures." Read the passage; show the strip.

#### 3 min. Look back

Add "diverse" to the board. Pairs say what a count cannot tell.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: draw ten squares and shade five.

**Skip.** Pip, the pairs and the heard six. Never ask your child about your family's languages, birthplaces or beliefs.

- **10 min. Different.** Read the nation's account aloud. Say what diverse means.
  **Say.** "What does a strip count? What can it not tell?" Expect: it counts neighbors; it cannot tell what anyone believes.
- **15 min. Count.** Count the shaded squares; say "some" or "many".
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X60]] Show the card and read it. Your child circles.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one strip. Longer: one more card.

### Words and supports

Read the student page aloud. Words: diverse, belief.

### Tomorrow

Day 61 repairs error F (a nation taken as gone), error J (removal taken as a choice) and error L (a count taken to show belief): 2, 2 and 1 minutes.

### Credit

Ours. The nation's facts come from its own pages, told in our words.
