# Holly Run strip and sort cards, day 60

Say aloud first: "Holly Run is made up. The strip is practice." Print "invented for practice" on the strip and every card.

## The ten-square strip

A row of ten squares. Three are shaded gray, seven are not. Label above: "Holly Run neighbors who speak a language besides English at home." Read aloud: count the shaded squares, "one, two, three"; say "some" (more than none, three squares); "many" is more than three squares. The word "percent" and any percent sign are never printed. Teacher line: "A strip counts. It cannot tell what any neighbor believes."

## Five sort cards

A language (two speech bubbles); a birthplace (a pin on a small map); a belief ("what a person thinks is true about important things", a thought bubble); a tradition (a table with a shared dish, said to be made up); the nation's language department (a desk with a book). Pairs sort into two bins: "can a number show it?" and "needs an account". Key: a language and a birthplace can be counted (a number can show it); a belief, a tradition and the nation's language department need an account, told by the people themselves. Pip says error L ("The strip tells what each neighbor believes") and is corrected.

## Pairs' strip

A second new strip for "Dell Run", invented: five squares shaded, five not, labelled "neighbors born in another town". Pairs count aloud and say "some" or "many". Five is more than three, so "many".
