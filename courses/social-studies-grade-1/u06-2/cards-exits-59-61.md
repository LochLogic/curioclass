# Exit cards, days 59 to 61

Each exit has a paper card every child marks and a spoken question heard from the day's rotating six. Say aloud first: "These towns are made up. These cards are practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines. Keep these closed until the day. No card reuses Hazel Town, the dated cards, the Constitution page, the Cherokee Nation's parts, the Holly Run or Dell Run strips, Rowan Town or Tansy Town, a repair card, or any end-packet case.

## Day 59 exit card: Pine Town

**Picture:** a card with three neighbors, drawn one at a time: one marches in a parade, one eats at a picnic, one sings a song. Under them a red stop sign that all three stand at. Caption read aloud: "Pine Town neighbors keep Pine Day with a parade, a picnic and a song. All of them stop at the same red sign." Under it three picture choices: the red-sign rule; the same picnic; the same song.

**Mark:** Circle what they all share. **Spoken (the rotating six, a separate ask):** "What do they share even though they celebrate in different ways?"

## Day 60 exit card: a passage and the Fern Town strip

**Passage, read aloud, from the Cherokee Nation's language page in our words:** "Children who are citizens of the Cherokee Nation can learn in Cherokee in immersion classes the nation runs today." Source 60C. **Strip:** ten squares, six shaded, four not, "Fern Town neighbors who speak Spanish at home", invented. Three picture choices: a nation running its own classes today; a people only in old pictures; a nation whose people all believe the same.

**Mark:** Circle what the passage shows. **Spoken:** "What does the strip show about the neighbors?" Or: "What does the nation's passage tell us that the strip cannot?" Only if a child leaves the nation's past open: "What happened to the Cherokee people's homelands long ago?" Teacher note: the United States forced most Cherokee people west in 1838; the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians stayed in North Carolina and is a nation today.

## Day 61 exit card: Elm Town

**Picture:** a small library with a door and a book sign. Caption read aloud: "Elm Town neighbors speak different languages. All use the same public library." Three picture choices: all use the same public library; I like Elm Town; the neighbors speak different languages.

**Mark:** Circle the detail that shows Elm Town is united. **Spoken:** "Tell me why Elm Town is united." Or: "What shows they are different?"
