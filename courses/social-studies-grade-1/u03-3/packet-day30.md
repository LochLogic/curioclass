# Day 30 packet: the observation (read aloud, closed until day 30)

Nothing here is taught before day 30. Models and charts stay covered; blank frames and rereading stay available. Read the three texts aloud once, then again as needed. Label each aloud as it is read: the first is a modern summary of a government page, not Addams's own words; the other two are made up. Print "invented for practice" under each made-up case.

## Text 1: a modern summary (about 45 words)

"In 1889, Jane Addams and Ellen Gates Starr opened Hull House in Chicago with other helpers. Addams was part of this group and helped lead its work. The group offered help to neighbors, including people who had moved from other countries. Addams also worked to make streets cleaner and places to play safer."

Credit printed once: "Our summary of a National Park Service page." Three pictures, plain black line drawing on white: a large house with a sign; a group at a table with neighbors; a street with a broom and a play space.

## Text 2: made up (the bus monitor)

"A bus monitor belongs to the school safety team. The monitor guides children onto the bus, reminds everyone to wait their turn, and helps them find seats. Other members check the line and help children stay safe."

Picture: a monitor at the bus step beside two other team members and a line of children.

## Text 3: made up (the puppet club)

"A puppet-club leader tells everyone they can help, sorts the tasks, listens to ideas, and guides the group to choose how to mend a torn puppet."

Picture: a leader holding a task list stands inside a ring of seated club members, who each hold a needle, thread or patch, with a torn puppet at the center.

## The four item sheets

1. **Item 1 sheet.** The puppet-club picture, with a circle for each person. Mark: circle the puppet leader among the members. Draw one thing the leader does for the group. Spoken or dictated: "What does the puppet leader do for the group?" Also asked: "What is the bus monitor's job on the school safety team?"
2. **Item 2 sheet.** The three-box frame, blank, with the three pictures along the top. Write or dictate a claim, a reason that is an action from the cases, and a closing. Directions: "What makes a good leader?"
3. **Item 3 sheet.** The three Addams pictures out of order. Mark: put the pictures in order. Tell or dictate what the group did first and what followed, and say a fact from the summary.
4. **Item 4 sheet.** A blank space for a drawn question, and the pair question cards on a strip. Pairs exchange explanations and questions.

## Record sheet

Names down the side; a column for each item part; a "heard" column for item 4. Use the one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Write the child's actual words for item 4.

## For the teacher

Do not use Addams or this episode in any earlier teaching. Do not say the neighbors could not help themselves. The summary says Addams worked to make streets cleaner and places to play safer; it does not say they became clean or safe. An answer that claims a result is partly shown. The Hull House neighbors are people who moved from other countries; say so once, in the summary's words, and ask no child about their own family or home. The two made-up cases are not the principal hook or the play leader story. Any quality the actions support may meet, such as working with others, listening or caring.
