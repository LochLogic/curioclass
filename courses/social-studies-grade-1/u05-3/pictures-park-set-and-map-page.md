# Day 52 pieces: the park photograph set and the map page

## The park photograph set

Six photographs of one small park that has water, enough to map: a wide view, a view of the water, a view of the path or walkway, a view of a place to sit or play, a view of the trees or grass, and a view of the park's entrance. Use the class's own photographs of a local park, taken by the teacher or the school and shown only with the school's permission, or six photographs the teacher chooses from a free public-domain set and credits on the back of each. Choose a park with a pond, a stream or a lake in it. The teacher states which place it is, whether it is a local park or another place, and says so aloud.

If the local place has no water, use the supplied set of a small park with a pond and say aloud: "This is another place." Never add a pond to a place that has none. Never use a home, a street or a neighborhood; no child is photographed.

## The map page

**Picture:** a letter-size page with a thick border. In one lower corner, a key box with room for six symbols, each with a line for its meaning. In the other lower corner, a blank direction indicator: a plain circle with a short arrow and the letter N, with the arrow left for the child to point in the right direction. At the top, a line for the name of the park.

**Task:** make a map of the supplied park from above: land and water drawn where the photographs show them, at least two places, a symbol for each, a key that says what each symbol means, and the north arrow pointed to the way the photographs let the teacher say north is. Save as practice. The teacher names north for the supplied park before children draw it.
