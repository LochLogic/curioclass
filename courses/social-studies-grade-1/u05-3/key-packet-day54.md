# Teacher key, the day 54 packet

Teacher only. Keep apart from packet-day54.md; never hand to children.

## Key

### Map C: the Reed region (invented)

**Teacher key for map C.** With the arrow pointing right, north is the right edge. East is down the page and west is up the page; south is the left edge.

### Items 1 to 6, with the answer in each

1. **Item 1 (map C).** Circle Reed and the bridge. The double line is the real bridge; the grouped squares stand for the town; a single line is a road. Map C is a made-up region seen from above and made small, so it can be seen all at once; the squares stand for the buildings.
2. **Item 2 (maps A and C and the relief).** Circle a continent (any of the seven named) and an ocean (any of the five named). A continent is a very large land area. An ocean is a huge connected body of salt water. On map C circle the river, the lake and the mountain; the river flows, here from the mountain toward the lake; the lake has land all around it; the mountain is very high land. A globe can show where people live: the city labeled on the globe view. On the relief, the fields, houses and pier are made by people; the hill, bay and woods were there. Map C tells that people built a bridge and a town and farm the fields.
3. **Item 3 (maps B and C).** On map B: circle Los Angeles, Washington, D.C. and the class's capital; the labels and the key tell which is the capital. On map C: place a token on the lake; the mountain is west of the lake; north of Reed is the farmland; Reed is south of the fields; north was found with the indicator.
4. **Item 4 (map B).** Circle the stars. A capital is a city where a state's or nation's government is centered. Washington, D.C. has the national government; the state capital has the state government. The biggest city is not always the capital; the starred city is where the government is centered.
5. **Item 5.** The revised day 52 map, handed in. Look-fors: the park's land and water are where the photographs show; each symbol has one meaning in the key; north is shown; at least two places; a direction word is used for one place.
6. **Item 6 (map C and its record).** Draw, write or dictate: a claim, a map detail, a record detail, a reason that links them and a closing. For example: food came along the road and across the bridge to Reed's market, and the record says more shops opened there; the bridge helped Reed be a place people go; that is one reason a town can grow. Other reasons from map C and its record meet if they join a map detail and a record detail. Spoken: one reason Reed became a place people go.
