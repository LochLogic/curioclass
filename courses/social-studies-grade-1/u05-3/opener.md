# Section 5.3 opener: a map of our own, a claim and the unit check

## What the section does

Four days, 52 to 55, of 30 minutes. Unit 5 asks: why did people build towns and cities where they did? Day 52: each child makes a map of a supplied park, with its land and water, a key and the four directions, from photographs, adding nothing the photographs do not show. Day 53: the map is revised from written feedback, and the class answers the compelling question with a claim, a map detail, an account detail, a reason and a closing. Day 54: the unit check, an observation on maps not seen before. Day 55: respond, reteach by item and try a fresh case.

## How the days run

Day 52: return and repair (5), step back (5), supported work (18), exit (2). Day 53: return and repair (5), revise the map (5), step back (5), supported work (13), exit (2). Day 54: directions and sources read aloud (5), items 1 to 6 (4, 5, 4, 3, 3, 6). Day 55: return evidence (5), reteach by item (10), fresh try (10), reflect (3), exit (2). Every child marks the exit card on days 52, 53 and 55; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six. The day 54 spoken parts are heard group by group across the paper parts.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A map that a stranger could use.** It has places where they are, land and water, symbols with a key, and the four directions. The map shows only what the photographs show; no pond is added to a place that has none.
- **One meaning for each symbol, written in the key.** A symbol with two meanings, or none, is repaired.
- **A claim needs a source detail.** "Cities grow because that is where cities are" is repeated, not evidence. Add what the map shows and what the account says, then the reason.
- **Different places, different reasons.** A harbor, a river crossing and a seat of government each helped a place grow. No single reason explains every city. "That is one reason a town can grow."
- **The check is evidence, not a rank.** Items 1 to 6 are scored from each child's own unaided answers on maps not seen before. Day 55 acts on what the check showed.
- **Every case is invented unless a card says it is real.** Alder, Larch, Reed and the observation card are made up and printed "invented for practice". The United States locator is real.

## The errors, in plain words

- **O and P:** a symbol with no meaning or two meanings; a pond added where there is none. Give each symbol one meaning in the key; hold the photograph beside the map.
- **T and U:** a claim repeated as evidence; one reason for every city. Join the claim to a source detail with "This helped because"; compare two sourced reasons.
- **C and K:** north is the top of the page; the bridge matters because it matters. Turn the map; point to the clue first, then the reason.
- **Items 1 to 6:** the day 54 errors are A, D, C, G, O and T, repaired on day 55 from the written repair cards.

A wrong answer is voiced by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child.

## Care

The park photographs show a documented park; no child's home, street or neighborhood is mapped. If the local place has no water, use the supplied set of another small park with a pond, labelled as another place. A child may point instead of write; an adult scribes. Distinguish limited English from a wrong location.

## Supplies for the section

The park photograph set and the map page, the teacher-made map of another place, written feedback cards, crayons, word bank and blank claim frames, the Harbor Map and the nations' name cards, the printed exit cards, the withheld day 54 packet, tokens, repair cards, fresh-try cards and the unit 5 record sheet.

## A room with nothing

Plain paper folded into a key box, crayons, a hand-drawn indicator; for day 54, three hand-drawn maps as in the packet piece.
