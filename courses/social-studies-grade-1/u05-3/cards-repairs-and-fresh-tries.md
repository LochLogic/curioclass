# Repair cards and fresh-try cards, section 5.3

Each repair card puts a wrong idea beside a right one so the class can see the difference. The wrong idea is spoken by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child. Say aloud: "Pip is making a mistake on purpose." Print "invented for practice" on every invented card. Used only when the exit or the check showed the error; at most five minutes, a small group when few missed it.

## O: a symbol with two meanings

**Pip:** shows a park map with a star in the key for "park" and a star elsewhere on the map for "dog", and says, "Both stars are fine."

**Right:** one meaning for each symbol, written in the key.

**Repair:** children give each symbol one meaning in the key; on day 53 each child fixes one symbol on the revised map.

## P: adding what is not there

**Pip:** adds a pond to the park map and says, "Parks have ponds."

**Right:** draw only what the photographs show.

**Repair:** hold the photograph beside the map; cross out what the photograph does not show; retry with a new bounded view.

## T: a claim repeated

**Pip:** says, "Cities grow because that is where cities are."

**Right:** add what the map shows and what the account says, then the reason.

**Repair:** pair the claim with a source detail card; join them with "This helped because"; retry on a new map.

## U: one reason for every city

**Pip:** says, "All cities grew because of the government."

**Right:** different places, different reasons.

**Repair:** two cards, harbor and capital; say what each source supports and what neither explains; retry.

## Day 55 repairs for items 1 to 4

The repair cards for A, D, C and G are the cards of sections 5.1 and 5.2, reused with new places. Item 1 repair A (the map is the place); item 2 repair D (a continent is a country); item 3 repair C (north is the top of the page); item 4 repair G (the biggest city is the capital). Use a new state and a new map each time.

## Fresh-try cards, day 55

New places of the same types, named by the teacher; never Reed, Larch or Alder, and never a case used on day 52 to 54 or in sections 5.1 and 5.2. Each is invented for practice unless it is a real state, which the teacher names and checks on the state's government page.

1. **Item 1, Willow Bend:** a small town map with a key (grouped squares are houses, a thick line is a road, a black bar is a bridge). Circle the bridge. Say what the black bar stands for.
2. **Item 2, a fresh world view:** a flat world map with names printed. Circle a continent; say what it is. Circle an ocean; say what it is. (Africa and the Arctic Ocean are good choices.)
3. **Item 3, Tamarack Lane:** a small map with the indicator pointing to the left edge of the page; a mill, a pond and a gate. Place a token on the place that is south of the pond.
4. **Item 4, a new state card:** a state outline with a starred capital and a dotted other city, the caption read aloud. Circle the capital; say why it is the capital.
5. **Item 5, a new park view:** a different park photograph; the child adds one place to their saved map with its symbol and key meaning.
6. **Item 6, Pebble Ferry:** a map of a town on flat land beside a wide river, and a record read aloud: "Farmers sent their grain down the river in boats." Say why the town grew there, with a map detail and a record detail.

The rotating six who were not heard on day 54 are heard now, one minute each, one ask.
