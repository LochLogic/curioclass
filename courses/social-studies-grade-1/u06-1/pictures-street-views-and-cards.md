# Street views and cards, days 56 to 58

Say aloud first: "These are drawings made for our class from what the places say about themselves. They show one street at one time. The towns on the cards are made up." Print "invented for practice" on every invented card. Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, large print. Photographs are not used; no photograph licence is needed. A caption says only what the drawing shows.

## Three street views (day 56, kept up days 57 and 58)

- **Sapporo, winter shop street.** Snow piled along the curb. People in scarves and boots. A snow shovel leaning by a shop door. A bicycle parked under a bare tree. Caption: "A street in Sapporo in winter. Snow is piled beside the road." Source 56A (Sapporo has snowy winters).
- **Nairobi street.** People walking past shops. Most wear jackets. A bus stop pole. Caption: "A street in Nairobi. People are wearing jackets." Source 56E.
- **Singapore street.** A shop street with trees along it. People in light clothes. A market stall with fruit. Caption: "A street in Singapore. People are wearing light clothes." Source 56C.

## Nine chart cards (day 56)

One home, one set of clothes and one street for each place. Sapporo: an apartment building with snow on its steps; a scarf and snow boots; the Sapporo street. Singapore: a block of apartments with a tree; a light shirt and a hat; the Singapore street. Nairobi: a row of low houses; a jacket and closed shoes; the Nairobi street. Each card says only what it shows. Each is one home, one set of clothes, one street, never "the homes of" a place.

## Retrieval card (day 56)

"Neighbors tell stories together every spring." Invented for practice. Ask: "What makes this a tradition? Must every neighbor join?" Answer: people repeat it and pass it on; joining is not required.

## Source routine, once (day 56)

Show the Sapporo winter page (source 56A) beside the Sapporo view. Who made it: the editors of an encyclopedia. When: today. Why: to tell readers about the city. What it helps us find out: that Sapporo has snowy winters, and a festival of snow sculptures each year. It does not tell us what any family does.

## Greeting and school cards (day 57)

- **Holly Run song (the model).** A class stands in a circle and a teacher holds a small drum. Caption: "Holly Run starts the day with a shared song." The speaker's words, read aloud: "We sing so everyone begins the day together." Invented for practice, said aloud to be made up.
- **Sorrel Town greeting (a sort card only).** Two neighbors tip their hats. No speaker's words. Invented for practice.
- **Nairobi sign.** A shop sign with two lines of lettering, one line in English and one in Kiswahili. Caption: "A sign in Nairobi with two languages." Source 56E (English and Kiswahili are official languages). The words on the lines are shown as plain stripes of lettering; no Kiswahili word is taught.

## Six sort cards and two bins (day 57)

Sort cards: the Sorrel Town greeting; the Holly Run song; the Nairobi sign; a snow pile; a river; a sandy beach. Bin one: "people made or learned it" (a custom or a language). Bin two: "land, water, weather". The sign, greeting and song go in the first; snow, the river and the beach in the second. A pond is the teacher's example for the second bin. Say maker, time and purpose for each card in one breath.

## Perspective sentence (day 57)

"Here, we start the day like this. There, they sing. For the people who use it, it makes sense because everyone begins together." Pip says error H ("That is wrong. We do it differently.") and is corrected: read the speaker's words first. Pip says error I ("The writing on the sign is part of the land.") and is corrected: people made the writing.

## Chart rows for the model (day 58)

The teacher models the Singapore column only: weather, warm days and a lot of rain (56C); what people do about it, light clothes. Said aloud: "Weather changes what we wear." Nairobi's two rainy seasons (56E) and its two official languages (English and Kiswahili) are not charted here. Pairs fill Sapporo and Singapore. The limit line: "Weather can change what we wear. It does not decide a word or a custom."

## Retrieval for the class, day 58

"Which of the three cities is farthest north?" Answer: Sapporo. Directions: north, south, east and west as unit 5 taught them. Map piece: map-three-cities.md.
