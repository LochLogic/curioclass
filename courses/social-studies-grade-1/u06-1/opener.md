# Section 6.1 opener: people live in different ways, three places today

## What the section does

Three days, 56 to 58, of 30 minutes. Unit 6 asks: what holds a country of so many different people together? This section starts far from home. Day 56: three present-day streets, Sapporo in Japan, Singapore and Nairobi in Kenya, and the difference between what a picture shows and what we think because of it. Day 57: customs and languages, and the idea that a way of life makes sense to the people who live it. Day 58: how land and weather shape a choice, such as a coat or a roof, without deciding a language or a custom; two places on a chart and on a map.

## How the days run

Day 56: hook (4), retrieval (2), teaching (8), supported work (11), exit (3), look back (2). Day 57: return and repair (5), teaching (9), supported work (10), exit (3), look back (3). Day 58: return and repair (5), reopen the map (3), model a row (5), supported work (10), exit (3), co-write (4). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A picture shows one street at one time.** It does not show how everyone in a city or a country lives. A caption may be true of the street and wrong about everyone.
- **Observation and inference are different.** "I see a jacket" is what the picture shows; "I think it is chilly" is a guess from it.
- **A custom is something people in a place do the same way; a language is the words they use.** People make or learn both. Land, water and weather are already there.
- **A way of life makes sense to the people who live it.** An unfamiliar greeting is not wrong.
- **Weather shapes a choice, such as a coat or a roof. It does not teach a word or decide a custom.**
- **Place names are the places' own names:** Sapporo (Japan), Singapore, Nairobi (Kenya). Say "in this picture", never "people in Japan" or "Africans".
- **Facts rest on checked pages** (sources 56A to 56E, in our words): Sapporo has snowy winters; Singapore has high, even temperatures and a great deal of rain; Singapore's government describes people keeping their own mother languages and using English together; Nairobi is Kenya's capital, Kenya has two rainy seasons, and English and Kiswahili are its official languages. The pictures are original drawings made for Curio Class, not photographs, and show only what the caption says.
- **Some facts are held** for the exits and the unit's end packet. Teach only what each day lists.
- **Greetings and school customs on the cards are invented** (Sorrel Town, Holly Run) and the teacher says so. No real greeting is shown.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** one picture shows how everyone lives. Show a second view beside it.
- **H:** an unfamiliar greeting is wrong. Read the speaker's words first.
- **I:** the writing on a sign is part of the land. Sort made-or-learned from land, water, weather.
- **B:** weather decides language and every custom. Weather changes a coat; it does not teach a word.

A wrong answer is voiced by Pip the puppet or shown on a teacher card, never by a named child.

## Care

No child is asked about their home, food, language, religion or where their family comes from. Nobody is asked which place is best or which they would choose. People live in cities and towns everywhere; no costume or festival picture stands alone.
