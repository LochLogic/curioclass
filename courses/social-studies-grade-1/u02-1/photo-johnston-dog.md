# The Johnston classroom photograph (days 11 and 14 to 17)

A real photograph. Print it large on one sheet. Print the credit and the caption under it in large type; the teacher reads both aloud, and children do not read them.

## Credit and caption

- **Credit:** Frances Benjamin Johnston, Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division.
- **Caption as the library gives it:** "Classroom scenes in Washington, D.C. public schools: studying live dog, 5th Division." Date: about 1899.
- **Say aloud:** "This is a photograph of another school, in Washington, D.C. It is not our school." The caption tells the place; the date tells it is old; Frances Benjamin Johnston, the photographer, is the known maker.

## What can be seen (a neutral description, read aloud for a child who cannot see the print)

A person in a long skirt and a light blouse stands at the left with a hand on a dog that stands on a desk. Children sit in rows at wooden desks and look toward the dog. Behind them is a wall with a chalkboard covered in writing and two framed pictures above it. A tall dark opening is at the right of the chalkboard.

## For the teacher

- Look first, name only what we see, then read the caption aloud. Ask the four source questions in this order: what kind (a photograph), who made it (known: the caption names the photographer), old or new (old), what could it help us find out (what one old classroom looked like, not ours).
- Never say who the children are, what they feel, or how well off they were: the caption gives none of that, and a photograph does not either. If a child asks why old schools were different from one another, say only: "In the past, some public schools kept children apart by race. That was unfair, and families went to court to change it." Say nothing more about the people in this photograph.
- The date is printed as about 1899 and the picture is not enlarged in a way that hides the caption.
