# Section 2.1 opener: the class calendar, past, present and future, and a year

## What the section does

Three days, 11 to 13, of 30 minutes. Day 11 opens unit 2, Clues from the past, with its big question, How can we find out what our school was like long ago? A real photograph from long ago (Frances Benjamin Johnston's classroom with a dog, Library of Congress) is shown beside a dated drawing of today's room, and the class asks whether it could tell us about our own school's past. It is another school's photograph. Nothing is answered today; section 2.2 answers it. Then the class learns the month page of its calendar: the date said as month, number, year, a season named from what we see outside, and a pattern read from dated drawings. Day 12 teaches past, present and future with yesterday, today and tomorrow. Day 13 lays twelve month pages in a row to make a year and places dated events on it. The exits are not scored; the scored versions are items 1 to 3 of the day 18 observation.

## How the days run

Day 11: hook and question board (4), retrieval and repair (6), teaching (9), supported work (9), exit (2). Day 12: calendar corner (2), reopen and hand-back (6), teaching (8), supported work (12), exit (2). Day 13: calendar corner (3), return and repair (5), the year strip (6), supported work (14), exit (2). Every child marks the exit card; the teacher hears the spoken part from one rotating six (days 11, 12 and 13, the first, second and third six on the record sheet). From day 13 the calendar corner also hears one fresh sentence about the class's own days from a group of six, and writes it under a small drawing for the picture feedback of days 14 to 17.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A calendar is made by people to keep track of days.** It is a page of squares: a square for each day, a row for each week. A year is twelve months, January to December. Say a date as month, number, year.
- **A season is a pattern, not a promise for a date.** A season word names what many places usually see for a few months. We name it from what the window shows. Dates do not tell the weather.
- **A drawn square is a record; an empty square says nothing.** A pattern is the same thing on dates a week apart (the same column on the next row), and only the recorded dates count. We never count days between dates.
- **Time words come with verb forms.** Yesterday goes with a past form (we went), today with a present form (we go), tomorrow with will (we will go). Score the time idea apart from the grammar: pointing, a home-language word or a dictated sentence shows the idea.
- **Order is by month first, then by day.** A year strip is a long row of months. The smallest day number does not come first.
- **Plans are dotted and records are solid.** A plan has not happened yet.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A: a card on the wrong date.** A drawing goes on the square with its date. The teacher says a date and children point to the square.
- **G: a few drawn squares taken for every day.** Point to a drawn square, then to an empty one: the record does not say about the empty one.
- **B: yesterday and tomorrow swapped.** Act a finished action and a coming action; put each on its square; say the form aloud together.
- **H: one verb form for every time.** "Yesterday we went, today we go, tomorrow we will go": say all three and point at past, present and future.
- **F: card order or day number gives time order.** Order by month first on the year strip, then by day within a month.
- **J: another school's photograph taken for ours (day 11).** The caption names the place. The teacher says once: "The caption tells which school." Day 14 and 15 return to it.

A wrong answer is always voiced by the made-up helper or "someone might say", never by a named child. No birthday, trip, family event or home photograph is asked for. Room 4 is pretend; the teacher says so each time.

## Supplies for the section

The real class calendar, a printed month page for each pair, the year strip with dotted and solid pens, the Room 4 cards, the six action cards and the strip, the dated drawing of our room made on day 11, the Johnston print with credit and caption, the printed exit cards and the unit 2 record sheet. Everything pictured is invented except the Johnston photograph.

## A room with nothing

Draw the month page on the board and fold a long strip of paper into twelve. Cards are scrap paper with a drawing and a date. Describe the photograph aloud from its caption if there is no print.
