# Month pages and the year strip (days 11 to 13)

The tools of the class calendar. Print on plain paper, black line on white, thick lines, no shading. Say aloud that a calendar is something people made to keep track of days.

## The month page (one for each pair, and a large one for the teacher)

- **Top strip:** a box for the month name (written by the teacher, read aloud) and a box for the year.
- **Squares:** seven columns, one for each day of the week, each headed by the weekday name in large print; five rows, one for each week. Each square holds its number in the corner and an empty space to draw in. The first day of the month sits in its proper column; squares before it and after the last day stay shaded grey and empty.
- **Week rows:** a thick line under each row so a child can run a finger along a week and down a column.
- **The class page:** the real month page of the room's calendar is this same layout, large, on the wall. The teacher says today's date as month, number, year and the day's helper draws one real class event on today's square. The calendar corner begins on day 12 and runs all year.

## The year strip (day 13 on)

Twelve month pages, small, laid in a row from January at the left to December at the right, each with its name in large print. Under the row: a today marker (a paper arrow the teacher moves) and two pens: a solid pen for records (what happened) and a dotted pen for plans (what will happen). Events go on the page of their month, and within a page on their day. A blank copy of the strip for each pair is kept in an envelope and a spare blank copy is held back for the assessment.

## For the teacher

- A season word names what we usually see outside for a few months, in a region. It is a pattern, not a promise for a date. Say "In our room we see ___ now" from what the window shows.
- Dates order events. Do not count days between dates. "One week apart" means the same column on the next row.
- Birthdays are never asked for. The events are the school's own days and pretend ones.
