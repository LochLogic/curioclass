# Day 46 records and pieces (held back until the check)

Keep these sealed until day 46. They appear in no teaching day. Say aloud: "These are recordings of real tests." Each card is headed with the date, plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no result written on any card. Each is a real, unlabelled record made by an adult, never an invented result; with no valid record, leave that evidence incomplete.

## Item 1 records (the cupboard label and the battery light), one sealed set for each group

Apparatus: a cupboard with a paper label on its inside wall and a small battery light on the shelf, in a room that can be made dark. Label records, one card each: the label with outside light absent, the little light off; the label with outside light present, the little light off. Light records, one card each: the little light off, no outside light, the label out of the picture or shielded; the little light switched on, no outside light, the label out of the picture or shielded. Children mark both objects' appearances; they are not told a conclusion. The label records are taken with the little light off. Item 1 records are dated photographs taken by an adult, printed as they are: the dark looks are printed dark, the page is not lightened, and no result is written on it. Each finished photograph must show the cupboard and its shelf in the same frame, with a printed caption giving the date and naming the look (for example "outside light off, little light off"). In the dark label look the label cannot be made out; in the lit look it can. The thick-line, no-shading rule applies to the piece pictures on the response sheet, not to these records. Until the photographs exist, run from paper: on the day before, an adult makes each record by hand from what the trial really showed, a black-filled frame for a look where nothing could be seen and a line drawing of the label or the glowing light where it could, each with its date and caption.

## Item 2 pieces

Four new pieces: a clear cup wall piece cut flat from a plastic cup, a thick, tightly woven dark cloth square chosen because it shows a shadow in rehearsal, tissue paper, and a differently shaped plastic mirror, with the day 45 stand, two screens and the flashlight. The clear cup wall piece, dark cloth square and tissue paper were not used on days 43 to 45 (days 43 and 44 used clear plastic, wax paper and cardboard); the mirror is a new shape. Children choose the order and predict each effect. The second screen is moved into the redirected beam and stays below faces. Each sample is compared with the empty opening.

## Check before use

The teacher rehearses every trial the day before and records what the screens really show. Nothing is filled in. Edges are trimmed and the mirror is plastic only, never glass; no lasers.
