# Section 5.2 opener: what do materials do to light?

## What the section does

Five days, 43 to 47. Section 5.1 showed that we see an ordinary object when light shines on it and a source because it makes its own light. Now the parked question comes off the board: what do materials do to a beam of light? On day 43 the class plans one fair test: a flashlight on a taped mark, a paper screen, and one piece at a time in the beam, clear plastic, wax paper and cardboard. Children mark a bright patch, a dim patch or a shadow. Day 44 adds a plastic mirror and smoothed foil and a second screen at the side, and asks two questions apart: does light go through, and does it go a new way. Day 45 sorts the trial cards into three hoops, counts each, builds a paper stand and draws a peer plan. Day 46 is the observed check: groups of five run the plan on four new pieces and answer from two sets of cupboard records. Day 47 returns the written feedback, repairs by letter and checks a fresh case. Nothing is scored on days 43 to 45 and 47 except the revision of the day 46 work. The record sheet uses one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken, and every answer is a point, a mark in a box or a few words. One group handles lights at a time. Every exit card is invented for practice, and the teacher says so aloud. The recording beside each card is a real, dated recording made by an adult, and the card supplies no result. If a trial fails, the named fallback is the adult-made dated photographs, said aloud to be recordings and marked indirect. A child may always pass.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Three groups, one separate question.** Clear plastic lets most light through, so the patch is nearly as bright. Wax paper lets some through and spreads it, so the patch is dim and has no clear edge. Cardboard blocks the light, so there is a shadow. A smooth mirror sends the beam a new way, and a patch on a side screen is turned light, not light through. The mirror stays in the blocks group; "does it turn the light?" is a yes-or-no question apart, never a fourth group. Crumpled foil turns light in many directions, which is why it is smoothed.
- **A fair test.** The light and the screen stay still and only the piece changes. If the flashlight moves, the patch changes for that reason. A bright room washes out the patch, so dim it gently, never dark.
- **Descriptions, not percentages.** The labels describe what we see. Clear plastic passes most light, not all.
- **Limits.** No speed of light, no wave idea, no account of how a mirror turns light, no shadow lengthening, no glass, no lasers.

## The errors, in plain words

- **E:** "Move the light for each piece so we can see." Repair: tape the light and change only the piece. Met on day 43, repaired on day 44.
- **C:** "The patch beside the mirror proves light went through." Repair: compare the screens behind and beside the fixed mirror. Met on day 44, repaired on day 45.
- **D:** "Mirrors need a fourth group." Repair after day 46 only, on day 47: put the mirror card in the blocks hoop and mark turns light apart.
- **A and B** from section 5.1 are repaired on day 43 and again on day 47.

## Safety and set-up

The day before each hands-on day, adults cut openings, trim plastic and cardboard, smooth foil and rehearse trials. Mirrors are plastic or edge-taped; no glass and no lasers. Never shine into eyes or look at the sun, and fix lights below faces, directing mirror beams onto screens. The stand sits on a low stable surface and has blunt pieces only. Children stay seated; the room is gently dimmed with a lit exit and a full-light alternative. A torch, white paper, kitchen wax paper, cardboard, smoothed foil and an edge-taped plastic mirror do everything.
