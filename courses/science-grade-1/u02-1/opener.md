# Section 2.1 opener: what jobs do outside parts do?

## What the section does

Six days, 10 to 15. On day 10 the class sees a paper tower tip under three gentle strokes of a folded paper fan, beside a photograph of trees that stood after a storm. Nobody explains why. The class hears the big question (how does a tree stay standing in a storm, and can we copy it to keep our tower from tipping?) and starts a part-and-job chart from a picture read-aloud about a plant. Days 11 and 12 add roots, stem, leaves and a dated shoot that leans toward a lamp. Days 13 and 14 add an animal side: parts that sense, move, take in and cover, and a chain from what happened to the part that senses to the part that moves. Day 15 asks which parts have a shape that could help people. Nothing is scored. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken and every answer is a touch, a drawing or a few words. The teacher keeps the part-and-job chart and the question board; each child holds an own drawing or arrow. All the exit cards are invented for practice, and the teacher says so each time. No live or wild animal comes into the room, and no plant part is tasted. A child may always pass; a partner may point instead.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Roots hold a plant and take in water.** A plant is not fed a meal and roots do not eat the soil. Say "roots take in water and hold."
- **A stem holds leaves up, and leaves face the light.** The plant makes its own food in its leaves from light, air and water; children are told only "it takes in water and uses light."
- **A plant responds.** A shoot leans toward a lamp because it grows more on one side. Children say only "it leaned toward the lamp," shown by dated pictures. Plants respond; they do not walk.
- **Animals sense, then move.** Eyes and ears sense; legs move. The chain "what happened, the part that senses, the part that moves" is a picture, never a mechanism.
- **A part's shape fits its job.** We copy the shape that does the job, never the picture of the animal. A pretty picture stuck on does nothing.
- **Pictures are the evidence.** The strips and the dog frames are said aloud to be invented for practice unless the class has its own real ones. We never say what a picture cannot show.
- **Storm note (day 10).** If your community has had a damaging storm recently, show the photograph without the storm words: say "in a strong wind" and leave out "some trees do fall in storms". If a child tells a storm memory, thank them, say they are safe at school now, and go back to the tower.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Roots eat the soil." Trace the water arrow into the root on the chart: "roots take in water."
- **B:** "A plant cannot move." Lay the shoot strip in date order and walk a finger along it.
- **C:** "The eyes carry the animal away." Point along the chain card: "eyes see, legs carry."
- **D:** "The petals keep it safe because they are pretty." Hold the wing cover beside the petals: "its shape does the job."

## Safety and set-up

Check for plant allergies first and offer photographs. Pick only identified, non-toxic plants and leaves, and keep sap, tasting and digging out of every day. Wash hands after days 11 and 12; use wipes at tables if there is one sink. The teacher alone handles the leaf press weights and the paper fan, and fans gently, away from faces. The ear cone on day 15 is a model held by the teacher, never put to an ear or mouth.
