# A dog turns toward a voice (day 14)

Four dated frames, printed one set for each group of five and one large for the teacher. Say aloud before you show them: "These pictures are invented for practice. A real dog is not in our room." If an adult has recorded a real pet dog turning toward its owner's usual voice, with the owner's permission, and the dated frames, timing and context are on file, use those frames in the same four places and keep the captions below. Never startle or handle an animal for a picture.

Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names. One actor: the dog, a medium dog with floppy ears, lying on a kitchen doorway rug. The owner is never drawn: a speech mark with no words comes from the kitchen at the edge of the page. Each frame has a clock picture.

## The four frames

1. **Frame 1, 5:00:** the dog lies on the rug with its head down on its paws. The kitchen door is open. No sound mark. Caption: "The dog lies still."
2. **Frame 2, 5:01:** a curved sound mark comes from the kitchen. The dog's head is still down. Caption: "A voice calls from the kitchen."
3. **Frame 3, 5:01:** the dog's head is up and its ears are turned toward the kitchen. Caption: "The dog lifts its head and turns its ears."
4. **Frame 4, 5:02:** the dog is standing and trotting toward the kitchen. Caption: "The dog walks toward the kitchen."

## Captions apart from what we think

Each caption says only what happened. A separate card, drawn as a thought bubble over the dog and headed **We think**, reads: "We think the dog heard the voice, because we saw its ears turn." The owner's usual evening call can be said aloud here as one more thing we think, never in a caption. The teacher says aloud that this is what we think, since we cannot ask the dog. Children lay the chain cards (what happened, the part that senses, the part that moves) on the frames: a voice calls, ears, legs.

**Spoken:** "What happened? Which part senses it? Which part moves the dog?" The key is in key-frames-dog-turns.md.
