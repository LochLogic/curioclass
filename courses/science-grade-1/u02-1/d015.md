# Day 15: Which shape could do the job?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Set out six sets of cards, the four examples, the chart and the cone. Choose today's six.

### Safety and set-up

The cone is a model of an ear, held by you alone at classroom volume and never put to an ear or mouth. Paper only.

### The day

#### 3 min. Return and repair

Error C (day 14's E14b): point along the chain card: "eyes see, legs carry." Whole class if more than a third made it; otherwise a small group.

#### 3 min. Warm-up

Look at the class tower from day 10. **Ask.** [[s02-P15-1]] "What job do roots do that the tower lacks?" Hear three.

#### 9 min. Four examples

Say the cards are made by us. Show four pairs, each a part's shape card and a job card: a nut's hard shell and keeping something safe; spreading roots and staying upright (the tower on a wide base); overlapping feathers and keeping rain out; a hare's wide ear and detecting, with the paper cone held up as a model of the ear. **Ask.** [[s02-P15-2]] "How does the shape of the shell do the job?" Expect: a closed, hard cover keeps it safe inside. Say: we copy the shape that does the job, never the picture of the animal. Put a shape card and a job card on each row of the design chart.

#### 12 min. Match

Say the cards are invented for practice. Each group matches four job cards to four of eight part cards: hold on to a ledge; push through water; dig into soil; stick to rough cloth. Four parts are pretty and fit no job here. **Ask.** [[s02-P15-3]] "Match each job to the part whose shape could do it." Then each child draws one arrow from a shape to its job. The six stay with you, 20 seconds each. Expect: the tendril to the ledge; the webbed foot to water; the broad paw to soil; the hooked burr to cloth. A pretty petal chosen for its colour: error D.

#### 3 min. Question board

Ask: "Which of these jobs could our tower use?" Raise: "How can someone else understand our idea?"

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "Invented." **Ask.** [[s02-X15a]] "Touch the part whose shape could keep the tool safe." **Ask.** [[s02-X15b]] "Draw one arrow from the part's shape to its job, or say how the shape helps." All touch or draw; the six say it, 20 seconds each. Expect: the wing cover; it covers the tool like a lid. The petals, "they are pretty", or a beetle drawn on the bag: error D.

#### 1 min. Pack

Cards into envelopes.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the twelve cards on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No cone: roll a sheet of paper. No cards: draw four jobs and four parts.

**Skip.** The groups, the chart and the board.

- **7 min. Four pairs.** Look at a nut shell, a tree's roots, a bird's feathers and a rabbit's ear. Say each one's job.
  **Say.** "How does the shell's shape do its job?" Expect: it is a hard cover that keeps it safe.
- **10 min. Match.** Draw a job: dig into soil. Draw two parts, a mole's paw and a pink flower.
  **Say.** "Which shape could do the job?" Expect: the paw.
- **5 min. Arrow.** Your child draws an arrow from the paw's shape to the job.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X15a]] and [[s02-X15b]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two examples and two matches; never the exit. Longer: find a shape in the room that does a job.

### Words and supports

Shape, job, hold, stick, push, dig, cover. Frame: "Its shape ___, so it can ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

If many gave error D, day 16 opens with Return and repair: the wing cover beside the petals, "its shape does the job". Day 16 sketches a root-like support for the tower.
