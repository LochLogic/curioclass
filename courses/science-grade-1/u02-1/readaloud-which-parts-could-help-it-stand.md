# Which Parts Could Help It Stand?

A picture read-aloud for day 10. Five pages, one plant part and one job on each. Before you show it, say who made it: "We made this picture book for our class. The garden in it is pretend, and no plant is in our room today." Show one page at a time and read the words under it. After each page, name the topic ("This page is about the roots") and ask the class for the detail ("What do the roots do?"). A child may point instead of speaking. Children do not read; the only printed word is the title on page 1.

Drawing style for all pages: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except the title. One actor in every picture: Tomas, a child in a sun hat with a small hand trowel who stands beside a squash plant. The plant stays in the same garden bed, the same place on every page. Pages 1 and 2 are drawn with the soil cut away so the roots show.

## Page 1

**Picture:** A garden bed with a young squash plant. Tomas stands at the edge, looking at it. The soil is cut away like a window, so thick roots with thin side roots spread out in it. A wide title across the top: Which Parts Could Help It Stand?

**Read:** Tomas looked at a squash plant. Under the ground, its roots hold it in the soil. Its roots take in water.

**Topic:** roots. **Detail:** they hold the plant and take in water.

## Page 2

**Picture:** The same plant, whole, with the soil cut away. Tomas points at the stem. The stem is long and rises from the ground to a ring of broad leaves.

**Read:** Above the ground is the stem. The stem holds the leaves up.

**Topic:** the stem. **Detail:** it holds the leaves up.

## Page 3

**Picture:** The same plant in the sun. Straight lines of light come from a sun in a corner, and every leaf faces it. Tomas shades his eyes with one hand and looks.

**Read:** Look at the leaves. The leaves face the light.

**Topic:** the leaves. **Detail:** they face the light.

## Page 4

**Picture:** A close view of one big yellow flower open on the plant. Tomas bends close and looks. Nothing else is near.

**Read:** On this plant there is a flower. A flower helps a plant make seeds.

**Topic:** the flower. **Detail:** it helps make seeds.

## Page 5

**Picture:** Later in the same garden bed. A fat squash fruit lies on the ground by the plant. A second, cut-open picture shows seeds inside the fruit. Tomas looks at the whole plant with the trowel set down.

**Read:** Later there is a fruit. The fruit keeps the seeds safe inside.

**Topic:** the fruit. **Detail:** it keeps seeds safe.

## For the teacher

Roots hold and take in water: never say roots eat. Not every plant has a flower, and the class chart says so when it gets a flower row. The squash is used because it shows all five parts; the plant parts on day 11 are real. After page 5, ask once: "Which part do you think helps it stand?" Accept any answer: this is a guess to test, not a score. Children draw their own plant on the day, with the five part cards.
