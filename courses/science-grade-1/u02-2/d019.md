# Day 19: A new problem, a copied part

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the class, part-and-job and design charts. Give each group its cards. Keep day 18's records to hand. Cut a 5 cm window in 30 slot cards; bag each with a 2 cm paper scrap and three 1 cm paper strips. Keep the camel eyelash part card face down until the exit. Key: key-cards-exits-16-19-21.md. Key: key-cards-day19-problems.md.

### Safety and set-up

A helper adult works beside you through Sketch and make and watches the scissors. Children cut seated; scissors are counted out and in; glasses on for cutting. Pieces are soft and blunt, never worn.

### The day

#### 3 min. Return and repair

Error D (day 18's exit): lay the stuck-on picture beside wider supports: "the wide shape holds it up; the picture does not." Whole class if more than a third made it.

#### 4 min. The class chart

Fill the chart's top row from day 18's group records: the tower, the part we copied (roots), the shape we tested (wide, touching the table). Fill two more rows from the part-and-job chart, the last box open. **Ask.** [[s02-P19-1]] "How did the shape we copied do its job?"

#### 4 min. Choose a problem

Say "Invented." Read the three cards: keep a paper egg from being bumped; keep paper rain off a paper garden bed, with a way in for a hand; tell a paper cart a wall is near. **Ask.** [[s02-P19-2]] "What must your shape do?" Each child picks one card; note the choices on the class list.

#### 14 min. Sketch and make

Each child sketches the problem, picks one of the eight part-and-job cards, and makes a soft blunt shape of paper and card, seated. Each checks it against the problem card, pointing to the working part. Hear six at their places, 1 minute each; write who on the record sheet.

#### 3 min. Say it

Pairs tell each other the part they copied. **Ask.** [[s02-P19-3]] "Why did you copy that part?" Hear four, 30 seconds each. Expect: its shape does the job. "It is pretty," or "it looks like the animal": error D.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "Invented." Show the camel eyelash card: a row of long hairs over the eye keeps sand out. Hand out the kits. **Ask.** [[s02-X19a]] "Lay the strips across the window the way the eyelashes keep sand out" (80 seconds). Look at six, the rest after class. **Ask.** [[s02-X19b]] "Say which part you copied and how its shape does the job." Partners say it (20 seconds); hear three, 15 seconds each. Expect: the eyelashes; gaps smaller than the scrap, open for air. Eyelashes drawn on the card: error D. A solid cover: shown with help.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Scissors counted in, glasses wiped and bagged, shapes into folders.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the cards on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw one problem and three parts. No slot card: cut a window about two fingers wide in a cereal-box card. No scissors: tear paper; if cutting, blunt child scissors, seated, with an adult watching.

**Skip.** The chart, the groups and the six heard.

- **4 min. Choose.** Draw a paper egg that must not be bumped.
  **Say.** "What must your shape do?" Expect: keep the egg from being bumped.
- **4 min. Part cards.** Draw three parts: a nut shell, feathers and a hare's ear. Say the job of each shape.
- **14 min. Make.** Your child picks one and makes a soft blunt shape of paper and card for the problem.
  **Say.** "Why did you copy that part?" Expect: its shape does the job.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X19a]] and [[s02-X19b]]: tell your child a camel's long eyelashes keep sand out of its eyes. Your child lays three strips across a card window the way the eyelashes do, so a scrap stays out and air gets in, and says which part was copied and how.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one problem and no pair talk; never the exit. Longer: make a second shape for a second problem.

### Words and supports

Problem, copy, part, protect, keep out, detect, soft, blunt. Frame: "I copied ___ because its shape ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Day 20 reteaches nothing, so error D waits for day 21: Return and repair with the working shape, point and say its job.
