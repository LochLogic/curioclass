# Section 2.2 opener: can a copied shape solve our problem?

## What the section does

Six days, 16 to 21. The class already has a part-and-job chart and the tower that tips. On day 16 the design cycle grows a step (problem, sketch, build, test) and each child sketches a root-like support, with one arrow from the shape to the job. On day 17 partners explain each other's sketches and the author redraws if the partner cannot say how the shape works. On day 18 children build the support from card and paper and the teacher tests it: three gentle strokes of the paper fan from one taped mark, stood or tipped. Changed supports are tested again. On day 19 each child picks a new problem (protect, keep out, detect), copies a part from the part-and-job cards and makes a soft blunt shape. Days 20 and 21 are the observation days. The problem is new: a paper museum object must stay untouched when a broad cardboard marker slides to its cover, while one side stays open for viewing. Nothing new is taught on days 20 and 21.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children cut seated, with child-safe scissors, and an adult pre-cuts thicker card. The teacher fans; no child does. Every prompt is read aloud; answers are a drawing, a touch or a few spoken words, and labels may be dictated. Exit cards are invented for practice and the teacher says so. On days 20 and 21 each child keeps one page and one cover. The record sheet uses one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A sketch is a plan drawing.** It shows the thing, the part we add, where it touches, and one arrow from the shape to the job. It need not look real.
- **Copying means taking the shape that does the job.** It never means sticking on a picture of the animal. A picture stuck on does nothing.
- **A fair test keeps the same marks and the same three strokes.** One stand is not always a stand: "sometimes, every time".
- **A support that spreads and touches the table holds the tower.** Cardboard feet only model spreading support. Roots anchor trees in soil, and some trees fall.
- **A cover has criteria.** It stands clear of the object, leaves a gap above it, and keeps one side open. A flat cover resting on the object fails the clearance.
- **Scoring waits.** Day 20 is preparation; the first-try work is scored after day 21, once a child has revised and explained it.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Roots eat the soil." Trace the water arrow into the root.
- **B:** "A plant cannot move." Lay the dated shoot strip in order and walk a finger along it.
- **C:** "The eyes carry the animal away." Point along the chain card: "eyes see, legs carry".
- **D:** "A tortoise picture on the cover makes it work." Put the arch that keeps the marker off beside it and point to the working shape.

## Safety and set-up

Scissors are used seated and counted in and out. Pieces are soft and blunt, with no sharp spikes, and nothing is worn on a head or body. Only the teacher fans, gently and away from faces, and stops if anything lifts. Test on a low clear table. The marker slide is slow, stops on contact and has an adult near. If the plain tower stands on day 16, move the fan mark one tape-length nearer before day 18. If no fan can be used, a hand-held folded paper moved three times stands in for it, and the teacher says so.
