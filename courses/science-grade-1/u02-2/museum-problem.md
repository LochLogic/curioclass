# The museum problem and the marker (days 20 and 21)

Held back from every earlier day. The first card is headed "invented for practice" and the teacher says so. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names.

## The problem card, read aloud on day 20

Headed: **Invented for practice.** A picture of a small paper vase on a card table in a museum room, with a taped line on the table a hand's length away and a broad cardboard marker waiting at the line. Read aloud: "A visitor's marker may slide along the table. The vase must stay untouched. Make a cover for the vase. The cover must stand over the vase and not rest on it. It must leave a gap above the vase. It must have one open side so a visitor can see inside. Use only paper, cardboard and tape. Nothing is sharp. Nothing is worn."

Three criteria, one picture each, kept beside the problem card: **stands over it** (an arch with a gap drawn above the vase); **a gap above** (a double arrow between the vase and the cover); **one open side** (an eye looking through the open side).

## The marker and the test

A broad cardboard marker, a strip about as wide as a hand, slid slowly along the table to the cover from one taped line, stopped on contact, with an adult near. One slide for each cover; the record notes touched the vase or did not touch it. The same starting line and direction every time. No striking or dropping. On day 21 only a changed cover is retested, once.

## The teacher's reading of a cover (not shown to children)

An enclosing arch that stands clear of the vase, with a gap above and one open side, meets the criteria. A flat cover resting on the vase fails the clearance. A tortoise picture used only as decoration does nothing (D). Other safe covers that meet the criteria count.
