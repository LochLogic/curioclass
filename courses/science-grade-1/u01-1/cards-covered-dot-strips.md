# Covered-dot strips (day 3)

Four paper strips for each pair, all invented, and the teacher says so aloud. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines. None of them shows a sandbox pole or a birdhouse post, because those are in the exits. Each strip is a post on a ground line with three round spots called left, middle and right. Two dots are drawn, in order, and the third spot has a paper flap taped over it.

| Strip | Post | Dots shown | Covered spot, the key |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a flagpole | left, then middle | right |
| 2 | a mailbox | right, then middle | left |
| 3 | a swing set | left, then middle | right |
| 4 | a signpost | right, then middle | left |

Strips 2 and 4 run the other way, so the children use the order of the first two dots and not a remembered word. Each pair predicts, says "I think ___, because I saw ___," and lifts the flap. Hear a rotating six. Error A: a child puts the covered dot on the middle spot, "it stays." Lay the two shown dots in order and walk a finger on.
