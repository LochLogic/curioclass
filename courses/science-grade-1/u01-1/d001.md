# Day 1: Why did the shadow move?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Teach this day after lunch. The day before, check asthma and dust lists and fix the stick (pencil upright in clay, base chalked) on taped paper in sun until after lunch. At arrival trace its shadow, write the date, draw a clock. If it runs right to left, say "ours is the other way round." Key: key-cards-exits-1-4.md.

### Safety and set-up

Never look at the sun; eyes on the ground. Clay and chalk stay out of mouths; if dust rises, use pencil dots. Count children out and in; in storms, use the tracing. After class, start the sky-watch archive: a dated folder with today's yard picture.

### The day

#### 8 min. Hook: one stick, two shadows

Two out, four at the ring, two back. The live shadow lies beside the tracing. One of the six chalks a dot at its tip; you read the clock.

**Ask.** "Why is our shadow stick's shadow on one side in the morning and the other side after lunch?" Offer no explanation.

#### 3 min. Retrieval: the weather strip

Say "This is invented." **Ask.** [[s01-P1-1]] "Touch the weather that happened twice." All point; hear the six, 15 seconds each. Expect: the sunny squares. A miss: match the sunny pictures.

#### 4 min. What we noticed

At seats: "What did you notice?" The six, 20 seconds each; scribe on the sky picture chart. Name it: a shadow is the shade the stick makes where it blocks sunlight (review).

#### 6 min. Draw the stick and its shadow

Say: "Draw the stick, its shadow, the sun, and an arrow from the sun to the ground." The sun goes where they think (3 minutes); correct nothing. Partners say "I think the shadow is here because ___" (1 minute). Hear the six, 20 seconds each.

#### 4 min. Dating a mark

Show your dot card: stick, shadow, a dot at the tip. Say the date, write it, draw the clock. "A date and a clock tell when." Children copy the dot, date and clock into the small box.

#### 3 min. Question board

Question board (kindergarten review): turn notices into two "Where will the next shadow be?" "Will it do this again?"

#### 5 min. Exit

Say "These are invented." Show the lamp-post cards. **Ask.** [[s01-X1a]] "Touch what is different on the two cards." All point. **Ask.** [[s01-X1b]] "Draw a flagpole on a school lawn, the sun, an arrow for its light, and the shadow." Look at six drawings. Show the swing and its three question cards. **Ask.** [[s01-X1c]] "Touch the question we can find out by watching the swing's shadow again and again." All point. Expect: the shadow; four parts; the shadow moving again tomorrow. Error A, "the sun stays put", or the post: shown with help.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Tub; wipes; sink.

### Other rooms

No yard or sun: saved tracings, said aloud to be from another day.

### At home

**Swap.** No yard: a sunny sill. No clay: tape a pencil to a cup. No chalk: pencil dots.

**Skip.** The ring, chart, archive and board.

- **8 min. Look twice.** Morning: stand the pencil on paper in the sun; trace its shadow, write date and time; an adult stays with them; hands washed. After lunch look again beside the tracing. Never look at the sun.
  **Say.** "What is different about the shadow?" Expect: it is on the other side.
- **8 min. Draw.** Your child draws the stick, shadow, sun and a light arrow.
  **Say.** "I think the shadow is here because ___." Expect: any reason.
- **6 min. A dated dot.** Dot the tip, write the date, draw a clock; your child copies.
- **5 min. A question.** Your child draws a question.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1a]], [[s01-X1b]] and [[s01-X1c]]: draw a post with its shadow left, then right: "What is different?" Your child draws a flagpole, shadow, sun and arrow. Then: "What can a swing's shadow tell us: will it move tomorrow, is the swing fun, what color is it?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the partner sentence. Longer: find a post outdoors.

### Words and supports

Sun, shadow, stick, shade, morning, after lunch, date, clock. Pointing, drawing and home-language answers count.

### Tomorrow

If many gave error A, day 2 opens with Return and repair: the tracing and look 1 in order, a finger walked between them. Day 2 plans three looks and predicts the third dot.
