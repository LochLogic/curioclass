# Day 1: Why did the shadow move?

## Teacher page

### Before class

The day before, fix the stick (pencil upright in clay, base chalked) on the taped paper where the sun stays until after lunch. At arrival trace its shadow, write the date, draw a clock. If it runs right to left, say "ours is the other way round."

### Safety and set-up

Never look at the sun; eyes on the ground. Clay and chalk stay out of mouths. Cloud: show a saved tracing, said aloud to be from another day. Clean-up, 2 minutes: tub, wipes, sink wash with lunch.

### The day

#### 8 min. Hook: one stick, two shadows

Two minutes out, four at the ring, two back. Eyes on the ground. The live shadow lies beside the tracing. One of the six chalks a dot at its tip; you read the clock.

**Ask.** "Why is our shadow stick's shadow on one side in the morning and the other side after lunch?" Offer no explanation.

#### 3 min. Retrieval: the weather strip

Say "This strip is invented." **Ask.** [[s01-P1-1]] "Touch the weather that happened twice." All point; hear the six, 15 seconds each. Expect: the two sunny squares. A miss: match the sunny pictures; ask again day 2.

#### 4 min. What we noticed

At seats: "What did you notice?" Six children, 20 seconds each; scribe each on the class sky picture chart. Name it: a shadow is the shade the stick makes where it blocks sunlight (review).

#### 6 min. Draw the stick and its shadow

Read aloud: "Draw the stick, its shadow, the sun, and an arrow from the sun to the ground." The sun goes where they think (3 minutes); correct nothing. Partners say "I think the shadow is here because ___" (1 minute). Hear the six, 20 seconds each.

#### 4 min. Dating a mark

Show your dot card: stick, shadow, a dot at the tip. Say the date, write it, draw the clock. "A date and a clock tell when." Children copy the dot, date and clock into the small box.

#### 3 min. Question board

Question board is review from kindergarten: turn notices into two "Where will the next shadow be?" "Will it do this again?" Read each with the class.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "These cards are invented." Show the lamp-post cards. **Ask.** [[s01-X1a]] "Touch what is different on the two cards." All point. **Ask.** [[s01-X1b]] "The girl wrote the date and drew a clock under each drawing. Say why." The six answer, 25 seconds each. Expect: the shadow; so we know which came first. Error A, "the sun stays put", or the post: shown with help.

#### 2 min. Set-up: sky-watch archive

You alone start a dated folder with today's picture of the yard.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Tub; wipes.

### Other rooms

No yard or sun: saved tracings, said aloud to be from another day.

### At home

**Swap.** No yard: a sunny sill. No clay: tape a pencil to a cup. No chalk: pencil dots.

**Skip.** The ring, chart, archive and board.

- **8 min. Look twice.** In the morning stand the pencil on a paper in the sun; trace its shadow, write the date and time. After lunch look again beside the tracing. Never look at the sun.
  **Say.** "What is different about the shadow?" Expect: it is on the other side.
- **8 min. Draw.** Your child draws the stick, its shadow and the sun with an arrow to the ground.
  **Say.** "I think the shadow is here because ___." Expect: any reason; do not correct it.
- **6 min. A dated dot.** Dot the shadow's tip, write the date, draw a clock; your child copies it.
- **5 min. A question.** Ask what your child wants to find out; draw it.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1a]] and [[s01-X1b]]: draw a post with its shadow on one side, then the other. Ask what is different, and why a date helps.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the partner sentence and the archive. Longer: find a post and say where its shadow lies.

### Words and supports

Sun, shadow, stick, shade, morning, after lunch, date, clock. Pointing, drawing and home-language answers count.

### Tomorrow

If many gave error A, day 2 opens with Return and repair: the day 1 tracing and look 1 laid in order, a finger walked from first to second. Day 2 plans three looks and predicts the third dot.
