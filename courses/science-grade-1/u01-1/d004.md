# Day 4: Does the moon follow a path too?

## Teacher page

### Before class

The day before, find a moon view with the sun screened by a building and check the moon is up in school hours; if not, plan the saved picture. Hold the exit cards back. Choose today's six. Moon pictures' key: key-moon-strips.md. Key: key-cards-exits-1-4.md. Key: key-cards-held-back-day4.md.

### Safety and set-up

Never look at the sun. Backs to the sun; no binoculars; count children out and in. Clean-up, 1 minute: logs, wipes (no clay or chalk today).

### The day

#### 4 min. Return and repair

Return and repair (kindergarten review). Error A (day 3's exit): show the chart's three dated dots; a child runs a finger along them and the class says what the next dot would do.

#### 8 min. Hook: the moon in time order

Say: "These pictures are invented. The moon does not rise at the same clock time every day." Each group puts its four pictures in time order by the clock pictures, which you read aloud (3 minutes).

**Ask.** [[s01-P4-1]] "Where did the moon go from the first picture to the last?" Hear each group, 20 seconds (2 minutes). Expect: gym roof, flagpole, far trees. Same place in every picture: error B. Point to picture 1: a pale moon in a bright afternoon sky is real.

#### 8 min. A look at the sky

Two out, four at the spot, two back. Backs to the sun; each child draws the moon where it is on the log beside the landmarks (3 minutes). If it is not up, the saved daytime moon picture, said aloud to be saved, is the look.

#### 6 min. Draw the path

**Ask.** [[s01-P4-2]] "Does the moon follow a path too?" Children copy the moons from their ordered pictures onto the log in a new color and draw a line through them (4 minutes). Compare it with the shadow dots: another path. Hear the six, 20 seconds each.

#### 4 min. Add to the chart

Add the moon's path. Write beside it: the moon can be up in the day. Question board (notices become picture questions, review from kindergarten): "What else shows at night?" Children draw it on the student page.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "Invented." **Ask.** [[s01-X4b]] "Touch where the next dot goes." **Ask.** [[s01-X4c]] "Can the moon be in the day sky? Touch yes or no." All point (1 minute). Show the moon strip with look three under its flap. **Ask.** [[s01-X4a]] "Touch where the moon will be at look three." All point; hear the six say why, 10 seconds each. Expect: left; yes; lower above the silo. "It stays": error B. The middle, "it stays": error A. No, "only at night": error C.

#### 1 min. Clean-up

Logs and pencils collected; wipes.

### Other rooms

No yard: the saved picture is the look. No printer: draw the pictures on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No pictures: draw four moons above a house, tree and fence.

**Skip.** The groups, the chart and the board.

- **8 min. In order.** Draw four moons with clock times; mix them. Your child puts them in order.
  **Say.** "Where did the moon go?" Expect: from one side to the other.
- **6 min. The path.** Your child draws a line through the moons in order.
  **Say.** "Does the moon follow a path?" Expect: yes.
- **6 min. A look.** If the moon is up, look together with the sun behind a building. Never look at the sun; no binoculars. Skip if not.
- **5 min. A question.** Your child draws what else shows at night.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X4a]], [[s01-X4b]] and [[s01-X4c]]: draw a moon low above a gate, then high above a mill, then a folded flap over a silo; ask where the moon will be under the flap. Draw a post with a dot on the right, then the middle; ask where the next dot goes. Ask if a moon can be in the day sky.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: three pictures a group. Longer: guess the next picture.

### Words and supports

Moon, path, first, last, in order, day sky. Pointing and home-language answers count.

### Tomorrow

If many gave error B or error C, day 5 opens with Return and repair: yarn from picture to picture on the moon strip, and the daytime picture with its clock.
