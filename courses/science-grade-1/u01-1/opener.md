# Section 1.1 opener: where is the sun, and where is the moon?

## What the section does

Four days, 1 to 4. On day 1 the class sees the teacher's morning tracing of a stick's shadow laid beside the live shadow after lunch. Nobody explains why it moved. The class asks the big question (why did the shadow move, and where will it be next?), each child draws the stick, its shadow and the sun-to-surface arrow, and a class sky picture chart starts. Day 2 plans three looks at one fixed stick, makes three dated dots and predicts the third. Day 3 compares another day's marks, names the pattern (the same thing happens again and again) and rehearses "I think ___, because I saw ___". Day 4 puts timed moon pictures in order, with one pale moon in the day sky, and draws the moon's path in a moon log. Nothing here is scored. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken and every answer is a touch, a drawing or a few words. The teacher keeps the chart and the question board; each child holds an own paper record. Never look straight at the sun; the moon log and the stick are enough. A child may always pass; a partner may point instead.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The shadow moves because the sun seems to move.** The shadow lies on the side away from the sun. Children say "the sun is on the other side." They are not taught why the sun seems to move.
- **A pattern repeats.** Dots went left, middle, right on day 2, and again on another day. Repeated records let us predict the next place.
- **The moon seems to move too.** Timed pictures show it low, then high, then lower across fixed landmarks. The clock times in the pictures differ on purpose and the moon does not rise at the same time each day.
- **The moon can be in the day sky.** A pale moon in a bright sky is a real observation.
- **Each card has its own view.** Left and right on one card cannot be compared with another card. Say this aloud. If our stick runs the other way round, say so.
- **All pictures here are invented for practice.** Say so every time. The fallback look when no moon is up is a saved picture, said aloud to be one.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "The sun stays put, so the shadow stays." Lay the three dated dots in order and walk a finger from dot one to dot three.
- **B:** "The moon is in the same place in every picture." Lay yarn from picture to picture on the strip in time order.
- **C:** "The moon comes out only at night." Look at the daytime picture and its clock picture and say what it shows.
