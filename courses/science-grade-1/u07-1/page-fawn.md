# Fawn in the grass (day 57)

A three-caption page, read aloud, one copy for each group of five and one large. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names. The fawn is brown with pale spots; the adult deer is larger with long legs. These are Curio Class drawings, not photographs. Say first: "We made this page for our class. A real fawn alone in the grass is fine and we never touch it."

## Caption 1

**Picture:** A fawn curled in tall grass, head low, ears flat. The adult is not in the picture.

**Read:** The fawn lies still and quiet in the grass. Lying still helps keep it hidden.

## Caption 2

**Picture:** The fawn alone and still. Far across a meadow, a small adult deer bends to eat.

**Read:** The adult deer goes off to eat. The fawn stays hidden.

## Caption 3

**Picture:** The adult deer standing over the fawn, the fawn's head under the adult.

**Read:** The adult deer comes back and feeds the fawn milk. Milk helps the fawn stay alive.

## For the teacher

Name hiding quietly as something the young does. The pattern is the same three links as the robin (the young does, the adult does, so the young stays alive) in a different form. A fawn that looks alone is normal. Do not say the adult left because she does not care, and do not give either deer a feeling. Error A (only the adult helps) is met here: trace the fawn lying still to staying hidden.
