# Section 7.1 opener: how do parents and young help the young stay alive?

## What the section does

Six days, 56 to 61. The compelling question is why a baby bird cheeps with its mouth open when its parent lands on the nest. Day 56 starts with a public domain photograph of a robin feeding young (Courtney Celley, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) with its caption covered, then a Curio Class picture account, "Cheep at the nest". Children say what they see, post questions and start a three-link picture model: what the young does, what the adult does, so the young stays alive. Day 57 reads the robin page, the fawn page and a bean plant page and fills a chart. Day 58 sets the words beside the pictures and asks what each shows. Day 59 lays the robin and deer chains side by side and reaches the class view: both generations act, their actions can help the young stay alive, and the actions differ. Day 60 is the observed check on invented duck pages and a sunflower page. Day 61 returns picture comments, repairs by letter and tries a fresh robin page.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every page is read aloud, and every answer is a point, a mark in a box, a drawing or a few words the teacher writes. A child may always pass. Every exit case is invented for practice and said aloud to be so. A rotating six is heard on each exit and the record sheet has a heard column. One scale on every record: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The pattern is a repeated relationship, not a rule.** A young action, an adult action and staying alive, in different forms: the robin cheeps and opens its mouth and the adult brings food; the fawn lies still and quiet and the adult comes back to feed it milk.
- **Hiding quietly is something the young does.** A fawn alone in the grass is normal and is never touched.
- **A picture shows what can be seen; words can report a sound or a reason.** Children say "the words" and "the picture". The word "source" is for teachers only, because units 4 and 5 used it for light and sound.
- **A cheep is a signal, as in unit 6:** a sound that goes across. It is not an agreed message, and nothing says every cheep gets food.
- **Plants.** Grown plants make seeds and a seed grows into a young plant. Plants do not feed their young.
- **Limits.** Animals and plants only. No child is asked about their own family and no human family is compared. No talk of how animals reproduce. Say what an animal does, not what it feels. No live nest, no nest visit and no wildlife handling.

## The errors, in plain words

- **Error A:** only the adult helps. Repair: trace the young action to staying alive.
- **Error B:** every animal does the same thing. Repair: one difference named, the link kept.
- **Error C:** a picture proves a sound. Repair: sort what we can see from what the words tell.
- **Error D:** a plant feeds its young like an animal. Repair: the seed sequence with arrows made and grew.
- **Error E:** grown plants cannot make young. Repair: grown plant, seed, young plant.

## Safety and set-up

Seated paper work. Per group of five: five response sheets, crayons, one packet in an envelope and a large chart. Adults print and cut the cards the day before; day 59 uses large pre-cut cards. Keep the aisles clear and replace damaged cards. No chemicals, live animals or special gear. Reserve two clean-up minutes each day. Keep the exit cards (alligator, sea otter, penguin) and the day 60 duck and sunflower pages in a sealed envelope until their day. Pip the puppet speaks one line at a time and is wrong on purpose: "The grown-up does all the helping", "All baby animals call for food", "I can hear the baby birds in the photograph!"
