# Cheep at the nest (day 56)

A picture read-aloud for the hook, shown after the robin feeding photograph. Before you show it, say who made it: "We made this account for our class from what is known about robins. It is not a film of one real nest." Show one page at a time and read only the words under it. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names. One robin family on every page: one adult and four young. The small curved marks beside a beak are call marks. They are a drawing of a cheep, and a drawing of a sound is not a sound.

## Page 1

**Picture:** A nest of grass in the fork of a tree. Four young robins sit in it with their beaks closed. No adult in view.

**Read:** Four young robins sit in a nest in a tree. The adult robin is away.

## Page 2

**Picture:** The adult robin lands on the rim of the nest with a worm in its beak. The young have lifted their heads.

**Read:** The adult robin lands on the nest. It has a worm in its beak.

## Page 3

**Picture:** The same nest. Every young robin has its beak wide open, with three call marks beside each beak. The adult stands over them.

**Read:** The young robins cheep, cheep, cheep. Their mouths are open wide.

## Page 4

**Picture:** The adult bends down and puts the worm into one open mouth. The other three beaks stay open.

**Read:** The adult puts the worm into one open mouth. Next time, a different young robin may get the food.

## Page 5

**Picture:** The nest again, the adult gone, the four young settled with beaks closed. A small bird shape flies off at the top of the page.

**Read:** The adult flies off to find more food. The young robins wait in the nest.

## For the teacher

After page 5, ask the compelling question: why does a baby bird cheep with its mouth open when its parent lands on the nest? Hear three guesses and post it on the paper question board. Say once that a photograph cannot make a sound, and that the words tell us about the cheeping. The account says what the young and the adult do. It does not say what any robin feels, and it does not say that every cheep gets food: the adult feeds one mouth at a time. A cheep is a signal the adult notices, not an agreed message. Keep the alligator, otter and penguin cases out of this reading: they are held back for the exits.
