# Day 56: Why does the baby bird cheep?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 55 ended unit 6. Print three-box pages and six packets. Rule the question board: young, adult, staying alive. Cover the photograph's caption. Hide the exit cards.

### Safety and set-up

Seated paper work. No live nest or handling of wild animals. Any child may pass.

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook

Show the photograph; hear three. Say, "We made this account from what is known about robins; it is not one real nest." Read the Cheep at the nest read-aloud. The adult is the parent. **Ask.** "Why does a baby bird cheep with its mouth open when its parent lands on the nest?" Post three guesses. Say once: a photograph cannot make a sound.

#### 4 min. Retrieval

Card M22, invented for practice: "Mark the parent plant and the young plant. What does the seed arrow mean?" Expect: a seed grew into the young one. Then one minute: "In unit 2 you saw animals sense something and respond. Who can name one?" Hear two. Say: "Today an adult responds to its young."

#### 3 min. Teacher models a question

You asked questions of a picture and of words in kindergarten (review). Show frame 2 large. Ask, "What is the young bird doing?" Answer from the picture: beak wide open. Ask the same of page 3's words: they cheep. Say: staying alive means the young keep living; the third box is for that.

#### 8 min. Groups look and ask

Six groups of five, one packet each. Five minutes, a minute a child: each points and says "I see ___." Three minutes: the group agrees one question; post it under young, adult or staying alive.

#### 4 min. Start the model

Draw on the class model: open mouth, adult with food, a third box with a question mark. Each child draws the same.

#### 4 min. Exit

As in kindergarten (review): the exit with picture cards, invented for practice, said aloud. Say, "Invented for practice." Show the alligator cards; read each word strip aloud. One minute, everyone touches; only the heard six count: **Ask.** [[s07-X56a]] "Touch the words that say what the young do." **Ask.** [[s07-X56b]] "Touch the picture that shows what the adult does." Three minutes, a rotating six at 30 seconds: **Ask.** [[s07-X56c]] "Say one thing you want to find out about how this helps them." "The babies do nothing" is error A.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Collect packets and pages.

### Other rooms

No display: hold up one large print.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed pages: draw a nest, open-beaked young and an adult with a worm.

**Skip.** The groups and the board.

- **8 min. Look.** Show a picture of a bird feeding its young. Your child says "I see ___" three times.
  **Say.** "What is the young doing? What is the adult doing?" Expect: the young opens its mouth; the adult brings food.
- **7 min. Ask.** Your child asks one question about it. Write it down.
- **10 min. Draw.** Fold paper in three. Draw the open mouth, the adult with food, and a question mark.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s07-X56a]], [[s07-X56b]] and [[s07-X56c]], asked aloud: read the words and describe the pictures in plain speech.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the retrieval. Never cut the exit. Longer: a second question.

### Words and supports

Young, adult, parent, cheep, staying alive. Frames: "I see ___." "I want to find out ___." Pointing counts.
