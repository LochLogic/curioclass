# Section 6.2 opener: can we depend on our device?

## What the section does

Four days, 52 to 55. Section 6.1 ended with each child's first near and farther trial. Day 52 repeats that trial and asks the new question: does the same device give the same mark when we try it again? That is what dependable means here. Day 53 gives a fresh problem, printed invented for practice: a helper at a display table far across the room must be told that a picture is ready. Each child adapts the device made on day 49 by adding or changing one designed part, cites an earlier trial for it and revises the sketch. Day 54 is the observed check: each child sends the new message at a near mark and at a farther mark, the receiver marks a picture before the sender's card is shown, and the child says how the device carries the message. Day 55 returns the written picture comments, repairs by letter, lets a child repair one named step and try again, and closes with the class's own answer to the compelling question.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken, and every answer is a point, a mark in a box, a drawing or a few words the teacher writes. One group at a time uses the one closed lane. Day 54 has no coaching and no new construction. Nothing is scored on days 52, 53 and 55 except the revision of earlier work and the replaced send and receipt marks. A child may always pass, and an honest failed trial is evidence.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Dependable.** The same device, tried the same way, gives the same mark each time so far. Two matching trials are a start, not a promise.
- **Evidence for a feature.** A child adds or changes one part and can name the trial that told them to. A borrowed flashlight alone is not building; a partner's making part is named on the sheet.
- **Light and sound paths.** A cardboard wall blocks light and leaves a clear path for sound (unit 5's block). No wave account, no workings of phones or radios.
- **Reading the receiver.** The friend's page is read as marked, not as the sender's card. Noticing a signal is not understanding it.
- **Limits.** Farther means a safe marked classroom distance, within the string length less one arm span. No ranking of classmates' devices.

## The errors, in plain words

- **Error F:** "It worked once, so it always will." Repair: a one-trial record beside a two-trial record. Met on day 52, repaired on day 53.
- **Errors A to F on day 54:** the gaps are repaired on day 55 in three groups. Group A with F compares sender's cards with friends' marks. Group C with D lays near and farther results side by side. Group B with E sorts a sketch beside a made device, then lays six strips touching.

## Safety and set-up

Rehearse the lane and the signals the day before, and lay the day 54 marks before class. Keep light low and aimed at cards, never at eyes. Keep sounds at classroom level and away from ears. Hold string low and gently straight, with no loops on bodies, and walk around it. Adults pre-punch cups and cut string and cardboard. Only one group tests at a time under direct supervision. If a device breaks on day 54 the turn records device failed, and day 55 mends it.
