# Day 5: When can we see stars?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Fill six tubs with the day and night sky card sets. Hold the exit cards back. Hang the class pattern chart. Set out day 4's logs, the timed moon picture sequence, the paper moon log and yarn. Date today's sky picture. Choose today's six.

### Safety and set-up

Paper only. Keep night pictures calm: some children fear the dark.

### The day

#### 4 min. Return and repair

Return the logs. Error B: yarn from picture to picture on the moon strip (2 minutes). Error C: say what the daytime moon picture shows (2 minutes).

#### 3 min. Warm-up

Read the class sky picture chart aloud (review).

**Ask.** [[s01-P5-1]] "What will the next shadow dot do?" Hear three. Expect: the same way again, because the dots went that way.

#### 9 min. Sort the sky cards

Say: "These cards are invented for practice." Clock times differ on purpose.

**Ask.** [[s01-P5-2]] "Sort the cards into day and night." (4 minutes) Expect: day 2, 4, 6; night 1, 3, 5, 7, 8.

**Ask.** [[s01-P5-3]] "Put a finger on the cards where stars show." (2 minutes) Expect: 1, 3, 5, 7; card 8's clouds cover them. Hear the six, 20 seconds each. "The stars went away": point to card 4, "we could not see them, but they are still there" (error D). "Moon only for night": card 6 and its clock (error C).

#### 6 min. The class pattern chart

Day and night headings; the sun under day only; "not seen" in the stars row under day.

**Ask.** [[s01-P5-4]] "What can we see in the night sky that we cannot see in the day?" Expect: stars. Draw them under night, write "clear and dark". Say once: "The stars are up there in the day too, but the daytime sky is too bright to see them."

#### 4 min. Revise the chart

**Ask.** [[s01-P5-5]] "Where can the moon be on our chart?" Expect: day and night. Draw it in both. Children dictate a line and you scribe it (2 minutes); each draws a night sky with stars on the student page (2 minutes).

#### 2 min. Question board

Raise: "Will these patterns hold at another place?"

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "Invented cards, a creek bridge." **Ask.** [[s01-X5a]] "Say why the afternoon card has no stars." [[s01-X5b]] "Touch the card where we could see stars." [[s01-X5c]] "Can the moon be in the day sky?" All touch; the six answer, two to an item. Expect: too bright, still there; the clear dark night; yes. "They went away": error D. "No, for night": error C.

#### 2 min. Set-up: sky-watch archive

Cards to tubs. You alone add today's picture and date to the archive.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the eight cards on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw eight skies on scrap paper (three sunny, three clear dark nights with dot stars, one cloudy night, one bright sky with a pale moon).

**Skip.** The groups, the chart and the board.

- **8 min. Sort.** Lay out the skies. Your child puts day and night apart and points to where stars show.
  **Say.** "Where can we see stars?" Expect: on the clear dark nights.
- **6 min. Why not in the day?** Show a sunny sky.
  **Say.** "Why are there no stars here?" Expect: the sky is too bright to see them; they are still there.
- **6 min. The moon.** Show the pale moon in a bright sky.
  **Say.** "Can the moon be in the day sky?" Expect: yes.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws a night sky with stars.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X5a]], [[s01-X5b]] and [[s01-X5c]]: ask why a sunny sky shows no stars; which of three skies shows stars; and whether a moon can be in the day sky.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: four cards a group. Longer: a cloud over a clear night; what does it hide?

### Words and supports

Star, night, day, clear, dark, cloudy, bright, not seen, still there. Pointing and home language count.

### Tomorrow

If many gave error C or error D, day 6 opens with Return and repair: the daytime moon picture, and "not seen, but still there" with the bright-sky card. Day 6 tests the chart on another school's records.
