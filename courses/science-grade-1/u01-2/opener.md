# Section 1.2 opener: when can we see stars?

## What the section does

Four days, 5 to 8. Day 5 sorts day and night sky cards and asks when stars can be seen. Day 6 tests the class's patterns against another school's invented records. Day 7 is the check: each child answers three unfamiliar sheets (a sun sheet, a moon sheet and a farm's star records) with a drawing, a mark and a few words, and the teacher hears group turns. Day 8 returns the sheets with feedback, repairs missed patterns and tries unscored fresh cards. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Day 7 is the only scored day.

## How the days run

Each day is 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken and every answer is a touch, a drawing or a few words. All the cards here are invented for practice, and the teacher says so each time. Real records from another place have not been shown to the class, so we say what the cards show. A child may always pass; a partner may point instead.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Stars are there in the day.** The daytime sky is too bright to see them. Not seen is not the same as not there.
- **A pattern predicts.** Clear dark nights showed stars twice, so the next clear dark night should too.
- **The moon can be in the day sky.** Timed pictures show the moon in a new place each look, and a pale moon can sit in a bright sky.
- **Records from another place test our pattern.** If another school's dots go left, middle, right, the pattern holds there too.
- **Each card has its own view.** Left and right on one card cannot be compared with another card.

## The errors, in plain words

- **B:** "The moon is in the same place in every picture." Lay yarn from picture to picture on the strip.
- **C:** "The moon comes out only at night." Look at the daytime picture and its clock and say what it shows.
- **D:** "The stars go away in the morning." Say "not seen, but still there" with the bright-sky card.
- **A:** "The sun stays put." Lay the dated marks in order and walk a finger along them.
