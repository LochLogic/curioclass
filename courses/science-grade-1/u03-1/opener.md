# Section 3.1 opener: are the young like their parents?

## What the section does

Six days, 22 to 27. The class is asked a question it cannot settle: are the little trees under our big maple like it, exactly like it? On day 22 the teacher shows the pressed leaves from unit 2 beside the dated seedling photograph and leaves the question open. Looking alike does not prove which tree a seedling came from, and standing under a tree does not either. To practice the move, the class studies drawn plant families in which the caption gives the parent and a seed path. Children lay three leaf blades with their bases on one tape line and see which tip is longest. Days 23 and 24 add a dog family and a cat family. Day 25 adds the other young and the other adults of each kind and ends in one account the class dictates. Day 26 is the observed assessment on an oak family and a rabbit family. Day 27 returns the feedback and asks for a fresh account. Nothing is scored on days 22 to 25 and 27. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken, and every answer is a point, a link line, a ring, a laid-out row of blades or a few words. The teacher keeps the class comparison chart and the question board. Each child holds an own record sheet. Every drawn family is invented for practice, and the teacher says so each time. No live animal comes into the room. A child may always pass, and a partner may point instead.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Alike but not exactly.** Young plants and animals share features with their parents, and they also differ. The two halves need each other: a shared feature is why we call them one kind, and a difference does not break the kind.
- **A caption supplies the parent.** In the drawn families the caption says which is the parent and a dotted seed path shows where a young plant came from. Nobody works out a parent from looks or from where something stands. The schoolyard question therefore stays open.
- **Young plants have parents too.** The seed path shows it. A leaf is evidence about its plant, not a young plant itself.
- **Variation among adults is normal.** Another adult of a kind may differ from the parent as much as a young one does.
- **Length is compared directly.** Blades are laid with their bases on one line and the tips show which is longer. No units, no counting. If two real leaves are nearly equal, call them about the same and do not force an order.
- **Limits.** No way of passing on features is taught, no predicting what a young one will look like, no hybrids, no animals that change form, and no child is compared with anyone in their family. Only plants and animals are compared.
- **If a child talks about their own family.** Listen, then say one warm line: "Families are made in many ways, and every family is a real family." Ask nothing more, compare no one, and go back to the plant or animal on the page. Tell the child privately that they can come and talk to you any time.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "The young are exact copies." Mark one feature that is the same and one that is not.
- **B:** "A different patch means it cannot be a cat." Point to the features two different-looking ones still share.
- **C:** "Plants cannot have young." Trace the seed path on the maple card with a finger.

## Safety and set-up

Pressed leaves are looked at, never touched, and are checked the day before for identity, dust and mold. A child with a plant-dust allergy sees only the photograph. Nothing is tasted, rubbed into eyes or crushed. Wash hands after day 22; use wipes at tables if there is one sink. An adult cuts all blades before class and checks every paper edge, and children use no scissors. Blades are life-size and kept in envelopes. Print every master at actual size with the bases on one line and check them against the teacher-only dimensions before cutting. Keep each day's exit cards sealed and keep the oak and rabbit cards sealed until day 26. If a school has no pressed leaves or seedling photograph, open day 22 on the maple cards alone and still put the question on the board.
