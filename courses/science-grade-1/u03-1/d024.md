# Day 24: Is it still a cat?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print 15 C24 cat family copies. Hang the class comparison chart and the unit 3 question board, with the maples and dogs on the chart. Lay out the D23 dog family copies and repair cards.

### Safety and set-up

Children work seated. The cats are drawings. Any child may pass.

### The day

#### 4 min. Open

Read aloud one claim from a pair's day 23 dog sheet, such as "alike in floppy ears". Read the board question: "Can all of one kind look the same?" Today repeats day 23's routine; nothing is new. The schoolyard trees stay unanswered.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Error A from day 23's exit. Lay the repair card beside a dog copy and read two invented sentences: "The young are exact copies." "They share fur, but their markings differ." **Ask.** "Which sentence fits what you see?" Expect: the second. In turn, each child marks one same and one not the same on the pair's dog copy.

#### 10 min. Pairs: the same routine on cats

Say: "This cat family is invented for practice." Fifteen pairs, one C24 copy each. Link each feature the cat parent shares with a kitten and ring each difference. Replace "looks the same" with a named feature: ears, fur, legs, tail. Hear six pairs, about a minute each, no speed target. Expect ears, fur, legs and tail linked; the markings ringed. The dog parent is a different kind.

#### 5 min. Revise the model

Add the cats to the chart as a second case against exact copying. **Ask.** "Is it still a cat if one thing is different?" Expect: yes; the ears, fur, four legs and tail are the same. "A different patch means it cannot be a cat" is error B.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "Invented." **Ask.** [[s03-X24a]] "Point to two things that are the same on the mouse and the young one." **Ask.** [[s03-X24b]] "Point to one thing that is not the same." All point on their cards; hear six, 20 seconds each. "That makes them different animals" is error B.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Copies into envelopes.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the cats on scrap paper. One projector: one picture at a time; each child still marks a record.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed sheet: draw a cat and a kitten, each with triangular ears, fur, four legs, a long tail and one dark patch in a different place on each.

**Skip.** The pairs, the repair card and the chart.

- **5 min. Look back.** Say "They are exact copies." Ask what is not the same.
- **10 min. Compare.** Your child draws a line between each feature that is the same and rings what is not.
  **Say.** "Is it still a cat if one thing is different?" Expect: yes; the ears, fur, legs and tail are the same.
- **5 min. Say it.** Your child names a feature, not "looks the same".
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X24a]] and [[s03-X24b]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one kitten only; keep the repair and both exit asks. Longer: find a second feature the other kitten shares.

### Words and supports

Cat, kitten, parent, alike, different, feature, patch, still. Frame: "Alike in ___; different in ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Error B, "a different patch means it cannot be a cat", is repaired first thing tomorrow: day 25 puts two cats with different patches side by side and children point to what stays the same.

### Credit

Ours, all of it.
