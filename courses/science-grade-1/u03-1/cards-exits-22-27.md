# Exit cards, days 22 to 25 and 27

Keep these out of sight until each day's exit. None is used in a hook, a model or a packet, and each shows new individuals, never a family from a teaching day. Say "this is invented for practice" first. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, one big word under each marking. Every card is captioned invented for practice and gives the parent in its caption. Animals are drawn in side view at the same size. Every child answers on paper or by pointing; the teacher hears a rotating six and writes who on the record sheet. Keys, wrong answers and blade sizes are in key-cards-exits-22-27.md (teacher only, never printed for children).

## Day 22: M22E

A different kind of plant from the maple. The parent EP has an oval blade with a smooth edge. Show the parent and its two young plants EY1 and EY2, each whole with roots, stem and its blade, and a dotted seed path from EP to each young plant. Beside them, another adult EA of the same kind, a small one, captioned "Same kind, another adult". Give each child three blades loose and unaligned, with a tape line: one from EA, one from EY2 and one from EP.

**Item s03-X22a.** Spoken: "Point to something that is the same on the parent plant and its young."

**Item s03-X22b.** Spoken: "Lay the three blades with their bases together, from shortest to longest."

## Day 23: D23E

A brown horse with two young beside it, all at the same size: a brown foal with a long face, a mane, four long legs and hooves, and a white star on its face that the parent does not have; and a calf. Caption: "One of the young is this horse's foal."

**Item s03-X23a.** Spoken: "Touch the young one that is this horse's foal."

**Item s03-X23b.** Spoken: "Pip says the foal is an exact copy of its parent. Is Pip right? Thumb up for yes, thumb down for no."

## Day 24: C24E

A white sheep with a black face and black legs, with three young at the same size: a white lamb with a black face and black legs; a white lamb with a black face, black legs and one black patch on its back; a duckling. Caption: "The sheep's lambs are here." Three small cards for item b: a black face, black legs and white wool, like the parent; the black patch on its back; it stands on grass.

**Item s03-X24a.** Spoken: "Touch every young one that could be this sheep's lamb."

**Item s03-X24b.** Spoken: "Touch the card that shows the lamb with the patch is still a sheep."

## Day 25: V25E

A pig family and a willow family, new to the class. Pig parent PP is pink with a black ear; young PY1 is pink with a black patch on the back and PY2 pink with a black patch on a back leg; another pig adult PA is pink with a black spot on the nose. All have a snout, four legs and a curly tail. Willow parent WP, young WY1 and WY2 and another willow adult WA all have long narrow blades with pointed tips, drawn at the sizes in the key. A seed path runs from WP to each young plant. No pig or willow card is used in any teaching day.

Two printed groupings, both shown and neither a model to revise. **Grouping 1:** two boxes with no heading, the four big adults, pigs and willows, in one and the four small young ones in the other. **Grouping 2:** two boxes, the four willows together and the four pigs together.

**Item s03-X25a.** Spoken: "Touch the grouping that puts the same kind together."

**Item s03-X25b.** Spoken: "Tell me why those go together even though they do not all look the same."

## Day 27: P27E and A27E (unscored)

**P27E.** A dandelion parent and its young plant, both with long leaves with jagged toothed edges, the young leaf drawn smaller, a seed path from parent to young. **A27E.** A gray goose and a gray gosling, both with a flat beak, feathers, two legs and webbed feet; the goose has a white neck band and the gosling has a white wing tip. Caption: "The goose is the gosling's parent."

**Items s03-X27a to s03-X27d.** Spoken, one ask each: "Point to something that is the same on the dandelion parent and the young one." "Point to something that is not the same." Then the same two asks for the goose and the gosling.
