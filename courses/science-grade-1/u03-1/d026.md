# Day 26: Tell about a new plant family and a new animal family

## Teacher page

### Before class

Set thirty sets of O26 oak blades and O26 and R26 family cards, one for each child. Lay out the comparison strip and masking tape for each child, and the day 23 and 24 packets with picture directions for waiting children. Ask a helper adult to move round. Keep the class comparison chart on the wall with no model answer.

### Safety and set-up

Children sit; blades are paper cut by an adult. Any child may pass, or point instead of speak.

### The day

#### 2 min. Launch

Say: "These families are invented for practice. I will not help with the answers." Hear the task: an oak family and a rabbit family. Nothing is scored out loud.

#### 6 min. Leaf ordering and picture records

Each child lays their own OY1, OY2 and OP blades, bases on the tape line, then marks the oak card and the rabbit card with link lines and rings. Give no help with the answer. If two blades are nearly equal, say "about the same" and do not force an order.

#### 24 min. Group turns

Rows [[s03-E26a]] and [[s03-E26b]]. Six groups of five, four minutes each, about 48 seconds a child. Each child points through the plant claim and then the animal claim and says the evidence: the parent, both young, the other adult and the other kind. Write the spoken reason on the child's record. Waiting children finish their marks, then sort the day 23 and 24 packets onto the class chart from the picture directions; the helper moves round. With no helper, the turns run in the same order and the picture directions are the only waiting work. Expect shared rounded lobes with the blades OY1, OY2, OP in order, and long upright ears and fur with the markings differing, the other adult different from the parent, the pine and the fish told apart by needles and fins. "Exact copies" is error A; "a different patch means it cannot be a rabbit" is error B; "plants cannot have young" is error C.

#### 3 min. Collect and clean-up

Collect the records and cards once, for scoring after class. Count the blades into the envelope and put pencils away.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the two families on scrap paper. One projector: show one family at a time; the turns still run at one table.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: draw an oak parent with two young and another adult, and a rabbit family, then cut three paper blades in three lengths.

**Skip.** The group turns, the record sheet and the waiting work.

- **25 min. The assessment.** Your child lays three blades bases together, shortest to longest, then marks both pictures with lines and rings. Do not help.
  **Say.** "What do these show about the plants? About the animals?" Expect: they share a feature with the parent but are not exact copies, and the odd one out is a different kind.
- **10 min. Look back.** Praise one thing. Correct nothing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: none; keep every part of the check. Longer: none; nothing is added to a check.

### Words and supports

Parent, young, adult, kind, alike, different, feature, shorter, longer. Read every prompt aloud; pointing, a scribe and home-language answers count.

### Tomorrow

Day 26 closes the check. The records hold each child's account, and scoring comes after class. Day 27 returns each child's picture comment, repairs error B and error C from day 25 and the gaps this record shows by letter, then asks for a fresh account of a maple and a dog.

Ours, all of it.
