# Day 25: Are all the young the same?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Set six V25 kind packets, two each for maple, dog and cat; each group also takes a comparison strip and masking tape. Lay out the C24 cat family copies and repair cards. Cut maple blades to key-cards-m22 and keep key-cards-exits-22-27 (both teacher only) for the keys.

### Safety and set-up

Seated. Any child may pass.

### The day

#### 3 min. Open

**Ask.** "Are all young the same?" Lay MY2 and MP on the tape line, bases together; say once, "the flat part of a leaf is its blade (review)." **Ask.** "Which tip is longer?" Expect: MP.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Error B from day 24's exit. Show the repair card: two cats with different patches side by side. Read the invented sentences: "A different patch means it cannot be a cat." "Their patches differ, but their other shared features show both are cats." Children point to what stays the same.

#### 7 min. Groups: one kind each

Say: "These are invented for practice." Six groups: two on maples, two on dogs, two on cats. Compare young with young, then adults with adults, then name the contrasting kind. Maple groups lay MY1, MY2 and MP on the tape line, then MP beside MA; blades that differ a little are "a little longer", never forced into an order.

#### 5 min. Report

Six groups, about 40 seconds each: one same and one different. **Ask.** "Which evidence changed our model?" Expect: the adults differ from each other too, not only the young.

#### 5 min. Revise: one shared account

The class dictates and you scribe: plants and animals, a feature alike and different for each, then the schoolyard question. Expect: "Young plants and animals are like their parents in some features and different in others. We do not know yet where the seedlings under the big tree came from." Claim no parent for them.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "Invented." Show the pig and willow families and both groupings. **Ask.** [[s03-X25a]] **Ask.** [[s03-X25b]] Hear six, 15 seconds each. "The small willow does not go with the big one" is error B; "plants do not have young" is error C.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Packets into envelopes.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the packets on scrap paper. One projector: one picture at a time.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed packet: cut three paper leaf shapes in three lengths, and draw a cat family with different patches.

**Skip.** The groups, the report and the class account.

- **5 min. Look.** Your child lays three leaf shapes with bases on a line.
- **10 min. Compare.** Young with young, then adults with adults.
  **Say.** "Are all the young the same?" Expect: no; they are alike in shape and different in length or markings.
- **5 min. Say it.** Say one sentence joining a plant and an animal.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X25a]] and [[s03-X25b]], answered alone: say two lists of a pig family and a willow family, one by size and one by kind.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one kind only; keep the repair and exit. Longer: a second difference among adults.

### Words and supports

Young, adult, kind, alike, different, feature. Frame: "Alike in ___; different in ___."

### Tomorrow

Today's misses go to day 27: error B, a difference that takes it out of its kind, and error C, plants having no young. Day 26 teaches nothing new: each child explains an unfamiliar plant family and animal family.
