# Day strip key for the adult who prints them

Teacher only. Keep this page off every child sheet: children never see a clock value. Times are standard time, copied on 2026-11-19 from the federal sunrise and sunset table (Astronomical Applications, U.S. Naval Observatory; Denver 39.74 north, 104.99 west, seven hours west; Boston 42.36 north, 71.06 west, five hours west). They are calculated records, not our own sightings, and they do not show cloud. If the school is in Denver or Boston, use the other city for the day 64 set and the held-back sets, and draw the class archive from your own town.

## How a strip is drawn

One bar for a whole day, midnight at the left end to midnight at the right end, the same length on every strip: 24 cm on a child's paper (1 cm to the hour) and 48 cm for the large strip (2 cm to the hour). The dark part is solid black. The light part is white, from the sunrise picture (a half sun with rays and an arrow pointing up) to the sunset picture (a half sun with rays and an arrow pointing down). A strip carries its date only; season words are read aloud or sit on the paper labels. No child is asked to read a time. The reports on the cards are the clock time on the wall.

## Checks before printing

Every bar has the same total length. Each strip is drawn from the exact times (the Oct. 1 light part is 11.75 cm, read as 11.8; the Feb. 2 light part is 10.23 cm, read as 10.2). Day 63 class strips (Oct. 1, Jan. 5, Mar. 2) have light parts of 11.8, 9.5 and 11.4 cm at 1 cm to the hour, so winter is about 2.3 cm shorter than fall and 1.9 cm shorter than spring: the gap shows. The day 63 exit pair (Oct. 25 and Jan. 25: 10.8 and 10.0 cm) is closer, so print the exit strips large. Nov. 3 (10.4 cm) is shorter than Oct. 1 and still fall; Feb. 2 (10.2 cm) is winter and nearly as long as Nov. 3, so on day 62 children compare each group's strips, not one pair.
