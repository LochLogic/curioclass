# Day 62: Fall, winter and spring

## Teacher page

### Before class

Unit 7 ended on day 61 and hands nothing forward. With no March strip on the chart, add the table's strip for the day before and say "from the table". Set the seven strips and three labels for each group. Seal the exit cards. Choose six not heard on day 9.

### Safety and set-up

Seated paper work. Never look at the sun. Any child may pass.

### The day

#### 4 min. Open

Hand back the day 9 sheets. Read the chart's dates aloud. Ask again: "Is it light or dark at half past five in the evening, and will spring be like fall or like winter?" Children say what they notice.

#### 3 min. Retrieve the weather watch and grouping

Read the labels: fall is September to November, winter December to February, spring March. **Ask.** "Point to the dates that belong with winter." **Ask.** "Point to a square with no report." Everyone points at once. A miss: line one dated strip up with its square.

#### 4 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 9's exit, one letter: error D first. D: trace two strips with a finger from the sunrise picture to the sunset picture. Error C, if D took no slot: lay the report card beside its strip; children touch the card that shows the whole day. Otherwise trace two strips.

#### 12 min. Sort and notice

Groups of five sort the strips under fall, winter and spring, 5 minutes. Set Pip's three equal bars beside the real strips, 2 minutes. Pip: "All the seasons have the same daylight." **Ask.** "Touch the ones that show our records." Expect the strips; Pip's bars are drawings (error A). Last, 5 minutes: each group says what it notices about the light parts, longer or shorter, without sliding them, 50 seconds a group. November is shorter than October and still fall: compare groups, not one pair.

#### 5 min. Revise the model

Each child marks, in a second colour on their day 9 bars, the light part they think now; the first stays. Redraw the class model as three boxes: fall, winter, spring. Scribe one sentence: "I think ___, because I saw ___."

#### 4 min. Exit

Say, "Invented for practice." Everyone touches, 1 minute: **Ask.** [[s08-X62a]] "Touch the strips that are in the wrong group." The rotating six, 30 seconds each: **Ask.** [[s08-X62b]] "What do the light parts show about winter?" Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Expect: winter's is shorter than the other two. "They are all the same" is error A. Moving October is shown with help.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Count strips and labels back.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw each strip on a bar of one fixed length.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: draw three bars of one length, one with a clearly shorter white light part. No labels: write fall, winter and spring on scraps.

**Skip.** The groups and Pip's bars.

- **6 min. Look back.** Trace a strip's light part.
  **Say.** "Where is the daylight?" Expect: the light part between the sunrise and sunset pictures.
- **10 min. Sort.** Sort the dated strips under fall, winter and spring.
- **6 min. Notice.**
  **Say.** "Which light parts are shorter?" Expect: winter's.
- **5 min. Guess again.** Your child marks a second guess on the day 9 bars.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s08-X62a]] and [[s08-X62b]], asked aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: sort three strips a group. Never cut Pip's bars or the exit. Longer: sort a new strip.

### Words and supports

Fall, winter, spring, longer, shorter, daylight. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who still thinks every season is the same (error A). Day 63 opens with Pip and the report, then repairs error A with Pip's equal bars under the real strips, 4 minutes.
