# Pip's cards, the report card, tokens and labels

Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading except the dark part of a strip, no brand names, one big word under each picture. Pip the puppet speaks one line at a time and is wrong on purpose; no child is the one who made the mistake. Say aloud that Pip's cards are invented for practice.

## Pip's lines

- Day 9, colouring the dark part of the large strip: "This is the daylight." The class says which part is the light part.
- Day 62, beside the three equal bars: "All the seasons have the same daylight." Children check it against the real strips (error A).
- Day 63, holding the report token cut flat: "It was light at half past five, so the day was long." Children point to the card that tells the whole day (error C). Then: "Winter has more daylight than spring." Children point to the light parts that say otherwise (error B).

If no report came, the card is left blank with only its date. Reports come from the class itself or from any adult who chooses to give one. No family is asked.

## Day 9 weather strip (retrieval, invented for practice)

Three squares in a row: a sun, a cloud, a sun. Date boxes under each: Sept. 3, Sept. 4, Sept. 5. The teacher says it is invented for practice. A miss: match the two sun pictures together and set today's weather in the next square.

## Day 62: Pip's three equal bars

Three bars of one length, labelled fall, winter and spring, each with its light part from the same place to the same place (the sunrise picture a quarter of the way along, the sunset picture three quarters of the way along). They are drawings, not records.

## Light and dark picture tokens (day 9)

Five large paper tokens a group: a white card with a sun picture (light) and a black card with a moon picture (dark). Tokens go on the record the group picks. They stay out of mouths and are counted back at clean-up.
