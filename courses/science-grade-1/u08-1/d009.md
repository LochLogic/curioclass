# Day 9: Is it light or dark at half past five?

## Teacher page

### Before class

After day 8. Print a paper prediction sheet each; cut tokens flat. Draw the three record picture cards. Seal an exit card per group (within three days of September 18, use September 4 or 30, strips-key.md). Choose six to hear.

### Safety and set-up

Seated paper work. Never look at the sun. Tokens stay out of mouths.

### The day

#### 4 min. Open

Set the September 10 report card beside the large strip. Read aloud: "Is it light or dark at half past five in the evening, and will spring be like fall or like winter?" Children say what they notice; write it on the board. No report: leave the square blank.

#### 4 min. Predict

On two bars as long as the strip, each child colors yellow the part they think will be light in winter and in spring. Any may pass.

#### 3 min. Retrieve the weather watch

Show an invented weather strip: sun, cloud, sun. **Ask.** "Touch the weather that repeats." **Ask.** "Point to where today's goes." A miss: lay the suns together.

#### 9 min. Record one date

Class, 4 minutes. Trace the large strip's white part from the sunrise picture to the sunset picture; color it yellow. Set the sun token (the report, one moment) and the stripe (the whole day) in the square. Pip colors the dark part: "This is the daylight." **Ask.** "Show me the daylight." Expect the light part; Pip is wrong on purpose. Alone, 5 minutes: trace and color the small strip's light part; draw a sun and a yellow dash in the square.

#### 4 min. Choose what to record

Pairs, 1 minute: "What would we need to record next?" Each group lays a token on a record card, 1 minute. Read the plan: monthly, the weather chart row adds the strip and any report.

#### 4 min. Exit

Say, "Invented for practice." Everyone touches, 1 minute: **Ask.** [[s08-X9a]] "Touch the part of the strip that is the daylight." The rotating six, 30 seconds each: **Ask.** [[s08-X9b]] "What goes in the square besides the report picture?" Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Expect the light part, because the report is one moment. The dark part is error D; "the report tells how long it was light" is error C.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Count tokens back.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw strips on a bar of one fixed length.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed strip: draw a long bar, mark a half sun with an arrow up and another with an arrow down, and make between them white and the rest black. No tokens: draw a sun and a moon on scraps.

**Skip.** The weather strip and the tokens.

- **6 min. Look.** Look at the strip and the report card.
  **Say.** "Which part is the daylight?" Expect: the light part between the two sun pictures.
- **5 min. Guess.** Your child colors the winter and spring bars yellow for the part they think will be light.
- **9 min. Record.** Trace the light part, color it, and draw a small sun and a yellow dash in the date square.
  **Say.** "Does the report show the whole day?" Expect: no, one moment; the strip shows the whole day.
- **5 min. Pick.** Ask what you could record next.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s08-X9a]] and [[s08-X9b]], asked aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one weather Ask. Never cut the trace or exit. Longer: why two "light" reports show no equal days.

### Words and supports

Daylight, light part, dark part, sunrise, sunset. Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who takes the dark part for daylight (error D) or one report for the whole day (error C). Day 10 starts unit 2. The monthly look traces a strip first; day 62 repairs one letter.
