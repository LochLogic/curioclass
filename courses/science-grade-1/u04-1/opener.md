# Section 4.1 opener: why does the band go blurry, and why does the sound stop?

## What the section does

Four days, 28 to 31. The class meets a shoebox guitar of stretched rubber bands. The teacher plucks a band low and away from faces, then gently stills it with one finger. Children notice that the band goes blurry while it sounds, and that the sound changes when it is stilled. The compelling question is asked aloud and no cause is given: why does the band go blurry when the guitar plays, and why does the sound stop when we touch it? Day 28 starts a three-box strip, "I did, I saw, I heard", and a class sound chart. Day 29 plans a test with a partner (what we change, what we keep, what we watch) and adds the child's own throat, felt with a fingertip. Day 30 repeats the test on a paper-covered box drum, felt at the box rim. Day 31 tests whether stilling the band or the drum stills its sound and revises the stopping arrow on the chart. Section 4.2 begins on day 32 with the reverse question, which waits on the board until then. Nothing is scored in this section. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken, and every answer is a point, a drawing, a felt mark or a few words. Groups of five take turns at one guitar or drum while the others draw their own strip. The teacher keeps the class sound chart and the question board and hears a rotating six at each exit. Every exit card is invented for practice, and the teacher says so aloud. The recording beside each card is a real, dated recording made by an adult, and the card supplies no result. A child may always pass, and a partner may point or hold the strip.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Vibrate.** Plucking moves the band back and forth very fast. Children call this vibrating, and show it with fast hands. The blur is the band moving too fast and too little to see apart. Nobody counts shakes.
- **The sound comes from the band, not the hand.** The hand only starts it. Children's first ideas ("the hand makes it", "the box makes it") are written in their words and tested, not corrected.
- **Stilling is a test, not a rule.** Holding the band, drum or bell gently still is compared with leaving it free. A short fading tail after the hold is normal, so children compare "much quieter". An unclear pair is repeated once and both records are kept.
- **A source may move too little to see.** The paper top of the drum moves too little to see, so it is felt with a fingertip on the box rim, never on the paper top. A tap that is not felt is recorded "not felt" and repeated once.
- **The throat is a second case.** A fingertip on one's own throat during a quiet "mmm" can feel a shake. Humming is optional; a child may whisper or watch a partner's record. Nothing is marked on hearing.
- **Limits.** No waves, wavelength, speed, pitch rules, ear anatomy or hearing tests. Sounds stay at classroom level, never near ears, and loudness is never raised to force a result.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "It keeps making sound when it is held still." Repeat the band's free and gently held pair and say what was heard. It may first be volunteered on day 30, is met on day 31 and is repaired on day 32.

## Safety and set-up

Everyone near a band wears eye protection. An adult fits intact non-latex bands low and away from faces, and checks them for damage the day before and again before each use. Plucks are gentle, one pluck per turn. Guitars sit on the table or floor, low, never near a face. The teacher counts and collects the bands at clean-up, and children wipe and bag their own glasses. Day 29 adds one hand wipe each before the throat touch; the touch is light, optional and one's own. Drum taps use gentle fingertips only, no sticks, and no eye protection is needed on day 30. If the school has no eye protection, children run no band trials: the teacher plucks from a distance, children feel their throats, and day 30's drum needs none. If a gentle pluck shows no blur, pluck slightly more, keeping the guitar low, or show the dated rehearsal photograph and say aloud that it is a photograph. A shoebox, household bands and a paper-covered tub do everything.
