# Section 6.1 opener: can our message reach a friend?

## What the section does

Four days, 48 to 51. The class starts from a sound everyone knows, the recess bell, and asks how a friend across the playground can be told it is time to come in without shouting. On day 48 the class hears a signal from a near marker, marks what it thinks the signal said, and finds that hearing a sound and knowing its meaning are two different things. Day 49 shows three kinds of device (a cup pair with string, a flashlight with a cardboard flap, a box drum), and each child sketches one, with an arrow from a shape to its job, and makes it. Day 50 measures a near mark and a farther mark with identical paper strips laid end to end. Day 51 sends the same message from the same device at both marks and records what the friend marked. Section 6.2 repeats the trial, improves the design and checks each child's own device.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken, and every answer is a point, a mark in a box, a drawing or a few words the teacher writes. The teacher hears a rotating six each day and writes who on the record sheet. One group at a time uses the one closed lane; the others sort invented trial cards at their desks. Every exit case is invented for practice, and the teacher says so aloud. A child may always pass.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A signal is not a message.** The signal is the sound or light that goes across. The message is what the sender wants the friend to know. The friend can read the signal only if both have agreed what it means. That is why the friend marks a picture before the sender's card is shown.
- **Two practice messages.** Line up (two quick taps or blinks) and wait (one slow tap or one long blink). Each has a picture and one pattern. No pattern copies any real emergency signal.
- **A cup pair.** The shake from one cup travels along a gently straight string to the other cup (review of unit 4: a vibration makes sound). There is no wave account. A string lying slack does not carry the shake.
- **Measuring with strips.** Identical strips laid end to end, touching, with no gaps or overlaps. The count of strips is the length. Half strips are not read. Math teaches this later, so day 50 teaches it here.
- **A fair distance test.** Keep the message, the signal and the string tightness the same, change only the distance, and watch the friend's mark. Only the two marks tried are known.

## The errors, in plain words

- **Error A:** "The friend noticed the signal, so the message worked." Repair: set the sender's card beside the friend's mark. Met on day 48, repaired on day 49.
- **Error B:** "My sketch is my device." Repair: a made device sends, a plan does not. Met on day 49, repaired on day 50.
- **Error E:** "Strips with a gap still count." Repair: lay strips touching and recount. Met on day 50, repaired on day 51.
- **Errors C and D:** "It worked near, so it works anywhere" and "It failed far because it was far" when the signal also changed. Met on day 51, repaired on day 52.

## Safety and set-up

Rehearse the signals and the lane the day before. Tape down the marks in one closed lane with a walking route around the string. Light is aimed down at a low card, never at eyes, and no lasers. Sounds stay at classroom level and away from ears. Never tap or shout into a cup, and hold the receiving cup slightly away from the ear. Use cotton string, no wire or elastic, kept low and gently straight; no pulling contests. An adult pre-punches the cups and cuts the string and cardboard. Scissors are blunt, used seated and counted back in. The farther mark is within the string length less one arm span, and the near mark is about half of it. Adults collect string and lights at clean-up.
