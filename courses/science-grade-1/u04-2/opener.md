# Section 4.2 opener: can sound make something move too?

## What the section does

Three days, 32 to 34. Section 4.1 showed that shaking things make sound. Day 29's parked question comes off the board: can sound make something else shake? On day 32 the class plans one test with Pip: change the sound from off to on, keep the strip in the same place, touch nothing, and watch the strip. The strip hangs from a stand apart from the speaker, and a child who asks "could the table be moving it?" is asking the right question and is shown the separate stands. The teacher alone runs the speaker on children's requests of "off" and "on". The rice test uses the same plan, with a few grains on taut film over a bowl, sealed under a raised clear cover. Day 33 is the observed assessment: groups of five plan, test and record on unfamiliar cases, both directions, and revise a two-arrow account. Day 34 returns the written feedback, repairs by letter and checks a fresh case. Nothing is scored on days 32 and 34 except the revision of the day 33 work. The record sheet uses one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 to 35 minutes. Children read very little: every question is spoken, and every answer is a point, a drawing in two boxes or a few words. Day 32 is a class demonstration: each child's turn is the one request of "off" or "on". Every exit card is invented for practice, and the teacher says so aloud. The recording beside each card is a real, dated recording made by an adult, and the card supplies no result. If a classroom-volume sound does not move the strip, the named fallback is the adult-made dated motion sequence cards, said aloud to be recordings and marked indirect. A child may always pass.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The speaker shakes, and the shaking can pass to what is near it.** Children are not told this; they test it. A null result is a result and is kept.
- **Competing causes.** A light strip can flutter from moving air or from the table, not from sound. The test is the same strip with the sound off and then on, hung from a separate stand, with no touching and no breath. A hand pressing the strip is not a sound-only test.
- **Two arrows.** The class model now holds "it shakes, so it sounds" and, only if both cases support it, "it sounds, so it makes things shake". Each arrow stands on a record the child can point to.
- **Limits.** No waves, wavelength, speed, pitch or ear anatomy. A motionless-looking material may move too little or too fast to see. Sounds stay at classroom level, never near ears, never raised to force motion. Children never touch the strip or the speaker.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "It keeps making sound when it is held still." Repeat the free and gently held pair. Repaired on day 32 and again on day 34.
- **B:** "Sound can't move anything." Lay the fixed-paper sound-off and sound-on records side by side and point to which box shows motion.
- **C:** "I held the strip while it played and it moved, so sound did it." Repeat the no-touch comparison and name what stayed the same.

## Safety and set-up

The teacher alone handles the speaker, its cord and controls, keeps the speaker on a dry surface and secures cords. Sound is classroom level and never near ears. The teacher alone handles the rice and loose film, seals a few grains under a raised clear cover without touching them, and nothing is eaten; loose film is bagged and disposed of. With the grains contained, no eye protection is needed on day 32; on day 33 everyone wears eye protection because of the held-flat flick. Edges on cards and foil are smooth. Day 34 uses paper only. Clean-up: glasses wiped and bagged by the child, speaker off and film bagged by the teacher. With no speaker, use the motion sequence cards on days 32 to 34 and say they are recordings.
