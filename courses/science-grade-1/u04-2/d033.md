# Day 33: Plan, test and tell what sound did

## Teacher page

### Before class

At the teacher table set the drum, ruler, speaker, and the ribbon on a stand with separate supports. Hang the class sound chart and question board. Print the two-panel sheet. Open the sealed records only to check them. Seat six groups of five. Put the familiar sound cards at each group's table. Count the glasses.

### Safety and set-up

Everyone wears eye protection. The ruler is held flat on the table edge, free end under 10 cm, and you flick it. Drum taps are gentle fingertips. You alone work the speaker, on a dry table, at classroom level, never near ears. Any child may pass.

### The day

#### 3 min. Launch

Point to the chart from day 32. Say: "This is a check. I will not help. Recordings are called recordings. You may pass." Groups take their places.

#### 2 min. Picture plans

Each child marks a plan in the plan boxes of both panels: what they will ask to see.

#### 18 min. Six group turns

Groups come to the teacher table, three minutes each: 30 seconds to agree a plan; 45 seconds of shared comparisons, each child one drum tap or one request; five reasons, 15 seconds each; 30 seconds to move. Groups 1, 3 and 5 run the ruler beside the drum live and take the ribbon from the held-back records; groups 2, 4 and 6 the reverse. Ask the item stem aloud once: [[s04-E33a]] "A plastic ruler is flicked beside this paper drum and we hear a sound. What will you compare to find which part is sounding? Plan together, test and record." [[s04-E33b]] "This thin paper ribbon hangs from a stand beside a playing speaker on the same table, and the ribbon may be moving. What could explain that? Choose comparisons, test and revise your account." Children quickly mark what they saw, first and next, as each trial runs. Each reason covers both arrows. Give no help. Mark the record sheet and the heard column.

Waiting children sort the familiar cards under "it shakes, so it sounds" and "it is held still, so it is quiet" and draw one reason. This is unscored.

#### 4 min. Accounts

Each child checks their own paired observations and draws or dictates their two-arrow account in the my arrow boxes.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Glasses wiped and bagged (2 minutes). You collect the ruler, drum and speaker.

### Other rooms

No printer: rule scrap paper into two panels of four boxes. No speaker or ribbon: use the held-back records for that item and mark it indirect.

### At home

**Swap.** No speaker: a phone playing a steady low sound, away from ears. No ruler: a card strip. No drum: a paper-covered tub. A paper strip hangs from a chair back.

**Skip.** The group turn and the record sheet.

- **38 min. The assessment.** Your child says what to ask to see; do not help. An adult flicks the strip flat on a table edge, free end short. Compare the strip alone, the drum tapped gently alone, then equal flicks free and held. Hang a paper strip on a chair apart from the phone; compare sound off and on, no touching or blowing. Your child draws what happened and both arrows.
  **Say.** "What did you see, and what does it show?" Expect: an answer from the drawings.
- **5 min. Look back.** Praise one thing. Correct nothing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: none; keep every part of the check. Longer: none.

### Words and supports

Plan, ask to see, free, held, sound off, sound on, arrow, recording. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing, a scribe and home-language answers count.

### Tomorrow

Day 33 closes the check; scoring comes after class. Day 34 hands back each child's picture comment to revise, repairs error A, B and C by group from days 32 and 33, and tries a fresh sound-and-motion case.

### Credit

Ours, all of it.
