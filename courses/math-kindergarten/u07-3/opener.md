# Section 7.3

Review flat, solid and cube; introduce sphere, cylinder, cone and face on day 127. A face means a flat surface here. Paper is a thin object, while a drawing can represent a flat form or a solid. A hollow model may represent a solid shape. Cube faces are squares; the whole cube extends beyond a face. A cylinder has circular flat ends and a curved side; a cone a circular flat base and point; a sphere no flat face. Informal part language is enough, no required edge totals, nets or volume. Review all six position words with gestures and a fixed front view before checking them. Whole cylinder construction is reserved; practice repairs oblong box frames. Optional tactile paper models stay available because geometry features are the content.

Read every word aloud; children do not read independently. Use 18 point print, high contrast, large stable pieces and tactile models. Accept usual communication, home-language reasoning and directed adult movement. Scribe labels when needed; note access separately from mathematical help. A child may pass in public talk. Patterns supplement color. Every scene and quantity is invented for practice; say this aloud.

Review the established routines Counting centers, Partner turns, Paper count-dot record and Return and repair from unit 1, rather than assuming planned entries were taught. Demonstrate with fresh partial records. Review count and numeral from units 1/2, composition and flat names from unit 3, equation/plus/minus/equals from unit 4, part/whole/split/partner of ten from unit 5, and Oral tens to one hundred from unit 6. Keep the ledger wording. If earlier teaching is missing, demonstrate it before use; never call a planned record evidence.

Before class, print only today's practice and sealed reserved master. Open teacher-key.md privately; never display its answers or print them for children. Reserved pictures appear only when their named question is administered, including any later unheard-child turn. Put all other copies away. Teaching, centers, repairs and home practice use supplied partial corrections, never independent reserved formats. Draw masters by hand when printing is unavailable; same mathematical layout and blank response room. One shared screen may enlarge paper, with no queue.

Use large adult-cut paper shapes, drawn outlines and frames, paper squares instead of counters, clean plain boxes/tubes and paper balls/cones instead of kits. Soft paper joins and rolled paper replace sticks/clay. Inspect for staples, residue and sharp edges; no glass, metal cans, small loose joins, mouthing or throwing. Keep builds low, spheres loose on a mat. Paper is default so no clay handwashing queue. Adult prepares broad ready-to-use joins before class. Children attach them or direct access help within the paid turn, with no adult queue.

The day is sixty minutes as its data budget states. Counting is simultaneous, five minutes; games use four familiar mixed asks with immediate feedback when five center minutes remain, untimed. On days 120 to 126 the funded position roster replaces this optional game when there is no spare time. Fifteen pairs alternate two 45-second turns simultaneously, 1.5 minutes. Hear at most two optional teaching contributions. Exit tickets are half sheets, at most two paper items; an extra judged paper item is a student-page Exit box. Six 45-second samples take 4.5 minutes, one ask per child. Paper marks never establish an unheard name or explanation.

Use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Pending describes scheduling, not another score. Return written feedback to the named receiving part, revise on a later day, then judge earlier work. More than ten sharing a letter triggers its five-minute repair including fresh partial retry. Two actions maximum in ten minutes, one in five. Further needs stay recorded; day 134 does not reteach. For one child, count 3, demonstrate 5, repair 5, paper/building 9, exit 8; split evidence into another sitting if needed.

## Common errors

A: pose mistaken for dimension, feel a thick stack beside a single circle. M: paper mistaken for represented solid, keep model beside picture. B: whole cube called square, trace one face while retaining the whole. F: cone called triangle, inspect its round base. C: name rejected after a turn, turn incrementally. D: color replaces shape features, correct a supplied feature group. O: flat ends mistaken for a flat whole, feel beyond one face. More than ten of thirty is many. Use only the named five-minute repair allocation; extra needs remain on the next-component roster.
