# Images, voices and narrators

Review third-person narration. The teller uses names or third-person pronouns. The teller may reveal some thoughts. Perspective is a way of seeing or thinking. Mark the exact words or actions that support a claim. Compare wording and the speaker's situation without ranking speakers or asking anyone to imitate a variety.

Keep the B1, B2 and C copies separate. Offer independent print first where reading is being checked. Cue the matching image and voice only when the item asks for them. Keep the captions exact and the visual descriptions neutral. A live performance can supply the featured pauses, stress and rhythm. Teach from released text while an unavailable check remains pending.

On 141 keep oral turns private and out of earshot. Put five pupils at each of six stations. Use one four-minute turn per child for purpose, two short readings, meaning, a reading check and changeover. Match each to the actual successive prose record. Keep first and corrected evidence with written cues. Use pending status for missing samples and incomplete range evidence.

Print the media effects sheet, quotation sheet, discussion card and scene notes. Keep the three-form practice card separate from the reserved lines and packets. Give written cues in the response slots and collect independent corrections on the dates in the record.
