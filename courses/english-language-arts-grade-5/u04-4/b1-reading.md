# Two Readings

The morning after the storm, Leena carried her notebook to the vegetable patch. Her family had rationed the garden tank's water all summer, measuring each bucketful. Another watering would take almost everything left in reserve for the dry weather. Her aunt had asked her to check the rain gauge before they drew any more.

There was nothing in its measuring tube, although Leena remembered rain rattling the porch roof. She recorded a zero anyway, leaving a question mark beside it in the margin.

Beside the gauge stood a straight-sided pail that Leena had left empty the previous evening. A sheet of water inside it reflected the palest stripe of the morning sky. She set a ruler against the bottom and crouched until the surface met her eye.

“Two inches,” she said. She lifted the ruler, wiped it, and lowered it again.

“Two inches again,” she said. Leena wrote, “The water in the pail was two inches deep.”

Since the containers disagreed, Leena examined the empty tube before taking it off its post. Near the base, a diagonal line caught the light whenever she turned the tube. Leena held it over a shallow tray and poured in a little water. Drops gathered along the line she had mistaken for a scratch on the surface. The tube was cracked.

She drew a single stroke through the zero so she could still read it. Beside it, she sketched both containers and traced the crack with a slanting pencil line. The question mark remained as she carried her notebook back to the house.

Her aunt reached for the tank key, and Leena opened the notebook between them. They tested the soil together before deciding to leave the tank closed for another day.
