# Investigating usable water

Begin with what each whole counts. Use one grid for all Earth's water and a different grid for freshwater. Read the column labels before shading. A reservoir is a place that stores water. Review freshwater from grade 3 and separate storage, access and safety in the released examples. Treat a grid as an approximation.

Review a stanza as a group of lines in a poem. A stage direction is a note for the performer in brackets, read as a direction. Review evidence as the detail that supports a statement. Use a brief label to show where a detail came from. An account is a telling of what happened or, here, what one named speaker emphasized. The four accounts describe the same drought from different responsibilities. Read the nation's account first, in its own historical context. Keep every speaker's role and reported action tied to its date.

Attach one detail to the point it actually supports. Compare details only after locating each one. When an answer follows the wrong column or point, go back to its heading or source label. Return written cues privately. Use released science prose as a private alternate. Keep talk about the readings and offer a pass.

Print the bilingual family note on day 124. Prepare the retrieval card, grids, source chart, paper or searchable excerpts, and the device-turn log. Keep all exit material and the sealed day 129 argument with the teacher. Use the passage release record for print, display, audio and home copies.
