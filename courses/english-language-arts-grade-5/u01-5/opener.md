# An informative literary essay

## What to notice

An observation is something noticed. A focus names the point the essay will explain. A category gathers related details. Ask what each quotation shows, then whether it belongs with the other evidence in its group. Compare two responses to the same event. Make each link express its actual relationship. The conclusion follows from the evidence the essay has already explained.

Order three released events and recall one sensory detail. Use the chapter map, then show essay components one at a time. Keep each exact quotation and source tag together. Read the opening as someone who missed the story. The reader should know whose responses matter and what makes them different.

## Support and feedback

If one voice disappears, identify what each character noticed in a fresh event. For a claim without support, locate an independent detail and explain its connection. For a vague word, ask what the reader can now identify. Use the next Return for one priority and parallel cards for other needs.

Practice one convention feature on a separate line. Keep the author’s own drafts private and unscored before revision. Discuss loss, relocation and work through the fictional family. A student may pass on sharing. Keep pending typing turns for unit 2 and the complete work on paper.

## Lay out

Chapter map, movable source slips, essay components, editing strip, dated folder pages, typing log and sealed exit passages. Keep the generated keys with the teacher.
