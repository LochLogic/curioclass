# Rights and the first chapters

## What the section does

Open with dignity, each person's worth. Students read the start of the declaration beside its explainer and follow the first four chapters of the novel. They keep a chapter map and use evidence to test an idea about a character. Review evidence as the line or page that shows an idea is true. Review infer as working out an unstated idea using text details.

## How the days run

Each day takes 90 minutes. Keep student print directions to two sides, 14 point, and 350 words. Print the readings separately. Give new required reading in class. In talk, fifteen pairs take two 45-second turns in parallel. The teacher staggers six turns across 4.5 minutes and dates what was actually heard. Start the next round with students still pending.

Send the family note on day 1. Use grade 4 portfolios to form initial reading groups. If a portfolio is missing, provide the same released reading leaves and start from observed needs. Give the private fall screen to five readers on each of days 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 and 10. Use day 11 for absences.

## What to notice

- A quotation is a source word sequence copied exactly inside paired marks. Put its line or page beside the response.
- Students work through each syllable before using meaning to confirm a word. Accept dialect differences.
- Group details by their main idea. A count of article paragraphs tells how the article is organized.

## Materials and access

Keep a role card per pair, a chapter map and logs per student, and a dated teacher record. Use a paper copy of each released glossary entry when the local digital version is unavailable. Cover reserved passages on class, accessible and home copies. Preview a father's death, the loss of housing and a move for work. Offer a private pass and invite a return to the page. Keep discussion on the characters.

## Tomorrow's priority

A changed quotation gets a comparison with a different exact source line. An unsupported inference gets a new detail to test. For merged ideas, sort fresh details under two headings. Give one specific cue for each response.
