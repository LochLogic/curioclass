# Theme and the whole story

## What the section does

Days 19 to 22 compare choices, use feedback, and connect the novel's opening and ending. Finish the chapter map on released events. Keep first drafts, written feedback and revisions dated. Review a summary as a brief account of important events without personal opinion.

## What to notice

A precise word identifies the intended subject or feature. A comparison needs details about both events and a reason for putting them together. A revision can change what a reader understands. A transition links ideas. An object can recur in a story as its meaning or use changes.

## Materials and access

Use chapter tabs, a draft and feedback page, and separate covered reading leaves. Keep the cold annex and separate language lines closed through day 21. Use a pencil map when a display is unavailable. Give directions aloud, provide equivalent accessible print, and date any assistance that changes independence.

Preview grief, housing loss, and hard farm work. Keep discussion about the story and allow a private pass. Hear six staggered 45-second turns during ordinary work. Day 22 uses an independent check, five minutes of key feedback and ten minutes for a fresh matched response. Return written comments on day 23.
