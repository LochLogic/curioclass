# Presenting and responding

Review the rhetorical-situation: reader, purpose, form. An explanation needs an ordered finding, facts from named sources and a visible feature that helps the audience understand. Keep each location and date with its source. Practice on released leaflet material before issuing the separate exit stimuli. Review the discussion moves chart: mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer.

Review partner rereading for meaning and expression. The audience sets the speech pace. Give listeners time to follow the idea. A pupil may pass in public talk and give the same evidence privately. Keep recordings restricted and tell pupils who can hear them. Offer a released 06 passage privately for pupils concerned by the drought and supply accounts. Keep family access private and keep current allocation disputes outside the discussion.

Print the two-audience planning sheet, recording roster, pending-turn log and the half slips. Arrange six local recording stations if available. Keep the diagram and listening materials in reserve until their exits. Use a released reading and note a pending exit if the held copy cannot be issued. Use dated park evidence when the local packet is unavailable. Staff or peers from another class can serve as new listeners. If only classmates are available, give them contrasting listener roles for practice and leave outside-audience evidence pending.

For A or V, trace the detail to its point and connect facts into a finding. For C, rehearse an audible phrase. For D, define a needed term for a visitor. For AP, connect the visual feature to the main idea. For AN, trace missing work to its dated folder. Verify the corrected evidence once in the unit record before day 156. Return private cues during its Response, then use separate released work for practice.
