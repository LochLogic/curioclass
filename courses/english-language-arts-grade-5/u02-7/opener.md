# Drafting a canopy narrative

## From a released arc to a private draft

Review the narrative arc with three released story events. Check where one event gives a reader a reason for the next. Review first-person narration: a teller uses I as a participant. Review dialogue as words spoken by characters. The orientation tells a new reader who is there and what is happening. The middle makes a response visible. The close follows a choice or event already in the story.

## Before class

Print the three-box arc, draft sheet and sealed exit leaves. Pull checked federal selections and released passages. Keep the held parts of the scientist account and stories away from models and word cards. Use selected writer notes for scene and summary on day 68. Read the whole canopy model after the day 68 exit has been collected. If the model is unavailable, compare scene and summary in released passages. Use the filed federal selections if the web is unavailable.

## Feedback and evidence

Use each pupil’s dated plan and source tags. A researched fact needs a title and location. Identify invented thoughts, dialogue and events as fiction. Use a different outline to find missing information or an irrelevant event, then let pupils draft their own openings, turning points and closes privately. Collect the dated day 72 draft as diagnosis and give one written priority. Continue private day 66 reading turns and dated corrections where scheduled. Offer a different reading when needed. All observation work stays in the classroom.
