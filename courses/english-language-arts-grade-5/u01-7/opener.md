# Rewrite, rehearse and write source notes

## What to notice

A critique gives a specific change a writer can try. An approach is a way to do the work. Keep the dated versions so the writer can explain what changed for the listener. A program note gives a listener enough setting to follow a performance. Keep source tags next to quotations and paraphrases. Model each component on its own.

Fluency includes accurate words, meaningful phrases and expression that fits the meaning. Cue one phrase boundary before a second reading. For the familiar teaching poem, locate a first choice and later reflection, then compare candidate theme lines. Prepare for discussion with a marked line. Give each person a turn and build on a specific reply.

## Support and feedback

For a recopy, identify one thing the listener still needs. For unclear phrasing, hear one fresh span and cue a meaningful break. Without audio, finish every private exit observation during day 37 Return and Partner rereading. Keep the purpose slips separate from the oral judgment. Use a quiet alternative for public passes.

Keep all independent reading leaves and the glossary entry covered until their release. Use the filed grade 4 poem about the pull of rest and duty if the teaching verse is unavailable. When an independent leaf is unavailable, mark its component pending and complete the other work.

## Lay out

Critique choice, rehearsal slip, private turn log, program components, source tags, covered reading leaves and accessible reference entry.
