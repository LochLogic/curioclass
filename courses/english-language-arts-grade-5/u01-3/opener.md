# Comparing responses

## What the section does

Days 13 to 18 compare characters and settings, explain figurative language, and trace themes across actions. Use earlier source tags and extend the chapter map. Review event as what happens in a story. Review response as what a character says or does about it.

## What to notice

Keep the same comparison question across both columns. Each detail should answer it. Review metaphor as a comparison that describes one thing as another. Review simile as a comparison using like or as. Use nearby words and actions to test a literal reading. Track when a verb puts an action or state. A timeline makes a shift visible.

## Materials and access

Use the two-column observation chart, picture match, hall route and labeled diagrams alongside released leaves. Draw a route from the manuscript if no image is available. Use the verb timeline and separate blank dictation lines. Keep the private word probe out of group work. Record reading and spelling separately. Reserve the designated story leaves and use a neutral bridge.

Preview loss, a move, illness in the heat, and missing work hours. Offer a quiet pass and a return to the page. Begin corrections on day 13 from written feedback before modeling a separate case. Take one turn per child. With fifteen pairs in parallel, stagger six 45-second turns for teacher observation.
