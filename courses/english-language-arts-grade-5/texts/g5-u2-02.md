# El Yunque Forest Service Selections

The passages in quotation boxes are from the Forest Service. The notes beside them explain the settings and dates.

## Welcome to El Yunque

The opening of the Forest Service's visitor page gives a quick way to locate the forest and see why it is unusual.

> Located in Puerto Rico's Northeast Region, El Yunque National Forest is the only tropical rainforest within the national forest system.

The forest extends into several municipalities, or areas with their own local governments. The page names Río Grande, Luquillo and six others. It also points readers to forest maps.

## Forest's Response to Hurricanes

Hurricane Maria damaged forests in Puerto Rico in 2017. Researchers used measurements made from the air from 2016 through 2020 to compare treetops before and after the storm.

> This study was conducted in collaboration with NASA scientists and revealed widespread loss of canopy height across the study area.

Damage varied from place to place. Openings in the canopy let light reach understory plants that had grown in shade. Changes in the cover also affected humidity. The researchers had measurements from the ground and from above, so they could study changes in the canopy and the small areas below it. Later, some surviving crowns spread sideways and vegetation grew upward. Both kinds of growth helped fill openings.

## Biennial Monitoring Evaluation Report for El Yunque National Forest

The Forest Service published this report in January 2023. It looked back at work from fiscal years 2020 through 2022. The following observations refer to the period the report covers.

### Puerto Rican parrot, page 17

> Through reintroduction efforts the population in 2021 was 17 birds

In this part of the report, the population means wild Puerto Rican parrots in El Yunque. The report states that the forest's wild population had fallen to zero by 2019. Reintroduction means putting animals back in a place where they once lived. The report describes further reintroduction and habitat restoration as work to continue.

### Coquí frogs, page 18

> Coquí surveys on El Yunque have been conducted since 2019, and only provide limited information.

The report looks at four kinds of coquí frogs, including ground coquí and tree-hole coquí. The survey results pointed to a decline in ground coquí and a possible decline in tree-hole coquí. The authors wanted more intensive surveys over a longer period to see which patterns held up.
