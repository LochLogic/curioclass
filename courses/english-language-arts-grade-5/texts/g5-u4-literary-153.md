# Before the Rain Reached It

## Where I start

For years I began this story with the first drop striking Grandma's address book. In that version the storm arrived without warning, just as a visitor might arrive unannounced.

Now I remember the clouds' dark underside and the explanation I gave my cousin. I called it the ridge's shadow and continued sorting Grandma's letters on the outdoor table. My cousin offered me an empty box with a lid, and I waved it away.

The leaves turned their pale undersides up just as a gust lifted Grandma's earliest letter. It caught on my shoe, and I pushed it straight into the box.

Together we carried the box inside, with the unsealed envelopes slipping sideways against its walls. After the storm, water beaded inside the address book's cover.

When I tell it now, I start with the box my cousin brought. It was there before the first drop.

## The dry shelf

Tess carried the seed bags to the porch steps, where afternoon sun reached the gravel. A paper tag named the family that contributed each batch of seeds for spring planting. She arranged the bags by name on a cloth spread over a crate.

Rain drew a dark stripe across the gravel at the far edge of the garden. Tess extended her palm beyond the shallow porch roof, and a drop struck her wrist. The stripe widened toward the crate.

Tess gathered the cloth at its corners, pulling the bags together against her chest. She carried them beneath the porch roof and placed them on a dry shelf. A paper tag dangled from one bag, its string worn to a single thread. She retied it before arranging the bags in the same order on the shelf.

Rain struck the empty crate as Tess added the last family name to her list.

## The two rings

All August, the jar wore a ring  
where last week's water ended.  
I left the ring there.  
Now rain lifts a new line  
above it.  
Both marks belong to this afternoon.  

## Room on the tray

(Small pots line an outdoor bench. The first drops spot the boards.)

**Rey:** Will you help me move the seedlings under the porch roof?

**Sol:** I'll bring the empty tray.

**Rey:** Leave space between the pots. The new stems bend when they touch.

(They lift the tray together.)
