# The Negro Speaks of Rivers

I’VE known rivers:  
I’ve known rivers ancient as the world and older than the flow of human blood in human veins.

My soul has grown deep like the rivers.

I bathed in the Euphrates when dawns were young.  
I built my hut near the Congo and it lulled me to sleep.  
I looked upon the Nile and raised the pyramids above it.  
I heard the singing of the Mississippi when Abe Lincoln went down to New Orleans, and I’ve seen its muddy bosom turn all golden in the sunset.

I’ve known rivers;  
Ancient, dusky rivers.

My soul has grown deep like the rivers.

## Teacher edition note

The reading has five groups separated by blank space. Read all ten verse lines. First read the opening and first refrain (lines 1 to 3), then the four river memories (lines 4 to 7), then the return to rivers and the repeated soul line (lines 8 to 10). Long lines wrapped in the magazine's narrow column have been joined. The semicolon in line 8 follows the June 1921 printing.

The Euphrates is in western Asia, and the Congo is in central Africa. The Nile and the pyramids recall ancient Egypt. The Mississippi and Lincoln turn the poem toward United States history and the era of slavery. Hughes gives the speaker memories that span many generations. The historical title uses a name for Black people that appeared in print in 1921. Use Black or African American in present-day discussion as appropriate.
