# Parts of a Literary Essay

## Observation and focus

A sign-up form can tell you what a place values.
In “Broccoli,” Ed and Maren both want people to claim what they leave.
Their ideas about the form show different ways to keep track.

## One evidence group

### Ed's concern

Ed recalls a group leaving supplies in the hall. “No one on the hall committee could reach them,” he says.
That experience explains why he looks for a pickup date on Maren's form.

## A second evidence group

### Maren's change

Maren replaces the address line with “how we can reach you.”
Her version asks for contact information a family can give even if it moves.

## A link between groups

Both want people to come back.
Ed follows the address rule, while Maren asks for a way to reach each owner.

## Closing fragment

The change keeps the need to reach people at the center.
It leaves room for people who may move.

## Use notes

Show one component or evidence group at a time using the released “Broccoli” form scene. On day 24, look at what each component does and whether headings help an unfamiliar reader. Use plain margin labels by day 27.
