# A Penny of Light

The thirsty culvert wears a collar of dust.  
The bank holds its breath.  
Grandpa says the bank keeps money safe.  
Here, roots cradle a penny of light.  

A grate lies across the mouth of the ditch.  
Through iron squares I see the leaves below.  
I listen for a splash.

## Teacher copy

Release this poem, picture, and sound passage first on day 141. Show the image panels in order, silently. The poem stands on its own for two unrehearsed oral readings.

**Recording score and caption.** Use this separate passage for 141a. Give each printed line three beats. Stress “cracked” and lift the pitch there. Keep the brief phrases distinct and let the voice rise again on “see.” Give the caption in the same line breaks when needed.

Water here.  
Empty tube.  
That tube cracked!  
One drop fell.  
One more fell.  
Now I see!  

**Key evidence.** 141a: The separate views distinguish the intact pail from the cracked tube. The lighter sky suggests hope. Alternating short and long marks give the image a repeating pattern. Stress on “cracked” points to the cause of the discrepancy. Rising pitch signals discovery. The repeated three-beat phrases balance the ending. 141b: The collar of dust shows that the thirsty culvert is dry. The bank holding its breath suggests a still river edge and suspense. 141c: “Through iron squares I see the leaves below” identifies a grate as a metal cover with openings. The third line also uses “bank” as a place that keeps money. For 141d and e, have students set a purpose and read the poem twice, then explain what they understood. For 141f, use a word the student has just read or repaired. Ask for print evidence before context confirmation.
