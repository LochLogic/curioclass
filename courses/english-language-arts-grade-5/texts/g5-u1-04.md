## Reading notes

Scene 1 uses dialogue and action to leave Kira with a question. Scene 2 gives Kira a first-person monologue. She slows down when the crate catches and carries the question to a specific next action. Read both scenes on day 29. Notice present perfect, past perfect and future perfect in the dialogue and monologue. The script returns to the events of chapter 14 from Kira and Amos's side of the shed.

Later, students choose another moment from the novel for their own monologues. Reread the two scenes for setting, scene order, pacing and conclusions. Use the work setting and characters for discussion.

### Cast and setting

- **Kira**, a farmworker on the late crew at Laurel Farm.
- **Amos**, a worker who moves crates and helps drivers check in.

The action takes place on the same day as the first trial of the new time sheet in *The Next Harvest*. Both scenes take place near the packing shed.

### Scene 1. At the wash table, late afternoon

*[A folded towel, two empty crates and a worn clipboard. A truck gives one short beep outside. Kira turns toward it.]*

**AMOS:** The driver will have left by five. I can bring the last crates down while there's still room.

**KIRA:** Wait. When did we start on these?

**AMOS:** We had already cleared the drain when the truck came.

**KIRA:** I have written that much. I haven't written the time.

*[She takes the clipboard down. Her finger stops at the new box.]*

**AMOS:** The truck has a receipt. You could ask the driver.

**KIRA:** I've asked him before. Last time he heard "crate" and brought me an empty one.

*[Amos lifts a crate. It scrapes the floor. Kira retrieves the towel beneath it.]*

**AMOS:** Ask while he can still hear you over the engine.

*[Kira folds the towel over the clipboard. She takes two steps toward the truck, then stops.]*

### Scene 2. Beside the pickup line, before five

*[Kira stands alone beside a painted line. The clipboard rests on a crate. She looks toward the waiting truck.]*

**KIRA:** I'm Kira, from the late crew. This is the first week we've had a box for time between jobs.

This afternoon we put the last carrots on the wash table. A little later, Amos pulled the first stack of empty crates down. While I cleared the drain, he carried them back past me. I heard each one drop on the stack. One. Two. The third caught on the edge. I waited for it to drop, with both hands in cold water. Then came the beep from outside.

When I looked up, the clock behind the basket made one skinny shadow. I had read the time from there before. I couldn't see it today. I dried my thumb, but the end of the towel was wet, too.

"What time did the truck get here?" That's what I practiced saying. But that would only tell me the time I looked up. I want to know when we started. Amos knows where he was when I was clearing the drain. The driver knows when somebody took the first crates.

*[She takes the towel off the clipboard and lays it flat.]*

Before the driver leaves, I'll ask him to show me the first time on his receipt. Then I'll ask Amos to stand with me at the board. By tomorrow we will have put the clock where the next crew can see it. Today we still have to put our two pieces of time together.

*[She lifts the clipboard, leaving the towel to dry, and walks toward the truck.]*
