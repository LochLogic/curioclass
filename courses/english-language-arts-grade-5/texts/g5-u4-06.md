# Where Earth's Water Is and a Shared River During Drought

## Where Earth's water is

**U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School, November 13, 2019**

> Of total freshwater, over 68 percent is locked up in ice and glaciers. Another 30 percent of freshwater is in the ground.

### One estimate of global water distribution

| Water source | Percent of freshwater | Percent of total water |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Oceans, Seas, & Bays | | 96.54 |
| Ice caps, Glaciers, & Permanent Snow | 68.7 | 1.74 |
| Groundwater, fresh | 30.1 | 0.76 |
| Groundwater, saline | | 0.93 |

### Table continued

| Water source | Percent of freshwater | Percent of total water |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lakes, fresh | 0.26 | 0.007 |
| Lakes, saline | | 0.006 |
| Rivers | 0.006 | 0.0002 |

Read each column with its heading. The first number column starts with freshwater alone. The other starts with all Earth's water. The selected rows omit some places water is stored. The estimates are rounded. *Saline* means salty. Freshwater has little salt, and may need treatment before it is usable for drinking. The distribution table counts water by where it is stored. Use it as evidence about how much is in each place.

## A shared river during drought

**Tanya Trujillo, U.S. Department of the Interior, October 15, 2021**

> Lake Powell and Lake Mead – the two largest reservoirs in the United States – are currently at historically low levels.

Trujillo told Congress in 2021 that long drought and low runoff reduced the amount of water held behind the dams. A reservoir stores water. She explained that agencies planned to release less water from Glen Canyon and Hoover dams in 2022, and that Mexico would receive less under a separate agreement. Those were reports and plans from 2021.

The Colorado River is a shared resource. The Bureau of Reclamation lists seven states in the United States that depend on it: Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah and Wyoming. Two states in Mexico and many tribal nations and communities also depend on it.

A watershed is land that drains toward the same place. Water moving across land can carry material with it. Stormwater can move from a school drive or parking lot into a storm drain or waterway. Finding out where a local drain leads helps explain why protecting that drain can protect water farther away.

## Teacher locator

For day 124, introduce the first four table rows beside the hundredths model. Hold back “Table continued” until students need an unseen detail. Use the displayed numbers as estimates. Set the hundredths model beside the table so very small amounts still have a source value when they occupy less than a cell. The passage on shared water is 2021 testimony with a separate federal list of basin users.
