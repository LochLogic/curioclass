# A Research Route

*Invented for practice.*

## B1. Preparing the route

Before a guided walk, a team sorted its blank recording sheets by route stop. Next, it assigned one observer to read each question and another to write the answer. Finally, the guide checked that each pair had the correct sheets. This sequence describes preparing one walk, not repeated observations across seasons.

## B2. Two recording methods

Both paper notes and audio notes can preserve an observer's words. Paper notes can be scanned without replaying sound; audio notes preserve the speaker's phrasing. During this walk, a noisy stream masked part of an audio note, so the listeners could not distinguish two spoken words. The noise affected what the recording captured; it did not change the words originally spoken. The team compared the methods and explained why this recording was unclear.

## B3. Protecting observations

A gust lifted loose recording sheets from a clipboard. An observer caught them before they left the shelter. The team fastened the sheets under the clip and put completed sheets into a closed folder. These steps addressed the risk of losing notes; they did not prove that every record was accurate. The guide still checked the written entries against the original observations.
