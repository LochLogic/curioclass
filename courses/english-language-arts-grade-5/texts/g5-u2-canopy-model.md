# A Pause in the Canopy

*Fiction set in El Yunque. The characters, photographs and events are invented.*

## Story

From a roofed platform in El Yunque, I could see the top of one tree close by. My aunt Elena, a researcher, had invited me to help her look through photographs. I had marked our rainforest in northeastern Puerto Rico on my class map. Now I wanted one picture to show how it felt to be there.

We spent the morning sorting prints at the platform table. The last print showed a dark part of the forest. I nearly slid it under the others. Then a spot of light, no wider than a coin, landed on my sleeve.

“Where did that come from?” I asked.

Elena tipped her chin. Two crowns left a notch above the platform. I held my arm still. The edge of the spot went soft, sharpened and slipped onto my wrist. For a moment it touched the blue thread that had come loose from my cuff. Farther down, a patch of leaves was bright under that same opening.

“Try a wider frame,” Elena said.

I lifted the camera. At first the bright patch filled the screen. I shifted the frame until the opening was in it as well. By noon we had sorted the prints and packed them in order. I put my new picture on top. The reader of my class page would be able to see where the brightness began.

## From the writer's notebook

I gave the audience a quick orientation by naming the place and the teller's job. One transition, “Then,” carries the reader from the morning summary into a single observation. Dialogue and the moving spot slow the pacing. The conclusion returns to the first goal of finding a picture for the class.

I put the prints in sequence with one clause about noon. A short summary can accelerate the telling. I used a pause for the moment when the light touches a loose thread. I could revise my frame after a peer critique, although I would keep the opening beside the bright patch.
