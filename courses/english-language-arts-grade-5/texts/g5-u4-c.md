# Signs Along the Way

## The upper path

Before the storm, my brother and I loaded the handcart with sacks of flour. Our delivery would get two neighboring families through the week while the mill remained closed. We planned to take the familiar shortcut across the low courtyard.

“The crossing will stay dry,” I assured him. I figured we could deliver everything before dark.

He pointed to the chalk line we had drawn beside the puddle after lunch.

“Let's check,” he said.

From the dry steps, I marked the edge of the water with a second line. The distance between the marks was already greater than the width of my hand. As we watched, water slipped over my fresh line and erased its thinnest end.

I had estimated from the size of the courtyard, assuming the puddle would spread slowly. I should have compared the old line with the new one. The crossing I'd promised was already disappearing.

We unloaded the cart and carried each sack separately along the steep upper path. At the second house, our neighbor pinched the corner of the sack to test it. A dusting of dry flour stayed on her fingers.

## Where the sound stopped

Nico arranged six empty cans beside the garden spout, each labeled with a row number. The first bed held the only seedlings that had survived the long spell without rain.

Nico opened the tap, and a short hiss came from its mouth. He put his ear near the pipe at the upper wall where the water entered. A low gurgle shook the metal. At the lower wall he leaned in again. The pipe there made no sound.

His grandfather came down the gravel path, carrying a spade across his shoulder.

“It's loud up there,” Nico said. “Down here, nothing.”

His grandfather marked the silent stretch with a rag and went for a wrench. Nico shut the tap and carried two cans past the unplanted beds to the reserve. He measured a scoop for each can and lowered the cover over the remaining water. Back at the first bed, he poured slowly beneath the leaves.

By dusk his grandfather had removed a clump of packed leaves from the pipe. The spout ran again, and Nico returned the numbered cans to their places beside it.

## A note from the river

O river, keep your counsel.  
Fold the hillside in your dark.  
Carry every fallen branch  
past the place where we turn home.  

When the roofs fall quiet,  
leave us one clear note  
to tell the stones apart.  

## Two columns

(At a public meeting, an official has offered to send a survey crew.)

**Mara:** Please submit the measurements.

**Official:** Shall I include both banks?

**Mara:** Yes. The committee needs the heights on both sides before it votes.

(The official adds a second column to the form.)
