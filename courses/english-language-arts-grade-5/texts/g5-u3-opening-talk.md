# An Opening Is Not the Whole Change

For years, big league clubs kept Black players off their teams. Jackie Robinson was already playing professional baseball when the Brooklyn club offered him a contract.

First, a club can change who gets a chance when people challenge an unfair practice. Reporter Wendell Smith helped bring Robinson’s play to the attention of Brooklyn executive Branch Rickey. Rickey signed Robinson to play for Brooklyn’s minor league team in Montreal. That contract gave Robinson a chance that earlier club decisions had denied Black players.

Second, changing who gets to play takes work from many people over time. A report could help a club decide whom to watch. In Montreal, Robinson led his league in batting over a full season. That record gave Brooklyn evidence of how he performed day after day. He took the field for Brooklyn the following spring. Later that season, a different club in Cleveland signed Larry Doby. His chance depended on a decision by that club.

Those actions did different jobs. Reporting drew attention to a player. A season of play gave clubs more evidence. Each new contract took a decision about who would get to take the field.

## Teacher notes

Read the script aloud. After listening, have students map each point to a supporting detail and explain what job that detail does.
