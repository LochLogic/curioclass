## Article 1. First read day 1

A declaration states ideas openly. In 1948, the United Nations General Assembly adopted this declaration about the rights of every person. An article is one numbered part of that larger text. The word universal extends across the whole group. A rule for one club applies to its members. A universal human right belongs to every person.

Article 1 starts with dignity, the worth every person has. You have it as soon as you are born. Your age, your work, and your belongings cannot add to it. The article also speaks of reason and conscience. People can think through choices and consider what is fair. The last part asks people to put those abilities to work by treating one another with care and respect.

## Article 2. First read day 4

Article 2 applies the declaration's rights to everyone. Entitled means a person has a claim to something. The article lists several differences, such as language and beliefs. It names them because none of these differences should be used to take rights away. The words other status leave room for differences that the list does not name.

The next paragraph considers the place a person belongs to. Some places have more power to make their own decisions than others do. Yet people have the same claim to these rights wherever they come from. A person's language or place of origin may matter to the way an office offers help. An office could offer directions in several languages so people can use the same service.

Invented for practice. A town offers a space for groups to keep supplies. The form lets people with one type of address apply and leaves others out. Ask what the form needs to find out. A way to reach each group might serve that purpose.

## Article 23. First read day 4

Two ideas run through Article 23. One is about choices and conditions at work. The first paragraph says people should be free to choose work. It also calls for fair conditions and protection when jobs are lost. The fourth says workers can join together in unions to protect their interests. A union is a group workers form to act together on matters such as hours, pay, or safety.

The other idea is about pay. The second paragraph says equal work should bring equal pay. The third calls pay for work remuneration. It says what a worker receives should support a life with dignity for the worker and family. Moreover, it calls for other forms of protection when pay alone cannot do that.

Invented for practice. Two people start packing at the same time. Both are supposed to be paid by the hour. A missing line on a time sheet leaves an hour off one person's pay. The exact time matters because the workers have already done the work. The article gives readers language for considering both the conditions the workers face and the pay they receive.

## Article 17. First read day 7

Property is something a person or group owns. The first paragraph protects ownership in both cases. Imagine a cart one person buys with saved money. Compare it with a set of tools several neighbors buy together. Each kind of ownership fits this paragraph.

The next paragraph protects against losing property arbitrarily. Arbitrarily means without a fair reason. Suppose a pickup driver takes a cart because it happens to be beside some chairs marked for donation. The driver's list gives a reason for taking the chairs. The cart's owner has a reason to ask why the cart was taken. The second paragraph gives readers language for asking whether the taking had a fair basis. Both paragraphs matter. The first says who can own something. The second asks what must be considered before it is taken away.

Invented for practice. Two people bought a bench together. A third person wants to set it outside for pickup. Before it goes, what would the third person need to find out?

## Article 13. First read day 9

Article 13 describes two related freedoms. One concerns choosing where to move and live within a state. A person who moves between towns within one country uses that freedom. Residence means where someone lives, rather than a place someone briefly visits. The other concerns leaving countries and returning to one's own country. If movement is restricted, people cannot choose freely where to go. Leaving does not erase the stated right to return. These are claims about what everyone should be able to do. The text leaves open how well those choices are protected in practice.

# Day 38 only. Reserved explainer

## Article 25. First read day 38

Article 25 names essential needs for living, such as food, clothing, housing and medical care. It also describes security when a person loses a livelihood, including through unemployment or illness, and says mothers and children are owed special care. A livelihood is a means of supporting life. The article says all children should receive the same protection, whatever their parents' marital status.

The two ideas belong together: people need support for daily life, and losing work or becoming ill does not make those needs disappear. These are the declaration's stated protections. Readers can inspect these claims in the article.
