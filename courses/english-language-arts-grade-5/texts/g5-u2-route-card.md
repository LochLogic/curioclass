# The Route Card

*Fiction set in El Yunque. The walk and the route card are invented.*

My teacher asked us to bring back one detail that someone who had missed the walk could find. I thought the quick part would be noticing something. Then I could spend the rest of the morning filling the boxes on my route card.

The card had three printed shapes, a square, a half circle and a triangle. Inés, our guide, had put a matching sign beside each stop. She unfolded her own copy on a table at the start of the walk. A crease split the triangle nearly in half.

“The marks name places,” she said. “Put an arrow beside a detail if you can show somebody where to look.”

We were in El Yunque, in northeastern Puerto Rico. From the start of the trail I could see crowns overlapping above us. Their edges gave the sky a torn look. I put my first arrow beside a sketch of the tallest tree.

Raúl looked from my arrow to the crowns. “Which one?”

I pressed my thumbnail into the card beside the arrow. I had thought the tallest tree would be obvious. At a different angle, I couldn't even pick it out myself.

At the square stop, a drop stood at the very tip of a leaf that stretched over the path. As Inés held the group at the sign, the drop swelled until it looked too big for the leaf to carry. It fell. A second drop was already forming by the time I heard the first one strike the ground. I drew the leaf's crooked stem so we could find it again.

Beyond the square, the path bent around a slab of stone. It was still wet from a morning shower, and one group at a time could pass comfortably. Inés asked the first group to go ahead with our teacher. The rest of us waited on the wide part of the marked path.

When my turn came, Raúl had already rounded the slab. Then the last blue sleeve in his group vanished from view. I could hear their voices, although the stone made it hard to tell how far they had gone. I took one quick step and planted both feet again. Inés was within arm's reach. I held up my card so she could see which group I meant.

“They're at the half circle,” she said. She called to our teacher, and he answered from the other side. A moment later she brought our group around the bend. I found Raúl beside a railing, with his card held flat against it. He made a little space for mine.

Before I could tell him what the voices had sounded like from the other side, a rough chirr ran out of the leaves. It broke into thin curls, like the shavings from a sharpener. The last curl seemed to hang in the air after the others had snapped off. I wrote *bird* and aimed a long arrow toward the branch I happened to be watching.

Raúl had been facing the far end of the railing. He had heard the same sound there. He drew a short line at the place where his shoes had been. I showed him the branch I'd picked. Together we watched it for a while. A leaf turned on its stem. The sound didn't come back. Inés had heard it too, but none of us could show where it started.

“Haste makes waste,” Raúl said. “You've almost used up that whole box with your arrow.”

He had left the middle of his own box open. I looked at my long line and then at the part of the card that said half circle. I rubbed out the arrow with the edge of my eraser. The word *bird* came away more slowly. When I brushed off the crumbs, I could still see where its letters had pressed into the card. Along the bottom I added the railing and two marks for our shoes. Above them I wrote *rough sound, heard from both marks*.

At the triangle, Inés spread the cards on a shelf under a roof. She picked two at random and asked partners to try reading each other's marks. Raúl tapped the crooked stem in my first box. He could point to the leaf from the path, he said, even if there was no drop on it when we went back. At the half circle he could tell exactly where we'd stood. I turned my card toward him so he could see the empty space above the two marks.

Back at school our teacher asked for one detail to put on a display. I put my pencil on the stem in the first box. Then I lifted the card and held the second box up to the classroom light. The faint letters were just visible from the back. I copied the two shoe marks onto the display card and left the space above them as wide as I could.

## Teacher notes

After collecting the unaided record, accept a connected account of the starting task, leaf at the square, momentary loss of sight of the first group, reunion at the half circle, disputed sound, paired review at the triangle and display choice. Inés stays beside the teller. The teller first rushes to locate and name the sound, then records only what the pair could establish. The erased arrow and the two shoe marks support that inference. Choosing the second box for display shows the change matters more than simply filling a box. Preview the brief loss of sight of the group privately as needed. The original and any supported or corrected record stay separate.
