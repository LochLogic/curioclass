# Section 3.6 opener

## Research notes that preserve people’s choices

A biography is factual life writing, distinct from a poem speaker. Review how a source tag preserves the profile paragraph or poem line beside each evidence note. Here pupils keep the individually titled biography and line beside a paraphrase. A heading gives the main idea of a group of notes, saying what the group is for.

Read for the athlete’s choice, the opportunity someone else made possible, and the known result. On day 111 use a released method step to model a single tag. Let pupils close the leaf before restating a fact and reopen it to check their meaning. A no-hitter describes hits. The released reading explains why fielders still matter. On 112 sort one relay detail and one fielding detail by the work each does. On 113 ask what one titled note contributes, then leave the full comparison plan to the pupil.

Use the passage cover log on every print, projected, digital, choice and audio copy. Issue only the current exit leaves, then collect before opening the next release. On 111 return earlier opinion comments and save the independent correction. Carry the original research, written cue and corrected notes into the comparison folder.

Print the source chart, partner rereading feedback card and each day’s half-slip exit. Use the digital source turn log for five individual turns per block when one device is available. Print keeps research moving. Date each missed digital action and route it to the next available slot. Preview racism, disability, polio, speech mockery, the earthquake and fatal crash. Keep public sharing optional. Use the athletes’ stated terms and questions about the text. Keep slurs, imitation, simulations and personal disclosures out of the work.
