# Writing and recording an announcement

Review the discussion moves chart. Mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, and explain what became clearer. In triads, rotate the facilitator, evidence finder and connector jobs. Prepare a source line before speaking. Give each child a brief private turn or a quiet way to respond.

Review audience, the listeners a reader reads for. For planning, review reader, purpose and form. A relevant fact helps answer the research question. An informative introduction names the subject and gives the listener a focus. A conclusion closes what the writer explained. Keep proposals labeled as proposals beside the facts that make them worth considering.

Use one released date, detail or ending at a time for practice. Drafting is private folder work. Review the peer look-for and teacher note. Check one thing, give a written cue, and let the writer move, add or explain an idea and log the change. Keep both drafts for day 152. Review series commas, opening replies, direct address and tags using separate practice lines.

Print the discussion card, model slip, source chart and script sheet. Prepare six local recording stations. Tell children who can hear their clip and allow a private live turn. Keep files with the teacher. If devices fail, collect other evidence and schedule a private recording makeup. Hold the check card and word forms separately from practice.
