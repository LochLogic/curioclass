# The Last Irrigation

Water slid into the head ditch. A leaf that had been lying on its side stood up, tipped over and sailed a little way. I followed it at a walk. Then the leaf stopped.

I knew how those peppers looked from the market side of the stand. Nobody there could see the dry roots through the crate. Watching that leaf, I imagined putting a green cloth over a row of empty boxes. My fear was a weight I kept shifting from one hand to the other. I tucked the calendar under my arm, left it there for two steps, and nearly dropped it.

Dad called from the first opening. “Let's see what it does here.”

I reached for the board at the second row. It was already loose. We could open another and give it a head start. I had lifted it an inch when Vale called my name. The stream at Dad's boots went thin as a shoelace.

I put the board back.
