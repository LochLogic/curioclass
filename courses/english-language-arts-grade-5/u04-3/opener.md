# How stories and poems show change

Review narrator, the teller of a story. A first-person narrator can report their own thinking. A third-person narrator can reveal a character's thoughts. Check what each passage actually tells. A summary is a brief account of important events without personal opinion. A theme is what a character's choices or a speaker's reflection can suggest about life.

Use one released illustration detail at a time. Ask what the detail makes clearer, what feeling it creates, or how it draws the eye. In rereading, name a purpose, mark a phrase and reread to hear how it sounds. Use the wording on the page first, then the nearby words to confirm a reading. Review stanza, a group of lines in a poem. Put a released scene's events in order before tracing what a later stanza contributes.

Preview drought and pressure on farms and herds. Offer released science prose or a cleared literary alternative privately. Teach the June 1921 poem with its historical title and neutral background. Explain the era of slavery where the allusion needs it. Issue the opening groups first, then the rest at the exit.

Print the story map, image notes and ranch sequence strips. Keep the final illustrations, unseen words and captions sealed as listed in the passage release record. Provide a printed or tactile image and a neutral description as needed. Use the supplied live score when a playback device is unavailable.
