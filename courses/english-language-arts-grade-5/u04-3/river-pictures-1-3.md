# Along One River

[page 1]

**Nia, above the bend**

The gate made its usual click. Grandpa took his hand away, and I put mine where his had been. From the gate we could see our rows fading from green to gray. My brother said the far rows looked like the wrong side of a blanket. I wanted to turn the whole field over and find its good colors.

Grandpa gave me a scrap of paper and asked where I thought the water went after it passed us. I drew it coming straight out at the bottom of the page. I left the rest blank.

From his shirt pocket Grandpa took a pencil with bite marks at one end. I drew the gate first, with the wheel as big as a dinner plate. Grandpa made a small circle for the place where the water had reached that morning. It sat halfway down our longest row.

**Ren, by the bridge**

After breakfast I carried a stool to the bridge. I used to count the sticks that passed under it. Now I could count the ones lying still. A long one rested on a pale shelf of stones. It made a straight black line that I could see from the road.

On my way back I passed the gate. Nia held up her paper. I turned it sideways and drew a curve. Then I drew the bridge so small she had to lean over my shoulder to see it. She asked me to come by later. I said I would if I could bring my little sister.

**Sal, below the bridge**

I brought a tin of pencils. Ren's little sister picked the shortest one. She drew blue from the bridge to the rock where our path reached the bank. Her line was thin enough to disappear under her thumb. She turned the paper round and tried it from the other side. The line came out the same.

We could hear the river. Nobody was sure how long there would be enough for all the work we had marked on the page. I told Nia where I was going to look the next morning. She said she'd send word when she got to the gate. Ren wanted to stand where the bridge cast its shadow.
