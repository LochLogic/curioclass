# Reading rights and discussing evidence

## What the section does

Days 7 to 12 use the article beside a story choice, then shift to independent reading, preparation and group discussion. Review the source tag. Preserve a source location beside each evidence note. Move from teacher-bracketed clauses to students finding the words they need.

## What to notice

Ownership and use can belong to different people. A right the declaration states gives a way to think about a fictional situation. Track whom a clause covers and what it protects. Review a fragment as an incomplete thought punctuated as if complete. Review a run-on as complete thoughts fused without a fitting boundary. In discussion, elaborate by adding detail to a partner's point.

## Materials and access

Use separate paper article and explainer leaves, source tags, role cards and a dated roster. A matching printed glossary works when the local digital entry is unavailable. Cover the practice and check leaves according to their release dates. Explain a pass privately. Preview loss of housing and property, missing hours and pay, and fear of interrupting work. Discuss what the characters and pages show.

## Evidence and feedback

Use dated feedback and independent correction on the next teaching day. Checks have five minutes with the key and ten minutes for a fresh response after collection. Teaching repairs take five minutes to compare and five for a different retry. Use one priority at a time. During teaching, hear six staggered 45-second turns while pairs work concurrently. Put pending students first at the named conferences. Keep private turn and paper support records.
