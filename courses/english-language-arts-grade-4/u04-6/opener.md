# Preparing a supported announcement

The announcement gives listeners a point they can follow and a concrete detail that supports it. A useful display clarifies that point. Use isolated fragments to inspect exact words, sentence links and punctuation. Give one written comment on the first draft and save both versions for the later writing audit.

Teach register through the relationship and purpose. A familiar audience may understand a short reference. New listeners often need the project named. To repair a missed connection, ask which specific detail a listener needs. A source date also gives limited information about what a writer could see. Keep the original wording and compare the makers’ access after collection.

Prepare the unit and presentation records, teacher-selected text leaves, drafts and small displays. On the recording day, set up six groups of five. Each formal slot takes 45 seconds plus 15 seconds to change speakers. Allow 45 seconds per child for the informal exchange in its own block. Give each listener one peer to follow. Keep recording and listening space quiet, keep cables off walkways, and keep recordings in the classroom. Use live observation if recording is unavailable. Enter observed speech and the listening note on separate records.

Use separate instructional fragments and withhold each check selection until its assigned time. Print the day’s half-sheet from the item data. Use actual reading observations when a criterion is due.
