# Learning opinion writing

An opinion takes a position on an issue. Review the grade 3 routine opinion with because: an opinion and a reason that is not the opinion again. Reason tells why. Evidence is the line or page that shows an idea is true. Group means put the facts about one thing together. Ask children to locate one idea at a time in the available opposing speech or memorial.

Review the paragraph-frame: topic, grouped facts, logical links, precise words and related closing. The essay-structure has an opening, two aspect sections and a related ending. Pick one part to inspect. A reader who missed the class still needs the issue and position. A list of source titles helps only if children can locate the part that supports a reason. Collect source notes separately from introductions.

The historical memorial and the modern opposing speech have different kinds of authority. Use the speech as a model of argument. It cannot show what any actual opponent said. Let children trace a reason to the passage that supplies it and label the source. Paper copies can stand in for projection.

Review sentence-combining: preserve complete thoughts when joining or separating clauses. A compound sentence has two complete clauses joined together. Use a comma before the coordinating conjunction joining them. Inspect a comma or conjunction in a new sentence. Let children try complete revisions independently. Keep their own drafts unjudged during these practice days.

On day 108, read the new passage aloud twice. Collect the listening papers before distributing print. Return day 107 and 108 evidence in the next Return and repair. Give each child one written move. The day 109 retry uses a different taught component.
