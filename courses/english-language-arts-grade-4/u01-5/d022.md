# Day 22: Inspect an essay and control sentences

## Teacher page

### Before class

Return day 21 tickets and P21-1/P21-2. Print the section 1.5 faulty essay, student page and covered half-sheet exit. Fold the student Exit box under until Exit. Set out the taught Williams profile and XXII; exclude companions. Open teacher-only inspection-key.md, exit-key.md and day answer key. Prepare source tags, section 1.5 feedback, day 19 reading feedback and the next five due reading records, reusing matching observations within ten days. Keep students' own notes unjudged in folders.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up and Opening

Return day 21's work. If many showed error N, inspect [[s01-K22-3]] again as a misplaced note, without producing a grouped plan. For error O, locate the printed source before accepting a claim. One earliest repair only; other needs use Choice workshop. Review heading as the main idea of a group of notes, saying what the group is for.

#### 10 min. Essay inspection

Review grade 3 joining short sentences without losing their meaning. Use [[s01-R11]] for three minutes in concurrent pairs. Hear at most two 45-second samples; other rehearsal stays unjudged. A correct combination preserves both thoughts. Record only heard speech.

Read the faulty essay, about one minute, with the Williams profile and XXII available. Introduce essay structure: an opening orients an unfamiliar reader, two body paragraphs develop distinct aspects, and an ending follows the explanation. Review day 14 supporting information beside its idea. Inspect one weakness at a time: an unnamed subject, mixed life/poem aspects, repeated features or unrelated lunch. Do not complete the essay or map every sentence's role.

Review grade 2 develop: add information that explains an idea. Review day 14 precise: specific enough to identify the intended feature. Review grade 3 conclusion: the last part that closes what a writer said. Children will inspect these features, without producing a judged essay.

#### 15 min. Sentence control

Review day 1 complete: expressing a whole thought. A fragment is an incomplete thought punctuated as if complete. Extend that distinction to a dependent clause. **Ask.** [[s01-P22-2]] Children choose before any key is shown. Explain that while leaves the reader waiting for a main thought; punctuation alone cannot supply it. Keep this selection for final independent review in the workshop.

Introduce run-on: complete thoughts fused without a fitting boundary. Inspect [[s01-K22-1]], then [[steps:s01-K22-1]]. Locate both complete thoughts in the correctly split version. A period and capital separate them while preserving meaning. A joining word must express a relationship supported by the ideas; adding because can invent a cause. Use no because-clause repair model before the exit.

#### 20 min. Choice workshop

First, children independently answer [[s01-P22-1]] and finalize P22-2. Collect those supplied-feature inspections. **Ask.** [[s01-K22-2]] Pairs inspect the false link before [[steps:s01-K22-2]]. Review meaningful links between ideas, rather than changing punctuation only.

Review grade 3 revise: move, add or explain information to help the reader. Children inspect a weakness in their own private plans, log one proposed change on section 1.5 feedback, and keep plans unjudged. One five-minute group repairs the earliest remaining day 21 need with the known misplaced-note fault. If more than ten miss the complete-thought choice, use that group slot for the whole class instead. Do not produce a completed source essay or a fragment repair together.

#### 10 min. Exit

Uncover [[s01-X22-1]] and [[s01-X22-2]] only now. Six minutes: children independently name the sentence jobs on the half slip. Unfold the student Exit box for the separate fragment repair. Both items are first seen now. Four minutes: collect and mark thirty paper pairs, eight seconds each. Accept equivalent role labels and any complete repair preserving the quiet-room idea, including a suitable independent clause. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen for each component; record help separately. Return day 23. After class, mark both practice choices together at ten seconds per child, five minutes, for day 23 return.

#### 10 min. Word work

Five minutes: review day 20 schwa in pencil and day 19 known-base replay. Children consult the reference, cover, write and compare every letter. Keep rehearsal unjudged.

Five minutes: review day 5 stress, greater emphasis on a syllable. Extend to word-stress shifts using [[s01-W22]], then [[steps:s01-W22]]. Read photograph, then photography; the strongest syllable moves from first to second. Use [[s01-W22-2]] and [[steps:s01-W22-2]] to contrast nation/national: both keep first-syllable stress. A suffix does not always move stress. Pairs say each pair twice naturally; no speed target or individual screen. If many stress every syllable equally, model the whole word again before they retry.

#### 15 min. Partner rereading

Review grade 3's partner rereading routine and phrase: words read together for meaning. Use taught Williams paragraphs 1 and 2 and whole XXII. Partners read successively for purpose, accuracy, natural rate and expression, checking letters and context when a word needs correction.

Return day 19 actual reading feedback. Hear only five due children, two minutes each, ten within fifteen. Reuse matching observations from the preceding ten days; record actual purpose/understanding, prose/verse phrasing and self-correction when observed. Unheard components stay blank. No repair group runs during teacher listening. Partner feedback does not create another individual screen.

#### 5 min. Independent reading

Children reread a taught source and update the reading log. Keep their day 21 source-tagged notes and today's proposed revisions for day 23 planning.

### Other rooms

Hand-copy the short faulty essay, keeping its four separate paragraphs. Preserve source line and paragraph numbers. Notebook columns replace source tags and feedback. Use a board and printed spelling reference.

### At home

**Swap.** Hand-copy the faulty essay and prompts without a printer. Use notebook feedback and a spelling reference.

**Skip.** Class observation rotations and teacher marking.

- **5 min. Repair.** Use day 21 feedback with [[s01-K22-3]].
- **10 min. Inspect.** Read the faulty essay. Try [[s01-P22-1]] and [[s01-P22-2]].
  **Say.** Does the opening identify its subject? Expect: no; the unfamiliar reader cannot identify it.
- **10 min. Sentences.** Inspect [[s01-K22-1]] and [[s01-K22-2]]. Consult the adult key afterward. Keep your child's own plan unjudged.
- **5 min. Words.** Read [[s01-W22]]. Reread a taught passage for meaning.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Uncover [[s01-X22-1]] and [[s01-X22-2]]. Your child responds independently. Keep dated work.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce private-plan inspection, preserving sentence teaching and both exits. Longer: compare the two supplied link choices for meaning; do not write a complete essay model.

### Words and supports

Review aspect, complete, develop, precise, conclusion, revise, phrase and stress. Run-on means fused complete thoughts; a fragment leaves a thought incomplete. Enlarge movable strips, offer signing or content scribing, and record help. Independent word reading stays the child's. Public sharing permits passing.

### Tomorrow

Day 23 returns X22, P22-1/P22-2 and actual R11 feedback. If many show error N, inspect one faulty essay component; if error T dominates, choose a complete thought using the taught while case. One Opening repair only; other needs use Choice workshop. Plan the essay and draft an introduction for the named reader. Carry day 21 source-tagged notes into that private planning. Keep incomplete observation records for scheduled rotations.
