# Section 2.6 opener: Develop and revise the first branch

## Teach one component at a time

The narrator is the teller of a story. An opening gives a new reader enough context to follow the character and situation. On day 69, help children locate one missing detail in a fragment, then leave their own expanded openings in the folder. Keep day 70's writing conditions unseen until launch and provide the registered sources. Return a dated written move on 71 and retain the independent correction for judgment on 72.

Dialogue is the words spoken by characters. It can show how a character responds to an event. Retrieve the comma and paired quotation marks from unit 1. Inspect a speech boundary on 71 and ask what a line contributes to a scene on 72. On 73, look at how an adjective describes and how two adjectives fit the noun. Before children revise their own work, have them locate an unsuitable word in a different fragment.

For error Z, identify which participant or part of the setting the reader cannot place. For error AO, locate where the speech starts and ends. For error AG, connect the speech to the event. For errors AB and AF, check the detail and the adjective order in separate examples. Use the unit record scale. Give the child one change at a time. Keep the original, written feedback and independent revision together.

## Before class

Gather the component inspection sheet, saved passages, half slips, generated answer keys, folder materials and dated evidence sheets. On 69 and 72 use the partner rereading feedback card with taught prose and the whole verse. Hear the scheduled five and keep the actual record. In the writing blocks hear six briefly and collect the other 24 written responses. Return their notes in the next named part. On 72 put written notes on eligible links, endings and precise words so children can revise them independently on 73.
