# Comparing trials in traditional tales

A quest is a journey with a purpose. A trial is a challenge, and a companion is a fellow traveler. Review theme as a message supported by events and details. A summary is a brief account of important events without personal opinion. Start by locating a teller, setting and action in an authorized reading. A story-map gives children a place to keep track of the action. Review the narrative arc by pointing out where a problem or turning point changes what happens next.

Read each telling’s own introduction. Keep the Akan, Greek and Japanese traditions visible even when two tales use similar patterns. Explain that an allusion makes meaning by referring to a known story. Review the figurative-language labels: literal object, imagined comparison and intended meaning. Use one familiar example to isolate the meaning of unusual effort. Give children the exact reserved reading only at its exit.

Keep the authorized leaves, tale notes and comparison listening notes with source notebooks. Use Heracles notes from day 85 or a unit 1 notebook if that map is unavailable. In the memorial, stock means securities that can be sold to raise money. Interest is the cost of borrowing. Explain the terms separately before children connect sources independently.

Use the private rereading record for five readers in a scheduled group. Ask what they are reading to find out and what the passage means. Listen to successive readings for accuracy, pace and expression. Notice a word the child decodes and confirms by rereading. On 102, give directions in two minutes. Listen for ten minutes and collect the separate summary in the last three. Use the separate word probe record for six readers at a time. Paper segmentation helps plan instruction. Enter oral evidence only on a turn you heard.

The tales contain deception, animal capture, captivity and conflict. The canal texts include dispossession, dangerous labor and illness. Children may pass in public discussion. Offer a written source note and use the private record for observed speaking.
