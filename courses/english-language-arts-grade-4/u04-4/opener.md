# Explaining a theme in writing

Trace the writing process through the essay folder: plan, draft, feedback, revision, edit. Explain the audience, purpose and form before students write. In an informative essay, an opening sets up the explanation. Related evidence belongs together. The ending follows from the evidence the reader has already seen.

Use the component leaves to inspect single faults. A quotation needs a clear connection to a point. A specific action word lets a reader picture what happened. A close follows what the writer has established. Students choose their own evidence for the folder drafts, which stay unjudged.

Keep first versions. Put one written comment beside the body draft and let the child revise one component. A school writing space can hold the same sequence. Use paper if devices are unavailable. Both settings preserve the draft, comment and revision.

Print only released sections of the novel. Keep the two independent checks and their keys separate from the writing folder. Before the next section's essay, finish the process conclusion briefly and keep the new companion scene closed until collection begins.
