# Section 2.1 opener: Read bodies and defenses

## Explain what a feature does

A structure is a body part. A behavior is something an animal does. A useful explanation links a part or action to what it helps the animal do. Review the main-idea-details chart and the note card with source tag. The topic names the subject. The main idea states what the reading explains about that subject. Teach the research steps in order: ask, locate, note, group, list, revisit.

Connect a paragraph to a diagram by tracing one arrow and reading the neighboring sentence. When an arrow merely gives order, check whether the words also explain a cause. Demonstrate one missing or inaccurate part at a time. Keep the reserved paragraphs and displays covered until their exits. Errors A, B, G, L and R call for a source detail, a complete idea, a real clue, a source location and an explained relationship respectively.

## Prepare and listen

Keep the overview, armor, poison, camouflage and mimicry leaves in separate stacks. Use registered Bodies That Protect and the saved federal selections. Print a current selection within the 350-word leaf limit at 14 point. Keep the original wording and punctuation. Give independent checks their own reading leaf. Keep the P44, reference cards, dictation blanks and teacher's word-probe key apart until collection. The same local copy replaces an unavailable live page.

There is no scored readiness check. On day 39 collect the familiar retrieval evidence in the regular opening. On day 44 collect first attempts and give written feedback before the later independent correction. Use the roster to hear the five due children in a fifteen-minute partner rereading block, up to two teacher minutes each. Partners reread for phrases and expression. Reuse matching dated evidence. Keep unobserved speech and reading blank.

No part requires live animals or imitating a defense. Use printed passages and diagrams. Invite private responses when a child passes in public talk.
