# Section 1.2 opener: Images, meaning and forms

## What the section does

Read the complete Frost, Rossetti and Dickinson poems, plus notebook parts 3 and 4.
Keep the checkpoint entry and comparison companions covered until day 9.
A simile uses an explicit comparison such as like; a metaphor presents one thing as another.
Both need context. Near-synonyms can differ in force. A conventional expression's meaning
comes from its use, not the literal meanings of all its words. Category names can overlap.

Day 8 reads the three whole taught tellings and scene. Perform the exact on-file oral
reading and stage the printed directions. Teacher-operated paper figures replace missing audio.
Use only floor-level seating and soft taps; nobody must act. The transcript alone cannot prove performance.

Use choice-cards.md, feature-labels.md, meaning-feedback.md, the carried log/roles/wall,
and separate complete reading copies. Print only the current day of each sheet.
Keep choice-key.md and exit-key.md teacher-only. Half slips hold two day 6 comparison items;
its third judged paper item is the student-page Exit box. Return it with the other work.

A/O: match claims to actual words. B/C: remove topic-only labels and personal opinions.
E: locate structural features separately. F: distinguish author from telling voice.
G: replay the actual direction. Y: match literal referent; Z: match conventional meaning.
AA: compare opposites; AJ: compare intensity. H/I/X/AL use letters, phrasing and reference correction.

Day 7 gives written feedback on a taught-poem meaning record; day 8 independently revises,
day 9 finalizes. It is not cold reading. Day 9's fresh collection uses 35 minutes;
written feedback takes fifteen. No reteaching or scored correction in that feedback part.
Day 10 holds the ten-minute instruction/revision route; judgment follows on day 11.

## Daily arrangements

Ninety minutes: a sixty-minute unit lesson and thirty-minute skills block.
Use each day block's exact part names and minutes. All pair work runs concurrently.
The task sheet has at most 350 student words and fourteen-point type.
Each original printed sentence has at most fifteen words. Complete reading copies and
lined writing paper are separate pieces. Whole notebook parts are about 1,050 words,
nine minutes at 120 words a minute, read with support in the named part.
Never distribute a complete manuscript containing future reserved companions.

Use only the permitted 2017 lesson design and the on-file replacements. Reading copies
carry their own author and rights lines. This section's cards and questions are original.
The source map names each adapted day's attribution. No recording or classroom book purchase is needed.

## Evidence and support

Use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen, one scale on record.md.
Blank cells remain unobserved. Distinguish a language support from an unsupported interpretation.
A scribe can record content without supplying an answer. Independent decoding remains the child's.
Students may pass publicly and give a private response. Ordinary or invented writing topics are welcome.
Never ask for personal loss, private family stories or racial reenactment.

Return all judged paper on the named hand-back day. One component gets the five-minute
Opening repair; others use one Choice workshop group or independent cards. More than ten
of thirty signals a common need. Teach the repair on a different taught case.
Own drafts remain unjudged where they share an assessment's form. Judged practice selects,
locates or fixes an isolated weakness. Do not display a completed held response.

## Words and routines

Retain the discussion moves chart: mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link,
explain what became clearer. The reading log keeps date, title, my purpose, one detail.
The sound and spelling wall marks the week's irregular words. Use familiar patterns before context.
A stanza is a group of lines in a poem. Evidence is the line or page that shows an idea is true.
Review theme as a message that comes back in more than one book; extend that familiar
message language to a supported claim within one text. A topic word alone is not that claim.
Infer means reach a conclusion from details; stated information is printed directly.
Narrator names the telling voice. The writer and narrator need not be the same person.

## Other rooms

Hand-copy current short cards when printing is unavailable. Enlarge or use tactile line dividers as needed.
Keep original text punctuation and line breaks. No digital tool is required.
At home use thirty to forty minutes: retrieve, read together, inspect one choice,
respond privately, reread. Keep dated work and supports without scores.
