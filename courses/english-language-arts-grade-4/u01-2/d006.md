# Day 6: Names for comparisons

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print whole A Birthday, November 12, current choices, feature and intensity labels, and student page. Cover the page's Exit box. Print the exact bench poem once with s01-X06 on a half slip. Teacher-only answers: choice-key.md, exit-key.md and day answer key. Gather day 5 tickets and cards, dictionaries, sound and spelling wall, reading log and unit 1 component record. Keep later companions out.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Return day 5's s01-X05, inspected choices and practice. If many day 5 tickets showed error A or error O, use [[s01-P6-4]] to distinguish a real supplied object from an imagined one. This locates one feature of a different taught case, without revisiting the sock case. Others inspect their feedback privately. One further need waits for Choice workshop.

#### 10 min. Read and gloss

Read the complete A Birthday, 107 words, then revisit November 12, about 210 words. Three reading minutes leave seven for glossing and tracking. Explain dais, a raised platform; vair, fur; halcyon, peaceful here; down, soft feathers; fleurs-de-lys, lily designs. Peacocks' eyes here means markings. Content: intense affection, joy and an imagined elaborate celebration. No child must discuss a relationship or birthday.

Review literal and nonliteral from grade 3: words meant exactly or another way. Use [[s01-P6-4]] once, without a second response if completed in Opening. Source punctuation and spelling remain unchanged.

#### 15 min. Comparison meanings

Teach simile, a comparison using like or as, through [[s01-P6-1]]. A word like is not always a comparison; here two unlike things are connected. Partners identify the comparing word before naming the form.

Attempt [[s01-K6-1]] before revealing [[steps:s01-K6-1]]. Teach metaphor as a comparison that describes one thing as another. **Ask.** [[s01-P6-5]] Expect the closeness label, from the nearby cards and nearly lost details. Keep literal object, imagined comparison and intended meaning on separate labels. No complete exit-shaped interpretation is modeled. Read the poem's first stanza again, keeping its repeated comparisons audible.

#### 20 min. Choice workshop

Introduce synonym as a word with similar meaning that may differ in force, and antonym as a word with opposite meaning. Alone, try [[s01-P6-2]] and [[s01-P6-7]]. Glad and delighted share a direction; delighted commonly signals stronger happiness. Sad points the other way. Context can change a word's force; these are not fixed numerical distances.

Use [[s01-P6-6]] to remove a reader opinion from a weak supplied summary. Then [[s01-P6-3]] selects an actual event from November 12. Review day 3 summary: a brief account of important events without personal opinion. Each child first drafts privately with [[s01-P6-11]], then partners give one accuracy or opinion note. No completed summary is supplied. Compare the summary's sentences with Rossetti's repeated like phrases; prose can have rhythm too. Review grade 1: prose versus poem, same events in lines or in sentences. Use the feature and intensity labels for the separate relationship choices. The teacher hears one five-minute group on the earliest day 5 unmet feature; all other practice runs concurrently. Collect choice work for day 7.

#### 5 min. Notebook

Use [[s01-P6-8]]. Children revise their own brief poem summary after the partner note. Both dated versions stay unjudged in the folder because this production form overlaps later assessment. No child must read a draft publicly.

#### 5 min. Exit

Turn to the covered Exit box, then return to Word review afterward. Release [[s01-X06]] alone on the half slip. Uncover [[s01-X06b]] and [[s01-X06c]] in the student-page Exit box simultaneously. The poem prints only on the half slip; the other prompts point to it. Three brief responses take four minutes; one minute collects both pieces. Print stems exactly. The half slip holds the bench response. Give each Exit box response its own two-line space. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen by component on one record.

#### 10 min. Word work

Review silent-e and vowel teams, familiar chart patterns from grade 1. Use [[s01-P6-9]]. Children first read chunks, then cover and spell, then consult dictionaries. Compare the whole letter sequence, rather than guessing from a sentence. Use the sound and spelling wall for the corrected form. No new cycle or assessment-word practice.

#### 20 min. Independent reading

Use [[s01-P6-10]], accessible taught copies only. Review the carried reading log routine. Teacher marking takes thirty children times thirty-five seconds: thirty for the three exit responses and five for the simile selection, seventeen and a half minutes. The remaining two and a half minutes hold transitions and handback notes. Other practice is self-checked using the day answer key and stays in folders; no added conference today. Mark the comparisons and relationships separately, without an overall points total. Retain actual observations; do not repeat the fall screen.

### Other rooms

Use exact hand-copied current cards without a printer. Share whole readings with tracking and keep the independent exit separate. A board copy replaces the sound and spelling wall. No device or purchased book is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy today's prompts, covering the Exit box. Share whole readings.

**Skip.** Class conferences and card collection.

- **5 min. Repair.** Inspect day 5 feedback. Try [[s01-P6-4]].
- **10 min. Read.** Read whole A Birthday and November 12. Try [[s01-P6-1]].
  **Say.** Which word links the comparison? Expect like.
- **10 min. Choose.** Try [[s01-K6-1]] before its steps, then [[s01-P6-2]] and [[s01-P6-7]].
  **Say.** Which word points the other way from glad? Expect sad.
- **5 min. Notebook.** Try [[s01-P6-6]], then draft with [[s01-P6-11]] and revise with [[s01-P6-8]]. Keep both private versions.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Uncover [[s01-X06]], [[s01-X06b]] and [[s01-X06c]]. Your child answers alone. Keep both pieces dated.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce optional summary elaboration; keep whole reading, distinct comparison labels, relationship choices and exit. Longer: reconsider the intensity labels in another taught line without writing a held response. Inspected work returns day 7. Own summaries remain unjudged folder work.

### Words and supports

Review literal/nonliteral, evidence and summary. New simile, metaphor, synonym and antonym name the distinctions practiced today. Read historical glosses aloud. Allow large print, signing, tactile line divisions or a composition scribe; record help without supplying meaning. Keep independent decoding separate from supported composition.

### Tomorrow

Return s01-X06, s01-X06b and s01-X06c with inspected practice in day 7. Error Y treats the comparisons literally: Opening matches another taught phrase to its real object. Errors AA and AJ confuse opposites or erase force: one Choice workshop group uses glad, delighted and sad. Each child gets one repair route. Day 7 reads whole Hope and notebook part 4, teaches conventional sayings, and retains actual successive-reading evidence.
