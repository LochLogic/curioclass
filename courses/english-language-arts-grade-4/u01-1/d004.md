# Day 4: Hear and mark a poem

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the whole Williams poem once per child, a taught Williams biography paragraph, student page, private fall card, and covered half-sheet exit. Keep the generated day answer key teacher-only. Cover the student page Exit box until Exit. Prepare three separate blank overlays for breaks, colors, and phrase lengths. Gather day 3 tickets, collected choice work, and the teacher record. Keep the map and log available. Teacher-only role feedback uses the same record scale.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Return day 3's s01-X03 and choice work. If many day 3 tickets showed error A, [[s01-P4-1]] selects a supported label from a different taught passage. Otherwise readers privately inspect the evidence in their returned response. Keep error C event repair for Choice workshop, one repair route per need. Do not reuse the exit's poem as a model.

#### 10 min. Read and hear

Read the complete 16-word Williams poem with shared tracking. Reread preserving line endings while carrying sense across them. Review poem from day 1 and line break from grade 1: where one line ends and the next begins; it puts a clue at a line end. Review image as sensory detail. Introduce verse as writing arranged in deliberate lines, and rhythm as the pattern of sounds and stresses heard while reading. Rhythm can vary; poetry need not have regular meter or rhyme.

Pairs try [[s01-K4-1]]. **Ask.** Where does one familiar word cross a break? Expect wheel/barrow or rain/water. Reveal [[steps:s01-K4-1]]. Mark just the break, without a complete image-and-rhythm explanation.

#### 10 min. Feature demonstration

Use a separate overlay for each feature. Pairs locate [[s01-P4-2]] before marking the colors; [[s01-P4-3]] marks phrase lengths independently. Read the one-word endings with audible space, then read across them with less space. The same words allow different phrasing; line endings guide attention without forcing identical pauses.

Use [[s01-R5]] for the last three minutes. Review beginning/ending and familiar sentence capitals, retrieved from earlier grades. Correct only the supplied practice sentence. Williams's source punctuation stays unchanged; a published poem is not a convention repair exercise.

#### 20 min. Choice workshop

Pairs use [[s01-P4-4]] to separate literal voice from an imagined sound description. For error Y, point to the gate's stated noise; a complaint is a comparison. Use [[s01-P4-5]] for day 3 error C; error D compares Ben's early action with his later permission question. These supplied choices never require a complete assessed response.

Teacher hears only children 7 to 12 in two-minute linked role exchanges on the taught poem, twelve minutes total. Review day 1 roles and grade 3 discussion moves. Hear preparation, a linked question or response, and a listening reflection. Other pairs audit their own role notes concurrently. One different group receives a five-minute event or feature repair; the remaining three minutes hold dated written role feedback. A child may pass publicly and arrange a private live exchange. Paper never substitutes for unheard oral evidence. Collect role notes for day 5.

#### 10 min. Notebook

Return to the already-read October 2 and 5 entries, about three reading minutes, then [[s01-P4-6]]. Keep the note brief and unjudged in the folder. No completed image-and-rhythm explanation is modeled or scored. Permission to decline sharing still holds. Retain the dated map and reading log through day 13.

#### 5 min. Exit

Release [[s01-X04]] for the first time, alone on paper. Print the exact stem with its line breaks and one response box on the half-sheet ticket. Allow four minutes to read and respond, one to collect. Also answer [[s01-P4-X1]] in the student page Exit box, using the poem printed only on the ticket. Three brief component responses fit the four writing minutes. Marking both pieces takes 30 children times 25 seconds, 12.5 minutes in Partner rereading below. Use the same shown, shown with help, not yet seen scale, with separate image and rhythm columns. Never infer a missing component from a good answer to the other.

#### 10 min. Word work

Hear children 26 to 30 using [[s01-P4-7]], two minutes each. These ten minutes finish the fall rotation: 9 + 7 + 9 + 5 = 30 children, sixty teacher minutes total, no duplicate diagnostic. Each turn includes meaning, unfamiliar long-word reading and a successive reread. Others use [[s01-P4-8]] independently, review of familiar closed/open chunks and reference spelling, then taught reading. No simultaneous teacher-led dictation.

#### 15 min. Partner rereading

Review grade 3 partner rereading: phrases and expression, no speed. Pairs use [[s01-P4-9]], the whole poem and the already-taught 80-word biography paragraph, twice each. Hear meaning in the phrasing and check letters before confirming a doubtful word. Reuse matching fall observations rather than adding a listening group. Teacher marks the ticket and Exit box for 12.5 minutes, leaving 2.5 minutes for handback notes.

#### 5 min. Independent reading

Alone, [[s01-P4-10]]. Choose an accessible taught notebook entry or biography for its purpose. Children update logs and keep them in folders. One short selection and its support record do not establish mastery of the whole reading range.

### Other rooms

No device is needed. Use traced copies instead of overlays; mark one feature on each. Share taught readings orally with tracking. Copy an exact private exit for each child. Printed or tactile words remain necessary for independent decoding.

### At home

**Swap.** No overlays: use separate sheets for feature marks. Share reading copies.

**Skip.** The class role rotation and fall screen.

- **5 min. Repair.** Inspect yesterday's ticket privately; try [[s01-P4-1]].
- **8 min. Read.** Read the whole Williams poem twice. Try [[s01-K4-1]] before reading its steps.
  **Say.** Where does a word cross a break? Expect wheel/barrow or rain/water.
- **7 min. Mark.** Try [[s01-P4-2]] and [[s01-P4-3]] separately.
  **Say.** Which colors appear? Expect red and white.
- **10 min. Notebook.** Revisit October 2 and 5. Try [[s01-P4-6]]. Reread the taught biography paragraph together.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Uncover [[s01-X04]] and [[s01-P4-X1]]. Your child responds alone. Keep both pieces dated.

### Shorter or longer

Uncollected practice stays in the folder with immediate descriptive feedback. No points are assigned.

Shorter: reduce notebook elaboration, keeping complete poem readings, separate feature tasks, diagnostic turns and exit. Longer: compare two ways of phrasing the taught poem without producing a complete exit-shaped explanation.

### Words and supports

Review day 1 image and poet, grade 1 line break, and day 2 evidence. Verse and rhythm are introduced through separate features. Use large print, tactile line separators, signing, or a composition scribe; record support. Preserve source line breaks and punctuation. Independent decoding remains the child's.

### Tomorrow

Hand s01-X04, s01-P4-X1, and role notes back on day 5. Error E uses short print alone as an explanation: Opening marks one audible pattern. Error Y gives an object a literal voice: Choice workshop matches a taught figurative phrase to its meaning. One five-minute route per need. Day 5 reads the complete Frost poem and novel part 3, then matches separate features. Pass the dated log, map, and actual-observation record to section 1.2. Children 13 to 30 still need live role observations in later scheduled windows; retain written role feedback for day 12 Choice workshop.
