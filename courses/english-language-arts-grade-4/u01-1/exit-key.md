# Teacher-only exit answers

Keep apart from all student cards and teaching packets.

## s01-X01

Many answers work: a coherent specific image suited to a reader who cannot see the scene.

Look for: Coherent description keeps the kite and fence situation. | Specific sensory detail helps the absent reader imagine it. | Accept invented colors, movement or texture. Do not require the sample wording. | Record shown, shown with help, or not yet seen by component; this first sample receives feedback, no grade..

Samples: The torn kite slaps the fence. Its red tail catches on a nail. | A kite is there. | The thing is nice.

## s01-X02

Changing smooth to rough suggests that he wants the words to match the texture. Accept other interpretations grounded in the printed detail.

Look for: Names a relevant printed detail. | Explains a supported thought using that detail. | Do not require an exact quotation or the model interpretation. | Record shown, shown with help, or not yet seen for each component..

Samples: He changed smooth to rough after feeling sharp ridges. He wants his description to match. | He feels ridges, but I cannot tell what he thinks. | He hates writing because stones are hard.

## s01-X03

Frustration or disappointment fits staring or pushing the plan away. Other grounded feelings work.

Look for: plausible initial feeling | actual supporting action.

Samples: The builder feels disappointed: the plan is pushed beneath the chair. | Happy because everyone loves building.

## s01-X04

Tap and drip suggest repeated striking sounds, such as rain on the lid and gutter. Their repeated three-part pattern creates a beat. Sings and rings suggest sounds, not literal voices. Accept other supported sound interpretations.

Look for: Explains a sound image supported by printed words; no requirement to identify rain as the only possible cause. | Explains an audible pattern, such as repeated groups of three sounds. | Figurative sings need not mean a literal mouth or voice. | Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen in separate image and rhythm columns..

Samples: I imagine little strikes on metal. The three taps and three drips make a repeating beat. | Tap makes a sound, but I cannot explain the pattern. | The lid has a singing mouth.

## s01-P4-X1

A supported account of one line ending: for example, a pause after the three taps groups the sounds before the lid is named. Other supported phrasing effects count.

Look for: Identifies a particular line ending. | Connects that ending to an audible grouping, pause or emphasis. | A line need not require a pause; supported different readings count. | Use the common shown, shown with help, not yet seen scale..

Samples: I wait after the three taps before finding out what makes them. | The first line ends after tap, but I do not explain its effect. | Every line must be read with the same long pause.

## s01-X03b

A tower falls; the builder puts the plan aside, reconsiders it, and starts a wider base.

Look for: fall, reconsideration, new base in sequence | no opinion.

Samples: The tower falls. The builder revises the plan and starts a wider base. | I like towers.
