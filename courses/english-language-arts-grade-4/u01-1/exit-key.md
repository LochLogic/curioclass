## s01-X03

Prompt: My paint runs out.
I drop my brush.
My picture waits
beside the sink.
A scrap of cloth
catches my eye.
I glue its stripes
where paint should go.

Explain the painter's first feeling using a detail.

Answer: Frustration or disappointment fits dropping the brush when the paint runs out. Accept another feeling supported by the poem.

Look for: plausible first feeling | supporting detail, such as dropping the brush | no unsupported generalization

Samples: The painter feels frustrated and drops the brush when the paint runs out. | Happy because everyone loves painting.

Wrong: Happy because everyone loves painting. (A). A general claim about painters does not use a poem detail.

## s01-X04

Text: g4-u1-x04; whole original assessment companion, printed once in this item stem

Prompt: Tap, tap, tap,
the loose lid sings.
Drip, drip, drip,
the gutter rings.

Explain how the repeated sounds create this poem's image and rhythm.

Answer: Tap and drip suggest repeated striking sounds, such as rain on the lid and gutter. Their repeated three-part pattern creates a beat. Sings and rings suggest sounds, not literal voices. Accept other supported sound interpretations.

Look for: Explains a sound image supported by printed words; no requirement to identify rain as the only possible cause. | Explains an audible pattern, such as repeated groups of three sounds. | Figurative sings need not mean a literal mouth or voice. | Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen in separate image and rhythm columns.

Samples: I imagine little strikes on metal. The three taps and three drips make a repeating beat. | Tap makes a sound, but I cannot explain the pattern. | The lid has a singing mouth.

Wrong: Only short print makes it a poem. (E). Observed response misses this task: Tap and drip suggest repeated striking sounds, such as rain on the lid and gutter. Their repeated three-part pattern creates a beat. Sings and rings suggest sounds, not literal voices. Accept other supported sound interpretations.

Wrong: The lid has a singing mouth. (Y). Treats the figurative sound description as a literal voice.

## s01-X03b

Text: g4-u1-x03; whole exit poem

Prompt: Use the painter poem. Summarize its important events.

Answer: The paint runs out. The painter drops the brush, notices striped cloth, and glues it onto the picture instead.

Look for: paint runs out | brush dropped | striped cloth noticed and glued onto picture | no personal opinion

Wrong: I like painting. (C). A personal preference replaces the event sequence.
