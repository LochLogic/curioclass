# Writing a release and a narrative scene

An informative release explains what the class did, in terms an unfamiliar reader can follow. Group related details and show where information came from. Keep a classmate note brief when both writers know the background. For feedback, choose one next move such as adding context, moving a misplaced fact or replacing a vague verb.

In the narrative scene, keep a reported action recognizable and label imagined speech as invented. Use a clear narrator, time transition and related closing reflection. Save the scene and its feedback separately from the release. Keep earlier reading selections and the independent script directions in separate folders.

Use the unit record, research leaves, a spelling reference and the scene discussion slip. Print only the assigned spans. During paired rereading, keep actual dated observations with their supports. During discussion, let anyone pass in public and use the paper slip to save their thinking.

Publish through the class workspace. Prepare capture and save access before class. Give each child a turn to save and show a file. The paper release still records composition if classroom publishing is unavailable. Enter digital publication and spoken language only when the individual evidence is available.
