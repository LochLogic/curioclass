# Comparing accounts of the vote

A firsthand account tells what its maker experienced or did. A secondhand account can gather information from later records. Ask who made each account, what that person could know, and what the account emphasizes. A date establishes when something was written. Use textual evidence to judge what its maker could witness.

Print only the released passages and the source inspection cards. Keep separate envelopes for later selections. Readers encounter Black women's organizing and the racism that limited voting access. Name both the work people chose and the barriers they faced. Skip reenactment and requests for personal disclosure.

For sentence-combining, preserve complete thoughts when joining or separating clauses. Teach the antecedent or relationship through one clause at a time. Keep full response sets for independent work. When a relative form points to the wrong word, draw an arrow to what it describes and ask which form fits.

Use the discussion note for each child and the private discussion record for turns you hear. The facilitator invites ideas. The evidence-finder locates a detail. The questioner asks a question that follows another idea. In groups of five, all speak and write. Teacher records identify the actual students heard.

Return one recorded priority per child during Return and repair. Inspect a mistaken attribution or overbroad claim, then ask for a different-case retry. Keep source leaves, notebook and first responses together. With no printer, display a released leaf and have students record their work on paper.
