# Following a family disagreement

Review **stage direction**, a note for the performer in brackets. The performer uses the note to guide an action or delivery. Read directions in a distinct voice for access. A **scene** is a part of a play. A character's speech and actions work together to show what the character cares about. A small change in a performed action can change what an audience infers. Inspect one separated component before students encounter a complete transfer task.

Use a seated reading with clearly assigned voices. Describe locations and actions aloud. Children can follow on the page or pass on public speaking. Keep scenes on separate released leaves. The play's family has a fictional concern about its farm. The course uses Nation-authored sources for Native Nations' own accounts of land and rights.

Review complete thoughts before adding verb forms. Teach how was, is and will be locate an action in time when joined to a verb ending in ing. To inspect a place phrase, identify the preposition and its object, then point to the action or thing it describes. Keep each inspection short and distinct from the independent response. Keep a spelling reference on hand.

When a child points only to dialogue for a performance claim, return to the written action. When an auxiliary is missing, have the child identify the time and repair the auxiliary on another sentence. Use familiar verse and prose to recall form. Save future scenes and the independent listening details for their assigned times. Collect listening responses before handing out print.
