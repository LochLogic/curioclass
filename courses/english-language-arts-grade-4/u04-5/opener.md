# Researching actions and evidence

Keep the independent essay, the research check and the class message work in separate folder sections. The essay uses a fresh scene and two familiar novel moments. The research check uses two fresh civic articles. Use the unit record for both and keep the original response with its feedback.

One article can focus on how people organized their work. Another can focus on what the work supplied. Two articles based on a single report still share one source of information. Ask what the detail lets the reader say and how far the claim can reach. Keep an article title attached to each research note.

Use one defect at a time during repairs. A source tag on the wrong note needs a change to the tag. A community result that the source never measured needs a smaller claim. A vague opening may need the project named for new listeners. Let children keep their own source notes and message plans in the folder as private work.

Use dated folders, pencils, a spelling reference, the unit record and labeled reading leaves. Print only the span needed that day. Keep the two composting articles sealed until day 146. Use saved authorized pages when live reading access is unavailable. Keep classroom messages within the classroom and let children choose what to explain.

Use partner rereading with a feedback card: phrases and expression, no speed. Hear five due readers in a 15-minute observation block. Copy a matching entry with its original date and supports. Record actual oral evidence on the presentation record and keep written notes separately.
