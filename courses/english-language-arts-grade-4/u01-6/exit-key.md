# Section 1.6 exit answer key

Teacher only. Keep separate from student copies.

## s01-X23

Source: g4-u1-01, January 17 four-step invitation and final two-line sign.

Shape: Compose one introduction for an unfamiliar reader from a new source pairing.

Ben changed his display sign. This explanation compares its directions with its invitation to choose. Other clear introductions identifying the display and change work.

- AE: He changed it.
- O: Visitors paid for tickets to enter.

## s01-X24

Source: g4-u1-01, September 7 observation from On the way home through floor was already dry.

Shape: Develop an event-and-quotation explanation from a reserved literary passage.

Someone shook a wet umbrella beside the bakery door. Ben imagines "a small indoor rain," making its falling water visible. Equivalent source-true explanations work. Accept paired single or double quotation marks. The source does not say that the umbrella was shaken indoors.

- O: Ben describes an outdoor storm.
- N: Umbrella, bakery, rain, floor.
- W: Ben calls it "a small indoor rain.
- AG: Ben writes "a small indoor rain."

## s01-X25

Source: g4-u1-01, February 14 three-line visitor card.

Shape: Develop a weak paragraph with a logical link and precise literary language.

The door closes. However, the hall keeps talking, so its sounds seem to continue beyond the door. Other accurate developments use a logical link and identify the hall or voices rather than vague things.

- P: Because a builder made the door, the poem repeats.
- Q: The things help readers hear things.
- O: A visitor unlocks the door with a key.
