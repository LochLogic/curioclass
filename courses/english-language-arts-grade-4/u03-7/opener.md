# Developing supported reasons

Review the paragraph-frame and essay-structure. Ask what one component does: set up the issue, give a reason, support it, link ideas or close. A conclusion is the part of a review that ends it and goes back to the opinion. A planned link such as for instance introduces an example. In addition adds a related idea. In order to gives a purpose. Ask what relationship the writer needs before choosing the words.

Keep the source label close to a detail so a reader can locate it. An original historical document can record a proposal. A modern reconstruction can model a way to argue. The history brings together records of what people did and experienced. Provide the authorized reading leaves if projection is unavailable. Keep each checked case and the independent assessment packets separate.

Trace the writing process through the essay folder: plan, draft, feedback, revision, edit. Review writing-process: retain dated stages and respond to a particular note. Keep the original day 113 paragraphs, the teacher’s written feedback and the day 114 revision. Use the revision history sheet to locate the versions. A child’s ongoing opinion draft is folder work. Keep separately scored responses on their own slips.

On 114, seat six groups of five. Each group gets eight minutes: two 45-second turns per child and 30 seconds of handover. Record an actual prepared contribution or question in the first turn. Listen for a linked reply and review in the second. The separately recorded question is part of the first turn. Waiting children revise their own work from written feedback. Leave any unobserved criterion blank. Use the unit record’s single scale.
