# Connecting scenes and arguments

Review **narrator**, the teller of a story. A narrator may participate or tell from outside the action. Look at the storytelling around dialogue. A character can say I in either kind of story. Give children a new single teller-label problem before asking them to compare versions independently.

Readers can use two texts to answer one question. Check each detail in its own source, then ask how the details fit. A family in an invented play can raise a question about a route. A Nation describes its relationship to its homeland in its own published account. Keep distinctions in who is speaking, what the source can show and what is still unresolved.

Use authorized play and history leaves, a source notebook and a dated folder. Keep scene revisions in separate folder work. Give feedback on one component using a fresh incident. The child's longer draft stays available for later rereading and private feedback.

Use the discussion-turn record for individual evidence. On day 99, run six groups of five for 7½ minutes each. Each child takes two 42-second turns. The remaining 30 seconds per group allow handover. Children waiting for their group annotate quietly. Paper notes document preparation and written reflection. The observed record captures speaking and listening. Use actual matching rereading records, including poetry when recorded, and leave missing observations blank.
