## Reading notes

This is Burn’s own explanation entered in the Tennessee House Journal on August 19, 1920, page 94. It is not his mother’s letter. The introductory line is taught on 126. Reason 1 is first released for exit 126, then taught on 127 and compared on 128. Withhold reason 2 until C1 on 129 and reason 3 until G1 on 151. Supply only the scheduled selection, not this whole file. Reasons 4 and 5 and the earlier defensive paragraphs are omitted; this is not the complete statement. The full document includes racist language in reason 5. Do not imply that this selection represents every view Burn expressed or every woman’s access to voting.

## Place and date

Nashville, Tenn., August 19, 1920

## Introductory line, instruction on day 126

I want to take this opportunity to state that I changed my vote in favor of ratification because:

## Reason 1, reserved for exit 126; instruction afterward

(1) I believe in full suffrage as a right;

## Reason 2, reserved for C1 on day 129

(2) I believe we had a moral and legal right to ratify;

## Reason 3, reserved for G1 on day 151

(3) I know that a mother’s advice is always safest for her boy to follow, and my mother wanted me to vote for ratification.

H. T. Burn.
