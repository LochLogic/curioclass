## Text

### Paragraph 1

A North American porcupine has a coat of modified hairs called quills. Each quill ends in a sharp point. The quills are loosely attached. They can come away when another animal touches them. Protection depends on contact, not on quills flying through the air.

### Paragraph 2

This observation scene is invented for practice. A wary porcupine turns its back toward an approaching animal. Its quills are exposed, or uncovered. The other animal recoils, drawing back. A photograph records the moment when both stand still.

### Paragraph 3

Quills are not the only protection. A porcupine can climb a tree and find refuge, a place of safety. Reading about its body and climbing helps explain how a slow animal can escape danger.
