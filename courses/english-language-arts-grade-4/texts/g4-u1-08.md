## Text

### First-person prose

1. I sat on the low step outside our classroom, holding my poem face down. There was a blank space where its ending should have been. Mina stopped beside me. I hoped she would keep walking, because I did not want her to see the unfinished page.

2. "I cannot find my ending," I said. Mina pointed toward the doorway and waited without taking my paper. I heard shoes crossing the hall: a quick shuffle, a pause, then several heavy steps. I turned the page over and wrote, "The hallway wears a coat of footsteps."

3. I lifted the poem so Mina could read it. She smiled. The ending had come from the same hallway I had hurried through that morning. This time I had stayed still long enough to hear it.

### Third-person prose

1. Ben sat on the low step outside the classroom, holding his poem face down. The page had a blank space where its ending should have been. Mina stopped beside him.

2. "I cannot find my ending," Ben said. Mina pointed toward the doorway and waited without taking his paper. Shoes crossed the hall: a quick shuffle, a pause, then several heavy steps. Ben turned the page over and wrote, "The hallway wears a coat of footsteps."

3. He lifted the poem so Mina could read it. She smiled. Other children continued through the hallway while Ben and Mina remained beside the step.

### Verse retelling, first person

My paper faces down.  
Its ending waits for words.  
Mina stops beside the step.  

I name the space I cannot fill.  
She points toward the doorway,  
keeps her hands beside her,  
waits.  

A shuffle,  
a pause,  
heavy footsteps in the hall.  

I turn my page.  
The hallway wears a coat of footsteps.  
I write the line  
and lift the paper.  
Mina smiles.
