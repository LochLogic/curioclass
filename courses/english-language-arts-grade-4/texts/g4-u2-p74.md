## Text

### Paragraph 1

A hagfish has glands along its sides. Glands are body parts that produce and release substances. When a fish predator tries to grasp it, glands can release slime through pores. Pores are small openings in the skin. The slime enters the predator's mouth and gill spaces. The hagfish does not swell its stomach to make this defense.

### Paragraph 2: listening transcript

Slime in the gill spaces can interfere with water flow. In filmed encounters, predators pulled away after receiving slime, leaving the hagfish free. This does not prove that every encounter ends in escape. A photograph can preserve a moment. A labeled diagram can trace where the slime travels. This observation scene is invented for practice. A telephoto lens records the distant fish without moving the camera closer.
