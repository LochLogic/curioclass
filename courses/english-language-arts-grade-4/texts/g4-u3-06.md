### Canal Timeline, 1808 to 1825

Read downward from earlier events to later ones. Gaps between rows do not represent equal lengths of time.

| Date | Event | What changed? |
|---|---|---|
| 1808 | New York authorizes investigation of possible canal routes. | Surveyors study a proposed connection; construction has not begun. |
| 1816 | Citizens present the canal memorial drafted by DeWitt Clinton. | Supporters offer reasons and estimates to persuade the legislature. |
| April 15, 1817 | New York authorizes construction of the Erie and Champlain Canals. | A construction decision follows the surveys and arguments. |
| July 4, 1817 | Ceremonial digging takes place at Rome. | Work on the original Erie Canal begins that summer. |
| October 22, 1819 | The section between Rome and Utica opens. | Boats can use a completed part while other construction continues. |
| October 1, 1823 | The Genesee Aqueduct is complete. | The Erie Canal operates from Albany to Brockport. |
| October 26, 1825 | The Erie Canal is complete. Clinton departs Buffalo with a group of boats. | The full connection between Buffalo and Albany can be traveled. |
| November 4, 1825 | Clinton pours Lake Erie water into the Atlantic Ocean in New York City. | A ceremony celebrates the lake-to-ocean connection. |

Land-rights history began before these rows and continued afterward. Canal dates do not mark the beginning or end of Haudenosaunee history. The work and its benefits also had human costs, including dangerous labor and illness.
