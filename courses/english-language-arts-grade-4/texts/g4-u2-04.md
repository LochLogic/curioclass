### About this adventure

A common octopus has a soft body and eight arms. It can change its skin's appearance, release ink and move by jetting water. In How Octopuses and Squids Change Color, Fox Meyer explains that "it can also spray ink." Different defenses can help in different situations. Camouflage can make the octopus difficult to notice. Ink and movement can help it escape after a threat approaches.

The following encounter, characters, thoughts and conversations are invented. The octopus does not actually talk to people or understand their conversation. The biological mechanisms come from the sources below. Read the opening, choose one route, and follow it to its ending. Then return to the fork and read the other route. The two endings are alternatives, not events that happen in sequence.

### 1. The fork

The pool lay between dark rocks like a window someone had left open. Beneath its bright surface, an octopus rested beside a ledge. We will call her Pip. Her arms curved along the uneven stone, and her skin looked rough and mottled against it.

Above the water, Zara knelt beside her brother Eli. Their aunt stayed with them on the viewing path.

"There!" Eli whispered. "The rock moved."

"Keep your hands out," their aunt said. "We can watch from here."

Zara looked again. What she had taken for another lump of rock had an eye. A small arm uncurled, then settled. She drew a crooked outline in her notebook.

Underwater, Pip moved toward the fork between two submerged ledges. One route followed the dark stone. The other crossed a pale, open patch toward a crevice. Beyond both, a channel connected the pool with deeper water.

A large fish moved into the channel. Its shadow slid across the opening. Pip stopped. The dark ledge lay close beside her, but the crevice offered shelter farther away. To reach it, she would have to cross the open patch.

Above her, Eli pointed without touching the water.

"Will it hide or go?" he asked.

Zara held her pencil still. The question seemed to hold the whole pool still with it.

**Choose:** To remain beside the dark ledge and rely on camouflage, go to 2A. To move toward the crevice using ink and jetting if needed, go to 2B.

### 2A. The dark ledge

Pip pressed close to the uneven stone. Her skin's pattern darkened in patches. Small projections changed its texture, and the edge of her body became difficult to distinguish from the ledge. She was still there, but the outline Zara had been drawing no longer seemed clear.

"I lost her," Zara murmured.

"She can't have gone far," Eli said.

Pip kept her arms near the rock. The fish passed the first opening, turned, and drifted back. Its shadow crossed her again. One arm tip lay against a paler patch of stone. Pip drew it closer, changing the shape of the outline.

Zara almost added another line to her drawing. Then she stopped. Her first outline had been too smooth. The animal and the rock made a broken edge that was harder to follow than the shape in her notebook.

"I'm going to leave that part unfinished," she said.

"Why?"

"Because I can't see where the rock ends."

The fish moved along the ledge. For a moment it remained so close that Zara forgot the pencil in her hand. Pip did not burst away. Her mottled body stayed against the dark background. Then the fish turned toward the open channel.

At last its shadow passed beyond the pool. The surface flashed, hiding the ledge from the children for a moment. When the view cleared, a single arm began to move again.

Go to 3A.

### 3A. Ending: An unfinished outline

After the fish had gone, Pip moved along the stone toward the shelter beneath the ledge. Her arms followed the uneven surface. The pool had not become empty when she seemed to vanish. A living animal had remained there, difficult to separate from the background.

Eli studied Zara's drawing. A blank space interrupted the outline.

"You could finish it now," he suggested.

Zara added an eye and a short curve. She left the rest open.

"That gap tells what happened," she said. "I could see her, and then I couldn't."

Their aunt waited while she wrote one final note. When the children stood to leave, Pip rested beneath the ledge. This time Zara knew where to look, but even knowing did not make every edge clear.

**End of route A. Return to 1 to explore the other choice.**

### 2B. Across the pale patch

Pip left the ledge and moved toward the crevice. Against the pale ground, her outline became easier for Zara to follow. The fish turned into the pool. It moved between Pip and the channel, close enough that the route back to the dark stone no longer lay clear.

"She's in the open now," Eli said.

Pip released ink. A dark cloud spread through the water, interrupting the children's view of her. At almost the same moment, she drew water into her mantle cavity. The muscular covering contracted, forcing water through her siphon. The jet drove her away from the approaching fish.

Zara lifted her pencil, startled by the sudden movement. The place where Pip had been was dark, but the octopus was moving beyond it. Ink had changed what could be seen. Jetting had changed where she was.

"Where did she go?" Eli asked.

"Wait," their aunt said softly.

For a moment, Zara saw only the spreading cloud. Then an arm reached the crevice. Another followed. Pip gathered her soft body toward the opening and slipped into the shelter. The fish turned through the darkened water behind her, then moved past the pale patch.

Zara followed its path with her eyes until it reached the channel. She could not tell whether the ink had distracted it or simply hidden part of the escape. She could tell that the octopus was no longer in the place where the fish had approached.

Go to 3B.

### 3B. Ending: Beyond the cloud

Later, the dark cloud thinned. Eli looked at the open patch.

"The ink didn't turn her into a rock," he said.

"No," Zara replied. "She moved while we were looking at the ink."

She drew two arrows. One showed the released cloud. The other led toward the crevice. Beside them she wrote, "Different actions, working together."

Only the curve of an arm remained visible inside the shelter. Then it drew farther back. The fish had gone through the channel, and the pool's bright window opened over the rocks again.

Zara closed her notebook carefully. The last thing she had drawn was not a complete octopus. It was a path from an exposed place to shelter, with a dark cloud beside it.

**End of route B. Return to 1 to explore the other choice.**
