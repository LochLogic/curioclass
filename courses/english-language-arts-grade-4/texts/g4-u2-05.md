## Text

### Bodies and behaviors

An animal's defense can involve both a body structure and an action. A hard covering, a sharp coat or skin that changes appearance can help in different ways. To explain a defense, connect the feature with what it allows the animal to do. A list of body parts alone leaves the mechanism unexplained.

### A covering that moves

The nine-banded armadillo has bony plates over much of its body. Jointed bands cross its middle. The hard covering helps protect it, while the bands allow bending. This armadillo cannot close completely into a ball. Its armor provides protection without making a sealed container around the entire animal.

### A coat and a change of position

The North American porcupine has quills along its back, sides and tail. When threatened, it can turn its back toward the threat and raise the quills. The quills are sharp and loosely attached. Contact can make them detach. The porcupine does not shoot them through the air. Body structure and position work together.

Climbing provides another possible response. A porcupine can use its claws to reach refuge in a tree. The sharp coat and the route upward are different defenses. An explanation should keep that distinction even when both appear in the same account.

### A soft body with other possibilities

A common octopus has no broad coat of bony plates. Its skin can change pattern and texture against a background. This camouflage can make it difficult to notice. If a threat comes closer, ink release and jetting provide other possible responses. The octopus can force water through its siphon to move away.

### Diagram: Compare the connections

| Animal | Body feature | Related action or effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nine-banded armadillo | Hard plates and jointed bands | Protection while bending |
| North American porcupine | Sharp, loosely attached quills | Back-turning presents the quills; contact can detach them |
| Common octopus | Skin structures and muscular mantle | Changing appearance; expelling water for movement |

The chart organizes part-and-function relationships. It does not show a sequence all three animals must follow. A reader uses the prose to understand the brief labels and the chart to compare the connections.

### Help, with limits

A defense offers help without promising that every encounter ends safely. Armor does not make a nine-banded armadillo into a sealed ball. Camouflage does not remove an octopus from the water. Accurate explanations preserve these limits. The central connection is between the animal's body, what it can do and how that possibility can contribute to survival.
