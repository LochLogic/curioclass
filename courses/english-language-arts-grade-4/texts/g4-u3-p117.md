### Inspect Before a Breakdown

This argument uses an invented example for practice.

Inspect the towpath regularly rather than waiting for a rope to break. Imagine a loose stone sticking into the place where a towrope passes. Each passing rope rubs against the sharp edge.

Finding and removing that stone could prevent damage before the next boat arrives. A report of one hazard does not mean that every mile is damaged. But it explains why checking only after a breakdown is too late.
