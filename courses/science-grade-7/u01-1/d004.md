# Day 4: Three explanations, one question board

## Teacher page

### Before class

Set out the card sets and a mat sheet of three mats for each group. Mark day 3's exits (s01-X3) for error E. Head chart paper "Question board". Sort the day 1 posted explanations into three: already in the powders, from the water, formed when the powders met. Print question slips.

### Safety and set-up

Paper work only; nothing is mixed. Count the cards back at the end, 2 minutes inside the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 3's exits. Error E, bubbles proving an explanation: students sort [[s01-P4-1]] alone.

**Ask.** Does the bubbling alone show a new substance? Expect: no for a, b and c, because each has a known source of bubbles; cannot tell for d. For d, expect a second thing to compare, such as the leftover liquid or solid before and after. A yes for all four is error E.

#### 5 min. Claim, evidence, reasoning

Retrieved from grade 6: a claim says what you think, evidence is an observation or reading, reasoning links them. Frame: I claim ___ because the evidence shows ___, which means ___. Model [[s01-K4-1]] on the board.

**Ask.** The sugar cannot be seen. What is the evidence that it is still there? Expect: the reading rose 3.5 g, the sugar's mass. "I cannot see it" is not evidence that it is gone.

#### 15 min. Three competing models

Groups of three draw the three explanations side by side on three mats with the cards (the gas was already in the powders; it came from the water; it formed when the powders met), as [[s01-P4-2]]. Use the day 2 drawings and the day 3 chart. Decide none: no model is marked as right, and a mat with a star is a model chosen before any evidence can pick it. Each mat has a key and the cup as its boundary.

**Ask.** What would you need to see to change your mind about your model? Expect: an observation that counts against it, such as a result the model says should not happen.

#### 10 min. Question board

Each student writes [[s01-P4-3]] on a slip and groups post them. Add the day 2 chart's questions. Add any of these the class has not asked: "Did matter disappear?", "Which combination?", "What is the gas?". Leave all questions open and unanswered. The board stays up all unit.

#### 10 min. Individual claim

Each student writes [[s01-P4-4]]: one claim our evidence so far can support, with evidence and reasoning, and one limit. Four look-fors: the claim says what can be supported, not what is proved; the evidence is an observation or a reading; the reasoning links them; one limit is named. Tell students which tier of evidence they cite: our reading, or card N1, which is invented and weaker. Collect the claims; you comment on them before day 6, which revises them.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X4]], alone, on paper. Count the cards back. The student page stays in the notebook.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, rule a line on plain paper for the boundary, and draw dark and light dots instead of cards.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards or mats: draw dots and rule a line for the cup. No printer: copy the questions onto paper.

**Skip.** Groups of three and the class board.

- **5 min. Bubbles.** Do [[s01-P4-1]].
  **Say.** "Do bubbles alone show a new substance?" Expect: no, cannot tell.
- **6 min. Claim.** Try [[s01-K4-1]], then read its answer.
  **Say.** "What is your evidence?" Expect: the reading rose 3.5 g.
- **12 min. Models.** Do [[s01-P4-2]]; mark none as right.
- **8 min. Question and claim.** Write [[s01-P4-3]], then [[s01-P4-4]].
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X4]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep two bubble cases, one of them case d. Never cut the three models, the claim or the exit. Longer: add a fourth model, such as "the gas came from the cup", and say what would count against it.

### Words and supports

Claim: what we think can be supported. Evidence: what we saw or read. Reasoning: why the evidence supports the claim. Limit: what the evidence cannot tell us. Frame: "I claim ___ because the evidence shows ___, which means ___. I cannot tell ___." Students may speak the claim first.

### Tomorrow

[[s01-X4]] tells you who treats "I cannot see it" as proof of gone (error D). If many did, day 5's warm-up opens with one supplied before-and-after record and one sentence. A second look at the same thing is not a second kind of evidence; write a comment. Day 5 compares before-and-after mass in open and flexible closed systems, starting from the day 1 reading.
