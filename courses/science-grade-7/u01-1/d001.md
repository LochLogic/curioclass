# Day 1: Where does the gas come from?

## Teacher page

### Before class

The day before, trial the scale with the cup and its 50 mL of water on it (the tray stays off). Check both labels and the safety data sheets, ask the nurse about asthma or eye sensitivities, find the eyewash or a running tap, and portion each powder, one eighth teaspoon, into its own paper twist. Clean goggles for every student. Write the unit's question on the board. Print the pages and send the family note home.

### Safety and set-up

Only the teacher mixes, at one tray, with every student in goggles at the set distance. Pour low so no dust rises: citric acid dust and splashes irritate eyes. No tasting or sniffing, nothing closed in a container, hair tied back, spills wiped at once. If powder reaches an eye, stop, rinse it for 15 minutes and send the student to the nurse. Clean-up is 3 minutes inside the safety part: waste into one cup for permitted disposal, tray wiped, cup rinsed, hands washed. Without goggles for everyone, do not mix: use card N1.

### The day

#### 15 min. Hook, demonstration and first observations

Goggles on in the first 2 minutes: strap snug, no gap. The unit's question is on the board: How can we make something new that was not there before? Students sort [[s01-P1-1]] alone in 3 minutes, to meet the notebook's two columns.

**Ask.** Which lines did someone see, and which are thoughts? Show of hands. Expect: a and c were seen; b and d are thoughts, because each says "must have". A thought under What we saw is not marked wrong: ask "Could you have seen that?"

In 4 minutes the teacher alone mixes at one tray. The cup with its water and spoon sits on the scale, the two full twists beside it. Read the scale three times aloud and show it, so the class sees its drift. Add both powders and say only what can be seen. When the fizz stops, wait a minute for the foam to settle, put the empty twists back beside the cup so only gas can leave, and read again. Students fill in [[s01-K1-1]]. Say which tier of evidence this is: our own reading. Expect a drop of 0.1 to 0.3 g. If the scale shows no change, a rise or whole grams only, say so, record it, and say aloud that card N1 (72.4 g before, 72.1 g after) is invented and stands in for the class's record. One scale, readings shared.

Groups of three fill one observation sheet in 6 minutes and post one explanation for the gas on a slip as they finish. Name no gas. Expect any idea: in the powder, from the water, formed when they met. Keep the posted ideas for day 4.

#### 25 min. Year safety sheet

Read the safety sheet aloud once, in three parts of about 3 minutes.

Students write [[s01-P1-2]] alone (3 minutes). Expect: dust and splashes in the eyes, so students watch from the set distance. "Because the teacher said so" gets the question back: what does it protect?

**Ask.** Which rule protects your eyes, and from what? Expect: goggles over the eyes, against splashes and dust.

Groups finish the sheet's last lines (5 minutes): one safety rule from today and what it protects. This is the group's exit, [[s01-X1]]. The 3-minute clean-up and the stop-signal rehearsal run together, students watching from their seats. Five minutes stay as slack for goggles off and the sink.

#### 10 min. Readiness check

[[s01-R1]] and [[s01-R2]], alone, on paper, 5 minutes each, each read aloud once. This is an access look, never graded. Write A or B on the record sheet for a gap. After class, read the group sheets against the exit's look-fors; they go back on day 2. The student page stays in the notebook.

### Other rooms

Without a screen, write each reading on the board. Without a printer, copy the columns and rules onto the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No scale: use card N1, 72.4 g then 72.1 g, said to be invented. No goggles for both of you: do not mix. Use card N1 and say it is invented. No printer: copy the two columns onto paper. No screen: read the numbers aloud.

**Skip.** The group sheet and posting, and the readiness check.

- **5 min. Two columns.** Do [[s01-P1-1]] together.
  **Say.** "Which lines did you see, and which are ideas?" Expect: a and c were seen; b and d are ideas.
- **12 min. Watch.** Both wear goggles. The adult alone stirs one eighth teaspoon each of baking soda and citric acid into 50 mL of water in a cup on a scale, reads the scale before and after, and keeps dust away. No tasting or sniffing. The child writes [[s01-K1-1]].
- **10 min. Safety.** Read the safety sheet aloud and answer [[s01-P1-2]].
  **Say.** "What does the rule protect?" Expect: eyes from powder and splashes.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: sort two lines of [[s01-P1-1]] and read the safety sheet in two parts. Never cut the goggles, the readings or the exit.

### Words and supports

Observation: something you see or read from an instrument. Inference: an idea you did not see. Frame: "I saw ___, so I think ___, but I cannot tell ___." Read every printed page aloud once. Goggles go over glasses.

### Tomorrow

The sheets and readiness show who mixes seen and thought (day 2's repair group sorts them), who treats look-alike powders as one substance (error A) and who treats mixing as a new substance (error B). Day 2 retrieves grade 6 particle drawings and returns the readiness items.
