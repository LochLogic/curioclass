# Day 6: Following mass across a boundary

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print ten sets of the day 6 records. Mark day 5's exits (s01-X5) for errors C and F. Have each student's day 4 claim, with your comment, and each group's three day 4 mats at hand.

### Safety and set-up

Paper work only; nothing is mixed. Count the cards and records back at the end, 2 minutes inside the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 5's exits. Errors C and F: students do [[s01-P6-1]] alone. Say aloud that the numbers are invented.

**Ask.** Where is the gas in pouch A, and where did cup B's gas go? Expect: pouch A's gas is inside the boundary, so its reading stayed; cup B's gas crossed the open top, and the 0.5 g lower reading shows gas has mass. A line drawn round the pouch only is error C or F.

#### 10 min. Teaching first

A boundary can be drawn round more than one thing. Draw one line round a vessel, its tube and a collector. Model [[s01-K6-1]]: the vessel falls from 98.6 g to 97.9 g and the collector rises from 30.2 g to 30.9 g. Inside the bigger boundary, the combined reading is 98.6 + 30.2 = 128.8 g before and 97.9 + 30.9 = 128.8 g after, and 0.7 g moved from the vessel to the collector. Matter crossed the vessel's boundary and stayed inside the larger one. Say the collector holds the gas, for example in an absorbent, so its reading rises.

**Ask.** Do equal combined readings tell us which gas moved? Expect: no, they show only that the matter crossed; equal mass does not name a gas (error J), and two different gases could read alike. Say that these records are invented.

#### 15 min. Groups

Groups of three do [[s01-P6-2]] on card 1 (3 minutes): the combined readings and the grams that moved. Then each student draws the larger boundary, a line round the cup and a collector box, in pencil on one of the group's three day 4 mats (6 minutes). The group fills the test grid, [[s01-P6-3]], card 2 (5 minutes): for each explanation and each record, fits or cannot tell. Expect every cell to fit, because each explanation can account for each reading. A cell saying does not fit treats mass as if it could rule an explanation out, which is error J.

**Ask.** Can mass records alone name where the gas came from? Expect: no; all three explanations stay possible. Allow 1 minute.

#### 12 min. Class claim

Return the day 4 claims first; students take 2 minutes to read the comment, then revise the claim as [[s01-P6-4]] with the mass evidence. Frame: I claim ___ because the evidence shows ___, which means ___. Four look-fors: the claim says what can be supported, not what is proved; the evidence is a reading or an observation; the reasoning links them; one limit is named.

**Ask.** Where did the mass go, and how do you know it did not vanish? Expect: it crossed the boundary into the air or the collector; the combined reading before and after is equal, or the lower open reading is matched by what left.

#### 3 min. Board

Answer "Did matter disappear?" with its limit: matter did not disappear, it crossed a boundary, and mass cannot name the gas. Leave "What is the gas?" and "Which combination?" open. Post new questions, such as which properties could tell substances apart.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X6]], alone, on paper. Count the cards and records back. The student page stays in the notebook.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, write the two cards on the board and students draw the boundary on notebook paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No mats or cards: draw a vessel, a tube and a collector on paper. No printer: copy the numbers.

**Skip.** Groups of three and the class discussion.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Do [[s01-P6-1]].
- **8 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K6-1]], then read its answer.
  **Say.** "What did the combined reading do?" Expect: it stayed the same.
- **12 min. Cards.** Do [[s01-P6-2]], then [[s01-P6-3]].
  **Say.** "Can mass name the gas?" Expect: no, it only shows what crossed.
- **6 min. Claim.** Write [[s01-P6-4]].
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X6]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: fill one row of the grid, not three. Never cut the combined-reading example, the "mass cannot name the gas" step or the exit. Longer: invent a second vessel-and-collector record with a different leak and say where the missing grams went.

### Words and supports

Combined reading: the two readings added. Cross: pass from inside a boundary to outside it. Limit: what the evidence cannot tell us. Frame: "The combined reading stayed ___, so ___ crossed the boundary, but we cannot tell ___." Read the cards aloud once. Students may answer orally.

### Tomorrow

[[s01-X6]] tells you who says gas has no mass (error C) or that the readings name the gas (error J). Error J, 3 minutes: day 7's warm-up opens with two coded closed cases of equal mass and different gases: what else must be compared? Error C, 2 minutes: one record and one sentence on where the mass went. Day 7, in the next section, compares bath-bomb ingredient properties and decides which tests could identify a substance.
