# Section 1.1 opener: where does the gas come from?

## What the section does

Six days of 50 minutes. The class meets the unit's compelling question with a teacher-only bath-bomb demonstration, records what it sees apart from what it thinks, and posts three explanations for the gas. No gas is named in this section. Days 2 and 4 build particle drawings and a question board. Day 3 finishes the safety rehearsal. Days 5 and 6 follow mass across a boundary, with a real reading from day 1 and invented records. Day 1 ends with two readiness items.

## How the days run

Day 1: hook, safety sheet, readiness. Day 2: repair from readiness, particle drawings, questions a mass reading can answer. Day 3: goggle fitting, scenario cards, tests that could count against a claim. Day 4: claim, evidence, reasoning, three competing models, the question board. Day 5: the words open system, flexible closed system and boundary, then four mass records. Day 6: following mass across a boundary, testing the three models. Each day ends with an exit of about 5 minutes. Set-up and clean-up are inside the 50 minutes.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Evidence is what we observe and measure.** What we saw and what we think go in separate columns from day 1.
- **A reading changes only when something crosses the boundary.** An open cup can lose mass to the air. A closed flexible pouch keeps everything inside, so its reading stays the same.
- **Gas has mass.** Equal readings in a closed pouch do not show that gas weighs nothing. The closed-pouch numbers on paper are tidied: a real puffed pouch pushes aside more air and reads a little lower on a 0.1 g scale, so none is run live. A collector holds the gas, for example in an absorbent, so its reading rises.
- **Mass records do not name the gas.** Equal combined readings show where mass went, not what it is.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Color tells them apart." Lay two white powders beside their solubility record.
- **B:** "Mixing makes a new substance." A supplied dissolve-and-recover record shows the salt returns.
- **C:** "Gas has no mass." An open-cup record beside a closed-pouch record.
- **D:** "It vanished, so it is gone." Count what is inside and outside the boundary.
- **E:** "Bubbles prove a reaction." Sort four supplied bubble cases.
- **F:** "A closed system that makes gas loses mass." Draw the boundary round pouch and gas.
- **J:** "Equal mass identifies the gas." Two coded closed cases with equal mass.

Wrong answers are voiced by "someone might say", never by a named student.

## Supplies for the section

Splash goggles (one cleaned pair for each student), scale reading to a tenth of a gram with a capacity of at least 300 g, tray, 250 mL plastic cup, water, baking soda and citric acid (household stand-ins, one eighth teaspoon each in paper twists), plastic spoon, particle cards and model mat sheets, goggle wipes or a soap and water tub, chart paper, and the printed sheets in this folder. Safety: the teacher alone mixes, at one tray; everyone wears splash goggles and sits at the set distance; no tasting or sniffing; hair tied back; spills wiped; waste collected for permitted disposal; hands washed after. Nothing is closed in a container.

## A room with nothing

Use the constructed records (record-card-day1, records-day5, records-day6). They are invented for practice, not observations, and are said aloud to be invented every time.
