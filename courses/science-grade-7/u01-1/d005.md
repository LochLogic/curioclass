# Day 5: Does the reading change?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print ten sets of the four record cards and cut them apart. Have the class's day 1 reading ready as record 1, or card N1. Mark day 4's exits (s01-X4) for error D. Read the day 4 claims against the four look-fors and write one comment on each, for example "Which part is a reading?"; they go back on day 6. Open the question board.

### Safety and set-up

Paper work only; nothing is mixed, and no closed container is run, so no scale is needed today. Collect the record sets at the end, 2 minutes inside the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 4's exits. Error D, matter treated as gone: students do [[s01-P5-1]] alone.

**Ask.** If 4 of 10 particles are left in the cup, where are the other 6, and is any matter gone? Expect: they left the cup with the gas, and no matter is gone; it moved.

#### 8 min. Words and boundary

Teach three words. A boundary is the line round what we count. An open system is open to the air, so matter can cross the boundary. A flexible closed system is a sealed pouch whose walls stretch and let nothing in or out. A reading to 0.1 g is the mass of everything inside the boundary. Model [[s01-K5-1]]: the open cup reads 141.5 g and then 141.0 g, so 0.5 g crossed the open top as gas.

**Ask.** What does a lower reading tell us about an open cup? Expect: something crossed the boundary, so gas has mass. "The mass is gone" is error D.

Now seal the same mix in a flexible pouch that stretches. The pouch puffs and the reading stays 141.5 g, because the gas is still inside the boundary. These numbers are invented.

Say why no closed trial is run today. The closed numbers are tidied: a real puffed pouch pushes aside more air and reads a little lower on a 0.1 g scale, so do not run it live. A live run would teach error C, that gas has no mass. All closed readings today are invented, said aloud to be invented. Name the three tiers of evidence: our own reading, a supplied record, an invented record, which is weaker.

#### 22 min. Records

Groups of three work the four records in order, each student writing an answer: [[s01-P5-2]], [[s01-P5-3]], [[s01-P5-4]] and [[s01-P5-5]]. Record 1 is the class's day 1 reading, or card N1. For each: what is inside the boundary, did anything cross it, and what does the reading show? Walk the rows. A closed reading that stays the same is read as "gas weighs nothing" (error C); a closed pouch that is said to lose mass is error F.

**Ask.** Why did the leaking pouch fall when the closed pouch did not? Expect: the hole let gas cross the boundary; the closed pouch kept all its gas inside.

#### 10 min. Class

**Ask.** What can mass readings show, and what can they not? Expect: whether matter crossed the boundary; not which gas it is, and an unchanged closed reading does not rule out trapped gas. Add to the board: "Did matter disappear?" has a partial answer. Gas identity stays open; bubbles alone are not proof of a new substance.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X5]], alone, on paper. This pouch is not one of today's records. Collect the sets. The student page stays in the notebook.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, write the four records on the board and students copy the numbers.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the numbers onto paper. No class reading: use card N1, 72.4 g then 72.1 g, said to be invented.

**Skip.** Groups of three and the class discussion.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Do [[s01-P5-1]].
- **6 min. Words.** Read the three words, then try [[s01-K5-1]].
  **Say.** "What does a lower reading tell us?" Expect: something left the cup.
- **18 min. Records.** Do [[s01-P5-2]] to [[s01-P5-5]]; say that closed records are invented.
  **Say.** "Why did the leaking pouch fall?" Expect: the hole let gas out.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X5]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: records 2 and 4 only, with a pair sharing 3. Never cut the open and closed contrast, the leaking pouch or the exit. Longer: invent a fifth record that shows trapped gas, and say what reading would expose it.

### Words and supports

Boundary: the line round what we count. Open system: open to the air. Flexible closed system: a sealed pouch with stretching walls. Frame: "Inside the boundary there is ___. Nothing crossed / ___ crossed, so the reading ___." Read the cards aloud once. Students may answer orally.

### Tomorrow

[[s01-X5]] tells you who says equal readings show gas has no mass (error C) and who says a closed pouch that makes gas should read lower (error F). Day 6's warm-up repairs both: an open-cup record beside a closed-pouch record, then a boundary drawn round pouch and gas. Day 6 follows mass across a boundary between a vessel and a collector.
