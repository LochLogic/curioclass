# Day 2: Drawing what we cannot see

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut the particle cards (24 to a set, ten sets) and count them. Print ten mat sheets and cut each into its three mats. Sort day 1's readiness sheets by A and B. Read the group sheets for seen against thought.

### Safety and set-up

Paper work only; nothing is mixed. Collecting cards and mats takes 2 minutes inside the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Grade 6 retrieval. Students draw [[s01-P2-1]] alone in 3 minutes, then compare in pairs for 2.

**Ask.** Where did the water go when it dried? Expect: the dots are spread out in the air above the cup, not gone; warm water has longer arrows, not dots farther apart. A box with no dots after drying is error D, matter treated as destroyed: fix it in the drawing comparison below.

#### 10 min. Return and repair

Give back the readiness papers and the day 1 group sheets (s01-X1). Day 1 left two gaps: look-alike powders (error A) and mixing as a new substance (error B). If more than a third missed one, teach it to the whole class; otherwise take that small group while the rest sort their day 1 group sheet lines into seen and thought. Each repair is 5 minutes.

Error A: [[s01-P2-2]]. **Ask.** Both look white. What tells them apart? Expect: how much of each dissolves, 4 g against 30 g.

Error B: [[s01-P2-3]]. **Ask.** Salt vanished in water. Is it a new substance, and what shows it? Expect: no, drying brings back crystals with the salt's own property, so it was only mixed in.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

A particle drawing is a model: a picture of what is too small to see, with a before, a during and an after, a key for each kind of dot, and a count. A dot is a part too small to see, never a grain you could pick up. Model [[s01-K2-1]] on the board.

Draw 6 dark dots for sugar in a clump at the bottom, light dots for water around them, and write the key. Redraw after stirring with the same 6 dark dots spread through the water. Write the count: 6 before, 6 after. A drawing with no dark dots after stirring claims the sugar is gone.

**Ask.** What could a before-and-after reading of the whole cup tell us about that claim? Expect: if the sugar were gone the reading would be lower; it stays the same if the sugar is still in the cup.

#### 10 min. Individual drawings

Each student has a mat; the group's cards are the key. The mat's line is the boundary: the open cup. Students draw [[s01-P2-4]]: the fizz before, during and after, with a count of particles inside the cup before and after. No atom words, no gas named. Walk the rows for a key and a count; a picture of bubbles with neither gets the question: what does each dot stand for? Draw on the mat, not on the student page, then copy the drawing into the notebook in the last 2 minutes.

#### 7 min. Compare in threes

**Ask.** What do the three drawings share, where do they differ, and which differences could mass settle? Expect: all show powder and water in the cup; they differ on whether particles leave the cup, and a mass reading could show that. Students finish [[s01-P2-5]].

#### 5 min. Class questions

Chart the questions a before-and-after mass reading could answer. This chart seeds the question board on day 4.

**Ask.** Which of our questions could a mass reading answer, and which could it not? Expect: "Did anything leave the cup?" yes; "What is the gas called?" no, because a reading gives grams, not a name.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X2]], alone, on paper. Collect it, then the cards and mats. The student page stays in the notebook.

### Other rooms

Without a printer, rule a line down plain paper for the boundary. Without card sets, draw dark and light dots.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards or mats: draw dark and light dots on paper and rule a line for the cup. No printer: copy the questions onto paper.

**Skip.** Groups of three and the class chart.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Do [[s01-P2-1]].
  **Say.** "Where did the water go?" Expect: spread out in the air, not gone.
- **8 min. Repair.** Do [[s01-P2-2]] and [[s01-P2-3]].
- **8 min. Sugar.** Try [[s01-K2-1]] before reading its answer.
- **10 min. Draw.** Do [[s01-P2-4]] on a sheet with a line for the cup.
  **Say.** "What could a mass reading tell us?" Expect: whether anything left the cup.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X2]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one repair item, not both. Never cut the sugar example, the count or the exit.

### Words and supports

Particle: a part too small to see. Model: a picture that stands for what we cannot see. Boundary: the line round what we are counting. Frame: "In my drawing, ___ stands for ___."

### Tomorrow

Return [[s01-X2]] on day 3. If many wrote that nothing is left so the matter is gone (error D), or that gas weighs nothing so the mass will not change (error C), day 3's Return and repair takes it, 3 minutes for C and 2 for D. Day 3 finishes the safety rehearsal and plans tests.
