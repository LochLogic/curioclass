# Section 1.3 opener: From bars to comparison diagrams

## The mathematical work

Review difference by aligning equal units at zero. The shared length has a partner
on the other bar; the difference is the length beyond it. On day 13 turn the paper
strips together with the grid still visible. Label both lengths and the gap. Withdraw
the grid after children can show what each length represents. A tape diagram keeps
the start and the lengths when grid marks are gone.

On day 14 cover the longer whole, the shorter whole or the gap one at a time. Say each
comparison direction in its own sentence. The named owner and the comparison word
together tell which tape is longer. The question then tells where the square belongs.
On day 15 review an ending amount and known change to reconstruct a start. On day 16
withdraw the supplied tapes and leave room for children’s own drawings.

## If a first mark needs repair

For D trace both first intervals. For E circle only the unmatched length. For F
place the square before calculating. For H and K name both owners and identify the
larger quantity. For A mark only the added steps. For G rejoin the parts before
accepting a split. Use the reserved minutes on fresh stock, and file the retry with
the first paper. Hear a rotating six and log whose turn took place.

## Before the section

Prepare the unequal practice bars, labeled tapes, paper covers and separate action or
still pictures. Draw equal grid cells on scrap as a stand-in for a master. Fold or
draw parallel strips and mark a common start. Use large paper squares for any counters.
Enlarge the cells or offer a tactile strip when useful. Read all cues aloud. A child
can point or use a neutral scribe to mark their own paper.
