# Private exit ticket key



Keep this key with the teacher. Read evidence before assigning a letter.

**s04-X58.** 26 equal spaces and 27 tick marks labeled in order from 0 through 26. Look at the whole line for a constant unit, consecutive labels and both endpoints. Accept hand-drawn marks consistently aligned to grid divisions.

Shown example. Uses 26 equal grid spaces, with 0 marking the first edge and 26 marking the last edge.

Not yet seen example. Labels the final edge 25 after treating the first tick as a traveled unit.

Possible errors. Ends at 25 (error A when the work shows counts the starting tick as distance already traveled); Unequal spaces under consecutive labels (error B when the work shows changes the size of one unit).

**s04-X59.** 11 equal spaces and 12 tick marks from 86 to 97, including both endpoints. Reconstruct the interior tick marks and use the boxes to distinguish traveled spaces from boundary marks. Judge the two counts together as one reconstruction.

Shown example. Draws ten interior ticks and labels the groups of spaces and ticks 11 and 12.

Not yet seen example. Records 12 for spaces after counting the two outer ticks with the interior ticks.

Possible errors. 12 spaces (error A when the work shows counts the boundary ticks as if they were the traveled spaces); 11 tick marks for 11 spaces (error A when the work shows treats the first boundary as the first space).

**s04-X60.** On the 90 tick, six spaces right of 84 and three spaces left of 93. Judge the position relative to either reference point. Count intervals. A scribe may place the flag where the child directs.

Shown example. Marks the sixth tick to the right of 84.

Not yet seen example. Marks 89 after counting 84 first.

Possible errors. 89 (error A when the work shows counts 84 first when moving right); 91 (error A when the work shows counts 93 first when moving left).

**s04-X61.** The star stands for 92, larger than the dot at 91. Look for marks tracing spaces from the printed references or equivalent labels that establish the comparison. Either drawing or 91 < 92 with point labels works.

Shown example. Marks one space left from 92 for the dot and two right from 90 for the star, then circles the star.

Not yet seen example. Labels both points 91 because the step count for the star starts at the 90 tick.

Possible errors. Both are 91 (error A when the work shows counts 90 as one of the two rightward spaces); Dot 92 and star 91 (error A when the work shows counts each starting tick as the first movement).

**s04-X62.** About 89. Accept 88, 89 or 90 when the estimate fits the position. Look for a point interpreted just left of halfway. A midpoint mark or a reasonable estimate near halfway can show the idea. Retain the first estimate.

Shown example. Marks a halfway point and writes 89 for the dot.

Not yet seen example. Writes 84 and shows four oversized units from 80 to the dot.

Possible errors. 84 with four stretched spaces (error B when the work shows uses unequal unit intervals to reconcile the guess with the 100 endpoint); 96 with sixteen crowded spaces (error B when the work shows uses unequal unit intervals to reconcile the guess with the 100 endpoint).
