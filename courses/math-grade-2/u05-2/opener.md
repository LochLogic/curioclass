# Representations and counting

Review digit and numeral. Put a numeral, a number name and its expanded form beside the same place drawing. The spoken names refer to groups and the expanded addends are their values. Show ten equal hundred-grids as one thousand. Its expanded form has one nonzero addend. Withdraw routine physical pieces on day 79 and keep paper drawings for repairs. Use covered place clues and familiar strategies on the fluency day. Save F before feedback, then revise in day 81 Warm-up.

Review number line, tick mark, reference point, jump and distance from Unit 4. Recall Announce, locate and check (planned only). A position and an interval have different jobs. A point is a location; equal spaces measure distance. Check the unit in each space before extending to three-digit values or counting across a hundred. Use a repeated paper edge to make equal ticks if needed. Keep the calculation jumps within 100, separately from large-number counting lines.

Use label cards, place drawings, familiar facts slips, printed equal-interval lines and paper flags. A pencil mark replaces a flag. Offer large print, tactile ticks and directed marks. Have children mark every paper independently. Read name choices aloud while leaving a target numeral silent. Hear six students per Practice block, 45 seconds each plus 30 seconds to record names. Cycle through everyone by day 83.

For errors B, C and D, return to the place columns and layered labels. For K or L, show the relevant exchange before the fresh retry. For M, cover space lengths; for N, model leftward removal separately from a rightward gap. For I, rename a completed hundred. For J, trace identical jumps. For E, align the hundreds. Name the private keys in Before class.
