# End-of-unit assessment key

Keep this copy with the teacher.

Use only the brief fixed-answer items for the five-minute marking part. Keep S11 and R with the teacher.

- **s01-S1.** 5 + 4 = □; the square is 9. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Subtracts the arrival instead of combining to find the result.
- **s01-S2.** 6 + □ = 14; the square is 8. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Combines the start and the already enlarged result.
- **s01-S3.** □ + 5 = 13; the square is 8. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Adds the change to the ending group.
- **s01-S4.** 17 - 6 = □; the square is 11. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Adds removed counters to the starting group.
- **s01-S5.** 19 - □ = 12; the square is 7. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Joins the starting and remaining groups.
- **s01-S6.** □ - 4 = 11; the square is 15. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Treats the remaining quantity as the starting amount and subtracts from it.
- **s01-S7.** 7 + 3 = □; the square is 10. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Finds the difference between two parts instead of their whole.
- **s01-S8.** 8 + □ = 19; the square is 11. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Adds a part to its whole as if the two groups were separate.
- **s01-S9.** Any two positive whole-number parts with a sum of 9, for example 4 and 5.
  Assigns positive counts to both colors. parts account for exactly nine tiles
  Error G. Accepts a split without rejoining to check against nine.
- **s01-S10a.** 6; 10 + □ = 16 is one matching equation.
  Error E. Adds the two counts instead of comparing them.
- **s01-S10b.** 6
  Error E. Treats the second direction as a combined total.
- **s01-S11a.** 13
  Error H. Chooses subtraction solely from the word fewer.
- **s01-S11b.** Aligned tapes with Noor at 8 and Eli at 13; show the extra 5 on Eli’s tape.
  Common start. Noor’s tape represents 8. Eli’s tape is longer with an extra 5
  Error K. The extra is assigned to the wrong person.
- **s01-S11c.** 8 + 5 = □ or an equivalent comparison equation with Eli’s quantity unknown.
  The equation represents the comparison with the square standing for Eli’s count.
  Error H. Uses fewer as the operation cue even when finding the larger quantity.
- **s01-S11d.** Eli has Noor’s 8 with 5 more, so Eli’s whole tape is 13.
  The reason identifies Eli’s aligned shared amount and additional 5. both make Eli’s longer whole
  Error H. Uses the comparison word without connecting it to the relative lengths.
- **s01-S12.** □ + 4 = 18; the square is 14. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error H. Takes more as an instruction to add while finding the smaller owner.
- **s01-S13.** 12 + 3 = □; the square is 15. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error K. Assigns the smaller quantity to Lian and subtracts.
- **s01-S14.** □ + 6 = 20; the square is 14. Accept equivalent equations for the same unknown.
  Error F. Combines given counts without locating the smaller amount.
- **s01-R1.** 8 + 9 = 17 | 17 = 9 + 8 | 17 = 8 + 9 | 17 - 9 = 8 | 17 - 8 = 9 | 8 = 17 - 9 | 9 = 17 - 8
  Seven distinct equations beyond the supplied one. each uses the same parts 9 and 8 and total 17
  Error I. Rejects reversed addition equality.
  Error J. Retains the removed part rather than the complementary part.
- **s01-R2.** Names the removed part and identifies the other part that remains from the same total.
  Identifies either 9 removed and 8 retained or 8 removed and 9 retained, from total 17.
  Error J. Does not distinguish the removed part and the part that stays.
