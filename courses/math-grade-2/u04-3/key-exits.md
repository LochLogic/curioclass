# Private exit ticket key



Keep this key with the teacher. Read evidence before assigning a letter.

**s04-X68.** 54 to 60 to 93, right 6 then right 33. Look for both rightward arrows, labels consistent with the unit ticks, and a finish mark showing 93. The combined distance is 39.

Shown example. Draws 54 to 60 labeled 6 and 60 to 93 labeled 33.

Not yet seen example. Labels the first jump 7 after counting both of its endpoints.

Possible errors. First jump 7 (error A when the work shows counts both endpoints as traveled spaces); Only 0 to 39 (error E when the work shows leaves out the first addend).

**s04-X69.** 17 dots went dark. The distance between 98 and 81 is 17. Look for a labeled move or bracket between the known counts. If the child counts up, the label must identify the gap as the number that went dark.

Shown example. Marks 98 to 81 with a left arrow labeled 17 dots.

Not yet seen example. Copies the number still lit onto the change arrow.

Possible errors. 18 dots (error A when the work shows counts both ends); 81 dots (error H when the work shows copies the number still glowing onto the change arrow).

**s04-X70.** 66 portraits are up, 89 fit altogether, and the jump counts room for 23 more. Look for labels that associate each known quantity with its endpoint and the remaining space with the distance. Short words, drawing marks or a scribe are sufficient.

Shown example. Labels the tail “up,” the tip “fit,” and writes 23 by the arc for the space left.

Not yet seen example. Writes 89 on the arc for the space left, using the tip as the distance.

Possible errors. 89 spaces left (error D when the work shows uses the larger endpoint as the distance); 66 spaces left (error H when the work shows copies the number already hung into the unknown).

**s04-X71.** 97. For example, 74 + 3 = 77 and 77 + 20 = 97. The note’s claim is one short. Look for an original route with matching written steps and a clear correction or a mark that rejects the note. Accept any valid route.

Shown example. Draws 74 to 77 to 97 and records matching steps that correct the claim.

Not yet seen example. Counts the 74 tick as the first of three spaces, then adds 20 to reach 96.

Possible errors. 96 (error A when the work shows counts the starting tick in the three-space move); 79 (error G when the work shows moves two ones instead of two tens).

**s04-X72.** A story with 70 at first, 25 more, then 9 fewer so 86 remain. Look for one actor, both changes in order, and marks tied to both arcs. Quick sketches, short labels or neutral scribing with read-back all work.

Shown example. Mika made 70 prints, printed 25 more, then recycled 9. She kept 86. The arcs are labeled +25 and −9.

Not yet seen example. Tells only the increase and stops with 95 prints.

Possible errors. Stops with 95 prints (error I when the work shows omits the second change); Adds nine on the leftward jump (error F when the work shows uses an increase for a leftward jump).
