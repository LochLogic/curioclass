# Closing records key

Keep this copy with the teacher.

- **s02-E-add.** 70 + 13 = 83. In both orders the parts are the same. Trade 10 of the 13 ones for one ten, leaving 8 tens and 3 ones.
  Drawing and equations account for the same tens and ones in both orders. Explains why changing the order and grouping preserves the sum.
  Examples: Seven tens and thirteen ones are eight tens and three ones. Both orders use the same parts. / 83 because I carried.
  Error A. 73. Drops the composed ten from 70 + 13.
  Error H. 83. Gives only a result.

- **s02-T.** 48 + 6 = □ gives 54, then 54 − 7 = □ gives 47. Check: 47 + 7 = 54 and 54 − 6 = 48.
  Labels both action tapes and writes an unknown equation for each step. Uses the first result as the next start and gives a check.
  Examples: Shows 48 + 6 = □, 54 − 7 = □, 47 left, and 47 + 7 = 54 with both actions labeled. / Shows an arrival, labels 54 left and stops.
  Error F. 54. Stops after the arrival.

- **s02-M.** (24 + 26) + (38 + 32) = 50 + 70 = 120. An equivalent valid grouping or tens-and-ones strategy fits.
  Records each original two-digit addend once and shows a useful valid grouping.
  Examples: 24 and 26 make 50, 38 and 32 make 70, for 120. / Adds only 24, 38 and 26 and writes 88.
  Error G. 88. Omits 32 star cards.

- **s02-E-sub.** 72 = 60 + 12; 60 − 40 = 20 and 12 − 6 = 6, so 26. Adding on, 46 + 4 + 20 + 2 = 72. This adds the 26 that subtraction leaves.
  Shows 6 tens and 12 ones preserve 72 and subtracts 46 correctly. Explains why the amount added to 46 equals the part left by subtraction.
  Examples: One of seven tens becomes ten ones, leaving six tens and twelve ones. Adding 26 to 46 makes 72 again. / I subtract the smaller digit.
  Error B. 34. Subtracts each smaller digit from the larger digit.
  Error C. 36. Keeps seven tens after trading one.
  Error H. 26. Gives a result with no reason about preserved value.

- **s02-S9.** One possible split is 16 and 20, since 16 + 20 = 36. Any two positive whole-number parts totaling 36 fit.
  Marks the divider between seats, labels two positive groups and gives an equation conserving all 36 seats.
  Examples: Marks after seat 16, labels 16 and 20, and writes 16 + 20 = 36. / Labels 16 and 19 as all 36.
  Error I. 16 and 19, totaling 35. Loses one of the original seats.
