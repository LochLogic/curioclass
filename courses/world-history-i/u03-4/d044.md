# Day 44: Trade, faith or the sword?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Return each folder: the map (s03-P42-2-v3, s03-P43-2) with written feedback, and clipped to it the warm-up arrow s03-P43-1, the timeline bands s03-P43-3 and the day 43 exit s03-X43-v2. Print the student page and the day 44 source sheet. Head three board columns: trade, faith, sword.

### The day

#### 5 min. Read feedback

Students read the feedback and their day 43 exit, then do [[s03-P44-1]] alone.

#### 8 min. Map revision

[[s03-P44-2]], alone. A student with an error H miss first redraws the two bands on the back of the day 43 ticket (3 minutes); a K miss sorts made here from brought here. Walk the room and confer with H or K misses first. Keep the first version; label each revision. An alliance no source records gets a dated note, never an invention. For option b, prompt: "What does Kilwa's coral-stone mosque show that Source C's 'wood' does not?"

#### 15 min. Discussion: trade, faith or the sword

[[s03-P44-3]]. Read Sources F and G aloud. Round one, in pairs: each speaker gives one sourced detail and a unit word for the kind of power (dynasty, empire, kingdom). Round two: answer a detail with a detail from another source. Then brief class contributions; record each detail and its source letter in the columns. Argue from sources, never in character.

**Ask.** Does this detail show power won, or power kept? Expect: E shows winning; A, D, F and G mostly show keeping; a strong claim can say the answer changes over time.

Expect Source J, Mongke's letter, to join faith and the sword: Heaven's one lord on earth, and war for those who refuse. Source G is a visitor's verdict on Mali's faith, not the people's own. Source A shows armed enslaved men at court; Source C records raids on inland neighbors, and raids like these often took captives, but the passage does not say so. Their own voices are missing. Read Source E's killing plainly, with no total. For error D, keep harms and gains in two columns: gains from trade do not erase the killing.

#### 17 min. Closing claim

[[s03-W44]], alone on lined paper, with the board, the sheets and the Chinggis-to-Khubilai card on the day 44 source sheet. Two frames on Side 2 help students who need them. Students weigh all three for one African ruler and the Mongol rulers, explain details rather than list them, and answer a counterclaim with evidence. Confer without supplying sentences.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s03-X44-v2]], alone, on paper. Collect tickets, claims and folders; the folders stay with you.

After class, score A2 from the folders. Write six feedback lines on each claim: the weighing, two explained details, the counterclaim, the limit, Chinggis to Khubilai, and the Silk Roads consequence with harms kept. No claim score today. Record the exit's three look-fors.

### Other rooms

Without a printer, read the sheets aloud and draw the three columns on paper. Students keep their own map choices and reasoning.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer or board: read the sheets aloud and draw three columns on paper.

**Skip.** Class contributions after the pairs.

- **4 min. Feedback.** Read the map feedback and the clipped ticket together.
- **8 min. Revision.** [[s03-P44-2]], alone. Keep the first version.
- **8 min. Discussion.** Read Sources F and G aloud. The parent is the partner, replying from another source.
  **Say.** "Was power won mainly by trade, faith or the sword?" Expect: a sourced detail and reply; trade gains do not erase killing or forced work.
- **10 min. Claim.** [[s03-W44]], alone on lined paper. Tonight, write six short feedback lines (the weighing, two details, the counterclaim, the limit, Chinggis to Khubilai, the Silk Roads with harms kept).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X44-v2]], alone. Keep it for day 46.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip class contributions after the pairs. Longer: test the claim against another option's source limit.

### Words and supports

Dynasty: a ruling family. Empire: rule over many peoples. Faith: religious belief. Counterclaim: the strongest answer against yours. When you read aloud, read each translator's word once, say what it means and why we do not use it, then use its substitute from the opener's list. The printed text stays as printed. Students may dictate. No student speaks for Mali, the Swahili coast or Mongolia.
