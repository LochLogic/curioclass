# Day 44: Trade, faith or the sword?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Return the map product, s03-P42-2 and s03-P43-2, with written feedback and day 43's exit, s03-X43, clipped to it. Set out folders, sources, lined paper and rulers. Print pages; open the answer key and record sheet. Head three board columns: trade, faith, sword.

### The day

#### 5 min. Read feedback

Students read map feedback and day 43's exit, then complete [[s03-P44-1]] alone. Locate the questioned content. If more than a third showed error H yesterday, feedback directs separate dated timeline bands; for error K, distinguish imported objects from African construction and rule. Confer individually otherwise.

#### 10 min. Map revision

[[s03-P44-2]], alone. Students revise dates, explanations and links using their option sheets. Keep first versions and feedback in the folder; label revisions. An undocumented alliance needs an absence note, never an invention. Retain schematic labels. Check substance against feedback without marks. The revised map remains practice until scored as A2 on day 45.

#### 15 min. Discussion: trade, faith or the sword

[[s03-P44-3]]. Read Sources F and G aloud, then revisit selected details on the day 42 sheets. Name maker, date, audience and transmission: Rubruck reports Mongke's words through an interpreter to King Louis IX; Ibn Battuta dictated in 1355 for readers at home. Polo spoke through Rustichello; Ibn al-Athir heard from people fleeing events.

**Ask.** Was a ruler's power in this age won mainly by trade, by faith or by the sword? Accept different weightings supported by evidence. In pairs, round one: each speaker names one source and detail; round two: reply using another source. Then take brief class contributions. Record details and source letters in three columns; details may connect columns. Argue from sources, never in character.

Expect Source F's account of different religious paths, Source G's prayer and justice, Source B's food exchange, and Source D's enforced currency. These show reported practices, not universal consent. Source A includes musicians and enslaved armed guards; Source C connects Kilwa's ruler with raids taking people. Recall enslaved salt miners at Taghaza without opening new sources. Their own voices are missing. Africans built and ruled these states. Kilwa's surviving mosque and palace are coral stone despite Source C's wooden buildings.

Read Source E's killing of women, men and children plainly, without a death total. For error D, keep harms and gains separate; trade gains cannot make up for conquest. Use the option d fact card to distinguish Chinggis's conquests, later khanates and Khubilai's Yuan government. Source D supports an inference about currency helping exchange under coercion, not proof of safety on every road.

#### 15 min. Closing claim

[[s03-W44]], alone on lined paper. Keep sources and the board available. Students weigh all three forms of power for an African ruler and the Mongol rulers, explaining evidence rather than listing details. Preserve conquest's harms when explaining a Silk Roads consequence. A counterclaim needs an evidence-based answer; an outsider's access or transmission can limit a claim. Confer without supplying sentences. Collect claims and folders, including revisions.

After class, write feedback on weighing, two explained details, the counterclaim, the source limit, Chinggis-to-Khubilai change and the Silk Roads consequence with harms retained. Give no claim score or mark today. Return it for five minutes of revision on day 45 before scoring in A2d.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s03-X44]], alone on paper. Keep this invented case unseen until now. Collect separately; record its three look-fors after class. Keep tickets for day 46.

### Other rooms

Without a printer, share handwritten sources and copy the frame onto paper. Use a paper chart instead of the board. Read sources aloud; retain independent map decisions and claim reasoning.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer or board: share handwritten sources and draw three columns on paper.

**Skip.** Class rounds and repeated contributions.

- **4 min. Feedback.** Read yesterday's map feedback and clipped exit together.
- **8 min. Revision.** Complete [[s03-P44-2]] alone. Keep the first version; give no marks.
- **8 min. Discussion.** Read Sources F and G aloud; compare day 42 sources. Be the partner, using another source.
  **Say.** "Was power won mainly by trade, faith or the sword?" Expect: a sourced detail and reply; trade gains do not erase killing or forced work.
- **10 min. Claim.** [[s03-W44]], alone on lined paper. Write feedback afterwards on evidence, counterclaim, limits, the change in Mongol rule and trade consequences. Keep it for tomorrow's revision.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X44]], alone. Keep for day 46.

### Shorter or longer

Shorten repeated class contributions, retaining both discussion rounds, map revision, claim and exit. Extend by testing the claim against another option's source limit.

### Words and supports

Dynasty names a ruling family; empire names rule over several peoples. The words can overlap. Gloss faith, coercion and counterclaim. Use Side 2's frame. Read glosses without repeating translators' labels in our speech. Accept dictation; distinguish language needs from reasoning gaps. Mali, Timbuktu, the Swahili coast, Shona communities and Mongolia are living places and peoples; nobody speaks for them.

### Tomorrow

Day 45 begins with five minutes to revise s03-W44 from written feedback, then scores that revised claim in A2d and the revised map as A2. The end-of-unit assessment follows, then marking. Put error D's harms-and-gains repair in claim feedback. Error I on today's exit treats wealth as everyone's gain; retain s03-X44 for day 46's feedback and response group, for the class if more than a third miss it.
