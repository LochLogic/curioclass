# Day 43: Mapping power, part 2

## Teacher page

### Before class

Return day 42's exit, s03-X42. Set out folders, rulers, paper and all three option sheets. Print pages; open the answer key and record sheet. Prepare the parallel timeline, or draw parallel board rows if unavailable.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Students complete [[s03-P43-1]] alone on day 42's exit, labelled invented for practice; then explain to a partner.

**Ask.** Who works, and who carries a cost? Expect a producer and a cost of carrying or paying the crossing tax. Error I gives the river alone as the explanation. If more than a third missed this yesterday, trace producer, carrier and tax together; otherwise confer during map work. Keep the repair in the folder.

#### 5 min. Teaching first

Together, reread excerpts aloud, naming maker, date and audience. These outsiders addressed readers at home; Ibn Battuta dictated in 1355, Polo spoke through Rustichello, and Ibn al-Athir heard from people fleeing events.

**Ask.** Which link to another region does your source record, and which does it not? Expect Source C's gold route; no alliance there. The Mongol card records an alliance sought, not achieved. Distinguish traded goods from ideas, practices and people's movement.

Model culture beyond rulers: Source A's musicians; option b's Swahili language and Shona builders; Source D's merchants using written currency, an inference about material culture, not evidence of other Yuan arts. Name missing evidence. For error N, list contributors before explaining the ruler's role.

#### 22 min. Alone: the map, part 2

[[s03-P43-2]]

Continue day 42's map, adding explanations on Side 1. Use two named sources; distinguish fact cards from eyewitnesses. Every link needs evidence or an absence note. Keep dated layers, centres, route arrows and a key; label schematics *not to scale*. Invented distances or borders are error B.

Confer using the look-fors without marks. For error K, distinguish imported goods from local construction: Africans built and ruled these states. Contrast Source C's wooden buildings with Kilwa's surviving coral-stone mosque and palace. Name enslaved armed guards in Mali and people taken in Kilwa's raids; their own voices are missing. Source E records killing women, men and children, without a supported total. Trade gains cannot make up for conquest.

#### 8 min. Pairs: share

Partners from different options swap maps and source sheets, writing one question about a link or date in the partner-question box. Return maps; authors point to evidence or note its absence. Investigate sources, never in character.

#### 5 min. Timeline

[[s03-P43-3]]

Draw separate Mali and Yuan bands on Side 2 and the class parallel timeline. Cards give Mali's beginning about 1235 and Yuan 1271 to 1368. Label Mali's end as not supplied; mark documented overlap in the 1300s.

**Ask.** Does sharing years make two states one? Expect separate states can exist simultaneously. For error H, align the bands on one year scale, keeping separate labels.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s03-X43]], alone on paper. Students draw the timeline and explain beneath it. Collect tickets and folders, including both map parts and partner questions.

After class, record the exit's three look-fors. Write map feedback, one line each: dated geography, politics, economy, culture, trade, alliance, war, exchange and a source limit. Give no map score today. Accept honest absence notes.

The map remains practice through day 44's revision, then is scored as A2 on day 45. Day 44's closing claim gets written feedback that evening and five minutes of revision on day 45 before scoring in A2d.

### Other rooms

Without a printer, copy the frame and share handwritten sources. Plain paper replaces squared paper; a paper timeline replaces the wall. Read chosen sources aloud before independent work.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: share handwritten sources and work on paper. No squared paper: use plain paper. No wall timeline: draw parallel rows.

**Skip.** Class sharing and unchosen source readings.

- **4 min. Warm-up.** Use yesterday's ticket for [[s03-P43-1]].
  **Say.** "Who works, and who carries a cost?" Expect: a producer and carrying effort or tax.
- **5 min. Sources.** Read the chosen option's sources and card aloud. Name who wrote, when and for whom.
  **Say.** "Which link is recorded?" Expect: supported trade or warfare; an absent alliance stays absent.
- **16 min. Map.** Complete [[s03-P43-2]] alone. Ask about one date. Keep written feedback for tomorrow; give no marks.
- **5 min. Timeline.** Draw [[s03-P43-3]] together. Leave Mali's end unspecified.
  **Say.** "Are these one state?" Expect: separate states sharing years.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X43]], alone. Keep ticket and map for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer

Shorten repeated sharing, keeping each partner question, map work, timeline and exit. Extend: check another option's link and its evidential limit.

### Words and supports

Alliance: agreement between powers. Exchange includes ideas, practices and people. Culture includes language, music and material practices. Use Side 1's frame; distinguish language from reasoning through dictation. Gloss translators' words inside sources. Mali, Timbuktu, the Swahili coast, the Shona and Mongolia are living places and peoples; no student speaks for them.

### Tomorrow

Day 44 returns the map with written feedback and day 43's exit clipped to it: five minutes to read, ten to revise. Error H merges states or misaligns dates; error K treats imports as foreign founders. If more than a third miss either, put the repair in map feedback: redraw separate bands or distinguish local building from imported goods. Then discuss trade, faith or the sword from sources and write the closing claim. Write feedback on the claim after class.
