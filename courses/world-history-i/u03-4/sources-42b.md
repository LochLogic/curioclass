# Days 42 to 44 sources, option b: the Swahili city-states, with Axum before them

Keep this sheet in your folder until day 44; the option a and d sheets are there for links to other regions. Ibn
Battuta was an outsider writing for readers at home, and his 1929 translator's words stay as printed. In your own
writing, name people as people.

**Fact card (ours).** Axum, in today's northern Ethiopia and Eritrea, was a kingdom of the first centuries CE to
about 900, trading through its Red Sea port of Adulis and minting its own coins; its king Ezana became Christian in
the 300s. It is the earlier comparison. The Swahili towns of about 1000 to 1500, among them Mogadishu, Mombasa,
Malindi, Kilwa and Sofala, stood along the Indian Ocean coast, each under its own ruler. Swahili is a Bantu language,
spoken today, with many Arabic words. Gold from the interior, where the builders of Great Zimbabwe (about 1100 to
1450) were ancestors of today's Shona, reached the coast at Sofala and was traded through Kilwa; ivory went out too,
and cloth, glass beads and Chinese porcelain came in.

**Source C. Ibn Battuta, Kilwa and the gold of Sufala, about 1331.** Dictated in 1355; translated by H. A. R. Gibb
(1929), pp. 112 to 113. Public domain.

> We stayed one night in this island [Mombasa], and then pursued our journey to Kulwa, which is a large town on the
> coast. ... I was told by a merchant that the town of Sufala lies a fortnight's journey [south] from Kulwa and that
> gold dust is brought to Sufala from Yufi in the country of the Limis, which is a month's journey distant from it.
> Kulwa is a very fine and substantially built town, and all its buildings are of wood. Its inhabitants are
> constantly engaged in military expeditions, for their country is contiguous to the heathen Zanj. The sultan at the
> time of my visit was Abu'l-Muzaffar Hasan, who was noted for his gifts and generosity. He used to devote the fifth
> part of the booty made on his expeditions to pious and charitable purposes, as is prescribed in the Koran.

Gloss: *Kulwa*, Kilwa; *Sufala*, Sofala; *contiguous to*, next to; *heathen Zanj*, his word for inland peoples who
were not Muslim; *booty*, goods and people taken in raids. Kilwa's surviving mosque and palace are built of coral
stone.
