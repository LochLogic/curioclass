# Day 42: Mapping power, part 1

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print pages; lay out paper, rulers and folders. Keep all three option sheets through day 44. Prepare day 41's exit if available. Open the answer key and record sheet. Send the family note home.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 41's exit, s03-X41: overlapping power terms and a court label's viewpoint. Students sort [[s03-P42-1]] alone, then explain aloud. Its printed statements are teaching paraphrases to test against Source A. If the exit is unavailable, these supply the starting point.

**Ask.** Which statement needs evidence from people outside the court? Expect the support claim; display cannot establish consent. If many showed error L, more than a third, rehearse together; otherwise confer during map work. This page stays in the folder.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

Introduce all three options. Option b maps Swahili towns with earlier Axum for comparison. Students choose and label their folders; everyone keeps every sheet.

**Ask.** What has to be on a map before someone else can trust it? Expect years, places, a key, scale information and supported routes. Separate periods into layers; avoid present-day borders, error B. Label schematic sketches *not to scale*: the cards supply no coordinates or distances.

Read Sources A, C and D aloud, then Source E without dramatic delivery. Identify maker, date and audience: outsiders addressing readers at home. Ibn Battuta dictated in 1355; Polo's account passed through Rustichello. Ibn al-Athir heard from people fleeing events he did not witness.

Name slavery: Source A's armed guards were enslaved people; Source C's raids took people captive. Their voices are missing. Source E records killing women, men and children; give no total. Trade gains never make up for conquest. Contrast Source C's wooden buildings with Kilwa's surviving coral-stone mosque and palace. Africans built and governed these African states.

#### 7 min. Worked example

[[s03-K42-1]]

Students try before the model. Sketch the pass market and label its deputy. Write *invented for practice; date not supplied*.

**Ask.** How does the label connect government to work? Expect collection for the king and copper produced and carried by workers. Read the answer key's first sample; model separating politics and economy. Use the look-fors:

Students record their version; a geographical feature alone does not explain wealth.

#### 25 min. Alone: the map, part 1

[[s03-P42-2]]

Students sketch on Side 2 with dated layers, centres, route arrows and a key. Explain politics and economy from Sources A and B, Source C and its fact card, or Sources D and E. Cards are summaries, not additional eyewitnesses. Separate evidence from inference; include workers, rulers, gains and costs. Confer using the look-fors, without scores or written marks.

Prioritize missing dates or routes; address error I by tracing work, carrying, collection and gains. Read Source B aloud to option a students; others reread their sheet. The map stays in the folder with the student. Continue on day 43; give written feedback after day 43, revision on day 44 and scoring as A2 only on day 45. Day 44's claim receives written feedback that evening and revision in day 45's first five minutes before scoring in A2d.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s03-X42]], alone on paper. Students draw their own schematic in the blank space. Collect tickets; record the three look-fors after class, without scoring the map product.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, copy the student frame and sources onto paper for shared reading; students copy only their own work. Use plain paper for schematic sketches if squared paper is unavailable.

### At home

**Swap.** No squared paper: use plain paper. No printer: read the source sheets together and write on paper.

**Skip.** Class sharing and repeated source readings for unchosen options.

- **4 min. Warm-up.** Sort [[s03-P42-1]] together.
  **Say.** "Which statement needs another voice?" Expect: support needs evidence beyond court display.
- **6 min. Read.** Choose an option. Read its sources and fact card aloud; explain difficult words.
  **Say.** "What makes a map trustworthy?" Expect: dates, places, a key, routes and honest scale information.
- **6 min. Example.** Try [[s03-K42-1]], then read its answer together. Label the date as not supplied.
  **Say.** "Who works, and who collects?" Expect: miners and carriers work; the deputy collects for the king.
- **15 min. Map.** Work alone on [[s03-P42-2]]. Use two named sources. Label the sketch not to scale. Keep it for tomorrow; give no marks today.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X42]], answered alone. Keep it for tomorrow's conversation.

### Shorter or longer

Shorten sharing, keeping sources, map time and exit. Extend by distinguishing observed and reported details in the chosen source.

### Words and supports

Politics: how power is held. Economy: work, production and exchange. Deputy: someone acting for a ruler. Read unfamiliar source words aloud and gloss them once; use enslaved people in our words. Students may dictate explanations while making their own geographic choices. Pointing to evidence before speaking helps distinguish language needs from missing reasoning. Use the printed frame on Side 2.

Mali, Timbuktu, the Swahili coast and Mongolia remain living places; the Shona are living people. Never ask students to represent them. No role play.

### Tomorrow

Return s03-X42 in day 43's warm-up. Error I omits producers and exchange, attributing wealth to the river alone. If many miss it, add a producer and a cost to an arrow together; otherwise confer during map work. Day 43 adds culture and trade, alliance, warfare and exchange links, finishes and shares maps, and adds separate Mali and Yuan bands to the parallel timeline.
