# Day 42: Mapping power, part 1

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the pages; lay out squared paper, rulers and folders. Day 41's exit ticket, s03-X41, goes back today with errors L and A marked. Open the answer key and record sheet. Send the family note home.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return s03-X41. Give a one-minute gist of Source A: Ibn Battuta, a visitor, describes the sultan's audience for readers at home. Students sort [[s03-P42-1-v2]] alone, one statement a row, then apply two words for power to Mansa Sulayman.

**Ask.** Which statement needs other evidence? Expect: "All his subjects support him" needs the voices of ordinary people of Mali; the gold and the armed enslaved men show the court's display of wealth and force. For Sulayman, expect dynasty or hereditary rule (one family) and empire (many peoples); naming only one word, or calling the words opposites, is error A. If more than a third missed error L or A on day 41, rehearse one example together; otherwise confer during map work.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

Introduce the three options (option b: the Swahili towns, with earlier Axum). Students write their choice on the folder; everyone keeps all three sheets.

**Ask.** What has to be on a map before someone else can trust it? Expect years in the title, places, a key, routes, and either a scale bar or a *not to scale* label. Journey times are not distances. Place towns from the sheet's "Where it lies" lines; never draw present-day borders, error B.

Name who made each source: visitors and a distant historian writing for readers at home (opener). Name slavery plainly: Source A's armed men were enslaved. Source C records raids on inland neighbours; raids like these often took captives, but the passage does not say so. Source E records the killing of women, men and children; give no total. Gains from trade do not erase the killing; both are recorded. Each sheet names the peoples' own histories it cannot quote.

#### 7 min. Worked example

[[s03-K42-1-v2]]

Students try first. Then model: place the pass, write "about 1300", draw the arrow from miners and carriers, and write one sentence each for politics and economy.

[[steps:s03-K42-1-v2]]

**Ask.** How does the label join government to work? Expect: the deputy collects for the king; miners and carriers produce what is taxed. Then model a second label from a real source: Source D, about 1298, Khanbaliq, an arrow from merchants to the Khan, "paper money forced on all". A river or a pass alone never explains wealth.

#### 25 min. Alone: the map, part 1

[[s03-P42-2-v3]]

Students plan on Side 2, then draw on squared paper. Read the chosen sheet now: Sources A, B and H for option a; C and I for option b; D and E for option d. The fact card is ours, not an eyewitness. Explain politics and economy from two named sources, with who worked and who gained; keep evidence apart from inference.

Confer with the look-fors, without scores, starting with students who have no date or route. For error I, trace who dug, carried, collected and gained. The map stays in the folder: finished on day 43, given written feedback, revised on day 44, then scored as A2.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

[[s03-X42-v2]], alone, on paper, with a sketch in the box. Collect the tickets; record the four look-fors after class. The map is not marked today.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, read the sheets aloud; students copy only their own work. A ruled grid on plain paper replaces squared paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No squared paper: rule a grid on plain paper. No printer: read the sheets together.

**Skip.** Sharing, and reading the options not chosen.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Sort [[s03-P42-1-v2]] together, then name two words for Sulayman.
  **Say.** "Which statement needs other voices?" Expect: support needs ordinary people of Mali, not the court.
- **6 min. Read.** Choose an option. Read its sheet aloud, glossed words first.
  **Say.** "What makes a map trustworthy?" Expect: dates, places, a key, routes and a scale bar or not to scale label.
- **6 min. Example.** Try [[s03-K42-1-v2]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "Who works, and who collects?" Expect: miners and carriers work; the deputy collects for the king.
- **15 min. Map.** Plan, then draw [[s03-P42-2-v3]] alone from two named sources. Keep it for tomorrow.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X42-v2]], answered alone. Keep it for tomorrow.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the second model label. Longer: mark each source detail as seen or heard by its writer.

### Words and supports

Politics: how power is held. Economy: work, production and exchange. Deputy: someone acting for a ruler. Gloss source words once; say "enslaved people" in our words. When you read aloud, read each translator's word once, say what it means and why we do not use it, then use its substitute from the opener's list. Students may dictate explanations while making their own map choices. Mali, the Swahili coast and Mongolia are living places; never ask students to speak for them. No role play.

### Tomorrow

Return s03-X42-v2 in day 43's warm-up, error I: wealth from the river alone, with no producer or exchange. If many missed it, add a producer and a cost to an arrow together. Day 43 adds culture and every link to other regions, shares maps, and adds separate Mali and Yuan bands to the parallel timeline.
