# Section 4.3 opener: needs and conditions

## What the section does

Five days, 69 to 73. The big question is: "What does a kind need, and what does the place give it?" Day 69 matches needs to conditions for the hare, the polar bear on the coast and a rainforest tree frog, with the wood frog as a separate comparison. Day 70 sorts three outcomes: does well, does poorly, cannot stay. Day 71 argues a cactus in a desert and in a bog, and children write item H alone. Day 72 plans a schoolyard inventory and revises item H. Day 73 visits the schoolyard spot, or uses photographs, and revises the habitat drawing. Each day ends with an exit. Day 73 holds the section exit.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Days 69, 70 and 73 end with the 5-minute weather record. On day 73 the adult prechecks the route, the weather, allergies and boundaries the day before. The class stays in sight and touches no living thing or water. Hands are cleaned at the door with fragrance-free wipes, one tub for each table. Every child may pass in public talk. The teacher hears the rotating six at each exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A habitat is a system.** Needs are on one side and conditions are on the other.
- **One place gives different outcomes to different kinds.** The same cold place suits the hare and not the rainforest frog.
- **"Does poorly" needs evidence.** Lost leaves or little fat show it. A guess does not.
- **A missing link means a kind cannot stay.** A need is not met by any condition.
- **A bull musk ox with little fat left has a greater risk.** It is not certain to die. Do not call all musk oxen poor survivors.
- **Dormancy is not poor survival.** The wood frog is a separate forest and wetland comparison. Part of its body water freezes and it thaws in spring.
- **The inventory is evidence of needs and conditions.** It is not a species list.
- **Scoring item H.** Four points: a thriving judgment from evidence, a poor-survival judgment, an inability-to-remain judgment, and arrows that explain each need and condition link.

## The errors, in plain words

- **C:** the thin bull will die. Read the card's last line and mark "greater risk".
- **F:** all kinds do equally well. Draw the missing link on the frog card.
- **G:** the tundra is too cold for anything. Point to a second kind whose needs are met.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies for the section

The need and condition cards, the outcome cards, the frame sheet, the cactus cards, the item H cards, the spot cards, the inventory sheet and clipboards (stiff card), fragrance-free wipes and the exit cards. Weather equipment on days 69, 70 and 73.

## A room with nothing

Use the cards read aloud as invented, a local photograph inventory indoors, and the dated adult weather record read aloud.
