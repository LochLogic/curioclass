# Day 101: What can we expect?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy the Lake station card for each child. Highlight a bar graph on the board with counters grouped by the key, for children who made error A on day 100. Copy the statement cards. Write the ten Dunmere spring rows on the board: 7 windy, 3 calm.

### Safety and set-up

An adult prechecks the weather; the class stays indoors in a warning; no one looks at the sun. The thermometer is non-glass, the jar plastic. Only the helper pair and the adult touch the jar; the adult empties it and handles ice. Helpers wipe hands inside the 5 minutes. Counters stay on the desk and never go near mouths.

### The day

#### 5 min. Weather record

The helper pair from the rotating six reads the thermometer to the nearest mark and the jar, 30 seconds each; the adult checks. Less than the first mark is "wet, too little to measure", never dry. Below zero is said in words. Children write [[s06-P101-6]]. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud, said to be supplied.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 100's pages, s06-P100-1, s06-P100-2, s06-P100-3 and s06-P100-4, and the exit tickets, s06-X100. Error A, 5 minutes: say the key first. Rebuild one bar with counters grouped by the key: 3 clouds is 6 days, 4 counters is 4 days only if each counter is 1 day. If many tickets showed error A, say aloud: a picture or a line is not a day; the key says what it stands for.

#### 5 min. Comparison

Review of "the record shows it, or goes beyond it". Pairs sort the eight statement cards about the ten Dunmere spring rows, [[s06-P101-1]]. Walk and listen. Card 5 is a fair usually, because seven of ten rows are windy, yet it still says more than the rows. One odd day is not a season.

#### 20 min. Product W

Alone, on paper, from the Lake station card. Read the directions once. Then do not help; point only to the card. Each child makes a table with all three measures, [[s06-P101-2]]; a bar graph of the days with rain or snow, counting by 2 and labeling both sides, [[s06-P101-3]]; a comparison of two seasons, [[s06-P101-4]]; and an expectation for one season, with how sure and what could still happen, [[s06-P101-5]]. Collect the papers. Do not score today. After class, tick the six-tick look-for checklist and add one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; it comes back on day 102.

#### 4 min. Model

The class revises the seasonal expectation model in a second color with "likely, not certain" and one limit, such as one day can differ. Say aloud: usual weather says what is likely, never what will happen. Each child may pass.

#### 3 min. Exit E101

Read the card once, invented for practice. Alone, [[s06-X101]]. Ask [[s06-X101b]] and [[s06-X101c]] aloud, 3 to 4 children each. Look for: autumn usually cool, winter usually cold; tomorrow likely cool, and a mild day could still come. Error B says tomorrow will be cool for sure. Note the letters.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Papers collected for the checklist outside class; counters counted back in.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the Lake station card on the board. No counters: use paper squares. One projector: show the statement cards.

### At home

**Swap.** No class record: the adult writes the Lake station card on paper, said to be supplied. No thermometer: write the sky and wind from a window.

**Skip.** The pairs, the helpers and the model.

- **5 min. The weather.** Look outside; never look at the sun. The child does [[s06-P101-6]].
- **5 min. Sort.** The child does [[s06-P101-1]].
  **Say.** "Which cards go beyond the rows?" Expect: "The ones that say every, never or next Monday."
- **25 min. Product W.** The child does [[s06-P101-2]], [[s06-P101-3]], [[s06-P101-4]] and [[s06-P101-5]] alone; read the card once, then wait.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X101]], answered alone. Then ask [[s06-X101b]] and [[s06-X101c]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the child compares two seasons in one sentence; never cut the key and the scale or the sureness. Longer: the child writes what the station would need to record to change the expectation.

### Words and supports

Compare, expect, usually, likely, sure, could still happen. Frame: "In ___ it is usually ___. I am ___ sure. A ___ day could still come." Read the card aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers.

### Tomorrow

Day 102 opens with the weather record. Then each child reads the checklist on product W and revises one part in a second color; the commonest letter gets a 2-minute whole-class example first. The revised product W gets a repair mark after day 115, never a score; the score comes from day 115's fresh work.
