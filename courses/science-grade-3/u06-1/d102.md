# Day 102: When one day is odd

## Teacher page

### Before class

After day 101, tick each child's six-tick look-for checklist on product W and add one question stamp, about 1 minute a child, never a score. Find the commonest letter and prepare a 2-minute example of it. Copy the six exception cards for each pair.

### Safety and set-up

An adult prechecks the weather; the class stays indoors in a warning; no one looks at the sun. The thermometer is non-glass, the jar plastic. Only the helper pair and the adult touch the jar; the adult empties it and handles ice. Helpers wipe hands inside the 5 minutes.

### The day

#### 5 min. Weather record

The helper pair from the rotating six reads the thermometer to the nearest mark and the jar, 30 seconds each; the adult checks. Less than the first mark is "wet, too little to measure", never dry. Below zero is said in words. Children write [[s06-P102-5]]. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud, said to be supplied.

#### 6 min. Return and repair: product W revision

Hand back day 101's pages, s06-P101-1, s06-P101-2, s06-P101-3, s06-P101-4, s06-P101-5 and s06-P101-6, with the checklist on product W, and the exit tickets, s06-X101. Give the commonest letter a 2-minute example first. If it is B, a season said from one day, lay one odd day beside the 30 rows. If it is A, a key ignored, say the key first and rebuild one bar with counters grouped by the key, 2 minutes. Each child changes one part in a second color and keeps the first answer, [[s06-P102-1]], 4 minutes. This revision is not scored. It gets a repair mark after day 115, beside the score from day 115's fresh work.

#### 15 min. Exception evidence

Pairs get six summer cards, each with one odd day. For each card they say the usual and whether it changes, [[s06-P102-2]]. Most of the 30 days match; one odd day is one row. Add a "but" box to the model. Hear six pairs each give one exception, 1 minute each, one pair at a time; the record names who. The rest keep sorting.
**Ask.** One windy day came in a calm autumn. Does autumn change? Expect: "No. Most days are calm. One odd day is one row."

#### 12 min. Model

5 minutes: the class adds "usually ___, but ___" to the model in a second color, and a rule for changing our minds, [[s06-P102-4]]. Expect: "Many odd days, not one." Write the number: about 15 days of 30, or more. 7 minutes: pairs apply it to a second season from the tables, [[s06-P102-3]]. Each child writes a sentence that says the usual, then but, then one odd day, and keeps the usual.

#### 4 min. Exit E102

Read the card aloud once, invented for practice. Alone, [[s06-X102]]. Ask [[s06-X102b]] aloud, 3 to 4 children. Look for: (a) usually cold, because 27 of 30 days were cold, and one warm day does not change it; (b) yes, 15 of 24 is more than half. Error B says tomorrow will be warm, from the one day. A child who says the usual never changes needs the count: 15 of 24. Note the letters.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Papers into folders. Second colors put away.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the six cards on the board. No class tables: use the Lake station card. One projector: show the cards in turn.

### At home

**Swap.** No class tables: use four invented seasons said to be invented. No thermometer: write the sky and wind from a window.

**Skip.** The pairs, the helpers and the rule.

- **5 min. The weather.** Look outside; never look at the sun. The child does [[s06-P102-5]].
- **10 min. Product W.** The child does [[s06-P102-1]] in a second color.
- **10 min. Odd days.** The child does [[s06-P102-2]].
  **Say.** "One windy day in a calm autumn. Does autumn change?" Expect: "No. Most days are calm."
- **5 min. A rule.** The child does [[s06-P102-4]] and [[s06-P102-3]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X102]], answered alone. Then ask [[s06-X102b]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs sort three cards; never cut the exit or the rule. Longer: write what would make us change our minds about winter.

### Words and supports

Usual, odd day, but, change our minds, many, one. Frame: "In ___ it is usually ___, but ___." Read the cards aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers.

### Tomorrow

Day 103 opens with Return and repair on this exit. Error B, one day taken for the whole season: the one warm day laid beside the winter record. A child who says the usual never changes: the count of 15 of 24. Then the class finds its town on the globe and asks whether this year's log describes the town's climate.
