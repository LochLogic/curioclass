# Day 100: Graphs of the seasons

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write the four rainy-day counts agreed on day 99 on the board, one for each season. Choose a model pair of counts, 12 and 5, different from every class count. Highlight one blank box on a season table. Lay key card 3 by each pair that missed the key retrieval on day 99. Count out about 20 counters for each group.

### Safety and set-up

No weather reading today. Counters stay on the desk and never go near mouths; count them out and back in. Squared paper stays flat; pencils only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 99's pages, s06-P99-1, s06-P99-2, s06-P99-3 and s06-P99-4, and the exit tickets, s06-X99. Error B, 3 minutes: hold up the highlighted blank box; children say "not recorded" and answer, [[s06-P100-1]]. If many tickets showed error B, say aloud: blank is not dry. Pairs that missed the key retrieval read key card 3 aloud, 2 minutes; the rest tell a neighbor what one sun stood for.

#### 8 min. Model a scale

Math review; nothing here is taught as new. Say the key first, every time. Picture graph: one cloud is 2 days. The counts are 12 and 5. 12 days is 6 clouds; 5 days is 2 clouds and a half cloud, because a half stands for 1 day. Bar graph: the side counts by 2, 0 up to 12. The 12 bar stops at the top mark. The 5 bar stops halfway between the 4 mark and the 6 mark. Write the title, the side label and the bottom label each time. Say the pair again from the graphs. Children fill the frame, [[s06-P100-2]].
**Ask.** How do you know how many days one picture stands for? Expect: "The key says. Read the key first."

#### 20 min. Group graphs

Each group graphs the four agreed rainy-day counts, as a picture graph or a bar graph, its choice. Key or scale first, then the pictures or bars, both labels and a title, [[s06-P100-3]]. A half picture is 1 day. Walk and ask each group to read its key. Hear six children from the rotating six read their key aloud and say what one picture or tick stands for, 1 minute each, one at a time; the record names who. A group done early graphs again the other way.

#### 6 min. Display and model

Hang the graphs beside the season boxes. The class reads one display at a time, key first, [[s06-P100-4]]. Then in a second color fill each season box with its counts: usually this many rainy days of 30. Keep usually.

#### 4 min. Exit E100

Read the card aloud twice, invented for practice. Alone, [[s06-X100]]. Ask [[s06-X100b]] aloud, 3 to 4 children. Look for: (a) 6 rainy days; (b) 8 rainy days. Error A counts pictures, 3, or counts the line, 4, and ignores the key or scale. Note the letters.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Counters counted back in; squared paper into folders.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the grid on the board. No counters: use paper squares. One projector: show the four counts.

### At home

**Swap.** No class counts: the parent writes four invented rainy-day counts, said to be invented. No graph sheet: use squared paper.

**Skip.** The groups, the rotating six and the question board.

- **5 min. The blank.** The child does [[s06-P100-1]].
  **Say.** "This box is blank. Was it dry?" Expect: "We do not know. Blank means not recorded."
- **10 min. Two counts.** The child does [[s06-P100-2]].
  **Say.** "What does one picture stand for?" Expect: "2 days; half a picture is 1."
- **15 min. Four seasons.** The child does [[s06-P100-3]] on squared paper, key or scale first.
- **5 min. A display.** The child does [[s06-P100-4]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X100]], answered alone. Then ask [[s06-X100b]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: groups draw one kind of graph; never cut the key first or the exit. Longer: graph the same counts the other way and say which is easier to read.

### Words and supports

Key, scale, picture graph, bar graph, half picture, label, title. Frame: "One ___ is ___ days. I read the key first." Read the cards aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers.

### Tomorrow

Then pairs sort statement cards, and each child makes product W alone from the Lake station records. The teacher reads the directions once and does not help.
