# Section 6.1 opener: finding seasonal patterns

## What the section does

Five days, 98 to 102. The big question is: "What weather can we expect here?" Day 98 opens the class weather-log puzzle and starts a seasonal expectation model, four season boxes. Day 99 fills season tables from the log and a station packet. Day 100 turns the counts into picture graphs and bar graphs with a scale of 2. Day 101 is product W, done alone. Day 102 revises W and adds exceptions to the model.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Usual weather is what most days of a season are like.** It says what is likely. It never says what will happen.
- **One day is not a season.** One odd day does not break a pattern. Many odd days might.
- **A scale does the counting.** One picture or one tick can stand for 2 days. Half a picture is 1 day.
- **Averages are supplied.** Children never work out an average.
- **A blank box means not recorded.** It is never a dry day.
- **Below zero is said in words.** A cold reading is written "below freezing" and is kept out of numbers and graphs.
- **No climate-change teaching.** If a child asks, answer plainly from the evidence and say it is a later grade's question.

## Scoring, outside class

Product W (day 101) gets a six-tick checklist and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child, and is revised on day 102. The six points come from day 115's fresh work on the same components; revised W is recorded beside them as a repair mark, not the score. Day 101 has no exit.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** the scale is ignored. **B:** one day guarantees the season.

## Supplies for the section

Season tables, dated rows, key cards, graph sheets, counters, squared paper, the product W card, a second color for each child. Weather equipment on days 98, 99, 101 and 102: a non-glass thermometer, a plastic rain jar, a direction card, the dated weather record.

## A room with nothing

Printed season pages and the dated adult record. Counters or squares cut from paper.
