# Force arrow cards and the swing picture

Picture style for every card: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names. Children of different skin tones and hair, no named child. Arrows are solid black with a clear head.

## Day 1, worked arrows (shown by the teacher, copied by each child)

**Card A.** A girl's hand pushes a tennis-ball-sized ball on a table. One short arrow starts on the ball where the hand touches it. It points to the right. Words below: "A push. The arrow points the way the push goes."

**Card B.** The same picture, but the push is harder. The arrow is twice as long. Words below: "A harder push. A longer arrow."

**Card C.** A boy pulls a toy on a string toward himself. One arrow starts on the toy and points along the string toward the boy. Words below: "A pull. The arrow starts on the thing pulled."

## Day 1, the swing picture

An empty playground swing seen from the side, with two chains and a seat, hanging still. Beside it, the class pendulum: a support, a string and four washers, hanging still. Words below: "A swing and a pendulum. What makes them start, stop and keep coming back?" This is shown only when no real swing can be seen from the window.

## Day 5, warm-up arrow cards (three, shown large, thumbs for start, stop or turn)

Each card is labelled "invented for practice" in small letters.

**Card 1.** A toy train car at rest on a flat track. One arrow behind it points the way the track runs. Answer: it starts.

**Card 2.** A wooden block sliding to the left on a rug. One arrow points to the right, against its motion. Answer: it slows and stops.

**Card 3.** A marble rolling forward along a groove. One short arrow points to the side. Answer: it turns toward that side.
