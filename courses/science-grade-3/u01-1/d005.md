# Day 5: Reading arrows

## Teacher page

### Before class

Set out the six trays with a small cardboard box each. Put one set of the eight practice cards in an envelope for each of the 15 pairs. Put the three warm-up arrow cards where all can see them. Check the weather from a window.

### Safety and set-up

Sliding checks are gentle, inside the tray, away from table edges. Floors stay clear. An adult prechecks the weather and handles the rain jar; nobody looks at the sun; the thermometer is non-glass. Clean-up is 3 minutes; hand wipes close the weather part.

### The day

#### 5 min. Weather record

A helper from the rotating six reads each look: thermometer to the nearest mark, rain jar, direction card, sky (never the sun). Write one dated row on the wall record; children copy it into [[s01-P5-1]]. Last minute: hand wipes.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Day 4's exit (s01-X4). Error A, 2 minutes: each child holds a book still between two hands and draws both arrows. Error B, 2 minutes: predict, then test two pushers on the tray box and read the ruler.

#### 4 min. Warm-up

Show the three arrow cards, said to be invented. Thumbs up for start, thumbs down for stop, thumbs sideways for turn. Children then write the answers, [[s01-P5-2]].

**Ask.** How do you know the third card turns? Expect: its arrow points to the side.

#### 12 min. Pairs with practice cards

Fifteen pairs, eight cards each, said to be invented. For each card the pair says what the object does, which arrow is stronger and where gravity points; write two cards in [[s01-P5-3]]. Each pair also checks two cards on the tray with a sliding box, 90 seconds each: 15 pairs times two checks is 45 pair-minutes on six sets, 7.5 minutes of the 12. Pairs waiting work their cards at tables. Hear a rotating six, 30 seconds each. Watch for error C: "the longest arrow goes farthest".

#### 5 min. Class model

Redraw the class arrow model from the day 1 drawing, beside it. Using the box evidence, write two lines: a push can change motion; two about equal pushes from opposite sides do not.

**Ask.** Which arrow would you change to make this ball stop? Why? Expect: an arrow pointing against its motion, because that push slows it.

#### 9 min. Exit, section exit ticket

Alone, on the exit ticket, [[s01-X5]]; read each card aloud and wait. Look for: card 1 starts moving left; card 2 slows; card 3 turns toward the top of the page; card 4 stays still, the pushes cancel; card 3 has the strongest push; gravity points down. Error B: "card 4 moves left". Error A: "nothing pushes it, it is still". Error C: "the longest arrow goes farthest". Note letters on the record sheet.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Wipe and stack trays and boxes. The adult empties the rain jar.

#### 2 min. Transitions

Collect the exit tickets; move to the next lesson.

### Other rooms

No projector: draw the cards on the board. No trays: slide a book on a smooth desk. No thermometer: write sky and wind from the window, and say so.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the page. No tray: a smooth table and a small book.

**Skip.** The pairs, the class model and the repair.

- **5 min. The weather.** Look out the window together. Do [[s01-P5-1]].
- **8 min. Three cards.** Do [[s01-P5-2]].
  **Say.** "What will it do? How do you know?" Expect: it starts, slows or turns; the arrow shows the push.
- **12 min. Practice cards.** Do [[s01-P5-3]]. Slide a book and push against it.
  **Say.** "Which arrow is stronger?" Expect: the longer one.
- **9 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X5]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one sliding check a pair and one card written. Never cut the warm-up, the cards or the exit. Longer: predict, with arrows, what a push from the side does to the resting box.

### Words and supports

Start, slow, turn, strongest, gravity (a pull down). Frame: "The arrow points ___ so it ___." Home languages count. A partner may point to the card.

### Tomorrow

Day 6, in the next section, opens with Return and repair, 2 minutes a letter. If many wrote "nothing pushes card 4" (error A), it draws a held box with two arrows. If many wrote "card 4 moves left" (error B), it tests two equal pushes again on the tray box. If many wrote "the longest arrow goes farthest" (error C), it lays each arrow card beside its own ruler record. Then groups plan tests that change one thing.
