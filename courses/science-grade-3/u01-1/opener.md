# Section 1.1 opener: what changes motion?

## What the section does

Five days, 1 to 5. On day 1 the class watches a pendulum of string and washers, and an empty playground swing if one is in view. The big question is: "What makes a swing start, stop and keep coming back?" Children learn the force arrow, draw a first explanation, and sit a readiness look in the last ten minutes. Day 2 repairs what the look showed and starts the weather record. Day 3 plans a fair test of two pushes on one box. Day 4 runs the test and revises the arrows. Day 5 practises the arrows on fresh cards and ends with the section exit ticket. Only the exits and the day 4 record are kept as evidence. The day 7 record, made in the next section, is the one that is scored.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Most days hold a short return and repair, a warm-up, the main work, an exit of three minutes and clean-up. Every child may pass in public talk. The teacher hears a rotating six each day, in a corner of the room.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A push or a pull is a force.** An arrow starts on the thing pushed, points the way the force goes, and is longer for a stronger force.
- **A still thing can have forces on it.** Two about equal pushes from opposite sides cancel. The thing stays where it was.
- **Balanced and unbalanced.** Pushes that cancel are balanced. When one push is stronger, the pushes are unbalanced and the motion can change.
- **A fair test changes one thing.** Keep the rest the same, watch one thing, and try each condition more than once.
- **A record shows a pattern.** The weather record starts on day 2. Repeated entries let us say what we expect next, and say it is not sure.

Forces are compared by feel and by the ruler. They are never measured in units. Gravity is only a pull down.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** a still thing has no force on it. Hold a box between two hands and draw both arrows.
- **B:** two equal pushes start a thing moving. Test it again on the tray.
- **C:** a longer arrow means it goes farther. Lay each arrow beside its own ruler record.
- **D:** changing two things is a fair test. Cross out all changes but one.
- **G:** a guess needs no record. Highlight the repeated entries.
- **H:** one try is enough. Run it again.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies for the section

String, four washers, a secured support, a ruler for every group, eye protection for the pendulum, six boxes, trays and soft balls, one thermometer, a plastic rain jar, a direction card and a dated weather record. The picture sets are the arrow cards, the weather and sky pictures, the plan table, the box test record, the day 5 practice cards and the exit cards.

## A room with nothing

A book on a smooth desk, two pencils as pushers and a ruler do the box test. For the weather, write what the window shows each day and say so. Draw the swing, and hold a key on a string at a low arc.
