# Section 2.1 opener: can a pull work across a gap?

## What the section does

Six days, 17 to 22. Day 17 is the hook: a steel clip on a thread leans toward a magnet it never touches, and a rubbed comb lifts bits of tissue. Each child draws an explanation first, before any word is taught. Day 18 models a testable question and sorts the question board into distance, materials, turning and uses. Days 19 to 21 are the class's own tests: how the gap changes the lean, how turning one magnet changes a pull into a push, and how the object and the number of magnets matter. Day 22 sorts questions, revises the arrow model and holds the section exit ticket. Day 18 also holds a 15-minute set-up for unit 3: pots, seeds and mealworms. Their care takes 2 minutes of days 19 to 30 and day 33.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Most days hold a short return and repair of 2 minutes a letter, the main work, a 3-minute exit and 5 minutes of clean-up. Days 17, 18, 21 and 22 start with the weather record. Every child may pass in public talk. The teacher hears a rotating six each day and marks who on the record sheet. Nothing in this section is scored. The scored records come in section 2.3.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A pull or a push can cross a gap.** The clip leans toward a magnet it does not touch. The arrow starts on the thing pulled and points across the gap.
- **Each magnet has two ends.** Children say "red end" and "blue end", never "pole". Unlike ends attract and like ends repel, so turning one magnet can flip a pull into a push.
- **Steel is pulled by either end.** A clip near either end becomes a temporary magnet, so it is pulled and never pushed. Aluminium is not pulled. Not every metal is.
- **A closer gap gave a bigger lean in our trials.** The pull weakens quickly as the gap grows, so a far gap can show nothing. None seen is not no force.
- **Two magnets are not promised to be twice as strong.** Say only what the trials showed.
- **A rubbed comb lifts light paper by a different cause.** Day 17 only shows it. Section 2.2 asks the questions.
- **A testable question names one change, what is kept and what is watched.**

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** a force needs contact. Lay a ruler across the gap and trace the arrow along it. None seen is not no force.
- **B:** every metal is pulled. Hold the same magnet near a steel clip and a foil square.
- **C:** turning a magnet pushes steel away. Turn the magnet near a hung clip, then near a hung magnet.
- **D:** changing two things finds the cause. Cross out every change but one.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies for the section

Two low-strength encased magnets per group, five counted clips, thread, a lean strip and a distance strip, and eye protection for every child. Foil squares on day 21. A comb, wool cloth and tissue bits on day 17. Pots, seeds and mealworm box on day 18. The weather kit on days 17, 18, 21 and 22. Count every magnet and clip out and back. Keep magnets from phones and tablets, and from mouths.

## A room with nothing

Safe magnets have no substitute. Supplied records, labelled with who, what and when, are weaker evidence than testing, and the class says so. A fridge magnet, a steel clip on a thread and a ruler do the day 19 and 20 work for one child. Static needs only a comb and a cloth.
