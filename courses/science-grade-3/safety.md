# Grade 3 science: room safety for the year

For the teacher. These rules hold every day. Each day's Safety and set-up section names the ones it uses. Where your school's rules ask for more, follow them.

## The stop rule

Teach it on day 1; say it again on each hands-on day. Anyone who feels unwell, gets hurt, or sees something break, spill or get loose stops, puts their hands down and tells the adult first. The adult makes the place safe, then decides. A reading that cannot be taken safely is not taken: use the day's invented record, said aloud to be invented.

## Eye protection

Impact-rated safety glasses or goggles (marked Z87.1), worn over a child's own glasses, on before anything swings, springs or blows: pendulum days 1, 8, 9, 12 and 14; magnet days 17, 19, 20, 21, 26, 27 and 31; the switch demonstration, day 24; the fan, day 110. Wipe them between children. Watering from a small cup needs none. Too few pairs: run that part as a demonstration, the class behind a line.

## Hand washing

Wash after soil, plants, the mealworm habitat, the rain jar and any outdoor look, and before snack. An adult watches children wash. No sink: wipes now, the sink next.

## Allergies

Check every child's allergy list with the nurse and the family before the year, and again the day before any new living thing or material.

- **Latex.** The day 23 balloon is latex-free, or there is none; comb and cloth are the default.
- **Foods.** No one eats or tastes anything in a lesson: no seed, leaf, bran or potato.
- **Pollen.** On day 29 a child with a pollen allergy sorts cards instead of brushing flowers.
- **Mold and dust.** A child with asthma or a mold allergy sits farthest from the pots and the habitat.

## Plants, seeds and soil

Seeds are untreated seed sold for sprouting or the classroom, never garden seed, which can be chemically coated; keep the packet (day 18). Damp the potting mix before pouring. Only the planter touches soil.

**Mold.** Look at every pot on living-record days (37 to 58). If mold shows, no one touches, shakes or sniffs it. Move children away, those with asthma or a mold allergy farthest. An adult in gloves and goggles lifts the whole pot into a sealed bag for the outside trash, wipes the spot with soap and water, opens a window if one opens, and washes hands. Drop that pot from the record; use the next pot or the supplied record, said aloud to be supplied. No mold is grown on purpose.

## The mealworms

The habitat stays closed and is looked at through the lid (days 18 to 58, 79 to 81). Children never handle them. Adults give daily care, weekends included. At the end of unit 3 an adult returns them to the supplier or keeps them; they are never released and never thrown away alive.

## Outdoor looks

Day 73. The day before, an adult walks the spots for stinging-insect nests, glass and standing water; if a nest is near, use the photographs. Keep the school's adult ratio, with at least one adult besides the teacher; a missing adult means the photographs. Bring any child's allergy medicine and an adult trained to give it. Closed shoes; look only, touching no living thing or water. In a weather warning, stay inside.

## Weather record and the sun

The thermometer is non-glass and the rain jar plastic. An adult empties the jar and handles any ice or snow. Nobody looks at the sun. In a warning, read indoors or skip.

## Heat and lamps

No child handles anything hot or very cold. The day 18 growing lamp is set up by an adult alone: a light that stays cool, on a ground-fault outlet, cord taped flat, water tray across the room, unplugged while pots are watered. Check your school's policy first.

## Electricity

Day 24: the teacher alone runs the nail and coil from one new D cell in a holder with a push switch, so the circuit is open unless pressed. At most 5 seconds on, then disconnect. Wire ends taped; never short the cell. Touch the nail and wire only a minute after; stop if anything warms. Children sit 1 m back.

Day 110: the adult alone plugs the guarded fan into a ground-fault outlet, runs it on low and unplugs it before anything is collected. Cord taped flat; the rain jar emptied first.

## Magnets and small parts

Large encased or ceramic classroom magnets only; no loose small strong magnets, which are dangerous if swallowed. Count magnets, washers, clips and paper bits out and back; nothing goes in a mouth. Fingers clear of pinches; magnets away from phones. Nothing is thrown; pushes stay in the tray (days 2 to 7, 15, 16). No child sits on a swing during an investigation; string stays off necks.

## Glass

Children use no glass in this course. If glass breaks, children step back and an adult sweeps it with a brush and pan.

## Clean-up

Every hands-on day has clean-up minutes: equipment counted back, tables wiped, eye protection wiped and boxed, hands washed. An adult wipes spills at once. Keep the poison control number by the door.
