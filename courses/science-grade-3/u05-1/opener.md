# Section 5.1 opener: reading a place's past

## What the section does

Five days, 82 to 86. The big question is: "How do we know places have changed, and what happens to living things when they do?" Day 82 shows three drawings with no captions: standing trunks with roots in rock, a map with one far-north dot and a treeless snowy plain. Children say how all three could be one place, fill a table of finds and draw a first past-environment model. Day 83 separates what a find shows from what we infer, adds guide cards and holds the support group from the day 81 readiness look. Day 84 applies the same reading to a desert valley with whale bones. Day 85 adds extinct forms, layers, older and younger, and a week's weather log set beside the long record. Day 86 reads a regional record and holds product F. Each day ends with an exit. Day 86 holds the section exit.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Days 82, 83, 85 and 86 hold the 5-minute weather record. Two helpers from the rotating six read the thermometer and the rain jar. Only the helpers touch the equipment. The adult checks conditions, empties the jar and handles any ice. Every child may pass in public talk. No one is asked about home or what a family believes. The teacher hears the rotating six at each exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A fossil is a find with a kind, a size and a place.** Kinds are plant print, shell, track, bone and trunk. Never a species name.
- **A guide card turns a find into a place.** Living things needed then what they need now. This steady-patterns idea is stated to children, not proved.
- **Seen and inferred.** A find shows what it shows. An inference goes past it, and a dotted line marks it.
- **A piece gives at least the size of the whole.** A trunk piece 80 centimetres long gives only a minimum.
- **Size alone gives no climate.** A big leaf or stump says a big plant grew. The guide card says what it needs.
- **Extinct means gone everywhere.** It needs a statement from a guide. Missing from one place is not extinct.
- **Older and younger only.** Deeper is older only when the layers are undisturbed. No dates and no numbers of years.
- **Deep time is stated plainly.** Land can change very slowly over ages that no one watched.
- **The Svalbard trunks.** The standing trunks grew when that land was warm and near the equator. The land has since moved. Do not say it was always Arctic. Do not teach why it moved.
- **If a child asks about belief or age,** answer with what the evidence shows. Nobody is asked for a belief.
- **Scoring product F.** Six points, one for each complete component. Feedback is written after class. Revision is day 93.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** it is dry or treeless now, so it always was. Match the fossil to the place it supports.
- **B:** none here, so extinct. Extinct means none alive anywhere.
- **C:** the bottom layer is always older. Only when the layers are undisturbed.
- **F:** a big find proves a hot place. A size and a guide card together say more.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies for the section

A non-glass outdoor thermometer, a straight-sided plastic rain jar, a direction card and the dated weather record. The hook drawings, find cards, table of finds, past-environment model sheet, Big Stump cards, guide cards, desert valley cards, whale cards, five layer cards for each pair, the time strip, new-find cards, the regional record, the exit cards and product F card. Second-colour pencils.

## A room with nothing

The teacher draws on the board. Cards are printed. Weather comes from the dated adult record read aloud. Say every size is invented for practice unless a card gives a park page.
