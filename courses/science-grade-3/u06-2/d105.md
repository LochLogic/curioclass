# Day 105: Warm, cold, wet and dry

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy the cards, the chart and the table for each pair. Write Cairo's two Januaries on the board: 14 degrees in 2019, 13 in 2020; the steps are whole degrees, so this is a small change. Choose the helper pair from the rotating six for the weather record. Keep the weather equipment on the teacher table.

### Safety and set-up

An adult prechecks the weather; the class stays indoors in a warning; no one looks at the sun. The thermometer is non-glass, the jar plastic. Only the helper pair and the adult touch the jar; the adult empties it and handles ice. Helpers wipe hands inside the 5 minutes.

### The day

#### 5 min. Weather record

The helper pair reads the thermometer to the nearest mark and the jar, 30 seconds each; the adult checks. Less than the first mark is "wet, too little to measure", never dry. Below zero is said in words. Children write [[s06-P105-1]]. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud, said to be supplied.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 104's exit tickets, s06-X104. Error D, one source left out: lay the two cards side by side and take one detail from each into one sentence. Day 104's written tasks stay in folders. No child is named.

#### 5 min. Map and key

Pairs find Cairo and Utqiagvik on the printed map and say what the key shows, [[s06-P105-2]]. Locations only.

#### 10 min. Read the cards

Read both places' atlas cards, then both station cards, once each. Freezing is 0 degrees, where water freezes; cold is told in months below freezing, never in numbers below zero. A millimeter is a very small length; the jar's marks are in millimeters. A wet month has 20 millimeters or more of rain or melted snow. A place is wet with 6 or more wet months of 12, and dry with fewer. Cairo has rainy days but no wet month. Cairo's two Januaries are on the board. Pairs fill the two-column chart, Cairo [[s06-P105-3]] and Utqiagvik [[s06-P105-4]].

#### 14 min. Combine and compare

Pairs build the wet, dry, warm and cold table for both places from the chart, [[s06-P105-5]] (5 minutes). Then each pair writes two comparisons with the numbers, [[s06-P105-6]] (6 minutes). Then Cairo's two Januaries, [[s06-P105-7]] (3 minutes). The two Januaries are not the same: that is year to year change, said in the words of day 103. If a pair writes only hot and cold, ask for the count.

**Ask.** Which place has more months below freezing? How many? Expect: Utqiagvik, 8 and 0.

#### 4 min. Exit E105

Read the card once, invented for practice. Alone, [[s06-X105]]. Ask [[s06-X105b]] aloud, 3 to 4 children. Look for: W is colder, 6 months below freezing and not 0; V is drier, 1 wet month and not 5, and today's rain is one day. Error D says W is drier because it is cold: a belief in place of the count. Error C says V is wet from today's rain. Note the letters.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Cards and tables collected. The adult empties the jar. Student pages stay in folders.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the counts of both places on the board. No thermometer: write the sky and wind from a window. One projector: show the table and fill it as a class.

### At home

**Swap.** No thermometer: write the sky and wind from a window. No printer: draw the table on paper.

**Skip.** The pairs and the helper pair.

- **5 min. The weather.** Look outside; never look at the sun. The child does [[s06-P105-1]].
- **5 min. The map.** The child does [[s06-P105-2]].
- **10 min. The cards.** Read the Cairo card; the child does [[s06-P105-3]], then [[s06-P105-4]].
  **Say.** "Which place has more months below freezing?" Expect: Utqiagvik, 8 and 0.
- **10 min. The table.** The child does [[s06-P105-5]] and [[s06-P105-6]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X105]], answered alone. Then ask [[s06-X105b]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one comparison, not two; never cut the counts, the two Januaries or the exit. Longer: write which place is warmer and how the cards show it.

### Words and supports

Wet month, months below freezing, warm, cold, dry, compare. Frame: "___ has more ___ than ___. The numbers are ___ and ___." Read the cards aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers.

### Tomorrow

Day 106 opens with the weather record, then Return and repair on this exit. Error C, one day taken as the climate: this year laid beside the many-year strip. Error D, a belief in place of the count: underline the number in each card, then answer from the number. Then the class reads three cards and does product C alone.
