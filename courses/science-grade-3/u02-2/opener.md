# Section 2.2 opener: from unseen forces to a useful latch

## What the section does

Six days, 23 to 28. Day 23 asks whether a rubbed comb lifts light paper bits more when it is closer. Day 24 shows a teacher-only switched magnet, and children ask what the switch changes. Days 25 to 28 move to design. The class supply cupboard door will not stay shut. Day 25 says the need. Day 26 adds criteria and limits. Day 27 sketches and tries one latch. Day 28 changes the users and revises the statement. Nothing here is scored; section 2.3 holds the scored records.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Days 25 and 26 start with the weather record. Each day takes a short return and repair of 2 minutes a letter, the main work, a 3-minute exit and a few minutes of clean-up. Two observers a day write the living record in 2 minutes. Day 27 reserves 10 minutes for six children to hand-pollinate flowering plants while the rest sketch.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Rubbing moves charge.** A rubbed comb pulls light bits across a small gap, more when closer. It works best on dry days. Say only that it pulls and that it needs rubbing.
- **A switched magnet is a magnet only while the switch is on.** A coil of wire around a steel nail, with current, lifts clips. With the switch off they fall. The wire warms. Only the teacher runs it. Children never build it.
- **A design problem has a need, criteria and limits.** The need says who needs what so that. A criterion is a way to check it. A limit is the most we allow of materials, time and cost. Engineers say constraint. Children say limit.
- **Criteria can pull against each other.** A latch that holds more may be harder to open. A new user changes the criteria. The limits stay.
- **Counts are in washers.** No grams. A hold of 0 or 3 or more is written as it is.
- **One latch only on day 27.** No comparing many designs and no fair test of a prototype.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** no pull seen means no pull. Say none seen at that gap.
- **D:** two changes find the cause. Keep one change.
- **E:** a list of things is the need. The need is who needs what, so that.
- **F:** it works, so the limit does not matter. Check each limit against the printed allowance.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies

Day 23: comb, wool cloth, paper bits, distance strips. A balloon on hair is by choice only, after allergy checks, inflated by an adult, who removes fragments. Day 24: the teacher demonstrator and eye protection for every child. Days 26 and 27: six cardboard cupboard models, 12 magnets, 18 washers, tape, eye protection. Count magnets and washers out and back. Keep magnets from phones and tablets.

## A room with nothing

For day 23, a comb, a cloth and torn paper at two gaps is enough. For day 24, use a supplied record naming apparatus, observer and date, and say it is weaker evidence. For days 26 and 27, a box lid, two fridge magnets and a few washers do the work for one child.
