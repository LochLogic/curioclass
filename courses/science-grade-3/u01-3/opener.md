# Section 1.3 opener: a second pattern, two checks and a response

## What the section does

Six days, 11 to 16. The class asks: "When should we change a prediction?" On day 11 a soft ball rolls in a bowl, a second thing with the same pattern. Day 12 shortens the pendulum string and each child makes a record alone, revised on day 13. Day 13 builds one class explanation and ends with the section exit ticket. Days 14 and 15 are check days. Each child makes a fresh record alone, scored outside lesson minutes; the revised day 12 and day 7 records get a repair mark beside the score. Day 16 responds to what the work showed, and the scored papers come back on day 17.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Days 14 and 15 give no help and no feedback. Feedback is a look-for checklist and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child. Scored work is the fresh check-day work; a revised record is repair evidence, never the score. A turn too quick to read is left blank. Seen data is never replaced by expected data.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A second thing, the same reasoning.** The bowl ball turns in order, and each turn is a little lower.
- **A failed prediction has two causes to check.** First check the readings. Then check what changed.
- **One explanation for the class.** Pushes can change motion. Equal opposite pushes cancel. At the bottom the string's pull up and gravity's pull down cancel. Gravity pulls the washer down the curve. It moves past the bottom because it is already moving. The order of turns repeats while the swings get smaller.
- **An old record does not cover a new push.** After a change, look again before predicting.
- **Approximate hand pushes are not equal for sure.** A child who says so is rewarded.

## The errors, in plain words

- **E and F:** turning-position cards in order, and successive marks on the class table.
- **G and H:** two matching turns as evidence, and run it again.
- **A, B, C, D:** repaired on day 16 in four groups.

## Supplies for the section

Bowls, soft balls, rulers, taped centimeter strips, secured pendulums with four washers and eye protection, a two-string paper basket and a cardboard sled with a tray. The record sheets and exit cards. The class record sheet is reused on days 14 and 15.

## A room with nothing

Use a round-bottomed bowl or a curved book spine for the second pattern. Use a key on a string for the pendulum. Draw the table on scrap paper.
