# Section 4.2 opener: differences that matter

## What the section does

Five days, 64 to 68. The big question is: "Which differences matter, and do they help everywhere?" Day 64 compares ten thick-coated and ten thin-coated hares of one kind, sorts picture cards by kind and fills a part, job, who it helps row. Day 65 is a paper moth search on patterned cloth, then the cloth is turned over. Day 66 reads the class counts and says what a paper trial can and cannot show. Day 67 compares thorn evidence and a bird mate-choice case, and sorts statements onto three shelves: survive, find a mate, have young. Day 68 revises the cause-and-effect model and children write item V alone. Each day ends with an exit. Day 68 holds the section exit.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Days 65 and 66 end with the 5-minute weather record. Day 65 is a hands-on search at tables, seated, with no racing. Nobody plays prey. The adult checks cloth allergies and clears tables the day before. Every child may pass in public talk. The teacher hears the rotating six at each exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Compare within one kind.** A hare with a thick coat and a hare with a thin coat are one kind. A hare and a fox are not.
- **Matched conditions and equal starting numbers matter.** Ten of each, the same search, repeated.
- **A difference can help. It does not promise.** Say "can help", never "will".
- **The paper search models being seen, not living.** Paper is not alive. One class trial does not show how real animals do.
- **A mate record and a young record are two observations.** A pair forming is not a chick hatching.
- **Thorn evidence shows protection.** It does not show that thorn size is inherited.
- **Scoring item V.** Four points: root and survival with the counts, plume and mates with the counts, young with the counts from the separate record, and model arrows that do not promise success.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** compare across kinds. Sort by kind first, then compare.
- **C:** a helpful feature always works. Find the exception in the table.
- **D:** bright colors hide best. Turn the cloth over and search again.
- **E:** a pair means young. Lay the pair card beside the chick card.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies for the section

Hare, musk ox and fox picture cards, row sheets, 20 precut paper moths a group (10 yellow and 10 green), two cloth squares a group, tally sheets, the class table, the two printed backgrounds, the shrub and finch cards, the item V cards and the exit cards. Weather equipment on days 65 and 66.

## A room with nothing

Use the printed tallies and the class table, read aloud as invented. No viewer is needed. The two printed backgrounds replace it.
