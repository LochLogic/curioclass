# Day 111: A rod, a wire and the ground

## Teacher page

### Before class

Draw the trial table on the board. Print a lightning card a pair. Choose the six children to hear.

### Safety and set-up

Paper and talk only; nothing is handled.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 110's exit tickets, s06-X110. Error E, 2 minutes: one chain, hazard, design, harm, with the hazard still happening. Error F, 2 minutes: the limit box first, on one goal card and one limit card. Error G, 1 minute: for the class roof record, name one condition not tested. No child is named.

#### 5 min. Hook

Show the drawing: a building with a pointed metal rod on the roof, a thick wire down the wall, a metal bar in the ground. Pairs say what they notice (2 minutes). Say it is a lightning protection system. Keep to the drawing; no one is asked about lightning they have seen.

#### 10 min. Read the system

Pairs name each part's job, [[s06-P111-1]] (5 minutes). Then say: a strike may land on the rod; the wire carries the strike's energy down; the ground lets it spread out into the earth. We do not learn how lightning forms yet. A rod protects the building; people still go inside when they hear thunder, as in kindergarten (review of day 15). The weather service says a rod does not stop a strike; it gives a strike a path away from the roof and walls.

**Ask.** What does the rod do, and what does it not do? Expect: it gives a strike a path; it does not stop strikes; it works only with the wire and the ground.

#### 14 min. Evidence and claim

Say the records are invented. Read the card once: ten model houses, three trials each. Goal: at most 3 houses damaged. Limit: at most 9 tokens. Nothing, 0 tokens: 9, 9 and 8 damaged. Rod alone, 3 tokens: 6, 7 and 6. Complete system, 8 tokens: 2, 2 and 1. Fill the table on the board, 2 minutes. Pairs mark the goal box and the limit box, [[s06-P111-2]] (3 minutes). Limit box first.

Each child then writes a claim, [[s06-P111-3]] (6 minutes). While they write, hear six children from the rotating six, 1 minute each; the record names who. Expect: the complete system has merit, 2, 2 and 1 is at most 3, 8 is within 9, less is damaged; the records do not show that lightning never hits, or that real buildings are safe. The rod alone costs 3 but misses the goal: it works only in the system. Then pairs swap claims and ask "what did you see that shows that?" (3 minutes). Each may pass.

#### 7 min. Arrows

Revise the arrows sheet from day 109 in a second color, [[s06-P111-4]] (4 minutes): lightning, rod, wire and ground, less damage. Then ask the class what stays the same (3 minutes).

**Ask.** What did the system change, and what did it not change? Expect: it lessened the damage; it did not stop lightning.

#### 4 min. Exit E111

Read the card once, invented for practice. Alone, [[s06-X111]]. Ask [[s06-X111b]] aloud, 3 to 4 children. Look for: the net with frame and slope has merit, and the solid roof breaks the token limit; the records do not show hail never cracks the glass, or that real greenhouses are safe. Error E says the net means hail never cracks the glass. Error G says every real greenhouse is safe. Note the letters. Student pages stay in folders.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the rod, wire and ground on the board; read the records aloud. One projector: show the drawing and the table.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the table onto paper.

**Skip.** The pairs, the swap and the heard six.

- **8 min. The parts.** Look at the drawing together. The child does [[s06-P111-1]].
  **Say.** "What does the rod do, and what does it not do?" Expect: it gives a strike a path; it does not stop strikes.
- **10 min. The records.** Read the records aloud. The child does [[s06-P111-2]].
- **10 min. Claim and arrows.** The child does [[s06-P111-3]] and [[s06-P111-4]].
  **Say.** "What did you see that shows that?" Expect: the counts, 2, 2 and 1, against 9, 9 and 8.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X111]], answered alone. Then ask [[s06-X111b]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: mark the table and write the claim; never cut the limit box or the exit. Longer: the child writes why the rod alone is not enough.

### Words and supports

Rod, wire, ground, system, damage, merit. Frame: "I think ___ has merit because ___, so ___. The record does not show ___." Accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers.

Error G: name one condition not tested. The directions for product H are read once. Then pairs choose one design from the section and write its merit claim.
