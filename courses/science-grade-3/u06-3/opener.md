# Section 6.3 opener: getting ready for the dangerous kind

## What the section does

Five days, 108 to 112. The big question is: "How can people get ready for dangerous weather so it does less harm?" Day 108 reads a real weather-service warning from another place and teaches hazard, harm and warning. Day 109 judges a flood wall from invented trial counts, with a goal and a limit. Day 110 tests a taped paper roof in a low fan. Day 111 reads a lightning protection system on paper only. Day 112 asks each child to make a merit claim, alone, in product H.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Weather cannot be stopped. Harm can be lessened.** The wall does not stop the flood. It keeps water from the houses.
- **A design has merit when it meets the goal within the limit.** Check the limit box first. The biggest count does not always win.
- **A model trial shows the model.** It does not promise real protection. Name one thing not tested.
- **A lightning rod does not stop a strike.** It gives a strike a path away from the roof and walls, and it works only with the wire and the ground. Paper only. No lightning mechanism is taught.
- **Voluntary sharing.** Some children may have lived through a flood, fire or storm. No one is asked to tell about it. Examples stay general.
- **No climate-change teaching.** Answer plainly from the evidence if a child asks.

## Safety on day 110

The adult alone places the house, plugs in and runs the fan on low, with the guard on. Everyone wears impact eye protection. Observers stand behind the marked line. Keep water away from the fan. Unplug before collecting parts.

## Scoring, outside class

Product H (day 112) is revised on day 113 and scored after day 116. Six points. It is never scored on its production or revision day.

## The errors, in plain words

- **E:** a design stops the hazard. **F:** the budget is ignored. **G:** a model trial guarantees real protection.

## Supplies for the section

Warning cards, arrows sheets, trial tables, goal and limit cards, a cardboard model house, a lightweight paper roof, tape, a guarded fan, eye protection, lightning evidence cards. Weather equipment on days 109 and 110.

## A room with nothing

Printed tables read aloud. The invented roof record. The dated adult weather record.
