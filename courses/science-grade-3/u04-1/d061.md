# Day 61: When does a bigger group help?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the heron record and squared paper for each pair, and set the wolf ladder cards in order on the wall with no numbers. Check conditions for the weather record.

### Safety and set-up

The thermometer is non-glass, the jar is plastic, and no one looks at the sun. Only the two helpers touch the equipment; the adult empties the jar and handles any ice. In a weather warning the class stays indoors. An adult prechecks the weather and goes with the helpers outdoors. Clean-up is 2 minutes.

### The day

#### 4 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 60's pages, s04-P60-1, s04-P60-2, s04-P60-3 and s04-P60-4, and tickets, s04-X60. Two repairs, 2 minutes each.
- Error B (all birds need a group to eat): show ladder card 1. One wolf has a bar one step tall, so even alone it gets food some of the time. A group helps; it is not a need.
- Error C (the group always gets fish): a taller bar means more hunts end in food, not every hunt. Say: helps, not always.

#### 31 min. Hunting counts

8 minutes: the wolf ladder. Say aloud that it is invented for practice: no numbers, bars only taller or the same. A taller bar means more hunts end in food. **Ask.** What happens to the bars as wolves are added? Expect: they rise to four wolves, then stay level. Alone, [[s04-P61-1]], 3 minutes. The bars stop rising after four wolves.

12 minutes: say aloud that the heron record is invented for practice. Pairs draw a bar graph on squared paper, one square for one trip, bars starting at the bottom line, each bar labeled, as in grade 2; today one square is one trip, [[s04-P61-2]], 8 minutes. Each child then writes a claim, [[s04-P61-3]], 4 minutes. Look for numbers from the graph and a so. Error C sounds like: more herons always means more fish. Three herons and four herons both had 7.

8 minutes: set the ladder beside the graph. **Ask.** What else would you want to know before saying a bigger group is always better? Expect: two records support the idea but do not prove it; we want other animals and bigger groups. Alone, [[s04-P61-4]], 3 minutes, then the class shares, 5 minutes. Any child may pass.

3 minutes: partners say claim, then evidence, then reason, 45 seconds each way, all pairs at once. While the pairs talk, hear three of the rotating six, 30 seconds each; hear the other three on day 62. A reason is the so: how the group helps.

#### 3 min. Exit E61

Show the gull graph card and read it aloud twice. Alone, [[s04-X61]]. Look for: no; three gulls had 8 trips of 10 and six gulls had 3. Error C says more gulls always means more fish. Error B says one gull cannot catch fish. Note letters on the record sheet.

#### 5 min. Weather record

Two helpers from the rotating six read the thermometer and the rain jar, 30 seconds each, while the class writes [[s04-P61-5]]. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud and say it is supplied.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Papers and squared paper are collected. The adult empties the jar; the helpers wash hands at two sinks.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the ladder on the board and read the heron record aloud; children rule squares on notebook paper. No weather tools: read the dated adult record aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No squared paper: rule boxes on plain paper, each the same size. No partner: the adult is the partner.

**Skip.** The repairs, the class talk and the weather record.

- **6 min. Ladder.** Draw six wolves in a row with bars one, two, three, four, four and four steps tall. The child does [[s04-P61-1]].
- **16 min. Herons.** The child does [[s04-P61-2]] and [[s04-P61-3]], one square for one trip: 2, 5, 7 and 7.
  **Say.** "What does the graph show, and how does the group help?" Expect: three herons did best, so more got fish.
- **5 min. Look again.** The child does [[s04-P61-4]].
- **7 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X61]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: draw only the bars for one, two and three herons. Never cut the bar graph, the question about what else to know, or the exit. Longer: say what a record would show if a group cost the herons food.

### Words and supports

Pattern, record, bar, evidence, claim, reason. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." Home-language answers count. A partner may point to the bars instead of writing; accept a drawn graph.

### Tomorrow

Day 62 opens with 2 minutes on error C from today's exit, then adds a lone moose card and a written claim. Error C reads: more gulls always means more fish. Error B reads: one gull cannot catch fish. The class writes the claim frame, adds arrows to its day 59 drawing, and writes item G.
