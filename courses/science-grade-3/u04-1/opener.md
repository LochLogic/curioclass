# Section 4.1 opener: how groups help

## What the section does

Five days, 59 to 63. The big question is: "Why do some living things survive in a place while others do not?" Day 59 shows two drawings of the winter tundra with no captions: musk oxen in a ring and a white hare on snow. Children say what they notice and what they nearly missed, then draw a first system with link lines. Day 60 reads four group cards: musk oxen, wolves, fish and honeybees. Day 61 reads a wolf ladder and an invented heron record and draws a bar graph. Day 62 adds the lone moose, the claim frame and item G. Day 63 revises after a partner questions the evidence. Each day ends with an exit. Day 63 holds the section exit.

## How the days run

Each day is 45 minutes. Days 59 to 62 end with the 5-minute weather record. Two helpers from the rotating six read the thermometer and the rain jar. Only the helpers touch the equipment. The adult empties the jar. Every child may pass in public talk. The teacher hears the rotating six at each exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A group can help.** More eyes watch, work is shared, and neighbors shelter one another. A group can also cost: more mouths to feed.
- **A group is not a need of every animal.** A lone animal can find food, rest and safety by itself.
- **A record supports an argument. It does not prove a cause.** The wolf ladder is read as a pattern: success rose, then stayed level.
- **Claim, evidence and reason.** The claim says what. The evidence says what was seen. The "so" says how it helps.
- **A ring helps without a promise.** Horns face out and calves stay inside. Some hunts still end in food for wolves.
- **Jobs.** A lookout watches while others dig or eat. A colony shares work among its members.
- **Scoring item G.** Four points: a food link, a protection link, a cold wind link, and a comparison of the small and large groups and their jobs. Each point needs its supporting record.

## The errors, in plain words

- **B:** every animal needs a group. Put the lone moose beside the group cards.
- **C:** the group always saves every one. Find the trip that does not follow the pattern.
- **F:** one kind lives anywhere. Draw the missing link on the turtle card.
- **G:** nothing lives on the tundra. Point to the hare and the musk oxen.

Wrong answers are voiced as "someone might say". No child is the example of a mistake.

## Supplies for the section

A non-glass outdoor thermometer, a straight-sided plastic rain jar, a direction card and the dated weather record. Two tundra drawings and the winter card, the group cards and chart, the wolf ladder, the heron record on squared paper, the moose card, the frame sheet, the item G cards and the exit cards.

## A room with nothing

Use printed cards, a drawn ladder and the dated adult weather record read aloud. Say every number is invented for practice.
