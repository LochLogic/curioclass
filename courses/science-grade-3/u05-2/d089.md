# Day 89: Judging a fish ladder

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the dam and ladder cards, one set a pair, and a trial table for each pair. Draw the paper river on the board: a dam across it, marked model fish at its foot, gravel upstream.

### Safety and set-up

Weather: the adult prechecks conditions, the class stays indoors in a warning, and no one looks at the sun. The thermometer is non-glass, the jar plastic; the adult alone empties the jar and handles any ice. Helpers wash their hands.

### The day

#### 2 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 88's s05-P88-1, s05-P88-2, s05-P88-3, s05-P88-4 and s05-P88-5, and the ticket s05-X88. If many made error E in day 88's exit (the wide bridge, because 10 is the most), put the goal box and the limit box on the board and check the wide bridge against both. If many made error D (every animal will use the tube), read the card: it covers marked model toads only. One child restates it; any child may pass.

#### 5 min. Weather record

Two helpers from the rotating six read the thermometer and the rain jar, 30 seconds each, while the class writes [[s05-P89-1]]. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud, said to be supplied.

#### 6 min. Dam model

Point to the paper river. Say a dam changed the river: marked model fish at its foot need the gravel upstream to lay eggs. People found out what the fish need first, and the ladder was built from that. A fish ladder is a staircase of small pools beside the dam; a fish rests in one pool, then jumps to the next. Say only this about how it works. **Ask.** What do the fish need, and what did the dam change? Expect: the gravel upstream, and the dam stops them reaching it. Children write it, [[s05-P89-2]]. Say these are model fish and invented numbers.

#### 12 min. Passage records

Read the goal card: at least 5 of 10 marked fish get above the dam, within 8 tokens. 15 pairs read ladder A, B and C and mark each box met or missed on the trial table, [[s05-P89-3]]. Walk round: error E calls ladder C best because 9 is the most; send the pair to the token box. **Ask.** Which ladder has merit? Expect: ladder A, 6 of 10 for 7 tokens; B misses the count, and C breaks the budget.

#### 12 min. Merit argument

6 minutes: each child writes a claim, evidence and reason, draws an arrow from the evidence to the claim, and says what the record does not show, [[s05-P89-4]]. The frame is on the page. 6 minutes: six children from the rotating six each read theirs aloud, 1 minute each, while the rest finish. **Ask.** What does the record not show? Expect: other river animals; it covers only the marked fish. Error D says the ladder helps every animal in the river.

#### 5 min. Exit E89

Read the card aloud twice, invented for practice. Alone, [[s05-X89]], 4 minutes. Ask [[s05-X89b]] aloud, 3 to 4 children. Look for: the stepped ledges, with the count and the limit, and that the record covers only frogs. Error E says the long slope, because 10 is the biggest. Error D says the ledges will help every marsh animal.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Adult empties the jar. Helpers wash hands. Cards counted back.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the river and the trial table on the board; read each card aloud; children answer on notebook paper. No weather tools: read the dated adult record aloud, said to be supplied.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed cards: the parent reads each card aloud. No outdoor thermometer: read a window thermometer or a weather page for today's row.

**Skip.** The helpers and the reading aloud.

- **5 min. Weather.** The child does [[s05-P89-1]].
- **6 min. The dam.** Read the river card aloud. The child does [[s05-P89-2]].
- **10 min. The ladders.** The child does [[s05-P89-3]].
  **Say.** "Which ladder has merit, and why?" Expect: ladder A; it meets the count and the token limit.
- **10 min. Argue.** The child does [[s05-P89-4]].
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X89]], answered alone. Then ask [[s05-X89b]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: mark ladders A and C only. Never cut the merit argument or the exit. Longer: write what record would show a fourth ladder has merit.

### Words and supports

Dam, ladder, goal, limit, merit, evidence, reason. Frame: "I think ___ has merit because ___, so ___. The record does not show ___." Read the cards aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn answers.

### Tomorrow

Day 90 opens with a 2-minute Return and repair on error E (the biggest count wins), the two boxes on the cards. Then the class reads how losing shade on a stream changes its water and its animals.
