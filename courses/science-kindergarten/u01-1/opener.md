# Section 1.1 opener: what changes a moving ball?

## What the section does

Four days, 1 to 4. On day 1 the class watches one ball roll from the same start tape and stop in different places, and once turn when a hand taps its side. Nobody explains why. The class asks the big question (why does our ball sometimes roll to the wall, sometimes stop halfway and once turn a corner?), draws a three-box strip (before, touch, after) and starts a class picture chart. Day 2 tests a gentle push against a harder push. Day 3 tests a gentle pull against a harder pull on a box. Day 4 keeps the push the same and changes which way it goes. Nothing here is scored. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with the scale shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 minutes: a five-minute warm-up that repairs the last exit, a short teaching part with Pip the puppet, about twelve minutes of trials in halves (half the class tests while half draws, then they swap), a rug talk, two minutes of clean-up and a three-minute spoken exit. Three balls or boxes run at a time. A child may always pass; the teacher can roll instead, or a child may direct a partner who pushes, and the plan is still the child's.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A touch can start, stop, speed up, slow or turn something.** Children say "it moved when it was touched." That is enough.
- **A push and a pull can be gentle or harder.** Children compare them by looking at the start and by laying stopping places side by side. A difference smaller than one tape piece is "about the same."
- **A fair test changes one thing.** Keep, change, watch: the ball, the start and the way stay the same while only how hard changes. Day 4 changes only which way.
- **Farther is not always faster.** A hard roll may stop nearer than a gentle one. Record what happened, repeat the pair once and keep both records. Never fix a result.
- **Why the ball stops is not taught.** The floor slows it, but children are not told. Their puzzle is answered only by what they test.

## The errors, in plain words

- **C:** "The cushion slid by itself." The touch is left out. Sort touch and no-touch cards and say "it moved when it was touched."
- **A:** "Use the big pram for the hard push." The pram changed as well as the push. Point to the pair that keeps the same pram.
- **B:** "A pull can't make it go." Show with hands how the drawer started when the knob was pulled.
- **F:** "Use the small tray to test which way." Push the same ball two ways toward two tape marks.
- **D:** Not met until day 5. If a child says the tapped ball turns before the hand arrives, show approach, touch and turn with three order cards.

If a third or fewer made an error, hear them in a two-minute small group at one lane during the next day's roll part. Wrong answers are voiced by Pip or "someone might say", never by a named child.

## Safety

Everyone present wears fitted impact eye protection for all moving-object work, demonstrations and clean-up; confirm stock first. Floor only, no throwing, wait behind the line, large intact balls, fingers clear. The teacher alone attaches the short thick string, with no loops, and the box is pulled in a straight line. No tasting or sniffing. Clear lanes and rehearse slow trials the day before. Wipe hands at clean-up.

## Supplies for the section

Three large soft balls, a box with a teacher-attached string, masking tape, paper strips, crayons, a puppet called Pip, chart paper, eye protection for everyone, hand wipes and bins, and the picture cards and strips in this folder. A room with nothing: a tightly taped paper ball, a scrap box with string and tape do everything. With no eye protection run no trials; use teacher-made dated photographs with real marks and say aloud they were made the day before. That path is weaker, because looking is not carrying out.
