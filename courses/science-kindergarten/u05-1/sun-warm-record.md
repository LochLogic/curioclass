# Dated matched records (days 44 to 48, and the class records on day 52)

A record the teacher makes, not a test. Photographs alone cannot show warmth; the record is the observer's comparison, written beside the photographs. Never infer warmth from how bright a photograph looks.

## What goes on each record

One card for each matched pair, one pair for each of sand, soil, a smooth rock and shallow water. On the card: the material in large letters, a photograph of the sunny sample and a photograph of the shaded sample taken from the same spot, the date, the place (a school area, never a home), the time of the look (morning or afternoon), the starting conditions, and the time the samples were out. Keep the depth of water, the amount of sample and the container alike within a pair. A one-line caption in the teacher's words, from the teacher's own touch with the back of a hand: "The sunny one felt warmer", "The shaded one felt warmer" or "About the same". Never write a degree. Never write a result you did not feel.

## The afternoon look (day 45)

The teacher sets out the afternoon pairs after the lesson, photographs them and writes the caption. Day 46 shows these photographs with the caption read aloud, and the children point to the warmer one. "About the same" is a fine answer. Morning and afternoon notes belong to this course day, not extra course days.

## When there is no sun, or a surface is too hot

Use the dated records alone and say aloud that they are records of another day, weaker than touching because the hand cannot feel a picture. A surface that feels hot or uncertain to the teacher is never offered to a child. The teacher checks first and replaces it with the record.

## Day 52: the class surface-observation records

The class records from days 44 to 48 are kept unchanged on the wall and read back in the closing explanation. The unfamiliar dated cards for the held-back items are separate and are made from a place withheld from teaching (see cards-assessment-warmth.md).

## Teacher background

Sunlight reaching a surface warms it. Surfaces differ and the hand tells only warmer, cooler or about the same. A shaded sample is warmed less because the sunlight is blocked, not because it is cold. Dark and hard surfaces in direct sun, such as pavement or metal, can burn even on a mild day (read 2026-11-19, CPSC playground burn fact sheet), so the teacher touches first and children touch briefly.
