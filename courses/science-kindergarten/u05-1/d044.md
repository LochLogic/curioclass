# Day 44: Which slide is warmer?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Touch both slides (or benches) yourself with the back of your hand and clear them. Hang the charts from units 1, 3 and 4. Put argument mats and a sun mat at each seat. Key: key-exits-44-48.

### Safety and set-up

You touch first. Children touch briefly with the back of a hand, for a count of three. Stay in sight and touch nothing unknown. Never look at the sun. Water break after.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Day 43 left strips G to J on the mats. Read each once, quietly, 30 seconds each. Each child alone revises their own argument mat from the strip that fits, at most two. No new interview.

#### 3 min. Retrieval

Each question goes to two of the rotating six, 20 seconds each; all show a hand motion. "What changed the ball's motion?" [[s05-P44-1]] "After trying the ramp, what did we do?" [[s05-P44-2]] "Say: I think ___, because I saw ___" about the chart [[s05-P44-3]]. Point a miss back to its chart.

#### 10 min. Out and touch the pair

Say: "Today is a real look." Two minutes out. Two lines of fifteen, one at each slide: each child touches that surface for a count of three, the lines swap, each touches the other [[s05-P44-4]]. About 2.5 minutes. Two minutes back.
**Ask.** "Why is the slide in the sun warmer than the one under the tree?" Any answer; a pass is fine. Expect: the sun is on it.

#### 4 min. Question board

Turn the children's notices into three picture questions: what is different? How can we compare fairly (day 45)? Does water do the same (day 46)?

#### 5 min. Start the picture model

Children direct you: the sun, an arrow to the sunny slide, none to the shaded one. Each child draws it on their mat [[s05-P44-5]]. Hear the rotating six, 15 seconds each. The sentence stays tentative: "I think the sunny slide is warmer, because the sun is on it and I felt it."

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "this is invented for practice, and not scored." Ask [[s05-X44a]]; everyone touches the card at once. Then [[s05-X44b]] for the rotating six. Mark on the scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

No sun, or a surface too hot: show the dated photographs with the recorded comparison and say they are weaker than touching.

### At home

**Swap.** No slide: use two steps or two benches. Check them first yourself.

**Skip.** The lines, the question board and the retrieval.

- **8 min. Touch.** A grown-up checks a place in the sun and a place in the shade; stay with a grown-up outdoors and never look at the sun. Your child touches each with the back of a hand for a count of three [[s05-P44-4]].
  **Say.** "Which felt warmer?" Expect: the one in the sun.
- **10 min. Draw.** Your child draws the sun and an arrow to the warmer place [[s05-P44-5]].
- **7 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X44a]] and [[s05-X44b]] Say they are invented; ask once each.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one board question. Never cut the touch, model or exit. Longer: find a second shaded place.

### Words and supports

Words: sun, shade, warmer, cooler, same. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing and any language count.

### Tomorrow

Day 45 opens with repairs in pairs: error A (the sun cannot warm things), trace the sunlight to the warmer picture, 2 minutes; error H (later makes it warm), compare sun and shade at one look, 1 minute.
