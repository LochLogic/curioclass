# Day 46: Do rocks and water warm too?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Thirty minutes ahead, set out a rock pair and a shallow water pair for each group, one sunny and one shaded, set down together. You alone fill the trays; no pouring. Touch every sample first and clear it. Lay out the afternoon-look photographs.

### Safety and set-up

Children touch cleared samples briefly, back of the hand. Trays sit on a stable surface; wipe spills at once with table cloths. Never look at the sun.

### The day

#### 4 min. Return and repair

Day 45's exit showed error C and error G. Two minutes each, in pairs with the day 45 pairs. For error C, swap one sample for the same material, so both are soil or both sand. For error G, match the time: put both out together.

#### 3 min. Afternoon record

Show the day 45 afternoon photographs and read each caption aloud. Children point to the warmer one [[s05-P46-1]]. "About the same" is a fine answer.

#### 9 min. Touch rock and water

Six groups at once, each at its own rock pair and water pair. Each child touches the four samples, about 8 seconds each. No pouring; a child may pass.

#### 3 min. Mark and say

Each child marks rock and water on the mat: sunny warmer, shaded warmer, or about the same [[s05-P46-2]]. The rotating six give one reason, 20 seconds each [[s05-P46-3]]. Expect: the sun was on it. If a child says later, go back to the pair at one look.

#### 5 min. Revise the model

Rock and water rows join sand and soil. The class dictates a sentence only as far as the records show: "Sunlight warms sand, soil, rock and water." A water tie stays "about the same, look again". Each child draws the sun and an arrow to the warmer sample for rock and water [[s05-P46-4]].

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "these cards are invented for practice, and not scored." [[s05-X46a]] then [[s05-X46b]]; all touch at once. Mark the rotating six on the scale.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

You empty the trays. Wipes at tables.

### Other rooms

No sun or a hot sample: the dated photographs with the recorded comparison, said to be weaker. No trays: a reused shallow food container.

### At home

**Swap.** No rock or tray: a flat stone and a shallow bowl, two of each. A grown-up checks them first.

**Skip.** The groups, the photographs and the clean-up.

- **12 min. Pairs.** Put one stone and one bowl of water in the sun, one of each in the shade, set out together. After 30 minutes your child touches the four [[s05-P46-2]].
  **Say.** "Why did that one feel warmer?" [[s05-P46-3]] Expect: the sun was on it.
- **8 min. Draw.** Draw the sun and an arrow to the warmer stone and the warmer water [[s05-P46-4]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X46a]] and [[s05-X46b]] Say they are invented; ask once each.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: rock only. Never cut the touch, the model or the exit. Longer: look again after clouds.

### Words and supports

Words: rock, water, warmer, cooler, about the same. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing and any home language count.

### Tomorrow

Day 47 opens with 2 minutes each: error B (water never warms), put the two water records side by side; error H (later makes it warm), sun and shade at one look. Then the class asks what a shade must do.
