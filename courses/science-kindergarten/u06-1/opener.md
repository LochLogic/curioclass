# Section 6.1 opener: where litter goes, and a school action

## What the section does

Four days, 55 to 58. The unit asks one question: where does the litter in our schoolyard come from, and what can we do about it? On day 55 the class walks a marked patch after lunch, counts the litter in each zone before anything is picked up, and starts a picture model of where a piece might go. Day 56 follows four original picture chains (paper, a bottle, a wrapper in a stream, a turtle and a plastic ring) to the land, water, air and living things they can touch, then chooses one school action and begins it. Day 57 shows with clean objects how that action lessens one effect. Day 58 asks the class what the action would change in a corner it has not seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 minutes. On day 55 six groups of five each own one zone of the patch and one tally card. Six children, one from each group, take a 2 minute shift with a glove and a grabber, in two shifts of three. The teacher alone bags. Everyone else sits in sight and says "I saw ___." On day 56 the teacher says each picture chain aloud and children point to what changes. The class chooses an action by hand, the teacher touching each hand once. On days 57 and 58 every child draws, points and says "If we ___, then ___." Every exit is spoken, with picture cards that are invented for practice; say that aloud first. The teacher hears a rotating six a day.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Seeing is not guessing.** "I saw" is for the patch. "It might" is for a journey. A dashed arrow marks a might.
- **Litter can move.** Wind and rain can carry loose pieces. The picture shows what can happen, never what did.
- **Each effect has a part.** Land, water, air and living things. A child may speak to one part; the class model holds all four.
- **An action leads to an effect.** If we do this, then less of that. The arrow names both.
- **Moving litter is not caring for it.** Shrubs are a place where living things are; litter tucked there is still litter.
- **Recycling depends on the place.** Our bin takes what it takes. Never "every bottle goes everywhere."
- **No one is blamed.** Nobody is named as the dropper. No child is asked about home waste or recycling.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Litter stays where it fell." **B:** "Reusing paper does not save new paper." **C:** "Litter in the shrubs is safe." **F:** "Every bottle is recycled everywhere."
- D, E, G and H are met in section 6.2.

## Safety

Teacher inspects the route, gloves and grabbers the day before, and the sinks. Non-latex gloves. Only dry paper or intact plastic that the teacher has cleared; glass, sharp things, food, bodily waste and unknown containers are never touched. Stay in sight, closed shoes, grabbers low and away from faces. Wash hands after the pick-up and after handling samples. If outside is unsafe, use dated photographs of the same bounded area and say they are photographs.

## Supplies

Six pairs of gloves, six grabbers, an adult bag, six tally cards, crayons, hand wipes, the class picture chart, the four picture chains, the three action pictures, the accepted-materials picture, the reminder cards, the exit cards, one clean cap-free plastic bottle for each group, reused paper, drawing paper. A room with nothing uses photographs for the patch and drawn bins and bottles for the clean samples.
