# Four picture chains (day 56)

Invented for practice, said aloud first. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no printed words except one caption each, no brand names. Children and adults in the pictures are of many skin tones, hair and abilities. One actor in each chain, so a child can say who did what. The teacher says each chain aloud, three minutes each, and children point to what changes. A picture shows what can happen, never what did: say "can" and "might", and mark a dashed arrow for a might. Nobody is blamed and no family is named.

## Chain 1: a tree and paper (land, living things)

**Frame 1.** A tall tree in a wood, a bird's nest on a branch. **Frame 2.** A worker with a saw cuts the tree (the worker is the only actor). **Frame 3.** A stump and bare ground, the nest gone.
**Spoken:** "A tree can be cut to make paper. When a tree is cut, the land is bare and the bird loses a place to live. People also plant new trees." **Marks:** land and living things.

## Chain 2: making a bottle (air)

**Frame 1.** A pile of raw material going into a factory building (the factory is the only actor). **Frame 2.** The same building with a gray haze above its chimney and over the nearby sky. **Caption:** a bottle comes out.
**Spoken:** "Making a bottle uses materials, and a factory can make the air hazy, and many people work to make it cleaner." The haze is a can. **Marks:** air.

## Chain 3: a wrapper in the wind (water)

**Frame 1.** A wrapper lies on the ground beside a playground, a stream beyond a fence. **Frame 2.** Wind lines and a dashed arrow carry the wrapper toward the stream (the wind is the only actor). **Frame 3.** The wrapper floats in the stream.
**Spoken:** "Wind or rain might carry a loose wrapper to the stream." The arrow is dashed because it is a might. **Marks:** water.

## Chain 4: a turtle and a plastic ring (living things)

**Frame 1.** A turtle on a pond bank, a plastic ring lying in the grass. **Frame 2.** The turtle's leg is caught in the ring (the turtle is the only actor). The chain ends here.
**Spoken:** "Litter can hurt a living thing. A plastic ring can catch a turtle's leg." **Marks:** living things.

## On the class drawing

Each chain adds a mark (land, water, air or living things) to the class drawing, with an arrow from the action to the effect, dashed if it is a might.
