# Day 55: Where does our litter go?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Walk the route the day before; check gloves, grabbers and both sinks. Pick six landmark zones.

### Safety and set-up

Each group of five has a tally card and crayons; its one collector wears non-latex gloves and holds a grabber. You hold the bag. Collectors touch only dry paper or intact plastic you cleared; glass, sharp things, food and unknown containers stay put. Grabbers low. Stay in sight.

### The day

#### 3 min. Hook and question

At the door ask aloud: "Where does the litter in our schoolyard come from, and what can we do about it?" Guess nobody; day 54 handed nothing here. Say: "Stay in sight; touch nothing unless I say." Count rule: litter is loose waste bigger than a bottle cap; leaves and sticks are not litter.

#### 2 min. Out

Groups stand in their zones.

#### 6 min. Tally

Before any pick-up, each group points once at each piece; one child marks the card: paper, bottle or plastic, other. The marker changes each row. Children count marks by touching each once; you count above ten. Write the weather and who is away. A low count is real: never add litter.

#### 4 min. Pick-up

Two shifts of three collectors, 2 minutes each. Clear each piece; you alone bag it. The rest sit in sight and say "I saw ___." Hear six.

#### 5 min. Back and wash

Walk in; one wipe each at the door. Collectors wash at the two sinks, three at a time, 45 seconds each.

#### 3 min. Retrieval, unscored

Children answer with a finger toward the kept chart. Ask: "Which picture row has more?" Then: "What comes after we try an idea?" Then: "Which part of the place gives the fish its water?" A miss: point to the chart, ask again.

#### 4 min. Start the model

On the class chart scribe the zone totals as rows, the schoolyard with litter, one dashed arrow where a piece might go ("a might: we did not see it"), and "I think ___, because I saw ___".

#### 3 min. Exit

Say it is invented. Show the paved corner. Ask [[s06-X55a]]; children mark box one. Ask [[s06-X55b]]; they draw a scrap and an arrow in box two. Hear six. A miscount: recount with a partner. "It stays where it fell" is error A.

### Other rooms

Dated photographs of the area, said to be photographs; no pick-up.

### At home

**Swap.** No patch: draw one corner with five scraps and a puddle. Handle no litter.

**Skip.** The tally cards, the pick-up and the charts.

- **10 min. Count.** Show your child your drawing. Your child touches each scrap once.
  **Say.** "How many pieces?" Expect: the last number said.
- **10 min. Might.** Your child draws a dashed arrow from one scrap.
  **Say.** "Where might one go?" Expect: toward the puddle or another place; "it stays" earns "could wind move it?"
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X55a]] [[s06-X55b]] Say it is invented; ask once each.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one shift only. Never cut the tally, the might arrow or the exit. Longer: count a second kind of litter.

### Words and supports

Words: litter, might. Frames: "I saw ___." "It might go ___." Pointing and any home language count.

### Tomorrow

Day 56 hears this exit first; if many said "it stays where it fell" (error A), trace a might arrow on three pictures.
