# Eats cards and food match cards (day 34)

One set for each table. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no printed words except a caption, no brand names. Children in the pictures are of many skin tones, hair and abilities. No food is in the room. No graphic feeding picture.

## The eats sort (eats or does not eat)

- **E1. A squirrel holding an acorn.** Our drawing, if the credited photograph is not used.
- **E2. A cottontail eating clover in a lawn.** A National Park Service photograph, "A young cottontail eating lunch, wary of being observed", credit printed: NPS Photo / T. Withers. The card is a photograph of a rabbit eating plants. If the photograph is not printed, use our own drawing of a rabbit with clover.
- **E3. A person eating apple slices at snack.** Our drawing of the teacher's own snack, never a child's lunch. No child is pictured and no home.
- **E4. The class plant being watered.** Our drawing: a cup of water over the pot.
- **E5. The shelf plant, now in the light.** Our drawing.

The two rows: eats (E1, E2, E3) and does not eat (E4, E5). The children count each row, touching each once, and say the last number.

## What the teacher says

"Animals eat. A plant is not fed a meal. It took in water and light." Never say a plant needs no food.

## Food match cards

Six cards in two sets: animals (a squirrel, a rabbit, a goat) and foods (an acorn, clover, grass). Different animals match different foods. The children match each animal to its food, then say which two differ. A rotating six says it aloud.
