# Section 4.1 opener: which needs repeat?

## What the section does

Four days, 32 to 35. The class's own fall photographs (a weed growing out of a cracked sidewalk and a hole a squirrel dug) stand beside the two class plants, and the children turn what they notice into questions on the question board. Day 33 asks what a plant and an animal both take in (water) and what the plant records show (light). Day 34 sorts who eats: animals eat, a plant is not fed a meal, and different animals eat different foods. Day 35 is a fluency day: a second place, the water near town, is checked against the three rows. Nobody is scored. Each day ends with two spoken exit questions.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 minutes and uses one rhythm: a short return and repair for the day before, a hook or warm-up, a sort or a drawing at six tables, a short share, the exit and a quick pack. The teacher hears a rotating six children a day and writes what each said in the heard column of the record sheet. Nothing is read by the children. Every word is spoken and every answer is pointed to, sorted, drawn or told in any language.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **An observation is what we saw. A pattern is what we saw again and again, in different records.** One plant shows one plant, so several living things are gathered before a pattern is named.
- **Water: every living thing in the records takes in water.** Plants too, even though they do not drink the way a goat does.
- **Light: the plant records show light.** The two class plants are dated records of these plants over these weeks, not a test that proves a rule about every plant.
- **Eating: animals eat and a plant is not fed a meal.** Different animals eat different foods.
- **Say what the pictures show, no more.** If the two plants look alike, say so aloud: that is a result too.
- **Not here:** photosynthesis, gases, food chains, growth measures and any deprivation test. Never say plants need no food.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Plants do not drink." A watered pot stands up beside a drooping one.
- **B:** "Water is enough." The window plant and the shelf plant, both watered, side by side.
- **C:** "The sunflower is eating the water." The goat eats; the sunflower is watered.
- **D:** "They all eat the same." The cow has hay and the cat has fish.

## Safety

The teacher alone handles pots, watering and soil; children look. No tasting or sniffing anything unknown, wash hands after soil, and check for allergies before a plant is chosen. Both plants are spider plants, checked as non-toxic to pets and people, and are inspected for mould the day before. No plant is left to suffer: the shelf plant is back in light before day 32. No food is brought in and no animal is handled.

## Supplies

Two class plants, the dated plant photographs, the saved fall photographs, the water and light cards, the eats cards, the park pond set, exit cards, repair cards, drop and sun marks, crayons and drawing paper. A room with nothing uses the dated photographs and the cards, and the teacher says aloud that no plant is in the room.
