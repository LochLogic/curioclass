# Dated plant photograph record (days 32, 33 and 41)

A record the teacher makes, not a test. About three weeks before day 32 the teacher sets up two spider plants, matched in size and both watered the same way. One stays at the window in normal light. The other sits on a dark shelf. The teacher alone handles the pots, waters both on the same days and takes a dated photograph of each from the same spot, once a week. The shelf plant is moved back into the light before day 32. No plant is left to suffer.

## What goes on the record

One row for each date: the date written large, a photograph of the window plant, a photograph of the shelf plant, and a one-line note in the teacher's words about what is seen (greener, paler, the same). A plain label on each: "window plant" and "shelf plant", with the word "both watered" beside the date.

## How to say it

Say aloud on days 32, 33 and 41: "These are these two plants over these weeks. They are not every plant." If the photographs show a clear difference, say what it is and no more. If nothing clear shows, say: "We do not see a clear difference. That is a result too," and do not keep any plant in the dark longer to get one.

## If nothing clear shows: the invented practice record

A second record, made by us and said aloud to be invented: two pots of the same bean seedling, both watered each day, one by a bright window and one in a closed box. Photograph one, after a week: the window seedling stands green; the boxed seedling is pale and bent. Photograph two, after a week in light: the boxed seedling is greener. Caption on the page: "Invented to show what a record looks like." This card is only used when the real photographs show nothing clear, and the teacher says so.

## A room with nothing

Use the dated photographs alone, and say aloud that no plant is in the room.

## Teacher background

Spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum) is a common houseplant, with long strappy leaves, often striped, that is commonly listed as non-toxic to cats and dogs; check your own plant before use. Even so, children observe and the teacher handles the pots, and the teacher checks for allergies first.
