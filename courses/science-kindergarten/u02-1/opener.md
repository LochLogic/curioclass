# Section 2.1 opener: what our weather records show

## What the section does

Five days, 10 to 14. The unit question is: why do we need our coats at morning recess but not after lunch? On day 10 the class meets two photographs of the coat hooks, one from morning recess and one from afternoon recess, beside that day's row of the class weather chart. Nobody is told an answer. Pip and the teacher read one dated chart row, the class starts a picture graph, and a sheet called "What we have seen again and again" is begun and left empty. Day 11 plans a morning look and an afternoon look at one spot. Day 12 touches one safe bench in the afternoon and compares it with the morning mark. Day 13 sorts dated squares into rows and counts them once each. Day 14 counts the class's first complete month and revises the pattern sheet. Nothing is scored. The record sheet notes what each child shows, with the scale shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 minutes. Day 10 has no warm-up: the hook, a short retrieval of the unit 1 three-box strip and the first chart row come first. Days 11 to 14 open with a short warm-up or a repair for the last exit, then teaching, then the children's work in groups of five at table copies, a two-minute clean-up and a three-minute spoken exit. Pip the puppet speaks one line at a time. Every ask is spoken; children point, touch, sort, draw or say. Any child may pass.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A pattern is what shows up again and again in records.** One day, or two, raises a question and answers none. The coat photographs are a question.
- **Warmth is a feel with three words: warmer, cooler, about the same.** The class does not use a number scale. Only a clear difference is marked; an unclear one is written "about the same".
- **"It varies, so we check each day" is a good finding.** Do not teach "mornings are cooler". The class decides from what it has seen, and says "this bench, this afternoon".
- **A bench is not the air.** Day 12 shows how a surface felt. It cannot show an air pattern, and the class says so.
- **Count each date once.** Touch each square once and slide it away; the last number says how many. Children count to ten with help. The teacher says larger totals and rows longer than ten are compared by matching squares.
- **A day can sit in two rows.** A rainy and windy day is one day in two rows, so rows cannot be added to get days.
- **Another observer's records.** The exits use pictures made from the records of a weather station at Denver International Airport, from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive. Every picture master carries that credit and the dates. Their key is teacher-only and is in the exit cards.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Afternoons are always warmer." The class has seen only some days. Lay a chart row where the afternoon was cooler or the same beside a row where it was warmer, and say "it varies, so we check each day".
- **B:** "Five rainy days" when a square was touched twice. Touch each square once and slide it into the box.
- **C:** "Two days, because it is in two rows." Put the same date square into both rows and say "one day, two rows".
- **Not a letter:** a pointed guess without a reason, or a matching slip, is marked "shown with help". No child is the example of a mistake. Wrong answers are voiced by Pip or "someone might say".

## Safety

Days 11 and 12 go outdoors: stay in sight, touch nothing unknown, never look at the sun. If the weather is unsafe (thunder, ice, unsafe heat, poor air) the look is made from a window away from the glass and the touch is dropped. The teacher alone checks the bench first, with the back of a hand held a few seconds; no one touches metal in direct sun. Children use the back of one hand briefly on a safe surface. Wipe hands afterward. On day 11 the teacher alone photographs a sidewalk weed and an animal-dug hole if one is in sight, for a later unit, with no child in the picture.

## Supplies for the section

The class weather chart and its squares, scrap-paper squares for bought cards, drawing paper, crayons, masking tape for one taped spot, a bench or low slide, hand wipes and a bin, Pip, and the cards and sheets in this folder. A room with nothing: scrap-paper squares do everything. Indoors, make the looks at a window and drop the touch; dated photographs and notes are weaker than looking, and the chart says so. If the morning mark is missing, use the invented bench pair on day 12, said aloud to be invented, and write "no real reading today" on the chart.
