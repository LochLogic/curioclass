# Day 51: Does our cover work at another patch?

## Teacher page

### Before class

At the look before the lesson, at a second sunny spot, set a matched open rock for each group and move that group's cover over a matching rock with its toy, both touched alike first and out the same time, at least 30 minutes. Check the route. Afternoon is not always warmer; say nothing of the time of day to the class. Key: key-exits-49-54.

### Safety and set-up

Children touch only cleared rocks, briefly, back of the hand; one too warm for your hand at a count of three is not cleared (shade it or use the photograph). Outdoor check the day before (safety page). Walk, do not run; rocks stay low. Stay in sight. Use rocks too large to mouth. Never look at the sun. Water break after.

### The day

#### 4 min. Return and repair

Day 50's exit showed error D and error E. Two minutes each, in pairs. For error D, trace the shadow edge with a finger onto the patch. For error E, pick the covered-and-open comparison over the picture of the cover.

#### 4 min. Out and back

Two minutes each way, each group carrying its cover on the tub lid.

#### 9 min. Second patch

Each child touches the covered and the open rock [[s05-P51-1]]. Five children a group, about 30 seconds each, groups at once: 2.5 minutes. A tie is marked no clear difference. Never say which you expect.

#### 4 min. Mark and say

Each group says which patch felt warmer, in relative words [[s05-P51-2]]: "The open one is warmer," or "about the same."

#### 3 min. Check the sentence

**Ask.** "Does our sentence hold at this second patch?" [[s05-P51-3]] If every pair agrees, say so. If one differs, revise the sentence only as far as the marks show.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say "these cards are invented for practice." [[s05-X51a]] then [[s05-X51b]]; all touch or say at once. Mark the rotating six on the scale.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Rocks and covers to the tub, hand wipes.

### Other rooms

No second spot or sun: read aloud the dated photographs of a covered and an open rock at another place, said to be weaker than touching.

### At home

**Swap.** No rocks: two flat stones. A grown-up checks each first.

**Skip.** The groups, the walk and the carrying.

- **12 min. Another patch.** Stay with a grown-up and never look at the sun. Use a stone too large to mouth. Put a flat stone in the sun at a new spot, a paper roof over it, and another beside it, open. After 30 minutes a grown-up checks for heat, then your child touches both briefly with the back of a hand and marks which felt cooler [[s05-P51-1]].
  **Say.** "Does the roof still keep it from warming as much?" [[s05-P51-3]] Expect: yes, or no clear difference.
- **8 min. Draw.** Draw the sun, the roof and an arrow to the warmer stone.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X51a]] and [[s05-X51b]] Say they are invented; ask once each.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two groups touch at a time. Never cut the touch, the Ask or the exit. Longer: move one piece and touch again.

### Words and supports

Words: covered, open, cooler, shaded, patch. Frame: "The ___ patch felt warmer." Pointing and any home language count.

### Tomorrow

Day 52 opens with 1.5 minutes each: error D (a cover beside it shades it), trace the shadow edge onto the patch; error H (later means the sun did it), touch the sunny and the shaded sample at one look. Then the class says why the slide in the sun is warmer, and each child is observed.
