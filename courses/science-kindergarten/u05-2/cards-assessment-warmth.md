# Unfamiliar dated warmth cards (day 52)

Held back, never taught from. The exhibit keeper and the exhibit are invented for practice; say that aloud and print "invented for practice" on the exhibit page. The dated records on the cards are real. Plain black line drawings for the exhibit scene; the record photographs are the teacher's own, with no identifiable child in any. Children in the drawings are of many skin tones, hair and abilities.

## Collecting the records

Before the unit, at a school place withheld from teaching, the teacher collects fresh dated pairs for sand, soil, one smooth rock and shallow water: a sunny and a shaded sample of each, at a morning look and at an afternoon look. For each pair the teacher records the material, the date, the place, the time of the look, the time the samples were out, the starting conditions and the observer's touch comparison: "sunny warmer", "shaded warmer" or "about the same". Keep water depth, sample amount and containers alike within a pair. Never infer warmth from a photograph. Never write a reading that was not felt. If a pair is a tie, the caption says so and the card stays in the set. Never replace a weak result with the expected one.

## The cards

- **Card S, sand pair (morning and afternoon).** Two photographs and the caption for each look.
- **Card O, soil pair (morning and afternoon).** The same.
- **Card R, rock pair (morning and afternoon).** The same.
- **Card W, water pair (morning and afternoon).** The same.
- **Card U, the unlike pair.** A sunny rock and a shaded tub of water, taken from the sun and shade of one look, for W1a.
- **Card T, the time record.** For each of the four sunny samples, the morning look and the afternoon look side by side, each with its caption, for W3a.

## W1a to W3b

**W1a.** "The exhibit is invented for practice; the dated records are real. Our exhibit keeper asks whether sunlight changes warmth. Touch the fair pair." Offer card S beside card U.
**W1b.** "Mark which sand sample felt warmer at the morning look." Read the morning caption on card S aloud once first.
**W2a to W2d.** Read the four cards' dated captions aloud once. Each child answers one card, set by the record sheet count (sand, soil, rock, water, then sand again), about 15 seconds: "Tell me one reason the sunny sand (or soil, rock, water) and the shaded one felt the way the cards show."
**W3a.** Read each caption on card T aloud once first. "For each sunny sample, touch the look where it felt warmer than its shaded match. Touch both looks if both say so, and neither if neither does."
**W3b and W3c.** "Touch a pair where the sunny one felt warmer, to show the sun did it." then "Say why that pair shows the sun did it."

## The keys follow the records

A key for W1b, W2 and W3 is whatever the caption on the real card says: accept the mark the caption gives, a tie when the caption says tie; a mark against the caption is shown with help. For W2 a reason fits when it names sun and shade for a pair captioned sunny warmer, or "look again" for a pair captioned about the same at both looks. If the teacher's pairs show a tie, the key is a tie. Write the key for each card on the teacher copy before the day, from the caption, and never change the caption to fit an answer.

## A room with nothing

Use dated photographs of pairs collected on another day, labeled as media, and say aloud that the hand cannot feel a picture.
