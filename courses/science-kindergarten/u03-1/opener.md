# Section 3.1 opener: what is the problem, and does the idea work?

## What the section does

Five days, 20 to 24. Day 20 opens with a ball that rolls down a low card ramp and stops short of a block tower. The class asks what needs to change before anyone builds, learns the three steps (what is the problem, try an idea, test it) with a hand motion for each, and draws the stop and the wanted place. Day 21 asks the question cards, including a new goal card and a materials card, watches each child's roll, and says the problem in one sentence: "The ball stops ___ the tower. We want it to ___." Day 22 builds one change, tests it and marks reached or not reached in a row of squares, and watches two pushes leave side by side. Day 23 tries the same build with a larger ball. Day 24 places a bumper, draws the path before and after the touch, and ties each change on the design chart to a result picture. Section 3.2 begins on day 25.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 minutes. Days 21 to 25 open with a short return and repair for the day before. Teaching is on the rug, children's work is in six groups of five at table sets, and three groups test while three draw, then swap. The teacher checks each build before it rolls and hears a rotating six. An exit of three minutes is spoken and pointed to; every child has picture cards. Clean-up (wipes, bags, eye protection) is inside the day.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A problem is what people want to be different.** It is not a thing to build. One goal has many good solutions, so we say the want first and the idea after.
- **Ask first.** A question the cards or a safe try can answer (where should it stop, what can we use) tells us what to test.
- **Farther is not faster.** A harder push usually starts faster and also stops farther on this floor, but where a ball stops is where the trip ended and how it started is only seen at the line. Speed is judged by watching two pushes leave side by side, never from the stopping place.
- **A bumper changes the path where it touches.** A bumper near the path does nothing until the ball meets it.
- **A ramp that reached once may not reach again.** Soft balls bounce and lanes vary. We do not explain why; we roll more than once and keep the misses. A miss is a result, not a mistake.
- **No friction, no gravity, no numbers for speed or push.** The class changes a ramp, a push or a bumper and sees what happens.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Let's build it now." Put the want picture before the idea picture and say the order.
- **B:** "We need a stool." Swap the tool card for the want card.
- **C:** "It went farther, so it was faster." Two lanes, two pushes, watch the start; then lay the stopping dots beside it.
- **D:** "The wall was close, so it turned." Roll past a bumper with a gap, then into it; put a finger on the touch.
- **E:** "It looks strong, so it worked." Match the result picture to the want picture, yes or no.
- **F:** "It reached once, so it always will." Lay the reached and missed pictures side by side and say sometimes.

## Safety

Balls are large soft balls, too big to mouth; no marbles. Ramps are folded card on two low books on the floor. The teacher alone cuts card, removes staples and tapes the lanes the day before. Children roll without throwing, keep faces back, stay outside the lanes when the teacher rolls, and retrieve a ball only when it has stopped. Stop and lower the ramp after any bounce or unstable support. Blocks fall away from hands and nobody aims toward people. Eye protection goes back in its bag at clean-up as in unit 1.

## Supplies for the section

For each of six groups of five: two large soft balls (and a larger ball on day 23), a folded card ramp, two low books, tape, three blocks, a card bumper from day 22, five sheets and crayons. For the room: Pip, chart paper, the design chart, the want and idea cards, the goal and materials cards, the idea and guess cards, the reached rows, the repair cards and the exit cards. A room with nothing: one soft ball and one folded-card ramp for the teacher alone, with labelled pictures of the stops. Children read and mark the pictures; this cannot show children testing, so say so.
