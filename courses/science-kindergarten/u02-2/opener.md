# Section 2.2 opener: forecasts, and getting ready

## What the section does

Five days, 15 to 19. Day 15 opens with a repair for day 14, then a short invented picture read-aloud, Before We Go Out, in which a child asks when and an office helper answers with a real forecast page. The class learns to ask "I want to find out ___" of a page, using four question cards, and keeps any question the page cannot answer on a "not on this page" card. Day 16 asks a new question of a second page for the same day. Day 17 reads old storm records for two places beside today's page, then chooses actions (plan indoor play before the afternoon; go into the school building if thunder is heard) and redraws the cause-and-effect chain as four boxes: records, patterns, prediction, plan. Day 18 observes every child on weather patterns, in six groups of four minutes. Day 19 observes every child on forecasts, in six groups of three minutes, and returns captions. Day 20, in the next unit, reads the chart again.

## How the days run

Each day is 30 minutes. Days 15 to 17 open with a repair or a warm-up, then teaching with a calm picture page, then children's work in groups at table copies, a one-minute pack and a three-minute spoken exit. Days 18 and 19 are observed: two minutes of set-up, six groups in turn, and the others sort familiar weather pictures. Every child has an own mat; the teacher hears one reason from each child and marks the one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child may point, use communication symbols, draw or say a question in any language.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A forecast says what may happen later.** It is made from records of past patterns and the latest observations, such as a radar picture. It can be wrong in either direction, so the school plans before and still follows its plan when thunder is heard.
- **The page says possible.** A forecast describes what could happen. A warning does not cause a storm.
- **Ask a question the page can answer.** When, where, what could happen, what do we do. A question the page cannot answer (what was the weather last week) is kept as "asked, not on this page" and is never an error.
- **Records show how many days, not damage.** The squares are days when thunder was reported at an airport station in an interval. They say Denver has more such days than Seattle in summer. They do not say a storm will come on any day.
- **Plan before; go in when thunder is heard.** When thunder is heard the school moves into the substantial school building and follows the school's plan for how long to stay. A tree is not shelter.
- **Real pages, invented story.** The weather pages are real, from northeastern Colorado. The recess stories are invented and said aloud to be. Calm pictures only; no disaster accounts. No child is asked about a home or about coats.

## The errors, in plain words

- **D:** "A storm will come, the page said." Put the word possible and the might-or-might-not picture beside the answer.
- **E:** "The forecast tells what happened this morning." Order three cards: what we saw, what the forecast says, what may come later.
- **F:** "We get ready when thunder comes." Move the indoor-play card before the storm card.
- **G:** "Denver and Seattle have the same storms." Match the squares of the two rows.
- **H:** "Stand under a tree." Go into the school building.
- **A and B** return on day 18 from section 2.1 and are revised on day 19; **C** is repaired in day 15's first five minutes.

If a child speaks about a frightening event, thank them quietly and move on; no one is asked to retell one.

## Safety

No outdoor work in this section and no made storm. Pages are paper; the forecast is media evidence. The lightning wording follows the weather service's lightning safety overview: no place outside is safe when thunderstorms are in the area, a substantial building is a safe place, and the school's own plan sets how long to stay inside.

## Supplies for the section

Six table copies of the forecast pages and record squares, question cards, the strip and repair cards, action cards, thirty weather mats and thirty forecast mats, crayons, the caption strips A to H, Pip, chart paper, and the pieces in this folder. A room with nothing: paper pages only and hand-drawn copies of the squares. No screen is needed; one shared device may show the pages large.
