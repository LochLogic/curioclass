# Forecast pages for one day in July (days 15, 16 and 17)

Two real forecast pages drawn as calm picture pages. Six table copies of each. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names. The only printed words are the two caption lines below, which the teacher reads aloud; children do not read.

**Caption printed at the foot of each page (the teacher reads it):** "Curio Class made this picture from the weather service forecast issued on 1 July 2024, in the morning, for northeastern Colorado. It is a Curio Class picture, not the official forecast." Page one says: "hazardous weather outlook, issued 3:46 am". Page two says: "forecast discussion, issued 3:45 am". No change is made to meaning; no percentage and no hazard picture beyond a plain cloud is drawn.

## Page 1: the outlook

**Picture:** A wide plain of flat land with a distant mountain outline at the left edge. Over the plain, one tall cloud with a small storm symbol in it. At the top right, a small sun with a smaller moon beside it, joined by a curved arrow (today and tonight). At the lower left, a small child on a slide with a question mark over a dotted line leading to the school door.

**What it says, in three pictures:** storms are possible today and tonight (sun, moon and cloud), and it is a time to plan (the dotted line to the school).

**Teacher key for the model question:** "I want to find out when the storms could come." The page answers it: today and tonight. "What do we do?" the page does not say outright; the class answers it with the strip: page, plan indoor play before the afternoon, indoor recess.

## Page 2: the discussion

**Picture:** A radar-like picture: a plain map outline with a cluster of small round cloud shapes at the left and a long arrow pointing right, the clouds fading as they go. At the right edge of the map, near the top corner, a single cloud with a storm symbol, and a clock beside it set for the afternoon and evening. Under the map, a small sun coming out.

**What it tells that page one did not:** earlier storms had already moved away (the fading clouds with the arrow), and stronger storms might come later in one corner of the area, in the afternoon and evening.

**Spoken (day 16):** "What can this page tell us that the first one did not?" **Key:** that earlier storms were already moving away, and where. A child who says "it tells what happened" has met the idea of an observation; the same page also says what may come later (error E is not scored here: practice only).

**Teacher note:** never say "a storm will come". Say "the page says storms are possible today". The might-or-might-not picture (page 1) shows one cloud with a question mark.
