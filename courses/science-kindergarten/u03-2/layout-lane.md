# The pretend delivery lane (days 27 and 28)

Held back. No child sees it, no card of it is shown and no part of it is taught from before day 27. It is a pretend delivery lane, said aloud to be invented; the rolls are real.

## The floor layout

One short straight lane on a cleared part of the floor, about the length of the rug, with a start line at one end.
- **The bay.** A broad U of low blocks beside the lane, near the far end, open toward the lane. It is a place to roll into, not a tower to knock down.
- **The walking strip.** Two parallel tape lines beyond the bay, on the side away from the lane, marked with a tape drawing of two footprints. It is a pretend walking strip kept clear of real classroom paths. People want balls delivered into the bay without chasing them into this strip.
- **The ramp.** On day 28 a fixed low card ramp at the start line, on two low books, and a pre-cut card bumper for each group.
- **The station.** One supervised station with the lane and a bay. Only the group at the station handles balls; the teacher alone makes the baseline rolls.

## What the teacher says on day 27 (once, before group 1)

"This is a pretend delivery lane, invented for practice. Here is a low bay of blocks, and here is a taped strip where people walk." Do not say what people want; the goal card says that.

## Baseline rolls on day 27 (the teacher alone)

A gentle push that stops short of the bay, then a second roll that misses the bay and passes it. The children watch from outside the lane.

## Picture of the layout

A bird's-eye line drawing: the lane as a long rectangle, the start line at its left, the U of blocks beside the lane near its right, the walking strip beyond the bay with two footprint marks, an arrow along the lane for the baseline roll. A room with nothing uses this drawing and the teacher's single ball and card ramp, and children mark the drawing instead of rolling; say that this cannot show a child rolling.
