# Section 3.1: Planning tests for lookalikes

## Purpose and science

Use observed properties and measured quantities to ask which tests could distinguish lookalikes. Appearance suggests candidates but cannot establish identity. A property is something we can see or feel about a material; a test can show more. Preserve the sorting chart: one column per property, one row per sample. Keep the choice model wording: job, property tested, what the test showed, our choice.

## Safety and fallback

Powders stay in labelled cups. Ask about food/powder allergies privately before use. Goggles stay on through cleanup; no tasting, sniffing or cooking. Provide trays, soap, towels, approved wipes and two sinks. Thirty 40-second washes across two sinks need ten minutes; collection/goggle care needs two more. Without this provision, teacher handles samples and children use invented records, without firsthand credit.

## Common errors and response

Letters A/B apply only when the stated claim matches. An unexplained answer gets a follow-up, not an assumed letter. Use the scheduled five-minute component move: compare two minutes, annotate two, submit one. More than ten misses replaces that group move with whole-class repair. Never add time or preview a held full response.
