# Day 3: Squeezed air, and a first shadow

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put the six shadow sticks, each in its tub of sand, on marked spots in a sunny place you can supervise. Mark each spot with a paint pen or nail; photograph it. Check each syringe: cap on, plunger moving, cap firm under a gentle press. Set out eyewear, a measure for each group, and chalk. Print the pages. Return day 2's exit ticket (s01-X2a and s01-X2b), marked for errors J, K and H. Open the record sheet.

### Safety and set-up

Eyewear on for every squeeze. The teacher checks each capped syringe. No needles. Press gently, away from faces. Outdoors, children stay in sight, never look at the sun, and carry only a measure. Each stick's place and the clock time are fixed before the class goes out. Clean-up takes 3 minutes inside the day.

### The day

#### 5 min. Syringe safety

Show a capped needle-free syringe. Press the plunger gently, pointing away from everyone. Never aim it at a face, pull the cap off while pressed, or press hard. Eyewear goes on.

#### 23 min. Squeeze and compare

First 2 minutes: reopen the day 1 drawing and ball readings. If many made error A on day 1's exit (s01-X1), lay an empty drawing beside a dotted one and ask which explains the gain.

From day 2's exit ticket, 2 minutes:

**If many made error J:** trace the six pieces from gate to shelf.

**If many made error K:** finish together: "This piece helps because ___ so the shelf can ___."

Groups of five, 10 minutes. Each child reads the syringe, presses gently, reads it again, and draws dots for both on [[s01-P3-1]]. Anyone needing hands-on help slides the dot panel: 12 dots in the wide box, the same 12 in the narrow. No syringe: use record N3 (25 mL, then 15 mL), invented.

Then 4 minutes, another syringe: [[s01-K3-1]]. Students try first, then model, then compare it with their own.

[[steps:s01-K3-1]]

**Ask.** What evidence says the syringe was not empty? Expect: the plunger stopped and pushed back; the volume fell but not to 0. Students write one reason on [[s01-P3-2]]. "It was empty" is error A.

**Ask.** What stayed the same, and what changed? Expect: the number of dots stayed; the space got smaller. Dots that disappear are error I: air does not vanish when it is squeezed. Board: this day answers what squeezing does inside.

#### 10 min. First shadow reading

Outdoors, supervised, all groups at once, each at its own stick. Use today's clock time each month, and write it down. Never look at the sun.

Children measure the shadow from the stick's foot mark to its tip, in cm, and write it on the shadow record with the place and clock time ([[s01-P3-3]]).

**Ask.** Why will next month use the same stick, mark and clock time? Expect: so a different shadow comes from the sun's path, not from a moved stick. A stick put somewhere new is error L. No outdoors: use record N3 (shadow 40 cm), invented.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X3a]] and [[s01-X3b]], alone, on paper. Collect them unscored.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

Syringes back capped. Collect chalk and measures. Wash hands. Drawings stay in folders.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. With no syringe, press a sealed bag gently, away from faces, or use record N3. With no sun, use N3's shadow.

### At home

**Swap.** No syringe: press a sealed zip bag or a bottle, away from faces. No chalk: use tape.

**Skip.** The group syringes and the safety talk with eyewear.

- **12 min. Squeeze.** Press, and do [[s01-P3-1]], then [[s01-P3-2]].
  **Say.** "What shows it was not empty?" Expect: it pushed back and would not close.
- **6 min. Another syringe.** Do [[s01-K3-1]].
  **Say.** "Did any dots go away?" Expect: no, the same dots sit closer together.
- **12 min. Shadow.** Outdoors, never look at the sun. Do [[s01-P3-3]].
  **Say.** "Why the same stick and mark next month?" Expect: so the shadows can be compared.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X3a]] and [[s01-X3b]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the second Ask. Never cut the dots, the evidence question or the exit. Longer: predict what the dots do if the syringe is pulled wider.

### Words and supports

Volume: how much room something takes, in mL. Evidence: what you saw or measured. We draw a few dots; real air has too many to draw, and none are added or taken away. Frame: "I know the syringe was not empty because ___." Read readings aloud. A child who cannot press may direct a partner.

### Tomorrow

Read the exits. Flat air drawn with fewer dots is error I, and a moved stick is error L: day 4 repairs the one most children made, for 3 minutes at the end of Revise. Day 4 squeezes a capped bottle, revises the air drawing and weighs dry soil in a pot.
