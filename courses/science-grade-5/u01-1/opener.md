# Section 1.1 opener: does air weigh anything?

## What the section does

Four days, 1 to 4, of 45 minutes. The class asks the compelling question for the unit and starts a question board. The first question is: what entered the ball? On day 1 the teacher weighs a flat ball, pumps it and weighs it again. The last 10 minutes are the readiness look. Day 2 rebuilds six large blocks into a new object and sets soil to dry. Day 3 squeezes air in a closed syringe, draws it with dots and sets a shadow stick for a monthly reading. Day 4 revises the air drawing and weighs a bean pot in grams. Nothing in these four days is scored. The scored explanation comes at the end of the unit.

## How the days run

Day 1: hook, the weighed ball, a first drawing, readiness. Day 2: repair from the readiness sheets, rebuild, soil. Day 3: syringe, dots, shadow. Day 4: compare drawings, revise, the pot. Each day ends with a four-minute exit ticket. Set-up and clean-up are inside the 45 minutes.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Air is matter.** A pumped ball reads more grams than a flat one. Something with weight went in.
- **Dots stand for parts too small to see.** A dot is never a grain you could pick up. Squeezed air keeps the same dots, closer together.
- **A fair weighing keeps the system the same.** Weigh the same ball before and after the air is pumped in, and dry the soil before it is weighed.
- **A fair monthly reading keeps the stick, the place and the clock time the same.** Never look at the sun.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "Air is nothing." Put an empty drawing beside a dotted one and ask which explains the gain.
- **D:** weighing two different balls. Ask what else differs besides air.
- **H:** wet soil counted as dry soil. Dry it first, then log water on its own line.
- **I:** "Squeezed air disappears." Count the dots in a wide box and in a narrow box.
- **J, K:** pieces lost in a rebuild, or "stiff is good" with no reason.
- **L:** a monthly reading taken with a changed setup.

Wrong answers are voiced by "someone might say", never by a named child.

## Supplies for the section

Ball, hand pump and one needle, tray, gram-reading kitchen scale, eyewear, large blocks, potting soil, trays, needle-free syringes, dot panels, shadow stick, chalk, meter stick, pots, bean seeds, water, a capped plastic bottle with only air in it, and the printed sheets in this folder. Safety: the teacher alone attaches the pump needle and stays below the pressure printed on the ball. The ball stays in a tray. Syringes have no needle and are never aimed at a face. Never look at the sun. Check plant and mold allergies before soil. Wash hands after soil and chalk.

## A room with nothing

Use the constructed records N1 to N4 (records-n1-n4). They are invented for practice, not observations, and are labeled so every time.
