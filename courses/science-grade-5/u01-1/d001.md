# Day 1: Does air weigh anything?

## Teacher page

### Before class

The day before, trial the ball: weigh it flat, pump counted strokes until it is firm but still dents under a thumb, and weigh again. Need a gain of at least 4 g; note the strokes, or try another ball. Let the air out. Print the pages. Cut six card pieces for each child into an envelope. Put up chart paper headed "What entered the ball?" Open the record sheet. Send the family note.

### Safety and set-up

One shared station. The teacher alone attaches the needle and pumps until the ball is firm (or to the gauge reading on it), with eyewear on; children near the pump wear eyewear too. The ball stays in the tray. Dry the scale and ball before weighing. Clean-up takes 2 minutes inside the day.

### The day

#### 12 min. Hook

Whole class at the station. Students predict alone on [[s01-P1-1]]. Zero the scale with the tray on it, weigh the flat ball, and show the display (document camera, or a child holding a large-number card). Write the grams on the board. Pump the counted strokes, take the needle off, and weigh the same ball again. Students write both readings and the gain on [[s01-K1-1]].

[[steps:s01-K1-1]]

**Ask.** What went into the ball? Expect: air; the ball gained grams, so it has weight. If someone says the weight is the same, or air is nothing, despite a clear gain, that is error A: read the two numbers aloud and ask what they say.

**Ask.** Why did we weigh the same ball both times? Expect: so only the air is different; unlike balls differ in more (error D).

Post the unit question, "What is everything made of, if we cannot see the pieces, and does any of it vanish when it changes?", and beside it "What entered the ball?" If the gain is under 3 g, pump 20 more strokes and weigh again. Still under, or no kit: read record N1 (flat 420 g, pumped 424 g after 40 pumps), say it is invented, and keep the real numbers on the board.

#### 17 min. Draw and compare

Alone for 6 minutes, students draw on [[s01-P1-2]]. In pairs for 4, they compare, then each sticks an I-wonder slip on the board. Show two drawings from the room, or draw two: an empty ball, and a ball with arrows of air going in.

**Ask.** Which drawing explains the gain in grams? Expect: the one with something going in.

**Ask.** Was the flat ball empty? Expect: no; it held some air, and the pump added more.

Offer dots as one way to draw parts too small to see. Students add dots on [[s01-P1-3]].

**Ask.** Could you pick up one dot? Expect: no; a dot stands for a part far too small to see.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket

[[s01-X1]], alone, on paper. Collect it unscored.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Ball, pump and needle go to the teacher. Wipe the tray, dry the scale. Drawings stay in folders.

#### 10 min. Readiness check

Hand out the card envelopes and readiness sheets and read the task aloud once. Children work alone for 6 minutes; for the last 4, circulate and mark gap M (missing piece) or P (property use) on the record sheet. Not scored.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, copy the prediction and the readings from the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No scale: use record N1 (flat 420 g, pumped 424 g). No pump: weigh a ball, then a ball with a bag of air taped on, and compare.

**Skip.** Pair talk and the card pieces.

- **5 min. Predict.** Write [[s01-P1-1]].
  **Say.** "Will the flat ball weigh more, less or the same after pumping?" Expect: any guess, with a reason.
- **12 min. Weigh.** Weigh a flat ball, pump it, weigh it again. Write [[s01-K1-1]].
  **Say.** "What went into the ball?" Expect: air, and air has weight.
- **14 min. Draw.** Do [[s01-P1-2]], then [[s01-P1-3]].
  **Say.** "Could you pick up a dot?" Expect: no, a dot is a part too small to see.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]], answered alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the pairs to one minute. Never cut the readings, the dots or the exit. Longer: predict what 10 more pumps add, then test it.

### Words and supports

Air: the gas all around us. Weight: how much the scale reads, in grams. Dot: a part too small to see. Frame: "The ball got heavier because ___ went in." Accept a drawing, a label or dictation.

### Tomorrow

Read the exit tickets. A bottle drawn empty is error A: day 3's opening repairs it if many made it. Two unlike balls is error D: day 2's Rebuild repairs it if more than a third made it. A dot called a grain is error B: day 2's closing question repairs it. The M and P marks set day 2's support or extension.
