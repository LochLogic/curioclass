# Day 72: Which way does midnight face?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read opener.md and teacher-only-answer-keys.md; prepare the generated tag key. Bring day 71's tickets, tallies, symbol labels and pattern notes. Lay out fifteen desk kits. Use orbit-locator.md's upper/lower diagram without its completed log. Side 2 uses component-tags.md's evidence/inference frame, without the later question. Keep current half slips covered. Paper stays on desks in normal room light.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Return day 71 s04-X71, s04-P71-tally, s04-P71-key and s04-P71-pattern privately. Review return and repair. Prioritize the most common gap. For labels, locate a group label, season label and symbol unit on returned work. If many tickets volunteer error D, note that a daily model cannot yet settle the seasonal question; reserve its explanation for today's teaching. Over ten matching gaps makes the move whole-class. Other gaps get private notes during pairs.

#### 7 min. Teaching first

Teach orbit: a path around another object. Earth goes around the sun over a year. Review day 69 spin: Earth turns around its own axis. These are different motions. At a fixed orbital position, spin changes an observer's view through one night. At matched midnight, the observer faces away from the sun.

Use the upper/lower practice locator. Keep far markers fixed; move Earth above, then below the sun. Trace sun through Earth outward. Expect upper, then lower marker. Earth's changed orbit position changes the midnight direction. Do not turn this consistent record into a faulty log. Sizes and distances are not to scale; this flat model cannot predict every star's visibility.

#### 19 min. Viewpoint investigation

Thirteen minutes in pairs: five for layout and predictions, four per partner manipulating the disk and tracing directions on [[s04-P72-view]]. Keep marker positions unchanged. Teacher checks which way the night side faces before children record. If many point sunward, locate the sun-facing side, then the opposite side.

Six minutes replace the unavailable simulation: six selected pairs demonstrate their consistent upper/lower views, one minute each. Others compare with their own seated model. No thirty-child public round; passing is allowed. Do not use conflicting month labels in practice.

#### 8 min. Evidence/model tags

Six minutes alone: [[s04-P72-tag]]. Review grade 4 observation, inference. N71 is supplied evidence, not something this class observed; the orbital explanation is an inference using a desk model. Collect every child's arrows and tag. Retain the shared orbital addition to day 69's Earth-sun-moon model. Two minutes: store pieces; preserve raw charts.

#### 6 min. Fresh evidence

Reveal [[s04-X72]] now. Five minutes alone; one to collect. After class: thirty sixty-second exit readings and thirty thirty-second practice scans take forty-five minutes. Review scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Error D requires the spin-alone claim; reversed directions alone need descriptive feedback. Return on 73.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the locator on paper and copy the held exit separately. Paper pieces replace a globe. Keep far markers stationary; no night travel or digital tool.

### At home

**Swap.** Draw paper Earth and sun circles; use fixed paper star markers.

**Skip.** Six-pair demonstrations.

- **5 min. Return.** Read day 71 notes together.
- **7 min. Model.** Teach orbit with the upper/lower locator.
  **Say.** "Which way does midnight face here?" Expect away from the sun, toward the outer marker.
- **12 min. Views.** Child moves Earth and completes [[s04-P72-view]], then [[s04-P72-tag]].
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s04-X72]] alone. Discuss its answer key afterward.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: fewer public demonstrations; retain both partners' views and exit. Longer: identify a visibility detail this flat model cannot predict.

### Words and supports

Orbit, spin, midnight. Frame: "At midnight, the night side faces __." Read neutrally. Raised fixed markers and a scribe preserve direction choices.

### Tomorrow

Day 73 returns E72, direction arrows and tags in five-minute Return and repair. Error D gets an outward-view move with Earth changing position, far markers fixed. Then teach the moon's own orbit.
