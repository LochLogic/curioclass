# Teacher only: assessment key

Keep private; name in Before class. Never include in student printing.

## s01-E1

Case A: the same dots before and after, closer together while the cushion is flat; the sealed cushion still holds the air. Case B: dots of the solid spread through the water; clear water does not mean the solid is gone. Each case earns four points: retained matter, explanatory arrangement, unseen scale and an explanation linked to the evidence. 8 points in all.

Look-fors: Case A, retained matter: the same dots in the cushion before and after, none vanish | Case A, arrangement: the dots are closer together in less space while flat | Case A, unseen scale: dots stand for parts too small to see, not visible grains | Case A, evidence: the explanation uses the sealed, flattened cushion to say the air is still there | Case B, retained matter: dots of the solid are drawn after it spreads, not gone | Case B, arrangement: the dots of the solid are spread through the water | Case B, unseen scale: dots are smaller than any grain; clear water does not mean nothing is there | Case B, evidence: the explanation uses the sealed pouch and the clear water to say the solid is still inside

Samples: Dots stand for parts smaller than grains. The cushion keeps all its parts in less space. The solid's parts spread through the water. Becoming clear does not prove disappearance. | Nothing remains inside either.

## s01-E2

The empty scale is zeroed, the whole pouch is weighed, a whole-gram value holds on the second reading, and the comparison says the matter was kept, within the limit of a scale that reads whole grams. 4 points, one for each. Score the child's actual readings and reasoning, not exact agreement.

Look-fors: zeroes the empty scale before weighing | weighs the whole pouch, not only the water | records a stable whole-gram value that holds on a second reading | says what the comparison shows about retained matter, with the limit of the scale

Samples: I zeroed the scale. The whole dry pouch settled at 104 g on two readings, matching the earlier 104 g. No change was detected within the scale's whole-gram limit, so the readings support retained matter through warming. | I weighed only the water.

## s01-E3

Heating 122 + 12 = 134 g, so 134/134 g. Cooling 138/138 g; shrinking changes space, not recorded total. Mixing 130 + 15 = 145 g, so 145/145 g. Six points: one matching complete graph and one evidence-linked explanation per change.

Look-fors: heating graph 134/134 g | heating explanation includes sleeve, 122 + 12 = 134 g | cooling graph corrects draft to 138/138 g | cooling explanation separates space from amount | mixing graph 145/145 g | mixing explanation includes pouch, 130 + 15 = 145 g

Samples: Graphs: 134/134, 138/138, 145/145 g. Adding sleeve and pouch restores complete totals. Shrinking does not change the recorded total. | Warming creates matter; packaging does not count.

## s01-E4

Both fall 3 g: 156 - 153 = 3 and 50 - 47 = 3. A possible common scale offset, not proof of lost pouch matter. Zero and verify scale with unchanged check object, then reweigh complete dry pouch. Two points.

Look-fors: identifies paired 3 g drops as evidence of possible scale offset | proposes zero/check then reweighing complete pouch

Samples: Both fell 3 g, suggesting a scale problem. Zero and verify the scale, then reweigh the complete pouch. | The lower reading certainly proves gas escaped.
