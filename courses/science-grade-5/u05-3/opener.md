# Section 5.3 opener

## How the section runs

Each day is 45 minutes. Use the part names and times in the section plan.
The full route is retained; no optional folded meeting is activated.
Paper replaces unavailable optional displays, with zero digital minutes.
Use individual paper for independent evidence, neutral reading or a neutral scribe when needed.
Read each supplied invented record aloud as invented for practice.
Never call a supplied estimate a classroom measurement.
Separate teacher-only keys before making student copies.
Every student may pass in public talk; use no child as the example of an error.
Six 45-second public turns use 4.5 minutes of the five-minute Share part.
Collect all thirty notes, but claim teacher listening only for the six heard.
Cycle the class list over five meetings; partners use simultaneous 45-second turns.
Return all papers on the named day, using handbacks.csv and record.md.
Initial ideas receive descriptive notes; later formative evidence uses the record's one scale.
Use score-record.md for the separate 24-point assessment.
No household water questions, personal hazard stories or water tasting.

## Continuity

Review weathering: breaking rock material down; erosion carries loosened material.
Review runoff (rain that runs over the ground) and key (a box that tells what each mark means).
Review climate as the range of usual weather over many years, including year-to-year changes.
Review earlier system-model boundaries, data-graph labels and scale-proportion conventions.
Directly explain any missing convention; earlier teaching is never presumed mastered.
Review the question board and rotating-six routine, with the formative record stated above.
All held exits use their own new case. Never show covered exit/end/retry sheets as practice.

## Ideas and evidence

Locate a store from its description, not from map area. Groundwater is water in spaces
below land, not a universal underground lake. Glacier means a slowly moving mass of land ice.
Polar ice means ice in polar regions; separate land ice from sea ice.
Fresh water has little dissolved salt; salt water has abundant dissolved salt.
All-water teaching uses about 97 salt and 3 fresh of 100 equal shares.
The fresh-only strip enlarges that subset: about 69 combined ice, 30 fresh groundwater,
and 1 surface and other fresh-water share. These are rounded estimates, not exact class readings.
Combined ice includes ice caps, glaciers and permanent snow, including polar land ice.
The full remainder includes stores beyond lakes and rivers, such as soil moisture and ground ice.
Do not assess atmospheric amounts, percentages or huge volumes.
For the small-store axis, supplied fresh-lake 0.26, river 0.006 and swamp 0.03 endpoints
are parts per 100 fresh-water shares. No child calculates conversions.
Large matching axes preserve small endpoint distinctions; do not round river to zero.
Archive A/B/C exits are invented portions, not competing Earth estimates.
Practice uses loose strips, individual descriptions and one swamp endpoint;
complete grid/bar construction and inset/whole explanations stay independent.
