# Day 118: teacher-only answer key

Teacher only. Keep separate from student copies. Scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Shown requires the listed evidence without scientific prompting; shown with help requires a prompt contributing science content; not yet seen includes missing evidence. Neutral reading, access support and scribing do not change the category; record them separately. No points or totals. Equivalent supported answers count. ## c5-E118-model

Answer: Roots spread through the soil and hold it in place. The exposed face loses soil; rooted soil remains. A labeled plant-land drawing connects roots to retained land material.

Look-fors: Names plant and land parts | Shows roots crossing soil with an action arrow | Uses both observations to explain contrasting soil movement

Meets: My arrow shows roots holding soil. Exposed soil falls; rooted soil stays. | Not yet: Plants and soil are nearby. (error AF)

## c5-E118-coast

Answer: Ocean water transports sand into the cove and deposits it between rocks, changing the land surface. The arrow links water to transported and deposited sand.

Look-fors: Ocean and land connected by an action arrow | Explanation uses transport and settling

Meets: Wave water moves sand into the cove, where it settles. | Not yet: Moving sand is rock breaking. (error AG)

- Moving sand is rock breaking. Error AG: Calls transport weathering rather than movement of existing grains.

## c5-E118-life

## c5-E118-air

Answer: Warmer ocean water transfers warmth to nearby cooler air, limiting nighttime cooling compared with inland air. This explanation fits the observations without proving water is the only influence.

Look-fors: Connects ocean and air through warmth transfer | Uses smaller coastal cooling and warmer-water observations

Meets: My arrow shows warmth from ocean water to nearby air, helping explain less cooling. | Not yet: Invisible air cannot interact. (error AH)

- Invisible air cannot interact. Error AH: Rejects an interacting system because it cannot be seen.

## c5-E118-all

## c5-E118-fresh

Answer: Out of 10,000 fresh-water shares: ice caps, glaciers and permanent snow 6,870; fresh groundwater 3,010; surface and other fresh water 120. Ice is largest, groundwater next. Greenland and Antarctica land ice join the ice category. Other stores are not only lakes and rivers.

Look-fors: Three bars with correct endpoints and labels | Whole is 10,000 fresh-water shares | Ice and groundwater dominance with polar land ice in combined ice category

Meets: Most fresh water is ice or groundwater; polar land ice belongs with glaciers. | Not yet: Ice holds 6,870 of every 10,000 all-water shares. (error AJ)

- Ice holds 6,870 of every 10,000 all-water shares. Error AJ: Changes the fresh-only whole to all water.

- The 120 shares are only lakes and rivers. Error AK: Deletes other stores from the remainder.

## c5-E118-limit

Answer: Global distributions do not identify a local usable supply or establish drinking safety. Ocean water is salty; much fresh water is frozen or underground. Separate access and quality evidence is needed.

Look-fors: States a defensible access or quality limit | Connects it to a graph category or global whole

Meets: The large salt bar does not promise usable fresh water for the greenhouse. | Not yet: Fresh means safe to drink. (error AL)

- Fresh means safe to drink. Error AL: Low salt does not establish drinking safety.
