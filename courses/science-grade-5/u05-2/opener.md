# Section 5.2 opener

## How the section runs

Each day is 45 minutes. Use the part names and times in the section plan.
The full route is retained; no optional folded meeting is activated.
Paper replaces unavailable optional displays, with zero digital minutes.
Use individual paper for independent evidence, neutral reading or a neutral scribe when needed.
Read each supplied invented record aloud as invented for practice.
Never call a supplied estimate a classroom measurement.
Separate teacher-only keys before making student copies.
Every student may pass in public talk; use no child as the example of an error.
Six 45-second public turns use 4.5 minutes of the five-minute Share part.
Collect all thirty notes, but claim teacher listening only for the six heard.
Cycle the class list over five meetings; partners use simultaneous 45-second turns.
Return all papers on the named day, using handbacks.csv and record.md.
Initial ideas receive descriptive notes; later formative evidence uses the record's one scale.
Use score-record.md for the separate 24-point assessment.
No household water questions, personal hazard stories or water tasting.

## Continuity

Review weathering: breaking rock material down; erosion carries loosened material.
Review runoff (rain that runs over the ground) and key (a box that tells what each mark means).
Review climate as the range of usual weather over many years, including year-to-year changes.
Review earlier system-model boundaries, data-graph labels and scale-proportion conventions.
Directly explain any missing convention; earlier teaching is never presumed mastered.
Review the question board and rotating-six routine, with the formative record stated above.
All held exits use their own new case. Never show covered exit/end/retry sheets as practice.

## Ideas and evidence

The tray supplies observations of land directing water and water transporting grains.
Students keep start/finish positions and uncertainty, not a rehearsed reciprocal-arrow answer.
The berm/notch case stays covered until 89's exit.
On 90 isolated map symbols and a dated source claim are distinct evidence types.
The complete P-Q-R route and safety-limits response are held for independent work.
No local map extract is supplied. Use the complete invented Pine Basin fallback,
and show the dated Cedar/Tolt source note separately. Never call this fallback local.
Day 91 rehearses existing displays orally; its new gust/stem display is independent paper evidence.

## Safety and set-up

Before class check sand/glove allergies through the school's confidential adult process.
Do not ask children to disclose illness. Provide a dry observer role without explanation.
Teacher portions clean damp sand, checks its safety sheet, stable trays and eyewash access.
Six simultaneous groups of five each need one shallow tray, two cups damp sand,
one cup water, pouring cup, towels, five splash goggles and five nonlatex glove pairs.
Add soap/wash water, a solid-waste container and teacher-approved goggle-cleaning supplies.
No dust, blowing or tasting; splash goggles and gloves stay on through wet cleanup.
Stop unsafe handling or missing protection. Teacher alone portions dry material.
Five two-minute observation roles plus five shared minutes fit the 15-minute observation part.
After one retry, preserve unclear evidence and read invented T89 separately.
Seven cleanup minutes: two collecting sediment/wiping, five removing gloves/washing.
Six wash points fit five 45-second users plus transition. With fewer, adult alone handles wet material;
children remain dry observers. Teacher finishes goggle cleaning after class at required contact time.
Never pour sediment down sinks. Paper T89 replaces all wet handling when equipment is unsuitable.
At home, one adult handles one protected tray; absent protection uses the same paper record.
