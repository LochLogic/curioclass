# Separate resource-protection cards

Read one card at a time. Keep source locations with component notes.
These are original factual summaries, not a six-card matching exercise.

## Soil and water

Farming can move soil and other materials into runoff.
Ground cover and soil-conservation practices can reduce that movement.
[S06-soil: Nonpoint Source: Agriculture](https://www.epa.gov/nps/nonpoint-source-agriculture), overview and conservation descriptions.

## Water use

Using water efficiently reduces wasted water and helps preserve supplies.
Our public-system example is a repaired leaking public pipe, invented for practice.
[S06-saving: Start Saving](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/start-saving), introduction and leak discussion.

## Habitat

Wetland plants, soil and water provide habitat for living things.
Protecting those connected parts can preserve habitat functions.
[S06-wetlands: Why are Wetlands Important?](https://www.epa.gov/wetlands/why-are-wetlands-important), introduction.

## Litter movement

Land litter can reach waterways and eventually the ocean.
Preventing entry interrupts that route.
[S06-ocean: Where Does Marine Debris Come From?](https://marinedebris.noaa.gov/discover-marine-debris/where-does-marine-debris-come), land-based sources.

## Air

Engines, factories and other operations can release pollutants into air.
Controlling releases at their source targets that route.
[S06-air: Emissions guide](https://www.epa.gov/emission-standards-reference-guide/basic-information-about-emission-standards-reference-guide-road), Overview of Mobile Sources.

## Space

People's equipment can add debris around Earth.
Preventing new debris helps protect near-Earth space.
[S06-space: Debris Mitigation](https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/mitigation/index.html), mitigation measures.
