# Section 2.1: Testing plant matter sources

## Purpose and science

An argument is a claim supported by observations and an explanation connecting them. Begin with the reported willow/soil difference, not a claim that the old experiment proved water alone. Soil weights were re-dried; tree dry weights were not reported. Fallen leaves and air were uncontrolled. Soil-free growing still includes minerals. A cutting can redistribute stored material. Height measures size rather than source or dry gain. Teach air and water as the chief material sources and sunlight as energy before exits ask for that distinction.

## Safety and alternatives

H20, A21, W19 and M19 are supplied practice. One reading retry, then label the supplied line separately. Failed or incompatible earlier readings start a new baseline.

Handling days use the equipment and protection in their day blocks. Check plant/soil/mold and nonlatex-material precautions privately. Adult handlers use eye protection for splash/dust hazards, gloves and apron; wash afterward. Missing protection or safe washing means paper records. Never taste or sniff samples. Never look at the sun. Teacher stores or disposes of deteriorating closed containers under school procedures.

## Common errors and evidence

A: mainly soil. B: water only. C: no minerals. D: light becomes stuff. I: growth identifies the source. Contrast a familiar component, collect an individual correction, retain uncertainty.

The practice sheets keep evidence components separate. Do not demonstrate a complete held answer. Use the source chart to distinguish observation, source mechanism and invented practice. General facts are checked in the course's S02 source rows. Exact practice species relationships and numbers are constructed unless the card says historical.
