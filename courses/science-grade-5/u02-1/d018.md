# Day 18: A tree gains matter

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print family.md, current pages and exit-18.md from the exits-18-22.md list. Cover exits. Keep teacher-keys.md, practice-teacher.md and generated answer keys private; record.md holds feedback. Set historical-card.md beside a blank question board. Have day 17's assigned retries ready. Plan bars in pounds on the board, marked approximate. No plant handling.

### The day

#### 12 min. Hook and retrieval

Return day 17's s01-R17a/b/c/d/e assigned retry, three minutes, descriptive feedback only. Keep readiness support for day 19, as promised. Display H18. Review: roots take in water; leaves face light, grade 4 day 105. Review day 1: air is matter; dots represent unseen parts, not visible grains. Children point to roots/leaves and recall their jobs.

Review question board: notices become questions, grade 4 day 1. Introduce: Where does a growing tree's matter come from? Review rotating six from grade 4 day 16: six 45-second notices fit five minutes; everyone can pass. Record who spoke.

#### 10 min. Read the account

Read H18 aloud. **Ask.** [[s02-P18-1]] Children locate both changes alone before discussion. Then reveal [[steps:s02-P18-1]]. Review day 9's data-graph: labeled bars start at zero. Draw separate before/after groups for tree and soil, in pounds; mark approximations. Do not graph soil's final value as an exact number.

Review relevant and sufficient evidence, grade 4 day 105. Error A claims nearly all growth came from soil: compare the large tree change and small soil loss. This comparison is suggestive, not a complete dry-matter account: tree dryness was unstated; shed leaves went unweighed; air was uncontrolled.

#### 19 min. Candidate inputs

Introduce matter source: incoming material. Review information source from day 5: the record supplying information. Teach claim-evidence-reasoning: claim, supporting observation, why they connect. Model one component: little reported soil loss makes a soil-only explanation doubtful, with the limits just stated.

Teach that leaves take in part of air. Roots take in water with small mineral inputs. Sunlight provides energy for making food, not plant stuff. **Ask.** [[s02-P18-2]] Allow six drawing minutes. Label routes possible; today's report does not establish their shares. Review day 8's weighing boundary: every included part matters when interpreting a total. Do not demonstrate a chamber account.

Pairs each explain one arrow for 45 seconds, simultaneously. Use remaining time to tag unresolved routes on the question board and collect both practice responses.

#### 4 min. Exit ticket

Reveal [[s02-X18]] only now. Read the exact stem neutrally; announce invented for practice. Everyone draws and writes alone. Collect all thirty. After class allow 30 times 20 seconds, ten minutes, for descriptive exit feedback; ten more for both practice responses. Initial drawings receive notes, no points or categorical mark. Error I assigns a unique source from an unchanged total. Return exit and practice on day 19.

### Other rooms

No projector: draw H18's table and the separate bar groups on the board. No printer: copy only today's stems and blank spaces; keep the exit covered. All work is paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No display: copy H18's table onto paper. No printer: copy today's questions and cover the exit.

**Skip.** Class turns and the public question board.

- **5 min. Remember.** Read day 17 feedback. Recall roots taking water and leaves facing light.
- **10 min. Compare.** Read the historical table together. Do [[s02-P18-1]].
  **Say.** "Which change is larger?" Expect: tree gain, about 164 pounds; soil loss is under one pound.
- **15 min. Draw.** Do [[s02-P18-2]]. Explain that water and air supply matter. Sunlight supplies food-making energy. Keep possible routes tentative for this record.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Give [[s02-X18]] alone. Discuss after collection, using the current answer key.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: reduce public notices, keeping comparisons, routes and exit. Longer: locate one omission in the historical record rather than adding a new case.

### Words and supports

Matter source: incoming material. Information source: the record. Claim: an idea to support. Evidence: an observation. Reasoning connects them. Read records aloud; accept dictation onto paper or enlarged drawing space.
