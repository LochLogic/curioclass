# Day 60: Rehearse a sky talk with a fact, a drawing and a sentence

## Teacher page

### Before class

Mark each child's latest note in the shared notebook with a pictured mark (the written feedback): a tick where a fact and its source label are there, a small question mark where one is missing, an arrow where the note is only a border. Pick the heard six and the two five-child groups heard on day 59. Fold the talk card and listener card. Have the calendar card and the two-column notes sheets ready.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Only if many missed day 59's exit (error G), 5 minutes. Say: "This is made up for practice." Read: "A red kite flies in the wind." Draw a bold star border on the board. **Ask.** "Does the border show a listener what the sentence says?" Expect: no. **Ask.** "What does a listener need to see?" Expect: the kite and the wind. Erase the border and draw only a kite and wind lines. Add at most two branches of the next three, the ones most missed. If many missed the commas (error S), write "kite bell drum" on a strip and the class says where the voice pauses; add the commas (the calendar card's contrasts show the pattern). If many said a fragment (error H), say "Kite flies" beside "The kite flies." If many missed the date comma (error V), circle the day in "May 2 2026" on the calendar card and add the comma.

#### 10 min. Reread

Whole class on the rug. Reread Stars in Daylight and Darkness, page 4, and The Changing Moon, page 5, with the notebook and the picture log beside them (6 minutes). Night is gentle here: the stars are quiet and far away. Then partners (4 minutes): each says one fact from the two pages and where it came from, using the frame "I read ___ in ___, page ___." The listener says it back. Hear four pairs. Review: a note has a fact and its source, from day 50.

#### 15 min. Revise the notebook contribution

Show the border contrast once (2 minutes): a page with only a bold star border, beside the note "Stars show at night. (Stars in Daylight and Darkness)". **Ask.** "Which one answers our question?" Expect: the note with the fact and its source. A border is not a fact (error O). Then each child reads the pictured mark on their note (a tick, a question mark or an arrow) and adds or fixes one fact beside the group question, in the notebook or on the two-column notes sheet, with a small picture and a source label (10 minutes). A child who asks has the label scribed. Walk round and mark the notes. Keep the revised note beside the first one. Last (3 minutes): partners say which source they named.

#### 25 min. Rehearse the talk

Review from unit 1: groups of three talk, maker, listener and asker, and Pip's wait, add, ask from day 38. Show the talk card, a strip with three spaces: the fact, the drawing, the sentence. Pip is the maker (4 minutes): Pip holds up a fact, a drawing and one sentence about the stars in the day. A raised hand held still means "wait". The class asks Pip one question: "Where did you read that?" Pip answers, then asks back, "Which picture do you mean?" and the class points and names it (error Z is repeating the question instead of answering it). The asker's questions are on the strip: "Where did you read that?" "Which picture do you mean?" "What do you mean by ___?"

Ten groups of three (9 minutes): maker, listener, asker, one minute each, three rounds, so each child is the maker once. The maker shows the note, shows the drawing and says one finished sentence. The listener says one thing heard. The asker asks one question; the maker answers it. Walk round and hear each group for about 50 seconds.

Then the two five-child groups from day 59 come to your table for 5 minutes each (10 minutes), with the question "Which source detail should our note include?" Write a note of each linked reply and of anyone who waits, asks or answers. The other twenty children say their talk again to a new partner, who says back one detail. Keep 2 minutes to say what a good asker did.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read once: "Stars are easier to see in a dark sky." A speaker says: "Our night log shows stars above a tent." Show two pictures: stars above a tent, and an empty bright sky. Say: "Point to the picture that supports the talk." Every child points; the rotating six say which line it goes with ([[s02-X60a]]). A good answer is the stars above the tent. The empty bright sky is error E: a picture the line does not support. Then, for the six only, each with their own drawing from the rehearsal in view: a partner asks, "What does your drawing help us see?" Say: "Say one finished sentence that answers your partner" ([[s02-X60b]]). A good answer says what the drawing shows in a whole sentence; one word, such as "Stars", is error H. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words, not scored. Say stripe and strip; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter i's name?" Expect: stripe. Blend /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/, "frog". Say "trip": first sound /t/, middle sound /i/, last sound /p/. Segment "grass" with counters: /g/ /r/ /a/ /s/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Cycle 10, lesson 52, explicit teaching first (5 minutes). Review from day 59: an l or an r can start a blend, and three consonants can stand side by side, each with its sound. Read flat, slip, glad, plum, crab, drum, scrub, split and strap from the cards. New today: -es. Write kiss and say: "Say the word with more than one kiss: kisses." The ending says /iz/ and adds a beat. When a word ends in s, sh, ch or x, we add -es, not -s. Map kiss and kisses, then wish and wishes; tap each sound, then the ending. Spell kisses on sound boxes (2 minutes). Then map plop, swish and grins, and read the reader On the Pond (adapted), the teacher first, then the class echoing, pointing under each word (3 minutes). The title word pond is read to them.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (6 minutes): say "The frog is on the grass." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped words the and is are named aloud. Watch the fr and gr blends and the double s. Then each child points to the capital and the period, and circles the blend in frog and grass (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table, first (3 minutes): children who made error L add the ending to a known base (kiss, kisses) and reread; children who made error I move one counter for each sound in grass; children who made error M stretch the word and mark every heard sound, then write. Then the group reads On the Pond, a page a child, and retells what the frog and the crab do (3 minutes). The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread On the Pond with a partner; chain kiss, kisses, wish and wishes with letter cards; spell crab and drum on sound boxes; read the word cards was, of, but and the to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the notebook marks, the heard-six column and the notes on the two five-child groups to the unit 2 record sheet with the cycle record. Name the foundations groups for day 61.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a paper strip into three spaces and draw a question mark on a second paper. One child: say the talk to a grown-up, who asks one question; answer the exit by pointing and speaking; then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No talk card: fold a paper in three and draw a fact, a picture and a speech bubble.

**Skip.** The group rounds and the rotation.

- **10 min. Note.** Your child tells you one sky fact and where they learned it. **Say.** "Where did you read that?" Expect: the name of the article or the picture log.
- **10 min. Talk.** Your child draws a picture for the fact and says one finished sentence. You ask: "What should I notice in your drawing?" Expect: a whole sentence.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Your child reads kisses and wishes and prints "The frog is on the grass."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X60a]] You say: "The moon is round on some nights." A speaker says: "Our picture log shows a round moon over a roof." Draw two pictures: a round moon over a roof, and a sunny playground. Ask: "Which picture supports the talk?" Expect: the round moon. [[s02-X60b]] Ask: "Say one finished sentence about your drawing." Expect: who or what and what happens.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair for your own misses, the notebook revision, one round of the talk, the exit and the rotation; cut the second say-again round. Longer: a child who finishes asks a second question about a partner's drawing and answers it.

### Words and supports

Words: rehearse, talk card, listener, asker, source, question, -es. A talk card holds a fact, a drawing and a sentence. Frames: "I read ___ in ___, page ___." "Which picture do you mean?" "I agree with ___ because ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Any child may pass. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who points to a picture the line supports (error E: a picture the line does not support) and who answers a partner in a finished sentence (error H: a fragment). Day 61 is a check on a score grid: a fresh two-source card is read aloud, and each child shows a connection, a notebook note, a labeled drawing and a spoken description with a feeling. Nothing new is taught. Reteaching waits for day 62, when day 60's lapses open the repair.
