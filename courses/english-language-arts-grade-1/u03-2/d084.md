# Day 84: Connect a bird's foot to its job

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print Feet That Help with its contents and diagram. Lay out the four word pictures, the arrow chart, the coat drawing with its glossary line, the eye and ear signs and the exit card face down. Copy day 83's exit marks and name today's heard six. The article is new today, so keep it covered until the reading.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This is made up for practice." Show a coat drawing with a big pocket and a small hood, a label line to the hood, and a glossary line: "hood: a cover for the head." Say the repair for error C from day 83's exit, in which the biggest-drawn part was used instead of the glossary. Review: the eye sign for what the picture shows and the ear sign for what the words tell, from day 80. Put an eye beside the drawing and an ear beside the glossary line. **Ask.** "What does hood mean? Which one tells you?" Expect: a cover for the head; the glossary line, the ear. Everyone says it: "The glossary tells what it means. The label tells where it is." Nobody is named. If many chose the biggest-drawn part on day 83's exit (error C), say the pair again with a fresh word, a sleeve.

#### 12 min. Words, then read Feet That Help

Show each word with its picture and have the class say it: perch (rest on a branch), webbed (joined with skin), claw (a sharp, curved nail at the end of a toe), grasp (take hold and grip). About 25 seconds a word. Then show the contents and read the whole article aloud once (about 170 words, 2 minutes), stopping at each heading to show the picture. Content note: a hawk's claws hold food; nothing is graphic. Read Feet for Perching again while each child touches their own toes, then their pointing finger: "Toes grip. Fingers grip." Pairs tell each other which bird's foot they liked best and why, two turns of 20 seconds each, all pairs at once.

#### 13 min. Connecting two facts, taught

Review: the connection arrow, from unit 2, a fact, an arrow and a joining word. Put up the arrow chart. Use the sparrow paragraph. Point to the sentence first: "The toes grip a branch." Say: "That is one fact: the part." Point to the next: "The sparrow can perch and not fall." Say: "That is the other fact: the job." Place the arrow between them and say it whole: "The toes grip a branch, so the sparrow can perch." Turn it round with the other joining word: "The sparrow can perch because its toes grip a branch." Review: so and because, from unit 2.

Contrast, aloud. Say: "This is made up to show a mistake." A child says: "A duck's beak helps it swim." **Ask.** "Is that in the article about feet?" Expect: no. Point to the exact sentence, "The feet push the water." Say: "Its webbed feet help it swim." Say the rule: "Point to the sentence first, then explain." A child who explains without a sentence has given a fact the source does not give (error B).

Pairs try the hawk. Each partner points to the sentence, "The claws grasp food and hold it." and then says: "The claws grasp food, so the hawk can eat." Hear two pairs, one minute each.

#### 10 min. Three questions

Pairs ask and answer these spoken questions, two turns each, read twice.

1. "What can the sparrow do with its toes?" Expect: grip a branch.
2. "Which two facts are joined?" Expect: the toes grip a branch; the sparrow can perch.
3. "How does gripping help it perch?" Expect: it holds on, so it does not fall.

Fifteen pairs take two turns of about 20 seconds at once; hear four pairs, one minute each.

#### 15 min. Draw the connection

Hand out the student page, a row of three boxes: part, arrow, job. Say: "Choose one bird from the article. Draw its part in the first box and what it helps the bird do in the third box. Point to the sentence in the article for each. Copy so or because from the board." Children draw for 11 minutes. Then hear six children, the heard six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes): each points to the sentence and says the connection. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child who draws a part with a job the article does not give has made error B; point to the exact sentence together.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once: "A hen scratches the ground with her feet. This helps her find seeds." The others colour their page; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures: the hen's feet with their toes and claws, the hen's beak, a pile of seeds. Say: "Point to the body part that belongs with this card." ([[s03-X84a]]) Then, with the card in view: "How does scratching help the hen?" ([[s03-X84b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the beak, or says a job the card does not give, has made error B.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say rob and robe; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: robe. Blend /t/ /r/ /u/ /k/, "truck". Say drum and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /d/, /u/, /m/. Segment crisp: /k/ /r/ /i/ /s/ /p/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 13, lesson 66. Teach first. Write backpack. Say: "A compound word is two words joined into one word." Clap it, two claps, and chop between the words: back | pack. Say: "Each part is a word you know." Chop and read cat | fish, pig | pen, hot | dog. Then write rabbit, clap it and chop rab | bit. Say: "Rab is not a word, so rabbit is not a compound word. It has two parts with one vowel sound each, like picnic and napkin." Children read the four compound words together. Then they spell backpack part by part on their page. The sound-spelling-map stays up. The cycle 12 reader is reread in the rotation today, until the cycle 13 words are mapped.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs T t and C c. Dictate two words: pigpen and hotdog, then the sentence "The cat is in the backpack." the, is and in are mapped words. Say each whole, then a word at a time. Children stretch each word, print it and read it back. Repair for a missing vowel (error M): tap the sounds again.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first: the six children the record names. For their first three minutes they move one counter for each sound on four fresh words you say: trip, flag, nest, grin (the repair for error L on day 83's snap item); then map, blend, read, spell and reread. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, with A Sunset Picnic: each child reads a page and spells a compound word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time. The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread the reader, chain words with letter cards, build and chop compound words, and counters on the same four words for any child whose snap item showed a cluster as one sound. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the connection page notes, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the three boxes on scrap paper and read the article from the page. One child: read the article, say the sparrow connection, draw one part and its job, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: look at a bird's feet in a picture book or a window. No counters: use coins or beans.

**Skip.** The rotation, the stations and the counters.

- **10 min. Connect.** Look at one bird's foot in a book. **Say.** "What does this foot help the bird do? Point to the words that say so." Expect: a part and a job from the page, joined with so or because.
- **10 min. Draw.** Your child draws the part, an arrow and the job, and says: "The ___ helps the bird ___ because ___."
- **5 min. Words.** Clap and chop backpack, then print "The cat is in the backpack."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X84a]] [[s03-X84b]] Read: "A hen scratches the ground with her feet. This helps her find seeds." Your child points to the body part that belongs with the card, then says how scratching helps.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the sparrow model, the draw and the exit. Cut the third question and the hawk pairs. Longer: a child joins a second pair of facts from the article with so.

### Words and supports

Words: perch, webbed, claw, grasp, compound word. Frames: "The ___ helps the bird ___." "The ___, so the ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can tie a body part to the job the card gives. If many pointed to the beak or gave a job the card does not give (error B), day 85 opens with a five-minute repair of day 84's exit: point to the exact sentence first, on a fresh card. Day 85 then uses a printed screen menu and its icons to find a bird fact.
