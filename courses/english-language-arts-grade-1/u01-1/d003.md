# Day 3: What the picture shows, what the words tell

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print Tools on the Farm with its picture on its own sheet and fold a sheet over the picture. Hang the eye and ear signs. Lay out the picture cards for the exits, the topic card and detail cards from day 2, the sort cards: tool words and picture symbols, the hoops, the six tool cards, the exit card with its picture, the poem and the place signs. Today's six are the next six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read the farm article

Whole class on the rug. Say: "Today's article is about a farmer. The picture is hidden, so listen to the words." Read Tools on the Farm whole, once (129 words, about 65 seconds). Content note: a hoe, a watering can and a tractor; no blade is handled, nobody is hurt. Show the picture.

Say: "Some facts are in the picture only. Some are in the words only." Review: the eye means the picture shows, the ear means the words tell.

**Ask.** What does the hoe do to the weeds? Expect: it cuts them. Reread the hoe paragraph and touch the sentence "The farmer pulls the hoe through the dirt to cut weeds and to loosen the soil." Say: "Only the words told us. The picture did not." Every child points in the air to the sentence.

Then ask what the picture shows. Expect: a straw hat, a red tractor, a brown dog, green rows. Take three answers; a child may pass.

#### 10 min. Reread and sort the two sources

First 4 minutes, a repair for the day 2 exit (error A). Read the ladder card again: "A builder uses a ladder. Its rungs let her climb up to the roof." Say: "Ask a question these two sentences can answer." Each child tells a neighbor one question; the neighbor points to the sentence. Say a good question back in a full sentence.

Then 6 minutes: sort. Pairs get the sort cards and the two hoops. The picture cards are a straw hat, a red tractor, a brown dog and green rows. The word cards are "The long handle lets the farmer stand tall" and "The rose breaks the water into drops." Read each card aloud. Pairs place each card in the eye hoop or the ear hoop, and say how they know. To test it, fold the sheet over the picture and ask: "Can I still tell?" Fifteen pairs at once; you hold the model hoops at the front.

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn card in hand, partners do three rounds of three minutes. Round 1: "Say one fact the picture shows." Round 2: "Say one fact the words tell." Round 3: "Say how the picture and the words together explain the work." Swap the card each round. Sit with one pair per round, three pairs in all; the other children are heard on a later day. Mark on the record sheet who spoke. Say a pause back as an invitation: "You could begin with The picture shows."

#### 20 min. Sort tool words

Review: a topic is what the whole text is about. This is new: a category is a group of things that go together. Show the six tool cards. Say: "The builder tools go in one group and the farm tools in another. Each group is a category." Place the hammer, level and wheelbarrow under the builder sign, and the hoe, watering can and tractor under the farm sign. Read each card aloud.

Pairs get their own six cards and sort them into two piles, builder tools and farm tools, then say the name of each category (5 minutes). Next, model a category plus one attribute: "a farm tool with a long handle." Pairs choose one tool and say its category and one thing about it, for example "a builder tool with a wheel" (5 minutes). Then each child says "It helps because ___" about the tool they picked, to a neighbor (4 minutes). Draw the tool on the student page and label it (6 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A farmer uses a bucket to carry water." Show the picture: a yellow bucket beside a pair of boots. Say: "Tell me one thing the picture shows that the words do not say." Hear today's six, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X3]]). A child who says it carries water gives a fact from the words (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Review: first sound, last sound and the rest of a word are kindergarten work. This is new: long and short vowel sounds, heard only.

- Say pin then pine, hat then hate, tap then tape. Say: "Listen to the middle." The class says which word has the long sound, the name of the letter, and holds up two fingers for the long one.
- Say each pair again, mixed: pine, hat, tape, pin. Thumb up for long, thumb down for short.

#### 10 min. Sounds to letters

Post the poem Look in a Book in large print. Read it with a finger under each word. Find the words with the sounds of the map. Point to cat, hat and map, and say each sound together. Review: sh in fish and dish, th in path and ch in chat are kindergarten sounds. Show the sound-spelling map for sh, th and ch. Children point to each word on the poem as you say it, and echo the line. Nobody reads the poem alone.

#### 10 min. Retrieval repair, letters, dictation

Repair first, only if the record shows a gap from day 1 or 2: that small group works up to 10 minutes. Otherwise teach B and b.

Say: "B has a stick, then a bump, then another bump. The little b has a stick, then a bump on the right." Children trace in the air, then print B and b on the student page. Say: "b faces the ball and d faces the door." Check for backwards b (error O). Then dictation: say "Sam sat." and children print it on the page, with a capital S and a dot at the end. Check as they go.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Six groups of five, six places, 5 minutes each. At the teacher table a group reads the reader Sam sat a second time in new groups: "Sam sat. Sam sat at a mat." You point; they read; each child reads one line alone (about 20 seconds each, five children, 2 minutes). The other five places: sound cards, word building, draw and label, tool pictures, quiet pairs. The chime moves everyone one place along.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the cards by hand, chalk two hoops on the floor, and read the article from the page. One child: read the article, do the eye and ear talk, the sort and the exit; skip the rotation.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the page. No hoops: use two bowls.

**Skip.** The rotation and the class talk.

- **5 min. Read the farm article.** Read it with the picture hidden, then show it.
  **Say.** "What does the hoe do to the weeds?" Expect: it cuts them.
- **5 min. Eye and ear.** Say one fact only the picture shows and one fact only the words tell.
- **5 min. Sort.** Sort six tool pictures into builder tools and farm tools. Say the name of each group.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws one tool and says "It helps because ___."
- **5 min. Sounds.** Say pin and pine; hat and hate. Which has the long sound?
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X3]] Read the card, show the picture and say: "Tell me one thing the picture shows that the words do not say."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: sort tool words in one round and skip the third talk round. Keep the farm article, the eye and ear sort and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes sorts the cards a second way and names the new category.

### Words and supports

Words: farmer, hoe, watering can, tractor, category. Frames: "The picture shows ___." "The words tell ___." "It helps because ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 4 reads Tools in the Kitchen and defines a tool by its category and use. If many children gave a fact from the words on today's exit (error B), open day 4's reread with four minutes: reread one kitchen fact and say whether the picture or the words told it.
