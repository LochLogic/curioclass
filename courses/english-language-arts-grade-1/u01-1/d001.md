# Day 1: How tools help people work

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the picture cards for the exits and Builders at Work with its picture on its own sheet, and fold a sheet to cover the picture. Post the big question. Lay out the three tool pictures, a turn card for each pair, the placement card in large letters, the exit card with its two pictures and six place signs. Today's six are the first six names on the record sheet; seat them in three pairs near your chair.

### The day

#### 12 min. Hook: read and hide

Whole class on the rug. Say: "I will read about builders. The picture is under this fold, so listen to the words." Read Builders at Work whole, once, without stopping (141 words, about 70 seconds). Content note: it names a hammer, a level and a wheelbarrow, and nobody is hurt. Partners whisper to each other for a minute.

**Ask.** What job do these words explain? Expect: a builder puts up the walls of a house. Say a short answer back as a full sentence.

Lift the fold. Say: "Some things are only in the picture." Point to the hard hats, the crane and the colors, and say: "The words did not tell us that."

**Ask.** What do you see that the words did not say? Expect: hard hats, a crane, yellow and red. Take three answers; a child may pass.

#### 8 min. The big question

Read the posted question, a finger under each word: How do tools help people do their work? Say: "We will keep asking this for many days." Show the hammer, level and wheelbarrow cards. Model the frame with one: "A wheelbarrow helps because a builder does not carry the whole load." Children point to a card and say "A ___ helps because ___" to a neighbor (1 minute), then three volunteers say it to the class (20 seconds each). Draw each of the three answers on the chart as a small picture with one word. Any answer about a job is welcome.

**Ask.** How does a tool help a builder? Expect: it moves bricks, pushes in a nail, shows if a board is flat.

#### 10 min. Reread, point, talk

Review: Read, Point, Talk, Draw is the routine you learned in kindergarten. You hear a text, point back to its words, talk with a partner, then draw. You hold every step today.

Reread the hammer paragraph from the large print, a finger under each word. Ask a child to come and touch the line.

**Ask.** Which words tell what the claw does? Expect: "The claw grabs a bent nail and pulls it out." A child who touches the line is right; read it once more together. Then ask the class to point in the air at the line while you echo it twice. Every child points and no child reads alone.

#### 10 min. Partner exchange

Review: the turn card, two turns each, and a child may pass. The child holding the card speaks, and the listener says one thing back. Fifteen pairs talk at once in three rounds of about three minutes. Round 1: "Say what the claw does." Round 2: "Say what the bubble in the level tells a builder." Round 3: "Say how the wheelbarrow helps a builder." Swap the card each round.

Sit with one of today's three pairs each round, three minutes a pair. Hear both children; write on the record sheet who spoke and who needed a nudge. Say a hesitation back as an invitation: "You could start with A claw helps because." If the whole class stalls on a round, reread its sentence aloud and go again.

#### 15 min. Draw

Hand out the student page. Say: "Draw one tool a builder uses: the hammer, the level or the wheelbarrow." Draw for 8 minutes. Write the three words large on the board; each child copies the word under the drawing, or says it and you write it. A child may point to a card instead. Then partners say the frame under the drawing, "A ___ helps because ___" (3 minutes; 15 pairs at once). Talk stays with the builders in the text; no child draws a parent's tool or names what they own at home. Collect the pages for the record.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Hold up the two pictures (a brush beside a dustpan, a pencil beside a book). Read the card and ask [[s01-X1]]. All children point or touch left for the first picture and right for the second (1 minute). Then hear today's six, 20 seconds each, asking each: "Why?" Mark each of the six on the record sheet: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Others are not yet heard, never missed. A child who points to the pencil and book gives a job the words do not give (error A).

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Review: rhyme, beats, the first sound and the rest of a word, and blending are kindergarten work. This is a check of what the class holds, in one voice.

- Rhyme: say the pairs; a thumb goes up for a rhyme. mat and sat (yes), map and mud (no), tap and lap (yes).
- Beats: clap and count cat (1), paper (2), banana (3).
- First sound and the rest: say mat as /m/ then /at/, and sat as /s/ then /at/; you say /t/ then /ap/ and the class says the word.
- Blend: say /m/ /a/ /t/ slowly, then smooth, and the class says mat. Then /s/ /a/ /t/.

#### 10 min. The cycle 1 poem, spoken

Say the poem Look in a Book (g1-u1-cr1) twice, with expression, with no print shown. Content note: a cat, a fish, a ship and a map; nothing frightening. Clap the beats (about four a line) as you say it a third time. Then say one line and have the class echo it, then you take the first line of each pair and the class the second.

**Ask.** Which words at the ends of lines sound the same? Expect: look and nook, sea and me (share and anywhere also). Review: rhyming words end the same.

#### 10 min. Letters and sounds

Hold up letter cards s, a, m, t, p, n one at a time. The class says the name and the sound together (review of kindergarten, 4 minutes). Post the line Tap a mat. in large print. Slide a finger under it left to right, then ask about where to start.

**Ask.** Where do we start reading this line? Expect: at the first word, on the left (Tap). Every child points in the air to the first word (1 minute). A child who points to mat starts at the last word (error G). Slide a finger under the line again and echo it three times. For the last 3 minutes, say each letter's sound and children hold up its card, six cards, or point to it on the board.

#### 10 min. Blend and read

Post the cards mat, sat, map, tap. For each word, touch each letter and say its sound, then sweep your finger under it and say the word, and the class echoes (4 minutes, one word a minute). Partners then spread the four word cards. Partner A reads a word aloud and B finds and touches it, then they swap (4 minutes; 15 pairs; stand by today's three pairs). Call out a word and the class holds up the card (2 minutes). Nobody reads alone for the class.

#### 15 min. Station procedures

Six groups of five, set before class by the record sheet's order. Show the six places in a ring, each with a place sign: the teacher table, two draw tables, sound cards, word building and tool pictures (1.5 minutes each to show). Tell what each place holds. Sound cards: sort picture cards by first sound. Draw and label: draw a tool and say a word for it. Word building: slide letter tiles to make mat. Tool pictures: partners say "A ___ helps because ___" about a picture. The signal is the chime (or a raised hand): everyone stops, tidies and moves one place along, quietly. Teach the move once with a practice (3 minutes). Then do two practice rounds of about 3 minutes each. A group sits at the teacher table for 2 minutes in each; you listen to them say the poem's first line together. Notice what each place needs and who is not sure what to do.

#### 10 min. Placement screen

This is a placement look, not a test, and it has no score. Each group of five leaves its place for 90 seconds at the teacher table and then goes back; the other groups work at the places they just learned (6 groups at 90 seconds is 9 minutes, and 1 minute to set the cards). Show the card from the placement piece in large letters: "A jar holds pencils. The jar sits on a mat." (invented for practice). Each child in the group of five takes one turn of about 15 seconds on one task:

1. Point to the first word. 
2. Say the sentence back in your own words.
3. Name one letter card (s, a, m or t) and say its sound.
4. Read mat from the card.
5. Read the word the from the card.

Give the tasks out in a different order in each group so every task is heard six times across the class. Write on the record sheet what each child pointed to and said and what help was needed. A child may pass. These notes set the repair groups for days 2 to 5.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the three tool pictures, the two exit pictures and the letter cards by hand, and read Builders at Work from the text page. No projector is used. One child: read the article, ask the same two questions, do the poem and the four words, and skip the stations.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the page. No turn card: take turns holding a rolled sock.

**Skip.** The stations, the placement look and the class talk.

- **5 min. Read and hide.** Read Builders at Work with the picture covered.
  **Say.** "What job do these words explain?" Expect: a builder puts up the walls of a house.
- **5 min. The picture.** Uncover it.
  **Say.** "What do you see that the words did not say?" Expect: hard hats, a crane, colors.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws a tool a builder uses and says "A ___ helps because ___."
- **5 min. The poem.** Say Look in a Book and clap the beats.
  **Say.** "Which words sound the same at the ends of lines?" Expect: look and nook.
- **5 min. Blend.** Say the sounds of mat, sat, map and tap and blend each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]] Read the card, show the two pictures and ask the question. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep one practice round of the stations, drop partner rounds 2 and 3, and cut the quick call-out in Blend and read. Keep the hook, the exit and the placement look. Longer: a child who finishes draws a second tool and says how it helps.

### Words and supports

Words: builder, tool, hammer, level, wheelbarrow, claw. Frame: "A ___ helps because ___." Pointing, drawing, signs and a home language all count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud; no child is asked to read it.

### Tomorrow

Day 2 rereads Builders at Work with its picture and asks questions the words can answer. If many pointed to the pencil and book picture (error A), day 2 opens its "Reread and question" part with four minutes: reread the card's two sentences and point to the picture that matches. The placement notes set the day 2 repair groups.
