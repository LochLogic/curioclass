# Day 2: Questions the words can answer

## Teacher page

### Before class

Put up the topic card and detail cards ("A builder uses tools") and the picture cards for the exits, and the eye and ear signs. Lay out the four detail cards, the picture fold cards, the exit card with its two pictures, the poem in large print and the six place signs. Today's six are the next six names on the record sheet; seat them near your chair.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read the article again, with the picture

Whole class on the rug. Show the picture of Builders at Work and read the article whole, once (141 words, about 70 seconds). Content note: tools named, nobody hurt.

Say: "I will ask a question that the words can answer." Ask yourself aloud: "What does the claw do?" Then reread the hammer paragraph and touch the sentence: "The claw grabs a bent nail and pulls it out." Say: "I asked, and the words answered. I pointed to the sentence."

Review: you learned in kindergarten that a question asks and a statement tells. A question ends with a mark that curls. A statement tells and ends with a dot.

**Ask.** Which sentence did the words use to answer? Expect: the claw sentence. Every child points in the air to where you touched.

Start the topic card. Read it, a finger under each word: A builder uses tools. Review: a topic is what the whole text is about, and a detail is one fact under it. Hold up the first detail card, the flat end pushing a nail. Say: "This is one detail. It sits under the topic." Place it. Children say with you: "The flat end pushes a nail."

#### 10 min. Reread and question

First 4 minutes, a repair for the day 1 exit (error A). Read the card's two sentences again: "A brush moves dust into a pan. The floor becomes clean." Show the two pictures. Every child points to the one that matches the words. Say a pointing child's reason back: "The brush and pan move the dust."

Then 6 minutes in pairs. Reread the level paragraph aloud. Partner A asks one question the words can answer, for example "What is inside the level?"; partner B points to the sentence in the large print or says it. Swap. Fifteen pairs talk at once; stand by one pair. A child may pass. If a question cannot be answered by the words ("What color is the level?"), say: "The picture might tell that. The words do not." Say it back as a good question for the picture.

#### 10 min. Picture beside sentence

Teach two signs. The eye means the picture shows. The ear means the words tell. Hold up the picture fold card: the picture on the left, a blank on the right. Read the hammer sentence: "The flat end of a hammer pushes a nail into wood." Ask which part of the picture goes beside this sentence.

**Ask.** Which builder in the picture is using this tool? Expect: the one kneeling with a hammer. Children point. Then fold the sheet over the picture and read the sentence again. Say: "Now only the ear is left."

Name what only the picture shows: the yellow hard hats, the crane. Name what only the words tell: the flat end pushes a nail in.

Partners fold their own card, point to one picture fact, hide it, and say one thing the words alone tell (3 minutes). A child may point instead of speak.

#### 20 min. Detail cards

Show the topic card and the four detail cards: the flat end pushing a nail, the claw pulling a bent nail, the bubble in the level, the wheel of the wheelbarrow. Children choose one and draw it on the student page, with a word label read aloud from the article (hammer, level or wheel). Draw for 10 minutes. Then partners lay their drawing under the topic card and say what it tells, in the frame "The ___ helps because ___" (5 minutes). Last, five children bring cards up and place them under the topic card; the class reads each aloud (5 minutes, 20 seconds a child, a different five from tomorrow).

Tell each pair: "Ask your partner one question your drawing can answer." This is practice for the exit.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A builder uses a ladder. Its rungs let her climb up to the roof." Show the two pictures: a ladder against a roof and a ladder lying on grass. Say: "Ask me one question that these words can answer." Hear today's six, 20 seconds each, and answer by pointing to the sentence. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X2]]). A child who asks about the color of the ladder asks a picture question (error A).

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Review: syllables, first sound and the rest, and segmenting are kindergarten work.

- Syllables: clap and count ham-mer (2) and wheel-bar-row (3).
- First sound and the rest: say sat as /s/ then /at/, and map as /m/ then /ap/.
- Segment: say tap, sat and map slowly, one sound at a time. The class holds up a finger for each sound.

#### 10 min. Message in print

Post the poem Look in a Book in large print. Read it aloud, a finger under each word. Point to the first word, Open. Ask where we start.

**Ask.** What is the first word of line one? Expect: Open. Show the capital O, the spaces between words and the dot at the end of a line. Review: you learned in kindergarten that words have spaces and sentences begin with a capital. Put up the sound-spelling map with s, a, m, t (sock, apple, moon, top). Say each sound together. Then children echo each line twice. Nobody reads it alone.

#### 10 min. Retrieval repair, letters, dictation

Repair first, only if the day 1 record shows a gap: take that small group (letters s, a, m, t or the four words mat, sat, map, tap), up to 10 minutes. Otherwise teach the letter pair A and a.

Say: "A tall hat with a line across. The little a is a circle and a stick." Children trace A and a in the air, then print on the student page. Then dictation: say "at" slowly, /a/ /t/; children print it on the page. Check as they go.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Six groups of five, six places, 5 minutes each. At the teacher table, a group reads the reader Sam sat together: "Sam sat. Sam sat at a mat." You point; they read; each child reads one of the two lines alone (about 20 seconds each, five children, 2 minutes). The other five places: sound cards, word building, draw and label, tool pictures, and quiet pairs. The chime moves everyone one place along. Hear the group, then move.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the detail cards by hand and write the poem large. One child: do the reread, the picture fold, the detail drawing and the exit; skip the rotation.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the page. No fold card: fold any sheet.

**Skip.** The rotation and the class talk.

- **5 min. Read with the picture.** Read Builders at Work and show the picture.
  **Say.** "What does the claw do?" Expect: it pulls a bent nail out.
- **5 min. A question.** Your child asks you one question the words can answer, and you point to the sentence.
- **5 min. Picture or words.** Fold a sheet over the picture. Ask what the picture shows and what only the words tell.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws one detail and labels it.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Clap ham-mer and wheel-bar-row. Say sat slowly: /s/ /a/ /t/.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X2]] Read the card, show the two pictures and say: "Ask me one question the words can answer."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the dictation and keep one round of the rotation. Keep the question, the picture fold and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes draws a second detail and asks a partner a question.

### Words and supports

Words: question, topic, detail, claw, level, wheelbarrow. Frame: "The ___ helps because ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 3 reads Tools on the Farm and sorts what the picture shows from what the words tell. If many children asked about color on today's exit (error A), open day 3's reread with four minutes on the ladder card: the picture answers color, the words answer the climb.
