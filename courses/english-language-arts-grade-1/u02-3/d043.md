# Day 43: The night-sky poems

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the poem chart, the exit pictures and the sample word card. Add two line cards from Quiet Sky: "The first small star blinks on" and "The quiet sky is kind." Draw three repair pictures: Rabbit running down a wet hill, slipped in mud, walking slowly and clean. Make the unit 2 fresh retry cards for day 44: read jet, spell leg. Write the six names for the exit, group 1 and the sample. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read the night-sky poems

Whole class on the rug. Say aloud: "Curio Class wrote these poems about the night sky." Review: in kindergarten you said poems together. Say: "A poem is made of lines and sound. It may have no events, so we do not retell it the way we retell a tale." Words before, with pictures (3 minutes): dew, pale, still. Read Quiet Sky aloud, showing each picture (4 minutes). Read it again and the class joins the last two lines of each page (4 minutes). Gently: the speaker is the person in the poem; no child is asked about their own night.

**Ask.** "What do you hear in the poem?" Expect: a cricket sings. **Ask.** "What does the speaker do on the last page?" Expect: looks up at the stars and stays (4 minutes).

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair first (4 minutes, from day 42's exit, error B). Read a made-up line: "Rabbit ran fast down the wet hill and slipped in the mud. The next day Rabbit walked slowly and stayed clean." Lay the three pictures. Lay the arrow card between the slip and the walking slowly. Say: "A topic is mud. A lesson is slow down where it is wet." Pairs say the action, the result and a lesson. If a few children gave a claim the line did not say (error E), point to the words that tell. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge and give the question the whole ten minutes.

Reread the last page of Quiet Sky. Review: on day 39 you learned that a feeling word tells how someone feels, and a sense word tells what we see, hear or touch. **Ask.** "Which words tell how the speaker feels?" Expect: the quiet sky is kind; I look up and I stay. The speaker feels calm and safe. Partners point to the line on the large page (6 minutes).

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, with the turn card (you used it in kindergarten and on day 38), one turn each, pass allowed, about one minute a child. **Ask.** "Which words help you see, hear or feel the night?" Starters: "I can hear ___." "I can feel ___." "I can see ___." Expect: cricket, sing, cool, warm, soft, blue. Hear four pairs, half a minute each.

#### 20 min. Poem chart

Hang the poem chart: a face, a hand, an ear and an eye. Review: these are the boxes on the feeling and sense chart from day 39. Model (6 minutes). Read the card "The air is cool and still." Place it under the hand and circle cool: "Cool tells what I touch." Read "I hear a cricket sing." Place it under the ear and circle sing.

Pairs sort the other four cards and say the word that tells (6 minutes): "I feel my blanket, warm" (hand, warm); "The sky is soft and blue" (soft under the hand, blue under the eye; either is right if the word is named); "The first small star blinks on" (eye); "The quiet sky is kind" (face). Watch for "I feel my blanket" under the face: the word feel is not a feeling here (error C). Check with the class, one card at a time (3 minutes). Then each child picks one line, draws what it helps them see or feel, and labels one word, by the map or by asking you to write it (5 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread the end of page 6 of the Chinese tale aloud, from "But they looked across". Show three pictures: a sad face, a loom, and a house. Say: "Point to the picture that shows how the people on the earth felt inside." Every child points. The rotating six then say the feeling word, 20 seconds each ([[s02-X43v2]]). A good answer is the sad face. The loom is error C. The house is error G. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. These marks go to day 44.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words: the class says whether the middle sound is the same in bed and bad, men and man, pet and pat, set and sat; a fist for the short e, a flat hand for the short a. Segment four sounds: /t/ /a/ /ng/ /k/, "tank", and /p/ /i/ /ng/ /k/, "pink"; tap each sound, then say the word. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Review: on day 39 you heard the endings in words like pink. Today they go on the map. Say: "When n comes before k, the two letters say /ng/ and then /k/." Map ank and ink (3 minutes). Decode: tank, rank, pink, wink, link (3 minutes). Spell on sound boxes, one box for each sound (2 minutes): sank and rink. Then read Sam Wants a Pet with the class, a finger under each word; the class reads the mapped words said and see twice first (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: collecting finished on day 40; the record stays closed until the sample. Dictation (6 minutes): say "Jen can get a pink net." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped word a is named aloud. Watch eight forms: J, e, g, p, k, c, a and n. Then each child points to the capital and the period (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. The cycle 6 sample is taken at the table, one child at a time, about 40 seconds each (4 minutes for a group). Show the word card and say: "Read this word." The child sounds it out ([[s02-A3]]). Remove the card and say: "Write the word I say." Say it once in a sentence, then once alone, and the child writes it on a sound-box sheet ([[s02-A4]]). Open the child's letter-form record and say: "Show me your b and your d" ([[s02-A5]]). Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Error K is a different middle vowel, error M a vowel left out, error AA a reversed b. The last 2 minutes of each group are matched reading of Sam Wants a Pet, one page each. The other children rotate through stations: reread Sam Wants a Pet with a partner; chain tank, rank, sank, pink and wink with letter cards; spell link and rink on sound boxes; draw a poem line again and say which box it goes in. No child waits more than one rotation without a station.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the sample marks to the unit 2 record sheet and write the heard-six column and the cycle record. Regroup for day 44: children who read and wrote the words go on together; children who need the vowel again meet in one group.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the six line cards on scrap paper and draw the face, hand, ear and eye on a sheet. One child: read Quiet Sky aloud in two sittings, sort the six lines with a grown-up, draw one line with a label, answer the exit by pointing, then read and write the sample words.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write the six lines on paper strips. No reader: use the words from class.

**Skip.** The talk pairs and the rotation.

- **10 min. Poem.** Read Quiet Sky aloud twice.
  **Say.** "Which words help you see, hear or feel the night?" Expect: cricket, sing, cool, warm, soft, blue.
- **10 min. Chart.** Your child sorts the six lines under a face, a hand, an ear and an eye drawn on a page, then draws one line with a word label.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child spells sank and rink, then prints "Jen can get a pink net."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X43v2]] You read the end of page 6 of The Weaver Girl and the Cowherd: people on the earth saw them, and felt sad. You show a sad face, a loom and a house. Your child points to how the people felt, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the poem, the chart, the exit and the sample; cut the pair talk to one minute a child. Longer: a child who finishes finds a second sense word in another line and says which box it goes in.

### Words and supports

Words: poem, line, speaker, feeling word, sense word, dew, pale, still. Frames: "I can hear ___." "I can feel ___." "I can see ___." "The speaker feels ___." Pictures first; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers; a scribe for labels. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 44 retells the Chinese tale and compares a poem with a fact. If many children pointed to the loom (error C) on today's exit, the opening of day 44's repair part goes to a four-minute repair: reread and mark the word that tells how the speaker felt. If many missed the sample (error K or error AA), the next foundations group on day 44 starts with a three-minute repair: isolate the spoken vowel, map it and blend again, and collect the missed letter pair again in the same rotation.
