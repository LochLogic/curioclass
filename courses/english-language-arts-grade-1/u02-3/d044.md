# Day 44: A tale, a poem and a fact

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make two sets of cards for the pairs. Set A: poem "The sky is soft and blue."; tale "Zhinu wove the clouds."; fact "Every morning, the sun comes up." Set B: poem "I hear a cricket sing."; tale "Niulang ran after her."; fact "After the sun sets, the night comes." Add a poem column to the two-column sheet. Write "I feel my blanket, warm." on a repair card. Write the six names for group 1. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 15 min. Retell the Chinese tale

Whole class on the rug. Say aloud: "This is a traditional Chinese tale." Review: you heard it on days 41 and 42. Reread it in three short groups of pages, showing each picture: pages 1 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 to 9 (7 minutes). Content note: the two are kept apart, then meet; no one is hurt. Partners hold the story map from day 41. Pairs retell the tale with the map, one child tells and then the other, about two minutes each (4 minutes). Expect: first, Zhinu and Niulang become a family; next, the Queen Mother draws the river of stars between them; last, the magpies make a bridge and the two meet. Hear two pairs, one minute each (2 minutes). Review: you said first, next, last on day 40. **Ask.** "What happens last?" Expect: the magpies make a bridge and they meet (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair first (4 minutes, from day 43's exit, error C). Hold up the card "I feel my blanket, warm." Read it. **Ask.** "Does warm tell how the speaker feels inside, or what the hand feels?" Expect: what the hand feels. Put the card under the hand on the poem chart and circle warm. Say: "A feeling word is for the face. Warm is for the hand." If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge and give the questions the whole ten minutes.

Reread pages 7 and 9 of the tale. Review: you said a lesson from the ending on day 42. **Ask.** "Which part of the tale tells a lesson?" Expect: the magpies make a bridge so the two can meet; or, people who care keep caring across a distance. Any lesson the events support is right (6 minutes).

#### 10 min. Poem against fact

Hang the two-column sheet from day 35: a tale, an information text. Add a third column, a poem. Say: "A poem is made of lines and sound. It gives a picture or a feeling, and it may have no events." Lay three cards, one under each column: the poem line "The moon peeks over the hill, a pale and gentle ring"; the tale line "The magpies made a bridge"; the fact "Some nights the moon is round and bright. Some nights it is a thin curve." Pairs, with the turn card, one turn each, pass allowed. **Ask.** "What does each one do?" Starters: "The poem ___." "The tale ___." "The fact ___." Expect: the poem gives a picture; the tale says what happens; the fact tells something you could check. Hear three pairs, half a minute each.

#### 20 min. Say the difference

Each pair gets the two sets of cards (poem line, tale event, fact). Pairs lay each set under the three columns (6 minutes). Walk and ask each pair to point to a story event or a fact before they name the column. Then each child says one difference to a partner, one turn each, one minute a child (2 minutes per pair, all pairs at once). Frames: "It is a poem because ___." "It tells a fact because ___." Each child draws one card and labels the kind (6 minutes). Check with the class, one card at a time (4 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Hold up two pages and read a part of each. *Quiet Sky*, page 1: "The first small star blinks on, as bright as morning dew." The star article, *Stars in Daylight and Darkness*, page 3: "The stars are still up there." Say: "Point to the page that tells a fact you could check." Every child points. The rotating six then say what the fact tells, 20 seconds each ([[s02-X44v2]]). A good answer is the star article page. The poem page is error D. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. These marks go to day 45.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words: the class says each word and shows a long i or a short i: sky, pin, fly, sit, try, win; a hand over the eye for the long i, a fist for the short i. Blend: /s/ /k/ /ie/, "sky"; /f/ /l/ /ie/, "fly". Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Cycle 7 begins with short e again: chain bed, red, net, ten (1 minute). New on the map: at the end of a short word, y can say its name, /ie/. Map my, fly and sky (3 minutes). Then two endings on bases you have mapped (3 minutes). Review: in kindergarten you met -ing at the end of a word and an action word tells what someone does. Show get and add s: gets. Show set: sets. Show fix and add ing: fixing. Say: "The s tells that one person does it now." Children read gets, sets and fixing from the cards. Last, pairs reread Sam Wants a Pet (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: collecting finished on day 40. Dictation (6 minutes): say "Tom is fixing the red net." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped word the is named aloud. Watch the forms T, f, x, g, d and n. Then each child points to the capital and the period (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table, matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level: the group reads Sam Wants a Pet aloud with expression, then spells gets and sets on sound boxes. Children who showed error K on the cycle sample start with 3 minutes on the middle sound: say fix and fox, isolate the middle sound with counters, point to the letter on the map and blend again, then try the unit 2 fresh retry cards: read the new short e word and spell the second. Children who reversed a b (error AA) print the missed b and d again on the record in the same rotation. The other children rotate through stations: reread Sam Wants a Pet with a partner; chain fix, fox, box and six with letter cards; spell my and sky on sound boxes; sort the six cards under the three columns again with a partner. No child waits more than one rotation without a station.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks to the unit 2 record sheet and write the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name the foundations groups for day 45.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the cards on scrap paper and fold a sheet in three for the columns. One child: retell the tale in two sittings with the map, sort the six cards under tale, poem and fact with a grown-up, draw one card, answer the exit by pointing, then spell my and sky.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write the lines on slips of paper. No sheet: fold a page in three.

**Skip.** The talk pairs and the rotation.

- **10 min. Retell.** Your child tells the tale from the pictures, first, next, last.
  **Say.** "Which part of the tale tells a lesson?" Expect: the magpies make a bridge so the two can meet.
- **10 min. Poem or fact.** Read "The sky is soft and blue" and "Every morning, the sun comes up."
  **Say.** "What does each one do?" Expect: the poem gives a picture; the fact can be checked.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child spells my and sky, then prints "Tom is fixing the red net."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X44v2]] You read two parts: Quiet Sky, "The first small star blinks on," and the star article, "The stars are still up there." Your child points to the fact, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the retell, the exit and the rotation; sort one set of cards, not two. Longer: a child who finishes writes a label for a fact card and a different label for a poem card.

### Words and supports

Words: poem, tale, fact, lesson, first, next, last, check. Frames: "It is a poem because ___." "It tells a fact because ___." "The lesson is ___." Pictures first; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers; a scribe for labels. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 45 rereads the Efik and Ibibio tale for event order and describing words. If many children pointed to the poem page (error D) on today's exit, the opening of day 45's repair part goes to a four-minute repair: point to a story event or an information statement before naming the kind of text.
