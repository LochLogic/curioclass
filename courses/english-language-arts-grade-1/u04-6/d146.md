# Day 146: Why animals need birds, and word parts

## Teacher page

### Before class

Name the heard six, the catch-up group and the pairs. Set out each pair's point card, two strips and card sets. Write the broom card (below) as you will say it. Keep the exit card face down. Do not use the words useful, harmful or harmless today.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Repair for day 145's exit (error C, s04-X145av2), 3 minutes. Say: "This card is made up for practice. A writer says the class needs a new broom. The old broom has no bristles, so dust stays on the floor. The broom is green." Pairs say one fact that tells why, using because, 90 seconds; hear two pairs. If many missed, say a second made-up card (a writer says the class needs a longer rope; the short rope drops the jumper; the rope is red) for 1 minute. Then, 2 minutes. Review: -ful means full of and -less means without (day 126), and an ending -s, -ed or -ing goes on a root (day 126). Write care, careful, careless and wait, waits, waited, waiting. Say: "Today new words use these parts."

#### 15 min. Read

Teach three words with pictures, 3 minutes: hunter (an animal that catches other animals to eat), chain (here, a line that shows who eats whom) and insect (a small animal with six legs). Read *Birds Help Animals*, pages 1 to 3, aloud once, whole, 2 minutes. Say plainly: "Hawks and owls are hunters. They eat other animals, and crows are one." Show only the labeled chain drawing, grasshopper, crow, hawk. Nothing is shown or described in detail. Reread page 1 and page 3 with their pictures, 3 minutes, and say the point: "Animals need birds because birds give them food." Children follow the arrows on the chain drawing and point to the grasshopper, the crow and the hawk, 3 minutes. Think aloud with the because test (days 133 and 142), 3 minutes: "Animals need birds because hawks and owls eat crows. That tells why, so it is a reason." Last, 1 minute: say the point together.

#### 15 min. Talk

Partners take two turns each time with the turn card, 60 seconds, then two children are heard, 40 seconds each, so the six heard are spread over the asks. Pass is allowed.

1. **Ask.** "What does the writer say animals need from birds?" Expect: food.
2. **Ask.** "Say a fact from page 2 that tells why." Expect: raccoons, opossums and snakes eat crow eggs, or hawks and owls eat crows.
3. **Ask.** "On the chain drawing, what does the crow eat?" Expect: the grasshopper.
4. **Ask.** "On the chain drawing, what eats the crow?" Expect: the hawk.

#### 20 min. Writing and language

Reasons, 6 minutes. Put the point card on the board. Lay the two strips under it, one at a time, and test each with because: raccoons, opossums and snakes eat crow eggs; hawks and owls eat crows. Pairs test them again and each child says "Animals need birds because ___" to a partner, 40 seconds each. Word work, 14 minutes. Board: -ful, full of; -less, without. Model, 3 minutes: cover the ending on careless; the root is care; the ending says without. Pairs sort joyful, thankful, colorful, cloudless and windless under full of and without by the ending, 3 minutes, and say each meaning aloud: joyful is full of joy. Then lay the ending cards on the root play, 4 minutes: play, plays, played, playing. Cover the ending with a finger; the word left is the root. Say when each happens: plays is now, played is before, playing is going on now. Last, 4 minutes: each child draws one of the five affix words as a picture and says its meaning to a partner. A child who says windless is full of wind is error F; point back to the ending. A child who says played has nothing to do with play is error G; lay the ending card back on the root.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is invented for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet and show its three pictures, 1 minute. All children mark a picture on the page, 1 minute. **Ask.** "Which picture shows sleeveless?" ([[s04-X146a]]) Collect the pages, 1 minute. Children file their folders, 2 minutes. The spoken asks are heard in the rotation below. The shirt with long sleeves is error F.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Blend /p/ /i/ with the long i and /l/ /i/ with the long i. Segment pie. Say sky, night and pie: which sound is the same in all three?

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 24, lesson 122. New today: ie says the long i sound in pie, lie, fried and cried. Map it, 2 minutes. Compare igh, ie, i_e and y on the sort board with the twelve sorting cards, 3 minutes. Chain on small boards, 20 seconds a word, saying each, tapping the sounds and writing it: pie, lie, fried, cried, 2 minutes. Then map three irregular words for Night Skies, 3 minutes: have, sometimes, through. Say each, point to the part that does not say its usual sound (the e in have is silent; the o in some says /u/; ough in through says /oo/) and write it from memory.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Dated printing set with your written feedback, 5 minutes. All children write the dictated sentence "Pie is on the high shelf." whole, then a word at a time, 5 minutes. It is not scored.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The catch-up group first, 10 minutes: the six children the cycle record names; map igh and ie, blend, read, spell, reread. Then four groups of six at your table, 5 minutes each, with Night Skies pages 1 to 10 read for meaning and expression, each child heard in turn for 35 seconds. The other groups reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. The heard six come one or two to a group; ask each, 15 seconds:

- **Ask.** With the exit card in view: "Say how yelps and yelped are alike." ([[s04-X146b]])
- **Ask.** With page 3 of *Birds Help Animals* in view: "Say why animals need birds." ([[s04-X146c]])

Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card or the page once), not yet seen. "They are different words" is error G.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Heard-six column, the cycle record for day 146 and the exit marks. The exit marks go to day 148's opening.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the cards on scraps and say the pages aloud. One child: say the point, sort the five words and lay the endings on play with a grown-up.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: say each word aloud.

**Skip.** The sound work.

- **10 min. Tell.** Say: "Hawks and owls eat crows. Raccoons eat crow eggs. Animals need birds because birds give them food." **Say.** "Why do animals need birds?" Expect: birds give them food.
- **10 min. Parts.** Say: "Careless means without care." **Say.** "What does careful mean?" Expect: full of care.
- **10 min. Words.** Print "pie" and "high."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X146a]] [[s04-X146b]] [[s04-X146c]] Read a short made-up page. Your child points to the picture for a made-up word ending in -less, says how two forms of one word are alike, and says why animals need birds.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the read, the sort, the ending cards and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes finds another word that ends in -ful or -less.

### Words and supports

Words: hunter, chain, insect, root, ending, full of, without. Frame: "___ means full of ___." and "___ means without ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. Note for the day 150 hearing: Night Skies carries "day"; it stays.

### Tomorrow

The exit and the rotation tell you who can use -less and who can name a root. Day 147 is the no-help sitting on a new text, so nothing is repaired or taught there and the affix and ending cards stay in the folder. A child who missed the -less picture (error F) or the root (error G) is repaired in day 148's opening, on a fresh card.
