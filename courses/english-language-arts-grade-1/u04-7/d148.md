# Day 148: A model paragraph and where its facts come from

## Teacher page

### Before class

Name the heard six, the catch-up group and the pairs. Put each pair's four strips (in a mixed pile) and two page tags on the table. Set the Lights Out card where all can see it. Keep the exit card face down. Do not write the words seed, day or light on any card today.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Repair for day 146's exit, 3 minutes, on cards made up for practice. Say: "This card is made up for practice. The boy was hatless." Cover the ending: the root is hat, and -less means without, so hatless means without a hat. Pairs say it back, 40 seconds each. Then say: "A girl jumps. A girl jumped." Cover the endings: both words have the root jump. Pairs say how the two are alike, 40 seconds each. A child who says hatless is full of hats is error F; a child who says they are different words is error G. Point back to the covered ending or the root. Then, 2 minutes. Review: an informative paragraph has four parts, a topic, a fact, a fact and a closing (unit 1 and day 141). Say the four parts together with a finger for each.

#### 15 min. Read

Say: "Today a writer shows us how a paragraph is made. This is a model, made up by Curio Class for practice." Show the card and read *Lights Out* aloud, whole, 2 minutes. Read it again slowly, one sentence at a time, with a finger under each, 3 minutes. Say the new word once, with the picture: pull means to move something toward you. Then reread page 2 of *Windows Birds Can See* and page 3 of *Marks Outside* aloud with their pictures, 4 minutes. Review: you heard both pages on days 141 to 143. Stop after each page and say what it tells. Last, 3 minutes: say the two facts from the model beside the pages and find each match together (bright lights at night pull birds toward windows; lights turned off do not pull birds), and children point to the page picture that shows each. Nothing is shown of a bird being hurt.

#### 15 min. Talk

Partners take two turns each time with the turn card, 60 seconds, then two children are heard, 40 seconds each, so the six heard are spread over the asks. Pass is allowed.

1. **Ask.** "What is the whole paragraph about?" Expect: lights at night and birds, or turning off lights for birds.
2. **Ask.** "Which page tells the fact in the second sentence?" Expect: page 2 of the window page (or page 3 of the marks page; both say it).
3. **Ask.** "What does the last sentence ask people to do?" Expect: turn the lights out for birds.

Say back each answer with the sentence it came from.

#### 20 min. Writing and language

Order the model, 6 minutes. Pairs read nothing alone: you say each strip aloud as a pair lifts it, and they lay the four in order: topic, fact, fact, closing. Say the order together. Check against the card. Tag the facts, 7 minutes. Pairs lay a page tag beside each fact strip: page 2 of *Windows Birds Can See* and page 3 of *Marks Outside*. You reread the page sentence each time and the pair says "The page says it." A strip with a tag from the wrong page is read against that page again, once. Label, 7 minutes. Each child draws the lit building from the card in the box on side 2 of the page and prints one word label under it for what the paragraph is about, night. Walk, and name the topic with each child in one sentence.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is invented for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet and show its three plan pictures, 1 minute. All children mark a picture on the page, 1 minute. **Ask.** "Which plan shows the main topic and two details?" ([[s04-X148a]]) Collect the pages, 1 minute. Children file their folders, 2 minutes. The spoken ask is heard in the rotation below. The plan with the slot as the topic, or with only a note drawn, is error Y.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Segment bright, sight and high into sounds and count them: three, three and two. Blend /s/ /igh/ /t/. Say bright, sight and tight: which sound is the same in all three?

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 24, lesson 124. No new pattern. Read the word cards, sight, tight, high and cried, 2 minutes: say each, tap the sounds, point to igh or ie. Compare igh and ie on the sort board, 2 minutes. Chain on small boards, 20 seconds a word, saying each, tapping the sounds and writing it: sight, tight, high, cried, 3 minutes. Then reread Night Skies pages 1 to 4 together for meaning, 3 minutes.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Dated printing set with your written feedback, 5 minutes. All children write the dictated sentence "The night is bright and high." whole, then a word at a time, 5 minutes. It is not scored.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The catch-up group first, 10 minutes: the six children the cycle record names; map igh and ie, blend, read, spell, reread. Then four groups of six at your table, 5 minutes each, with Night Skies read for expression, each child heard for 35 seconds (6 times 35 seconds is 3.5 minutes, 1 minute for the asks below and 30 seconds to change groups). The other groups reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. The heard six come one or two to a group; ask each, 15 seconds:

- **Ask.** With the exit card in view: "Say a closing for this page." ([[s04-X148b]])
- **Ask.** Read the two made-up pages aloud: "Page A: The mailbox is by the door. Page B: Notes go in the slot." Then: "Which page tells where notes go?" ([[s04-X148c]])

Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card or the pages once), not yet seen. "Mailboxes are the best" is error X; page A for where notes go is error W.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Heard-six column, the cycle record for day 148 and the exit marks. The exit marks go to day 149's opening. After class, judge day 147's assessment sheets against their keys and move the marks to the record sheet; none is returned before day 157, and nothing from them is repaired today (their repair is day 157).

### Other rooms

No printer: write the four sentences on scraps and say the pages aloud. One child: lay the four strips in order with a grown-up and say where each fact came from.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: say each sentence aloud.

**Skip.** The sound work.

- **10 min. Tell.** Say: "Bright lights at night can pull birds toward windows. People can turn off lights they do not need. Then birds are not pulled toward the glass. Please turn the lights out for birds." **Say.** "What is the whole paragraph about?" Expect: lights and birds at night.
- **10 min. Order.** Cut the four sentences apart, mix them and lay them in order, and say which is the closing. **Say.** "Which sentence comes last?" Expect: please turn the lights out for birds.
- **10 min. Words.** Print "sight" and "high."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X148a]] [[s04-X148b]] [[s04-X148c]] Read a short made-up page about a mailbox. Your child points to the plan with the topic and two details, says a closing for it, and says which of two pages tells where notes go.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the read, the order, the tags and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes says a closing for the model in different words.

### Words and supports

Words: topic, fact, closing, source, pull. Frame: "The page says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. Note for the day 150 hearing: Night Skies carries "day"; it stays.

### Tomorrow

The exit and the rotation tell you who names the topic and two details and who closes on the topic. Day 149 opens with a 3-minute repair for error Y, on a fresh card, then plans the window bird portrait from the two pages with a page tag on each fact.
