# Day 149: Plan the window bird portrait

## Teacher page

### Before class

Name the heard six, the catch-up group and the pairs. Put the plan pages and the pairs' strips out. Keep the exit card face down and the sample card (day 149 only) in your folder. Do not write the words seed, day or light on any card today. Make three cards (nest boxes, the steps inside, the lunch) and five detail strips from *Volunteers Build Bluebird Boxes* (see Talk).

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Repair for day 148's exit (error Y, s04-X148a), 3 minutes, on a card made up for practice. Say: "This card is made up for practice. Our class has a book shelf. The books stand in rows. A sign tells the shelf's name." Draw a topic box and two detail boxes. Say: "Which one holds both details, the rows or the book shelf?" Test each against the two details: the book shelf holds both, and the rows hold only one. A child who picks one detail is error Y; test it again against the other detail. Then, 2 minutes: touch each part of the plan page on the board, topic, fact, fact, closing, and say what each does. Review: the four parts (day 148).

#### 15 min. Read

Say: "Today you plan your own portrait. First one more page for practice." Read *Volunteers Build Bluebird Boxes* whole, aloud, with its pictures, 3 minutes. Say the picture words: cavity (a hollow space in a tree) and volunteers (people who help for free). Then reread the portrait's sources, 3 minutes: *Windows Birds Can See* pages 1 and 3 and *Marks Outside* pages 1 and 2. Review: you heard these pages on days 141 to 143. After each page, children say in a few words what it tells, 3 minutes. Each child takes out the research organizer from days 143 and 144 and checks each note against its page tag, 4 minutes: a note with no page tag, or a tag from the wrong page, is fixed by finding the line on the page together. This revised organizer is kept for day 152. Children point to the picture on each page that goes with a note, 2 minutes. Nothing is shown of a bird being hurt.

#### 15 min. Talk

Review: a topic is what the whole card is about, and a detail is one fact under it (kindergarten and unit 3). Pairs get the three cards and five detail strips from *Volunteers Build Bluebird Boxes*: the staff cut and marked the wood; the children fitted the boxes together; steps inside help baby birds climb; the children wrote a welcome on each box; everyone ate lunch together. Pairs sort each strip under a topic card, yes if the strip is about it and no if it is not, 4 minutes. Then partners take two turns each time, 60 seconds, and two children are heard, 40 seconds each, so the six heard are spread over the asks. Pass is allowed.

1. **Ask.** "Which one topic holds every detail of the bluebird page?" Expect: nest boxes.
2. **Ask.** "Which card holds only one detail?" Expect: the steps inside, or the lunch.
3. **Ask.** "What goes in the topic box of our window portrait?" Expect: bird-safe windows.

Then the bluebird response, 3 minutes, kept for day 152: each child draws what the bluebird page is mainly about, prints one word label, and draws two details from the page with a word label each. Nobody is shown how; point to the page picture if a child asks.

#### 20 min. Writing and language

Hand out the plan pages. Topic box, 3 minutes: copy the word window from the board in the topic box, and test it as in the opening, one fact at a time. Facts, 8 minutes: each child takes from the folder the strips "Glass can show a reflection, and a bird may fly toward it." and "Marks show a bird that the glass is there." and sets each in a fact box. You reread the page lines; each child pulls the matching tag, page 1 of *Windows Birds Can See* and page 3 of *Windows Birds Can See*, and sticks it in the small box beside the fact. A partner puts a finger on the page line to check each. A fact with no tag, or a tag from the wrong page, is error W; find the line on the page together. Closing box, 3 minutes: print one word label, visible, and show a partner by pointing how it goes back to the topic. Drawing, 6 minutes: a paper window with marks. Do not tell where the marks go. Word labels only; each fact stays a strip. The plan page goes in the portrait folder.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is invented for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet, 1 minute. **Ask.** "Make the plan on your page: draw one fact from page A in box A and one fact from page B in box B." All children draw, 2 minutes ([[s04-X149a]]). Collect the pages, 1 minute. Children file their folders, 1 minute. The spoken asks are heard in the rotation below. A plan with a fact from page A only is error W.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Segment fried, pie, night and high and count the sounds. Say the vowel sound in each: the long i. Blend /f/ /r/ /i/ /d/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 24, lesson 125, with the cycle progress sample. The heard six, 4 minutes, one at a time: show the held word flight to read, take it away, then dictate the word tried to write, 40 seconds each. The other children chain on small boards, 20 seconds a word, saying each, tapping the sounds and writing it: pie, fried, night, high, sight, 4 minutes. Last, 2 minutes: read the five word cards aloud together. The two sample words stay in your folder and are never used as practice. Note the heard six on the cycle record; the cycle record names the next catch-up six.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Dated printing set with your written feedback, 5 minutes. Dictate "tried" and each child writes it beside one printed word they choose, 5 minutes. It is not scored.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The catch-up group first, 10 minutes: the six children the cycle record names; map igh and ie, blend, read, spell, reread. Then four groups of six at your table, 5 minutes each, with Night Skies read for expression, each child heard for 35 seconds (6 times 35 seconds is 3.5 minutes, then 1 minute for the asks below and 30 seconds to change groups). The other groups reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. The heard six come one or two to a group; ask each, 15 seconds an ask:

- **Ask.** With the exit card in view: "Say one fact from page A." ([[s04-X149b]])
- **Ask.** With the exit card in view: "Say one fact from page B." ([[s04-X149c]])

Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. "Clay is gray" is error W.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Heard-six column, the cycle record for day 149 and the exit marks. The exit marks go to day 150's opening.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the four boxes on a plain sheet and say the pages aloud. One child: sort the strips under the cards, then set two fact strips with a grown-up who finds each line on its page.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: say each sentence aloud.

**Skip.** The sound work.

- **10 min. Topic.** Say: "Some children built nest boxes. They cut wood and fitted the pieces. They wrote a welcome on each box." **Say.** "What is the one topic that holds all of that?" Expect: nest boxes.
- **10 min. Plan.** Draw four boxes in a column. **Say.** "Say a fact about windows that a page says." Expect: glass can show a reflection, or marks show a bird the glass is there. Write one word in a box.
- **10 min. Words.** Print "fried" and "high."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X149a]] [[s04-X149b]] [[s04-X149c]] Read a short made-up pair of pages about clay. Your child draws a plan with a fact from each page and says one fact from each page.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the sort, the two fact boxes with their tags and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes adds a second tagged fact box.

### Words and supports

Words: topic, detail, fact, page tag, volunteers, cavity. Frame: "The topic is ___." and "The page says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. Note for the day 150 hearing: Night Skies carries "day"; it stays.

### Tomorrow

The exit and the rotation tell you who can find a fact on a named page and tag it. Day 150 opens with a 3-minute repair for error W on fresh pages, returns each plan, and drafts the paragraph from it, four lines; the plan leaves out where the marks go.
