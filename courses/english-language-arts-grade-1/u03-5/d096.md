# Day 96: Find facts about one beak with a heading, a label and the glossary

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 95's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages, the folders and the article. Fold each folder and put the label chart on its cover. Make the repair cards and the word cards. Put out the syllable chart, letter cards, the sound-spelling map and the readers. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 95's exit showed a detail picked in place of the topic (error A). Lay out three cards: a duck's feathers, keep water out, and stays dry. **Ask.** "Which card is the topic? The other two tell about it." Expect: a duck's feathers. Slide the two detail cards under it. Say the topic again with the class: "The topic is the duck's feathers." Say the rule: "The topic is what every detail is about." If many made error A on day 95's exit, repeat with a hawk's claws as the topic and the details grasp food and hold it.

#### 8 min. Reread

Review: Beaks Find Food, which you heard on days 79 and 95. Read pages 1 and 2 aloud, three minutes, then the glossary on page 3, one minute. Review: a heading and a diagram label, from days 80 and 81. Children put a finger on the heading Strong Beaks for Seeds, then on the label beak beside the sparrow's head. Hear them say the heading and the label together, two minutes. Close with one minute: each pair says the heading of a second part to each other.

#### 12 min. Locate facts

Review the route from days 81 to 83: the contents leads to the page, the heading leads to the fact, a label tells where a part is, and the glossary says what a word means. Today all four find one fact. Two minutes to show the pieces on the article, one at a time.

Four minutes, you lead with the sparrow. Finger on the contents entry Strong Beaks for Seeds, page 2. Turn to page 2, finger on the heading, then the sentence under it: "The beak cracks hard seeds." Finger on the label beak to show which part does it. Turn to the glossary. **Ask.** "What does the glossary say crack means?" Expect: to break open. Read the fact again with the meaning: the beak breaks hard seeds open. The cover is put away: the first page picture of four birds does not show which fact goes with which beak.

Six minutes, pairs by the same route. Pairs at tables one to three take the duck, the other pairs take the hawk. Each pair finds the contents entry, the heading, the fact and the glossary word (strain for the duck, hooked for the hawk) with a finger and says them to each other, all at once. Walk among them. A child who looks at the cover picture to find the fact has used the cover in place of a feature (error D): put the cover away and trace the route again from the contents.

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the turn card. **Ask.** "What would be lost if the diagram stood alone?" Pairs take turns, three minutes, all at once; pass allowed. Hear six pairs, one minute each. Expect: the diagram shows where the beak is, but only the words tell what it does, it cracks hard seeds. Close with one minute: point to the label and then to the sentence, and say which tells where and which tells what.

#### 20 min. Notes

Review: a note card has a picture, one word and the source label, from day 91, and B on the label chart is Beaks Find Food. Give each child a folder and two note cards. Three minutes to set up: each child picks one beak from the article. Twelve minutes: each child makes two notes about that beak. Card one: a picture of the beak's shape with one word, such as thick or flat. Card two: a picture of what the beak does with one word, such as cracks or strains. Words are copied from the word cards. Each card gets the label B. The child points to the line on page 2 that backs each card before it goes in the folder. Five minutes at the end: hear six children, one minute each, while the others file their cards. **Ask.** "Where on the page is this fact?" Expect: a finger on the heading and the line under it. A card with no label B is not yet a note.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish filing; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures. Say: "Point to the part that gets the food." ([[s03-X96a]]) Then, with the card in view: "What does the glossary tell you about thin?" ([[s03-X96b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the cover has used the cover in place of a feature (error D). A child who says thin means soft has given a meaning the glossary does not (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say mad and made; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: made. Blend /g/ /r/ /a/ /p/, "grape". Say lake and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /l/, /a/ (long), /k/. Segment shake: /sh/ /a/ /k/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 15, lesson 78. Review: a_e, from days 94 and 95, the silent e at the end makes the a say its name. Show pairs on cards: tap and tape, cap and cape, mat and mate. Say each and tap the sounds; children say which column of the syllable chart: closed or silent e. Map the new words on cards: take, lake, snake. Say lake, tap /l/ /a/ /k/, write l, a, k, e: the e is silent. Chain on letter cards, one at a time: lake, take, snake, snack. Say the contrast: snack has a short a and ends in a consonant, snake has the long a and a silent e. Decode, then spell, two words: take and lake. About 40 seconds a word. The reader Kate and Jake Bake a Cake is reread in the rotation.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs S s and A a (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate: "Jake has a snake." Say it whole, then a word at a time; has and a are mapped, and Jake is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Jake and snake the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread on cap and cape, mat and mate, tap and tape, about 40 seconds a word. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, reread for meaning and expression: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Ben's Donut, chain words with letter cards, sort closed and silent e word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the notes you saw, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who used the cover in place of a feature (error D) for day 97's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a sheet into a folder, cut scrap paper into note cards, and read the article from any printed bird page with a contents and a glossary. One child: trace the route to one fact with a finger, make two note cards, label them B, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: use any bird page with a heading and a glossary. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Find.** Show your child the contents, the heading and the label beak on the article. **Say.** "Where do you start to find the fact about the sparrow's beak?" Expect: the contents, then the heading.
- **10 min. Glossary.** Read the glossary line for crack. **Say.** "What does crack mean?" Expect: to break open.
- **5 min. Note.** Your child draws one note, a picture, one word and the letter B.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X96a]] [[s03-X96b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points to the part, then says what the glossary tells.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the route, the notes and the exit; cut the second fact by pairs. Longer: a child makes a third note from the heron's part and labels it B.

### Words and supports

Words: contents, heading, label, glossary, note, source. Frames: "The heading is ___." "The label says ___." "The glossary says ___ means ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can use a feature to find a fact and who can say what a glossary line means. A child who used the cover in place of a feature (error D) is named for day 97's five-minute repair. Day 97 compares beak facts from two pages and keeps notes labelled by source.
