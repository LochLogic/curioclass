# Day 98: Say how a beak helps its bird

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 97's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages and the connection-sentence sheets, each with the three look-fors at its foot. Make the repair cards and the word cards. Take the cycle 15 sample words from your folder; they stay in your hands. Keep the shared answer chart on the wall. The exit pictures stay face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 97's exit showed decoration offered in place of a located fact (error G). Show a bordered blank card beside a note: a picture of small ridges, the word comb and the label D. **Ask.** "Which card holds a fact you can point to?" Expect: the labeled note. Then lay two source lines: page B, "The beak strains the mud and water out." and page D, "Tiny ridges in the beak work like a comb." Say: "These are made up from the pages." Pairs say one located fact and its label to each other, all at once, one minute. Hear three pairs. Then three pairs at a time come to the shared answer chart and stick one strip with a fact and its label, about 20 seconds each round, five rounds. If many made error G on day 97's exit, repeat with a hawk's hooked beak, page B.

#### 8 min. Reread

Review: Beaks Find Food, from days 79, 95 and 96. Read the sparrow part and the hawk part again aloud, three minutes. Then read the first sentence of page 1 and the last sentence of page 2: "Each shape helps a bird get its own kind of food." and "Each beak helps its bird get food." Point to the words helps and get, and say they join the beak to the food. Review: the connection arrow from day 84, part, arrow, job, with so and because. Two minutes: children point to the words that tell what the beak does to the food, cracks in the sparrow part, tears in the hawk part. Last three minutes: pairs say what the beak does to the food for the duck's beak, taking turns.

#### 12 min. The connection, modeled

Show the connection-sentence sheet: a part box, an arrow, a food box and three lines, part, food, how. Model one sentence on the hawk. Draw the hooked beak in the part box and meat in the food box and the arrow between. Say the three lines: part, the hooked beak; food, meat; how, it tears the meat into small bits. Then join them: "The hawk can eat because its hooked beak tears meat into small bits." Say it again the other way round with so: "The hooked beak tears meat into small bits, so the hawk can eat." **Ask.** "Which words join the beak to the food?" Expect: because, or so, or the arrow. Say the rule: "A connection names the part, the food and how."

Then, six minutes, pairs. Each child picks one beak from the notes in the folder, the sparrow, the duck or the heron. The frame is on the board: "The ___ can ___ because its beak ___." Pairs say a sentence to each other, taking turns, all fifteen pairs at once. Hear four pairs, 30 seconds each. Expect, for example, "The sparrow can eat seeds because its beak cracks them." A child who says only "it has a thick beak" has named the part and not the link: ask "What does the beak do to the food?"

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the turn card. **Ask.** "How does the detail explain survival?" Pairs take turns, three minutes, all at once; pass allowed. Hear six pairs, one minute each. Expect: the beak gets the food, and a bird needs food to survive, from day 79. Close with one minute: say the chain with the class, beak, food, survive.

#### 20 min. Write and feedback

Twelve minutes of writing. Each child fills the sheet: draws the part and the food and the arrow, copies the labels from the word cards, and prints one connection sentence on the lines, or says it to you and copies under your hand. About a third of the class may need you for about 20 seconds each; a child who can print goes on alone. The sentence must rest on a note in the folder, with its label.

Feedback begins as soon as the first children finish, and goes on for the last eight minutes. The three look-fors are at the foot of the sheet: it names the part; it names the food; it joins them with so, because or an arrow. For each child, tick each look-for you see, write one prompt word for what to add, such as food? or how?, and say it in a short line. That is about 20 seconds a child, ten minutes for thirty, so do the first twelve finishers while the others write. Say plainly to yourself: eight minutes alone would not be enough. A food claim no note backs is not kept (error B): ask "Which note says that?" Mark shown, shown with help (after one prompt), not yet seen. Keep each sentence with its feedback in the folder for day 99 and for day 118.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud the part of Heron by the Water headed Waiting. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures: the heron striking a fish with its beak, the heron's long toes, a fish alone. Say: "Point to the picture that shows how the heron gets food." ([[s03-X98av2]]) Then, with the page in view: "Say one sentence that connects the beak to the food." ([[s03-X98bv2]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the part once), not yet seen. A child who says only "it has a long beak" has named the part and not the link (error A). A child who says "it eats seeds" has made a claim the page does not make (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say gap and gape; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: gape. Blend /s/ /n/ /a/ /k/, "snack". Say name and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /n/, /a/ (long), /m/. Segment flake: /f/ /l/ /a/ /k/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 15, lesson 80. Review: a_e from days 94 to 97, the silent e makes the a say its name. The progress sample is taken on the rotation today; the class work is a review sort. Show cards in a chorus, all at once: tap, tape, can, cane, mad, made, cap, cape. Each child says the word and which column of the syllable chart it belongs in, closed or silent e. Then chain, one at a time on letter cards: made, make, take, tape, cake, bake. Decode, then spell, two words: made and cake. Teach only the cycle's correspondences and mapped words. The held sample words are not used here.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs D d and M m (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate: "Jake made a cake." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a is mapped, made was mapped on day 94, and Jake is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Jake, made and cake the e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names. For their first five minutes they map, blend, read, spell and reread; for the last five they take the sample, 50 seconds each. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, one child at a time, 50 seconds each: show the held word card of [[s03-A15]] and say "Read this word." Take the card away and say the dictated word of [[s03-A16]] once in a sentence and once alone: "Write the word I say." Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child who writes only the consonants has left the vowels out (error M). The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, chain words with letter cards, sort closed and silent e word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the sentence marks, the sample marks and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Use today's results to name tomorrow's catch-up six: this is the same-block regrouping. List by name the children who named only the part (error A) for day 99's repair, and those who left the vowels out of the dictated word (error M) for day 99's three-minute rotation repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the part box, the arrow and the food box on scrap paper and read the article aloud from any printed bird page. One child: say the sentence for one beak, draw the arrow, copy the labels while you scribe the sentence, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No sheet: draw a box, an arrow and a second box on scrap paper. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The sample, the rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Reread.** Read the sparrow part of the beak article aloud. Point to the words that tell what the beak does to the food.
- **10 min. Connect.** **Say.** "What does the beak do to the food, and what does the bird get?" Expect: it cracks the seeds, so the sparrow can eat.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws the beak, an arrow and the food, and says the sentence with so or because.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X98av2]] [[s03-X98bv2]] Read the part of Heron by the Water headed Waiting. Your child points to the picture, then says one sentence.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the modeled sentence, the writing, the exit and the sample; give feedback to the first twelve and the rest tomorrow. Longer: a child writes a second sentence for another beak and checks it against a note.

### Words and supports

Words: connect, because, so, survive, food, part. Frames: "The ___ can ___ because its beak ___." "The beak ___, so the bird can ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick the picture that shows how a beak gets food and who can say a sentence that joins the beak to the food. A child who named only the part (error A) is named for day 99's five-minute repair, and a child who left the vowels out of the dictated word (error M) for day 99's three-minute repair in the rotation. Day 99 gathers each child's notes, checks one fact in a second page and revises today's sentence with its feedback.
