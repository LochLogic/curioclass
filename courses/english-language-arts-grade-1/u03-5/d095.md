# Day 95: Name the topic and key details of the beak article

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 94's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages, the article and the exit card with its three pictures. Make the topic card and detail cards and the word cards. Keep the research-cycle chart on the wall. Put out the syllable chart, letter cards, the sound-spelling map and the reader Kate and Jake Bake a Cake. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 94's exit showed a paragraph that stopped with no closing (error F). Show a fresh paragraph on a card: "A hawk has sharp claws. The claws grasp food and hold it." Read it, then stop and say "And." **Ask.** "What ending goes back to the topic?" Pairs say one to each other, all fifteen at once, one minute. Hear three pairs. Expect: "Sharp claws help the hawk hold its food." Write the best one under the paragraph. Say the rule: "A closing goes back to the topic." If many made error F on day 94's exit, repeat with a duck's webbed feet: "Skin joins its toes. The feet push the water."

#### 5 min. Assign the product

Point to the research-cycle chart (review: it has run the unit since day 90). Say the job in three short pieces and have the class echo each: "Name the topic. Give two facts a page backs. Finish it with a closing." Then say the whole job: "Explain how one bird's beak helps it get food." Review: the paragraph-frame, topic, fact, fact, closing, from unit 1 and day 93. Say: "Nothing is written today. For the next days you collect notes, then you write." Partners say the job back to each other, all pairs at once, two minutes. Hear three pairs, 30 seconds each.

#### 12 min. Words, then read Beaks Find Food

Show six cards, one at a time: bird, food, beak, bill, shape, survive. Review: beak and survive (stay alive) from day 79. New today: bill, another word for a beak, and shape, what something looks like. The class says each word, then you say it in a sentence and the class says the sentence back. About 40 seconds a word, four minutes. Then read Beaks Find Food aloud from page 1 to the end of page 2, three minutes. Review: you heard it whole on day 79. Last, four minutes: fingers on the heading Strong Beaks for Seeds and the first sentence under it, then the same for each of the other three parts. Review: a heading leads to the fact, from day 81.

#### 13 min. Topic and details, taught

Review: the topic card with detail cards beneath it, the main-idea-details picture, from days 79 to 82. Say the review words: "A topic is what a part is about. A detail is one fact under it." Read aloud the last part, Hooked Beaks for Meat. Put the card "the hawk's beak" on top. **Ask.** "What are all three sentences about?" Expect: the hawk's beak. Set three detail cards beneath: "hooked", "tears meat into small bits", "then it can eat". Say the rule: "The topic is what every detail is about." Now the new idea, key details. Say: "Our job is how a beak helps a bird get food. A key detail is a detail about the beak that helps with that job." Point at the three cards: each tells about the hawk's beak, so each is key. Retell once with the frame: "The topic is the hawk's beak. A key detail is it tears meat."

Then the pairs' turn, four minutes. Read aloud the first part, Strong Beaks for Seeds. **Ask.** "What is the topic, and what is one key detail?" Pairs answer to each other, all at once, two minutes. Hear four pairs, 30 seconds each. Expect: the sparrow's beak; it cracks hard seeds, or it is thick and strong. A child who names seeds as the topic has taken a detail for the topic (error A): ask "Are all the sentences about seeds, or about the beak?"

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the turn card from unit 1; each partner takes a turn and may pass. Lay out three detail cards, each with a small picture, and read them: "A duck has a flat beak." "The beak strains the mud and water out." "A duck has webbed feet." **Ask.** "Which details are about the beak?" Pairs take turns, three minutes, all at once. Hear six pairs, one minute each. Expect: the first two. The third tells about the duck's feet, a different topic. Close with one minute: slide the two beak cards under the topic card "the duck's beak" and set the feet card aside.

#### 10 min. Record

Each child draws one beak from the article, the sparrow's, the duck's, the heron's or the hawk's, in the box on the student page, and copies one word label for the food from a word card: seeds, mud, fish or meat. Eight minutes. Walk among them and hear six children while the others draw, one minute each. **Ask.** "Tell me one thing about your beak that helps it get food." Expect a key detail, such as it cracks seeds or it catches fish. A child who tells a fact about the bird's feet has left the topic: ask "Is that about the beak?" Each child keeps the drawing for the folder tomorrow.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish drawing; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures. Say: "Point to the picture that is the topic of the card." ([[s03-X95a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Say one detail that tells about it." ([[s03-X95b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the small fish has picked a detail in place of the topic (error A). A child who says "it builds a nest" has said more than the card (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say tap and tape; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: tape. Blend /k/ /a/ /n/, "can". Say cake and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /k/, /a/ (long), /k/. Segment shave: /sh/ /a/ /v/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 15, lesson 77. Review: a_e from day 94, the silent e at the end makes the a say its name, and the syllable chart with closed, open and silent e columns. Add a_e to the sound-spelling map. Show pairs, one at a time: can and cane, mad and made. Say each and tap the sounds; children say which column. Map the new words on cards: bake, make, Kate, plate, the reader's words. Say bake, tap /b/ /a/ /k/, write b, a, k, e: the e is silent. Review: was and of, from day 80 (a says /u/ in was, f says /v/ in of); a, has, is and the are mapped from unit 1.

Now the repair for error I, a picture guess. Show a picture of a cake beside the word card cane. Say: "A child read this as cake." Set the picture aside, face down. Map c, a, n, e, tap /k/ /a/ /n/, blend "cane". Then read "Dad has a cane." and confirm it makes sense. Say the rule: "Letters first. The picture only checks." About 40 seconds a word.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs M m and C c (every letter form was taught by day 27). Decode, then spell, two words: make and bake. Dictate: "Jake can make a cake." Say it whole, then a word at a time; can and a are mapped, and Jake is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Jake, make and cake the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?" A child who writes mak: show tap beside tape and say the e comes at the end.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread on the pairs tap and tape, can and cane, mad and made, about 40 seconds a word. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, read for the first time today after its words are mapped: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Ben's Donut, chain words with letter cards (can, cane, bake, make), sort closed and silent e word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who picked a detail in place of the topic (error A) for day 96's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the topic card and detail cards on scrap paper and read the article from any printed bird page. One child: hear the article, name the topic of one part and a key detail, draw a beak and copy a label, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: read one short part of any bird book aloud. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the first part of the beak article aloud, Strong Beaks for Seeds, twice.
- **10 min. Topic.** **Say.** "What are all the sentences about?" Expect: the sparrow's beak. **Say.** "Say one detail." Expect: it cracks hard seeds.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws the beak and copies one word, such as seeds.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X95a]] [[s03-X95b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points to the topic, then says one detail.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the topic teaching, the exit and the cycle work; cut the pairs on the first part. Longer: a child names the topic and a key detail of a second part, such as the duck's.

### Words and supports

Words: topic, detail, key detail, bill, shape, survive. Frames: "The topic is ___." "A key detail is ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can name the topic of a card and who can say a detail it backs. A child who picked a detail in place of the topic (error A) is named for day 96's five-minute repair. Day 96 locates facts about one beak with a heading, diagram and glossary.
