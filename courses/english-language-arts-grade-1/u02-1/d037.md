# Day 37: The question that changes the tale

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the water rising strip with its face card and ear card, the exit card with its pictures and a copy of Sam's Box for each child. Write the six names for today's exit and rotation group 1. Hang the story map from yesterday. The tale is a story people tell: nobody acts it out, and the water is gentle. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 15 min. Reread the difficult events

Whole class on the rug. Say aloud: "This is a story the Efik and Ibibio people of southern Nigeria tell." Review: you heard pages 1 to 3 yesterday. Read pages 4 and 5 (3 minutes), showing each picture. Then the repeated question, with the strip (8 minutes). Hang the house with its three marks, at the knee, at the head and at the roof. The Water asks, "Is it still safe?" The class says it with you, and a child answers for the Sun, "Yes." Slide the blue strip up to the first mark, and ask the class to say what the Water asks next. Do it three times, one mark each time. Each time say the pattern: the Water asks, the Sun says yes, the water rises. Last (4 minutes), pairs say the pattern to each other, with a finger on the strip.

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

If many children laid the three picture cards of day 36's exit (s02-X36b) in the wrong order (error A), open with four minutes. Read a made-up line: "Duck found a seed. Duck planted the seed. A plant grew." Lay three pictures in the wrong order, and ask a pair to say first, next, last and fix the order. Then the class does the questions in six minutes. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge and give the questions the whole ten minutes.

**Ask.** "What does the Sun say each time?" Expect: yes. **Ask.** "What happens after?" Expect: more water comes in and the water rises. **Ask.** "What does the Sun not notice?" Expect: that the water is getting high. Partners point to the words in the large print.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, with the turn card, one turn each, pass allowed. Say: "Which repeated question changes the tale?" Starter: "The question is ___." Pairs then lay three cards in a chain: an event, its result and a possible lesson. The first card is the event, "Sun said yes." The second is the result, "The water rose." The third is left blank for the lesson. Hear three pairs. Expect: "Is it still safe?" Look-fors: each partner gives a turn and a child adds to the other's idea.

#### 20 min. Lesson, and feeling and sense words

Review: you made a connection arrow with so on days 34 and 35. Model (12 minutes). Lay the arrow card between "Sun said yes" and "the water rose". Say: "The Sun said yes, so the water rose." Lay a second arrow between "the water rose" and a blank card. **Ask.** "What could the Sun have done?" Expect: listened to the warning, or said not yet. Write on the blank card, and read it: "Listen to a warning before you promise." Say: "A lesson is something a tale could teach. This tale could teach that." Each child draws the event that shows it and says "The Sun said yes, so ___." (6 minutes of the twelve).

First look (8 minutes). A feeling word tells how someone feels. Hold up the face card and read page 3: the Moon met the Sun with a broad smile. **Ask.** "How does the Moon feel?" Expect: happy. A sense word tells what you can hear, see or touch. Hold up the ear card and ask what the water might sound like as it comes in. Children say a word, such as splash, and you write it. Say: "You will learn these words well on day 39. Today we only look."

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read aloud: "Badger rushed to stack logs. The logs fell with a crash. Badger felt upset. Next time Badger stacked slowly, and the logs stayed up." Show three pictures: Badger stacking slowly with the logs standing, a pile of fallen logs, and Badger asleep in the sun. Say: "Point to the picture that shows what Badger could learn." Every child points. The rotating six then say which word tells how Badger felt, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s02-X37]]). A good answer points to Badger stacking slowly with the logs standing. The pile of fallen logs, the result with no lesson, is error B. These marks go to day 38.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words: the class says the first sound, the vowel and the last sound in jam, wig, jot and wax; a finger up for each. Blend and segment four sounds: /w/ /a/ /k/ /s/, "wax". Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Decode (3 minutes): jam, jot, wig, wax, mix. Spell on sound boxes (4 minutes): jam, wig and mop. Mapped words (3 minutes): from, wants, says. Review: you have read these since day 34. The class reads each card twice and spells the part to remember aloud.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: each child prints y, z and capitals A to F into the letter-form record (4 minutes) and adds the day. Revise q to x with a finger on the page. A form not yet made is incomplete, never wrong. Dictation (4 minutes): say "Sam has a wig and a pot." Children print a word at a time with the map, then point to the capital and the period (2 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table, matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level: the group reads Sam's Box aloud with expression, one page each, then spells two words, jam and wig, on sound boxes. The other children rotate through stations: reread Sam's Box with a partner; chain jam, mad, mop and map with letter cards; spell box and pot on sound boxes; slide the blue strip and say the repeated question with a partner. No child waits more than one rotation without a station.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks to the unit 2 record sheet and write the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name tomorrow's six.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the house and its three marks on paper and cut a strip of blue paper. One child: hear pages 4 and 5 with a grown-up, move the strip as you say the question, draw the event that shows a lesson, answer the exit by pointing and print y, z and A to F.

### At home

**Swap.** No strip: draw three water lines on a house. No reader: use the cards from class.

**Skip.** The talk and the rotation.

- **10 min. The question.** Tell your child that the Water asked the same question again and again. Ask what the Sun said each time.
  **Say.** "What does the Sun say each time, and what happens after?" Expect: yes, and the water rises.
- **10 min. Lesson.** Your child draws the Sun saying yes and the water rising, and says, "The Sun said yes, so the water rose."
- **10 min. Letters.** Your child prints y, z and A to F, then "Sam has a wig and a pot."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X37]] You read a new card about a mole that dug fast, so the tunnel roof dropped, and next time dug a little at a time, so the roof stayed. You show three pictures. Your child points to what the mole could learn, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reread of the strip, the lesson, the exit and the rotation; cut the feeling and sense look to the face card. Longer: a child who finishes draws a different lesson the tale could show and says its event.

### Words and supports

Words: repeat, question, lesson, feeling word, sense word, so. Frames: "The Sun said yes, so ___." "A lesson is ___." Pictures first; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers; a scribe for labels. The blue strip can be felt. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 38 rereads the tale whole and puts its events in order. If many children picked the pile of fallen logs, a result with no lesson (error B), on today's exit, day 38's retell and lesson part opens with a four-minute repair: connect one action to its result, then say a lesson.
