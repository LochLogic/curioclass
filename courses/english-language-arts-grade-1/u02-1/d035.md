# Day 35: A tale and a fact

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the two-columns sheet, the sentence lines, the two exit pictures and a copy of Sam's Box for each child. Write the six names for today's exit and rotation group 1. Read the tale to yourself first: it is a story people tell, never a lesson in science, and the water is gentle. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read the tale whole

Whole class on the rug. Words first (4 minutes), each with its picture: tale, invite, promise, warn, friend. Say each once; the class says it back. Review: you knew friend and invite from kindergarten. New today: tale, promise and warn. A tale is a story people tell.

Say aloud: "This is a story the Efik and Ibibio people of southern Nigeria tell." Read the retelling whole, showing each picture, with a pause after pages 2, 3, 4 and 5 (about 300 words, 3 minutes of pausing). At each pause ask one thing, and a child answers by pointing or in a few words. Page 2: "Who are the friends?" Expect: the Sun and the Water. Page 3: "What did the Sun promise?" Expect: to build a big yard. Page 4: "What did the Water ask at the gate?" Expect: if it was safe. Page 5: "Where did the Sun and the Moon go at the end?" Expect: up into the sky. Then reread page 4 of *Sky Patterns* (1 minute): "The sky grows dim, so the stars and the moon can shine." Thank the Efik and Ibibio people for the tale.

#### 10 min. Compare, with retrieval first

If many children picked the day 34 strip that changes the order (error A) or the strip that adds rain (error E), open with four minutes: reread page 2 of *Sky Patterns*, point to each part of the pattern, and lay the strips again: the same three, again, in the same order. Then the compare begins. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge.

Retrieval (4 minutes). Review: in kindergarten you told a story from an information book. Hold up two covers, the tale and *Sky Patterns*. Pairs point to the one that gives facts. Hear two pairs.

Compare (6 minutes). Lay the two-columns sheet on the rug: a tale, and an information text. Lay out six page pictures, three from each text, mixed. Children come up one at a time and put a picture in a column, saying "I know because ___." Under the tale write two features: characters who talk and act; a lesson. Under the information text: real facts; the word so joining two facts. Hear four children. Mark nothing here.

#### 10 min. Sentence kinds

Review: in kindergarten you learned the period, the question mark and the exclamation mark. Pip the puppet says four lines, one at a time, and after each the class shows the end mark with a hand gesture. Line one: "The Sun has a small house." A statement tells; the hand makes a dot. Line two: "Where is the Water's house?" A question asks; the hand makes a hook. Line three: "Tell the tale again." A command tells someone what to do; the hand makes a dot. Line four: "What a huge compound!" An exclamation shows a strong feeling; the hand makes a line and a dot. Say each name and the class says it back. Then join two short sentences with and: "Sun came. Moon came." becomes "Sun came and Moon came." Children say it, one at a time with a partner, then say a pair of their own about the room.

#### 20 min. Connect two facts

Review: a connection arrow joins two things with so or because; you made one on day 34. Model on the class sky picture record (6 minutes). Hold the fact card "The sky grows dim." Hold the fact card "The stars and the moon can shine." Lay the arrow card between them with so on it. Say: "The sky grows dim, so the stars and the moon can shine." **Ask.** "What does the arrow join?" Expect: two facts from the page. **Ask.** "Which word joins them?" Expect: so. Say where the facts are found: *Sky Patterns*, page 4.

Pairs (8 minutes). Each pair gets a second pair of facts from page 3: "Light fills the sky." "We can see the trees." Pairs lay the arrow between them, say the sentence with so, then each child draws the two facts with an arrow. A label of one word on each side is enough. Walk and ask, "Which word joins them?"

The heard six explain aloud (6 minutes, one minute each, the rest of the class drawing): "Say the connection and where it is found." Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. A good answer names both facts, says so and gives the page.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread two parts aloud, showing each picture. From the tale, page 5: "The Sun and the Moon climbed up to sit on the roof." From *Sky Patterns*, page 1: "The sky has the sun, the moon and the stars." Show two pictures: the Sun and the Moon on a roof, and children looking at the sky. Say: "Point to the picture that goes with the story words." Every child points. The rotating six then say why, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s02-X35v2]]). A good answer points to the Sun and the Moon on the roof. A child who says why says that in the tale the Sun and the Moon do things, as characters. The other picture is error D. These marks go to day 36.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken pairs: the class says the vowel in each word and holds up a finger for its sound. Pairs: pot and pat, mop and map, box and bus. Blend five sounds: /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/ /s/, "stops"; tap each sound, then say the word. Segment "frogs": /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/ /z/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Decode (3 minutes): pot, jog, box, wet, mix. Review: o was mapped yesterday with j, w and x. Spell on sound boxes (3 minutes): spell dog and mop. Mapped words (2 minutes): from, wants, says. The class reads the three cards twice.

Then read Sam's Box (2 minutes): you read the title to the class; the class reads page 1 with you, a finger under each word. Children read the rest in the rotation.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: each child prints i to p into the letter-form record (4 minutes) and adds the day. Revise day 34's forms a to h with a finger on the page. Contrast b and d (2 minutes): b is a line, then a bump; d is a round, then a line. A form not yet made is incomplete, never wrong. Dictation (4 minutes): say "Sam has a pot." Children print a word at a time with the map, then point to the capital and the period.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table, matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level: the group reads Sam's Box aloud, one page each, then spells two words from it, dog and pot, on sound boxes. The other children rotate through stations: reread Sam's Box with a partner; chain pot, dot, dog and dig with letter cards; spell mop and box on sound boxes; draw a tale picture and a fact picture and label each with one word. No child waits more than one rotation without a station.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the connection marks to the unit 2 record sheet and write the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name tomorrow's six.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the two columns on a folded page and the cards on scrap paper. One child: listen to the tale in two sittings with a grown-up, sort the six pictures, say the connection, answer the exit by pointing and print i to p.

### At home

**Swap.** No sheet: fold a page in two. No reader: use the cards from class.

**Skip.** The sentence kinds and the rotation.

- **10 min. Tale and fact.** Tell your child one thing from the tale, then one fact about the sun. Ask which is a tale.
  **Say.** "Which one is a tale, and how do you know?" Expect: it has a character who talks.
- **10 min. Connect.** Say, "The sky grows dim, so ___." Your child finishes it and draws the two ideas with an arrow.
- **10 min. Letters.** Your child prints i to p, then "Sam has a pot."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X35v2]] You read the tale's line about the Sun and the Moon on the roof (page 5) and the first page of Sky Patterns, and show two pictures. Your child points to the one that goes with the story words, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the tale, the connection, the exit and the rotation; cut the sentence kinds to the four lines. Longer: a child who finishes joins two sentences about the room with and, and draws them.

### Words and supports

Words: tale, invite, promise, warn, friend, so, statement, question, command, exclamation. Frames: "I know because ___." "The sky grows dim, so ___." Pictures first; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers; a scribe for labels. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 36 reads the tale again for who and where and its first events. If many children picked the picture of a fact and not the story words (error D) on today's exit, the opening of day 36's reread and question part goes to a four-minute repair: point to the story words or the fact words before naming the picture.
