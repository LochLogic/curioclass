# Day 88: Talk about what Mina noticed

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 87's exit marks and name today's heard six. If many made error C or error B on day 87's exit, say nothing about it today: day 88 has no repair slot, so name those children for day 89's repair groups. Set out the grid, the three word cards, Pip and the turn cards. Put the sample words in your folder. Print the story pictures of pages 4, 5, 8 and 9.

### The day

#### 10 min. Read Mina at the Window

Say: "This story is made up." Words first, with a picture each, about 25 seconds a word: notice (see something because you look closely), observer (a person who looks closely and tells what they see), perch (rest on a branch). Review: observe and notice, from kindergarten and day 79; perch, from day 84. Then read the whole story aloud (about 450 words, 4 minutes), stopping to show the pictures of pages 4, 5, 8 and 9. Content note: Mina worries a bird is sick and a hawk glides over the yard; the sparrows dart away, and no bird is hurt.

Review: the talk rules and Pip's linked turns, from day 79. Pip says: "The sparrow cracked a seed with its thick beak." Say: "This is made up to show a mistake." A partner says: "I like the red bird." **Ask.** "Does that answer Pip?" Expect: no, it is a liking, not an answer to the speaker (error K). Then the partner says: "You said it cracked a seed. What did it do next?" Say the rule: "Say back what the speaker said, then add or ask." Tell the class the four things you will watch in the groups: wait for the speaker, answer the child before you, ask if something is not clear, answer a question.

#### 30 min. Observed conversation

Six groups of five children, five minutes each, one minute a child, at your table with the story pictures and the grid. The other twenty-five reread the story pictures with a partner, retelling quietly, and draw the bird part they liked best on scrap paper. A child with a fear of birds or an allergy watches from the window route or looks at pictures only.

In each group, one ask at a time, about four minutes:

1. Round one, about 15 seconds a child: "Take your turn about what Mina noticed, and listen to the others." ([[s03-A7]])
2. Round two, 15 seconds a child, each answering the child before: "Answer the child before you, then add one thing." ([[s03-A8]])
3. Round three, 10 seconds to ask and 10 to answer, each child to the next: "If something is not clear, ask a question about it." ([[s03-A9]]) Then the partner answers: "Answer from the story." ([[s03-A10]]) If no question comes, ask the partner's child one yourself about a page they did not mention.

The fifth minute is for the next group to sit down. Mark the grid, one mark in each of four columns: waits for the speaker (A7), links a turn (A8), asks about something not understood (A9), answers a question from the story (A10). Shown means unaided; shown with help means after one prompt; not yet seen otherwise. A child may pass at any turn. An unrelated turn, such as "I like the red bird", is error K.

#### 15 min. Observation and article fact

Pairs retell the story with the story-map for two minutes, both at once: who (Mina), where and when (the window seat, a cold morning), what happens (she notices three things). Review: the story-map, from unit 1. Say: "A story is not research evidence. The story gives me something to notice; the article gives the fact that explains it." Your model, a different pair: Mina saw a thick beak crack a seed, and Beaks Find Food says a thick, strong beak cracks hard seeds. Review: an observation is what we saw and a fact is what the article says, from unit 2.

Hand out the sheet. Say: "Draw what Mina saw the sparrow's toes do on the branch. Then draw the article fact that explains it, from Feet That Help." Children draw for ten minutes. Then they copy two source labels from the board: Mina at the Window under the first drawing and Feet That Help under the second. Review: a source label, from unit 2. Look for: each drawing matches its label, and the article fact comes from Feet That Help. A child who writes the story as the fact has used the story as proof; point to the reminder at the top of the sheet.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet: Lea waited by the window, and a bird took a sip from a puddle and tipped its head up. The others finish their sheets; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures: a bird at a puddle, a bird flying away, a cat. Say: "Point to the picture that goes with a question you could ask a partner about what Lea saw." ([[s03-X88a]]) Then, with the card in view: "A partner asks, 'What did the bird do?' Answer from the card." ([[s03-X88b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the bird flying away, or says it flew away, has given something the card does not say (error B); a child who says "I like birds" has given an unrelated turn (error K).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say not and note; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: note. Blend /g/ /l/ /a/ /d/, "glad". Say drip and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /d/, /i/, /p/. Segment blend: /b/ /l/ /e/ /n/ /d/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 13, lesson 70. Silly words: say that a Martian might use these made-up words, and write one at a time: jibnet, tondip, fenlup, kimsap. Review: a closed syllable, from day 79. For each, a child chops it between the middle consonants, reads each part, then blends them; partners tell each other how they read it, all at once, 20 seconds a word. Then spelling with style: say a word and the class spells it aloud in a whisper voice or a giant voice while a finger writes it on the desk: backpack, hotdog, catfish, hilltop. Clap the two beats first. Keep sand, bed, bug and box cards out of today's stations; the sample words are held. The progress sample is taken in the rotation.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs P p and D d (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate: "Pat has a hotdog." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a and has are mapped. Children say each word slowly, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; hotdog is two parts, hot and dog. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first: the six children the record names. For their first five minutes they map, blend, read, spell and reread; for the last five they take the sample, 50 seconds each. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, one child at a time, 50 seconds each: show the held word card of [[s03-A11]] and say "Read this word." Take the card away and say the dictated word of [[s03-A12]] once in a sentence and once alone: "Write the word I say." Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child who writes only the consonants, as in "sndbx", has left the vowels out (error M). The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Pam's Backpack, chain words with letter cards, build and chop compound words. Use today's results to name tomorrow's catch-up six: this is the same-block regrouping.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the grid marks, the exit marks, the sample marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children with error K and error M for day 89.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw two boxes on scrap paper, and keep the grid as a list of names with four columns. One child: read the story aloud, take Mina's side in a talk, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No story: tell it from the pictures. No window: look at a bird picture. No second person: say both parts yourself, out loud.

**Skip.** The six groups, the sample and the stations.

- **10 min. The story.** Read Mina at the Window aloud. Say notice, observer and perch first.
- **5 min. Talk.** **Say.** "I will say something about Mina. Say it back, then ask me one question." Expect: your child says what you said and asks a question about the story.
- **10 min. Draw.** Your child draws what Mina saw, then the fact from a bird book, with two labels.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X88a]] [[s03-X88b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points, then answers.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the story, the groups, the exit and the sample. Cut the silly words and the retell. Longer: a child draws a second fact from another article and labels its source.

### Words and supports

Words: notice, observer, perch. Frames: "You said ___. What ___?" "Mina noticed ___." "The story says ___. The article says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. A child who is not heard in the group can be heard in a pair.

### Tomorrow

The grid, the exit and the sample tell you who can wait, link a turn, ask and answer, and who can read and spell the held words. An unrelated turn (error K), a thing the card does not say (error B) and a spelling with the vowels left out (error M) are named for day 89, where day 88's misses are returned and retried. Day 89 is a response day, with a new card.
