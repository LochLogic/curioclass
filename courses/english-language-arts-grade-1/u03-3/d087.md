# Day 87: Draw a bird's foot and fix one detail by the words

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 85's exit marks and name today's heard six. Day 86's assessment stays in your folder: its misses wait for day 89's repair groups, so nothing from it is repaired today. Draw the practice menu on the board: Handle and Top, each with a small umbrella. Print the foot sheets and page 2 copies. Put out the eye and ear signs, the sort cards and the exit card face down. Keep the day 88 sample card in your folder.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This is made up for practice." Show the practice menu. The page the Handle choice opens has the heading Holding On and says: "An umbrella has a handle. A hand holds it." Turn the cover, a whole umbrella, face down. Say the repair for error D from day 85's exit, in which the cover was used in place of the menu: "The cover tells me which book, not which page." Review: a menu and its icon, from day 85. The whole class traces the route with a finger in the air as you say it: choice, icon, page, heading, fact. **Ask.** "Which choice leads to the fact about the handle?" Expect: Handle, with its small umbrella. Everyone says: "Choose, follow the icon, find the fact." Nobody is named. If many used the cover on day 85's exit (error D), say it again on a second card: the choices Bell and Seat, each with a small bicycle; the Bell page, heading Ring Ring, says "A bike has a bell. The bell tells people to move."

#### 8 min. Reread Feet That Help, page 2

Review: the eye sign for what the picture shows and the ear sign for what the words tell, from day 80. Read page 2 once, the sparrow, duck and hawk paragraphs, with the diagram and the photograph of the duck's foot in view. Then say four facts, one at a time, and the class shows the eye or the ear: "The toes bend round the branch." (eye, the diagram) "The label lines name the toes and the branch." (eye) "The sparrow can perch and not fall." (ear) "The feet push the water." (ear) About 20 seconds a fact. If many say eye for a words fact, point to the sentence and say it with the class.

#### 10 min. Three questions

Pairs ask and answer these spoken questions, one at a time, two turns each; read each twice. The sparrow diagram and paragraph are in view.

1. "Which words say what the foot does?" Expect: "The toes grip a branch."
2. "What does the picture show that the words do not?" Expect: how the toes bend round the branch, and the label lines.
3. "How do the picture and the words together tell the key idea?" Expect: the picture shows the toes round the branch, the words say they grip, so the sparrow can perch.

Fifteen pairs take two turns of about 20 seconds each; all pairs talk at once. Hear four pairs, one minute each.

#### 22 min. Annotated drawing, then revise

Twelve minutes, drawing. Two minutes: your model, made up to show a change. On the board draw a duck's foot with five separate toes and no skin. Say: "I drew first. Now I check the words." Point to the sentence: "Skin joins the duck's toes." Say: "My drawing has no skin between the toes, and the words say there is skin. I change one detail: I add skin between the toes. I changed the toes because the words say skin joins them." Say the rule: "A change comes from the words, not from a wish." A revision changes one detail because the words say so; it is not a redraw. Then hand out the foot sheet. Say: "Draw one bird foot from the article: a sparrow, a duck or a hawk. Copy two labels from the board: toes, branch, claw or webbed. Then copy the words Feet That Help into the source box and one word from the page, like grip or push." For ten minutes children draw and copy; walk among them. The labels and the source words are copied, not read from sound spellings.

Ten minutes, revise. Partners trade sheets. Each partner points to one sentence on page 2 and says what it says. The drawer looks for one detail in the first drawing that the sentence says differently or leaves out, and draws that detail again in the second box, keeping the first box as it was. Four minutes each way; two minutes to finish. A child who changes a detail the sentence does not mention has changed it from a wish (error C: a picture detail kept where the words give another fact). Point to the sentence together and try again.

#### 10 min. Talk

Partners say, with a finger on the sentence: "I changed ___ because the words say ___." All fifteen pairs say it to each other at once for three minutes. Then hear six pairs, 50 seconds each, five minutes in all; the others finish colouring their second box. Mark the heard pairs: shown, shown with help (one prompt, you point to the sentence), not yet seen. Look for: the change is tied to a sentence pointed to.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once to the class from the card sheet: a goose eats grass with its beak, and a drawing beside the card shows a goose with a fish in its beak. The others finish their sheets; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures: a goose with grass in its beak, a white goose with a fish, a goose with a bigger fish. Say: "Point to the picture the drawing should be changed to." ([[s03-X87a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Which words on the card tell you to fix the drawing?" ([[s03-X87b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the white goose has kept a picture detail where the words give another fact (error C); a child who says geese eat fish gives a claim from outside the card (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say pet and Pete; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: Pete. Blend /s/ /k/ /i/ /n/, "skin". Say grin and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /g/, /i/, /n/. Segment drift: /d/ /r/ /i/ /f/ /t/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 13, lesson 69. Sort it out: show six cards, one at a time, hotdog, rabbit, catfish, napkin, pigpen and picnic. Say each word, clap it and chop it; children say whether it is two words joined (a compound word) or a rabbit word, two parts with a vowel sound in each that are not words. Review: a compound word and a closed syllable, from day 84 and day 79. Put each under its column: compound, rabbit. Then the chain on letter cards: pigpen, hotdog, catfish, bandstand. For each, clap, chop, say each part and write it with a finger on the desk, pig | pen, hot | dog, cat | fish, band | stand. The sound-spelling-map stays up. About two minutes a word. The reader Pam's Backpack is reread in the rotation today.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Children decode, then spell, two words: bandstand and hilltop. Print the pairs A a and C c (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate: "A catfish is in the pond." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a, is, in and the are mapped words, and pond ends in the blend nd. Children stretch each word, print it and read it back. Repair for a missing vowel (error M): tap the sounds again.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first: the six children the record names meet you to map, blend, read, spell and reread. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, with Pam's Backpack: each child reads a page and spells a compound word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time. The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread the reader, chain words with letter cards, build and chop compound words. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the talk marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Keep the foot sheets in a folder for day 90; tonight write one line on each, the detail that was changed and the sentence it came from.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw two boxes on scrap paper, one for the first drawing and one for the changed detail, and read page 2 from the page. One child: draw one foot, check one detail against the words, change it, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: use a picture of a bird's foot from a book. No signs: draw an eye and an ear on paper.

**Skip.** The rotation, the sort and the stations.

- **10 min. Words and picture.** Look at a bird's foot in a book. **Say.** "Which words say what the foot does? What does the picture show that the words do not?" Expect: the words tell the job; the picture shows how the foot looks.
- **10 min. Draw and fix.** Your child draws the foot, copies two labels and says: "I changed ___ because the words say ___."
- **5 min. Words.** Clap and chop bandstand, then print "A catfish is in the pond."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X87a]] [[s03-X87b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points, then says which words.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the model, the drawing, the revision and the exit. Cut the second question and the sort. Longer: a child changes a second detail and points to its sentence.

### Words and supports

Words: label, detail, revise, source. Frames: "I changed ___ because the words say ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. Enlarged foot sheets for any child who wants one.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can set a drawing right by the words. A child who points to the white goose has kept a picture detail (error C); a child who says geese eat fish has given a claim from outside the card (error B). Day 88 has no repair slot, so both wait for day 89's repair groups. Day 88 reads a story about a child at a window and holds the observed conversation.
