## Sky Patterns

[page 1]
Look up. The sky is never just empty. The sky has the sun, the moon and the stars.
*Picture: children of different ages stand at a school window and look up at a wide blue sky with a few clouds.*

[page 2]
The sky has patterns. A pattern is something that happens again and again, in the same order.
*Picture: a row of three small pictures: a bright sun, a dim evening sky, a dark sky with stars, then the same three again.*

[page 3]
Every morning, the sun comes up. The sun rises. Light fills the sky, so we can see the trees and the street and each other.
*Picture: a sun just above a row of houses and a tree, long light across a street, a child in a coat walking to school.*

[page 4]
Every evening, the sun goes down. The sun sets. The sky grows dim, so the stars and the moon can shine.
*Picture: an orange sky, the sun touching the roofs, a child and a grown-up at a window with a lamp turned on behind them.*

[page 5]
The moon has a pattern too. Some nights it is round and bright. Some nights it is a thin curve. Then it grows round again.
*Picture: four small moons in a row: a thin curve, a half, a big round moon, a half again.*

[page 6]
Patterns help us know what comes next. After morning comes the afternoon. After the sun sets, the night comes. What patterns can we find in the sky?
*Picture: a child holding a notebook with pictures of a sun and a moon drawn in it, smiling up.*

## Sun Across the Sky

[page 1]
The sun is a star. It is a very hot ball of light. It is so bright that we must never look right at it. We can see what it does by looking at the sky and the ground around us.
*Picture: a child in a sun hat looks down at long shadows on a playground; the sun is a small yellow circle in a corner of the sky.*

[page 2]
First, the sun rises. In the morning it comes up on one side of the sky. The sun does not move up. Our Earth is turning, and that makes the sun seem to rise.
*Picture: a round blue-green Earth with a small arrow showing it turning, and a morning sun on the edge of the picture.*

[page 3]
Next, the sun climbs. It looks higher and higher. Our shadows grow short, because the sun is high. At the middle of the day, the sun is at its highest.
*Picture: two children stand on a sunny field with short shadows beneath them; a bright sun high above.*

[page 4]
Then, the sun sinks. It looks lower and lower. Our shadows grow long again, because the sun is low.
*Picture: the same children with long shadows stretching across the field; the sun low in a gold sky.*

[page 5]
At last, the sun sets. It goes down on the other side of the sky. The sun sets, so the sky gets dark. Then it is night where we live. On the other side of Earth, the sun is rising and it is morning there.
*Picture: a sunset over hills with a quiet dark blue sky beginning at the top; a small inset picture of the round Earth with one side light and one side dark.*

[page 6]
The next day, the pattern starts again. Rise, climb, sink, set. The sun does this every day, because our Earth keeps turning.
*Picture: four small pictures in a circle: a rising sun, a high sun, a low sun, a setting sun, with arrows between them.*

## The Changing Moon

[page 1]
The moon does two things in the sky. It is easy to mix them up, so let us look at them one at a time.
*Picture: a child looks up at a pale moon in a blue evening sky and holds up two fingers.*

[page 2]
First, the moon moves across the sky, like the sun. It rises on one side and sets on the other. We can see the moon at night, and sometimes in the daytime, too.
*Picture: a pale moon above a row of houses in the afternoon, a child and a dog on the sidewalk below it.*

[page 3]
Second, the moon changes shape. Some nights it is a round circle. Some nights it is a half. Some nights it is a thin curve. Some nights we cannot see it at all.
*Picture: five moons in a row: a thin curve, a half, a full circle, a half the other way, and an empty dark circle.*

[page 4]
The moon does not make its own light. The sun shines on it, so it glows. The moon is like a ball with the sun shining on one half.
*Picture: a bright sun on the left, a gray ball in the middle with its left half glowing, and a child on the right looking toward it.*

[page 5]
The moon travels around Earth. As it goes, we see more of its lit half, or less of it. That is why the moon seems to change shape. It takes about a month to go from a round moon to a thin curve and back to round.
*Picture: a small Earth with a moon drawn at four spots around it, each moon showing a different shape in a small box next to it.*

[page 6]
So the moon has two patterns. It moves across the sky each day. It changes shape over many days. Both patterns come back again and again.
*Picture: a calendar page with small moon drawings in a growing row, beside a picture of a moon rising and setting.*

## Stars in Daylight and Darkness

[page 1]
When the sky is dark, we can see many stars. Some are bright. Some are faint. Some seem to make shapes, like a pot or a bear or a bowl.
*Picture: a dark blue night sky with many stars; a few are joined by faint lines into a simple pot shape; two children look up from a porch with a blanket.*

[page 2]
Stars are huge balls of hot, glowing gas, and they are very, very far away. That is why they look like tiny dots of light.
*Picture: a night sky with dots of light, and a small inset of one star drawn as a big glowing ball.*

[page 3]
Where do the stars go in the day? They do not go anywhere. The stars are still up there.
*Picture: a bright blue daytime sky; faint star shapes drawn in dotted lines to show they are there.*

[page 4]
In the daytime, the sun lights up our sky. The sky is so bright that we cannot see the other stars. At night, the sun's light is on the other side of Earth, so the sky is dark and the stars shine out. Our sun is a star too, and it is the one star we can see in the day.
*Picture: a split picture, a bright day sky on one side with a sun, a dark night sky with stars on the other.*

[page 5]
The stars seem to move across the night sky, a little like the sun and the moon. A group of stars stays in the same shape, but the whole group rises and sets, because our Earth is turning.
*Picture: three small night pictures in a row: stars low on one side, higher in the middle, low on the other, with a small arrow.*

[page 6]
Day and night, the sky goes on with its patterns. Some we see in the daytime. Some we see at night. We can keep a record of both in pictures.
*Picture: a class picture log on a wall: a row of drawings of suns, moons and stars with dates written under them; children point to them.*
