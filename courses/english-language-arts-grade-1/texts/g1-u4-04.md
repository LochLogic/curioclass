# A City Nest: Two Views

- **title:** A City Nest: Two Views
- **text_id:** g1-u4-04
- **author:** Curio Class, adapted from EL Education
- **year:** 2026
- **kind:** informational
- **form:** short informational text with two sides (a debate), six pages
- **word count:** 235 words (text only, not picture notes)
- **use:** paired. The teacher reads it aloud whole on day 132, pausing after each view ("What does this side want?"); children follow the pictures and point to the because clause on each side. Children do not decode it alone; single sentences may be reread chorally from day 133. Both positions are given with their reasons; no one classroom opinion is required.
- **read on days:** 132 (first whole reading), 133 (reread and sort six reason strips), 134 (locate reasons), 136 to 138 (the model's reasons, drafting), 139 (independent paragraph), 140.
- **source and license:** Adapted from EL Education K-5 Language Arts, CC BY 4.0, file G1M4U2L4_ModuleLessons-SupportingMaterials-0719.doc (the sentence strips for "What's Best? The Debate about Pale Male's Nest", grade 1 module 4 unit 2 lesson 4). Newly worded and reordered; the old claim that a nest may be removed when it holds no eggs or young is left out; no pictures reused. License: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

Reasons to sort on day 133 (six strips): Leave it up: (1) a tall building is like a tree, a hawk's own nesting place; (2) the young need the nest as they grow; (3) food is easy to find in the city (rats, mice, pigeons). Take it down: (4) feathers, bones and bits of the nest fall to the sidewalk, which is not safe; (5) the street is crowded with bird watchers; (6) people need room to walk. A seventh idea, that bird watchers love to see the hawks, is a bonus reason for leaving it up. For the 132 to 134 cards: "The nest is brown" is not a reason; "The young need it as they grow" is.

Teacher note, the true history behind the text: a red-tailed hawk called Pale Male, who arrived in Central Park in 1991, nested on a Fifth Avenue building in New York City. In December 2004 the nest was removed, a public campaign followed, and the building added a new nesting cradle; he nested there again for years. He is presumed to have died in 2023. The text says "some years ago" and one hawk so children do not think the nest is there today. No child takes down or touches a nest.

[page 1]
Some years ago in New York City, a red-tailed hawk built a nest on a tall building. A nest on a building was new to many people. They did not agree about what to do. Here are two views.
*Picture: a tall city building with a big stick nest on a high stone ledge, one hawk standing beside it, small windows below.*

[page 2]
View 1: Leave the nest up.
Some people said, "Leave the nest up." Hawks like to nest in trees. A tall building is like a tree, so it is a good place for a home.
These people said, "A hawk needs a nest because the young need it as they grow."
*Picture: a hawk nest on a ledge with two fluffy young hawks inside, a parent hawk watching, a tree drawn beside the building to match.*

[page 3]
Food is easy to find in the city, too. Hawks hunt rats, mice and pigeons. Some bird watchers also wanted the nest to stay. They said, "It is great to see the hawks, because we love to watch birds."
*Picture: a hawk on a ledge looking down at a pigeon in the street, a few people on the sidewalk with binoculars looking up and smiling.*

[page 4]
View 2: Take the nest down.
Other people said, "Take the nest down." Bits of the nest and old feathers and bones fall to the sidewalk. People walk there, so it is not safe.
*Picture: a sidewalk with a few sticks, feathers and a bone on it, a person looking down and stepping around them, the nest high above.*

[page 5]
These people also said the street was too crowded, because so many bird watchers came to see the hawks. They said, "People need room to walk."
*Picture: a crowded sidewalk with many people, some holding binoculars, one stroller squeezed against a wall.*

[page 6]
Both sides care about people and hawks. But they do not want the same thing. One side says stay, because the hawks need a home. The other side says leave, because the sidewalk should be safe and clear. Each side gives reasons to support its point.
*Picture: two plain signs side by side, one with a picture of a nest and a check mark, one with a picture of a sidewalk and a check mark, a hawk above them.*
