# Ben and the Bird: a five-chapter story

- **title:** Ben and the Bird (five chapters: 1 The Bird by the Gate, 2 Staying Back, 3 The Waiting, 4 The Voice on the Phone, 5 The Helper Comes)
- **text_id:** g1-u4-01
- **author:** Curio Class
- **year:** 2026
- **kind:** literary
- **form:** chapter story, read aloud, with page breaks and picture notes
- **word count:** about 1300 words (text only, not picture notes)
- **use:** anchor (the year's long text; read by the teacher, one chapter at a time, children follow the pictures and the class story map)
- **read on days:** 120 (chapter 1), 121 (reread chapter 1), 122 (chapter 2), 124 (chapter 3), 125 (reread, adult-call scenes), 126 (chapter 4), 127 (reread of difficult passages), 128 (chapter 5, whole story map), 129 and 130 and 153 (comparison passages), 156.

Teacher note: read each chapter aloud, slowly, showing the pictures. An injured bird can upset a child. In this story the bird is never hurt in front of us and never handled by Ben; it ends in a trained helper's care, and the text says plainly that children call a grown-up and never pick up a wild bird. Nobody is asked about a yard, a garden or a pet at home; the garden is a shared place in the story. The chapter 1 sentence "Then he stops" is the decision the hook on day 120 pauses at. Chapter 3 holds both meanings of fly, the action and the insect, for day 124. Chapter 1 explains the word trained in its own sentence, for day 121.

## Chapter 1: The Bird by the Gate

[page 1]
Ben likes the green gate at the edge of the garden. On Saturdays his dad works in the garden, and Ben likes to help him dig. Ben's gate is the first thing he runs to.

Today Ben stops at the gate. Something small is on the ground.

It is a bird. It is a robin with an orange chest. It sits very still.
*Picture: a boy of about six with brown skin and a yellow cap stands at a green garden gate, looking down. A small robin sits in the dirt near the gate post. A man with a spade is far behind him, among green rows.*

[page 2]
Ben comes a little closer. The robin does not fly away. One wing hangs low. Its feathers look rumpled.

Ben thinks about what he knows. A bird's wings help it fly. A bird that can fly can find food and get away from danger. This bird cannot fly. This bird needs help.

Ben wants to pick the robin up. His hands want to scoop it up and keep it safe.

Then he stops.

"A wild bird is not mine to pick up," Ben thinks. "I could hurt it. I need a grown-up."

Ben sees a hurt bird by the gate. He calls his dad.

"Dad! Come quick! There is a bird, and it is hurt!"
*Picture: Ben stands with his hands behind his back, away from the bird. His mouth is open as he shouts toward the garden. In the background his dad looks up from his spade.*

[page 3]
Dad comes to the gate. He does not run, and he does not reach for the bird. He looks from a few steps away.

"You did the right thing, Ben," Dad says. "You did not touch it, and you called me."

"Can we fix its wing?" Ben asks.

Dad shakes his head. "I do not know how to fix a bird's wing. Neither do you. We need a trained helper. A trained helper has learned how to care for hurt wild birds. That is her job."

"Where do we find a trained helper?" Ben asks.

"I think the wildlife center can help," says Dad. "Let me see."
*Picture: Dad, tall and kind, crouches a few steps from the robin. Ben stands beside him. Dad holds up one hand as if to say wait.*

## Chapter 2: Staying Back

[page 4]
Dad takes out his phone. Ben listens.

"Hello," says Dad. "We found a robin in the garden. It cannot fly. One wing hangs down. We have not touched it."

Dad listens for a long time. He says, "Yes. Yes. I see." Then he says, "Thank you. We will."

Dad puts his phone away.
*Picture: Dad holds a phone to his ear with a serious, listening face. Ben holds Dad's sleeve. The robin sits still near the gate post.*

[page 5]
"The woman on the phone is a bird helper," Dad says. "She told me what to do while we wait."

"What do we do?" Ben asks.

"We stay back," Dad says. "A hurt bird is already scared. If we come close, it may use up its strength trying to get away. So we give it space."

Ben nods. "What else?"

"We keep it quiet. We keep people and dogs away. And we do not give it food or water, because the wrong food can make a hurt bird sicker."

Ben looks at the robin. He feels a little sad. He wanted to give it something.

"Staying back is a way to help too," Dad says.
*Picture: Dad and Ben stand together, a good way back from the robin. Ben looks sad but listening.*

[page 6]
Just then a girl walks by the gate with a big brown dog on a leash. The dog sniffs at the air. It pulls toward the robin.

Ben knows what to do. He steps in front of the dog's path and speaks in a quiet voice.

"Please keep your dog back," he says. "There is a hurt bird by the gate. A helper is coming."

The girl pulls her dog away. "Oh! Thanks for telling me," she says. "I will walk the other way."

Ben feels proud. Staying back is hard, but it is work for a helper.
*Picture: a girl with long braids and a big brown dog turns away from the gate. Ben stands with one hand held up. The robin is a small shape behind him.*

## Chapter 3: The Waiting

[page 7]
Now there is nothing to do but wait.

Ben and Dad sit on an old bench a long way from the robin. The sun is warm. The garden is quiet.

Ben feels worried, and he feels restless. His feet will not stay still. He wants the helper to come now.

"How long will it be?" he asks.

"I do not know," Dad says. "She is coming as soon as she can. Waiting is hard, Ben. But it is part of helping too."
*Picture: Ben and Dad sit side by side on a wooden bench in the sun, far from the gate. Ben swings his feet. The robin is tiny in the distance.*

[page 8]
A fly buzzes past the bench. It lands on the gate post, close to the robin. The robin does not even look at it.

Ben watches. A hurt bird does not chase a fly.

Another bird sings in a tree. It lifts its wings and begins to fly. It sweeps across the garden and lands on a high branch.

Ben looks back at the robin. "I wish it could fly like that," he says quietly.

"Maybe it will," Dad says. "That is what the helper is for."
*Picture: a small fly sits on the gate post. Beyond it the robin sits still. High in a tree another bird spreads its wings.*

[page 9]
Ben draws the robin with a stick in the dirt. He draws a round body, an orange chest and one low wing.

He thinks about the robin's wing. He thinks about the helper's hands. He thinks about the gate.

Ben wanted to pick up the bird. Now he is glad he did not. He is waiting, and the bird is safe, and soon someone who knows how will come.

The robin blinks. It is still there.
*Picture: Ben crouches near the bench and draws in the dirt with a stick. Dad watches him. Far away, the robin sits by the green gate.*

## Chapter 4: The Voice on the Phone

[page 10]
Dad's phone rings. Dad puts it on speaker so Ben can hear.

"Hello, this is Ms. Torres from the wildlife center," says a warm voice. "I am the trained helper who will come for your robin. I am on my way."

"What is your job?" Ben asks.

"My job is to care for hurt wild birds," Ms. Torres says. "I check the wing. I find out what is wrong. I give the right food and a quiet, safe place to rest. I have learned how to do it, so I do it every day."
*Picture: Dad holds his phone flat between himself and Ben. Both lean in to listen. The gate and the robin are in the background.*

[page 11]
"Is the robin going to be all right?" Ben asks.

"I cannot promise," Ms. Torres says. "But the bird has a better chance because you called. A hurt wild bird needs a trained helper. You and your dad did exactly what helps most. You stayed back, and you made the call."

Ben feels warm inside. He did not fix the wing. But he did something that mattered.

"We will wait here," Ben says.

"Good," says Ms. Torres. "Stay back and keep it quiet. I will see you soon."
*Picture: Ben, smiling a little for the first time, looks at the phone. Dad gives him a thumbs up.*

## Chapter 5: The Helper Comes

[page 12]
Ben waits away from the bird. A trained helper arrives.

A small white van stops by the garden. A woman steps out with a soft towel and a cardboard carrier box. She has a name tag. It says Ms. Torres.

She waves to Ben and Dad. Then she walks slowly toward the green gate. She does not hurry. She does not make any loud sounds.
*Picture: a white van parked by the garden. Ms. Torres, a woman with dark hair in a ponytail, carries a towel and a carrier box. Ben and Dad watch from the bench.*

[page 13]
Ben watches from far away. He holds his breath.

Ms. Torres kneels near the robin. She moves slowly. She lays the towel gently over the bird, and she lifts it with both hands, the way a trained helper knows how.

The robin does not flap. Ms. Torres sets it in the carrier box and closes the lid.

"There," she says quietly. "It is safe."
*Picture: Ms. Torres kneels near the gate post, holding the towel-wrapped robin close, her head bent over it. Ben watches from the bench, hands over his mouth.*

[page 14]
Ms. Torres walks over to the bench, carrying the box with care.

"Its wing is hurt," she says. "But it is awake and alert. I will take it to the wildlife center, where it can rest and be looked after. I will call you and tell you how it does."

"Thank you," says Ben.

"Thank you," says Ms. Torres. "You called a grown-up, and you stayed back. That was the best way to help a bird that needs it."

Ben feels glad all the way down to his toes.
*Picture: Ms. Torres stands by the bench holding the carrier box in front of Ben and Dad. Ben smiles up at her.*

[page 15]
The white van drives away. The gate is empty, and the garden is quiet.

Ben looks at the spot by the green gate where the robin sat. He thinks about his wish. Today he did not pick up a wild bird. Instead, he did something better. He called for help, and help came.

"If I find a hurt bird again," Ben says, "I will call a grown-up. And the grown-up will call a trained helper."

"That is right," says Dad.

Ben picks up his shovel. The robin is in careful hands now. The garden is waiting, and Ben is ready to dig.
*Picture: Ben and Dad walk back toward the garden rows with a shovel and a spade. The green gate is empty behind them. In the sky a small bird flies far away.*
