Teacher note. Read aloud by the teacher, each article whole at its first reading. Each article has one picture. For days 1 to 3 and 8 the picture is held back or shown on purpose: what the picture shows and what only the words say is listed under each article. The articles name only teacher-shown tools; no child handles a blade. Each article gives one tool whose design has a reason (the hammer's claw, the hoe's long handle, the ladle's long handle), which day 25 uses.

## Article 1: Builders at Work

A builder puts up the walls of a house. A builder uses tools to do the job. Tools help a builder work.

A builder uses a hammer. The flat end of a hammer pushes a nail into wood. The other end has a claw. The claw grabs a bent nail and pulls it out. So one hammer does two jobs.

A builder uses a level. A level has a little bubble inside a tube of liquid. When the bubble sits in the middle, the board is flat. If the bubble slides to one side, the builder must fix the board.

A builder uses a wheelbarrow. It has one wheel and two handles. The wheel holds up part of the weight. A builder does not have to carry the whole load. So she can move bricks and sand across the building site.

Picture: Three builders in yellow hard hats stand by half-built walls of a house. One kneels with a hammer. One carries a long yellow level. One pushes a red wheelbarrow full of bricks. A blue sky with one crane behind them.

Only in the picture: the hard hats, the yellow and red colors, the crane. Only in the words: what the claw does, what the bubble tells, how the wheel helps, and the job (building walls).

## Article 2: Tools on the Farm

A farmer grows food. A farmer uses tools to help plants grow.

A farmer uses a hoe. A hoe has a long handle and a flat head. The farmer pulls the hoe through the dirt to cut weeds and to loosen the soil. The long handle lets the farmer stand up tall. So the farmer does not have to bend over all day.

A farmer uses a watering can. The spout has a cap with tiny holes. The cap is called a rose. The rose breaks the water into fine drops, like gentle rain. So small plants are not hurt.

A farmer uses a tractor. A tractor has two very big wheels at the back. It pulls heavy machines across the field. A farmer could not pull them alone.

Picture: A farmer in a straw hat stands in a field of green rows with a hoe. A red tractor sits at the edge. A brown dog lies in the shade. A watering can with a long spout rests on the ground.

Only in the picture: the straw hat, the red tractor, the dog, the green rows. Only in the words: what the hoe does to weeds and soil, why the handle is long, why the rose has holes, what the tractor pulls.

## Article 3: Tools in the Kitchen

A cook makes meals. A cook uses tools to get food ready.

A ladle is a kitchen tool for soup. It has a long handle and a deep bowl. The cook dips the ladle into a big pot and lifts soup into a bowl. The long handle keeps the cook's hand away from the hot pot.

A rolling pin is a kitchen tool for dough. It is round like a tube, with a handle at each end. The cook rolls it over dough made of flour. The dough gets flat and thin.

A funnel is a kitchen tool for pouring. It is wide at the top and narrow at the bottom. The cook sets the narrow end in a small bottle and pours oil into the wide top. The oil goes into the bottle, not onto the table.

A ladle, a rolling pin and a funnel are all kitchen tools. Each one helps a cook do a job.

Picture: A cook in a white apron stands at a counter. A ladle rests in a steaming pot. A wooden rolling pin lies on flat dough dusted with flour. A funnel sits in the neck of a bottle of oil. A yellow window shows a garden behind.

Only in the picture: the white apron, the steam, the yellow window, the dust of flour on the counter. Only in the words: what each tool does, why the ladle has a long handle, that the funnel keeps oil off the table.
