# Poems about the Sky

- **title:** Poems about the Sky (Quiet Sky, Sun's Day, Moon's Walk, and a prose version of Moon's Walk)
- **text_id:** g1-u2-02
- **author:** Curio Class
- **year:** 2026
- **kind:** literary
- **form:** poems (three) and one short prose rendering
- **word count:** about 358 words (text only, not picture notes)
- **use:** paired (read aloud by the teacher, then reread chorally; children do not decode these alone)
- **read on days:** Quiet Sky 43, 44; Sun's Day 63, 68, 69, 70, 71 (model for the sun poem); Moon's Walk 64, 65 (with the prose version), 66, 67; all three reread 72 to 75.

Teacher note: the speakers (the sun, the moon) are imagined. The facts under them match g1-u2-01 (the sun rises, climbs, sinks and sets; the moon travels and changes shape). Children are asked to tell what is invented and what is a sky pattern. Night is gentle throughout.

## Quiet Sky

[page 1]
The sun has gone to sleep.
The sky is soft and blue.
The first small star blinks on,
as bright as morning dew.
*Picture: a dusk sky going from orange to deep blue, one small star above a rooftop.*

[page 2]
The air is cool and still.
I hear a cricket sing.
The moon peeks over the hill,
a pale and gentle ring.
*Picture: a hill with a pale moon rising, a child on a porch step, a cricket drawn on a fence post.*

[page 3]
I feel my blanket, warm.
I look up, and I stay.
The quiet sky is kind.
It keeps the night away,
and keeps the morning on its way.
*Picture: a child in a blanket on a porch, looking up at stars, a grown-up sitting nearby.*

## Sun's Day

[page 1]
I wake up in the east.
I stretch my golden arms.
First, I climb above the hill,
and warm the sleepy farms.
*Picture: a smiling sun with soft rays rising over a hill, a small farm and a rooster below.*

[page 2]
Next, I am high and bright.
I see the children play.
Their shadows hide beneath their feet,
tiny, at midday.
*Picture: children jumping in a sunny yard with very short shadows.*

[page 3]
Then, I slide down the sky.
The shadows stretch out long.
The birds fly home to rest,
and I hum a quiet song.
*Picture: long shadows of children and trees across a lawn, birds flying toward a tree, a low gold sun.*

[page 4]
At last, I sink out of sight.
The sky turns pink, then deep.
Good night! I tell the world.
I will come back after sleep.
*Picture: a sunset behind a hill, pink clouds, a small yellow wave of the sun's hand, lights on in the windows of a house.*

## Moon's Walk

[page 1]
I am the moon, and I walk.
I step across the dark.
First, I hang above the roofs
and watch a dog out in the yard.
*Picture: a round moon above a row of roofs, a dog in a fenced yard looking up.*

[page 2]
Next, I light a silver road
on the quiet lake below.
A little boat rocks, rocks, rocks,
and I glow, glow, glow.
*Picture: a moon shining a path of light across a lake, a small rowboat with a lantern.*

[page 3]
Then, I climb to the top of the sky.
The owls hoot, soft and slow.
I am a thin curve some nights,
and some nights a round O.
*Picture: an owl on a branch under a high moon, two small moons in the corner: a thin curve and a full circle.*

[page 4]
At last, the morning comes.
I fade to pale, and smile.
The sun comes up to say good day.
I will walk again in a while.
*Picture: a pale moon in a light morning sky as the sun rises behind a hill.*

## Moon's Walk, in sentences (prose version, read with Moon's Walk on day 65)

[page 1]
I am the moon, and I walk across the sky at night.

First, I hang above the roofs and watch a dog in a yard. Next, I make a silver road on the lake, and a little boat rocks on it. Then, I climb to the top of the sky, and the owls hoot, soft and slow. Some nights I am a thin curve, and some nights I am round. At last, the morning comes. I fade to pale, and the sun comes up to say good day.

I will walk again soon.
*Picture: a moon above a lake, a dog, a boat and an owl in four small pictures, in order.*
