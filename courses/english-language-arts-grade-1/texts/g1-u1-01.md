Teacher note. Read aloud by the teacher, whole, on day 10. Kofi tells the whole story, so the narrator is always Kofi; Gran speaks only inside quotation marks, and Kofi also speaks inside quotation marks (pages 2, 7, 11 and 12). Pages 4 to 6 hold the first attempts (hands, then the wrong screwdriver); pages 8 to 10 hold the later attempt (the big screwdriver, then the right one). Pages 5, 6 and 7 hold the work Gran does; pages 9 to 11 hold the work Kofi does: that is how the work changes. Every picture holds some detail the words do not say, and the words hold some the pictures do not show (for days 11 and 14). Children read parts of it only as supported shared reading; the story is not a decodable text.

## Text

[page 1]
I am Kofi. On Saturday I go to Gran's house. Gran is my grandparent. Behind her house is a shed full of tools.
Picture: A boy of about six in a green raincoat walks up a garden path toward a small gray shed. A woman with short gray curls and a blue apron waves from the open door. Puddles shine on the path.

[page 2]
Today I bring my bicycle. It is red, and it used to be my cousin's. Rain taps on the shed roof, so Gran and I will work inside.
"Let's see what your bicycle needs," says Gran.
Picture: Inside the shed, a red bicycle leans against a workbench. Rain streaks the little window. Tools hang on the wall in neat rows.

[page 3]
I already know what it needs. The seat is loose. It wobbles when I sit on it. The bell will not ring. It only goes thunk. And the basket strap hangs down like a tired noodle.
Picture: Kofi sits on the bicycle with his arms out for balance; the seat tips to one side. A wicker basket hangs crooked on the front. A small silver bell sits on the handlebar.

[page 4]
Gran lifts the cover off her old toolbox. "We will fix the seat first," she says.
First I try with my hands. I twist the seat. I push the seat. It is still loose.
"Hands are not enough," I say.
Picture: Gran holds the open lid of a dented red toolbox. Kofi grips the bicycle seat with both hands and scrunches up his face.

[page 5]
I look in the toolbox. I pick a screwdriver with a yellow handle. I put its tip on the bolt behind the seat. I turn and turn. The tip slips off with a scrape. The bolt does not move. The seat is still loose.
Picture: Kofi holds a long yellow screwdriver against a bolt under the bicycle seat. His tongue sticks out as he tries. Gran stands close with her hands on her hips, watching.

[page 6]
"A screwdriver turns a screw," says Gran. "That is a bolt. A bolt needs a wrench."
Gran picks a wrench. Its mouth fits the bolt just right. She turns it. Click, click, click. The bolt is tight.
Picture: Gran fits a silver wrench onto the bolt. Kofi leans in close to see. A tiny gray cat watches from the top of a shelf.

[page 7]
I sit on the seat. It does not wobble. It is still!
"Now I know," I say. "I look at the part first. Then I pick the tool."
"Good thinking," says Gran.
Picture: Kofi sits straight and proud on the bicycle. Gran gives him a thumbs up. The seat sits level.

[page 8]
Now the bell. I press the little lever. Thunk. The bell has slipped around the bar. A small screw holds it, and the screw is loose.
"This time you do the work," says Gran. "I will watch."
Picture: Kofi presses the lever on the bell and frowns. The bell has turned to face the floor. Gran sits on a stool with her hands folded in her lap.

[page 9]
I try the big screwdriver first. Its tip is too wide for the little screw. It will not fit.
"Try again," says Gran. "Look at the screw first."
Picture: Kofi holds the big yellow screwdriver beside a tiny screw. The tip is far wider than the screw head. Gran points at the screw.

[page 10]
I look at the screw. It is small. I find a little screwdriver with a thin tip. It fits! I turn it, and the screw gets tight. I press the lever.
Ring, ring! The bell works.
Picture: Kofi holds a thin screwdriver against the bell's small screw. His smile is wide. Little lines of sound curl out from the bell.

[page 11]
Last is the basket strap. I loop the strap around the handlebar and pull it tight. I do it all by myself. The basket does not hang down. It sits up straight.
"Look, Gran!" I say.
Picture: Kofi pulls a brown leather strap tight around the handlebar. The basket sits straight and holds a small yellow flower in a jar.

[page 12]
The rain stops. I buckle my helmet. Gran and I look at all we did.
At first Gran turned the wrench and I watched. Later I turned the screwdriver and Gran watched. The work changed.
I ring the bell again. Ring, ring! "Again!" says Gran, and she laughs.
Picture: In the wet garden, Kofi sits on the red bicycle with a blue helmet on. Gran stands beside it holding the wrench. A rainbow stretches over the shed roof. The tiny gray cat sits in the doorway.
