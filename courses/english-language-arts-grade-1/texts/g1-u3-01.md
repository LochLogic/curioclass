# Beaks Find Food
(read whole on day 79; located in on days 81, 95 to 98)

[page 1]
Contents
Strong Beaks for Seeds, page 2
Flat Beaks for Mud, page 2
Long Beaks for Fish, page 2
Hooked Beaks for Meat, page 2
Glossary, page 3

A bird needs food to survive. A bird gets food with its beak. A beak is also called a bill. Birds have beaks of many shapes. Each shape helps a bird get its own kind of food.
*Picture: four birds in a row, a sparrow, a duck, a heron and a hawk, each in its own picture frame, each showing its head close up. No labels.*

[page 2]
Strong Beaks for Seeds
A sparrow has a thick, strong beak. The beak cracks hard seeds. Then the sparrow eats the food inside.

Flat Beaks for Mud
A duck has a flat beak. The duck dips its beak in the pond mud. The beak strains the mud and water out. Bits of food stay in the beak. The duck eats them.

Long Beaks for Fish
A heron has a long beak. The heron stands still in shallow water. A fish swims near. The heron strikes fast. It catches the fish with its beak.

Hooked Beaks for Meat
A hawk has a hooked beak. The hooked beak tears meat into small bits. Then the hawk can eat.

Beaks are different. Each beak helps its bird get food.
*Diagram: a sparrow's head, side view, with a label line to the beak: "beak". Photograph beside it of a sparrow on a fence cracking a seed.*

[page 3]
Glossary
beak: the hard part of a bird's mouth. A beak is also called a bill.
crack: to break open.
strain: to let water drain out and keep the food in.
hooked: curved like a hook.

# Wings in Motion
(first reading, day 82)

[page 1]
Contents
Wings Push the Air, page 2
Round Wings Flap, page 2
Wide Wings Glide, page 2
Fast Hummingbird Wings, page 2
Glossary, page 3

Most birds have wings. Wings help most birds fly. A bird's wings are covered with feathers.
*Picture: a flock of small birds taking off from a field, with one large bird gliding high above them.*

[page 2]
Wings Push the Air
A bird flaps its wings. The wings push the air. This helps lift the bird up. The bird flies.

Round Wings Flap
A sparrow has round wings. The wings flap. Flapping moves the sparrow through the air.

Wide Wings Glide
An eagle has big, wide wings. It can glide on air that rises. It does not flap all the time. It sails high in the sky.

Fast Hummingbird Wings
A hummingbird's wings beat very fast. A hummingbird can hover. It can stay in one place in the air.
*Diagram: a sparrow in flight, seen from above, with label lines to "wing" and "feathers".*

[page 3]
Glossary
wing: the part of a bird that moves to push air.
glide: to move through the air without flapping.
hover: to stay in one place in the air.
rise: to go up.

# Feathers Protect
(first reading, day 83; read closely day 91)

[page 1]
Contents
Feathers for Flight, page 2
Down for Warmth, page 2
Fluffing Up, page 2
Feathers for Water, page 2
Glossary, page 3

Only birds have feathers. Feathers cover a bird's body. Feathers protect a bird in cold, wind and rain.
*Picture: a robin on a snowy branch, its feathers lightly fluffed.*

[page 2]
Feathers for Flight
Wing feathers help a bird fly. They are light, but they are strong.

Down for Warmth
Soft down feathers lie close to the bird's body. Down traps air. The trapped air helps keep the bird warm.

Fluffing Up
On a cold day, a bird can fluff up its feathers. It looks round, like a ball. The fluffed feathers help it stay warm. On a hot day, a bird can smooth its feathers down. This helps it cool off.

Feathers for Water
A duck has feathers that keep water out. The duck stays dry in the pond.
*Diagram: a bird's body in cutaway, with label lines to "wing feathers" and "down". Photograph: a fluffed-up sparrow on a branch on a cold morning.*

[page 3]
Glossary
feather: one of the light, flat parts that cover a bird.
down: soft feathers that lie close to a bird's body.
trap: to hold in.
protect: to keep safe.

# Feet That Help
(first reading, day 84; menu route, day 85)

[page 1]
Contents
Feet for Perching, page 2
Feet for Swimming, page 2
Feet for Grasping, page 2
Glossary, page 3

Birds do many things with their feet. Different feet help birds do different jobs.
*Picture: three bird feet in a row on a plain background, a sparrow's feet on a twig, a duck's feet on water, a hawk's feet on a post.*

[page 2]
Feet for Perching
A sparrow has four toes on each foot. Three toes point forward. One toe points back. The toes grip a branch. The sparrow can perch and not fall.

Feet for Swimming
A duck has webbed feet. Skin joins the duck's toes. The feet push the water. The duck swims.

Feet for Grasping
A hawk has sharp claws. The claws grasp food and hold it. Strong feet help the hawk eat.
*Diagram: a sparrow's foot gripping a branch, with label lines to "toes" and "branch". Photograph: a duck's webbed foot seen from below.*

[page 3]
Glossary
perch: to rest on a branch.
webbed: joined with skin.
claw: a sharp, curved nail at the end of a toe.
grasp: to take hold and grip.

# Duck at the Pond
(first reading, day 92; second source for beaks, feathers and feet)

[page 1]
Contents
Where a Duck Lives, page 2
Beak, page 2
Feathers, page 2
Feet, page 2
Glossary, page 3

Here is a mallard duck. A mallard lives near ponds, lakes and slow rivers.
*Picture: a mallard duck swimming on a pond, reeds along the edge, a child and an adult watching from the shore at a safe distance.*

[page 2]
Where a Duck Lives
A duck spends its days on and near the water.

Beak
A duck has a flat beak. It dips its beak in the water and the mud. Tiny ridges along the inside of the beak work like a comb. They strain the mud and water out. Bits of food stay in. The duck eats them.

Feathers
Down feathers lie close to the duck's body. Down helps keep the duck warm. The outer feathers keep water out. The duck stays dry.

Feet
A duck has webbed feet. Webbed feet push the water. A duck swims well.
*Diagram: a mallard from the side with label lines to "beak", "feathers" and "webbed foot".*

[page 3]
Glossary
mallard: a kind of duck.
pond: a small pool of water.
ridge: a thin raised line.
dry: not wet.

# Heron by the Water
(first reading, day 97; second source for beaks)

[page 1]
Contents
Where a Heron Lives, page 2
Long Beak, page 2
Long Toes, page 2
Waiting, page 2
Glossary, page 3

Here is a great blue heron. It is a big gray bird. It lives near marshes and shores.
*Picture: a great blue heron standing in shallow water at the edge of a marsh at dawn.*

[page 2]
Where a Heron Lives
A heron lives near shallow water. It lives where fish swim.

Long Beak
A heron has a long, pointed beak. The beak works like a spear. The heron uses it to catch fish.

Long Toes
Birds that wade have long toes. Long toes spread out a bird's weight. A heron can stand on soft ground and not sink.

Waiting
A heron stands very still. It waits for a fish to swim close. Then it strikes with its beak. The fish is caught.
*Diagram: a heron from the side with label lines to "long beak" and "long toes".*

[page 3]
Glossary
heron: a tall bird that catches fish in shallow water.
marsh: soft, wet ground with grass and reeds.
wade: to walk in shallow water.
spear: a long pole with a sharp point, used for catching fish.
