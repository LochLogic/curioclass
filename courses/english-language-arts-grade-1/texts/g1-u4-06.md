## Volunteers Build Bluebird Boxes

[page 1]
Some birds nest in holes. A hole in a tree is called a cavity. Bluebirds, tree swallows and wrens all use cavities. When there are not enough holes, people can build nest boxes for them.

One spring, a group of young volunteers built twenty nest boxes. The group is a youth club called the Deer Creek Defenders. They built the boxes at the Green Bank Observatory in Green Bank, West Virginia.
*Picture: a long table outdoors with many small wooden boxes, each with a round hole. Children of different ages and a few adults stand around it.*

[page 2]
Before the children came, the Observatory staff got ready. They cut the wood into pieces. They marked each piece with a label. They drilled the holes where the nails and screws would go. This made the building easier, so the children could put the boxes together.
*Picture: a stack of cut boards with labels on them, and a drill on a bench. Labels: pieces, labels.*

[page 3]
Then the children built. Each child worked on a box. They fitted the pieces together, one by one, just as the labels showed.

A man named Dave Curry, who used to work at the Observatory, helped design the boxes. He added small steps cut into the inside wall. The steps are called kerfs. Baby birds can use them to climb up to the hole.
*Picture: close-up of an inside wall of a box with small grooves cut into it, and a child's hand pointing at them. Label: steps for baby birds.*

[page 4]
Before they finished, the volunteers did something kind. On every box they wrote a welcome message, or they signed their name. Each box said hello to the birds that would live in it.

When the work was done, everyone ate lunch together. The Observatory cooked hot dogs for the group.
*Picture: children writing with markers on the sides of finished boxes. Labels: welcome, a name.*

[page 5]
Some of the new boxes will replace older boxes. Bluebirds and other cavity birds need places to nest. Boxes made by people have helped bluebird numbers recover.

So the volunteers built twenty nest boxes. They gave the birds new places to raise their young.
*Picture: a row of twenty finished boxes on the grass in the sun, ready to be carried away. A bluebird perches on the end of the table.*
