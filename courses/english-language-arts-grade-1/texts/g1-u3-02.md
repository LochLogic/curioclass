# Mina at the Window

- **title:** Mina at the Window
- **text_id:** g1-u3-02
- **author:** Curio Class
- **year:** 2026
- **kind:** literary
- **form:** picture-book story, 11 pages
- **word count:** about 446 words (text only, not picture notes)
- **use:** paired (read aloud by the teacher; children join in, retell with the story map and do not decode it alone)
- **read on days:** 88 (whole, with the science talk); reread 108, 119.

Teacher note: Mina watches from inside, by her own choice, and never touches a bird or a nest. She notices, wonders, draws, tests a guess and decides to check it; her feelings show in what she does (the pencil stops, she bites her lip, she grins, she lets out a breath). The hawk glides over and leaves; no bird is hurt. Facts a child can pair with the articles: toes grip (Feet That Help), thick beak cracks seeds (Beaks Find Food), fluffed feathers keep a bird warm (Feathers Protect), short round wings start fast and a hawk glides on wide wings (Wings in Motion).

[page 1]
Mina had a favorite seat. It was the window seat, with a soft cushion and a good view of the big tree.
*Picture: Mina, a girl with brown skin and a yellow sweater, sits on a cushion at a wide window. A bare tree fills the glass.*

[page 2]
Every morning Mina opened her notebook. She was an observer. That meant she watched closely and drew what she saw.
"Today I will notice three things," she said. She wrote the number 3 at the top of the page.
*Picture: Mina's notebook open on her knees, a pencil in her hand, the number 3 at the top.*

[page 3]
It was a cold morning. Frost made white lines on the glass. A little brown sparrow landed on a branch.
*Picture: the window from inside, frost in the corners, a sparrow on a thin branch.*

[page 4]
The sparrow held on tight. Its toes curled around the branch. The branch swayed, and the sparrow did not fall.
Mina drew the toes. "Good grip," she wrote.
*Picture: Mina's drawing of a bird's foot around a twig, with her pencil beside it.*

[page 5]
Then the sparrow puffed up. Its feathers stood out until it looked like a round brown ball.
Mina's pencil stopped. She bit her lip. "Is it sick?" she whispered.
Her shoulders dropped. She pressed one hand on the cool glass.
*Picture: Mina's face close to the glass, worried, her hand flat against it. The round sparrow is a blur beyond.*

[page 6]
Mina did not look away. She remained at the window, very still, and she watched.
The sparrow did not fall. It did not hide. It hopped along the branch, still puffed up.
"That is strange," Mina said. "It does not act sick."
She wrote a wonder: Why does it look so round?
*Picture: Mina writing, her eyebrows up; the round sparrow hops along the branch.*

[page 7]
Under the tree hung a little seed tray. Mina and Dad filled it every week.
The puffed-up sparrow hopped to the tray. It picked up a seed. Its thick beak went crack. It ate the seed inside.
Mina grinned. She drew the thick beak.
*Picture: the seed tray hanging from the tree, the sparrow on its edge with a seed, Mina smiling in the window above.*

[page 8]
Then a shadow slid across the yard.
Mina's heart thumped. A hawk, with wide wings spread, glided high over the tree.
The sparrows shot into the bushes. Whir! Their round wings were quick.
The hawk circled once and sailed away. The yard was still.
Mina let out a long breath. "They got away," she whispered.
*Picture: a hawk gliding over a backyard, small sparrows darting into a bush, Mina's wide eyes at the window.*

[page 9]
Soon the sun came over the roof. Warm light fell on the tree.
The round sparrow shook itself. Its feathers lay smooth again. Now it looked small and neat.
"It was cold," Mina said slowly. "I think it fluffed up to stay warm."
*Picture: sun on the tree, the sparrow smooth and small on its branch, frost melting on the glass.*

[page 10]
Mina closed her notebook and carried it to the kitchen table, where Dad was reading.
"I noticed three things," she said. "Toes that grip. A beak that cracks seeds. A bird that puffed up."
"And what do you wonder?" asked Dad.
"I wonder if I am right about the puffed-up bird," said Mina. "I think it kept warm. Now I want to check."
*Picture: Mina and her dad at a kitchen table, the notebook open between them, a mug of tea steaming.*

[page 11]
That night Mina wrote at the top of a new page: Tomorrow, check my idea.
She felt proud and a little bit eager. She looked out the window one more time. The tree stood quiet in the dark.
*Picture: Mina in pajamas at the window, the notebook under her arm, a few stars above the tree.*
