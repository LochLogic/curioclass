# The Weaver Girl and the Cowherd

- **title:** The Weaver Girl and the Cowherd (a Chinese tale, retold by Curio Class)
- **text_id:** g1-u2-04
- **author:** Curio Class (retelling of a traditional Chinese tale)
- **year:** 2026
- **kind:** literary
- **form:** pourquoi tale, picture-book read-aloud
- **word count:** about 462 words (text only, not picture notes)
- **use:** paired (read aloud by the teacher)
- **read on days:** 41 (first whole reading), 42, 44, 46, 48, 49, 57

Teacher note: this is a story people in China and nearby countries have told for a very long time, and still tell. It is told here as a story, not as science. Credit it aloud: "This is a traditional Chinese tale." The tale is linked to the Qixi festival (the seventh day of the seventh month in the Chinese calendar), and to two bright stars that people see on opposite sides of a wide, pale band of stars in the summer sky. The tale has many tellings; this is one. The two are apart, but they are not unhappy forever: they meet and they care for each other. Teachers say gently that the family is sad to be apart and glad when they meet. No costumes, accents or acting out.

[page 1]
Long ago, in a story people in China still tell, there lived a weaver girl in the sky. Her name was Zhinu. She wove the clouds. At sunrise she wove them pink and gold. At sunset she wove them orange and rose. She worked every day, and her clouds were the loveliest in the sky.
*Picture: a girl at a large wooden loom among the clouds, weaving soft pink and gold cloth that drifts away; a pale sky around her.*

[page 2]
Far below, on the earth, lived a cowherd named Niulang. He took care of his ox. He led it to green grass every morning and brought it home every night. He was kind, and he worked hard, and his ox was his best friend.
*Picture: a young man with a gentle brown ox beside a stream and green hills; a few birds above them.*

[page 3]
One day Zhinu came down from the sky to see the earth. She saw the river, the green hills and the cowherd walking by his ox. Niulang looked up and saw her smile. They talked and laughed together, and they became friends. Soon they became a family. They built a little home, and they were very happy.
*Picture: the weaver girl and the cowherd laughing by a small house with a thatched roof and a garden; the ox in the yard; light clouds above.*

[page 4]
But up in the sky, the clouds went gray. Nobody was weaving them. Zhinu had forgotten the loom. Her grandmother, the Queen Mother, grew worried. "Come home," she called. "The sky needs you."
*Picture: a gray, gloomy sky with a silent loom; an older woman in long robes looking down from a cloud, with a hand cupped to her mouth.*

[page 5]
Zhinu was sad to go. Niulang ran after her. He ran up the hill and up the path and as high as a person can go. But her grandmother took a silver pin from her hair and drew one long line across the sky. The line became a wide, shining river of stars. Zhinu was on one side, and Niulang was on the other. The river was too wide to cross.
*Picture: the cowherd on a hilltop, reaching up; a shining river of stars across the dark sky, the weaver girl on the far side, small, reaching down.*

[page 6]
Every day Zhinu wove the clouds, and she wove them well. Every day Niulang took care of his ox, and he worked well. But they looked across the river of stars and missed each other. Birds and people on the earth saw them, and felt sad.
*Picture: a split picture: the weaver girl at her loom on one side, the cowherd with his ox on the other, a river of stars between.*

[page 7]
Then the magpies came. They are black-and-white birds with long tails. One by one they flew up into the sky, wing to wing, tail to tail, and made a bridge over the river of stars. Zhinu stepped onto the bridge. Niulang stepped onto it, too. They met in the middle, and they held hands.
*Picture: a bridge made of many magpies spanning the river of stars; the two figures walking toward each other along it, smiling.*

[page 8]
Now, every year, on the seventh night of the seventh month, the magpies make the bridge again. Zhinu and Niulang meet. They are glad. They say good night, and they go back to their work. They know they will meet again.
*Picture: a summer night; the bridge of magpies lit by starlight; the two figures waving to each other as they head home.*

[page 9]
On clear summer nights, people look up. They see two bright stars on opposite sides of a long, pale river of stars. People say those two stars are Zhinu and Niulang. They remember that when you care about someone, you can keep caring across a great distance, and friends and helpers can help you meet.
*Picture: children and grown-ups of different ages on a blanket on a summer lawn, looking up, with two bright stars drawn on either side of a pale band of stars.*

Choices in this telling: the cowherd's ox helps in other tellings and is kept here only as his friend; the grandmother, called the Queen Mother here, is a family figure (some tellings name the Jade Emperor's family); the pair have no children here, to keep the telling short and gentle; the clothes-taking episode that appears in some tellings is left out.
