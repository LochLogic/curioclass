# Why the Sun and the Moon Live in the Sky

- **title:** Why the Sun and the Moon Live in the Sky (an Efik and Ibibio tale, retold in Curio Class words)
- **text_id:** g1-u2-03
- **author:** Curio Class (retelling); tale of the Efik and Ibibio people, collected by Elphinstone Dayrell
- **year:** 2026 (retelling); Dayrell's collection 1910
- **kind:** literary
- **form:** pourquoi tale (a story that tells why something is so), five pages
- **word count:** 333 words (text only, not picture notes)
- **use:** anchor. The teacher reads it aloud whole with picture pauses; children listen and join the repeated question and answer ("Is it still safe?" "Yes."). Children do not decode it alone.
- **read on days:** 35 (first whole reading), 36 (who and where, first events), 37 (the repeated question and the rising water), 38, 39 (feeling and sense words), 40, 45, 46, 47.
- **source and licence:** Retold from "Why the Sun and the Moon live in the Sky", tale XVI, pages 64 to 65, in Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria, West Africa by Elphinstone Dayrell (1910), public domain in the United States, free at Project Gutenberg (ebook 34655). The wording here is our own; the events follow the collected version. The tale belongs to the Efik and Ibibio people, and the credit is spoken aloud to them. Dayrell's was a colonial-era record made by an outsider, and its limits are named on the teacher page.

Teacher note: say aloud "This is a story the Efik and Ibibio people of southern Nigeria tell." It is a story people tell, never wrong science. The events are a flood that fills a house and lifts the sun and moon to the sky: no death, no injury, no violence, no religion, no slur; fish and water animals only. Night is not part of it; the water is described gently. Words from the plan's list: tale, house, friend, invite, warn, promise, remain (the text says "stayed"; "remain" is taught as a word on day 36), compound (a big yard with houses; the tale's use only). Feeling and sense words for day 39: broad smile, knee-deep. No acting out of the tradition, no accents or costumes. The required tradition-specific sensitivity read has not been done on this retelling.

[page 1]
This tale comes from the Efik and Ibibio people of southern Nigeria. It tells why the sun and the moon live in the sky.
*Picture: a sun and a moon drawn together above a plain outline of a river country, no faces on the map.*

[page 2]
Long ago, the Sun and the Water were good friends. They both lived on the earth.
The Sun often visited the Water. But the Water never visited the Sun.
At last the Sun asked, "Friend, why do you never come to my house?"
The Water said, "Your house is too small. I have many people, the fish and all the water animals. If we come, there will be no room for you."
"Build a very big compound," said the Water. "A compound is a big yard with houses in it. I warn you, it must be huge."
*Picture: the Sun, a round figure with warm rays, standing at a small house door, a wide blue Water figure listening, fish drawn at the Water's feet.*

[page 3]
The Sun made a promise. "I will build it."
He went home to his wife, the Moon. She met him at the door with a broad smile. He told her what he had promised.
The next day the Sun began to build. He built a huge compound. When it was done, he invited the Water to visit the day after.
*Picture: the Sun and the Moon building a long wall and a big yard together, bricks stacked, the Moon smiling.*

[page 4]
The Water came to the gate. "Is it safe for me to come in?" he called.
"Yes, come in, my friend," said the Sun.
The Water flowed in with the fish. Soon the water was knee-deep.
"Is it still safe?" asked the Water.
"Yes," said the Sun. More water came in.
*Picture: the compound with water around the knees of the Sun and the Moon, fish swimming by, the house door open.*

[page 5]
Soon the water was as high as their heads.
"Shall more of my people come?" asked the Water.
"Yes," said the Sun and the Moon. They did not know what would happen.
More water came in. The Sun and the Moon climbed up to sit on the roof.
Once more the Water asked. Once more they said yes.
The water rose over the roof. There was no place left on the earth. So the Sun and the Moon went up into the sky.
They have stayed there ever since. That is why the sun and the moon live in the sky.
*Picture: the Sun and the Moon rising from the water-covered roof into a wide sky, the Water spread below with fish, a calm blue-gold light.*
