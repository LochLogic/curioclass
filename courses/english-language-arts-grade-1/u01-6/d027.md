# Day 27: Say how it helps

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the closing sheets, Card F, the item cards and the observation sheet. Lay out the models, the model record sheets, the precut paper and pre-torn tape, the question strips and the letter sample sheets. Write the six names for the rotation across the top of the observation sheet, children not yet heard in this window. Put a small timer or a clock where only you see it. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 26's marks. If many children gave a judgment with no fact (error AA, "Make it nicer") on day 26's exit, take four minutes. Write nicer and fact on the board. Say: "Nicer is a feeling. A fact is something you can see or test." Then a new bin, made up for practice: "Our paper bin holds chalk. A tight flap stays shut, so we cannot get a piece out." **Ask.** "What change would help?" Expect: a loose flap or one that lifts. Hold up one finger for the fact (the flap stays shut) and one for the change. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge. Use any minute left to name today's plan: finish the model and say how it helps.

#### 15 min. Finish the model

Each child finishes the paper model with the precut paper and pre-torn tape (10 minutes). Say: "Make the part that does the job strong." Then each child completes one sentence on the model record sheet, "The ___ helps because ___" (3 minutes), and reads it to a neighbor (2 minutes). Walk and ask, "Which part helps?" and "What fact comes after because?" Look-fors: the model has the revised part; the sentence names a part and a fact.

#### 20 min. Rehearse

Trios with the question strips: maker, listener, asker. Review: you used this talk on day 21, one speaks, one adds, one asks. Show the move with the lantern (3 minutes): the maker says two facts, "The string helps because it lets the lantern hang. The holes help because they let light shine out." The listener says one fact back. The asker points a strip and asks, "Which part?" and the maker points. Ten trios take three rounds of 4 minutes, so each child is the maker once (12 minutes). Sit with two trios, 3 minutes each, and hear the six children of the rotation (5 minutes). Look-fors: two facts; each with because; the answer to the asker stays on the part. A child may pass the maker turn and point instead.

#### 15 min. Closing

Read the sign model's last sentence aloud: "The sign helps people find our room." Say: "A closing says the job." Show the lantern closing: "The lantern helps us see in a dim corner." On the closing sheet each child draws the job done (7 minutes), then says one closing sentence with the frame "The ___ helps us ___" to a partner (3 minutes). Hear the rotation six, 1 minute each, and write each closing on the line (6 minutes). A child may point to the picture instead.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread the hoe paragraph of Tools on the Farm aloud and show the article picture. Ask: "What fact explains how the long handle helps?" Every child points to or marks a picture, then hear the rotation six say it, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X27v2]]). A good answer is that it lets the farmer stand up tall, so the farmer does not bend over all day. "It has wheels" is a part the paragraph did not give (error A). "The farmer has a straw hat" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). "It is good" gives a judgment with no fact (error AA). These marks go to day 28.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend: /z/ /i/ /p/, "zip"; /j/ /u/ /g/, "jug". Segment with counters: jug, three counters. Isolate the first sound of jug, /j/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map

No new pattern. Review the cycle 4 patterns on the sound-spelling map: short u, qu and the plural -s. On the sound-box sheet each child spells jugs, tubs and nuts (6 minutes). The class reads the cards jugs, mugs, rugs, hugs and quit (2 minutes). The ending says /z/ in jugs and /s/ in nuts. Walk and sit with three children.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Teach Z and z. Say: "Z is a line across the top, a slant down, and a line across the bottom. The little z is the same, smaller." Children trace and print. Dictation: say "Zan has a zip." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: the capital Z, zan, the mapped word has, a, zip and the period. Then letter samples (4 minutes): each child prints the pairs G g to M m on side one of the letter sample sheet, and you write the date. Z is collected from day 28. Forms still missing are recorded as incomplete, never wrong. Watch b and d.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Observation rotation 1 of 5. Six different children, 5 minutes (300 seconds) each, at your table; no new teaching. The other twenty-four read known texts and spell independently: Pat's Lunch with a partner, the hammer poem refrains, and words on the sound-box sheet. Every child supplies this evidence once in the window of days 27 to 31.

*Order and seconds, one child at a time (275 seconds, about 25 to change children).* Card F carries items 1, 12, 13 and 14.
1. Sentence features, s01-A16, 10: on Card F, point to the first word, the capital letter and the mark at the end of "The cup tips."
2. Long and short vowels, s01-A17, 10: cap and cape, said aloud.
3. Blending, s01-A18, 10: /s/ /l/ /i/ /p/.
4. Sounds in a word, s01-A19, 10: cup, first, middle and last.
5. Segmenting, s01-A20, 15: stamp, every sound.
6. Digraphs, s01-A21, 15: th, ch, sh on cards.
7. Reading, s01-A22, 15: chip and shut.
8. Mapped words, s01-A23, 10: the and is.
9. Plural ending, s01-A24, 5: cap and caps.
10. Spelling, s01-A26, 20: chin and the.
11. Spelling by sound, s01-A27, 15: thud, not shown.
12. Purpose, s01-A28, 25: read Card F to find why the pup is sad, then point.
13. Rate and phrasing, s01-A29, 90: read Card F aloud, then once more.
14. Checking, s01-A30, 25: find what the cup does, reread to check.

Hold the quiet pace cue yourself: a small timer or a look at a clock, never shown or heard by children. Mark each item shown, shown with help or not yet seen on the observation sheet, with the error letter if one fits, and move to the next item at once. A child who is not yet seen gets the stem once more, then the first sound as help; if it is still not seen, record it and teach nothing new today. The repair waits for day 33. The letter samples (s01-A25) and the earlier reading records (s01-A31, s01-A32) take no minutes today. A child who is away is heard on the next rotation.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the observation marks to the record sheet and file the observation sheet. File the closing sheets, the dated letter samples and the models. Mark the exit marks and the six who were heard. Name tomorrow's six for rotation 2.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the item words on cards and read Card F from a lined page; record on a lined page. No tape: fold paper and join with a glue stick. One child: finish the model with a grown-up, say two facts with because, say a closing, do the exit and the letters; skip the observation.

### At home

**Swap.** No tape: use a glue stick. No model: draw it.

**Skip.** The trios and the observation.

- **10 min. Two facts.** Your child shows the model and says two facts: "The ___ helps because ___."
  **Say.** "Which part helps, and why?" Expect: a part and a fact after because.
- **10 min. A closing.** Your child draws the job done and says "The ___ helps us ___."
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints Z z and "Zan has a zip."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X27v2]] Read the hoe paragraph of Tools on the Farm. Ask: "What fact explains how the long handle helps?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the closing, the exit and the observation; cut the rehearsal to the two trios you sit with. Longer: a child who finishes says a third fact, with because, about a part.

### Words and supports

Words: finish, fact, because, part, closing, job. Frames: "The ___ helps because ___." "The ___ helps us ___." "Which part?" Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers; the observation items may be answered by pointing. A quiet corner for the observation, and extra seconds where the dialect differs. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 28 reads the sign model again to find its parts and writes a topic sentence about the tool. Rotation 2 hears six more children. If many children gave a part the paragraph did not give (error A, "It has wheels"), a picture fact (error C, "The farmer has a straw hat") or a judgment with no fact (error AA, "It is good") on today's exit, its first part opens with four minutes: reread the hoe paragraph, point to the sentence that gives the fact, cover the picture first when the slip was a picture fact, and try a new card.
