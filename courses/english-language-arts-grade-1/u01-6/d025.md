# Day 25: Say why you changed it

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the change drawing sheets, the sound-box sheets, the exit card with its three pictures and the paragraph copies for each pair. Lay out the day 24 models, the hearing note slips, the hearing lines and the reader Pat's Lunch. Choose cohort 3, the last ten names on the record sheet, for the hearing, and cohort 2 for the retry. Divide the class into two groups of fifteen for the share. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 24's marks. If many children said a part the card did not give (error A), a picture fact (error C) or a judgment with no fact (error AA) on day 24's exit, take four minutes. Reread the pocket card line by line: "A paper pocket holds cards. Tape joins its sides. Its open top lets cards slide in." **Ask.** "Which sentence says what the open top does?" Children point to the third line. For error A, point to the sentence that holds the fact. For error C, cover the picture, reread, then uncover: "Do the words say it?" For error AA, review: on day 24 you said a fact is something you can see or test. Then a new card, made up for practice: "A paper lantern hangs on a string. Tape joins its sides. Its round holes let light shine out." **Ask.** "How do the holes help the lantern?" Expect: light shines out. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge. Use any minute left to name today's plan: say why you changed your model.

#### 10 min. Reread

Whole class on the rug. Read these paragraphs aloud, words first, picture hidden: the funnel (Tools in the Kitchen, day 4), the hoe, the ladle and the watering can (3 minutes). Review: on day 21 a designed tool was made on purpose for a job. Now say: "A design change is a change made on purpose because a fact says it will help." The class says it with you (1 minute).

Show the funnel as the worked change. Reread: "The cook sets the narrow end in a small bottle and pours oil into the wide top." **Ask.** "Which words say where the narrow end goes?" Expect: in a small bottle. Hold up three fingers and say each: the fact, the narrow end fits a small bottle; the change, a narrow end; the reason, the oil goes into the bottle and not onto the table. Say: "A narrow end is a change on purpose, because a fact says it helps" (3 minutes). Review: you used because in kindergarten. Each child turns to a neighbor, finds the ladle words about its long handle and tells the fact and what the handle does (3 minutes). Hear three children say it. A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs with the turn card. Give each pair the hoe, ladle and watering can paragraphs in large print. Review: you used the starters "I would add ..." and "Which part?" on days 10 and 21. Show the move with one pair at the front (2 minutes): the first child points to a fact ("The long handle lets the farmer stand up tall.") and says how it could change a model ("I could add a long handle to my model."); the listener asks "Which part?" and the first points to the part on the day 24 model. Then swap. Fifteen pairs talk for 6 minutes, so each child speaks once and asks once. Sit with three pairs, 1 minute each, and hear six children (2 minutes). Look-fors for the six: (1) a fact that is in the paragraph; (2) a change to their own model; (3) the listener's question names a part. For a fact only the picture shows, say: "Cover the picture. Do the words say it?"

#### 20 min. Draw the change

Each child takes their model and the change drawing sheet: a box for the model before, an arrow and a box for the model with one change. Say: "Pick one fact from the paragraphs. Change one part because of it." Children draw the model as it is (4 minutes), then draw the one change (6 minutes) and print one word label for the part they changed, saying the sounds or copying from the board (3 minutes). Walk and ask, "Which fact says it will help?" A child who names a change with no fact (error AA, "I made it nicer") hears: "Nice is a feeling. Find a fact in the words." Hear today's six, 1 minute each, and write the sentence each says on the line under the boxes: "I changed my ___ because ___." In the last 4 minutes each child tells a partner the same sentence. A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Linked share

Two groups of fifteen, 5 minutes each. The group sits in a ring. Each child says the sentence once, 20 seconds: first a short add-on to the child before ("I would add that ...", or "I add that ..."), then their own change. The first child begins with only the change. The ring makes linked exchanges: each add-on must stay on the same part or the same fact. If an add-on drifts, ask the speaker, "Which part did you mean?" The other group rereads Pat's Lunch in pairs. Swap groups after 5 minutes. Hear who linked and who started a new topic, and mark it for the record. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A deep box holds our counters. A flat sheet lets them slide off." Show the three pictures: a sheet with a raised edge, a longer sheet and a bluer sheet. Ask: "Which change would stop the counters sliding off?" Every child points, then hear today's six say why, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X25]]). A good answer is the raised edge, because it keeps the counters on. "A lid would stop them" is a part the card did not give (error A). "The box is brown" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). "A new sheet is nice" gives a judgment with no fact (error AA). These marks go to day 26.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend: /h/ /u/ /g/, "hug"; /k/ /w/ /i/ /t/, "quit". Segment with counters: hugs, four counters. Isolate the first sound of hug, /h/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map

Review: you met the plural ending -s on day 21. A cycle progress sample, not scored: on the sound-box sheet each child spells mugs, rugs and huts. Say the word, tap a box for each sound, print a letter in each box (6 minutes). The ending says /z/ in mugs and rugs and /s/ in huts. Keep the sheet in each child's folder. The class reads the cards mugs, rugs, huts, quit and hugs (2 minutes). Walk and sit with three children.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Teach X and x. Say: "X is two slanting lines that cross in the middle. The little x is the same, smaller." Add x to the sound-spelling map: x says /k/ /s/ together at the end of a word, as in six. Children trace and print. Dictation: say "Six cats sit." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: the capital S, six, cats and sit, then the period.

#### 30 min. Rotation

**Hearing, cohort 3 (15 minutes).** Ten children, 90 seconds each, the same three pieces as yesterday. The other twenty reread Pat's Lunch in pairs and finish their change drawings. The child sits with you and reads with your help, a finger under the words. Prose, Kofi and the Bicycle, page 7: "I sit on the seat. It does not wobble. It is still!" Two poem lines, The Hammer Song: "Tap, tap, tap, the little hammer sings." Information, Tools on the Farm: "A farmer grows food. A farmer uses tools to help plants grow." Write the note on a slip: what you heard, the next step and tomorrow's retry. Give the child a copy. A note is a next step, never a grade.

**Retry, cohort 2 (15 minutes).** Cohort 2 is the ten children heard yesterday. Each rereads the line their written note named, with a finger under the words, 90 seconds each. The other twenty read Pat's Lunch with a partner. Update the note: what changed and what comes next. Read the note to the child. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Collect the change drawings and store the models in the labelled box. Mark the record sheet: the exit marks, the six who were heard and their look-fors, who linked in the share, and the hearing notes. Name tomorrow's six.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw two boxes and an arrow on folded paper and write the hearing lines on a card. One child: reread the funnel words with a grown-up, draw one change to the model, say the sentence, do the exit and the letters; skip the share, the hearing and the retry.

### At home

**Swap.** No model: draw a paper thing and its change. No funnel: roll a sheet of paper into a cone.

**Skip.** The share, the hearing and the retry.

- **10 min. The funnel.** Read: "The cook sets the narrow end in a small bottle and pours oil into the wide top. The oil goes into the bottle, not onto the table."
  **Say.** "Which words say where the narrow end goes?" Expect: in a small bottle.
- **10 min. A change.** Your child picks one part of a paper model, draws one change and says "I changed my ___ because ___."
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints X x and "Six cats sit."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X25]] Read "A deep box holds our counters. A flat sheet lets them slide off." Draw three sheets: one with a raised edge, one longer, one bluer. Ask: "Which change would stop the counters sliding off?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the draw, the share and the exit; cut the talk to the ladle. Longer: a child who finishes draws a second change with its own fact.

### Words and supports

Words: design change, fact, change, because, narrow, handle, part. Frames: "I changed my ___ because ___." "I would add that ..." "Which part?" Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. A scribe may write a word label. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 26 puts a partner's question on the model: a partner points How, Why or What at one part, and the maker answers and revises. If many children gave a judgment with no fact (error AA, "A new sheet is nice"), a picture fact (error C, "The box is brown") or a part the card did not give (error A, "A lid would stop them") on today's exit, its first part opens with four minutes: contrast "nice" with a fact and a change that follows it, one fact on each finger, then reread the card, point to the sentence, and try a new card.
