# Day 105 cold passage: Woodpeckers, three pages and a printed menu (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Read on day 105 only; the class has never heard it. Keep the pages face down until the trace opens. Print the three pages and the menu for each child, with "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of each. The teacher reads page 2, A Beak for Pecking, aloud once; children may follow the print. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading. It is read for s03-A24v2 and s03-A24bv2.

## Page 1

> **Woodpeckers**
>
> Contents
> A Beak for Pecking, page 2
> Gripping Feet, page 2
> Glossary, page 3
>
> A woodpecker is a bird that lives in woods and parks. It finds its food in trees.

Picture: a woodpecker on the side of a tree trunk.

## Page 2

> **A Beak for Pecking**
>
> A woodpecker has a strong, pointed beak. It pecks holes in tree bark. Bugs hide under the bark. The woodpecker pulls the bugs out with its long tongue.
>
> **Gripping Feet**
>
> Two toes point up and two toes point down. The toes grip the tree. The woodpecker can cling to the trunk and not slip.

Diagram: a woodpecker on a tree trunk, side view, with label lines to "beak" and "toes".

## Page 3

> **Glossary**
>
> bark: the hard outside of a tree.
> peck: to hit with the beak, again and again.
> cling: to hold on tight.

## Printed menu

A frame line like a screen with two choices, each with a small bird icon: Beaks, Feet. A book icon at the corner points to the glossary. Beaks leads to A Beak for Pecking on page 2; Feet leads to Gripping Feet. It does not show live electronic operation.

The routes to the bug fact (a woodpecker pecks holes in bark and pulls out the bugs with its long tongue): the contents entry A Beak for Pecking, page 2; the heading A Beak for Pecking; the label beak on the diagram; the glossary line peck; the menu choice Beaks and its bird icon; the book icon to the glossary.

Facts checked 2026-11-24. National Park Service, Mississippi National River and Recreation Area, "Downy Woodpecker" (a straight, chisel-tipped bill; an extremely long, barbed, sticky tongue reaches insects under bark; two toes forward and two back for a strong grip on bark; found in open woodlands, suburban lots and city parks), https://www.nps.gov/miss/learn/nature/birdsdown.htm (sources.csv g1-u3-f49). West Virginia Division of Natural Resources, "Woodpecker Adaptations" (stout, chisel-like beaks drill holes in trees; the probing tongue pulls insects out of wood or bark; two toes forward and two back give a firm grip), https://wvdnr.gov/woodpecker-adaptations/ (sources.csv g1-u3-f50). Page 1 said the bird "lives in the woods"; it now says "woods and parks", since many woodpeckers live in towns and parks too. A few kinds, such as the three-toed woodpeckers, have three toes; the text keeps the usual four-toed foot. The unit texts do not teach woodpeckers.
