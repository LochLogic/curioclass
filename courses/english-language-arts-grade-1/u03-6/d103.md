# Day 103: Revise the paragraph and prepare a research organizer

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 102's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages and an organizer for each child. Make the two unfinished drafts and the closing strip. Put each child's folder with its feedback note on the desk. Set out the cycle 16 sample cards where you sit, the sound-spelling map, the syllable chart, letter cards, the readers and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 102's exit showed a paragraph that stops with no closing (error F). Read two unfinished drafts: "A hummingbird has a long, thin beak. It sips from flowers. And then." and "A nuthatch has a sharp beak. It pries bark. And." **Ask.** "Where does each paragraph go back to its topic?" Expect: nowhere, both stop. Lay a fresh closing strip: "A long, thin beak is just right for sipping." Slide it under the first draft. Each child says a closing for the nuthatch to a partner, two minutes, all at once. Say the rule: "A closing goes back to the topic." If many made error F on day 102's exit, repeat with a duck draft that stops after one fact.

#### 8 min. Read the feedback

Each child takes the draft and its feedback note from the folder. Partners read the note aloud to each other, three minutes. Then model one revision on the hawk chart draft: the note says "Check the line for tears meat." Point to the line in Beaks Find Food, page 2, and read it. The draft says "cuts"; the page says "tears." Cross out "cuts," write "tears," and say "I changed cuts to tears because the page says tears." Review: the source label from day 91. Say: "A revision fixes what the source says." Hear two partners at the end, one minute each.

#### 17 min. Revise

Each child makes the changes the note asks for, in the same boxes on a new sheet, and keeps the first draft beside the revised one; the filled frame is taken away, so the child writes each box from the draft and the pages. Say: "Change what the note asks, and check the line." A child with no change to make adds a source label where one is missing. Walk round. Hear six children, 40 seconds each, say "I changed ___ because ___"; scribe for any child who asks. With one adult, scribe the heard six first and the rest at the stations. The revised paragraph is scored out of class for day 104. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 15 min. Research organizer

Show the organizer page and read its parts: the question, "How does the beak help the bird get food?"; two note boxes, each with a line for the note and a small box for the source label; beside each note, where it was found, with four small pictures to circle: heading, label, glossary, contents. Model the first note with the hawk: write "curved like a hook," the label B, and circle the glossary. Each child then fills the organizer from the notes folder, twelve minutes, all at once. Hear three children at the end, one minute each. **Ask.** "Where on the page did you find it?" Expect: a finger on a heading, label, glossary line or contents entry, and a circled picture to match.

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the turn card. **Ask.** "Which note needs correcting against the source?" Pairs swap organizers and check each note against the page, taking turns, four minutes, all at once. Hear six pairs, one minute each. Expect: a note with no matching line is the one to correct; a note whose line is found is kept. Pass is allowed.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the two pictures. Say: "Which picture matches a fact you can check on this page?" ([[s03-X103a]]) Then show Feathers Protect, pages 1 and 2, and read aloud: "A duck has feathers that keep water out. The duck stays dry in the pond." Say: "Show me where on these pages you found that fact." ([[s03-X103bv2]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread once), not yet seen. A child who points to the beak has chosen a part the card does not give (error B). A child who points only at the cover has not used a feature on the page (error D).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say rod and rode; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: rode. Blend /p/ /r/ /i/ /z/, "prize". Say dive and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /d/, /i/ (long), /v/. Segment froze: /f/ /r/ /o/ /z/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 16, lesson 85: i_e and o_e. Review the sorting of closed and silent e words from days 99 to 102. Show word cards one at a time, tap the sounds and place each in a column of the syllable chart: hid and hide, pin and pine, hike, hole, nine, smile, stone. Regroup by pattern: closed in one pile, silent e in another, all at once on desks, three minutes. Decode, then spell, two words on cards: nine and hole. The progress sample is in the rotation. About 40 seconds a word.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs K k and H h from the dictation. Dictate: "Mike hid a bone." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a is mapped, and Mike is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Mike and bone the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names. For their first five minutes, the cycle 16 sample at your table, 50 seconds each; then for five minutes map, blend, read, spell and reread hike, hole, nine and stone. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. Each child does the sample: show one held word from your folder, say "Read this word"; remove the card, then say the other held word in a sentence and once alone and ask the child to write it. Half the group read the first word and write the second; half do it the other way. The held words stay out of every other part of the day. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Mike and Dave Hike and Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, chain pin, pine, fine, mine with letter cards, sort closed and silent e word cards. Write each miss in the record for day 106's catch-up. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the revision marks, the sample marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Put each organizer in its folder with the first draft and the revision. List by name the children who pointed only at the cover (error D) for day 106's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the organizer on folded paper, four boxes and four small marks for where it was found. One child: revise the draft yourself from the note, fill the organizer, say where each note was found, and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No second page: use any other page about the same bird. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation, the stations and the sample.

- **10 min. Revise.** Read the feedback note on the draft. **Say.** "Which line says it? Change one word." Expect: a finger on the line and a changed word, then "I changed ___ because ___."
- **10 min. Organizer.** Your child fills in the question, one note with its label, and circles where it was found: heading, label, glossary or contents.
- **5 min. Check.** **Say.** "Which note needs correcting against the page?" Expect: one with no line.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints K k, H h and "Mike hid a bone."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X103a]] [[s03-X103bv2]] Read the card, then the duck lines of Feathers Protect. Your child points to the picture, then shows where the duck fact was found.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the revision, the exit and the cycle work; fill one note box of the organizer. Longer: a child writes where a third fact was found.

### Words and supports

Words: revise, correct, organizer, heading, label, glossary, contents. Frames: "I changed ___ because ___." "I found it in the ___." "The page says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can match a fact to a page and show where it was found. A child who pointed only at the cover (error D) is named for day 106's five-minute repair. Days 104 and 105 are a check: no help, no hints, and a miss is answered on 106 or 107. Day 104 hands out two cards and teaches u_e and e_e.
