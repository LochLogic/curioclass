# Section 3.6 opener: the research paragraph, the mid-unit check

## What the section does

Days 100 to 105 turn the notes into a paragraph. Children see a model with a topic, facts and a closing, draft one from the beak sources, revise it with written feedback and fill a research organizer. Days 104 and 105 are the mid-unit check on a fresh bird.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. Days 100 to 103 end with a five-minute exit read aloud to you on a new bird card; the child points, says or holds up fingers and you hear the day's six children. Days 104 and 105 give no help and no exit. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A paragraph has three jobs.** The topic sentence names it, facts explain it, a closing returns to it.
- **A liking is not a fact.** "I like sparrows" cannot go in the middle.
- **A note shows where it came from.** The organizer holds the note and the place it was found.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** a detail named as the topic. Sort again.
- **E:** a liking in place of a fact. Contrast it with a located fact.
- **F:** a piece with no closing. Say a closing that returns to the topic.
- **B:** a fact no source gives. Point to the sentence.
- **D:** the cover used to find a fact. Follow the contents, heading or label.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet and keep the sheet in your folder.
