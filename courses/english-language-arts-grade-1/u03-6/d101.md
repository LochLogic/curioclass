# Day 101: Write my topic and my first fact

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 100's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages and the frame page for any child without one. Make the hawk card and the duck repair card. Put each child's beak notes folder on the desk. Put out the syllable chart, letter cards, the sound-spelling map, the readers and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Day 100's exit showed a detail picked in place of the topic (error A). Read the hawk card: "A hawk has a hooked beak. The beak tears meat into small bits." **Ask.** "What is the whole card about? Say it in a few words." Expect: the hawk's beak. Write it on the topic label, then say the topic again with the class: "The topic is the hawk's beak." Say the rule: "The topic is the part. The facts tell about it." If many made error A on day 100's exit, repeat with the duck card, "A duck has a flat beak. It strains food from mud."

#### 8 min. Words, then reread the sources

Show six word cards with pictures, 20 seconds each: first and last, organize (to put things in order), include (to put in), fact and paragraph (review, day 100). Then read aloud, 30 seconds each: Long Beaks for Fish in Beaks Find Food, Long Beak in Heron by the Water, Flat Beaks for Mud in Beaks Find Food, and Beak in Duck at the Pond. Pairs point to the feeding sentence in each, all at once, three minutes.

#### 12 min. Choose and organize

Each child picks the heron or the duck. Heron writers take the two notes from day 97 out of the folder. Duck writers have no beak notes yet (the day 92 notes are about feathers), so they make two now: a picture, one word and a label, one note from B, Flat Beaks for Mud, and one from D, Beak in Duck at the Pond, five minutes. Everyone lays the two notes in the fact boxes of the frame page, then says the plan to a partner, "topic, fact, fact, closing," two minutes. The frame has four boxes: topic, fact, fact, closing. Hear three pairs, one minute each, while the others settle.

#### 20 min. Draft the topic and first fact

Model on chart paper, hawk only. Start from the heading Hooked Beaks for Meat and write: "A hawk's hooked beak is how it eats meat." Then the first fact, "The hooked beak tears meat into small bits," with B beside it. Say: "Say your topic sentence your own way, if it names the bird, its beak and food." Each child prints, or has scribed, the topic sentence and the first fact in the first two boxes, and the source label beside the fact. Walk round. A child who writes "I like herons" has put a liking where a topic goes (error E): ask "Which page says it?" Hear six children, 40 seconds each, and scribe for any child who asks: with one adult, scribe the heard six today and the rest on day 102.

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the turn card. **Ask.** "Why is that source fact useful to the reader?" Pairs take turns, two turns each, all at once, four minutes; heron and duck writers sit together. Hear six pairs, one minute each. Expect: the reader can check it on the page; it tells what the beak does. Pass is allowed.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the two pictures. Say: "Which picture is the topic of a paragraph about this card?" ([[s03-X101a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Say a topic sentence." ([[s03-X101b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the feathers has chosen a part the card does not give (error B). A child who says "I like toucans" has put a liking in place of a topic sentence (error E).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say hop and hole; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: hole. Blend /s/ /l/ /i/ /d/, "slide". Say hose and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /h/, /o/ (long), /z/. Segment stone: /s/ /t/ /o/ /n/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 16, lesson 83: i_e and o_e. Review the silent e from days 99 and 100. Show pairs, one at a time: hid and hide, pin and pine. Say each, tap the sounds and place it in the closed or the silent e column. Map the new words: five, dime, smile, stone. Decode, then spell, dime and stone on cards. Mark only the sounded vowel with a dot, all at once. The reader Mike and Dave Hike is reread for expression in the rotation. About 40 seconds a word.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs D d and F f from the dictation. Dictate: "Dave hid a dime." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a is mapped, and Dave is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Dave and dime the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread five, dime, smile and stone. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Mike and Dave Hike, reread for expression: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, chain hid, hide, side, slide with letter cards, sort closed and silent e word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the draft marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Put each frame page in its folder. List by name the children who said a liking in place of a topic sentence (error E) for day 102's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a sheet into four boxes and write the sentences in them. One child: pick the heron or the duck, write the topic and first fact with a label, and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No bird page: use any short page on how a bird's beak gets food. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation, the stations and the notes for the second bird.

- **10 min. Read.** Read your child the feeding lines of one page about a heron or a duck. **Say.** "Which line tells how the beak gets food?" Expect: a finger on the line.
- **10 min. Write.** Your child says, then prints, a topic sentence that names the bird, its beak and food. **Say.** "Which page says it?" Expect: the page you read.
- **5 min. First fact.** Your child prints one fact with its page label.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints D d, F f and "Dave hid a dime."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X101a]] [[s03-X101b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points to the picture, then says a topic sentence.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the notes, the draft, the exit and the cycle work; cut the reread of four pages to two. Longer: a child writes a second topic sentence for the other bird.

### Words and supports

Words: first, last, organize, include, fact, paragraph. Frames: "My topic is ___." "A fact is ___." "Source ___ says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can name the topic and say a topic sentence. A child who said a liking (error E) is named for day 102's five-minute repair; a child who picked the feathers (error B) is asked for the page line first. Day 102 adds a second fact and a closing.
