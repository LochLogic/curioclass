# Day 100: Find the topic, the facts and the closing

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 99's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages. Cut the model strips and labels, one set for each pair, and keep the extra strip. Write the four sentences of the day 94 class paragraph on four strips. Make the two hawk note cards. Put out the sound-spelling map, the syllable chart, letter cards, the readers and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 99's exit showed a note found in one source only (error B). Lay two lines on the table: from Beaks Find Food, labelled B, "The hooked beak tears meat into small bits"; from Bills at Work, labelled W, "A hawk's hooked bill rips meat." Hold up the first note card, "the hawk tears meat." **Ask.** "Which line says it?" Expect: a finger on the B line first, then on the W line; the labels B and W go on the card. Hold up the second card, "the hawk dives fast." **Ask.** "Which line says it?" Expect: no line does, so the card gets the word check. Say the rule: "Point to the line first, then say the note." If many made error B on day 99's exit, repeat with a duck: B, "The duck dips its beak in the pond mud"; W, "A duck's wide, flat bill sifts food from mud and water."

#### 10 min. Words, then the model

Show four word cards with pictures, 20 seconds each: topic sentence, the sentence that names what a paragraph is about; fact, something a source says; closing, the last sentence, which goes back to the topic; source, the page a fact came from (review: the labels B, H and W, from days 91 and 99). Read aloud Hooked Beaks for Meat, page 2 of Beaks Find Food. Say: "I will write a paragraph from that page. It is a teaching chart made for today." Write it one sentence at a time and read each aloud as it goes down: "A hawk's beak helps it get food. The hawk has a hooked beak. The hooked beak tears meat into small bits. That is how a hawk's beak gets its food." Read all four again, pointing under each sentence. The class reads it with you once.

#### 15 min. The three parts, taught

Give each pair the four strips and the three labels: topic, facts, closing. Place strip one under topic together: "It names what the paragraph is about, the hawk's beak." Pairs slide strips two to four under a label and say what each does, all at once, four minutes. Hear three pairs, one minute each. Expect: strips two and three are facts, one says what the beak is like and one what it does; strip four is the closing, which goes back to the topic and tells no new fact. Point to the page for each fact: both are in Hooked Beaks for Meat, page 2, source B.

Then the contrast, three minutes (error E). Lay the extra strip, "I like hawks. They look fierce.", in the facts column. **Ask.** "Can we point to a line on the page that says it?" Expect: no, so it comes out. Say the rules: "A fact comes from a source. A liking is not a fact." "The closing goes back to the topic." If a child keeps the liking, ask "Which line says it?"

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the turn card. **Ask.** "How does each fact help explain the beak?" Pairs take turns, two turns each, all at once, four minutes. Hear six pairs, one minute each. Expect: the hooked beak says what the beak is like; tears meat says what it does with food. Pass is allowed.

#### 15 min. Sort and draw

Seven minutes: pairs lay the four strips of the day 94 class paragraph in a mixed order, then sort them under the three labels: "Feathers help a duck." "Down keeps it warm." "Feathers keep the water out." "Feathers help the duck in many ways." Expect: topic, two facts, closing. Walk round; each pair says why the last one is the closing. Eight minutes: each child draws the heron's beak or the duck's beak on the student page and writes a word label. Hear six drawings, 40 seconds each. **Ask.** "What does the beak in your drawing do?" Expect: the heron's catches fish; the duck's strains mud. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Exit

The others finish their drawings; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Reread aloud the Beak part of Duck at the Pond, page 2, and show the two pictures. Say: "Which picture is the whole part about?" ([[s03-X100av2]]) Then say: "This card is made up for practice," and read the crossbill card from the card sheet: "Which sentence closes the paragraph? Hold up one to four fingers." ([[s03-X100b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread once), not yet seen. A child who points to the mud alone has picked a detail in place of the topic (error A). A child who holds up two or three fingers has taken a fact for the closing (error F).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say slid and slide; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: slide. Blend /s/ /m/ /o/ /k/, "smoke". Say bike and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /b/, /i/ (long), /k/. Segment pride: /p/ /r/ /i/ /d/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 16, lesson 82. Review: i_e and o_e from day 99, where the silent e makes the vowel say its name. Show pairs on cards, one at a time: hop and hope, pin and pine, slid and slide. Say each and tap the sounds; children say which syllable chart column, closed or silent e. Map hike, hole, nine, smile and stone. Decode, then spell, two words on cards: hike and hole. Before the reader, map its irregular words as parts to remember. Review: a, has, is and the from unit 1; to from day 90; his, where the s says /z/ as in was (day 80). Cover the picture on page 6 of Mike and Dave Hike and read the sentence: a child who guesses a word from the picture is asked to map h-o-l-e, blend, and confirm in the sentence (error I).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs M m and B b from the dictation (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate: "Mike has a bike." Say it whole, then a word at a time; has and a are mapped, and Mike is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap a finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Mike and bike the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread hike, hole, nine and stone. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Mike and Dave Hike, read for the first time today after its patterns and words are mapped: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, chain hop, hope, hole, mole with letter cards, sort closed and silent e word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the drawing marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who picked the detail (error A) for day 101's repair and those who took a fact for the closing (error F).

### Other rooms

No printer: write each model sentence on its own scrap strip and the three labels on paper. One child: slide the four strips yourself, say what each does, draw a beak, and answer the exit aloud with fingers.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: write each sentence on its own paper. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. The three parts.** Read: "A hawk's beak helps it get food. The hawk has a hooked beak. The hooked beak tears meat into small bits. That is how a hawk's beak gets its food." **Say.** "Which sentence finishes the paragraph?" Expect: the last one, because it goes back to the topic.
- **5 min. A liking.** Read "I like hawks." **Say.** "Is that a fact?" Expect: no, no page says it.
- **10 min. Draw.** Your child draws a heron's or a duck's beak and writes one word.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints M m, B b and "Mike has a bike."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X100av2]] [[s03-X100b]] Read the duck's Beak part and the card the page shows. Your child points to the picture, then holds up fingers.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the model, the strips, the exit and the cycle work; cut the sort of the day 94 paragraph. Longer: a child finds the topic, two facts and the closing in the heron's page of Beaks Find Food.

### Words and supports

Words: topic sentence, fact, closing, source. Frames: "The topic is ___." "A fact is ___." "The closing goes back to ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can name the whole topic and who can pick the closing. A child who pointed to the mud alone (error A) is named for day 101's five-minute repair; one who held up two or three fingers (error F) is noted for day 102's closing work. Day 101 drafts the topic and first fact of a beak paragraph.
