# Day 104 cold passage: Penguin Wings (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Read on day 104 only; the class has never heard it. Keep it covered until the check opens. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of the page. The teacher reads it aloud twice; children may follow the print. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading. It is read for s03-A22v2 and s03-A23v2.

> **Penguin Wings**
>
> A penguin is a bird, but it cannot fly. Its wings are stiff and flat. They work like flippers. Under the water, the penguin flaps its wings. The wings push the water, and the penguin zooms along. Its wings help it swim fast to catch fish.

Picture under the text: a penguin swimming under water, its wings stretched out like flippers.

Three pictures for s03-A22v2, cut apart, each alone in a box: the penguin's wings; the water alone; the penguin's feet.

Facts checked against NOAA National Ocean Service, "Can penguins fly?" (penguins cannot fly in the air; their wings are built for swimming), https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/penguinsfly.html (sources.csv g1-u3-f5, checked 2026-11-20), and Animal Diversity Web, University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, "Sphenisciformes" (stiff wings modified as flattened flippers, used to swim underwater; prey includes small fish such as anchovies, squid and krill), https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Sphenisciformes/ (sources.csv g1-u3-f47, checked 2026-11-24). "Short" was cut: neither page says the wings are short. The unit texts do not teach penguins.
