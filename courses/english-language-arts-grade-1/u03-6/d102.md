# Day 102: Add a second fact and a closing

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 101's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages and a feedback note slip for each child. Make the three hawk cards, the hawk chart draft. Put out the folders, each child's frame page, the syllable chart, letter cards, the sound-spelling map, the readers and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 101's exit showed a liking in place of a topic sentence (error E). Lay three cards: "I like hawks. They are cool." "A hawk's hooked beak is how it eats meat." "I think hawks are fun to watch." **Ask.** "Which card is a topic sentence about the beak and food?" Expect: the second; the other two are likings and tell no fact. Then each child says one topic sentence about the heron or the duck to a partner, all at once, two minutes. Review: a topic sentence, from day 101. Say the rule: "A topic sentence names the bird, its beak and food." If many made error E on day 101's exit, repeat with a sparrow: "I like sparrows" beside "A sparrow's thick beak cracks seeds."

#### 8 min. Reread

Reread, 30 seconds each: for the heron, Long Beaks for Fish in Beaks Find Food and Long Beak in Heron by the Water; for the duck, Flat Beaks for Mud in Beaks Find Food and Beak in Duck at the Pond. Each child points to a second fact, a line not used on day 101, and says its label, B, H or D. Walk round; ask a child with no finger on a line "Which line says it?"

#### 10 min. A second fact

Model with the hawk chart draft: "A hawk's hooked beak is how it eats meat. The hooked beak tears meat into small bits." B. Find the next fact in the page first: read the glossary of Beaks Find Food, page 3, point to "hooked: curved like a hook," then write "A hooked beak is curved like a hook," label B. Say the rule: "Point to the line, then write the fact." Pairs then read their drafts to each other and point to the source line for the next fact, all at once, six minutes, two turns each. Hear three pairs, one minute each. A fact with no line is held back.

#### 17 min. Write the second fact and the closing

Reread the topic sentence on the hawk chart aloud, then write under the facts: "A hawk's hooked beak is made for tearing meat." Say the rule: "A closing goes back to the topic." Say: "Write the closing your own way." Each child prints, or has scribed, the second fact with its label in the third box and a closing in the last box. A list that stops (error F) is asked "What is the paragraph about? Say that again at the end." With one adult, scribe the heard six first, 40 seconds each, and the rest on day 103.

#### 15 min. Written feedback and partner reading

Review: written feedback, a kind, specific, helpful note, from days 65 and 98. Partners read their paragraphs to each other, two minutes each. **Ask.** "Does the new fact strengthen the same explanation?" Expect: yes, it tells more about the same beak; a fact about another part does not. Pairs talk, six minutes; pass is allowed. At the same time you write one note on each draft, 30 seconds a draft for about 30 drafts, naming one fact to check and one thing done well, for example "Check the line for tears meat. Your topic is clear." No child is conferenced aloud. The notes go in the folders.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the two pictures. Say: "Which picture shows a fact you could add?" ([[s03-X102a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Say a closing sentence that returns to the topic." ([[s03-X102b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the puffin swimming has chosen a picture the card does not give (error B). A child who says "and then it swam away" has stopped with no closing (error F).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say pin and pine; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: pine. Blend /g/ /l/ /o/ /b/, "globe". Say joke and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /j/, /o/ (long), /k/. Segment glide: /g/ /l/ /i/ /d/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 16, lesson 84: i_e and o_e with the ending -s. Review: -s on a taught base, from unit 2 (tugs). Show hike and hikes, bone and bones: the e stays and -s is added; in hikes the s says /s/, in bones it says /z/ as in has. Map hikes, bikes, bones and holes, one at a time, and read each with its ending sounded. Decode, then spell, two words on cards: hikes and bones. The reader Mike and Dave Hike is reread in the rotation.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs R r and V v from the dictation. Dictate: "Dave rode a bike." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a is mapped, and Dave is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Dave, rode and bike the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread hikes, bones, holes and smile. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Mike and Dave Hike, reread for expression: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Kate and Jake Bake a Cake, chain bike, hike, like, Mike with letter cards, sort the word cards hike, hikes, bone, bones, hole, holes. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the draft marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Check each folder holds the notes, the frame page and the feedback note. List by name the children who stopped with no closing (error F) for day 103's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: write each feedback note on a scrap and tape it to the draft. One child: read both pages, add a second fact with its label and a closing, say the feedback yourself, and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No second page: use any other page about the same bird. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation, the stations and the partner reading.

- **10 min. Second fact.** Read the second page aloud. **Say.** "Which line says your next fact?" Expect: a finger on a line that adds to the same beak.
- **10 min. Closing.** Your child prints the second fact with its label, then a closing. **Say.** "What is the paragraph about? Say it again at the end." Expect: the bird's beak, going back to the topic.
- **5 min. Note.** You write one note: a fact to check and one thing done well.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints R r, V v and "Dave rode a bike."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X102a]] [[s03-X102b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points to the picture, then says a closing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the second fact, the closing, the exit and the cycle work; cut the partner reading and write the notes only for the heard six. Longer: a child adds a second closing in other words.

### Words and supports

Words: second, strengthen, closing, check. Frames: "A new fact is ___." "Source ___ says ___." "So the beak helps ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick a fact to add and who can close by returning to the topic. A child who stopped with no closing (error F) is named for day 103's five-minute repair; one who chose a picture the card does not give (error B) is asked for the line first. Day 103 revises the paragraph from the feedback note and fills a research organizer.
