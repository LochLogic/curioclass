# Day 39: Words that show a feeling or a sense

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the chart and its six word cards, the exit pictures and a copy of Sam's Box for each child. Draw three picture cards for the repair: Frog sitting on a log, Frog jumping into a pond, Frog swimming. Write the six names for today's exit and rotation group 1. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 15 min. Reread for feeling and sense words

Whole class on the rug. Say aloud: "This is a story the Efik and Ibibio people of southern Nigeria tell." Review: you know this tale in order from day 38. Read it whole, showing each picture (5 minutes). Review: on day 37 you looked at the face card and the ear card. A feeling word tells how someone feels inside. A sense word tells what your eyes, ears or skin tell you (2 minutes). Hang the chart: a face box, a sense box with an ear, a hand and an eye drawn on it, and a house box for words that only name a thing (1 minute).

Picture talk on pages 3 and 4 (7 minutes). **Ask.** "How does the Moon feel when she meets the Sun at the door?" Expect: happy. Read the words again: "a broad smile". **Ask.** "What does knee-deep water feel like?" Expect: wet, deep, up to the knees. Say: "Those words help a listener picture it." Children say each phrase with you.

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair first (4 minutes, from day 38's exit, error A). Read a made-up line: "Frog sat on a log. Frog jumped into the pond. Frog swam to the reeds." Lay the three pictures in the wrong order. A pair says first, next, last and fixes it, with a finger on the time words. Then three more children fix it. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge and give the questions the whole ten minutes.

**Ask.** "Which words tell how someone feels?" Expect: a broad smile. Read page 3 slowly. Children put a hand on their face when they hear the feeling words (6 minutes). Partners point to the picture that shows the smile. Hear three pairs, one minute each.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, with the turn card. Review: you used the turn card in unit 1. One turn each, pass allowed, about one minute a child, and the partner adds "I heard ___ too." Say: "Which words help you feel what it was like?" Starter: "The word ___ helps me picture ___." Hear four pairs. Look-fors: each child waits; one names a word from the tale; one says what it helps picture. Practice only; nothing here is scored.

#### 20 min. Feeling and sense words

Model (5 minutes). Hold up the word card "broad smile" and read it. **Ask.** "Does it tell how someone feels, what you can hear, see or feel, or does it only name a thing?" Expect: how someone feels. Put it in the face box. Do the same with "knee-deep" (sense box, hand) and "house" (house box). Say: "A word that names a thing is not a feeling word. House is in the house box; broad smile is in the face box." This is error C, said aloud before anyone makes it.

Sort (4 minutes). The class sorts the other three cards, read aloud, one child to place each: huge (sense, eye), splash (sense, ear), fish (house box). Each time the class says why.

Draw (4 minutes). Each child picks one word from the chart and draws it, then adds a word label with the map or asks you to write it. Say: "Tell a partner what your word helps a listener picture" (3 minutes, pairs, one turn each).

Hear the six, 30 seconds each (3 minutes), while the others draw a second word. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Look-fors: (1) puts the word in the right box; (2) says what it helps a listener picture.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read two lines from the poem *Moon's Walk*, page 4: "At last, the morning comes. I fade to pale, and smile." Say: "Find the feeling word in these lines and tell what it helps you imagine about the moon." Every child tells a neighbor; hear the rotating six, 20 seconds each ([[s02-X39v2]]). A good answer is smile, and a happy or glad moon. Pale tells what the moon looks like and morning is a thing: both are error C. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen. These marks go to day 40.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken pairs: the class shows a fist for the vowel in bed (like egg) and wiggling fingers for the vowel in bad (like ant). Pairs: bed and bad, men and man, pet and pat, set and sat. Blend four sounds: /s/ /t/ /e/ /p/, "step"; tap each sound, then say the word. Review: in kindergarten you listened for the endings in bank, pink and junk. Today we only listen: the class says each word, then the last two sounds together (/ng/ /k/). Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Short e (3 minutes). Review: you learned short e in kindergarten; the picture is an egg and the sound is /e/, as at the start of egg. Add e to the map. Spell on sound boxes (3 minutes): bed, red, net and ten. One counter for each sound, then the letters. Mapped words (3 minutes): said and see. Say: "The part to remember in said is ai: it says /e/." In see, the two e letters say one sound. The class reads each card twice. Then read pages 1 and 2 of Sam's Box with a finger under each word (1 minute).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: each child prints capitals O to V into the letter-form record (4 minutes) and adds the day. Revise G to N with a finger on the page. A form not yet made is incomplete, never wrong. Dictation (4 minutes): say "Sam has a red net." Children print a word at a time with the map, then point to the capital and the period (2 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. The group that needs the vowel again from day 38's sample (error K, a wrong vowel, or error M, a letter left out) uses its first 3 minutes on the repair. Say pot, isolate the vowel /o/ and point to o on the map; blend /p/ /o/ /t/. Then say pet, isolate /e/, point to e and blend again. The other groups read one page of Sam's Box aloud, then spell bed and net on sound boxes. The other children rotate through stations: reread Sam's Box with a partner; chain bed, red, rod, rot, not and net with letter cards; spell wet and jet on sound boxes; draw and label a feeling or a sense word from the tale. No child waits more than one rotation without a station.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the word marks to the unit 2 record sheet and write the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name tomorrow's six and the new foundations groups.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the chart and six word cards on scrap paper. One child: hear the tale in two sittings, sort the six cards with a grown-up, draw one word, answer the exit by pointing, spell bed and net.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write the six words on scraps. No letter cards: write the letters on scraps.

**Skip.** The group talk and the rotation.

- **10 min. Words.** Read pages 3 and 4 of the tale aloud. Your child puts a hand on their face for a feeling word.
  **Say.** "Which words help you feel what it was like?" Expect: broad smile, knee-deep.
- **10 min. Sort.** Your child sorts the six word cards into the face, sense and house boxes, then draws one.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child spells bed and net, then "Sam has a red net."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X39v2]] You read two lines from the poem Moon's Walk: "At last, the morning comes. I fade to pale, and smile." Your child finds the feeling word and tells what it helps them imagine about the moon, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the sort, the exit and the rotation; cut the talk to one pair. Longer: a child who finishes finds a second word on page 5 and sorts it.

### Words and supports

Words: feeling, sense, smile, deep, picture, face, hand, ear, eye. Frames: "The word ___ helps me picture ___." "___ tells how someone feels." Pictures first; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers; a scribe for labels. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 40 retells the tale and says why it is a story. If many children pointed to the pale moon or the morning sky on today's exit (error C), the first minutes of day 40's Read part go to a four-minute repair: reread the line from page 3 and mark the face on the word that says how someone feels.
