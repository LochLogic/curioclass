# Day 92: Gather feather notes from two pages and a picture

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 91's exit marks and name today's heard six. Print the student pages, the note cards, the class duck picture and the exit pictures and card. Put the label chart on the wall. Set out the word picture cards, a turn card for each pair, the sound-spelling map, letter cards, the sentence pictures, the reader Ben's Donut and the record sheet. The exit pictures and card stay face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This page is made up for practice." Day 91's exit showed the cover used in place of a feature (error D) and a meaning the glossary does not give (error B). Use a fresh page: the contents of Feet That Help, the entry Feet for Grasping, page 2, with the cover put away. All children trace the route in the air: entry, page, heading, fact (2 minutes). **Ask.** "What does the glossary say a claw is?" Expect: a sharp, curved nail at the end of a toe. Say the rule: "Say what the glossary says, not what you already know." Hear six children, 20 seconds each, trace the route on their own copy (2 minutes). If many made error D on day 91's exit, repeat with the contents of Wings in Motion, the entry Wide Wings Glide.

#### 12 min. Words, then read Duck at the Pond

Show the word picture cards, about 20 seconds a word: mallard, a kind of duck; pond, a small pool of water; ridge, a thin raised line; dry, not wet (review, from day 91). Read the whole of Duck at the Pond aloud (about 150 words, about 2 minutes), pausing at each heading and pointing to it. Review: a heading shows where to look, from day 81. Reread the Feathers section with fingers under the words, the class saying it with you (2 minutes). **Ask.** "Which heading would you go to for how a duck stays warm?" Expect: Feathers. All children point to it on their copy at once.

#### 8 min. Compare two sources

Review: the two columns from day 90, and the label chart from day 91. Put Feathers Protect, the paragraph Feathers for Water, beside Duck at the Pond, the section Feathers. The label for the first is F and for the second is D. Partners point to what both pages say and what only one page says, taking turns with the turn card: both say the duck's feathers keep water out, so the duck stays dry; only D says down lies close and keeps the duck warm. Pairs hold only the paragraph Feathers for Water, not the rest of page 2 (the page Down for Warmth says it too), so say: "Find the page that says it." All pairs work at once, 4 minutes. Hear four pairs, one minute each. If a pair says a fact neither page gives, point to the line together and ask "Which page says it?"

#### 20 min. Gather notes

Say what is made: two notes about feathers and one picture observation. Show the class duck picture. A note comes from a page and carries its label. An observation comes from the picture and is kept in its own column, because a picture is not a page. Each child makes two note cards about feathers, one from F and one from D: a small picture, one word and the label, such as water, warm, down or dry. Then, on the student page, the child draws one thing seen in the class duck picture under "I saw" and copies one article fact beside it under "The article says", with its label. Fourteen minutes making. Hear six children, one minute each (6 minutes): "My note is ___. It is from ___." The others finish and swap notes with a partner to check the labels. A note with no label is not yet used.

#### 10 min. Talk

Partners with the turn card say: "Which source says it?" The speaker reads a note, the listener points to the line on the right page and says its label. Review: restate before you reply, from day 88. All fifteen pairs talk at once, two turns each, about 40 seconds a turn (3 minutes). Then hear six pairs, 50 seconds each (5 minutes). Two minutes settle the cards. Pass is allowed. Mark: shown, shown with help (you say the first words), not yet seen. Planned question: "What does each source say about the feathers?"

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud the part of Duck at the Pond headed Feet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures: a duck's webbed feet, a splash of water, a duck's beak. Say: "Point to the picture this part is mostly about." ([[s03-X92av2]]) Then say "This card is made up for practice", read the card with two sources labeled X and Y, and ask: "Tell me one note from X and one from Y, and say which is which." ([[s03-X92b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread it once), not yet seen. A child who points to the splash has picked a detail in place of the topic (error A). A child who gives a claim neither source makes has said more than the sources (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say pin and pine; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: pine. Blend /b/ /l/ /o/ /k/, "block". Say dress and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /d/, /e/, /s/. Segment trick: /t/ /r/ /i/ /k/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 14, lesson 74. Read and write connected sentences with the cycle patterns. Show two sentence cards and read them with the class, one at a time: "Ben has a robot." "The robot is at the motel." After each, show two pictures and ask which matches, to check the meaning. Words to map first if a child stalls: robot, motel, Ben; the, has, is and a are mapped. Review: an open syllable ends in a vowel that says its name, from day 89, and a closed syllable ends in a consonant, from day 79. Say the first part of robot and motel and whether it is open or closed. Silent e and vowel teams are not taught yet.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs N n and R r (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate two sentences from the cycle: "Ben has a robot." "Bo has a donut." Say each whole, then a word at a time. Say that Ben and Bo are names, so each begins with a capital letter. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread on taught open words (be, hi, no, robot, motel), about 40 seconds a word. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Ben's Donut, reread for expression: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Ben's Donut, chain words with letter cards, sort open and closed word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the talk marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who picked a detail in place of the topic (error A) for day 93's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the duck on scrap paper and read the articles from the page. One child: read both pages, make one note from each with its label, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No duck picture: use any bird picture. No second page: use two bird books or two bird pages. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the feathers part of two bird pages aloud. Ask which words are the same in both.
- **10 min. Notes.** Your child makes two note cards, a picture, one word and the page name on each. **Say.** "Which page says it?" Expect: your child points to the line and names the page.
- **5 min. Look.** Look at a bird picture. Your child draws one thing seen, in a separate box from the page facts.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X92av2]] [[s03-X92b]] Read the part of Duck at the Pond headed Feet, then the card the page shows. Your child points, then says one note from each source.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the notes, the exit and the cycle work; cut the compare to pairs only. Longer: a child finds a third note, such as feet or beak, on Duck at the Pond and adds its label.

### Words and supports

Words: mallard, pond, ridge, source, note. Frames: "Source ___ says ___." "I saw ___. The article says ___." "My note is ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick the topic from two sources and name where each note came from. A child who picked a detail in place of the topic (error A) is named for day 93's five-minute repair, and a claim neither source makes (error B) is heard there too. Day 93 uses these notes to draft a feather paragraph together.
