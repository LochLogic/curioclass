# Day 91: Read Feathers Protect closely and find evidence in its features

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 90's exit marks and name today's heard six. Draw the fresh repair cards. Print the note cards, the label chart, the student pages and the exit card with its three pictures. Put out the word picture cards, a turn card for each pair, the sound-spelling map, letter cards, the reader Ben's Donut and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." The commonest miss on day 90's exit was error A: the thing a part works on was picked in place of the body part. Lay out three cards: a hawk's foot, sharp claws, and a hawk holding food. **Ask.** "Which card names the part we are researching? The other two tell about it." Expect: the hawk's foot. Slide the two detail cards under it. Say the topic again with the class: "The topic is the hawk's foot. Sharp claws and holding food are details." Say the rule: "The part is the topic. What it works on is a detail." If many made error A on day 90's exit, repeat with a duck's flat beak as the topic, and the details strain mud and dip in the pond.

#### 12 min. Words, then close reading

Show the word picture cards one at a time and say each meaning once, about 20 seconds a word: wet and dry; compare, to say what is the same and what is different; gather, to pick up from many places; record, to write or draw it so it is kept; support, a fact that backs up what you say; glossary and label (review, from day 83). Read Feathers Protect in three passes. First pass, what it says: read the whole article, then each child tells a partner one fact. Second pass, where the features sit: children point to the contents, a heading, the diagram label and the glossary. Third pass, why a fact is true. **Ask.** "How do we know down keeps a bird warm?" Expect: the page says down traps air, and trapped air keeps the bird warm. The line "smooth its feathers down" uses down as a direction; the glossary's down is soft feathers, so look at which one the sentence needs.

#### 12 min. Evidence in the features, taught

Review: the contents and the heading from day 81, and the glossary and the label from day 83. Model, four steps. Question: "How does down keep a bird warm?" One, the contents sends you to Down for Warmth, page 2. Two, the heading Down for Warmth sits over the paragraph that answers. Three, the glossary says what a word means: down is soft feathers that lie close to the body; trap is to hold in. Four, the label down on the diagram points at the soft feathers in the cutaway. Then the fact: "Down traps air. The trapped air helps keep the bird warm." Say: "The heading shows where to look. The glossary says what a word means. The label shows which part." Pairs then ask each other with a turn card: "How does a duck stay dry?" They go to the heading Feathers for Water, point to the sentence, and name the glossary word feather as the part. All fifteen pairs work at once, 4 minutes. Hear three pairs, one minute each, while the others work. If a pair goes to the cover, ask which feature tells them where to look (error D).

#### 8 min. Three questions

Pairs ask and answer these spoken questions, one at a time, two turns each; read each twice.

1. "Where does the heading send you?" Expect: to the paragraph that answers the question.
2. "What does the glossary tell you?" Expect: what a word means.
3. "How do the label and the words fit together?" Expect: the label names the part, the words tell what it does.

All pairs talk at once, about 20 seconds a turn (3 minutes). Hear four pairs, one minute each.

#### 8 min. Talk

Partners with the turn card say, with a finger on the page: "What is your evidence?" The listener points to the heading, the label or the glossary line that the speaker used, then swaps. All pairs talk at once for four minutes. Hear four pairs, one minute each. Pass is allowed. Mark: shown, shown with help (you say the first words), not yet seen.

#### 10 min. A source note

Review: a source label says where a note came from, from unit 2. Show the label chart: B for Beaks Find Food, F for Feathers Protect, D for Duck at the Pond (read on day 92). Model one note card: a picture of down, the word warm, the label F. Each child makes one note card about a different fact from Feathers Protect, a small picture, one word and the label F copied from the chart: fluff, cool, strong, or dry. Six minutes making. Then hear six children, 40 seconds each (4 minutes): "My note is ___. It is from ___." The others finish and colour.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures. Say: "Point to the picture that matches the fact you found." ([[s03-X91a]]) Then, with the card in view: "How do the heading and the glossary help you explain the fact?" ([[s03-X91b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. The word on the card is new today and the card says what it means. A child who points to the cover has used the cover alone (error D). A child who gives a meaning the glossary does not give has said more than the card (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say pet and Pete; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: Pete. Blend /s/ /k/ /u/ /m/, "scum". Say drum and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /d/, /u/, /m/. Segment stamp: /s/ /t/ /a/ /m/ /p/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 14, lesson 73. Review: open and closed syllables, from days 89 and 79. Build with letter cards and take apart: ro | bot, do | nut. Then the chain, one at a time: open, basic, motel, robot. Clap, chop, say the vowel in each part and whether the part is open or closed: o | pen, ba | sic, mo | tel. Children write each word in two parts with a finger on the desk. About 40 seconds a word. Silent e and vowel teams are not taught yet. The sound-spelling map and the syllable chart (closed, open) stay up.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs K k and R r (every letter form was taught by day 27). Decode, then spell, two words from the chain: open and basic. Dictate: "A robot is on the desk." Say it whole, then a word at a time; a, is, on and the are mapped. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; robot is two parts. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread on taught open words (me, we, no, go, donut), about 40 seconds a word. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Ben's Donut, reread for meaning and expression: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Ben's Donut, chain words with letter cards, sort open and closed word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the talk marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who used the cover alone (error D) for day 92's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the note cards on scrap paper and read the article from the page. One child: find one fact by its heading, make one note card with its label, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: use any bird book with a heading. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Read.** Read Down for Warmth aloud. Point to its heading and the glossary line.
- **10 min. Evidence.** **Say.** "Where does the heading send you? What does the glossary tell you?" Expect: the heading shows where to look; the glossary says what a word means.
- **5 min. Note.** Your child draws one small picture, adds one word and writes the label F.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X91a]] [[s03-X91b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points, then says how the heading and glossary helped.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the model, the note and the exit; cut the three questions to one. Longer: a child finds the same fact by a label on the diagram and says which feature was quickest.

### Words and supports

Words: compare, gather, record, support, glossary, label. Frames: "The heading shows ___." "The glossary says ___." "My evidence is ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can use the heading and the glossary to explain a fact. A child who used the cover alone (error D) is named for day 92's five-minute repair, and a meaning the glossary does not give (error B) is heard again there. Day 92 reads Duck at the Pond and gathers notes from two pages, each with its label.
