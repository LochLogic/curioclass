# Day 94: Add a closing and check the paragraph against its notes

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 93's exit marks and sample marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Print the student pages and the exit card. Make the planted draft and the three note cards, and mark the wrong line in your own folder before class. Keep the class paragraph from day 93 on the wall. Put out the syllable chart, letter cards, the sound-spelling map, the reader Ben's Donut and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 93's exit showed a detail picked in place of the topic (error A). Lay out three cards: a duck's flat beak, dips in mud, and strains out food. **Ask.** "Which card is the topic? The other two tell about it." Expect: the duck's flat beak. Slide the two detail cards under it. Say the topic again with the class: "The topic is the duck's flat beak." Say the rule: "The topic is the part. The facts tell about it." If many made error A on day 93's exit, repeat with a sparrow's thick beak as the topic and the details cracks seeds and eats the food inside.

#### 10 min. The closing, taught

Review: the paragraph-frame from day 93, and a closing sentence that says the job, from unit 1 (days 27 and 30). Today the last box gets filled. Read two endings for the same two facts, "Feathers help birds fly. Feathers keep birds warm. And." and the same two facts with "Feathers help birds in many ways." **Ask.** "Which one finishes the paragraph? How do you know?" Expect: the second, because it comes back to the topic; the first just stops. Say the rule: "A closing goes back to the topic." Then model one closing for the class paragraph from day 93: "Feathers help the duck in many ways." Write it in the closing box. Every child says a closing of their own to a partner, all at once, 2 minutes.

#### 15 min. Revise against sources

Say: "This draft is made up for practice. One line in it is wrong." Show the planted draft, three lines, and the three note cards beside it: round wings; wings flap; moves through the air. Read the draft aloud with the class. Then go line by line and ask each time "Which note says it?" Pairs point to the note; all pairs work at once, 4 minutes. Hear three pairs, one minute each. The class finds the line no note backs, "Its long wings help it glide." Say the rule: "A fact stays only if a note backs it." Then fix it together: take out the line and write one a note does back, "Its round wings flap to move it through the air." Now check the class paragraph from day 93 the same way, fact by fact, pointing to its notes and their labels. Review: the label on a note, from day 91.

#### 20 min. Closing and revision

Fourteen minutes. Each child prints a closing sentence in the last box of the paragraph-frame page, copying from the word cards where needed. Then the child rereads their own fact and points to its note. The child changes one detail: a word the note does not say, a vowel left out, or a missing source label. The child says: "I changed ___ because the note says ___." Six minutes: hear six children, one minute each, while the others finish. A child who stops with no closing has left the paragraph unfinished (error F): ask "What is the paragraph about? Say that again at the end." A child whose closing is a new fact is asked to point to its note.

#### 5 min. Reread

The class reads the finished class paragraph aloud together, with you pointing under each word, topic, two facts and the closing. Planned question: "How does the ending finish the paragraph?" Expect: it goes back to the topic, feathers help the duck.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures. Say: "Point to the part picture that the draft should use." ([[s03-X94a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Say a closing sentence that finishes the paragraph." ([[s03-X94b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the feathers has kept a part the card does not give the job to (error B). A child who says "and then it went home" has stopped with no closing that returns to the topic (error F).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say tap and tape; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: tape. Blend /f/ /l/ /a/ /m/, "flame". Say plan and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /p/, /a/, /n/. Segment grab: /g/ /r/ /a/ /b/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 15, lesson 76, explicit teaching first: a_e. New today: the silent e at the end makes the a say its name. Show pairs on cards, one pair at a time: tap and tape, can and cane, mad and made. Say each word and tap the sounds: tap has /t/ /a/ /p/, a short a; tape has /t/ /a/ /p/ with a long a, and the e is silent. Say the contrast: a closed syllable has one vowel and ends in a consonant; silent e has a vowel, a consonant, then e. The syllable chart now has three columns: closed, open and silent e. Children say which column each word belongs in. Review: the endings -s, -ing and -ed on taught bases, from unit 2: tugs, mixing, kicked. Read each with its ending sounded. The reader Ben's Donut is reread in the rotation until the cycle's patterns are mapped. About 40 seconds a word.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs J j and K k (every letter form was taught by day 27). Decode, then spell, two words: tape and made. Dictate: "Jake has a cake." Say it whole, then a word at a time; has and a are mapped, and Jake is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Jake and cake the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names. Three minutes, for error M on day 93's sample: stretch and map every sound, then write the word, on fresh taught words, clap, stem, plum, one at a time, about 30 seconds a child. Then they map, blend, read, spell and reread for the rest of their ten minutes. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Ben's Donut, the cycle 14 reader: each child reads a page and spells a taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Ben's Donut, chain words with letter cards, sort closed, open and silent e word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the revision marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who stopped with no closing (error F) for day 95's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the planted draft and the notes on scrap paper. One child: read the draft, find the line no note backs, fix it, print a closing, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No draft: read your child's own two sentences from day 93. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Closing.** **Say.** "Which ending finishes the paragraph: one that goes back to the topic, or one that just stops?" Expect: the one that goes back to the topic.
- **10 min. Check.** Read your child's two sentences. Your child points to the note that backs the fact and changes one detail.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints a closing sentence.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X94a]] [[s03-X94b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points to the part, then says a closing sentence.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the closing teaching, the planted draft, the exit and the cycle work; cut the class paragraph check. Longer: a child writes a closing for a different part, such as wings, and points to its note.

### Words and supports

Words: closing, revise, check, note. Frames: "The ending is ___." "No note says ___." "I changed ___ because the note says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick the part the facts give the job to and who can finish a paragraph by going back to the topic. A child who stopped with no closing (error F) is named for day 95's five-minute repair. Day 95 reads the beak article and names its topic and key details.
