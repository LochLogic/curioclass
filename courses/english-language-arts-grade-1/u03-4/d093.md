# Day 93: Draft a feather paragraph together

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 92's exit marks and name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Draw the four boxes of the paragraph-frame on chart paper. Print the paragraph-frame pages and the student pages. Print the word cards for copying. Put the class notes from day 92 where all can see them. Take the cycle 14 sample words from your folder; they stay in your hands. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Day 92's exit showed a detail picked in place of the topic (error A) and a claim neither source makes (error B). Lay out three cards: a sparrow's feet, four toes, and grip a branch. **Ask.** "Which card is the topic? The other two tell about it." Expect: the sparrow's feet. Slide the two detail cards under it. Say the topic again with the class: "The topic is the sparrow's feet." Then: "Does a page say sparrows swim?" Point to the page: it does not, so it stays out. Say the rule: "The topic is the part. A fact needs a page to point to." If many made error A on day 92's exit, repeat with a duck's webbed feet as the topic.

#### 5 min. Reread notes

Pairs reread their two note cards and the picture observation from day 92, taking turns. Each pair picks one fact it can point to, with its label. All fifteen pairs work at once. Walk among them.

#### 10 min. The frame, taught

Review: the paragraph-frame (topic, fact, fact, closing) from unit 1, days 21 and 22. Today the class writes the topic and two facts; the closing comes tomorrow. Show the four boxes. Model one topic sentence on a different part: "Wings help birds move." Say: "The topic sentence names the part and what it does." Contrast two sentences about wings: "I like wings." and "Wings push the air." **Ask.** "Which can go in the paragraph? Why?" Expect: "Wings push the air", because a page says it; "I like wings" is a liking, not a fact. Say the rule: "A fact has a note and a label behind it."

#### 25 min. Joint draft

Ten minutes, the class draft. Pairs offer a topic sentence and a fact aloud (2 minutes). Take one topic and two facts the notes can back, and write them in the boxes on the chart as you say each word: "Feathers help a duck." "Down keeps it warm." "Feathers keep the water out." For each fact ask "Which note says it?" and point to the note and its label. Then stretch and map the words with taught patterns, one counter for each sound, the class together: help, duck, it, out. Say each, move a counter for each sound, write a letter for each counter. Words with untaught patterns are copied from the word cards: feathers, down, keeps, warm, water. Review: stretch and map every sound, from day 89.

Fifteen minutes, each child's page. Each child prints the topic sentence in the first box and one fact in the second box of the paragraph-frame page, copying the cards where needed. Then the child says why the fact is there, pointing to the note and its label: "My fact is ___ because the note says ___." Hear six children, one minute each, while the others print. A child who prints only the consonants has left the vowels out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?" A fact with no note behind it is moved to the side and a note is found first.

#### 10 min. Read back

The class reads the draft aloud with you pointing under each word (3 minutes). Then check it fact by fact: point to the topic sentence, then to a note, and ask "Does this note back this fact?" The class answers together, yes or no. Planned question: "How does each fact keep to the topic?" Expect: each fact tells about feathers on the duck. Fifteen pairs then read their own two sentences to each other, all at once, 3 minutes.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures. Say: "Point to the picture that is the topic of a paragraph about this card." ([[s03-X93a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Say a topic sentence for it, with one fact." ([[s03-X93b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to one bright color has picked a detail in place of the topic (error A). A child who gives a claim the card does not make has said more than the card (error B).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say mad and made; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: made. Blend /s/ /p/ /i/ /t/, "spit". Say trunk and the class says the segments: /t/ /r/ /u/ /n/ /k/. The cycle progress sample is taken on the rotation today.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 14, lesson 75. The progress sample is taken on the rotation. Today's class work is a review sort with the sort cards: robot, hotel, basic, motel, open, donut; each child says the first part and whether it is open or closed, in a chorus, all at once. Review: open and closed syllables, from days 89 and 79. Then chain, one at a time on letter cards: be, we, no, hi. Teach only the cycle's correspondences and mapped words. The held sample words are not used here.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs T t and H h (every letter form was taught by day 27). Dictate: "The robot is in the hotel." Say it whole, then a word at a time; the, is and in are mapped. Children stretch each word, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap, marking every sound. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?" A word with a missing vowel is stretched again beside the same word with every sound written.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names. For their first five minutes they map, blend, read, spell and reread; for the last five they take the sample, 50 seconds each. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, one child at a time, 50 seconds each: show the held word card of [[s03-A13]] and say "Read this word." Take the card away and say the dictated word of [[s03-A14]] once in a sentence and once alone: "Write the word I say." Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child who writes only the consonants has left the vowels out (error M). The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Ben's Donut, chain words with letter cards, sort open and closed word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the talk marks, the sample marks and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Use today's results to name tomorrow's catch-up six: this is the same-block regrouping. List by name the children who picked a detail in place of the topic (error A) for day 94's repair, and those who left the vowels out (error M) for day 94's three-minute rotation repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the four boxes on scrap paper. One child: choose one fact from the notes, say a topic sentence and the fact aloud, and print them while you scribe the rest. Do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No notes: use a bird page and say one fact from it. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The sample, the rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Notes.** Look at your child's two note cards from day 92 and the labels on them.
- **10 min. Topic.** **Say.** "Say one sentence that tells what the paragraph is about, then one fact a note backs." Expect: a topic such as feathers help a duck, then a fact with a note and a label.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints the topic sentence and one fact; copy the hard words from a card.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X93a]] [[s03-X93b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points, then says a topic sentence with one fact.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the frame, the exit and the sample; cut the read back to the chart only. Longer: a child writes a third fact from a note and names its label.

### Words and supports

Words: paragraph, topic, fact, note, label. Frames: "The topic is ___." "My fact is ___." "The note says ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. A child who prints with a scribe still says the sentence.

### Tomorrow

The exit and the sample tell you who can name a topic and a fact, and who can read and spell the held words. A child who picked a detail in place of the topic (error A) is named for day 94's five-minute repair, and a vowel left out (error M) for the three-minute rotation repair. Day 94 adds a closing, finds a planted wrong fact and starts silent e.
