# Day 90: Compare two pages and ask a question a page can answer

## Teacher page

### Before class

Name the heard six and the six catch-up children from day 89's cycle record. Copy day 89's exit marks. Print the research question strips, the student pages, the exit card and its three pictures. Chart paper: two columns headed Beaks Find Food and Feathers Protect. Put out the research-cycle chart, the sound-spelling map, a turn card for each pair, letter cards, the reader Ben's Donut and the record sheet. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This page is made up for practice." Day 89's exit showed two misses: the cover used in place of a feature (error D) and a fact the page does not say (error B). Use a fresh page: the contents of Wings in Motion, with the entry Fast Hummingbird Wings, page 2, and the cover put away. **Ask.** "Which feature tells you where to look for how a hummingbird stays in one place?" Expect: the contents entry, then the heading. All children trace the route in the air, entry, page, heading, fact (2 minutes). Then ask: "Does the page say what a hummingbird eats?" Point to the page: it does not. Say the rule: "The cover tells which book, not which page. Say only what the page says." Hear six children, 20 seconds each, trace the route on their own copy (2 minutes). If many made error D on day 89's exit, repeat with the contents of Feathers Protect, the entry Fluffing Up.

#### 12 min. Compare two sources

Review: the two columns from day 88, where one column held what Mina saw and the other what the article says. Today each column is one article. Read two short pieces aloud, twice each: Flat Beaks for Mud from Beaks Find Food, page 2, and Down for Warmth from Feathers Protect, page 2 (about 2 minutes). Pairs each point to one fact on each page, all at once, taking turns with the turn card (3 minutes). Hear six children, 30 seconds each (3 minutes). Then the class fills the chart (2 minutes): under Beaks Find Food a picture of a beak in mud and the word strain; under Feathers Protect a picture of soft down and the word warm. Say each article's title as you write it: a note says where it came from. **Ask.** "What is the same about the two pages?" Expect: each tells what one body part does for a bird. Pass is allowed.

#### 10 min. Ask a research question, taught

Review: the research-cycle chart (ask, find, note, share) from kindergarten and unit 2. Today is the first step, ask. Say: "A research question is one a page we are given can answer." Model: "How does down help a bird stay warm?" Show the lines in Feathers Protect: "Down traps air. The trapped air helps keep the bird warm." Then two questions no page in our set can answer: "Which bird is the nicest?" and "What will the hawk eat tomorrow?" **Ask.** "Can a page we hold answer it?" Expect: no, for both; a liking or a tomorrow is on no page. Say the check: "Is there a heading or a line that could answer it? If not, it is not yet a research question." Then the frame for the day: "How does a ___ help a bird ___?" **Ask.** "Which page answers: how does a wing help a bird move?" Expect: Wings in Motion, under Wings Push the Air. Children hold up the cover of the page they choose; all at once.

#### 18 min. Pose your question and revise the drawing

Twelve minutes, work. Each child picks one body part, beak, feather, wing or foot, and takes that strip: a picture and the question starter ("How does ___ help the bird eat?", "stay warm?", "move?", "hold on?"). Each of the four questions has a page that answers it. The child copies the starter, fills the blank with the part word from the board, and sets it as the heading. A child who chose foot adds the heading and one source label to the day 87 foot drawing. Any other child draws the chosen part, big and plain, in the frame on today's page. The source label is the title word from the board of the page that answers the question: Beaks, Feathers, Wings or Feet. Walk among the children; labels are copied from the board.

Six minutes: hear six children, one minute each, say "My question is ___. The page that answers it is ___." The others finish and say theirs to a partner. Mark: shown, shown with help (you say the first words), not yet seen. A question no page can answer, such as one about liking, is sent back: "Which heading could answer it?"

#### 10 min. Talk

Partners with the turn card say: "My question is ___. I will look in ___." All fifteen pairs talk at once, two turns each, about 40 seconds a turn (3 minutes). Then hear six pairs, 50 seconds each (5 minutes). Two minutes settle the sheets. Pass is allowed: the partner says theirs and the child points to the page. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Look for: the question names a part and its job, and the page named could hold the answer.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. The others finish their sheets; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures. Say: "Point to the part you would research to learn how the pigeon gets food." ([[s03-X90a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Say one question about that part that a source could answer." ([[s03-X90b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the seed has picked the thing acted on in place of the body part (error A). A question no page can answer, such as one about liking, is not yet a research question.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say rob and robe; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: robe. Blend /s/ /t/ /e/ /m/, "stem". Say flip and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /f/, /i/, /p/. Segment crunch: /k/ /r/ /u/ /n/ /ch/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 14, lesson 72. Review: an open syllable ends in a vowel that says its name, from day 89 (me, we, be, go, no; ro-bot). Show the reader's words on cards: be, no, we, hi, donut, robot. Say each, clap it and chop it: do | nut, ro | bot, the first part open, the second closed. Map the irregular words first: said (ai says /e/) and to (o says /oo/) are new today. Review: was, from day 80 (a says /u/, s says /z/), and his from kindergarten. Chain on letter cards, one at a time: robot, donut, hotel, basic; clap, chop, say the vowel in each part. About 40 seconds a word. Silent e and vowel teams are not taught yet. The sound-spelling map stays up.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs B b and D d (every letter form was taught by day 27). Decode, then spell, two words from the chain: robot and donut. Dictate: "Ben has a donut." Say it whole, then a word at a time; has and a are mapped, and Ben is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children say each word slowly, tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; donut is two parts, do and nut. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap the sounds again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread on taught open words (me, we, no, go, robot), about 40 seconds a word. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table with Ben's Donut, the cycle 14 reader, read for the first time today after its words are mapped. Each child reads a page and spells one taught word on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread Pam's Backpack, chain words with letter cards, sort open and closed word cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the talk marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. List by name the children who pointed to the seed (error A) for day 91's repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the strips on scrap paper and read the articles from the page. One child: pick one part, copy its question, find the page that answers it, and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No articles: use any bird book or a bird picture. No partner: say both parts yourself.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the beak part and the down part of the two bird pages aloud. Ask what each tells about a bird.
- **10 min. Ask.** **Say.** "Which page could answer: how does a wing help a bird move?" Expect: your child names the page about wings.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws one bird part, copies its question above it and writes the page name.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X90a]] [[s03-X90b]] Read the card the page shows. Your child points to the part, then says one question a page could answer.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the question teaching, the exit and the cycle work; cut the talk to pairs only. Longer: a child asks a second question about another part and names its page.

### Words and supports

Words: research, question, source, compare. Frames: "My question is ___. I will look in ___." "A page can answer ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. A child not heard in the six can be heard in a pair.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick the body part for a job and who can ask a question a page can answer. A child who pointed to the seed (error A) is named for day 91's five-minute repair, where details are sorted under one topic again. Day 91 reads Feathers Protect closely and traces each fact to a heading.
