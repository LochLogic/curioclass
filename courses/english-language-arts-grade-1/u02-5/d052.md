# Day 52: What we saw and what we read

## Teacher page

### Before class

For each pair make four cards: a drawing of light on a floor by a window; a drawing of two children with short shadows; and two fact cards, "The sun rises on one side of the sky." and "At the middle of the day, the sun is at its highest." Write the repair card Tess's Boots: "First, Tess put on her boots. Then Tess opened the door. Last, Tess ran out." with three pictures.

### The day

#### 15 min. Reread Sun Across the Sky

Whole class on the rug. Reread pages 2 to 5, one picture pause each, with the shared notebook beside you (5 minutes). Read aloud the notes on the notebook page and the log boxes the class has drawn (3 minutes): the log holds what we saw, and the article holds what we read. Say aloud: "Nobody looks at the sun." Then each child draws box two of the picture log, the sky near the middle of the day, from the window (7 minutes), with a one-word label. No window, or a gray day: draw the sky as page 3 shows it, and say that this box is a picture made for practice.

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair for the children whose day 51 exit showed error A (4 minutes). Say aloud: "This card is made up for practice." Read the repair card Tess's Boots, then lay the three pictures out of order: ran, boots, door. Reread the words first, then, last, and each child places the pictures once. **Ask.** "Which came last?" Expect: Tess ran out.

Then partners look at page 3 and at their own log box (6 minutes). **Ask.** "Which page tells a fact? Which drawing is what we saw?" Expect: page 3 tells that shadows grow short when the sun is high; our log drawing is what we saw. One partner points to the page, the other to the log. Hear four pairs, half a minute each.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, with the turn card, two rounds, pass allowed. Round 1 (3 minutes), one turn each: **Ask.** "How can a drawing and an article help each other?" Starter: "The article tells ___ and our drawing shows ___." Expect: the article tells why and our drawing shows it happening; with both, our answer has two parts. Round 2 (3 minutes), new partners: each says back what the new partner said, then adds "I would add ___". Hear four pairs, half a minute each (2 minutes).

#### 20 min. Two columns

Say: "An observation is what we saw. A fact is what the article says. They go in two columns, and the note names which one it is." Model (4 minutes) on the notes sheet. Say aloud: "This example is made up." Under What we saw: "The sun was behind the roof at going-home time." Under What we read: "The sun sets on the other side of the sky." Each has a source label: log, article.

Pairs move the four cards to the right column (5 minutes), one pair per sheet, talking it through. Walk round; do not move cards for them. Then each child draws one observation from the log beside one fact from the article (8 minutes), with its source label, log or article, and a name with a capital letter. Hear the day's six, half a minute each, while the rest draw (3 minutes): "Which of these did you see?"

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Show two cards: a class picture log card, a drawing of a flag with a long shadow beside it, and a fact card read aloud: "Shadows grow long when the sun is low." Say: "Point to the card that tells what we saw." Every child points; the rotating six also say where the other card comes from ([[s02-X52]]). A good answer is the drawing of the flag. The fact card is error E: it is a source we read, not what we saw. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words, not scored. Segment into sounds with counters: "plum" (four), "spin" (four), "blend" (five). Blend: /t/ /r/ /a/ /p/, "trap"; /g/ /r/ /i/ /n/, "grin". Say "bank" and "pink": both end with two sounds, /ng/ and /k/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Read bank, pink, sick and which from the cards (3 minutes); in pink and bank the n and k say /ng/ then /k/, as on day 43. Spell pink and rock on sound boxes, one sound a box (4 minutes). Pairs reread Sam's Rock, a page each, pointing under each word (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (6 minutes): say "Rick has a pink sock." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped word has is named aloud. Watch the forms R, p and k. Then each child points to the capital and the period (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table: the group reads all of Sam's Rock aloud, a page a child, and tells where the rock was hidden and where Jan found it; then spells tick on sound boxes. The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread Sam's Rock with a partner; chain pink, sink, sick and sock with letter cards; spell bank and deck on sound boxes; read the word cards rock, luck, which and neck to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the repair marks to the unit 2 record sheet with the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name the foundations groups for day 53.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a sheet in two for each pair, and draw the four cards on scrap paper. One child: reread the article page by page, sort the four cards, draw one observation beside one fact, answer the exit by pointing, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No notes sheet: fold a page in two.

**Skip.** The talk pairs and the rotation.

- **10 min. Reread.** Read page 3 of Sun Across the Sky aloud. **Say.** "What does the article say?" Expect: our shadows grow short, because the sun is high.
- **10 min. Two columns.** Your child draws one thing seen from a window, in a left box, and one fact from the article, in a right box. **Say.** "Which one did you see?" Expect: the window drawing.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child reads bank and pink and prints "Rick has a pink sock."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X52]] You hold up your child's window drawing and a card with a sentence from the article. Ask: "Which one tells what you saw?" Expect: the drawing.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair for your own misses, the four-card sort and one drawing, the exit and the rotation; cut the talk to one round. Longer: a child who finishes adds a second pair of notes with source labels.

### Words and supports

Words: observation, fact, article, log, seen, read. An observation is what we saw; a fact is what the article says. Frames: "I saw ___." "The article says ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can tell what we saw from what we read (error E: a source taken for an observation). Day 53 builds a two-source answer. Its repair part opens with four minutes on error E for the children who need it: point to what we saw, then to what we read.
