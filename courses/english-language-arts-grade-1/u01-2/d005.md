# Day 5: Reading the hammer poem with my voice

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print The Hammer Song and Builders at Work in large print. Hang the eye and ear signs and the sound-spelling map. Lay out the turn cards, the six word cards, the exit line (it is said aloud, no picture), the place signs and the chime. Today's six are the next six names on the record sheet. Nobody handles a real hammer; the teacher alone may show one.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read and join

Whole class on the rug. First 4 minutes, a repair for day 4's exit (error V). Say: "A hammer is a thing." Ask: "Is that all we can say about a hammer?" Then add the kind and the use: "A hammer is a builder tool for nails." Review: the pattern "A ___ is a ___ tool for ___" is from day 4. Everyone says the new sentence together, then to a neighbor.

Then the poem. Show The Hammer Song in large print and read it whole, twice, a finger under each word (56 words, about 30 seconds a reading). Content note: a hammer, nails and boards; nothing frightening. Say: "Listen to my voice. I say Tap, tap, tap softly, like three small taps. I say the little hammer sings as one smooth chunk." A chunk of words said together is a phrase. Show it with a hand sweep under each phrase. Say Bang, bang, bang loud and the big hammer booms in the same way, and Hush. The hammer rests at last slowly and quietly.

Echo reading is from day 2 (review). You read one line, the class echoes it, all twelve lines (4 minutes). Then you read and the class joins the refrains, soft Tap, tap, tap and loud Bang, bang, bang (2 minutes). Point to tap in the print: the class has read tap before and reads it aloud. Bang is echoed, never read from the print.

#### 10 min. Connect poem and article

Reread the hammer paragraph in Builders at Work (about 40 words): "The flat end of a hammer pushes a nail into wood." Show the poem and the article side by side.

**Ask.** Which poem words name the hammer action? Expect: tap, tap, tap and bang, bang, bang. Children point to both in the print. Then: **Ask.** Which article sentence says what the hammer does to the nail? Expect: the flat end pushes a nail into wood. Point to that sentence. Say: "The poem tells how it sounds. The article tells the job." Each pair holds a turn card and tells a neighbor one poem line and one article sentence that go together (4 minutes). Sit with three pairs; a child may pass.

#### 10 min. Act and order words

Words get stronger or weaker. Soft to loud, acted, class together: tap (one finger on a knee), knock (a fist on a palm), bang (a clap). Say the three in order, then mixed; the class acts each. Sizes, ordered with hands: tiny (fingers close together), little, big, gigantic (arms stretched up and out). The poem has tiny things and the big hammer. Pairs say a word and the partner shows the size with hands (3 minutes), then swap. Say it back with a frame: "Knock is louder than tap." "Gigantic is bigger than big." Nobody acts with a real tool.

#### 20 min. One sentence

Hand out the student page. Say: "Draw a job a hammer does." Draw for 8 minutes. Then each child says "A hammer ___" to a partner (3 minutes): for example "A hammer pushes a nail into wood." Then hear about 8 children at 45 seconds each and write their sentence under the picture while the others add color (6 minutes); the rest are written on a later day. The child writes one word label, such as nail (3 minutes). Look-fors: the job comes from the article or the poem; the label matches the drawing.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "I will say a line made up for practice." Say: "Wipe, wipe, wipe, little sponge. Wipe the table clean." Everyone says it back with a wiping hand (1 minute). Then hear today's six, 20 seconds each: "Say it back the way a sponge would wipe." Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X5]]). Good: three phrases, Wipe, wipe, wipe / little sponge / wipe the table clean, with a steady beat. A child who says each word alone with a stop after it reads word by word (error R).

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Review: words that rhyme end with the same sounds, from kindergarten. Say pairs from the poem; a thumb goes up for a rhyme: sings, things (yes); booms, rooms (yes); slow, go (yes); last, fast (yes); slow, last (no). Then say tap slowly, one sound at a time. The class holds up a finger for each sound: three.

#### 10 min. Map and read

Point to the sound-spelling map. Review the words on cards: sat, mat, map, tap and the (day 1). Slide a finger under each word, say its sounds, sweep and say it; the class echoes (4 minutes). Pairs spread the cards; A reads one word and B touches it, then swap (4 minutes; stand by three pairs). Hold up the and a: they are mapped words and are read whole (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Repair first, only if the record shows a gap from day 4 (the letter C or c, the words a cat, or first and last sounds): that small group works up to 10 minutes. Otherwise teach D and d.

Say: "D is a stick with a big curve on the right. The little d is a ball and then a tall stick. b faces the ball and d faces the door." Children trace D and d in the air, then print them. Check for a backward d (error O). Then dictation: say "a map" and children print it, a word at a time.

#### 30 min. Cycle progress sample

Six groups of five, six places, 5 minutes each. At the teacher table each child takes about a minute: reads two word cards from sat, mat, map, tap, pat and nap, reads the whole word the or a, and spells one word you say (for example tap). Mark who was heard and what help was needed on the record sheet. The other five places: sound cards, word building, draw and label, tool pictures, quiet pairs (echo Look in a Book or reread Sam sat). The places and the chime are from day 1. Use the marks to make tomorrow's groups for the next set of sounds.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the poem and the article large by hand; make letter squares from scrap paper. One child: read and echo the poem, connect it to the article, act the words and do the exit; skip the sample.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed poem: read it from the page. No turn card: take turns by passing a pencil.

**Skip.** The sample, the rotation and the class talk.

- **5 min. Read the poem.** Read The Hammer Song twice, a finger under the words. Your child echoes each line, then joins Tap, tap, tap softly and Bang, bang, bang loudly.
  **Say.** "Which words tell the hammer's sound?" Expect: tap, tap, tap and bang, bang, bang.
- **5 min. Act the words.** Tap, knock, bang from soft to loud; tiny, little, big, gigantic with hands.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws a job a hammer does and says "A hammer ___."
- **5 min. Sounds.** Say sings and things, then go and slow. Do they rhyme? Say tap slowly, one sound at a time.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints D d and then the words "a map".
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X5]] Say "Wipe, wipe, wipe, little sponge. Wipe the table clean." Ask: "Say it back the way a sponge would wipe."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the knock and bang acting and hear only five children say their sentence. Keep the two readings, the connect part and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes says a second hammer job and points to the poem line that goes with it.

### Words and supports

Words: hammer, nail, tap, knock, bang, tiny, little, big, gigantic, phrase. Frames: "A hammer ___." "Knock is louder than tap." Pointing, acting and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 6 builds on a partner's tool explanation and asks which part. If many children said each word alone on today's exit (error R), day 6's reread part opens with four minutes: clap each phrase of one article line, then say it.
