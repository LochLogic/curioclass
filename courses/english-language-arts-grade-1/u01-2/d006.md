# Day 6: Building on a partner's words

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print Tools in the Kitchen with its picture. Set out the picture cards for the exits. Hang the eye and ear signs and the sound-spelling map. Lay out Pip, the turn cards, the Pip lines, the picture cards for the exits (the mop card), the four word cards, the reader Sam sat, the place signs and the chime. Today's six are the next six names on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 10 min. Reread the kitchen article

Whole class on the rug. First 4 minutes, a repair for day 5's exit (error R). Show one article line: "A ladle is a kitchen tool for soup." Clap once for each chunk: A ladle / is a kitchen tool / for soup. Then say the line as three chunks, smooth inside each chunk and a small rest between. The class claps and says it with you, then a neighbor does it.

Then reread the ladle paragraph (about 25 seconds) with the picture shown. Content note: a cook, a hot pot of soup, no child handles anything hot. Ask yourself aloud: "What does the paragraph say about the handle?" and touch the line: "The long handle keeps the cook's hand away from the hot pot."

#### 10 min. Model a turn

Pip is a sock puppet. Pip says one line about the ladle: "A ladle lifts soup." Review: you used the turn card in kindergarten; one speaks, then the listener adds. Hold up the turn card.

You add a fact and join it to Pip's: "A ladle lifts soup. The long handle keeps the cook's hand away from the hot pot." Say: "I built on what Pip said." Then ask one question to make it clearer: "Which part is long?" Pip answers: "The handle." Say: "A question helps me understand."

Review from kindergarten: Pip says a line and the class decides if Pip stays on the topic or leaves it. Pip says each line and you ask: does this stay on the topic, or leave it? "A ladle has a deep bowl." (stays) "My cat has a ball." (leaves) "A funnel pours oil into a bottle." (stays) "I like the color blue." (leaves). The class shows a thumb up for stays and a thumb down for leaves. Pip makes every mistake, never a child. Children add one fact that stays on the topic, to a neighbor.

#### 15 min. Partner talk

Fifteen pairs talk at once, with a turn card each. Partner A says one fact about a kitchen tool: "A funnel is wide at the top." Partner B builds on it with a second fact about the same tool, then asks one question: "Which part is narrow?" A answers. Then swap, two linked exchanges each. Sit with three pairs and hear them, about 2 minutes each; move on when you have heard one answer to a question. A child may pass. Say a stray answer back as a question: "Does that tell about the funnel?"

Stop at 12 minutes. Two pairs say their exchange to the class (3 minutes).

#### 20 min. Draw the explained tool

Hand out the student page. Say: "Draw a tool a partner explained today." Draw for 8 minutes. Then each child says "It helps because ..." to a partner (3 minutes): for example "It helps because the long handle keeps the hand away." Hear about 8 children at 45 seconds each while the others add color (6 minutes). The child writes one word label (3 minutes). Look-fors: the reason comes from the article or a partner's fact; the label matches the drawing.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Pip reads: "A mop has soft strings. It soaks up spills." Pip adds: "It helps with cleaning." Show two pictures: a mop on a wet floor and a broom. Ask: "Add one thing about the mop to what Pip said." The whole class tries to a neighbor, then hear today's six, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X6]]). A good answer adds a fact from the card. A child who says "My dog likes the mop" has left the topic (error AE).

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Say a short and a long vowel pair; the class says which has the long, saying-its-name sound: hat, hate; mat, mate; can, cane. Then count the sounds in hat: three. Review: counting the sounds in a word, from day 3. Do not show how the long words are spelled.

#### 10 min. Map short a and th

Point to the sound-spelling map. The sound /a/ is in the map: say a, a, apple; then tap under the letters. The two letters t h make one sound, /th/, as in that. Review: you learned in kindergarten that two letters can make one sound; the name is digraph. Say it: th is a digraph.

Chain on word cards or tiles, one change at a time: at, cat, hat, that. For each, the class says the sounds, then the word. Pairs build the chain with tiles (4 minutes); sit by three pairs.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Repair first, only if the record shows a gap from day 5 (the letter D or d, the words a map, or rhymes): that small group works up to 10 minutes. Otherwise teach E and e.

Say: "E is a stick with three arms. The little e is a circle with a bar and a gap." Children trace E and e in the air, then print them. Then dictation: say "that cat" and children print it, a word at a time. Check as they go.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Six groups of five, six places, 5 minutes each, in new groups. At the teacher table a group reads Sam sat again: "Sam sat. Sam sat at a mat." Each child reads one line alone (about 20 seconds each, 2 minutes in all). The other five places: sound cards, word building, draw and label, tool pictures, quiet pairs. The chime moves everyone one place along.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: read the article from the page and draw the exit pictures by hand. No sock: a paper bag with a drawn face is Pip. One child: read the ladle paragraph, take a turn with Pip, draw and do the exit; skip the rotation.

### At home

**Swap.** No sock puppet: use any toy as Pip. No turn card: take turns by passing a pencil.

**Skip.** The rotation and the class talk.

- **5 min. Reread.** Read the ladle paragraph in Tools in the Kitchen.
  **Say.** "What does the long handle do?" Expect: keeps the cook's hand away from the hot pot.
- **5 min. Build on a turn.** You say one fact about the funnel; your child adds a second. Your child asks "Which part is narrow?"
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws a tool and says "It helps because ..."
- **5 min. Sounds.** Say hat, hate; mat, mate. Which has the long sound?
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints E e and then "that cat".
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X6]] Say "A mop has soft strings. It soaks up spills. It helps with cleaning." Ask: "Add one thing about the mop."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the Pip lines and hear only five children say their reason. Keep the reread, one built turn and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes asks a partner a second question about the tool.

### Words and supports

Words: ladle, handle, funnel, topic, because, clear, digraph. Frames: "It helps because ..." "Which part ...?" Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 7 writes a topic sentence and tool facts from a read-aloud using a paragraph frame. If many children left the topic on today's exit (error AE), day 7's models part opens with four minutes: add to what Pip said, on the mop.
