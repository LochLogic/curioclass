# Day 7: A paragraph about a tool

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut the Classroom Sign Model into four sign-model strips. Ready the topic card and detail cards, the picture cards for the exits and the my tool, three parts sheet. Print Builders at Work. Hang the eye and ear signs and the sound-spelling map. Lay out Pip, the turn cards, the word cards, Pat's Mess, the place signs and the chime. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read the sign model

Whole class on the rug. Read the Classroom Sign Model whole, once, with its picture shown (26 words, about 15 seconds): "We used tools to make our classroom sign. A crayon made the letters dark. Tape joined the paper pieces. The sign helps people find our room." Read it a second time and point to each sentence.

Lay out the four strips. Say: "The first sentence names the topic. It tells what the paragraph is about. The next two sentences are facts. The last sentence is the closing. It says why the tools help." Each strip gets a name card: topic, fact, fact, closing. This pattern of four sentences is a paragraph frame.

**Ask.** Which strip tells what the paragraph is about? Expect: the first. Children point to it. **Ask.** Which strip says what the tape did? Expect: the third. Pairs hold the strips in order and read them aloud with you, a finger under each (6 minutes). Sit with three pairs. A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Teacher models

First 4 minutes, a repair for day 6's exit (error AE). Pip reads: "A mop has soft strings. It soaks up spills." Pip says: "My dog likes the mop." Ask the class: "Does that add to what Pip said about the mop?" Thumb down. Pip tries again: "Its strings soak up water." Thumb up. Say it back: "Add a fact about the mop, not about me."

Then model a paragraph about builders from Builders at Work. Think aloud and say each sentence, then write it on chart paper, one strip at a time:

- Topic: "Builders use tools."
- Fact: "A hammer pushes in nails."
- Fact: "A level shows if a board is flat."
- Closing: "Tools help builders."

Say: "I checked each fact in the article. The closing says why." Point to each sentence while the class says it.

#### 10 min. Partner practice

Pairs say a topic and two facts for the wheelbarrow, from the article, using the turn card: partner A says the topic ("Builders use a wheelbarrow."); B says one fact ("It has one wheel and two handles."); A says the second ("A builder can move bricks and sand."). Then swap the first and the last roles. Fifteen pairs talk at once; sit with three. Say a fact that is not in the article back as a question: "Which sentence says that?"

#### 20 min. My tool, three parts

Hand out the student page. It has three boxes: a tool and its label, a fact, and its job. Say: "Pick one of the nine tools from the three articles: hammer, level, wheelbarrow, hoe, watering can, tractor, ladle, rolling pin, funnel." Draw the tool in box 1 and write a word label (8 minutes). Say a fact aloud to a partner and draw it in box 2 (4 minutes). Draw its job in box 3 (4 minutes). The closing is said aloud, not written: "Tools help people." Each pair says all three parts to each other.

Hear and scribe for today's six at 90 seconds each: write the child's fact under box 2 as they say it (4 minutes). Look-fors: the fact comes from an article; the job matches the fact; the label matches the drawing. The rest are scribed on a later day.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A clip holds our drawings. Its jaws grip the paper. We can find our drawings together." Show two pictures: clipped drawings and a loose pile. Ask: "Tell me one fact about the clip that I could write after the topic." Everyone tells a neighbor, then hear today's six, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X7]]). A good answer is a fact from the card, such as "Its jaws grip the paper." A child who says "It is nice" or "I like it" has given an opinion, not a fact (error AA).

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend and segment, the class holding up a finger for each sound. Say the sounds, the class blends: /m/ /a/ /p/ (map), /n/ /a/ /p/ (nap), /p/ /a/ /t/ (pat). Then say a word, the class segments it: pan, man. Watch for a dropped sound (error I): a child who says "ap" for map gets the first sound back, /m/.

#### 10 min. Mapped words

Review: these are mapped words, read whole because their letters do not follow the sounds. You learned mapped words in kindergarten: the, a, has. Hold up each card; the class says it, then points to the part to remember (the th and e in the, the single letter a, the s in has that says /z/).

Chain on tiles, one change at a time: pat, pan, man, map. For each, the class says the sounds, then the word. Pairs build the chain (4 minutes); sit by three pairs.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Repair first, only if the record shows a gap from day 6 (the letter E or e, the words that cat, or the th sound): that small group works up to 10 minutes. Otherwise teach F and f.

Say: "F is a stick with two arms. The little f has a hook at the top and a bar across." Children trace F and f in the air, then print them. Then dictation: say "a fat cat" and children print it, a word at a time. Check as they go.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Six groups of five, six places, 5 minutes each. At the teacher table the group reads Pat's Mess for the first time: you read it aloud once, then each child reads one line with a finger under the words (about 20 seconds each, 2 minutes in all). The other five places: sound cards, word building, draw and label, tool pictures, quiet pairs. The chime moves everyone one place along.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the four sentences on strips by hand. No sock: a paper bag with a drawn face is Pip. One child: read the sign model, say a topic and two facts, draw the three boxes and do the exit; skip the rotation.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: write the four sentences on separate lines. No turn card: pass a pencil.

**Skip.** The rotation and the class talk.

- **5 min. The sign.** Read the four-sentence sign paragraph twice and point to each sentence.
  **Say.** "Which sentence tells the topic?" Expect: We used tools to make our classroom sign.
- **5 min. Builders.** Say a topic and two facts about the wheelbarrow from the article.
- **5 min. Three boxes.** Your child draws a tool, a fact and the job, and says each part.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Blend /m/ /a/ /p/ and /p/ /a/ /t/.
- **5 min. Print.** Your child prints F f and then "a fat cat".
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X7]] Read "A clip holds our drawings. Its jaws grip the paper. We can find our drawings together." Ask: "Tell me one fact about the clip."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: scribe only three children in My tool, three parts. Keep the sign model, the builders paragraph and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes says a closing for their tool.

### Words and supports

Words: paragraph, topic, fact, closing, grip, tool, because. Frames: "A ___ ..." "It helps because ..." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 8 is the mid-unit check and teaches nothing new. Children who gave an opinion, not a fact, on today's exit (error AA) join a small group in day 9's Talk part, and day 8's record names error AA.
