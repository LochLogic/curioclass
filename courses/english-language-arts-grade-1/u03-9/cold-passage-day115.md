# Day 115 cold passage: Owls, three pages and a printed menu (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Read on day 115 only; the class has never heard it. Keep the pages face down until the check opens. Print the three pages and the menu for each child, with "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of each. The teacher reads page 2, Quiet Feathers, aloud once; children may follow the print. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading. It is read for s03-A27v2, s03-A28v2 and s03-A29v2.

## Page 1

> **Owls**
>
> Contents
> Quiet Feathers, page 2
> Warm Feet, page 2
> Glossary, page 3
>
> Most owls hunt at night. Soft feathers cover an owl's body.

Picture: an owl sitting on a branch at dusk.

## Page 2

> **Quiet Feathers**
>
> An owl's wing feathers have soft, fuzzy edges. The soft edges let the owl fly with almost no sound.
>
> **Warm Feet**
>
> Many owls have feathers on their legs and toes. The feathers help keep their feet warm.

Cutaway diagram: an owl's body with one wing spread, the outer feathers cut away to show the layers, with label lines to "wing feathers" and "leg feathers".

## Page 3

> **Glossary**
>
> edge: the outside line of a thing.
> fuzzy: soft, like fluff.
> hunt: to look for food to catch.

## Printed menu

A frame line like a screen with two choices, each with a small bird icon: Feathers, Feet. A book icon at the corner points to the glossary. Feathers leads to Quiet Feathers on page 2; Feet leads to Warm Feet. It does not show live electronic operation.

Three pictures for s03-A27v2, each alone in a box: the owl's wing feathers; a tree alone; the owl's feet.

The routes to the quiet-flight fact (soft, fuzzy edges on the wing feathers let the owl fly with almost no sound): the contents entry Quiet Feathers, page 2; the heading Quiet Feathers; the label wing feathers on the cutaway diagram; the glossary line edge; the menu choice Feathers and its bird icon; the book icon to the glossary.

Facts checked against National Park Service, Big Bend National Park, "Owls" (soft, comb-like edges and fringes on the flight feathers muffle noise), https://www.nps.gov/bibe/learn/nature/owls.htm (sources.csv g1-u3-f10, checked 2026-11-21). The night-hunting line is checked against the same page (owls hunt at night, when their prey is out). Feathered feet checked against U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, "8 Fascinating Facts About Snowy Owls" (their feet are covered with feathers, which insulate them in the cold Arctic), https://www.fws.gov/story/8-fascinating-facts-about-snowy-owls (sources.csv g1-u3-f51, checked 2026-11-24). The unit's bird texts do not teach owls.
