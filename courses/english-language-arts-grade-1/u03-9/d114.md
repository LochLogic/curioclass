# Day 114: Check each clue by its route

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 113's exit marks and name today's heard six, the pairs for the talk and the six catch-up children whose records show error L, M, N or O. Put each child's route card, clues, strips and two pages on the desk, and return the written feedback on days 112 and 113. Make the repair clues below. Wings in Motion, pages 1 to 3, and the two exit pictures stay face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Repair for day 113's exit (error A, and s03-X113av2; or error E, and s03-X113b). Use the commoner. Say: "These clues are made up for practice." Read: "An eagle has big, wide wings. It can glide on air that rises." Show two topic cards, wings and feet. All children at once, point. **Ask.** "Which topic card holds both clues?" Expect: wings. If error E was commoner, lay three cards and read each once: "I like eagles." "An eagle has big, wide wings." "Eagles are fun." Hear three children say which card is a fact a page supports; the other two are likings and are no clue.

#### 5 min. Words

Show a picture card for each word and say it once, children repeating: verify, route, source, check. Review: to verify is to check a fact in a page (day 97); a route is the steps followed to a fact (day 111); a source is where a fact came from (day 110). Check means to look again and see that it is right. The route card from day 111 is out.

#### 20 min. Verify each clue independently

Review: the route goes from the contents line to the page, then the heading to the sentence; the glossary says what a word means and does not prove the fact (day 111). Each child, alone, takes clue 1 and finds its fact again on the two pages: a finger on the contents line, then the heading, then the sentence that gives the fact; the label or the glossary line only if it helps. On the route card, number the boxes in the order used and print the page on the line. Then the same for clue 2. Give no help. Hear six children, two minutes each, inside the twenty: "Show me the page that says your clue." Expect: a finger on the contents line, then the heading, then the sentence. A child who reaches for the cover is shown the contents line first (error D).

#### 20 min. Revise

Eight minutes alone: each child reads each clue against the sentence on the page and fixes any clue the page does not support (error B), using a sentence from the page; then prints the source label (P1, D2, H1, K2 and so on) in the box on the student page for each clue. Eight minutes in pairs: the partner follows the route for each clue and says "The page says ___." Four minutes: each child tidies the clues, route cards and strips into the riddle folder. The letter pairs are revised in the printing part.

#### 5 min. Talk

Turn card, pairs, all at once, two minutes, then six pairs heard, thirty seconds each, three minutes: "Which route was fastest, and why?" Expect: "Contents then heading, because the contents gave the page." Review: listen, restate ("You said ___"), then reply (day 107). A child may pass; the partner then shows the route with a finger.

#### 5 min. Exit

Show Wings in Motion, pages 1 to 3, and read aloud: "A bird flaps its wings. The wings push the air." Show the two pictures, the contents line Wings Push the Air and the glossary line wing. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which line sends you to this fact? Point to it." ([[s03-X114av2]]) Then, with the pages in view, the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each: **Ask.** "Point to the words that say what the wings do." ([[s03-X114bv2]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread once), not yet seen. A child who points to the glossary only has used it in place of the contents line (error D). A child who says "They keep it warm." has named a fact the page does not state (error B). Name day 114's misses for day 116's repair.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only, on the words the oral record shows were missed on days 112 and 113; if none, use these. Say cap and cape; which has the long vowel sound? Blend /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/. Give the first, vowel and last sound of drum. Segment flag.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Consolidate; no new pattern. Small groups at the sound-spelling map: map, blend, read, spell and reread. Chain hope, hoped, hoping; name, named, naming; note, noted; nicer, nicest. Say each -ed word and tell which sound it ends with: /t/, /d/ or /id/. Spell named and safest, about 40 seconds a word. Review: the three sounds of -ed (day 112); -er and -est on silent-e bases (day 113).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs the written feedback marked on days 108 to 113, revised, kept for 118 (every form was taught by day 27). Error N: show a reversed form beside its correct form; model the start and the direction (d starts at the round part, then the stick); then the child rewrites a different taught letter alone. Then each child tries again the sample word the feedback marked, on a strip. Dictate: "Mike rides the bike." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Mike is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Mike and bike the last e is silent, and the is a mapped word: mark its irregular part and blend it as mapped (error O reads it with a short e). Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names. Error L: counters, one for each sound, on smile. Error M: stretch and map each sound of slide. Error O: mark the irregular part of the and blend it as mapped. Error N: the letter model. Then fresh words already taught. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, about 50 seconds a child; the heard-six column names them. Each child reads one page of A Day at the Lake, then echo-reads one sentence of the chosen bird page after you, a finger under each word. The rest reread and spell in pairs at stations. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six. Write each miss in the cycle record.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the route marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Put the route cards and clues in the riddle folder. List by name the children who used the glossary only (error D), for day 116.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the route card as three boxes on paper and read each page aloud. One child: check two clues by their routes and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: use a short bird page with a contents line, a heading and a glossary line.

**Skip.** The rotation.

- **15 min. Check two clues.** Your child says two clues about a bird from a short page, then finds each one again. **Say.** "Show me the page that says it." Expect: a finger on the contents line, then the heading, then the sentence.
- **5 min. Talk.** Your child says which route was quickest.
- **5 min. Words.** Say hope, hoped, hoping. Your child says what changed.
- **5 min. Print.** "Mike rides the bike."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X114av2]] [[s03-X114bv2]] Read the wing lines of Wings in Motion. Your child points to the line that sends you to the fact, then to the words that say what the wings do.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the route for each clue and the exit; skip the talk. Longer: a child checks a third fact on the other page.

### Words and supports

Words: verify, route, source, check, page. Frames: "I found it by ___." "The page says ___." "You said ___. I think ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can use the contents line to find a fact's page and point to the words that say what a part does. A child who pointed to the glossary only (error D) waits for a five-minute repair on a fresh page on day 116, because day 115 is a check with no reteaching. Day 115 is the check: a fresh card, no help.
