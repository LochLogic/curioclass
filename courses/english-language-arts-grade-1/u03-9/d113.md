# Day 113: Sort the facts and make two clues

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 112's exit marks and name today's heard six, the six catch-up children and the pairs for the talk. Set one set of four topic cards on each pair's desk. Put each child's four note strips, drawing and the two pages of the chosen bird (expert bird page sets) in front of them. Make the repair card below. Take the sample words from your folder. Mark Fluffing Up in Feathers Protect. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Repair for day 112's exit (error B, and s03-X112a): a child who chose a part the sources do not state. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Show a hummingbird drawn with two labels, wings (to the wings) and tail (to the tail). Read the source once: "A hummingbird's wings beat very fast. A hummingbird can hover." All children at once, point. **Ask.** "Which label does the source not give? Point to it." Expect: tail. Cross it out and leave the tail unlabelled. Put a finger on the word wings in the source: the words are the proof, the picture is not.

#### 8 min. Words

Show a picture card for each word and say it once, children repeating: topic card, clue, shape, use. Review: a topic card names what a group of facts is about, with the facts beneath it (day 79); a clue is one true fact that helps someone guess the bird, with the name held back (days 108 and 110). New today: shape is what a part looks like; use is what the part does. Then show the model topic card for the beak: a beak picture with two lines under it, the first "flat beak" (shape), the second "strains food from mud" (use). Read it aloud: shape first, use second.

#### 17 min. Sort the notes

Model, three minutes, on three made-up strips from the texts (Review: days 82, 83 and 97). Lay the four topic cards. Sort "A hummingbird's wings beat very fast." under wings and "Down helps keep a bird warm." under feathers. Read "A heron lives near shallow water." and say: "It names no body part, so it fits no card. A note that fits no card is set aside." Then pairs, ten minutes, all at once: each child lays its four notes under the cards and says "This is about ___." Hear six pairs, one minute each, inside the ten: **Ask.** "Which card does this note go under?" Expect: beak or feet, by the part the note names. A child who puts a note under the card of another part has taken a detail for the topic (error A); point to the part word in the note. Last, four minutes: partners check each strip by pointing to its part word.

#### 15 min. Order two clues

Model, three minutes, on the model topic card for the beak: the shape line becomes the first clue, "I have a flat beak." The use line becomes the second, "My beak strains food from mud." The bird's name stays back. Each child, ten minutes, picks the two notes that sit under one card, puts the shape note first and the use note second, says each as a clue to a partner and draws each in a box on the student page with a word label. A child with no shape note or no use note finds it again on the page. Frames: "I have ___." "My ___ helps me ___." Hear six children, one minute each, inside the ten: "Say your clues in order." Last two minutes: a child who said the bird's name starts again with "I have".

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn card, pairs from different tables, all at once, three minutes, then six pairs heard, fifty seconds each, five minutes (two minutes to set up): "Which clue does not help the listener tell your bird?" Expect: "I have a beak, because many birds have one." Review: listen, restate ("You said ___"), then reply (day 107). A child may pass; the partner then reads a clue aloud.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read aloud from Fluffing Up, Feathers Protect, page 2: "On a cold day, a bird can fluff up its feathers. It looks round, like a ball. The fluffed feathers help it stay warm." Show the feathers and wing topic cards. All children mark one on the page. **Ask.** "Which topic card holds these facts?" ([[s03-X113av2]]) Then say: "This card is made up for practice," read the avocet card from the card sheet, and the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each: **Ask.** "Say the first clue of a riddle, without the bird's name." ([[s03-X113b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread once), not yet seen. A child who points to the wing topic card has named the wrong topic (error A). A child who says "I like my bird." has given a liking, not a fact (error E). Name day 113's misses for day 114's repair.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say nice, nicer, nicest; what do you hear added? Blend smoke: /s/ /m/ /o/ /k/, long o. Give the first, vowel and last sound of wide. Segment safe.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 18, lesson 95: -er and -est on silent-e bases. Review: -er and -est on a known base, taller for two and tallest for three or more (unit 2, day 61), and the e drops before an ending that starts with a vowel (day 109). New today: the same endings on silent-e bases, the e dropped. Show nice, nicer, nicest and safe, safer, safest. Say: "Nicer compares two. Nicest compares three or more." Read the base, add the ending and blend again. Then late, later, latest and wide, wider, widest on word cards. Decode, then spell nicer and latest, about 40 seconds a word. Reread A Day at the Lake for expression. The cycle sample is taken in the rotation.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Hand each child the written feedback on the day 112 pages. Revise any pair it marks, two minutes, on the back of the page kept for 118 (every form was taught by day 27; a reversed form is error N: model its start and direction). Dictate: "Dave rides a safer bike." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Dave is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Dave and bike the last e is silent, in rides the e stays, and in safer the e of safe is dropped. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: five minutes of map, blend, read and spell, today nicer and safer, then the sample below. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, one child at a time, about 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The sample, four minutes a group, from the folder card: each child in turn reads two word cards, wave and the (20 seconds each child). Remove the cards. All six write on small boards what you say, each word once in a sentence and once alone: wave, the and drape (drape has not been taught: write every sound). Then each child reads one sentence of A Day at the Lake, one minute in all. The rest reread, chain and spell nicer, nicest and safer in pairs at stations. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six. Mark shown or not yet seen. Error M is wv or th for the; error O is the read with a short e. Write each miss in the cycle record.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the clue marks, the sample marks and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Write brief feedback on each child's sample, naming the sound to look at and not the word, to be returned on day 114. Put the strips, drawing and clues in the riddle folder. List by name the children who chose the wrong topic card (error A) or gave a liking (error E), for day 114.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the four topic cards on paper and read each page aloud. One child: sort four notes, make two clues and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write beak, feet, wings and feathers on four slips of paper.

**Skip.** The rotation.

- **10 min. Sort.** Your child lays four facts from two short bird pages under slips named for the part. **Say.** "Which card does this fact go under?" Expect: the slip for the part the fact names.
- **10 min. Clues.** Your child says two facts as clues, what a part looks like first, what it does second, with no bird name.
- **5 min. Words.** Say nice, nicer, nicest. Your child says what was added.
- **5 min. Print.** "Dave rides a safer bike."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X113av2]] [[s03-X113b]] Read the short card the page shows. Your child points to the topic that holds the clues, then says the first clue with no bird name.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the sort, the exit and one clue; skip the talk. Longer: a child makes clues from the other page of the bird too.

### Words and supports

Words: topic card, clue, shape, use, set aside. Frames: "This is about ___." "I have ___." "My ___ helps me ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick the topic card that holds a set of clues and say a first clue without the bird's name. A child who pointed to the wing topic card (error A) gets a five-minute repair on a fresh set of clues on day 114, and a child who gave a liking (error E) hears the contrast on three cards in the same slot. Day 114 finds each clue again by a route.
