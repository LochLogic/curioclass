# Day 107: Repair one gap and try again

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make the group list from the 104 and 105 records: each child has one gap, in at most four groups. Name today's heard six and the six catch-up children. Draw the four repair cards. Print the swallow card for each child. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 20 min. Respond and repair

Say: "These cards are made up for practice. Today you fix one thing and try again. Nothing is scored again." Repair is by gap at your table, at most four groups, 5 minutes each. The other children read their own paragraph aloud to a partner. A group of more than seven is heard as a rotating six, 40 seconds each. Mark each repair: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If more than a third of the class missed one component, you model it to everyone for 5 minutes and that group's slot goes to the whole class; the other groups keep 5 minutes each.

- **Features (error D, and day 106's exit, s03-X106b).** Card: "Contents: Claws, 2. Heading: Sharp Claws. Glossary: grasp, to take hold and grip. A hawk has sharp claws. The claws grasp food." (restates Feet That Help). **Ask.** "Show me the contents entry, then the heading, then the sentence that says the claws grasp food." Expect: three fingers in that order, before any explaining. Say: "The page says it first."
- **Topic and evidence (errors A, B, C).** Card: "A duck has feathers that keep water out. The duck stays dry in the pond." (restates Feathers Protect). Lay a topic card, "the duck's feathers", and three strips: "keep water out", "stays dry in the pond", "has webbed feet". **Ask.** "Which strips go under the topic because the card says them?" Expect: the first two; the third waits outside because this card does not give it.
- **Writing and participation (errors E, F, G).** Read an unfinished paragraph: "A hawk has sharp claws. They grasp food. And." **Ask.** "Say a closing that goes back to the topic." Expect: "So its claws help it get food." A child who offered only a border says one located fact instead.
- **Foundations (errors H to O).** Word cards tube, tune and mule. Tap each sound, count the vowel sounds, blend, then spell tune with letter cards. Say: "Where is the vowel?"

#### 35 min. Supported source rereading and a fresh retry

Show the swallow card. Read it aloud once, then twice more with children following each line with a finger, ten minutes in all: "Contents: Tail, 2. Heading: Forked Tail. Glossary: forked, split into two points at the end. A swallow has a long, forked tail. It catches insects in the air." Each child retries only the missed component on this card, with its contents, heading and glossary pages in front, twelve minutes, all at once at desks:

- **Topic group.** Lay the topic strip "the swallow's tail" and three detail strips: "long and forked", "catches insects in the air", "short and round". Sort the strips the card gives under the topic.
- **Evidence group.** Say three lines: "The tail is long and forked." "The tail is short and round." "The swallow catches insects in the air." Thumbs up for each line the card says; point to the line.
- **Features group.** Trace the contents entry, the heading and the glossary line for the word forked, and say what each finds.
- **Writing, participation and foundations groups.** Say or print a topic sentence for the card; a child with no gap does the same, then adds a second fact. A foundations child reads the card words aloud with you, then spells mules.

Walk round and hear six children, 40 seconds each, four minutes inside the twelve. Mark each on the 104 and 105 rows: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. Meeting answers must match this card. Then partners take turns, six minutes, all at once: one says what the card says about the swallow, the other restates it ("You said ___") and then replies. Review: the turn card. Hear six pairs, one minute each, in the last six minutes. A reply that does not answer the partner is error K; ask "What did your partner say?" first. Pass is allowed.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. Show the two pictures. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which picture matches the fact on the card?" ([[s03-X107a]]) Then, with the card in view, the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each: **Ask.** "Tell me one thing the card says about the hornbill's beak." ([[s03-X107b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who picks the fruit alone has named a detail, not the topic (error A). A child who offers a fact the card does not give has made error B. Name day 107's misses for day 108's repair.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say tub and tube; which has the long vowel sound? Blend /f/ /l/ /u/ /t/. Give the first, vowel and last sound of mule. Segment plume.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 17, lesson 89: review of u_e and e_e (day 104) and of the two-syllable words of day 106 (inside, sunrise). Review: plural -s on taught bases (day 21). Show flute, mule, tune and note; add s: flutes, mules, tunes, notes. The silent e stays and the s says /s/ or /z/ after it. Map each, count the vowel sounds (error H counts letters), and decode. Spell mules and tunes, about 40 seconds a word. Read the sentence strips "Pete hums tunes." and "June has a flute." and ask "Does that make sense?" Then say who the strip is about (Review: Cones and Tubes, reread for meaning).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print E e, F f, G g and H h, four pairs, dated; the page goes in the separate folder kept for 118 (every letter form was taught by day 27; feedback written after class). Dictate: "Pete hums tunes." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Pete is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Pete and tunes the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, 50 seconds a child, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. Each reads a page of Cones and Tubes again for meaning and expression, then spells a taught word (cute). The rest reread, chain cut, cute, mute and tub, tube, tune with letter cards, and match the cards flutes, mules and tunes to pictures, in pairs at stations. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the repair marks, the retry marks, the exit marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Mark the retry on the 104 and 105 rows; nothing is rescored. Put each retry in the child's folder. List by name the children who named a detail as the topic, for day 108.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the swallow card on paper and read it aloud. One child: repair your child's one gap, retry on the swallow card and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No card: use a short bird page with a contents, a heading and a glossary.

**Skip.** The rotation.

- **10 min. Repair.** Read a short bird page twice. **Say.** "Show me where it says one thing, then tell me." Expect: a finger on a line first.
- **10 min. Retry.** Read a fresh card. **Say.** "What is the card mostly about?" Expect: the part, not one detail.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Add s to flute, mule and tune; clap inside and sunrise.
- **5 min. Print.** E e, F f, G g, H h and "Pete hums tunes."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X107a]] [[s03-X107b]] Read a short bird card the page shows. Your child points to the picture, then says one thing it says.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair, the retry and the exit; read the swallow card once. Longer: a child with no gap writes a second topic sentence for the swallow card.

### Words and supports

Words: topic, detail, source, contents, heading, glossary, forked. Frames: "The card is about ___." "The page says ___." "You said ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can name what a card is mostly about and say one fact it gives. A child who picked a detail for the topic (error A) gets a five-minute repair on a fresh card on day 108. Day 108 launches the expert-bird riddle and drawing task from the bird story and poems.
