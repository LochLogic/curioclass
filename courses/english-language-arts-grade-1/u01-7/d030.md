# Day 30: A closing, and a fact made better

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the checklists, the exit card with its picture, word card V with the sort cards and the observation sheet. Write groups 4 to 6 and the six names for rotation 4. Write three closing strips for the demonstration. Write a short note on each child's letter sample sheet: one letter to print again. Real tools: teacher only. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 18 min. Word observation

The second three groups of five, groups 4 to 6, 6 minutes (360 seconds) each, at your table, exactly as on day 29. The other twenty-five reread their booklets quietly and draw. Read word card V aloud once to each group (20 seconds; "This card is made up for practice"). Each child takes about 68 seconds: s01-A33, 12, sort scoop, sieve, sand and flour into tools and materials; s01-A34, 12, "What is a scoop?"; s01-A35, 12, one school use; s01-A36, 12, act out look, peek and glance; s01-A37, 8, which is smaller, the little or the tiny scoop; s01-A38, 12, why Mina uses a scoop, with scoop, sand and because. Mark each item on the record sheet with the error letter if one fits (errors U to Z). A child not yet seen gets the stem once more; if still not seen, record it and teach nothing new. The repair waits for day 33. A child who was away on day 29 is heard now.

#### 12 min. Text questions and talk

Whole class on the rug, then pairs. Read the Classroom Sign Model aloud once (1 minute). Show the three question strips, How, Why and What. Review: you used these on day 26. Model one question, with a child as the maker: point a strip at the second sentence and ask, "How did the crayon help?" Expect: it made the letters dark. Say: "A good partner question points at one part and asks for one more fact." (3 minutes)

Pairs, with the turn card and three strips. Child A reads the booklet to the partner by saying it, page by page. Child B holds one strip on one page and asks one question about that part: "What does the handle do?" A answers aloud, and if the answer is not on the page yet, says what to add (4 minutes). Swap (4 minutes). Look-fors: one question about one part; the maker answers from the model, not from the picture; the answer says a fact. A child may pass the maker turn and point to the part instead.

#### 25 min. Add the closing and revise

Four minutes of repair first. If many children gave a part the card did not give (error A, "It cuts paper"), a fact only the picture shows (error C, "It is yellow") or a judgment with no fact (error AA, "It is nice") on day 29's exit, say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A clip is a tool for our papers. Its bent wire squeezes them flat." **Ask.** "What fact explains how the clip helps?" Expect: its bent wire squeezes the papers flat. Reread, point to the sentence that says it, cover the picture first for error C, and give one fact for each finger for error AA. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge.

Model the closing (5 minutes) with your basket draft on the board: page 1 "My basket is a tool for paper scraps." page 2 "Its wide mouth lets scraps drop in." page 3 "Its handle lets us carry it." Say: "Review: on days 21 and 22 you laid out four frame cards, topic, fact, fact and closing. Today they are four boxes to tick." Show the checklist. Lay down three strips: "Its handle lets us carry it." "I like my basket." "The basket helps us keep our room tidy." **Ask.** "Which strip closes the job?" Expect: the third, which says the job. The first repeats a fact and the second is a feeling. Say: "A closing says the job. It is not a new fact and it is not the same fact again."

Children work (13 minutes). First revise (5 minutes): each child thinks of the partner's question, then changes one fact, naming the part and what it does, and writes what changed in the revision box on the student page, a word or a picture. Then close (8 minutes): draw the job done on page 4 and print "The ___ helps us ___." A child who cannot yet print says it and you scribe it for copying. Last, each child ticks the four boxes on the checklist; a partner may check one box (3 minutes, inside the 13). Walk and ask, "Which question made you change it?" and "What job does your tool do?" Look-fors: the revised fact names a part and what it does; the closing says the job and adds no new fact.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "Our paper tube carries rolled posters. Its closed ends keep them from sliding out." Show the picture: a paper tube with closed ends and the tip of a rolled poster showing at one end. Say as a partner: "How does that help when we walk to the rug?" Ask: "Say a closing sentence that answers your partner." Every child says it to a partner, then the rotating six say it to you, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X30]]). A good answer says the job: the tube lets us carry the posters without losing them. "Its closed ends keep them in" repeats one fact and does not close the job (error AC). "I like the tube" is a judgment with no fact (error AA). "The poster is red" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). These marks go to day 31.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend: /d/ /i/ /g/, "dig"; /m/ /u/ /d/, "mud". Segment with counters: dig, three counters. Isolate the first sound of mud, /m/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and read

No new pattern. The class reads Pat's Lunch together once (3 minutes). Pairs reread it, a page each, and read the last page twice (4 minutes). The class reads the cards buns, tubs, nuts and hugs, then each child spells hugs and tubs on the sound-box sheet (3 minutes). Walk and sit with three pairs.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Return the letter sample sheets with your written note, one letter circled. Each child prints the circled letter again beside the first try and finishes any missing form (4 minutes). Dictation: say "Pat has a hat." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: the capital P, pat, the mapped word has, a, hat and the period. Walk and look at b and d.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Observation rotation 4 of 5. Six different children, 5 minutes (300 seconds) each, at your table; no new teaching. The other twenty-four read known texts and spell independently. Every child supplies this evidence once in the window of days 27 to 31.

*Order and seconds, one child at a time, as on day 27 (275 seconds).* Card F carries items 1, 12, 13 and 14. 1. s01-A16, 10: on Card F, point to the first word, the capital letter and the end mark of "The cup tips." 2. s01-A17, 10: cap and cape, which has the long vowel. 3. s01-A18, 10: blend /s/ /l/ /i/ /p/. 4. s01-A19, 10: cup, first, middle and last sounds. 5. s01-A20, 15: stamp, every sound. 6. s01-A21, 15: th, ch, sh on cards. 7. s01-A22, 15: read chip and shut. 8. s01-A23, 10: read the and is. 9. s01-A24, 5: read cap and caps. 10. s01-A26, 20: spell chin and the. 11. s01-A27, 15: spell thud, not shown. 12. s01-A28, 25: read Card F to find why the pup is sad, then point. 13. s01-A29, 90: read Card F aloud, then once more. 14. s01-A30, 25: find what the cup does, reread to check.

Hold the quiet pace cue yourself, never shown or heard by children. Mark each item on the observation sheet and move on at once. A child who is not yet seen gets the stem once more, then the first sound as help; if still not seen, record it and teach nothing new. The repair waits for day 33. The letter samples (s01-A25) and the earlier reading records (s01-A31, s01-A32) take no minutes.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the observation marks and the word marks to the record sheet and file the observation sheet, the booklets, the checklists, the letter samples and the models. Mark the exit marks and the six heard. Name tomorrow's six, the last rotation.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw four boxes on a lined page for the checklist, write the sort words on cards and read word card V and Card F from a lined page; record on a lined page. No question strips: write How, Why and What on three scraps. One child: answer a grown-up's question about a part, revise one fact, add a closing, do the exit and the letters; skip the observation.

### At home

**Swap.** No question strips: ask the question yourself. No model: use the drawing.

**Skip.** The word groups and the observation.

- **10 min. A question.** You ask one question about one part of the draft, "What does the ___ do?" Your child answers aloud and changes one fact in the booklet.
  **Say.** "What does the handle do?" Expect: what the part does, such as "It lets us carry it."
- **10 min. A closing.** Your child draws the job done and says, "The ___ helps us ___."
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints "Pat has a hat."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X30]] You are the partner. Your child answers alone, aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the partner question, the closing, the exit and the rotation; cut the second question turn. Longer: a child who finishes writes a closing for a classmate's tool and says why it closes the job.

### Words and supports

Words: question, change, closing, job, part. Frames: "The ___ helps us ___." "What does the ___ do?" Sound boxes; scribing for a child who cannot yet print; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. A quiet corner for the observation, and extra seconds where the dialect differs. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 31 rereads the sign model, the hammer poem, the bicycle story and a line of a tool article, pairs find the facts their drafts rest on, each child edits the capital letters and ending marks and submits the explanation, and you score it once. Rotation 5 hears the last six children. If many children gave a closing that repeats a fact (error AC, "Its closed ends keep them in"), a feeling (error AA, "I like the tube") or a fact only the picture shows (error C, "The poster is red") on today's exit, the edit part opens with four minutes: lay the strips, point to the one that closes the job, and close a fresh piece.
