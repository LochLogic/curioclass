# Day 28: Name the tool in a topic sentence

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the topic sentence sheets, booklets, exit card with its picture, sign-model strips (one set a pair, one in large print) and observation sheet. Write the six names for rotation 2 across the observation sheet: children not yet heard. Draw the jug and basket on cards. Lay out the models and record sheets. Real tools: teacher only. Read the student page aloud.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 27's marks. If many children gave a part the paragraph did not give (error A, "It has wheels") or a fact only the picture shows (error C, "The farmer has a straw hat"), take four minutes. Reread the hoe paragraph; children point to the handle sentence. Then say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "My paper tray holds crayons. Its low sides let us reach in." Show the picture: a flat paper tray with four crayons and a blue stripe on one edge. **Ask.** "What fact explains how the tray helps?" Expect: its low sides let us reach in. For error C, cover the picture, reread, then uncover: "Do the words say the stripe is blue?" If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge. Use any minute left to name today's plan.

#### 15 min. Read and analyze

Whole class on the rug. Read the Classroom Sign Model aloud once, a finger under the words (1 minute). Say: "Review: you met this sign on days 7 and 22. It has four parts: a topic, two facts and a closing." Lay the four large strips in a row. Point to the first, "We used tools to make our classroom sign." Say: "This is the topic sentence. It tells what the whole paragraph is about: the tools and the sign they made." Point to the next two: "These give a fact about each tool." Point to the last: "This closing says the job." (5 minutes)

Now your own paragraph. Say: "It also starts with a topic sentence that names your one tool and what it is for." Write the frame on the board: My ___ is a tool for ___. Show the jug picture, a paper jug pouring water into a tub. Say: "My jug is a tool for pouring water." **Ask.** "What does my sentence name?" Expect: the tool, a jug, and its job, pouring water. (4 minutes)

Pairs with the basket picture, a basket of paper scraps. Each child says the frame to a partner: "My basket is a tool for ..." Any real job is right. Hear two pairs aloud. A child may pass and point to the part that shows the job. (5 minutes)

#### 10 min. Reread and question

Pairs hold the four sign-model strips mixed. Read the sign model aloud with the class, a finger under the words (2 minutes). Then four **Ask.** questions; each pair puts a finger on a strip and you read that strip aloud. 1. "Which sentence names the tool and tells what the paragraph is about?" Expect: the topic strip. 2. "Which sentence gives a fact about the crayon?" Expect: the second strip. 3. "Which gives a fact about the tape?" Expect: the third strip. 4. "Which says the job the sign does?" Expect: the closing strip. Four questions take 6 minutes. Then pairs put the strips in order (2 minutes). If a pair points to a fact strip for the topic, ask, "Does it tell the whole paragraph, or one tool?"

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, with the turn card and the model record sheet from day 27. Review: in kindergarten you asked a partner a question to find out more. Child A says two sentences about the model: the frame, "My ___ is a tool for ___," then the fact from the record sheet, "The ___ helps because ___." Child B asks, "Which sentence names the tool?" and A says that sentence again. They swap (4 minutes). A second round with a new partner (4 minutes). Hear two children say both sentences to the class (2 minutes). Look-fors: the answer is the first sentence, not the fact. A child may pass and point to the model's part.

#### 15 min. Write a topic sentence

Each child has a topic sentence sheet: a box to draw in, the frame in print and a row of sound boxes. Model on the board (3 minutes): draw the jug and say the frame. The tool word has three sounds, /j/ /u/ /g/: tap one box for each and print j, u, g. Then say the job word, "pouring," and spell it the way it sounds. Say: "Your spelling only has to match the sounds."

Children draw their paper model in the box (3 minutes), say the frame to a partner, then print the tool word and then the job word in the sound boxes (7 minutes); a longer word gets more boxes drawn. A child who cannot yet print says the words to you, and you scribe them for the child to copy. Walk and ask, "Which sound comes next?" and "What is it for?" Look-fors: the tool is named; the job is said; each sound has a letter. Three children read their sentence to the class (2 minutes). Children keep the sheet; tomorrow it opens the booklet.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "Our paper rack is a tool for our drawings. Folded feet help it stand. Slots hold each drawing. The rack keeps our work together." Show the picture: a paper rack with folded feet and three slots, a drawing in each. Ask: "Say a sentence that names the tool and what it is for." Every child says it quietly to a partner, then the rotating six say it to you, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X28]]). A good answer names the rack and its job with the drawings. "It is good" names no tool (error AB). "Folded feet help it stand" is a fact with no tool named (error AB). "The slots are brown" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). These marks go to day 29.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend: /h/ /u/ /g/, "hug"; /r/ /a/ /m/, "ram". Segment with counters: ram, three counters. Isolate the first sound of hug, /h/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and read

No new pattern. Pairs reread Pat's Lunch aloud, two pages each, a finger under the words (5 minutes). Then each child spells buns and tubs on the sound-box sheet (3 minutes). The class reads the cards buns, tubs, nuts and hugs (2 minutes). The ending says /z/ in buns and tubs and /s/ in nuts. Walk and sit with three pairs.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

No new letter. Dictation: say "Sam has a big tub." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: the capital S, sam, the mapped word has, a, big, tub and the period. Then letter samples (4 minutes): each child prints the pairs N n to T t on side two of the letter sample sheet, and you write the date. Z is printed with U to Z tomorrow. Forms still missing are recorded as incomplete, never wrong. Watch b and d.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Observation rotation 2 of 5. Six different children, 5 minutes (300 seconds) each, at your table; no new teaching. The other twenty-four read known texts and spell independently: Pat's Lunch with a partner, the hammer poem refrains, and words on the sound-box sheet. Every child supplies this evidence once in the window of days 27 to 31.

*Order and seconds, one child at a time, as on day 27 (275 seconds, about 25 to change children).* Card F carries items 1, 12, 13 and 14. 1. s01-A16, 10: on Card F, point to the first word, the capital letter and the end mark of "The cup tips." 2. s01-A17, 10: cap and cape, which has the long vowel. 3. s01-A18, 10: blend /s/ /l/ /i/ /p/. 4. s01-A19, 10: cup, first, middle and last sounds. 5. s01-A20, 15: stamp, every sound. 6. s01-A21, 15: th, ch, sh on cards. 7. s01-A22, 15: read chip and shut. 8. s01-A23, 10: read the and is. 9. s01-A24, 5: read cap and caps. 10. s01-A26, 20: spell chin and the. 11. s01-A27, 15: spell thud, not shown. 12. s01-A28, 25: read Card F to find why the pup is sad, then point. 13. s01-A29, 90: read Card F aloud, then once more. 14. s01-A30, 25: find what the cup does, reread to check.

Hold the quiet pace cue yourself, a small timer or a look at a clock, never shown or heard by children. Mark each item shown, shown with help or not yet seen on the observation sheet, with the error letter if one fits, and move on at once. A child who is not yet seen gets the stem once more, then the first sound as help; if still not seen, record it and teach nothing new. The repair waits for day 33. The letter samples (s01-A25) and the earlier reading records (s01-A31, s01-A32) take no minutes. A child who is away is heard on the next rotation.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the observation marks to the record sheet and file the observation sheet, the topic sentence sheets, the dated letter samples and the models. Mark the exit marks and the six heard. Name tomorrow's six.

### Other rooms

No printer: write each sign sentence on its own scrap of paper, draw the sound boxes on a lined page, write the item words on cards and read Card F from a lined page; record on a lined page. No sign-model strips for pairs: show the large print strips and have pairs point from their seats. One child: read the sign model with a grown-up, find the topic, write one topic sentence, do the exit and the letters; skip the observation.

### At home

**Swap.** No model: draw the tool. No sound boxes: draw a box for each sound.

**Skip.** The pairs and the observation.

- **10 min. Name the tool.** Your child shows or draws the paper model and says, "My ___ is a tool for ___."
  **Say.** "Which part names the tool?" Expect: the word after My.
- **10 min. Print it.** Your child prints the tool word and the job word, one letter for each sound, and draws the tool.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints "Sam has a big tub."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X28]] Your child answers alone, aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the analysis, the topic sentence sheet, the exit and the observation; cut the second round of the talk. Longer: a child who finishes writes a second topic sentence for a tool in the articles.

### Words and supports

Words: topic sentence, tool, fact, closing, job. Frames: "My ___ is a tool for ___." "Which sentence names the tool?" Sound boxes; scribing for a child who cannot yet print; pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. A quiet corner for the observation, and extra seconds where the dialect differs. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 29 hears three groups of five on word work, rereads a tool article, and drafts the topic sentence and the first fact in the booklet. Rotation 3 hears six more children. If many children gave a sentence that names no tool (error AB, "It is good") or a fact only the picture shows (error C, "The slots are brown") on today's exit, the draft part opens with four minutes: contrast "It helps" with "My rack helps," name the subject in a fresh opening, and cover the picture first when the slip was a picture fact.
