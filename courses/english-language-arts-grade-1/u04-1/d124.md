# Day 124: Waiting, and a word with two meanings

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read day 123's exit marks and find the commonest miss. Make the repair card below and its three pictures. Hand back yesterday's printing sets, each with one written line, and day 123's exit tickets (s04-X123c). Name today's heard six and the catch-up group; the sample runs in the rotation, so keep the sample words in your folder and out of sight. Cut the six sort cards apart and mix them. Keep the exit card face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Hand back day 123's exit tickets (s04-X123c); each child looks at what Nia does in their drawing. Repair for day 123's exit (error AB): a child who picked or drew an action the story does not give. If another miss was commoner, use that one. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "Ivy's kite catches in a low branch. Ivy tells Mum. Mum lifts it down with a long pole." Lay three pictures down: Mum lifting the kite down with a pole, Ivy climbing the tree, Mum buying a new kite. Say: "Jon says Ivy climbs the tree. Ana says Mum lifts the kite down with a pole. Whose action is on the card?" Children show a thumb for Jon or for Ana (about 10 seconds). Expect: Ana. Then you touch the sentence on the card that says it. Pairs say which picture is not on the card, in 15 seconds, and three pairs are heard. A child who thumbs for Jon took the action that seemed likely, not the one the card gives.

#### 15 min. Read chapter 3

Read pages 7 to 9 aloud with the pictures (about 300 words, three minutes). Reread page 8 slowly: the word fly shows up twice. Say: "Hold up one finger when you hear fly." Say the safety words together: "We never pick up a wild bird. We call a grown-up." Write on the chart: Ben waits. A child upset by the hurt bird may look at the picture of Ben and Dad on the bench.

#### 15 min. Talk and model comparison

Review: compare, say how two things are the same and different (kindergarten, day 49). Say: "This time we compare what happens to two children, not how things look." Model with a sort, aloud, with no child speaking. Lay the Both and But picture on the wall. Take the pictures from chapter 2, page 5, and the second story, page 2, and think aloud: "Ben's dad is told by a helper to stay back. Coach Lee tells Nia to stay back. Both are told to keep away from the bird." Place the card in the Both column. Then: "Ben's dad passes on a list of rules. Coach Lee gives one rule." Place the two cards in the But column. Read the finished frame aloud once. Then partners try page 7 of Ben and the Bird beside page 4 of Nia Calls for Help. Partners use the turn card, two turns each, 40 seconds, then six children are heard, 15 seconds each. Three asks, about 2 minutes 30 seconds each, with the words bench, grass, restless and worried said once with their pictures.

1. **Ask.** "Six cards lie on the table. Sort them into Ben, Nia and both." Expect: Ben on the bench and Ben swings his feet go under Ben; Nia on the grass and Nia holds her knees go under Nia; the two waits away from the bird go under both.
2. **Ask.** "Touch the words on page 7 and on page 4 that tell where each child waits." Expect: an old bench; the grass.
3. **Ask.** "Lia says Ben and Nia are different because Ben sits with his dad and Nia sits with her coach. Max says they are different because Ben's bird is a robin and Nia's bird is a dove. Whose answer is about what happens to the children?" Expect: Lia's.

#### 8 min. Fly

Today's new idea: some words have two meanings, and the sentence beside the word tells which one. Review: the sentence clue (day 121); the sentence clue card hangs on the wall. Show the two fly cards, each with a picture and a sentence from page 8. Read the first sentence aloud: "A fly buzzes past the bench." Point to the words beside fly that give the meaning: buzzes, past the bench. Say: "This fly is a small insect." Read the second: "Another bird lifts its wings and begins to fly." Point to lifts its wings. Say: "This fly is what a bird does in the air." Then lay three sentence strips beside the two cards: "A fly lands on the gate post." "The bird can fly to a branch." "The fly sits on the bench." Pairs sort the strips under the insect picture or the bird picture and touch the words that told them (3 minutes, six pairs heard, 20 seconds each).

#### 6 min. Pronouns again

Today's new idea: more pronouns. Review: a possessive noun shows what belongs to someone (day 121), and a pronoun takes the place of a noun (day 123). A possessive pronoun does the same job for what belongs: Ben's dad is his dad, Nia's coach is her coach, and Ben and Dad's bench is their bench. Show the cards his, her and their. Read each sentence and children hold up the card: "Ben's cap is yellow; ___ cap is yellow." (his) "Nia's shirt is red; ___ shirt is red." (her) "Pip and Tim wait; ___ bench is old." (their) Then say something and nothing from pages 1 and 7: "Something small is on the ground. There is nothing to do but wait." Say: something means a thing we do not name, and nothing means no thing at all. Children say one sentence each with a partner, 15 seconds, and three pairs are heard.

#### 6 min. Both and But chart

Partners sort the six cards from today's talk once more onto the Both and But picture, one card at a time, and say thumb up or thumb down for each placement (20 seconds a pair, six pairs heard). Read the finished frame aloud: Both wait away from the bird. But Ben sits on a bench and Nia sits on the grass. Each child then copies one word label under Both or But from the card, bench or grass, a word you say first.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet and show its three pictures. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which comparison picture fits?" ([[s04-X124a]]) While the rest finish the page, the heard six come to your table, with the card in view, 30 seconds each; three are asked stem b and three stem c. **Ask.** "What is one thing the same?" ([[s04-X124b]]) **Ask.** "What is one thing different?" ([[s04-X124c]]) The rest also draw alone. **Ask.** "Draw one thing Ben does while he waits for the helper." ([[s04-X124d]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who names how things look, not what the children do (error A), goes to day 125's opening repair. A drawing that picks up the robin gets the rule said again: we never pick up a wild bird.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Blend /p/ /or/ /k/. Segment storm. Say the first, middle and last sound of corn. Say kit and kite: which has the long vowel sound?

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 19, lesson 100. No new teaching: read and spell only the sounds and words already mapped. Warm-up chain on small boards, 20 seconds a word: park, dark, cork, fork. Say each, tap the sounds, write it. Review the mapped words with the sound-spelling map: again, would, bye, knew, does; read each whole.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print Uu, Vv, Ww, Xx, Yy, Zz, twelve forms, 5 minutes, the last dated set. While they print, walk and read each child's feedback line on yesterday's set aloud, about 8 seconds each. Dictate "The corn is by the barn." whole, then a word at a time, 4 minutes. No new letter forms are taught; new-letter teaching finished on day 27. Collect all five dated sets.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The cycle 19 progress sample runs here. The catch-up group first, 10 minutes: the six children the cycle record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread, and each child reads one reserved word ([[s04-A1]]) from your folder, then the card goes away and each writes one word you dictate ([[s04-A2]]), 1 minute each. Then four groups of six at your table, 5 minutes each, with Looking for Mars, the whole book, each child heard in turn, 20 seconds in the book and 20 seconds on the sample, 40 seconds in all. The other groups reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations, with A Day at the Lake for the rest. Mark each sample on the record, never say the words aloud to the class, and put the word cards back in the folder.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Fill the heard-six column, the cycle record, the sample marks and today's exit marks. Sort the children into the new groups for cycle 20 from the sample. Name day 125's opening repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: say the cards aloud and draw the pictures on paper. One child: reread pages 7 and 8, find the clue for each fly, say his, her and their, and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No picture cards: say each pair aloud and act it out.

**Skip.** The sort, the sample and the sound chains.

- **10 min. Reread.** Read pages 7 and 8 of chapter 3 aloud. **Say.** "Where do Ben and Dad wait?" Expect: on an old bench, a long way from the robin.
- **10 min. Fly.** Read: "A fly buzzes past the bench." Then: "A bird lifts its wings and begins to fly." **Say.** "Which fly is the insect? Which is what a bird does?" Expect: the first is the insect; the second is the bird.
- **5 min. His and her.** Say: "Ben has a cap. It is his cap." Your child says it for Nia: her.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X124a]] [[s04-X124b]] [[s04-X124c]] [[s04-X124d]] Read a short made-up card your child has not seen. Your child points to the picture, says one thing the same and one thing different, then draws one thing Ben does while he waits.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reading, the fly cards, the exit and the printing. Cut the pronoun cards to his and her. Longer: a child who sorts quickly says one more thing the same, using both.

### Words and supports

Words: bench, grass, worried, restless, fly, his, her, their, something, nothing. Frames: "Both ___." "But ___." "Fly here means ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can compare what happens to two children. A child who named how things look (error A) gets a fresh card in day 125's opening repair, and the drawings (s04-X124d) come back. The sample marks tell you the groups for cycle 20. Day 125 rereads both stories and compares what the children do to get help; chapter 4 follows on day 126.
