# Day 24: Make the model

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the model record sheets, the sound-box sheets and the exit card with its picture. Cut paper pieces ahead and tear tape strips, one set a child. No blades and no dispenser for children. Lay out the day 23 plan sheets, the hearing note slips, the hearing lines and the reader Pat's Lunch. Choose cohort 2, the next ten names on the record sheet, and cohort 1 for the retry. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 23's marks. If many children gave a judgment with no fact (error AA), a swapped job (error A) or a picture fact (error C) on day 23's exit, take four minutes. Write two words on the board: nice and fact. Say: "Nice is a feeling. A fact is something you can see or test." Show a made-up card for practice: "A rolling pin is round like a tube. It rolls over dough. The dough gets flat and thin." Hold up one finger for each fact: round like a tube; rolls over dough; dough gets flat and thin. **Ask.** "Is 'it is a nice pin' a fact?" Expect: no, it is a feeling. Children say a fact for each finger. For error A, reread the card and point to the sentence that holds the fact. For error C, cover the picture, reread, then uncover: "Do the words say it?" If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge. Use any minute left to name today's plan: make the model.

#### 10 min. Reread

Whole class on the rug. Read the sign model aloud, a finger under the words, then point to the tape sentence: "Tape joined the paper pieces." Show a join with a precut paper piece and a pre-torn tape strip: press the strip over the edge where two pieces meet (2 minutes). Say: "You will join with tape, like the sign." Each child tries one join on scrap paper, then points to where the tape sits and tells a partner, "The tape joins the ..." (5 minutes). Sit with three pairs, 1 minute each. A child may pass.

#### 25 min. Make the model

Each child builds the tool from their day 23 plan with the precut paper and the pre-torn tape: no blades and no dispenser. A room with nothing folds paper and joins it with a glue stick. Say: "Your model has a job. Build the part that does the job first." Build for 18 minutes. Walk and ask, "Which part helps with the job?" and "What is that part made of?" Children who finish early test it: does it hold, stand or fold the way the plan says? After 18 minutes each child shows the model to a partner and says, "My model helps with ..." (4 minutes). In the last 3 minutes set the models on the desks and walk the class past three of them. Look-fors for this part: (1) a part that matches a box of the plan; (2) the join holds; (3) the child names the part that does the job.

#### 15 min. Record

Hand out the model record sheet: a big drawing box, two label lines and the sentence "The ___ helps because ___". Each child draws the model, prints two labels, one word each, and says the sentence to a partner (7 minutes). Review: you used because in kindergarten. Say: "The paper folds helps because it holds the cards. A fact goes after because." Hear today's six, 60 seconds each (6 minutes), and write the sentences each child says. Look-fors: names a part; a fact about what it does; uses because; closes with the job. Use the last 2 minutes to read two written sentences back to their children. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A paper pocket holds cards. Tape joins its sides. Its open top lets cards slide in." Show the picture: a paper pocket with an open top and three cards. Ask: "How does the open top help the pocket?" Every child points to the part, then hear today's six say it, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X24]]). A good answer is that cards slide in. "The top is taped shut" is not on the card (error A). "The pocket is pink" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). "It is a good pocket" gives a judgment with no fact (error AA). These marks go to day 25.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend: /w/ /i/ /n/, "win"; /w/ /i/ /g/, "wig". Segment with counters: nuts, four counters; buns, four counters. Isolate the first sound of win, /w/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map

Review: you met the plural ending -s on day 21. On the sound-box sheet, decode and spell tubs, buns and nuts: say the word, tap a box for each sound, print a letter or letters in each box (6 minutes). The ending says /z/ in tubs and buns and /s/ in nuts. The class reads the cards tubs, buns, nuts and wig (2 minutes). Walk and sit with three children.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Teach W and w. Say: "W is down, up, down, up: a zigzag with two points at the bottom. The little w is the same, smaller." Children trace and print. Dictation: say "Win has a wig." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: the capital W, win, the mapped word has, the letter a and wig, then the period.

#### 15 min. Hearing, cohort 2

Ten children, 90 seconds each, the same three pieces as yesterday. The other twenty reread Pat's Lunch in pairs and finish their record sheet. The child sits with you and reads with your help, a finger under the words. Prose, Kofi and the Bicycle, page 7: "I sit on the seat. It does not wobble. It is still!" Two poem lines, The Hammer Song: "Tap, tap, tap, the little hammer sings." Information, Tools on the Farm: "A farmer grows food. A farmer uses tools to help plants grow." Write the note on a slip: what you heard, the next step and a retry. Give the child a copy. A note is a next step, never a grade. A child may pass.

#### 15 min. Retry, cohort 1

Cohort 1 is the ten children heard yesterday. Each child rereads the line their written note named, with a finger under the words, 90 seconds each. The other twenty read Pat's Lunch with a partner. Update the note: what changed and what comes next. Read the note to the child, never as a grade.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Store the models in a labelled box. Collect the record sheets. Mark the record sheet: the exit marks, the six who were heard and their look-fors, and the hearing notes. Name tomorrow's six.

### Other rooms

No tape: fold the paper and join with a glue stick. One child: make a model from the plan with a grown-up, record it, do the exit and the letters; skip the hearing and the retry.

### At home

**Swap.** No tape: fold the paper and join it with a glue stick. No precut paper: use a scrap sheet.

**Skip.** The hearing and the retry.

- **10 min. The sign.** Read: "We used tools to make our classroom sign. A crayon made the letters dark. Tape joined the paper pieces. The sign helps people find our room." Say: "A fact is something you can see or test."
  **Say.** "What does the tape do?" Expect: it joins the paper pieces.
- **10 min. A model.** Your child makes a small model from paper and tape and shows you the part that helps.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints W w and "Win has a wig."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X24]] Read "A paper pocket holds cards. Tape joins its sides. Its open top lets cards slide in." Ask: "How does the open top help the pocket?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: build one part of the model and keep the record and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes adds a part and tells what it is for.

### Words and supports

Words: model, part, join, fact, test, helps, because. Frames: "The ___ helps because ___." "My model helps with ..." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. A scribe may write a word label. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 25 explains a design change: each child says what to change on the model and why. If many children said a part the card did not give (error A), a picture fact (error C) or a judgment with no fact (error AA) on today's exit, its first part opens with four minutes: reread the card and point to the sentence, then retry on a new card.
