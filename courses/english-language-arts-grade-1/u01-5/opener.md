# Section 1.5 opener: a classroom job, an explanation model, and a paper tool

## What the section does

Days 21 to 24 move from reading about tools to designing one. Children pick a small classroom job, read a short model that explains how a tool helps, plan a paper tool, make it, and say how its parts help. They use the four-part frame from earlier in the unit: name the tool, give a fact, give a fact, finish.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60: a topic block, then a foundations block. Day 21 starts with a demonstration only you do: pour dry rice into a small jar with no funnel, then with a funnel. Say what a designed tool is: made on purpose for a job. On day 24 children handle precut paper and pre-torn tape only. No blades and no dispensers come out. On days 23 and 24 you also hear ten children each for 90 seconds read; you leave a short written note and the child tries again the next day. A note is a next step, never a grade.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A designed tool is made for a job.** The job comes first; the tool is shaped to do it.
- **A tool and a material are different.** A tool does the work; a material is what the tool works on or is made from.
- **A fact explains a part.** "It has a handle" is a fact; "it is nice" is not. Ask: which part does the work?
- **A closing sentence says the job.** It finishes the explanation; it does not add a new fact.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** a fact that is not on the card. Reread the card and point to the line.
- **C:** a fact that only the picture shows. Cover the picture and reread the words.
- **AA:** a judgment with no fact ("it is nice"). Ask for one fact per finger.
- **AC:** stopping before the closing sentence. Lay the four strips in a row.

Each repair fits the opening five minutes of the next day.
