# Day 22: How a sign helps

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the sign-model strips (one set a pair, plus one in large print), the tool and material cards, the sound-box sheets and the exit card. Hang the word-sort chart with two columns, tool and material. Lay out the sound-spelling map, the door-gap picture, the reader Pat's Lunch and the chime. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 21's marks. If many children gave a detail the card did not give (error A), a picture fact (error C) or a name with no fact (error AA) on day 21's exit, take four minutes with a new card. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Draw three quick pictures on the board: a mat staying put by a door, a bird on a branch and a clock on a wall. Read: "Our door mat has a rough back. The rough back keeps the mat still." **Ask.** "Which job could the rough back help with?" Expect: the mat staying put. Children point to the line that says it. For error A, reread the card line by line and point to the line that gives the fact. For error C, cover the pictures, reread the words, then uncover: "Do the words say the mat is red?" For error AA, "It is a mat" is a name, so ask for one fact for each finger: the back is rough; it keeps the mat still. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge while the rest read the sign model's picture. Use any minute left to say today's plan: read how a sign helps.

#### 15 min. Shared reading of the sign model

Whole class on the rug. Review: you met the classroom sign model on day 7 as a paragraph frame. Today you read it to find how a tool helps. First act out two words: join (put two papers together) and dark (a dark line beside a pale one) (1 minute). Read the model aloud once, whole, a finger under the words, the picture hidden: "We used tools to make our classroom sign. A crayon made the letters dark. Tape joined the paper pieces. The sign helps people find our room." Show the picture. Read it again and the class echoes each sentence (4 minutes).

Cut the large-print copy into four strips and lay them in order on the floor. Put a name card on each: topic, fact, fact, closing. Say: "The first sentence is the topic: what the paragraph is about. The next two are facts: what each tool did. The last sentence is the closing: how the sign helps." Point to each as the class says it (4 minutes). **Ask.** "What did the crayon do?" Expect: it made the letters dark. **Ask.** "What did the tape do?" Expect: it joined the paper pieces. Children point to each strip. Show the three pieces in the picture and ask which fact the picture shows and which only the words say (3 minutes). After reading, children say sign, join and dark again with a motion (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Reread and question

Reread the four strips while the class points. **Ask.** "Which sentence is the topic?" Expect: the first, "We used tools to make our classroom sign." **Ask.** "Which sentence tells what the crayon did?" Expect: the second, "A crayon made the letters dark." Children point to the line, then to the picture, words first. Every child points for a neighbor (3 minutes). Then hear today's six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes): each points to the line and says it. A child who points only to the picture is asked, "Show me the words that say it." A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Word work

Review: on day 1 you sorted things by what they do. Today you sort tools from materials. A tool does the work. A material is what a tool works on or is made from. Put up two heading cards, tool and material. Pairs sort the six cards from the articles: hammer, ladle and hoe are tools; wood, dirt and dough are materials. For each, say why: "The hammer is a tool. It pushes the nail into wood. The wood is a material." (4 minutes)

Review: on day 4 you defined a tool. Define by category and attribute: "A crayon is a tool that makes dark marks." Each child tells a partner, "A hammer is a tool that pushes in nails." Say where tape joins paper at school (2 minutes).

Act out three ways to see with the door-gap picture: look (eyes wide), peek (one eye through the gap in your fingers) and glance (a quick look and away). The class does each when you say the word (2 minutes). Review: on day 5 you ordered tiny and little; show with your fingers. Review: in kindergarten you used because to give a reason. Say: "The sign has tape because tape joins the pieces." (2 minutes)

#### 15 min. Say and arrange

Pairs, a mixed set of four strips each. They arrange the strips in order (3 minutes), read each one with a finger under it (3 minutes), then say the whole model with because in the closing: "The sign helps people find our room because ..." Expect any fact from the model: the letters are dark, or the tape joined the pieces (3 minutes). Hear today's six, 45 seconds each (5 minutes), three pairs, a child each. Look-fors: the four sentences in order; the closing says the job; because gives a fact from the model. A child may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read, with the four sentences printed in a column: "Our box holds chalk. Its sides keep pieces together. Its lid keeps dust inside. The box helps us tidy." Ask: "Point to the closing sentence and say it." Every child points, then hear today's six say it, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X22]]). A good answer is "The box helps us tidy." A child who points to the lid sentence stopped before the closing (error AC). "Chalk is for writing" is not on the card (error A). These marks go to day 23.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend and segment spoken u words, the class holding up a finger for each sound. Blend: /t/ /u/ /b/, "tub"; /n/ /u/ /t/, "nut"; /b/ /u/ /n/ /z/, "buns". Segment with counters: nut, three counters; buns, four counters. Isolate the last sound of nut, /t/, and the middle sound of bun, /u/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map and read

Review: you learned the plural ending in kindergarten and met -s yesterday. Show tub and tubs, nut and nuts, bun and buns. The ending says /z/ in tubs and buns and /s/ in nuts. Decode the cards tub, nuts, buns and quit: sound out each, blend, point to the ending s. Quit has qu, which says /k/ /w/. The class reads the mapped words the, a, is, has, look, and, in, his and with from the map (3 minutes). Chain on tiles: tub, tubs, bun, buns. Pairs build and say each (4 minutes); sit with three pairs.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Teach U and u. Say: "U is a curve: down the left, round the bottom and up the right. The little u is the same, smaller, with a short stick on the right." Children trace and print. Dictation: say "The tub has nuts." Children print a word at a time, with the map: the capital T, the mapped words the and has, tub, and nuts with its ending.

Spelling transfer, on the sound-box sheet: say "drum." Children say each sound, tap four boxes and print a letter or letters in each box. Say: "Tub is a word we have learned, so we spell it the way we learned. Drum is a new word, so we print a letter for every sound we hear." Accept drum, drm or any spelling that keeps all four sounds.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Three groups of ten, 10 minutes each. At your table, first read of the reader Pat's Lunch after the patterns and mapped words are ready. Read the title to them. The group reads the nine pages with you, a finger under the words (3 minutes). Then each child reads one page alone, with your help, about 30 seconds each (5 minutes); the tenth child rereads page 9. **Ask.** "What does the cat do?" Expect: it runs at the tub and takes a bun (2 minutes). The other two groups sit in pairs: one builds tub, tubs, bun and buns with tiles and says each; the other reads Pat's Map and Pat's Mess to each other. The chime moves each group along.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Fill the word-sort chart: children stick the tool and material cards in the right column. Mark the record sheet: the exit marks, the six who were heard and their look-fors, and who made each repair. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the four sentences on strips by hand and draw the word cards. One child: read the model and arrange the strips, sort the six cards, do the exit and the letters; skip the rotation and read the reader aloud to a grown-up.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: write the four sentences on separate lines.

**Skip.** The sorting, the pair work and the rotation.

- **10 min. The sign.** Read twice and point to each: "We used tools to make our classroom sign. A crayon made the letters dark. Tape joined the paper pieces. The sign helps people find our room."
  **Say.** "What did the crayon do?" Expect: it made the letters dark.
- **10 min. Tool or material.** Say: "A tool does the work. A material is what it works on." Ask which is the hammer and which is the wood. Expect: the hammer is the tool; the wood is the material.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints U u and "The tub has nuts."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X22]] Read "Our box holds chalk. Its sides keep pieces together. Its lid keeps dust inside. The box helps us tidy." Ask: "Point to the closing sentence and say it."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reading, the arranging and the exit; cut the verbs look, peek and glance to one picture. Longer: a child who finishes says a closing with because for the hammer and the wood.

### Words and supports

Words: sign, join, dark, tool, material, define, closing, because, look, peek, glance. Frames: "A ___ is a tool that ___." "The ___ helps because ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 23 plans a tool: each child draws the job, the tool and two materials and says what each is for. If many children stopped after the lid sentence (error AC) or gave a detail the card did not give (error A) on today's exit, its first part opens with four minutes: lay the four strips in a row, point to the one that says what the tool is for and close a fresh tool.
