# Day 23: Plan a tool

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the plan sheets, the hearing note slips, the exit card and its picture, and the paragraph copies for each pair. Write the model plan for the sign on the board: job, tool, two materials with their jobs, job done. Post the supply chart: paper, tape, crayon. Choose cohort 1, the first ten names on the record sheet. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 22's marks. If many children stopped after the lid sentence (error AC) or gave a detail the card did not give (error A) on day 22's exit, take four minutes. Lay the four sign-model strips in a row. **Ask.** "Which strip says what the tool is for?" Expect: the last, "The sign helps people find our room." Say: "A paragraph is not finished until the closing says the job." Then a fresh tool, made up for practice. Read: "We made a paper fan. Its folds push the air. A stick lets us wave it." Hold up a finger for the topic and each fact. **Ask.** "Say a closing sentence for the fan." Expect: the fan helps us stay cool. For error A, reread the card and point to the sentence that holds the fact: a fact is on the card. If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge. Use any minute left to name today's plan: plan a tool.

#### 10 min. Reread

Whole class on the rug. Read the wheelbarrow paragraph, the level paragraph and the classroom sign model aloud, words first, then the pictures (3 minutes). Review: the wheelbarrow and the level are from Builders at Work, which you heard on days 1 and 2. Pairs hold the large-print paragraphs. Each child points to one fact and says it to a partner (4 minutes), then swaps. Walk and listen for a fact that is on the page. Hear three children say a fact aloud to the class (3 minutes). A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Reread and question

Partners find the evidence before you answer. Read the wheelbarrow paragraph again. **Ask.** "Which words say what the wheel does?" Pairs point to the words and wait; you do not answer yet (2 minutes). Then take answers: "The wheel holds up part of the weight." Do the same with the level: **Ask.** "Which words say what the bubble tells?" Expect: "When the bubble sits in the middle, the board is flat." (2 minutes). Say: "The words that say what a part does are the evidence." Hear today's six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes): each points to the words and says them. For a fact only the picture shows, say: "Cover the picture. Do the words say it?" A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, using the sign. Review: you used the starter "Which part?" on day 10. Show the move with one pair (2 minutes): the first child says what the paper is for ("The paper makes the pieces of the sign."); the listener asks "Which part?"; the first points to a piece in the picture. Then the pair swaps for the tape ("The tape joins the pieces.") and the crayon ("The crayon makes the letters dark."). Fifteen pairs talk for 5 minutes, each child saying the purpose of at least one material and asking at least one question. Sit with three pairs, 1 minute each, and hear six children (3 minutes). Look-fors for the six: (1) names a material; (2) says what it is for; (3) the listener asks a clearing question.

#### 20 min. Plan sheet

Show the model plan on the board for the sign: job, find our room; tool, a sign; paper, makes the pieces; crayon, makes dark letters; job done, people find our room (2 minutes). Say: "Your tool is made of paper and tape. Tomorrow you build it, so plan with what is on the supply chart." Each child keeps the job they chose on day 21 or picks a new one from the menu. The plan sheet has five boxes: the job, the tool, a first material and what it is for, a second material and what it is for, and the job done. Children draw in each box and print one word label for each part, saying the sounds or copying from the board (14 minutes). Walk and ask, "Which part helps with the job?" and "What is the paper for?" In the last 4 minutes each child tells a partner: "My job is ... My tool is ... The paper is for ... The tape is for ..."

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "A tray's sides stop balls rolling away. A paper handle helps lift the tray." Show the picture: a tray with a paper handle on top and two balls in it. Ask: "What does the handle do?" Every child points to the part, then hear today's six say it, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X23]]). A good answer is that it helps lift the tray. "It is good" gives a judgment with no fact (error AA). "It stops the balls rolling" gives the sides' job to the handle (error A). "The handle is brown" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). These marks go to day 24.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Blend: /v/ /a/ /n/, "van"; /t/ /u/ /b/ /z/, "tubs". Segment with counters: van, three counters; tubs, four counters. Isolate the first sound of van, /v/. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map

Review: you met the plural ending -s on day 21. Show tub and tubs, bun and buns. Pairs build each on tiles, saying the sounds: tub, then add s, /z/. Count counters to match: one bun, two buns (4 minutes). The class reads the cards cats, mats, pans, tubs and buns (3 minutes). If you know the word tub, the ending is a clue to what tubs means.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Teach V and v. Say: "V is two slanting lines that meet at a point at the bottom. The little v is the same, smaller." Children trace and print. Dictation: say "A van has a tub." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: the capital A, van, the mapped word has, tub and the period.

#### 15 min. Hearing, cohort 1

Ten children, 90 seconds each. The other twenty reread Pat's Lunch in pairs and finish their plan sheet labels. The child sits with you and reads three short pieces with your help, a finger under the words. Read along and let the child lead. Prose, Kofi and the Bicycle, page 7: "I sit on the seat. It does not wobble. It is still!" Two poem lines, The Hammer Song: "Tap, tap, tap, the little hammer sings." Information, Tools on the Farm: "A farmer grows food. A farmer uses tools to help plants grow." Write the note on a slip while the next child sits down: what you heard (words read alone, where you joined, a self-correction), the next step and tomorrow's retry. Give the child a copy. A note is a next step, never a grade. A child may pass.

#### 15 min. Matched reading

Small groups of five reread Pat's Lunch. Sit with the three groups who read least smoothly on day 22, 4 minutes each: each child reads one page and rereads it for meaning and expression. The other three groups reread in pairs. Ask, "What does the cat do?" before and after.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Collect the plan sheets. Mark the record sheet: the exit marks, the six who were heard and their look-fors. File the hearing notes in each child's reading record. Name tomorrow's six.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the five boxes on folded paper and write the hearing lines on a card. One child: read the sign model, plan the tool with a grown-up, do the exit and the letters; skip the hearing and matched reading.

### At home

**Swap.** No plan sheet: fold a page into five boxes. No tape: draw the join.

**Skip.** The paragraph pairs, the hearing and the matched reading.

- **10 min. The sign.** Read: "We used tools to make our classroom sign. A crayon made the letters dark. Tape joined the paper pieces. The sign helps people find our room."
  **Say.** "What is the tape for?" Expect: it joined the paper pieces.
- **10 min. A plan.** Your child picks a job, draws a tool of paper and tape in five boxes: the job, the tool, two materials and their uses, the job done.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints V v and "A van has a tub."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X23]] Read "A tray's sides stop balls rolling away. A paper handle helps lift the tray." Ask: "What does the handle do?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the plan sheet and the exit; cut the paragraph reread to the wheelbarrow. Longer: a child who finishes adds a third material and says what it is for.

### Words and supports

Words: plan, tool, material, job, paper, tape, crayon, purpose, evidence. Frames: "The ___ is for ___." "Which part?" "My job is ... My tool is ..." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. A scribe may write a word label. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 24 makes the paper model from the plan and records how its parts help. Cohort 2 is heard and cohort 1 reads again with its written note. If many children gave a judgment with no fact (error AA, "It is good"), swapped the parts' jobs (error A, "It stops the balls rolling") or gave a picture fact (error C, "The handle is brown") on today's exit, its first part opens with four minutes: contrast "nice" with a fact you can see or test, and give one fact for each finger on a new tool.
