# Day 21: A job for a designed tool

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the paragraph-frame cards (one set a pair), the job menu cards, the fresh teller card (Pia) with its two pictures and the exit card with its three pictures. Set a small jar, a funnel and dry rice on a tray that only you reach. Lay out the teller strip, the speech card, the sound-spelling map and the reader Pat's Map. Real tools: teacher only.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Read day 20's marks. If many children called Mr. Ray the teller on day 20's exit (error F), take four minutes. Review: the teller says I (day 13). Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the fresh card and show its two pictures. First panel: "I am Pia. My paint jar falls over. Blue paint runs on the table." Second panel: "Mr. Cole brings a cloth. 'Hold the jar,' Mr. Cole says. I hold it. He wipes up the paint." **Ask.** "Who is telling this story?" Expect: Pia. Put the teller strip on "I am Pia" and on "I hold it", and the speech card on Mr. Cole's words. Contrast day 20: Lee told the scooter story and Mr. Ray only wiped the wheel, as Mr. Cole only wipes the table. Say: "The one who does is not always the one who tells. Listen for I." If only a few slipped, work with them at the rug edge. Use the last minute to say the plan for today: find a classroom job that a tool could help.

#### 15 min. Hook and read

Whole class on the rug, the tray at your knee. First act out pour (turn a jar over a dish), narrow (fingers close together) and load (a heavy thing carried). You alone handle the funnel and the rice. Show the jar and its small opening. First try, no funnel: pour the rice toward the jar. Some rice spills on the tray. Say: "That did not work." Second try, the funnel set in the jar: pour. The rice goes in and none spills (4 minutes). **Ask.** "What was different the second time?" Expect: the funnel.

Say: "A designed tool is made on purpose for a job. The funnel is shaped for pouring into a small opening." The class says "designed tool" with you (1 minute).

Review: you heard Tools in the Kitchen on day 4 and Builders at Work on days 1 and 2. Read the funnel paragraph aloud with the picture hidden: "A funnel is a kitchen tool for pouring. It is wide at the top and narrow at the bottom. The cook sets the narrow end in a small bottle and pours oil into the wide top. The oil goes into the bottle, not onto the table." **Ask.** "What job does the funnel do?" Expect: it pours oil into a small bottle. Then read the wheelbarrow paragraph: "A builder uses a wheelbarrow. It has one wheel and two handles. The wheel holds up part of the weight. A builder does not have to carry the whole load. So she can move bricks and sand across the building site." **Ask.** "What job does the wheelbarrow do?" Expect: it helps a builder move a load. Show each picture only after its words (7 minutes). A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Reread and question

Reread pages 9 to 11 of Kofi and the Bicycle aloud, words first, then the pictures (2 minutes). Page 10: the little screwdriver with a thin end fits, and the bell rings. Page 11: Kofi pulls the strap tight and the basket sits up straight. **Ask.** "Which part of the tool does the work?" Expect: the thin end of the little screwdriver, because it fits the small screw. Children point to the words that say it, then to the picture. **Ask.** "What job does the strap do?" Expect: it holds the basket up straight. Every child tells a neighbor the part and its job (3 minutes). Then hear today's six, 30 seconds each (3 minutes): each points to the words, then says which part does the work. A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Talk

Groups of three, one tool picture card to a group: the funnel, the wheelbarrow or the little screwdriver. Review: you used the turn card starters on day 10, "I would add ..." and "Which part?". Show the move with one group at the front (2 minutes): the first child says what one part does ("The narrow end fits the jar."); the second adds ("I would add that the wide top catches the rice."); the third asks "Which part?" and the first points to it. Ten groups talk for 5 minutes, three rounds, so each child speaks once, adds once and asks once. Sit with two groups, 2 minutes each, and hear six children. Back on the rug (3 minutes), three children say what a partner added. Look-fors: names a part; says its job; builds on a partner or asks.

#### 15 min. Frame and choose

Review: the paragraph frame has four parts, topic, fact, fact and closing (day 7, from the sign model). Give each pair the four funnel cards mixed. Read each card aloud. Pairs put them in order: "A funnel is a tool for pouring." "Its wide top catches the rice." "Its narrow end fits the jar." "The funnel helps us pour." Name the parts: topic, fact, fact, closing (5 minutes).

Read the product assignment aloud: "Explain how your tool helps our classroom. Name it, give two facts, and finish." Say: "Name it is the topic. Two facts are the middle. Finish is the closing. In the next days you plan and make a tool, then explain it." (2 minutes)

Show the three job menu cards and say: "These jobs are made up for practice. They are about school." Read the word under each: roll (pencils rolling off a desk), carry (a child with an armful of pencils walking to the rug, some dropping) and reach (a glue stick on a high shelf). Each child points to the job a tool could help, all at once; scan the room and note each choice (2 minutes). Each child draws the chosen job on the student page and prints its word from the card (6 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "Our paper scraps scatter. A tray with low sides holds things in." Show three pictures: scraps staying together, a cat asleep on a chair and books standing in a row. Ask: "Which job could a tray with low sides help with?" Every child points to a picture, then hear today's six give the reason aloud, 20 seconds each. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X21]]). A good answer is the scraps staying together, because the low sides hold things in. A child who says "It is a tray" gave a name with no fact (error AA). "The tray is blue" is a fact only the picture shows (error C). "It carries markers" is not on the card (error A). These marks go to day 22.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Review: in kindergarten you heard a long u, which says its name, and a short u. Say each word and the class says "long" or "short": cub (short), cube (long), tub (short), tube (long). Then blend: /t/ /u/ /b/, "tub"; /b/ /u/ /n/, "bun". Segment nut with three counters. Not scored.

#### 10 min. Map

Review: you learned short u in kindergarten. Add u with the picture of a sun to the sound-spelling map. New today: add qu with the picture of a queen. Say: "U says /u/. Qu says /k/ /w/ together."

Review: in kindergarten you learned that a plural names more than one. Show the card cat, then cats: "one cat, two cats." Say the end of cats: /s/. Show pan, then pans. The ending says /z/ here. Say: "The letter s at the end can say /s/ or /z/. It means more than one." If you know cat, the ending is a clue to the meaning of cats. Chain on tiles, one change at a time: cat, cats, mat, mats, pan, pans. The class says the sounds, then the word. Pairs build the chain (4 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Teach T and t. Say: "T is a tall stick with a hat across the top. The little t is a stick with a short bar across." Children trace and print. Dictation: say "Tim has mats." Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds: Tim begins with a capital T and the sentence ends with a period. Has is a mapped word.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Three groups of ten, 10 minutes each. At your table each child reads one page of Pat's Map aloud, about 30 seconds each (5 minutes) (5 minutes), then each child reads one word card: cat, cats, mat, mats, pan or pans (3 minutes). The other two groups sit in pairs: one pair reads Pat's Mess to each other, then the hammer poem's refrains. The chime moves each group along.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet: the exit marks, the six who were heard and their look-fors, the job each child pointed to (roll, carry or reach), and who is still not seen on error F. Name tomorrow's six. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No funnel or rice: draw the jar, the funnel and the spill on the board and tell the first try, where the rice spills, and the second, where it does not. No printer: draw the job menu on three cards. One child: watch or hear the demonstration, point to a job, do the exit and the letters; skip the groups and the rotation.

### At home

**Swap.** No funnel: roll a sheet of paper into a cone.

**Skip.** The group talk, the repair and the rotation.

- **10 min. The funnel.** Pour dry rice into a small jar, first with no funnel, then with one. Say: "A designed tool is made on purpose for a job."
  **Say.** "What was different the second time?" Expect: the funnel; the rice went in.
- **10 min. A job.** Say three jobs: pencils roll off a desk, a child carries pencils to the rug, a glue stick sits too high. Your child picks one, draws it and says its word.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints T t and "Tim has mats."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X21]] Read "Our paper scraps scatter. A tray with low sides holds things in." Show scraps in a pile, a cat asleep and books in a row. Ask: "Which job could a tray with low sides help with?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the demonstration, the job menu and the exit; cut the groups of three to the two you sit with. Longer: a child who finishes points to a second job and says which part of a tool from the texts could help.

### Words and supports

Words: designed tool, job, funnel, wheelbarrow, pour, narrow, load, part, closing. Frames: "A ___ is a tool for ___." "I would add ..." "Which part?" Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 22 reads the classroom sign model, a short paragraph that explains how a tool helps. If many children gave a name with no fact (error AA, "It is a tray"), a fact the card does not give (error A, "It carries markers") or a picture fact (error C, "The tray is blue") on today's exit, its first part opens with four minutes: reread the card line by line, point to the line that says it, cover the picture first when the slip was a picture fact, and try a new card with the same ask.
