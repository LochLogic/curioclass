# Day 79: How does a bird's body help it survive?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 78 closed unit 2 with the cumulative reading and spelling check; its marks are already recorded. Put the record sheet on your desk and name today's heard six. Hang the sound-spelling-map. Lay out the four head pictures, the article, the card sets and the exit card face down. Name six groups of five and the six catch-up children the record shows.

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook and question

Show the four bird-head pictures from page 1 of Beaks Find Food, in a row, with no words and no names. Say: "Look with care. What do you notice?" Pairs point and say (2 minutes); fifteen pairs talk at once, so listen at two or three. Hear four pairs say one thing, 30 seconds each. Expect: the beaks are not the same shape. Put the class question on chart paper and read it aloud twice: "How do a bird's body parts help it survive?" A child may pass.

#### 12 min. Words, then read Beaks Find Food

Show each word with its picture, say it, and have the class say it: bird, food, observe (look with care and tell what you see), survive (stay alive), detail (one fact about a topic), topic (what a page is all about), beak, bill (another name for a beak). About 20 seconds a word. Then read the whole article aloud once (about 190 words, 2 minutes), stopping at each heading to show the picture. Content note: a hawk tears meat and a heron catches a fish; say so plainly and show only the heads. A child who is afraid of birds may watch from the window side. Then read page 1 again, with each child following under the words with a finger (2 minutes). Say: "A bird needs food to survive." Children say it back.

#### 8 min. Retrieval

For six minutes, review last year's and unit 1's work, one prompt at a time.

- Show the title card: "Tools at Work, by Curio Class." Say: "This is the title and who made it." Say it together once.
- Review: you learned in unit 1 to say the topic and details of Builders at Work. Say: "Tell your partner the topic and two details." Expect: the topic is the tools a builder uses; one detail is that a hammer pushes a nail, another is that a level shows if a board is flat. Circle among the pairs and hear three, one minute each.

For two minutes: lay the three blank cards out of order, topic, facts and closing, and have the class put them in order, as in unit 1. Then each child points to the source label on their kept unit 2 notebook page and says where that fact came from. Write on the record sheet who did each alone. A miss is rebuilt in one step in the catch-up group or the day 81 repair; nothing is taught here.

#### 12 min. Topic and details, taught

Review: in kindergarten and in unit 1 you put detail cards under a topic card. Show the topic card with the sparrow's head: "A sparrow's beak." Say: "The topic is what the whole page is about." Put it on top. Read the two detail cards, "thick and strong" and "cracks hard seeds", and set them under it. Say: "This is about a sparrow's beak. One detail is thick and strong. Another detail is that it cracks hard seeds." Say it again and children say it with you. Point out that each detail tells something about the beak; none of them is the whole page.

Pairs have the duck's cards: the topic card "A duck's beak" and two detail cards, "flat" and "strains mud and water". Say: "Put the topic on top and the two details under it. Say it with the frame: This is about ___. One detail is ___." (4 minutes.) Walk between pairs. Then hear three pairs say it to the class, one minute each. Mark on the record sheet who did it alone. A child who says "It is about mud" has named a detail as the topic (error A): move the topic card back up and ask, "Is mud the whole page, or one fact?"

#### 8 min. Match and open the question

Pairs get the four beak drawings and the four feeding sentences you read aloud: "It cracks hard seeds." "It strains mud and water." "It catches a fish." "It tears meat into small bits." They match each sentence to a drawing and say why (4 minutes). Then reread the class question and ask: "What do we think?" Write three or four ideas as pictures and a few words on the chart under the question. These ideas are what the class thought before teaching. A child may pass; every idea is kept.

#### 10 min. Talk rules and linked turns

Show the talk-rules card and read its three lines: "Listen with care." "One speaker at a time." "Ask if you are confused." Pip shows a turn, one line at a time: "I will wait until you finish." "I will add to your idea." "What do you mean by that?" Review: Pip and linked turns, from unit 2.

Pairs take two linked turns on "How does a beak help a bird?" and one clarifying question, "What do you mean?" (6 minutes). Partner A says an idea; partner B adds to it; then B asks "What do you mean?" once. Everyone may pass. Hear three pairs, 80 seconds each (4 minutes): 15 pairs talk at once, so you hear three and note the rest from walking. Fifteen pairs times two turns of about 20 seconds fits inside the six minutes.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once to the class: "A hummingbird has a long, thin beak. It drinks nectar, a sweet drink inside flowers." The other children quietly add colour to a head picture; the heard six come to your table one at a time (40 seconds each, 4 minutes). Show the three pictures: a hummingbird with its long beak, a flower alone, a pile of seeds. Say: "Point to the picture the card is mostly about." ([[s03-X79a]]) Then, with the card in view: "Tell me two things the card says about the hummingbird." ([[s03-X79b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once and point), not yet seen. A child who points to the flower has taken a detail for the topic (error A).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken, not scored. Clap robin, two claps, and split it: rob, in. Say /s/ /u/ /n/; the class says "sun". Blend /c/ /a/ /t/, then read cat on the board. Say pin and pine; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound, the vowel that says its name?" Expect: pine. Blend /s/ /l/ /e/ /d/, "sled". Segment crab: /k/ /r/ /a/ /b/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 12 starts here. Say: "A word can have two parts. Each part has one vowel sound." Write picnic. Clap it: two claps. Say: "A closed syllable has one vowel and ends in a consonant, and its vowel is short." Find the two consonants in the middle, c and n, and chop between them: pic | nic. Read each part, then blend. Do the same with sunset (sun | set), napkin (nap | kin) and rabbit (rab | bit, chop between the two b's). Children clap, chop with a hand, and read each word with the class. Then every child finds the vowel in each part on the board and says its sound. The sound-spelling-map stays up. Because this cycle's spellings are only now being taught, the rotation uses the cycle 11 reader until they are mapped.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Say each word and children print it a part at a time: picnic, then rabbit. Print the pairs A a and R r from the dictation. Then dictate the sentence, "A rabbit is on the mat." Say it once whole, then a word at a time; a, is and the are mapped words. Children stretch each word, print it, and read it back; walk round for a missing vowel (error M). From today the ten-minute catch-up group replaces independent practice, for the children the record names.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Begin with the ten-minute catch-up group: the six children the record names meet you at the table to map, blend, read, spell and reread, from the cycle 11 reader and its words. For the next 20 minutes four groups of six come to the table, 5 minutes each: each child reads a page of I Look Out and spells one word from the chain on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time. Six children times 50 seconds is 5 minutes. The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full today. The other children work in pairs at four stations: reread I Look Out, chain picnic, sunset, napkin and rabbit with letter cards, spell a word on sound boxes, read the word cards a, is and the. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the heard-six column, the cycle record and the exit marks to the unit 3 record sheet: 2 for shown, 1 for shown with help, 0 for not yet seen. A child outside the six is not yet heard, never missed. Collect the pages.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the cards on scrap paper and read the article from the page. One child: do the hook, read the article, sort the sparrow cards, do the exit aloud and chop the four words. One projector: show the head pictures and the article page on the screen; the cards stay on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read it from the page. No cards: draw two boxes.

**Skip.** The talk rules, the rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Read and say.** Read the first page of a short bird book or article aloud, then the picture. **Say.** "What is this page about?" Expect: one bird part, such as its beak, not one small fact.
- **5 min. Topic and details.** Draw a box for the topic and two boxes under it. Your child tells you the topic and two details.
- **10 min. Words.** Clap picnic and rabbit. Chop between the two middle letters, read each part, and print "A rabbit is on the mat."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X79a]] [[s03-X79b]] Read: "A hummingbird has a long, thin beak. It drinks nectar, a sweet drink inside flowers." Your child points to what the card is about, then tells you two things.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the article, the topic and details, the exit and the chop. Cut the match cards. Longer: a child says a second detail for the duck's beak.

### Words and supports

Words: topic, detail, observe, survive, beak, bill. Frames: "This is about ___." "One detail is ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once. Every card is read aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can pick the topic and who names a detail instead (error A). Day 80 opens with a five-minute repair of day 79's exit for those children, then rereads page 2 of Beaks Find Food and separates what the picture shows from what the words tell.
