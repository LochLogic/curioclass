# Day 82: What wings help a bird do

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print Wings in Motion with its contents and diagram. Lay out the topic card, the four detail cards, the single-feather card, the practice contents and the exit pictures face down. Copy day 81's exit marks and name today's heard six. The article is new today, so keep it covered until the reading.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This is made up for practice." Show a practice contents: "Seeds, page 2. Roots, page 3." Say the repair for error D from day 81's exit, in which the cover was used instead of the contents. Put the cover away. **Ask.** "Find the roots fact. Where do you start?" Expect: with the contents entry, Roots, page 3. Children trace with a finger: the entry, the page, the heading Roots, the sentence "Roots hold a plant in the soil." Say it together: "The contents leads to the page and the heading."

#### 12 min. Words, then read Wings in Motion

Show each word with its picture and have the class say it: wing, push, glide (move through the air without flapping), hover (stay in one place in the air), rise (go up). About 20 seconds a word. Then show the contents and read the whole article aloud once (about 195 words, 2 minutes), stopping at each heading to show the diagram or picture. Content note: nothing here is graphic. Then read the opening paragraph again, "Most birds have wings. Wings help most birds fly. A bird's wings are covered with feathers.", and have each pair reread it, one sentence each, with a finger under the words (3 minutes).

#### 12 min. Topic and details

Say: "This is about wings." Put the topic card up: the flying sparrow and "Wings help a bird fly." Show the single-feather picture next to the wrong sentence: "The article is about a feather." **Ask.** "Is it about a feather or about wings?" Expect: wings; a feather is only one detail. A child who names the feather as the topic has named a detail as the topic (error A).

Model one detail aloud: "Push the air is a detail. It tells how wings work. It goes under the topic." Place it under the topic card. Then the class sorts one more together, "glide". Pairs sort the other two cards, quick turns and stay in one place in the air, say each with the frame, "This is about wings. One detail is ___.", and tell where in the article they heard it. Hear two pairs, one minute each. Review: the topic card and the detail cards, from day 79.

#### 8 min. Three questions

Pairs ask and answer these spoken questions, two turns each, read twice.

1. "What do wings help the bird do?" Expect: fly.
2. "Which two details go under the topic?" Expect: any two of push the air, quick turns, glide, stay in one place in the air; none is the feather.
3. "What covers a bird's wings, and is that the whole article?" Expect: feathers; no, it is one detail; the article is about wings.

Fifteen pairs take two turns of about 20 seconds; hear four pairs, one minute each.

#### 8 min. Retell

Each child retells the topic and two details to a partner with the turn card, using the frame, "This is about wings. One detail is ___. Another detail is ___." (3 minutes). Then hear six children, the heard six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child who gives only one detail, or the feather as the topic, is helped with the cards.

#### 10 min. Draw

Hand out the student page. Say: "Draw the topic in the top box. Draw two details under it. Copy the word wings." Children draw for 8 minutes; for the last two minutes each partner tells the other what is in the three boxes. Walk round and mark: the topic box shows wings; the two boxes under it show details.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread aloud the part of Wings in Motion headed Wide Wings Glide. The others add color to their drawing; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the three pictures: an eagle's big, wide wings, the sky alone, an eagle's foot. Say: "Point to the picture this part is mostly about." ([[s03-X82av2]]) Then, with the page in view: "Tell me two things this part says." ([[s03-X82bv2]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the part once), not yet seen. A child who points to the sky has taken a detail for the topic (error A).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say tub and tube; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: tube. Blend /b/ /r/ /i/ /m/, "brim". Say frog and the class says the first sound, the vowel sound and the last: /f/, /o/, /g/. Segment swim: /s/ /w/ /i/ /m/.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 12, lesson 64. Reread the chain on the board, chopping and reading: basket, kitten, magnet, hidden, napkin, picnic. Then read a whole sentence with the cycle's patterns and check its meaning: "A rabbit hid in the sunset grass." Children read it together, then point to the word that tells where the rabbit hid. **Ask.** "Where did the rabbit hide?" Expect: in the grass. Children read it again, with their voices telling the meaning. The sound-spelling-map stays up.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs K k and P p. Dictate two sentences: "Kim sat on a napkin." and "Pat hid a basket." Say each whole, then a word at a time. Say that Kim and Pat are names, so each begins with a capital letter; each sentence ends with a dot. Children stretch each word, print it and read it back. Repair for a missing vowel (error M): tap the sounds again.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first: the six children the record names meet you to map, blend, read, spell and reread. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, with A Sunset Picnic: each child reads a page and spells sunset on a card, 50 seconds each, one child at a time. The heard-six column names the six whose reading you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread the reader, chain words with letter cards, spell a word on sound boxes, sort the four wing detail cards under the topic card. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the retell notes, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the cards on scrap paper and read the article from the page. One child: read the article, sort the four details under the topic, retell, draw and do the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw a topic box and four small boxes. No printed article: read a short bird book.

**Skip.** The rotation and the stations.

- **10 min. Read.** Read a short page about a bird's wings or feet. **Say.** "What is this page about?" Expect: one body part, such as wings, not one small fact.
- **10 min. Topic and details.** Your child draws the topic in one box and two details under it, then tells them.
- **5 min. Words.** Read "A rabbit hid in the sunset grass." and print "Kim sat on a napkin."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X82av2]] [[s03-X82bv2]] Read the part of Wings in Motion headed Wide Wings Glide. Your child points to what it is mostly about, then tells two things.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reading, the sort, the retell and the exit. Cut the third question. Longer: a child retells with a third detail.

### Words and supports

Words: wing, push, glide, hover, rise. Frames: "This is about ___." "One detail is ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can tell the topic from a detail. If many pointed to the swimmer (error A), day 83 opens with a five-minute repair of day 82's exit: sort two details under one topic on a fresh card. Day 83 then reads Feathers Protect and uses the glossary and a label.
