# Day 83: Use the glossary and a label to explain feathers

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print Feathers Protect with its cutaway diagram and draw the bird-body outline on the board. Lay out the repair cards, the text-feature cards, the counters and the exit pictures face down. Keep the cycle 12 sample card in your folder, out of sight; no word on it has been taught or practised. Copy day 82's exit marks and name today's heard six.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Say: "This is made up for practice." Show a topic card, "A lunch box", and two detail cards, "has a lid" and "holds a sandwich". Say the repair for error A from day 82's exit, in which a detail was taken for the topic. Put "has a lid" on top and ask: "Is this the whole page, or one fact about it?" Move it down. The class sorts both under the topic and says: "This is about a lunch box. One detail is it has a lid." Everyone says it; nobody is named.

#### 12 min. Words, then read Feathers Protect

Show each word with its picture and have the class say it: soft, warm, protect (keep safe), identify (name what it is), describe (tell what it is like), retell (tell it again in your own words), feather, down. About 15 seconds a word. Then show the contents and read the whole article aloud once (about 175 words, 2 minutes), stopping at each heading to show the picture. Content note: only birds in cold, wind and rain; nothing is graphic. One line says a bird can smooth its feathers down. Say aloud: "Here down means toward the body. In the glossary, down means soft feathers. It is the same word with two jobs." Read the glossary page aloud once.

#### 12 min. Glossary and label, taught

Show the heading Down for Warmth and read it: "Soft down feathers lie close to the bird's body. Down traps air. The trapped air helps keep the bird warm." Model aloud: "I do not know what down is. The glossary is a list of words and what they mean, at the back. It says: down, soft feathers that lie close to a bird's body. Now I know." Show the glossary feature card beside the label card. Then show the cutaway diagram: "A label names a part. This label says down, and its line points to the part close to the body." Say: "The glossary tells what the word means. The label tells where it is." Children say: "glossary, label" with the icons.

Review: the contents, heading and label, from days 80 and 81. Pairs find the glossary line for trap on page 3 and the label for wing feathers on the diagram, with a finger. Say: "Read me the glossary line for trap." Expect: to hold in. Walk among the pairs for five minutes; hear two pairs, one minute each. A child who goes to the cover or the heading for a meaning is steered to the glossary at the back.

#### 8 min. Three questions

Pairs ask and answer these spoken questions, two turns each, read twice.

1. "Which feathers help with warmth?" Expect: down.
2. "How does the glossary explain down?" Expect: soft feathers that lie close to a bird's body.
3. "How do the label and the words show where down lies?" Expect: the label points to a part by the body; the words say it lies close to the body.

Fifteen pairs take two turns of about 20 seconds; hear four pairs, one minute each.

#### 10 min. Retell

Lay the topic card, "Feathers protect a bird", and the detail cards: down keeps a bird warm, a bird fluffs up when it is cold, a duck's feathers keep water out. Review: the topic card with detail cards under it, from day 79. Say: "Choose two details." In pairs, with the turn card, each child retells the topic and two details (4 minutes). Then hear six children, the heard six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child who gives only a detail has named a detail as the topic (error A). The last two minutes: collect the cards.

#### 8 min. Draw and label

Hand out the student page, which has the bird-body outline with a wing, a chest and a tail. Say: "Copy the label down on the chest. Copy the label wing feathers on the wing." The labels are on the board; children copy, not read. Walk round and mark: each label sits on the right part. Review: a label names a part.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read aloud the glossary line for wing from Wings in Motion: "wing: the part of a bird that moves to push air." The others add color to their outline; the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). Show the Wings in Motion diagram, a sparrow from above with the labels wing and feathers. Say: "Point to the label for the part the glossary tells about." ([[s03-X83av2]]) Then reread page 1 of Feathers Protect, "Feathers protect a bird in cold, wind and rain.", and ask: "What does protect mean? Use the glossary." ([[s03-X83bv2]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the line once), not yet seen. A child who points to the biggest-drawn part has used the picture, not the glossary (error C).

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say bit and bite; **Ask.** "Which has the long vowel sound?" Expect: bite. Blend /g/ /r/ /a/ /b/, "grab". Review: in unit 2 you moved one counter for each sound. Give each child counters and say: "Move one counter for each sound in snap." ([[s03-A3]]) Watch the desks; write the heard six's counters and what each says. The rest are marked as they work.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 12, lesson 65. Reread the chain, chopping and reading: basket, kitten, magnet, hidden, napkin, picnic, cactus, sunset. Children spell napkin and picnic part by part on their page. Then read a sentence, "A rabbit hid in the sunset grass.", and check its meaning. Teach only this cycle's spellings and mapped words: a, the, is, was. The progress sample is taken in the rotation, one child at a time.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print the pairs R r and B b. Dictate: "A rabbit is in the basket." Say it whole, then a word at a time. Children stretch each word, print it and read it back, marking every sound. Repair for a missing vowel (error M): tap the sounds again.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The progress sample is taken from every child. Take the six catch-up children first, in their ten minutes, one at a time, 50 seconds each, then map, blend, read, spell and reread. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, 50 seconds each, one child at a time: show the word card from your folder and say: "Read this word." ([[s03-A1]]) Remove the card and say: "Write the word I say." ([[s03-A2]]) Say the word once in a sentence, then once alone. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The heard-six column names the six whose sample you write up in full. The rest work in pairs at stations: reread A Sunset Picnic, chain words with letter cards, spell a word on sound boxes, sort the feather detail cards. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the retell notes, the sample marks and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Regroup within the block using the sample: children who read and wrote both words stay in the small groups; any child who read or wrote neither goes to tomorrow's catch-up list. The rest stay where they are. A child outside the six is not yet heard, never missed.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the outline and the cutaway on the board and read the article from the page. One child: read the article, find the down line and the glossary line, draw and label, do the exit aloud; the sample is taken as one child at a time.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed article: read a short bird book with a glossary. No counters: use coins or beans.

**Skip.** The rotation, the stations and the sample.

- **10 min. Glossary and label.** Find a word in a book's glossary. **Say.** "What does this word mean? Which label shows where it is?" Expect: the meaning from the glossary line; the part from the label.
- **10 min. Draw and label.** Your child draws a bird, labels its wing, and says one fact about feathers.
- **5 min. Words.** Spell napkin and picnic part by part, then print "A rabbit is in the basket."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X83av2]] [[s03-X83bv2]] Read the glossary line for wing, then "Feathers protect a bird in cold, wind and rain." Your child points to the wing label and says what protect means.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the glossary and label model, the exit and the sample. Cut the third question. Longer: a child finds a second word in the glossary and says what it means.

### Words and supports

Words: glossary, label, down, protect, retell. Frames: "The glossary says ___." "The label shows ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can use the glossary and the words, not the biggest-drawn part (error C). Day 84 opens with a five-minute repair of day 83's exit: mark a picture symbol or a word symbol beside each source on a fresh pair. If many counted a cluster as one sound on the snap item (error L), day 84's rotation takes three minutes to move one counter for each sound on four fresh words. Day 84 then reads about feet.
