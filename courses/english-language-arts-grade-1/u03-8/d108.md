# Day 108: Start the bird riddle from a story and two poems

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 107's exit marks and name today's heard six, the six catch-up children and the six children for the drawing. Print a student page and a riddle card for each child. Make the repair card and its strips. Set out the poem pages, the Mina page and the Feathers Protect page, the word cards, the sound-spelling map and the record sheet. Keep the two held words of the cycle 17 sample in your folder. The exit pictures stay face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Repair for day 107's exit (error A, and s03-X107av2): a child who named a detail, such as the fish alone, as the topic. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read it once: "A sparrow has round wings. The wings flap." Pairs have three strips: "the sparrow's wings", "round", "crack hard seeds". All pairs at once, two minutes: lay the strip that says what the whole card is about at the top. **Ask.** "Which strip says what the whole card is about?" Expect: the sparrow's wings. Then slide "round" under it; "crack hard seeds" waits outside because this card does not say it. Hear three pairs say "The card is about ___." Review: a topic is what the whole card is about and a detail is one fact under it (kindergarten).

#### 5 min. Launch

Say: "This riddle is made up for practice." Say it: "I have a flat beak. I have webbed feet. What am I?" Children say a duck. Review: in kindergarten you heard riddles; a riddle gives clues and each clue narrows the answer. Ask which clue helped first. Show a riddle card: the frame "I have ___. I ___. What am I?", two clue lines, the name hidden on the back and the drawing under a flap. Name the task: "Make a riddle about an expert bird. Hide its name, give two true clues from the pages and draw the bird. We share on day 119." Today is the start: you listen, talk and draw one bird part.

#### 12 min. Two poems

Hold up the two poem pages. Read Feather Coat whole, then Wing Clues whole, at a pace children can follow (about 77 words in all, under a minute). Read both again; on the second reading children say the last word of each line with you and clap the beat. Then three short asks, children pointing on their own pages, you walking round: **Ask.** "Point to one clue word." Expect: wings, toes or beak. **Ask.** "Point to where a line ends and the next line begins." That is a line break: it puts a clue at the end of a line. A line break is the poem's choice, not a fact. **Ask.** "Which bird do the four clues describe?" Expect: a sparrow (the teacher's key; say it only after children have guessed). Say that a coat with no buttons is imagined and the feathers under it are real.

#### 10 min. Story and article

Reread page 5 of Mina at the Window (Review: you heard it on days 88 and 106): "Then the sparrow puffed up. Its feathers stood out until it looked like a round brown ball. Mina's pencil stopped. She bit her lip." Then read the opening of Feathers Protect: "Only birds have feathers. Feathers cover a bird's body. Feathers protect a bird in cold, wind and rain." About 55 words in all. **Ask.** "Point to a body part the story page names." Expect: its feathers. **Ask.** "Point to a body part the article opening names." Expect: feathers. Do not ask what everything shares; the exit does that on other parts. A child who points at Mina's lip has chosen a body part the story gives; accept it and ask which one a bird has.

#### 10 min. Discussion routine, reviewed

Review: the turn card, from grade 1 unit 1 and day 107: listen, restate ("You said ___"), then reply. Show it with a puppet in two turns: the puppet says "Wings help it fly"; you say "I like blue birds" and then correct it with "You said wings help it fly. I think feathers keep it warm." That reply is an error K and the second is right. Pairs practice once, all at once, three minutes, on "Which clue was easiest to find?" Hear six pairs, 50 seconds each, five minutes; a pair that skips the restating gets one prompt: "What did your partner say?" A child may pass.

#### 13 min. Talk and draw

Turn card, pairs, all at once, five minutes: "What does the poem imagine that the article explains?" Expect: the poem imagines a coat; the article explains that feathers protect a bird. Walk round and listen to six pairs, 40 seconds each, inside the five. Then eight minutes: each child draws one bird part from the poem or the article on the student page and prints one word label (wings, feathers, beak or toes); a scribe prints it for a child who asks. Hear six different children, 40 seconds each: "Tell me about your part." Keep the drawing in the riddle folder.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read aloud three short parts not worked today: Mina at the Window, page 4 ("The sparrow held on tight. Its toes curled around the branch."), the third line of Wing Clues and the last two sentences of Feet for Perching in Feet That Help. Show the two pictures. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which picture shows what all three parts are about?" ([[s03-X108av2]]) Then, with the parts in view, the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each: **Ask.** "Tell me one thing the article part says." ([[s03-X108bv2]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread once), not yet seen. A child who points to the branch alone has named a detail, not the topic (error A). A child who offers a fact the part does not give has made error B. Name day 108's misses for day 109's repair.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say not and note; which has the long vowel sound? Blend /k/ /l/ /u/ /z/. Give the first, vowel and last sound of Pete. Segment theme.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 17, lesson 90: review of u_e and e_e (day 104) and of the two-syllable words of day 106. Show tub and tube, cut and cute, tune, mule, mute, dune and rule on cards. Map each, count the vowel sounds and decode; the silent e makes the vowel say its name (error H counts letters, not sounds). Chain tub, tube, tune, dune with letter cards, then cut, cute, mute, mule, rule. Combine known syllable types: inside and sunrise, each in two parts. Spell mule and tube, about 40 seconds a word. Read the strip "June has a tube." and ask "Does that make sense?" Then say who the strip is about (Review: Cones and Tubes, reread for meaning). The held words of the sample do not appear anywhere today before the sample.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print I i, J j, K k and L l, four pairs, dated; the page goes in the separate folder kept for 118 (every letter form was taught by day 27; feedback written after class). Dictate: "Luke has a flute." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Luke is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Luke and flute the last e is silent. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: for five minutes the cycle 17 sample at your table, 100 seconds each at most; for five minutes map, blend, read, spell and reread tube, tune, mule and cute. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. Each child does the sample from your folder: show the first held word, say "Read this word" (s03-A25); remove the card, say the second held word once in a sentence and once alone and ask the child to write it (s03-A26); then show the strip "Pete has a cute mule." and ask the child to read it. Every child reads the first word and writes the second; keep the cards away from the stations. Mark each of the three: shown, shown with help (you map the word once), not yet seen. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six. The rest reread Cones and Tubes in pairs for expression, chain tub, tube, tune, dune with letter cards and sort the cards cut, cute, mute and mule by whether the vowel says its name, in pairs at stations. The marks set the groups for the next days.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the repair marks, the exit marks, the sample marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Put each riddle card and each drawing in its folder. List by name the children who named a detail as the topic (error A) and the pairs who skipped the restating (error K), for day 109.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the riddle card on folded paper and read the two poems from the teacher page. One child: do the repair, hear the poems, draw one part, answer the exit aloud and take the sample.

### At home

**Swap.** No poem page: say a short rhyme about a bird you know and point to where each line ends.

**Skip.** The rotation and the sample.

- **10 min. Riddle.** Say: "I have a flat beak. I have webbed feet. What am I?" Your child draws a bird and hides its name. **Say.** "Which clue helped most?" Expect: a body part, such as the beak or the feet.
- **5 min. Poem.** Read one bird rhyme twice. Your child points to where a line ends.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Say not and note. Which has the long vowel sound? Expect: note.
- **5 min. Print.** I i, J j, K k, L l and "Luke has a flute."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X108av2]] [[s03-X108bv2]] Read the three short parts the page names. Your child points to the picture, then says one thing the article part says.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the poems, the exit and the sample; give the story and article one minute each. Longer: a child draws a second bird part and labels it.

### Words and supports

Words: riddle, clue, poem, line break, label, topic, detail. Frames: "The poem says ___. The article says ___." "You said ___. I think ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can name what three cards share and say one fact the information card gives. A child who picked a detail (error A) gets a five-minute repair on a fresh set of three cards on day 109; if many replies skipped the partner's point (error K), the repair is the puppet exchange instead. Day 109 checks a scientific drawing: a label is checked against its sentence.
