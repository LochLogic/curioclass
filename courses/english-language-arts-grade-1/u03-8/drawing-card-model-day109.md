# Day 109 drawing cards (the model and two pair cards)

Three printed cards, each a Curio Class original, invented for practice, with that line at the foot. Every sentence restates Beaks Find Food, Feet That Help or Heron by the Water (g1-u3-01). Plain black line drawings, thick lines, no shading. A label is a word beside a part. A label is checked against the sentence, not the picture. These are teaching cards; the exit card uses another bird.

**Model card (the teacher's).** A hawk drawn standing on a post, side view, with a thin line from each label to a part. Sentence beneath: "A hawk has a hooked beak. The hawk has sharp claws." The two labels are swapped: the label claws points to the beak and the label beak points to the claws. The class finds both and swaps them.

**Pair card 1 (nothing wrong).** A sparrow's foot gripping a twig. Labels: toes (to the toes) and branch (to the twig). Sentence beneath: "A sparrow has four toes on each foot. The toes grip a branch." Every label is right; the pair checks each and says so.

**Pair card 2 (a label the sentence does not give).** A heron's head and neck. Labels: beak (to the beak) and toes (to the neck). Sentence beneath: "A heron has a long, pointed beak." The label toes names no part of this drawing and the sentence does not give it; the pair crosses it out and leaves the neck unlabelled.
