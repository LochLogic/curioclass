# Day 110: Choose an expert bird and find two facts

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 109's exit marks and name today's heard six, the six catch-up children and the pairs for the talk. Print the four page sets, enough for each child to take two pages, and two note strips for each child. Set out the four picture cards on four tables and the choice list. Make the repair card. The exit card stays face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Repair for day 109's exit (error C, and s03-X109av2; or error B, and s03-X109bv2): a child who trusted the picture, or gave a label the sentence does not state. Use the commoner. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Show a sparrow in flight with two labels, wings (to a wing) and toes (to a foot). Read the sentence once: "A sparrow has round wings." All children at once, point. **Ask.** "Which label does the sentence not give?" Expect: toes. A true label still needs a page that says it. Put a small eye beside the picture and a small pencil beside the sentence: the sentence is the proof. Hear three pairs say "The page says ___."

#### 8 min. Words, then two pages

Show a picture card for each word and say it once, children repeating: expert, source, clue. An expert knows a lot about one thing. A source is where a fact came from (Review: a note carries a source label, days 91 and 99). A clue is one true fact that helps someone guess (Review: riddle and clue, day 108). Then read the sparrow pages to everyone, about 32 words: "Strong Beak, page 2. A sparrow has a thick, strong beak. The beak cracks hard seeds. Four Toes, page 4. A sparrow has four toes on each foot. The toes grip a branch." Show the contents line at the top, the heading, the page label P1 and P2, and the glossary line at the foot.

#### 10 min. Choose

Read the duck, heron and hawk pages once each, about 30 words each (D1 and D2, H1 and H2, K1 and K2), two minutes in all. Each child then points to one of the four bird picture cards, four minutes, and takes that bird's two pages and two note strips at its table, four minutes. A bird may be chosen by many children. No child is asked to say why or to report on home. Write each choice on the choice list. A child who cannot choose picks the bird nearest; the choice changes on day 112 if it must.

#### 22 min. Find facts

Model, four minutes, on a made-up page: "Wings in Motion, page 2, label W: A hummingbird's wings beat very fast. It can hover." Point to the line, say "wings beat fast", print the page label W in the box of a note strip and point the arrow at the line. Say: "A fact is on the page; the arrow shows where." Review: a note has its label (days 91 and 99).

Then each child works at a table with the bird's two pages, twelve minutes: find one fact on each page and make a note on each strip: a word or a short phrase, or a picture, or a scribed line; the page label (P1, P2, D1, D2, H1, H2, K1 or K2) in the box; the arrow to the line. Read a page aloud again for any child. Hear six children, one minute each, six of the twelve minutes: "Show me where the page says it." A child who gives a fact the page does not state has made error B; point to the exact sentence first. Last, three minutes, partners at the same table point to each other's arrow. Three minutes to tidy and put the pages and strips in the folder.

#### 10 min. Talk

Turn card, pairs from different tables, all at once, three minutes, then six pairs heard, fifty seconds each, five minutes (two minutes to set up): "Which two facts together make a clue only your bird has?" Expect: "My bird has a thick, strong beak and four toes." The partner says whether that fits their bird too. Review: listen, restate ("You said ___"), then reply. A child may pass; the partner then reads a note aloud.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet. Show the two pictures. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which expert-bird picture fits both sources?" ([[s03-X110a]]) Then, with the card in view, the heard six come to your table, 40 seconds each: **Ask.** "Which source tells what the eyes help it do? Say U or V." ([[s03-X110b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who points to the plant alone has named a detail, not the bird (error A). A child who says U has mixed the two sources up. Name day 110's misses for day 111's repair.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say ride and riding, use and using; what do you hear added? Blend /j/ /o/ /k/ /s/. Give the first, vowel and last sound of hiked. Segment rides.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 18, lesson 92: -ing on silent-e bases, the dropped e, and the ending read as a whole. Review: the e drops before -ing and -ed and stays before -s (day 109). Show ride and riding, use and using, bake and baking, make and making, take and taking. Read the base, add the ending and blend again (error J omits the ending: a child who reads riding as ride reads the base, adds ing and blends again). Then the words of the reader: hiked, hoped, joked, named and smiled, each a base with the e dropped and ed added; was is mapped, and a, the and to are known. Decode, then spell: making and hiked, about 40 seconds a word. The reader A Day at the Lake is read only after these are mapped.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print Q q, R r, S s and T t, four pairs, dated; the page goes in the separate folder kept for 118 (every letter form was taught by day 27; feedback written after class). Dictate: "Dave is riding." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Dave is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Dave the last e is silent, and in riding the e of ride is dropped. Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Rotation

The ten-minute catch-up group first, the six children the record names: map, blend, read, spell and reread; today ride, riding, make, making. Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at your table, 50 seconds each, one child at a time; the heard-six column names them. Each child reads the pair ride and riding (a child who says ride for riding shows error J), then one page of A Day at the Lake, then spells making. The rest reread Cones and Tubes in pairs for expression, chain ride, rides, riding with letter cards and spell the words of the reader on small boards, in pairs at stations. Thirty children: six catch-up, four groups of six. Write each miss in the cycle record.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the note marks, the cycle record and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Put each note strip and the child's two pages in the riddle folder. List by name the children who named a detail as the bird (error A) or mixed the two sources, for day 111.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy each page onto a strip and read it aloud. One child: choose a bird, find two facts, make two notes and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No pages: use any two short bird pages and call them page 1 and page 2.

**Skip.** The rotation.

- **10 min. Two facts.** Read two short bird pages. Your child points to one fact on each and says the page. **Say.** "Show me where the page says it." Expect: a finger on the line.
- **5 min. Clue.** Your child says two facts that make a clue only that bird has.
- **5 min. Words.** Say ride and riding. Your child says what was added.
- **5 min. Print.** Q q, R r, S s, T t and "Dave is riding."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X110a]] [[s03-X110b]] Read the short card the page shows. Your child points to the bird, then says which source tells what a part helps the bird do.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the choice, the notes and the exit; skip the talk. Longer: a child makes a third note from the second page.

### Words and supports

Words: expert, source, clue, page, note. Frames: "The page says ___." "My bird has ___." "You said ___. I think ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can name the bird two sources describe and say which source tells what a part helps the bird do. A child who named a detail as the bird (error A), or said the other source, gets a five-minute repair on fresh sources on day 111. Day 111 models the route from the contents to the heading to the glossary.
