# Day 48 cold passages: On the Blanket and The Moon Is Rock (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Read on day 48 only; the class has never heard them. Keep both covered until the check opens. Two tiny pieces on one card set, side by side: a story and an information text. Print each in large print with its picture, and "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of each card. The teacher reads each aloud; children may follow the print. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading.

## Card 1, story (s02-A9v2, s02-A9bv2, s02-A10v2, s02-A11v2, s02-A12v2)

> **On the Blanket**
>
> Owen and Mom spread a soft blanket on the grass. They lie down and look up at the night sky. The moon is big and round. Mom points to a bright star near it. Owen smiles. He feels happy.

Picture: a boy and his mother lying on a blanket on the grass, looking up at a round moon and one bright star.

The feeling word is happy. The touch word is soft. Bright, big and round are words for what you see, not touch.

## Card 2, information (s02-A11v2, s02-A12v2)

> **The Moon Is Rock**
>
> The moon is a big ball of rock. It has almost no air and no rain. No plants grow there. People have flown to the moon in a spaceship. They walked on it and left footprints in the moon dust.

Picture: the gray, rocky ground of the moon close up, with one boot print in the dust. No flag and no names.

Facts checked 2026-11-24. NASA Space Place, "All About the Moon" (a rocky place with almost no atmosphere; twelve astronauts set foot on its dusty surface), https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-moon/en/ (sources.csv g1-u2-f7). NASA Science, "Earth's Moon" (no liquid water; bootprints show where astronauts worked), https://science.nasa.gov/moon/ (sources.csv g1-u2-f8). The text says "almost no air", not "no air", to match both pages.
