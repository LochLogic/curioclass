# Day 47 cold passage: Mira Waits for the Stars (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Read on day 47 only; the class has never heard it. Keep it covered until the check opens. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of the page. The teacher reads it aloud twice; children may follow the print. Plain black line drawings on white, thick lines, no shading. It is read for s02-A6v2, s02-A7v2 and s02-A8v2; the three event pictures and the three lesson cards are on card-t-cloud-lantern.

> **Mira Waits for the Stars**
>
> Mira wants to see the stars. She runs out to the yard. The sun is going down, and the sky is still bright. Mira looks and looks. She sees no stars at all.
>
> "Where are the stars?" she asks.
>
> Grandma sits down on the step. "Wait with me," she says. "The stars are there all day. The sun is so bright that we cannot see them."
>
> So Mira sits and waits. The sky turns dark blue. One star peeks out. Then two, then ten!
>
> At last, the whole sky is full of stars. Mira hugs Grandma. "I am glad we waited," she says.

Picture under the story: a child and her grandmother sitting on a back step, looking up at a dark sky full of stars.

Words to say before reading: "A yard is the grass around a home. To peek is to look out a little."

The lesson the story teaches: waiting can bring good things. The facts it leans on (stars are in the sky by day; the bright sun hides them) are the unit's own, from Stars in Daylight and Darkness (texts/g1-u2-01); the story does not retell any unit text. Fact checked 2026-11-24 against Caltech IPAC (NASA), Cool Cosmos, "Why do stars only come out at night?" (the stars are in the sky both day and night; bright sunlight hides the much fainter stars), https://coolcosmos.ipac.caltech.edu/ask/212-Why-do-stars-only-come-out-at-night- (sources.csv g1-u2-f6).
