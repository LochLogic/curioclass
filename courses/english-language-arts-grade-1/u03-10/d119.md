# Day 119: Share the riddle game, then respond

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read the unit record. The completion group is every child with no day 115 record or no cohort entry on days 116 to 118, at most 12, cut by least evidence. The rest are recorded not yet seen; days 158 and 159 pick up RF.1.3 completion. Sort the exit misses of days 115 to 118 into four repair groups. Lay the six drawings out as a gallery.

### The day

#### 40 min. Complete missing evidence

The completion group comes to your table one at a time, 3 minutes each, no more than 12 children, which uses 36 of the 40 minutes and leaves 4 for seating. No completed record is repeated, so cite the recent valid record instead. A child missing the day 115 record gets the day 115 pages, Feathers Protect and the menu: find the fact by the contents and heading, and say what the page is about. A child missing a cohort entry gets the held cards: catnip, pilot and cupcake counted by vowel sounds, then divided, then the ending list read. Review: these are the same tasks as days 115 to 118 (days 79 and 99, 111 and 115). A child may ask for a word again: shown with help. Say what you saw in one line to the child; the note goes on the record. Every observation ends by minute 40, before the midpoint of the day.

Everyone else works in pairs and hears no one: rehearse the riddle card so the name is held back; set a drawing on a desk for the gallery; reread Wing Clues and A Day at the Lake a page each. A child who is hurried through the 3 minutes is not recorded; stop at minute 40 and write unfinished.

#### 20 min. Share the riddle and drawing game

This is a share, not a mark. Say: "These riddles are the ones you wrote." Six groups of five, all at once, at desks with the drawings. Each child reads the riddle with the name held back (1 minute), the others guess and point to the drawing in the gallery (1 minute), and the child names the source label of one clue (1 minute). Five children at 3 minutes is 15 minutes, with two minutes to settle and three to move. You join each group for about 2 minutes, six groups, 12 minutes, moving as a child changes. Review: listen, restate, then reply (day 107). The completion group takes part in the share, as everyone does. Hear what children say; no one is scored here. A child may pass; the group guesses from the drawing.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say fin and fine; which has the long vowel sound? Blend /s/ /l/ /i/ /d/. Give the first, vowel and last sound of gate. Segment stone.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Review; no new pattern. Whole class on small boards, then six tables of five at the map, a minute a table. Review: the e dropped before -ing and -ed (day 109); the e kept before -er and -est (day 112). Chain nice, nicer; wide, wider; skate, skated. Decode sunrise, pancake, napkin and baking, counting the vowel sounds, not the vowel letters. Spell wider and baking, about 40 seconds a word. Reread A Day at the Lake, pages 13 to 16, for expression. The matched readers wait for the rotation.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Dictate "Mike has a kite." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Mike is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Mike and kite the last e is silent. Then each child prints two pairs the written feedback marked (practice, not collected). Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 10 min. Rotation

Four groups at your table, 2 minutes each, with the matched reader. Six children are heard in all, from the heard-six column, about 80 seconds each; you hear the rest of each group read one line. The other groups reread in pairs, a finger under each word. Mark the heard six: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move today's completion rows, the share notes and the heard-six column to the unit record. The retries below never rescore earlier records. Sort the day's misses into the four repair groups: topic and evidence (errors A, B and C), features (error D), writing and participation (errors E, F and G), foundations (errors H to O).

#### 10 min. Repair by gap

Four groups at your table, about 2 minutes each, one repair per child, with 2 minutes to move. If many children made error C on day 118's exit, or error B on day 117's exit, or error A on day 116's exit, or error D on day 115's exit, model that component to everyone first, then hear the group. If more than a third missed one component, the model comes before the groups. Topic and evidence (errors A, B and C): the topic is the whole bird, not one detail; a fact needs a page; a label is checked in the sentence, with a picture symbol or a word symbol marked beside each source. Features (error D): follow the contents line and the heading, not the cover. Writing and participation (errors E, F and G): a fact, not a liking; a closing line; a drawing that says something, not decoration. Foundations (errors H to O): tap the vowel sounds (error H); divide, then blend (error I); keep every ending (error J). The other groups read their riddle to a partner and say back the shape clue and the use clue.

#### 10 min. Fresh retry

Reread the exit lines of Heron by the Water and show the two pictures. The completion group (at most 12) each retry only the missed component, about 30 seconds each, six minutes. If fewer than six are in the group, top it up to six from the heard-six column. Topic: **Ask.** "Which picture is this part about?" ([[s03-X119av2]]) Features: **Ask.** "Point to where the glossary says what marsh means." ([[s03-X119bv2]]) Writing: **Ask.** "Say a topic sentence." Participation: **Ask.** "Say one fact and point to its source." Foundations retries use the reserved lists: bathtub, omit and reptile for counting and decoding; lasts, buzzes, biting, poking, bumped, tamed, drifted, chased, cuter, cutest, colder and coldest for endings. Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread once), not yet seen. A child who points to the fish alone has named a detail, not the bird (error A). A child who points to the cover for the glossary has used the cover alone (error D). Retries never rescore earlier records.

### Other rooms

No printer: set the drawings on desks and read the heron lines aloud. One child: share the riddle with a grown-up who guesses, take the missing tasks at the table, and answer the retry aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No gallery: lay the drawing and two other pictures on a table.

**Skip.** The completion tasks, the rotation and the repair.

- **10 min. Share.** Your child reads the riddle with the name held back and you guess. **Say.** "Which clue told you?" Expect: your child says the clue.
- **5 min. Source.** Your child names the source label of one clue.
- **10 min. Reread.** Your child rereads a favorite bird page from the unit, a finger under each word.
- **5 min. Print.** "Mike has a kite."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X119av2]] [[s03-X119bv2]] Read where a heron lives from Heron by the Water. Your child points to the picture the part is about, then to the glossary line for marsh.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the completion group, the retry and one repair group. Longer: a child with every record shown writes a third clue and tests it on a partner.

### Words and supports

Words: share, gallery, guess, source, glossary. Frames: "The clue is ___." "The source label says ___." "I guess ___ because ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The retry tells you who can find the whole topic and the glossary line in a part of Heron by the Water. Any child who still points to the fish alone (error A) or the cover alone (error D) carries that into day 120, which launches the bird-care question and chapter 1 of the long story. Unfinished completion entries are recorded not yet seen, and the course record on days 158 and 159 picks up RF.1.3 completion.
