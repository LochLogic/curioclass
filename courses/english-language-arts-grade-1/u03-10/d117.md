# Day 117: Peer question, revise and edit

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy day 116's marks. Name today's heard six, the partner pairs and cohort 2 (children 11 to 20 on the class list). List the children who gave a liking on day 116's exit (error E) for the repair. Print the strips. Make the three repair cards below. Keep the cohort word cards out of sight. Day 116's error A waits for day 119.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

Repair for day 116's exit (error E): a child who gave a liking where a fact belonged. Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Show three fresh cards: "I like ducks." "A duck has webbed feet." "Ducks are fun." All children at once, thumbs up for a fact, thumbs down for a liking. **Ask.** "Which card is a fact you could check on a page?" Expect: the webbed feet card, and "I like" is a feeling. Then each child says one fact clue about their own bird to a partner, with the name held back. Hear three children. Review: a fact is a clue; a liking is not (day 116).

#### 10 min. Words and spelling

Say what the day does: a partner asks one question and you change one clue. Review: a peer question asks for one change and you answer from the source (kindergarten, day 73; grade 1, unit 2.8). Review: feedback is what a reader tells the writer so the writing gets better, then you revise (grade 1, days 65 to 73). New: a strip is a question card a partner holds up after hearing your riddle. Show the three strips: What is the part? Where does the bird use it? How does it help the bird? Say each aloud; children say each back. New: revise means change a clue so it answers the question. New: edit means check the spelling of taught words. Put the riddle card and the first draft side by side on the chart; the first draft stays.

#### 12 min. Peer feedback

Model first, three minutes. Chart the day 113 beak riddle with the clue "I have a thing." and hold up the strip "What is the part?" Think aloud: "The strip asks about the part. The page says flat beak. I change the clue." Write "I have a flat beak." and add the label. Then partners, six minutes, from different tables: read your riddle aloud to the partner, hold the name back, and the partner picks one strip. Each partner says the question aloud and does not tell the answer. Three minutes: you hear six children read their riddle and say which strip they were given, 40 seconds each. Expect: the child names the strip and what it asks.

#### 18 min. Revise

Each child changes the clue the strip asks about, using only the page, and adds a source label. Give no help with the facts: point to the page. The first draft stays beside the revision. If the page does not answer the question, the child keeps the clue as it was and says so; a new fact no page gives is error B. A liking in place of a clue is error E. A child who cannot print says the new clue to you or a scribe. Walk round, six children, two minutes each: "Read me the new clue and show me where the page says it." Three minutes at the end: both versions go in the riddle folder.

#### 10 min. Edit

Review: the sound-spelling map shows which letters spell a sound (kindergarten, day 3; grade 1, day 109). Show the chart line "Pete rids a bik." Read it as written. Children tap the vowel sound in each word; the silent e is missing in two words. Fix the line together: rides, bike. Then each child checks the taught spellings in the revised clue against the map and fixes one. A spelling not yet taught stays as the child wrote it and gets a circle. The marks go on the page.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Read the first card once from the card sheet and show its two pictures. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which picture fits the peer's question?" ([[s03-X117a]]) Then everyone marks the spelling card. **Ask.** "Which spelling matches the sound-spelling map? Point to it." ([[s03-X117c]]) The heard six come to your table with the first card in view, 30 seconds each. **Ask.** "Say a clue that answers the question." ([[s03-X117b]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A liking in place of a clue is error E, for day 118's opener. A vowel left out of the spelling is error M, for the foundations retry on day 119.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say rod and rode; which has the long vowel sound? Blend /g/ /l/ /i/ /d/. Give the first, vowel and last sound of bone. Segment smile.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Consolidate; no new pattern. Whole class on small boards, then six tables of five at the map, a minute a table. Review: the e dropped before -ing and -ed (day 109). Chain nice, nicer, nicest and safe, safer. Decode wider, napkin, pancake and inside, counting the vowel sounds, not the vowel letters. Spell nicer and napkin, about 40 seconds a word. Reread A Day at the Lake, pages 5 to 8, for expression. Then each child echo-reads the revised clue after you, a finger under each word.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Dictate "Luke baked a cake." Say it whole, then a word at a time; Luke is a name, so it begins with a capital. Children tap one finger for each sound and print a letter for each tap; in Luke and cake the last e is silent, and in baked the e is dropped before the -ed sound. Then each child prints two pairs the written feedback marked (practice, not collected). Repair for a vowel left out (error M): tap again and ask "Where is the vowel?"

#### 30 min. Observation cohort

Cohort 2, ten children not yet recorded, one at a time at your table, 3 minutes each. Cards stay in the folder: lunchbox, bobcat and flagpole; their divided forms lunch-box, bob-cat and flag-pole; and the ending list hens, brushes, lifting, packed, spelled, melted, finer, finest, rakes, raking, raked. First, half a minute. **Ask.** "Count the parts in each word by its vowel sounds." ([[s03-A33]]) Review: every syllable has a vowel sound (days 79 and 99). Expect two, two, two; flagpole has two vowel sounds, though its letters a, o and e look like three (error H). Second, 45 seconds. **Ask.** "Read each word, part by part." ([[s03-A34]]) Expect each part read, then blended; a word said from a picture or a guess is error I. Third, one minute and three-quarters. **Ask.** "Read each word." ([[s03-A35]]) Expect every ending kept: packed ends /t/, spelled /d/, melted adds a part, and raking is not rake (error J). Fill only the cells a recent record has not shown, and cite that record. A child may ask for a word again: shown with help.

The other twenty work in pairs at three stations, ten minutes each, and you hear no one else: reread A Day at the Lake a page each; chain bake, baked, baking and nice, nicer, nicest with letter cards; read the revised clue to a partner, who says back the part it names.

Ten children at 3 minutes fills all 30 minutes with no room to walk, so seat the next child as the last one leaves. A child with a recent shown entry takes less. A child not finished at minute 30 is recorded as unfinished and heard on day 119; do not hurry one.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the cohort rows, the exit marks and the heard-six column to the record sheet. Write one line for each cohort child who showed error H, error I or error J; the retry is on day 119. List by name the children who gave a liking (error E) for day 118, and those who made up a fact (error B) for day 119. Put every revision and first draft in the riddle folder.

### Other rooms

No printer: write each strip on a slip of paper and say the exit cards aloud. One child: a grown-up asks the three strips in turn, the child answers one, and takes the three observation tasks and the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: ask "What is the part?", "Where does the bird use it?" or "How does it help the bird?" aloud.

**Skip.** The observation, the stations and the talk.

- **10 min. Ask.** A grown-up hears the riddle from day 116 and asks one of the three questions. **Say.** "Where does the page say it?" Expect: a finger on the sentence.
- **10 min. Change.** Your child changes the clue the question asks about, using only the page, and adds the source label. The first draft stays.
- **5 min. Check.** Your child checks the spelling of one taught word against the sound-spelling map.
- **5 min. Print.** "Luke baked a cake."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X117a]] [[s03-X117b]] [[s03-X117c]] Read a short made-up card your child has not seen. Your child points to the picture the question fits, says a clue that answers it, then points to the right spelling.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the edit part to one word; keep the peer question, the revision and the exit. Longer: a child with a finished revision answers a second strip on the other clue.

### Words and supports

Words: strip, question, revise, edit, label. Frames: "Where does the page say it?" "I changed ___." "The question asks ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can match a peer question to the change it asks for. A child who gave a liking (error E) gets a five-minute repair on a fresh strip on day 118. A child who made up a fact (error B) waits for day 119. Cohort 2's misses in counting (error H), blending (error I) and endings (error J), and any vowel left out (error M), are retried on day 119. Day 118 finishes the scientific drawing.
