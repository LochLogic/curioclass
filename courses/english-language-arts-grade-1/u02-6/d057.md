# Day 57: A moon fact and a story are different

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make the day 56 repair card: "Mia's log: On Monday the moon was round. On Friday it was a half." with two strips, a round moon then a half, and a round moon in two places. Make the fact and tale picture cards, four for each pair (listed in the Talk part). Lay the two columns on the floor. Open the shared notebook page. Pick today's heard six.

### The day

#### 15 min. Reread a fact and a tale

Whole class on the rug. Reread The Changing Moon, pages 2 and 3, with their pictures (5 minutes). Then read the first page of The Weaver Girl and the Cowherd aloud, with its picture (4 minutes). Say aloud: "This is a traditional Chinese tale, a story people tell." Review: you heard this tale on day 41. Say the name Zhinu as you did then. It is a story, not science; nobody acts it out. Review: the two columns from day 35 (section 2.1), a tale and an information text (3 minutes): a tale has people who talk and act; an information text gives facts a person could check. Say: "Our notebook keeps only facts we could check, and it names where each came from" (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair, only if many missed day 56's exit (error E), 5 minutes. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "Mia's log: On Monday the moon was round. On Friday it was a half." Show the two strips. Read the log again and point to the words round and half. **Ask.** "Which strip says what the log says?" Expect: the round moon then the half. Each child points. Then lay the arrow card from the first detail, round, to the second, half. The log says its shape changed; it does not say the moon moved.

Then partners point (5 minutes). **Ask.** "Which page told us a fact?" Expect: The Changing Moon. **Ask.** "Which page told us a story?" Expect: the tale. Hear four pairs, half a minute each.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs sort the four fact and tale picture cards onto the two columns (4 minutes). Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Read each aloud: "A weaver girl lived in the sky." "The moon makes no light of its own." "Her name was Zhinu." "The moon moves across the sky." The first and third go under tale; the second and fourth under information. Then pairs with the turn card, one turn each, a minute a child (3 minutes). **Ask.** "How do we know it is a story?" Expect: it has people who talk and do things; it is told, not checked. Hear four pairs, half a minute each (3 minutes). No child is asked to be the example of a mistake.

#### 20 min. Moon facts for the notebook

Application: nothing new is taught. Say the three facts the class may choose from, all on The Changing Moon (2 minutes): the moon can be seen in the daytime; the moon changes shape; the moon makes no light of its own. Each child draws one small picture of the fact on a note card (6 minutes), copies the label "The Changing Moon" from the strip card (2 minutes), and places it on the notebook page in the facts column (2 minutes). Story events stay out of the facts column. Pairs read their note to a partner and say which page it came from, one turn each, a minute a child (2 minutes). Take the day's six one at a time, 40 seconds each (4 minutes): ask "Is your note a fact or a story?" and "Where did it come from?" The rest add a second picture. Keep 2 minutes for second tries.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Show two cards and read each aloud: "Some nights, clouds hide the moon." with a cloud over a moon, and "The moon put on a hat and danced with the clouds." with a moon in a hat. Say: "Point to the card that tells a fact for our notebook." Every child points; the rotating six also say how they can tell ([[s02-X57]]). A good answer is the card about the clouds; a person could see it. The dancing moon is error D: both cards name the moon, but only one is a fact. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words, not scored. Say tape and tap; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter a's name?" Expect: tape. Blend /s/ /k/ /i/ /p/, "skip". Say "stiff": first sound /s/, middle sound /i/, last sound /f/. Segment "swim" with counters: /s/ /w/ /i/ /m/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Review from day 56: reread hill, bell, kiss, miss, mess, puff, jazz from the cards, the chain from cycle 9 lesson 48 (2 minutes). Cycle 9, lesson 49: connected sentences. Read the chart "Stan has a doll and a bell." (4 minutes): you read it, the class echoes it, pointing under each word. Review: has is a mapped word, and a name begins with a capital. Doll has the double l, one sound. Partners read it again, one each. Spell doll on sound boxes (2 minutes). Partners read the reader Sam and Nell on the Hill, a page each (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (8 minutes): say "The doll is still." then "Jess yelled and Stan smelled the mess." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped words the and is are named aloud. Watch the capital T, J and S. Then each child points to each capital and each period (2 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table: the group reads Sam and Nell on the Hill aloud, a page a child, and tells what the children did when the jam spilled (4 minutes); then spells smelled on sound boxes (2 minutes). The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread Sam and Nell on the Hill with a partner; chain kiss, miss, mess and less with letter cards; spell Stan and still on sound boxes; read the word cards was, of, but and yelled to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the repair marks to the unit 2 record sheet with the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name the foundations groups for day 58.

### Other rooms

No printer: write each sort card on a strip of paper. One child: reread the two pages, sort the four cards, draw one note for the notebook, answer the exit by pointing, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write each line on a slip of paper.

**Skip.** The talk pairs and the rotation.

- **10 min. Fact or story.** You read: "The moon changes shape." and "A weaver girl lived in the sky." **Say.** "Which could we check? Which is a story?" Expect: the moon changing shape can be checked; the weaver girl is a story.
- **10 min. A note.** Your child draws one moon fact and writes "The Changing Moon" under it.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Your child reads "Stan has a doll" and prints "The doll is still."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X57]] You read: "The moon changes shape." and "The moon put on a coat and ran home." Ask: "Which tells a fact for a notebook?" Expect: the moon changes shape.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair for your own misses, the sort, one note, the exit and the rotation; cut the pair talk. Longer: a child who finishes adds a second fact note and says which page it came from.

### Words and supports

Words: fact, story, tale, notebook, moon, shape, daytime. A fact is something a person could check. A tale is a story people tell. Frames: "This is a fact because ___." "This is a story because ___." "It came from ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who sorts by what a card does, not by its topic (error D: both cards name the moon). Day 58 reads about stars. Its repair part opens with five minutes on error D: point to one made-up event or one thing a person could see, then name the notebook's purpose.
