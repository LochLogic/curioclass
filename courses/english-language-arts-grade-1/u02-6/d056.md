# Day 56: The moon moves and the moon changes shape

## Teacher page

### Before class

Pin the two moon strips side by side with their label cards, and keep the arrow card in your hand. Draw the repair card for day 55's exit: the sentence "The rain stops, so the sun comes out" with two drawings, a border of raindrops and a grey cloud with the sun showing. Fold a note page for each child. Pick today's heard six.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read The Changing Moon

Whole class on the rug. Words before, each with its picture (3 minutes): moon, night, change, shape, thin curve, half. Say aloud: "Nobody looks at the sun. The picture on page 4 is the record." Read the article whole, pausing after pages 2, 3 and 5 to look at the pictures (9 minutes). On page 2 say "seems to move" aloud while the page keeps its word. Review: a pattern happens again and again in the same order, from day 34, and the sun's rise and set from Sun Across the Sky. On page 3, if a child worries about the dark circle, say: "The moon is still there on those nights." Last, read page 6 aloud and say the two patterns in your own words (3 minutes): the moon moves across the sky, and the moon changes shape.

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair, only if many missed day 55's exit (error G), 4 minutes. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "The rain stops, so the sun comes out." Show the border of raindrops and the grey cloud with the sun showing. **Ask.** "Which drawing helps a listener see the sentence?" Expect: the cloud with the sun. Each child points, then draws the one detail a listener needs on scrap paper.

Then reread pages 2 and 3 (6 minutes), showing each picture. **Ask.** "Which page says the moon moves across the sky?" Expect: page 2. **Ask.** "Which page says it changes shape?" Expect: page 3. Partners point to the page on the large print copy, then to the word moves or changes on the cards. Hear four pairs, half a minute each.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs with the turn card, two rounds, pass allowed. Review: the turn card from day 1, one turn each. Round 1 (3 minutes). **Ask.** "What are the moon's two patterns?" Expect: it moves across the sky; it changes shape. Round 2 (3 minutes): each child tells their partner one moon picture and which pattern it shows. Pip, the talk puppet from day 38, waits for one line at a time: "I will wait until you finish." Hear four pairs, a minute each (4 minutes). Nobody is the example of a mistake.

#### 20 min. Two patterns, two notes

Model (8 minutes). Say: "These notes are made up for practice." Note one on the wall strip: a moon above a barn roof, then above a tall tree, with the word card moves and the label "The Changing Moon". Say: "The moon is the same round shape. Only where it is changes." Note two: a thin curve and a half, with the word card changes and the same label. Say: "Here its shape changes." Then read page 5 and show the arrow card from a small Earth with a moon beside it to a thin curve: "The moon goes around Earth, so we see more or less of it." The arrow joins the first detail to the second. Review: the connection arrow from days 51 to 54.

Each child makes their own page (10 minutes): two notes, each a small picture, the word moves or changes copied from the card, and the label; and one arrow. A moon that moves is drawn the same shape in two places. A moon that changes is drawn in two shapes. Watch for error E: a moving moon drawn as a changing shape. If it shows, point to the two moons and ask which part of the page tells where it is. Close with two pairs showing their notes, and pin the best two on the shared notebook (2 minutes).

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread page 5 of *Sky Patterns* aloud: "Some nights it is round and bright. Some nights it is a thin curve. Then it grows round again." Show two strips: a round moon, a thin curve, a round moon; and one round moon low, then higher, then high. Say: "Point to the strip that shows what the page says." Every child points; the rotating six also say which words tell the change ([[s02-X56v2]]). A good answer is the strip of a round moon, a thin curve and a round moon again. The strip of one shape moving is error E: a claim the page does not make. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words, not scored. Say rain and ran; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter a's name?" Expect: rain. Blend /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/, "stop". Say "spill": first sound /s/, middle sound /i/, last sound /l/. Segment "smell" with counters: /s/ /m/ /e/ /l/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Review from days 54 and 55: at the end of a short word, l, s, f and z are often doubled and say one sound, as in hill and mess; an s-blend keeps both sounds side by side, as in stop; the ending ed says /d/ after /l/, as in spilled (3 minutes: read hill, bell, kiss, miss, mess, puff, jazz and stop, step, skip, spin from the cards, one a child in turn for six children, the rest echo). Why the letter doubles: a short word with one short vowel and an ending /l/, /s/, /f/ or /z/ is often written with that letter twice; the double is one sound. Ed is read /d/ after a sound you hum, like /l/: yelled, spilled, smelled (2 minutes: cover the ending, read the base, add ed, reread). If a child drops the ending (error L), do the same. Partners read Sam and Nell on the Hill, a page each, pointing under each word (4 minutes). Spell kiss on sound boxes (1 minute).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (6 minutes): say "The bell is on the hill." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped words the and is are named aloud. Watch the capital T and the double l in bell and hill. Then each child prints their own name with its capital, copied from the name card, and points to the capital and the period (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table: the group reads Sam and Nell on the Hill aloud, a page a child, and tells what happened to the jam (4 minutes); then spells yelled on sound boxes (2 minutes). The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread Sam and Nell on the Hill with a partner; chain hill, bell, kiss and miss with letter cards; spell mess and puff on sound boxes; read the word cards was, of, but and stop to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the repair marks to the unit 2 record sheet with the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name the foundations groups for day 57.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the two strips on folded paper and use a pencil line for the arrow. One child: read one page of The Changing Moon at a time, draw the two notes and the arrow, say "The moon goes around Earth, so we see more or less of it," answer the exit by pointing, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: draw them on plain paper.

**Skip.** The talk pairs and the rotation.

- **15 min. Two notes.** Your child draws the same round moon in two places, and a thin curve then a half. **Say.** "Which drawing shows where the moon is? Which shows its shape?" Expect: the two places; the thin curve and the half.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child reads hill and mess and prints "The bell is on the hill."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X56v2]] You read page 5 of Sky Patterns: the moon is round, then a thin curve, then round again. Draw two strips: round, thin, round; and one round moon in two places. Ask: "Which strip shows what the page says?" Expect: round, thin, round.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair for your own misses, the two notes, the exit and the rotation; cut the second round of talk. Longer: a child who finishes adds a third note, the moon seen in the daytime, from page 2.

### Words and supports

Words: moon, night, change, shape, thin curve, half, moves. A thin curve is a very narrow moon. Frames: "The moon ___." "I see ___ in the picture." "The moon goes around Earth, so ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who keeps the two patterns apart (error E: a claim the page does not make, the strip of one shape moving). Day 57 records moon facts and tells a story from information. Its repair part opens with five minutes on error E: read a short log, point to the words, then join its two details with the arrow card.
