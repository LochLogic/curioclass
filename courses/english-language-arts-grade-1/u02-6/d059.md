# Day 59: Describe the stars with a picture and a sentence

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make the day 58 repair card: "Thick fog can hide a far hill. On a clear day we can see the hill." with two log pictures, a clear hill and a clear hill with a red house. Write the three board contrasts from the calendar card piece, each shown once. Have the calendar card and the because and so cards ready. Pick the heard six.

### The day

#### 15 min. Reread the stars article

Whole class on the rug. Reread Stars in Daylight and Darkness, pages 1, 5 and 6, with their pictures (8 minutes), and show the class picture log and the day-and-night barn log beside them (2 minutes). Say aloud: "Nobody looks at the sun." Night is gentle here: the stars are quiet and far away. The class chooses one picture to describe by pointing (2 minutes); count the pointing hands. Then say what a listener would need to see in it (3 minutes). Application day: nothing new is taught.

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair, only if many missed day 58's exit (error E), 5 minutes. Say: "This card is made up for practice." Read: "Thick fog can hide a far hill. On a clear day we can see the hill." Show the two log pictures. Read the card again and point to the words hide and see. **Ask.** "Which picture goes with the card?" Expect: the clear day with a hill. The house is not on the card. Lay the arrow card and the so card from "fog hides the hill" to "we see less": "Fog hides the hill, so we see less."

Then partners point (5 minutes). **Ask.** "What does the picture show that the words do not?" Expect: for page 1, the two children on a porch under a blanket. Hear four pairs, half a minute each.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs with the turn card, one turn each (3 minutes). **Ask.** "What does our picture help a listener see?" Expect: a detail the words need, such as the dark sky and the stars. Then the board (7 minutes), each contrast shown once and taken down. Review: a sentence has a who or what and what happens, and an end mark, from day 35. "Stars above" and "The stars are above the roof." **Ask.** "Which tells who or what and what happens?" Expect: the second. "sun moon stars" and "sun, moon, stars." **Ask.** "Which keeps the three words apart?" Expect: the one with commas. "May 2 2026" and "May 2, 2026." **Ask.** "Where does the comma go in a date?" Expect: after the day. Review: a month begins with a capital, from day 50. Nobody is the example of a mistake.

#### 20 min. Describe it, draw it, write one line

Each child draws the detail a listener needs in a star picture of their own choosing (6 minutes), then says one finished sentence about it to a partner, who says back one detail (3 minutes). Say the frame aloud: "The stars are ___." Then each child copies the date from the calendar card at the top of the page, with the capital on the month and the comma after the day (4 minutes). A child who asks has the sentence scribed. Take the day's six one at a time, 40 seconds each (4 minutes): they say their sentence with the drawing in view. The rest say it to a second partner. Keep 3 minutes for second tries.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Read once: "The stars come out when the sky is dark." Show two pictures: a dark sky with stars above a pond, and a bright daytime pond with ducks. Say: "Point to the picture that helps a listener see what the sentence says." Every child points; the rotating six say what the picture shows a listener ([[s02-X59a]]). A good answer is the dark sky with stars. The ducks are error G: a picture that does not show what the sentence says. Then show two printed lists and read each aloud: "hat scarf mittens" and "hat, scarf, mittens". Say: "Point to the list written right." Every child points; the six say what the commas do ([[s02-X59b]]). A good answer is the list with commas; no commas is error S. Last, the six also say one finished sentence about their own star picture ([[s02-X59c]]). A good answer says who or what and what happens; "Stars above" is error H. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words, not scored. Say kit and kite; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter i's name?" Expect: kite. Blend /f/ /l/ /a/ /g/, "flag". Say "plum": first sound /p/, middle sound /u/, last sound /m/. Segment "brick" with counters: /b/ /r/ /i/ /k/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Cycle 10, lesson 51, explicit teaching first (4 minutes). Review: an s-blend keeps the s and the next sound side by side, from day 54. Today an l or an r can start a blend too, each letter with its own sound: fl, gl, pl and cl; br, cr, dr and tr. Map them, then read flag, glad, plan, plum and club, then brick, crab, drip, drum and trip. Three consonants can stand side by side, each with its sound: scrub, strap and split. Cover each word and read it a sound at a time. The words with an ending -es, -er or -est come on later days, so none is used. Spell plum on sound boxes (2 minutes). The cycle 10 reader comes on day 60, so partners read Sam and Nell on the Hill again, a page each, and the class retells what happened (4 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (6 minutes): say "The crab is on the flat rock." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped words the and is are named aloud. Print each word letter by letter. Watch the capital T and the cr and fl blends. Then each child points to the capital and the period, and circles the blend in crab and flat (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table, first (3 minutes): children who made error L on the day 58 sample read the base, add the ending ed and reread; children who made error M stretch the word, mark each heard sound with a counter, then write. Then the group reads Sam and Nell on the Hill, a page a child, and spells plan on sound boxes (3 minutes). The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread Sam and Nell on the Hill with a partner; chain flag, glad, plan and plum with letter cards; spell brick and drip on sound boxes; read the word cards was, of, but and the to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the repair marks and the sentence marks to the unit 2 record sheet with the heard-six column and the cycle record. Name the foundations groups for day 60.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the contrasts on the board and the date on a strip of paper. One child: reread the pages, draw one star picture, say a finished sentence, copy the date with its comma, answer the exits by pointing and speaking, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No calendar card: write today's date on a strip.

**Skip.** The talk pairs and the rotation.

- **10 min. Draw and say.** Your child draws a night sky with the detail a listener needs. **Say.** "Say one finished sentence about your picture." Expect: who or what and what happens, such as "The stars are above the roof."
- **10 min. Date.** Your child copies today's date with the comma after the day.
- **5 min. Sounds.** Your child reads flag and plan and prints "The crab is on the flat rock."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X59a]] You say: "The moon comes up when the sky is dark." Draw two pictures: a dark sky with a moon, and a bright playground. Ask: "Which helps a listener?" Expect: the dark sky. [[s02-X59b]] You write "red, blue, green" and "red blue green". Ask: "Which is written right?" Expect: the one with commas. [[s02-X59c]] Ask: "Say one finished sentence about your picture." Expect: who or what and what happens.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair for your own misses, the drawing and sentence, the exit and the rotation; cut one contrast. Longer: a child who finishes says a second sentence with because or so and draws what it adds.

### Words and supports

Words: describe, listener, detail, picture, sentence, comma, date. A comma is a small mark that keeps words apart. Frames: "The stars are ___." "The picture shows ___." "Today is ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who draws what a listener needs (error G: a picture that adds nothing), who separates a list with commas (error S: no commas) and who says a finished sentence (error H: a fragment). The date comma (error V) shows on the page. Day 60 rehearses a science talk. Its repair part opens with five minutes on error G: say what the listener needs, then draw only that detail; when many missed errors S, H or V, it adds a comma strip, a who-and-what sentence, or the circle-the-day move inside that repair.
