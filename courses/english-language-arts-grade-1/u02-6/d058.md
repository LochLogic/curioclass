# Day 58: The stars are there in the daytime

## Teacher page

### Before class

Draw the barn log: one barn, fence and hill, a bright day sky with no star marks, and the same view at night with a few star dots and a moon. Make the day 57 repair cards: "The sun was a sleepy king who yawned." and "The sun is a star." Write a text fact card: "The sun lights the sky, so we cannot see the stars." Set out the cycle sample word cards. Pick the heard six.

### The day

#### 15 min. Read Stars in Daylight and Darkness

Whole class on the rug. Words before, each with its picture (3 minutes): star, bright, faint, daylight, dark. Say aloud: "Nobody looks at the sun." Show the barn log beside the article. Read pages 1 to 3, then pause (4 minutes); read page 4 and pause on its split picture, day sky and night sky (4 minutes); read pages 5 and 6 (4 minutes). Night is gentle here: the stars shine out and the sky is dark and quiet. Review: the sun is a star, from Sun Across the Sky, and the class picture log from day 50.

#### 10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair

Opening repair, only if many missed day 57's exit (error D), 5 minutes. Say: "These cards are made up for practice." Read: "The sun was a sleepy king who yawned." and "The sun is a star." Both name the sun. **Ask.** "Point to one thing in the cards that is made up, or one thing a person could check." Each child points. **Ask.** "Which card could go in our notebook?" Expect: the sun is a star, because the notebook holds facts from our sources.

Then reread page 4 (5 minutes). **Ask.** "What does the sun's light do to the stars?" Expect: it lights the sky, so we cannot see them. Partners point to the words on the large print copy. Hear four pairs, half a minute each.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs with the turn card, two rounds, pass allowed. Round 1 (3 minutes), one turn each: **Ask.** "Where do the stars go in the day?" Expect: they are still there; the bright sky hides them. Round 2 (3 minutes), one turn each: say the frame "The sun's light ___, so ___." Review: because and so, from day 54, tell what makes a thing happen. Hear four pairs, a minute each (4 minutes). Pass is always allowed.

#### 20 min. The text and the log

Model (6 minutes). Show the barn log. Say: "This log is made up for practice." Lay the text fact card beside the day picture and draw the arrow from the card to the day picture: "The sun lights the sky, so we cannot see the stars." Point to the night picture: here the dark sky lets the stars show. Say the label aloud: "Stars in Daylight and Darkness." Review: the connection arrow joins a fact in the text to a log picture. Add nothing the page does not say.

Each child draws two log pictures on their folded page, a day view and a night view of the same place (10 minutes): the day sky with no star marks, the night sky with star dots and a moon. They draw an arrow from the text fact to the day picture and copy the label from the card. Pairs say their arrow with so, one turn each, a minute a child (2 minutes). Keep 2 minutes for second tries. Fasten the finished log pages beside the shared notebook. Watch for error E: a day picture with star marks, or an arrow to a claim the text does not make.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread page 2 of the star article aloud: "they are very, very far away. That is why they look like tiny dots of light." Show two log pictures: a star as a tiny dot far above a hill, and a star as a small ball near a roof. Say: "Point to the log picture that goes with the page." Every child points; the rotating six also say the arrow, "Stars are far away, so they look like tiny dots" ([[s02-X58v2]]). A good answer is the tiny dot far above the hill. The small ball near a roof is error E: a claim the page does not make. Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken words, not scored. Say mad and made; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter a's name?" Expect: made. Blend /s/ /n/ /a/ /p/, "snap". Say "stuff": first sound /s/, middle sound /u/, last sound /f/. Segment "spill" with counters: /s/ /p/ /i/ /l/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Whole class with the sound-spelling-map and letter cards. Review from day 57: read stop, step, skip and spin from the cards (1 minute). Cycle 9, lesson 50: the sound pattern is the same, so today the s-blend takes snap and smell: each has the s and the next sound side by side. Read the chart "Stan and Jess smell the mess." (4 minutes): you read it, the class echoes it, pointing under each word. Spell stuff on sound boxes (2 minutes) and read the word cards was, of and but (1 minute). The cycle progress sample is taken in the rotation; teach only these taught patterns today. Partners read Sam and Nell on the Hill, a page each (2 minutes).

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (6 minutes): say "Stan and Jess smell the mess." Before they print, stretch each word and mark each sound with a counter. Write "Stn" on the board beside "Stan" and ask which has every sound; the missing vowel is error M. Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped word the is named aloud. Watch the capital S in Stan and Jess and the double s in Jess and mess. Then each child points to the capitals and the period (4 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. At the table is the cycle 9 progress sample, one child at a time, about 50 seconds each: show one word card alone and say "Read this word." (the card is the sample word with an ending ed, [[s02-A17]]), then remove it. Say the dictated word once in a sentence, "The bee will buzz," then once alone, and say "Write the word I say." The child writes it on a slate ([[s02-A18]]). Record shown, shown with help or not yet seen on the cycle record, with the support used. The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread Sam and Nell on the Hill with a partner; chain stop, step, skip and snap with letter cards; spell smell and mess on sound boxes; read the word cards was, of, but and stop to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the repair marks and the sample marks to the unit 2 record sheet and the cycle record. Regroup for day 59 from the sample: a child who read a base without its ending, or wrote a word without a vowel, goes to the group for that repair. Name the foundations groups.

### Other rooms

No printer: fold a page in two and draw both views, and draw the arrow with a pencil. One child: read the article a page at a time, draw the two log pictures and the arrow, answer the exit by pointing, then the foundations parts; the sample is taken by you one to one.

### At home

**Swap.** No log page: fold plain paper in two.

**Skip.** The talk pairs, the rotation and the sample.

- **15 min. Day and night.** Your child draws one yard by day with no stars and by night with a few star dots and the moon, and an arrow from "The sun lights the sky, so we cannot see the stars" to the day picture. **Say.** "What does the sun's light do to the stars?" Expect: it hides them; they are still there.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child reads snap and smell and prints "Stan and Jess smell the mess."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X58v2]] You read page 2 of the star article: stars are very, very far away, so they look like tiny dots of light. Draw two pictures: a tiny dot far above a hill, and a small ball near a roof. Ask: "Which picture goes with the page?" Expect: the tiny dot.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the repair for your own misses, the barn log, the exit, the sample and the rotation; cut the second round of talk. Longer: a child who finishes adds the moon to the day picture and says why it can show in the daytime, from The Changing Moon.

### Words and supports

Words: star, bright, faint, daylight, dark, log, arrow. A star is a huge, far-away ball of glowing gas that looks like a dot. Faint means hard to see. Frames: "The sun's light ___, so ___." "The stars are still ___." "It came from ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who joins a card to the log picture it matches (error E: a claim the page does not make, the ball near a roof). The sample tells you who drops an ending when reading (error L) or a vowel when spelling (error M). Day 59 describes a picture. Its repair part opens with five minutes on error E: read a short card, point to the words, then join its two details with the arrow card. Its rotation opens each table group with three minutes for error L and error M.
