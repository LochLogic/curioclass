# Section 2.6 opener: the moon and the stars

## What the section does

Days 56 to 59 read about the moon and the stars. Children keep the moon's two patterns apart (where it is, and what shape it shows), sort stories from information, join a text fact to a day-and-night picture log, and describe the star pattern in one finished sentence with a useful drawing. Nobody looks at the sun; the class picture log replaces any home observation, and night is described gently.

## How the days run

Each day is 120 minutes in two blocks of 60. The topic block holds the reading, the talk and the work; the foundations block holds oral sound work, letters and sounds, a rotation of small groups and a short record. Every exit is spoken to you: you read the card aloud, the child points, sorts, draws or speaks, and you hear the day's six children. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". A child may pass at any time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The moon has two patterns.** It moves across the sky, and its shape changes from night to night. They are different things.
- **A story and a fact are different.** A tale is a story people tell; the notebook keeps only what the information text says.
- **A bright sky hides the stars.** They are still there by day; bright light makes faint stars hard to see.
- **A list needs commas, and a date needs one between the day and the year.**

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** events in the wrong order.
- **D:** sorting a text by its topic, not by what it does.
- **E:** a claim the page does not make. Point to the sentence.
- **G:** a drawing that does not help a listener.
- **H:** a group of words that is not a sentence.
- **S, V:** missing commas in a list or a date.
- **L, M:** an ending dropped in reading, a vowel dropped in spelling.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet and keep the sheet in your folder.
