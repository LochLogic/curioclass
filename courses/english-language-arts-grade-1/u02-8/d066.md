# Day 66: Plan and write a moon poem together

## Teacher page

### Before class

Make six event pictures for the class to choose from: the moon over a barn, moonlight on a river, a deer in a field, an owl on a post, a dark hill, and the morning sky turning pale. Hang the shared moon poem chart and the arc chart. Fold the eight-box sheets. Copy the heard six from the day 65 column.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

From day 65's exit, two branches. First, error F (a list of facts instead of events), 3 minutes: say "These are made up for practice." Show "yellow, warm" beside two ordered actions, a child opening a gate and then walking in. **Ask.** "Which tells what happened, in order?" Expect: the second. Children draw First and Next on two squares, then add an ending square. Second, error H (a fragment), 2 minutes: show "dog naps" beside "The dog naps on the step." **Ask.** "Which tells who and what happens?" Expect: the second. Add one detail together.

#### 10 min. Reread

Reread page 1 and page 4 of Moon's Walk (g1-u2-02) and page 2 of The Changing Moon (g1-u2-01). Say: "Our poem rests on a fact: the moon is seen in the dark sky, and it moves across the sky." Our poem must not say anything that breaks the fact. Imagined parts are fine, as in Moon's Walk. Review: a narrative poem tells events in order with time words and an ending, from day 65.

#### 10 min. Plan

Show the six event pictures. The class picks two, or three, to be the events of our moon poem; partners point to their choice and say its time word. Pick by a show of hands and let the class settle (4 minutes). Write the plan on the shared chart: First (event), Next (event), At last (event), then an ending line to come. For example: First the moon rises over the barn. Next its light lies on the river. At last the morning sky turns pale. Writing-process chart: the class points to plan, then draft.

#### 20 min. Draft together

Fifteen pairs each give one line aloud for the event you point to, about one minute a pair, 15 minutes. Five pairs per event: first pairs say a line for First, next five for Next, last five for At last. Write each line on the chart as it is said and read it back. Each line may add a detail to the event it belongs to; keep to the plan and the fact. Then the class says an ending line together: how the poem comes to rest (3 minutes). Read the whole poem once. Any pair may pass; offer the line to a neighbor. This is shared writing: you write, the children say. It is a made-up poem for practice, not a fact page.

#### 10 min. Eight-box sentence sheet

Show the picture of a cart and two goats in a field. Review: a statement, a question, a command and an exclamation, and joining two parts with and, from days 35 and 40. Each pair has a sheet of eight boxes: row one is the four kinds, row two the same four joined with and. One partner says boxes for statement and question, simple and joined; the other says command and exclamation (2 minutes each). Both point to the box as they speak. Hear the heard six alone, one simple and one joined sentence of the kind you name, 40 seconds each, 4 minutes. Scribe what is said; do not correct it on the sheet. Say "These sentences are made up for practice."

#### 5 min. Exit

Read the notes aloud, invented for practice ([[s02-X66a]]). Show the two strips. Every child circles 1 or 2 on the student page. Hear the heard six on [[s02-X66b]], with the notes in view. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken, not scored. Say not and note; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter o's name?" Expect: note. Blend /f/ /ī/ /n/ /d/, "find". Say "loud": first sound /l/, middle sound /ow/, last sound /d/. Segment "found" with counters: /f/ /ow/ /n/ /d/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 11, lesson 58. Review final clusters, -ed said /id/ and ou. Chain with the cards: found, loud, shout, cloud, lifted, melted, sink, king (4 minutes). Pairs reread I Look Out for meaning and expression: you read a page, the pair echoes it, then each reads a page smoothly (6 minutes). The class says what the child sees and how the child feels at the end.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27. Dictation (8 minutes): "I found a bank. The wind is soft." Children print a word at a time with the map; the capital I is named aloud. Then each child prints their first name with its capital (2 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. Supported reading (5 minutes): each child reads aloud one sentence from page 2 of The Changing Moon with you, 30 seconds each (3 minutes), with written feedback on a picture note, then rereads yesterday's sentence for 20 seconds as the revised reading (2 minutes). Use the last minute for the group to reread page 2 of I Look Out. The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread I Look Out with a partner; chain found, loud and cloud with letter cards; spell shout and king on sound boxes; read the word cards look, see, a and the to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks, the eight-box notes and the heard-six column to the unit 2 record sheet. Keep the shared poem chart up for tomorrow.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the six event pictures and fold a page into eight boxes; draw the strips on the board. One child: choose two events, say them with time words, say one box of each kind, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No chart: write the plan on a sheet of paper.

**Skip.** The pairs, the draft round and the rotation.

- **10 min. Plan.** Your child picks two things the moon could do tonight and says them with First and Next. **Say.** "What happens first? What happens next?" Expect: two events with time words.
- **10 min. Sentences.** Look at a picture of a toy cart and two goats. Your child says a statement, a question, a command and an exclamation.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child reads found, loud, cloud and king and prints "I found a bank."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X66a]] You read: "In the morning a rooster crows. A farmer comes out. The farmer feeds the pigs." Draw two strips: crow, then feed; and feed, then crow. Ask: "Point to the strip that guides the plan." Expect: crow, then feed. [[s02-X66b]] Ask: "Say the first event and the next, with a time word." Expect: two events with First and Next.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the plan, the draft round for ten pairs, the exit and the supported reading; keep two kinds of sentence in the eight-box sheet. Longer: a pair that finishes adds a third event with Then.

### Words and supports

Words: plan, draft, event, ending, statement, question, command, exclamation. Frames: "First ___. Next ___." "At last ___." "I can add ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Any child may pass. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who points to the plan's order (error A: reversed order) and who says events with time words (error F: a list of words). Day 67 opens with repairs for both: reread the time words and place two picture cards; then draw first and next, and add an ending. Day 67 gives kind, specific feedback on our poem and revises it on paper and on a shared device.
