# Day 65: Look at a poem that tells events in order

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang the writing-process chart: five picture boxes, plan, draft, share, revise, publish. Write on the arc chart two columns, Moon's Walk poem and Moon's Walk in sentences. Have the two feedback note cards, a green card and a white card for each pair, and a picture card for each pair from Moon's Walk (the dog, the boat, the owl). Copy the heard six from the day 64 column. Check that each child's day 64 feedback note is ready for the 20-second reread.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

From day 64's exit, two branches. First, error E (a real moon called imagined), 3 minutes: say "These cards are made up for practice." Hold the card "The moon moves across the sky" and the card "The moon smiles". **Ask.** "Which is a sky fact, and which is imagined?" Expect: the first is a sky fact; the second is imagined. Point to a sky fact, then to an imagined part. Second, error H (a fragment), 2 minutes: show "moon bright" beside "The moon is bright." **Ask.** "Which tells who or what and what happens?" Expect: the second.

#### 15 min. Read and compare

The class rereads Moon's Walk chorally, a page at a time, echoing you (review of echo and choral reading from unit 1; 4 minutes). Then read the prose version (g1-u2-02) once, slowly. Use the arc chart to name, in each version, the events, the details, the time words and the ending, one column for the poem and one for the prose (8 minutes). Events: the moon hangs above the roofs, makes a silver road, climbs, fades at morning. Details: a dog, a boat, owls. Time words: First, Next, Then, At last. Ending: the morning comes and the moon will walk again. Say: "The poem is in lines. The prose is in sentences. Both tell the same events in the same order." Show where the details sit in each (3 minutes).

#### 10 min. Talk

Partners use the turn card (review from kindergarten). **Ask.** "Which events happen in both? Which words tell the order?" Partners point to a picture of each event on the chart and say its time word. Expect: First, Next, Then, At last in both. Hear three pairs say it to the class. Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Kind, specific, helpful feedback

Review: in kindergarten you added one detail after a partner asked a question. Today you learn what a good note sounds like. Point to the writing-process chart; the class is at share. Pip is the writer. Pip reads a made-up two-line draft: "First the moon rose. Next the moon went high." Read the first note card aloud: "Tell where the moon walks." Read the second: "What did you eat today?" For each, every pair holds up the green card if the note helps the writer or the white card if it does not (3 minutes). **Ask.** "Which note helps the writer, and why?" Expect: the first, because it asks for a detail about the moon. The second changes the topic (error P). Pip adds one thing: "The moon went high over the hill." Say: "A kind, specific, helpful note asks for a detail on the same topic." Children say a kind note for a partner's drawing from yesterday (4 minutes); no one is the example of a mistake.

#### 10 min. Expand a sentence

Review: a complete sentence says who or what and what happens (kindergarten). Show the poem's own growth: "I walk." then "I am the moon, and I walk across the sky at night." Point to who, where and when. Pairs get a picture card from Moon's Walk. Each partner says what happens in a finished sentence, then makes it longer with where or when (3 minutes each). Hear the heard six, 40 seconds each.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read the two cards aloud, invented for practice ([[s02-X65a]]). Every child circles A or B on the student page. Hear the heard six on [[s02-X65b]]. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken, not scored. Say hid and hide; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter i's name?" Expect: hide. Blend /l/ /i/ /s/ /t/, "list". Say "tent": first sound /t/, middle sound /e/, last sound /t/. Segment "lamp" with counters: /l/ /a/ /m/ /p/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 11, lesson 57. Teach first (4 minutes): -ed said /id/ after t or d. Say planted, lifted, melted; hear the extra syllable; show base, then ending. Chain with the cards: planted, lifted, melted, hunted, tent, wind. Then the reader I Look Out begins (6 minutes), now that ou and the final clusters are on the map and the words a, i, in, look, see and the are mapped. You read a page, the pair echoes it, then each reads a page smoothly. The class says what the child sees.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27. Dictation (8 minutes): "Dad lifted the soft tent." Children print a word at a time with the map; the and the capital D are named aloud. Then each child prints their first name with its capital (2 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. Supported reading (5 minutes): each child reads aloud one sentence from the prose version of Moon's Walk with you, 30 seconds each (3 minutes), with written feedback on a picture note, then rereads yesterday's phrase for 20 seconds as the revised reading (2 minutes). Use the last minute for the group to reread page 1 of I Look Out. The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread I Look Out with a partner; chain lifted, melted and planted with letter cards; spell hunted on sound boxes; read the word cards look, see, a and the to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the heard-six column to the unit 2 record sheet. Keep the feedback notes for the revised reading tomorrow.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the two notes on the board and show hands in place of the cards. One child: compare the two versions, sort the two notes, add one detail to a sentence, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: write the two notes on paper.

**Skip.** The pairs and the rotation.

- **10 min. Compare.** Read Moon's Walk and its prose version aloud. **Say.** "Which events happen in both?" Expect: the moon hangs above, makes a silver road, climbs, fades at morning.
- **10 min. Note.** You read a made-up draft: "First the sun came up." Your child says a kind note that asks for a detail on the same topic. **Say.** "What do you want to know about the sun?" Expect: where or when.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child reads lifted, planted and tent and prints "Dad lifted the soft tent."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X65a]] You read card A: "First a fox ran to the wall. Next the fox sat. At last the fox went home." Card B: "A fox is red. A fox is quick." Ask: "Point to the card that tells what happened, in order." Expect: card A. [[s02-X65b]] Say: "A fox ran." Ask: "Say it again with one more detail." Expect: a whole sentence.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the compare, the feedback, the exit and the supported reading; shorten the expand part to the heard six. Longer: a pair that finishes writes a note for the other pair's drawing and says why it helps.

### Words and supports

Words: poem, prose, event, detail, time word, ending, feedback. Frames: "First ___. Next ___." "Tell where ___." "The note helps because ___." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Any child may pass. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who points to events in order (error F: a list of facts instead of events) and who adds a detail in a finished sentence (error H: a fragment). Day 66 opens with repairs for both: draw first and next events and add an ending; then say who or what and what happens, then add one detail. Day 66 plans and drafts the class moon poem.
