# Day 64: Tell what the moon might see

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write five line cards: "The moon moves across the sky" and "The moon changes shape" (The Changing Moon); "I step across the dark", "I watch a dog in a yard" and "I fade to pale, and smile" (Moon's Walk). Set two hoops, one with a star (sky fact), one with a cloud bubble (imagined). Copy the heard six from the day 63 column. Put out the two repair picture cards and one short Moon's Walk line card for each child.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening repair

From day 63's exit, if many pointed to the reversed strip (error A), 5 minutes. Say "These cards are made up for practice." Reread the four time words on the arc chart together. Read: "First the dough went in the bowl. Next the bread came out hot." Show two picture cards, the dough and the bread. **Ask.** "Which picture goes first?" Expect: the dough. Children place the two cards under First and Next and say the line again with the time words.

#### 15 min. Read Moon's Walk

Say each word with its picture and have the class say it back: walk, silver, glow, curve. Read Moon's Walk whole (g1-u2-02) while the class looks at the pictures; children listen and do not read. Then read page 2 of The Changing Moon aloud (g1-u2-01): the moon moves across the sky and can be seen at night, and sometimes in the daytime. Say: "The moon in the poem talks and walks. A real moon cannot. That part is imagined." Review: imagined and a sky fact, from day 63's Sun's Day.

#### 10 min. Reread and voice

Read the poem again a page at a time. Each child follows with a finger and says one short phrase after you, all together (6 minutes; the moon's name, the dog, the boat, the owl). Contrast two ways to take part (error N): "watching a partner read" and "saying the phrase with your voice". Then the heard six each say one phrase alone, 30 seconds each, 3 minutes. Any child may pass and say it with you.

#### 10 min. Talk

Hold up the five line cards and read each. Class sort (5 minutes): point to the hoop, sky fact or imagined; say why in a few words. Expect: moves across the sky and changes shape, sky facts; steps across the dark, watches a dog and smiles, imagined. **Ask.** "What does the imagined moon see? Which detail is a sky fact and which is invented?" Partners use the turn card (review from kindergarten), point to a picture in the poem and say one sentence (3 minutes). Pip says: "The moon sees a boat. The boat is invented." Any child may pass.

#### 15 min. Describe and draw

Say: "These ideas are made up for practice." Each child draws one thing the moon might see at night (6 minutes) and marks it with a star for a sky fact or a cloud bubble for imagined. Then each tells a partner in one finished sentence: "The moon sees ___." (4 minutes). Say "Moon bright" beside "The moon is bright." to remind them what finished means (review of complete sentences, from kindergarten). Hear the heard six alone, 40 seconds each, 4 minutes. Do not use the exit picture.

#### 5 min. Exit

Reread two lines of *Quiet Sky*, page 2: "The moon peeks over the hill, a pale and gentle ring." Show the two pictures: a moon with eyes peeking over a hill, and a plain moon above a hill. Every child circles 1 or 2 on the student page ([[s02-X64av2]]). Hear the heard six on [[s02-X64b]] with its picture. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken, not scored. Say hop and hope; **Ask.** "Which word has the sound of the letter o's name?" Expect: hope. Blend /s/ /o/ /f/ /t/, "soft". Say "nest": first sound /n/, middle sound /e/, last sound /t/. Segment "lift" with counters: /l/ /i/ /f/ /t/, four sounds.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 11, lesson 56. Teach first (5 minutes): say melt and count its sounds with counters, /m/ /e/ /l/ /t/; the last two sounds are two letters, lt. Do the same with the final -ft, -st, -nt, -nd, -ng and -nk. Then ou: say "out", "cloud", "found" and show that ou makes the sound in out. Then the chain with letter cards (5 minutes): belt, melt, lift, soft, list, nest, tent, hint, wind, sing, king, bank, sink; then out, cloud, found, loud. Read each; spell every third. The reader for this cycle starts tomorrow; today the pairs read On the Pond again.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Letter forms: new-letter teaching finished on day 27, so today is use. Dictation (8 minutes): "The sun is out." and "Lift the soft tent." Children print a word at a time with the map; the mapped word the is named aloud. Then each child prints their first name with its capital (2 minutes).

#### 30 min. Rotation

Five groups of six, 6 minutes each at your table; today's heard six go first. Supported reading (3 minutes): each child reads aloud one short line of Moon's Walk with you, 30 seconds each; write one line of feedback on a picture note while the next child reads (a hand where the finger followed, a mouth where the voice joined). Then the day 63 repair, 3 minutes: use the one this group's marks need. Error L: read the base, add the taught ending, reread, with "wish" and "wishes" (s02-A30). Error M: stretch the word and mark each sound with a counter before writing "bigger" (s02-A31). The other children rotate through four stations, 6 minutes each: reread On the Pond with a partner; chain belt, melt and lift with letter cards; spell nest and king on sound boxes; read the word cards a, and, in and the to a partner.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Move the exit marks and the heard-six column to the unit 2 record sheet. Keep each child's feedback note for tomorrow's 20-second reread.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the hoops on the floor and the two exit pictures on the board. One child: sort the five lines, say one sentence about the moon, draw it, then the foundations parts.

### At home

**Swap.** No hoops: draw two circles on paper, one a star and one a cloud.

**Skip.** The pairs, the rotation and the reading feedback.

- **10 min. Poem.** Read Moon's Walk aloud. **Say.** "Which part is a sky fact? Which part is invented?" Expect: the moon moves across the sky; the moon walking and smiling is invented.
- **10 min. Draw.** Your child draws one thing the moon might see and says one finished sentence about it.
- **10 min. Sounds.** Your child reads lift, tent, bank and out, and prints "The sun is out."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X64av2]] You read from Quiet Sky: "The moon peeks over the hill, a pale and gentle ring." Draw a moon with eyes peeking over a hill and a plain moon above a hill. Ask: "Point to the picture that is imagined, not real." Expect: the moon with eyes. [[s02-X64b]] Draw a boy on a porch under the moon. Ask: "Say one finished sentence about what the moon sees." Expect: a whole sentence.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the sort, the drawing, the exit and the supported reading; cut the second half of the chain. Longer: a child who finishes adds a second thing the moon sees and says which is a sky fact.

### Words and supports

Words: moon, imagined, sky fact, walk, silver, glow, curve. Frames: "The moon sees ___." "That is a sky fact." "That is imagined." Pointing, drawing and a home language count as answers. Any child may pass. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who points to the imagined picture (error E: a real moon called imagined) and who says a finished sentence (error H: a fragment). Day 65 opens with repairs for both: point to a sky fact, then to an imagined part; then say who or what and what happens. Day 65 compares the moon's poem with a prose version.
