# Day 15: Say it like talking

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the story with its pictures and the hammer poem in large print. Lay out a turn card for each pair, counters or tiles, the chain cards, the two sentence cards, the exit line card, the reader Pat's Mess, the place signs and the chime. Hang the sound-spelling map. Day 14's exit marks give the opening repair (errors C and A). Today rereads and practises; nothing is new, and no one is timed.

### The day

#### 15 min. Reread aloud

Whole class on the rug. Review: echo and choral reading with phrase and beat (day 5). You say a phrase, the class says it back, then all say it together.

First 4 minutes: the repair for day 14's exit (errors C and A). Children who gave only a picture detail, or a reason the page did not give, sit with you and the page 10 picture. The others look at the same picture with a partner and point to three things. Show it: Kofi holds a thin screwdriver at the bell's screw, and little lines of sound curl out of the bell. Say: "The picture shows lines of sound." Now point to "Ring, ring! The bell works." and add it: "The picture shows lines of sound, and the words say the bell works." **Ask.** "Why does the bell ring now?" Before anyone answers, they point to the sentence. Expect: "I turn it, and the screw gets tight." A child who says "Kofi is happy" gave a picture fact; send them back to the sentence. If many children made these errors, run it with the whole class.

Next 4 minutes: page 10, the supported passage. Read it once with the picture, a phrase at a time: "I look at the screw. / It is small. / I find a little screwdriver with a thin tip. / It fits!" The class echoes each phrase, then says the page together with your finger under the words. Nobody decodes it; this is shared reading.

Last 7 minutes: The Hammer Song. Read it once, soft on the taps and strong on the bangs (1 minute). Then say each line and the class echoes it (3 minutes), then everyone says it together (3 minutes). Show the difference once. Say the last line like a robot: "The. Shelf. Stands. Strong. And. Fast." Then like talking: "the shelf stands strong and fast." Children choose the talking voice. The word "bang" is echoed, not read; the ng spelling comes in unit 2.

#### 10 min. Reread and question

Pairs with the turn card. First 2 minutes: partners echo the two refrains, "Tap, tap, tap" soft and "Bang, bang, bang" strong. Then 3 minutes: **Ask.** "Which words sound strong?" Frame: "The strong words are ... because ..." Expect: "Bang, bang, bang", and "the big hammer booms". Hear today's six at their pairs, about 35 seconds each (3 minutes), then give the answer yourself in the last 2 minutes. Sit with three pairs. A child may pass.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, turn card, 4 minutes. Partner A answers, partner B says back what they heard and asks one question to clear up ("Do you mean the taps or the bangs?"), and A replies. Then swap. **Ask.** "Which words make the poem sound like hammering?" Expect: tap, tap, tap and bang, bang, bang repeat, like each hit of a hammer. Hear today's six pairs, 40 seconds each (4 minutes). In the last 2 minutes you say the answer and the pairs tell a neighbor once more.

#### 20 min. Write one sentence

Each child says one sentence about page 10 to a partner (3 minutes). Model it: "Kofi finds a little screwdriver." Then each child draws one picture from page 10 and prints one word label under it, spelling it by its sounds (8 minutes). Hear today's six, 1 minute each (6 minutes): the child says the sentence, you write it under the drawing, and the child reads it back with you. The others add color, and each reads their sentence to a neighbor at the end (3 minutes). The page stays in the child's folder.

Look-fors for the six: (1) says a whole sentence; (2) the sentence matches page 10; (3) prints a label.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is made up for practice." Show the card with the printed line "Sam has a pad. Sam has a map." Read it aloud once. Ask: "Say the line like talking, after me." The whole class echoes it once (1 minute), then you hear today's six one at a time, 20 seconds each (2 minutes). Mark each: shown, shown with help or not yet seen ([[s01-X15]]). A good answer says each sentence as one smooth phrase. A child who says one word at a time with a pause after each has read word by word (error R). Do not time anyone.

#### 5 min. Oral sound work

Spoken blends. Say the sounds with a small gap and the class blends: /f/ /l/ /a/ /t/, "flat"; /h/ /a/ /n/ /d/, "hand". Push a counter for each sound. Isolate the middle sound in each word, /a/. Watch for a dropped consonant (error I) and for two sounds counted as one (error K): one counter for each sound.

#### 10 min. Map and read

Cycle 2 closes. Review: the mapped words the, a, is and has have a part to remember. Point to each on the map and tap the part to remember. Then a chain with letter tiles, changing one sound at a time: that, than (the last sound changes), math (the first sound changes), bath (the first sound changes again). The class reads each word as it is built and taps the th, one sound for two letters. Then two sentence cards, read once each by you and once by every pair with a finger under the words: "Is that the bath?" "That is the bath." Point out the capital letter, the question mark and the period. Sit with three pairs.

#### 10 min. Letters and dictation

Repair first, only if the record shows a gap from day 14 (the letter M or m): that small group works up to 5 minutes. Then teach N and n. Say: "N is a tall stick down, a slant down to the line, and a tall stick up. The little n is a short stick down and then a hump." Children trace, then print. Dictation: say "that man has a hat". Children print a word at a time, with the map for the sounds.

#### 30 min. Rotation

Six groups of five, six places, 5 minutes each. At your table each child reads the cat's refrain on one of pages 5 to 7 of Pat's Mess, "The cat can tap the ...", for meaning, and reads the four chain words (about 55 seconds each). The other places: sound counters (flat, hand), word building with tiles (that, than, math, bath), echo the hammer poem in pairs, draw and label (finish the day's drawing), quiet pairs retelling the page 10 passage. The chime moves everyone one place along.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Mark the record sheet: the exit marks, the six who were heard and their look-fors, the day 14 repair group. Name tomorrow's six. Keep the student pages in the folders.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the poem and the exit line large on the board. No counters: use buttons. One child: reread page 10 and the poem with the adult, answer the exit, and print N n and the dictation; skip the rotation.

### At home

**Swap.** No turn card: pass a pencil. No counters: use buttons.

**Skip.** The repair, the rotation and the class talk.

- **10 min. Read and say.** Read the hammer poem aloud, soft on the taps and strong on the bangs, and say each line for your child to echo.
  **Say.** "Which words sound strong?" Expect: "Bang, bang, bang", and "the big hammer booms".
- **10 min. Draw.** Your child draws one thing from the bicycle story, prints one word, and says one sentence about it.
- **5 min. Letters.** Your child prints N n and the words "that man has a hat".
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X15]] Show a card with "Sam has a pad. Sam has a map." Read it once. Say: "Say the line like talking, after me."

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the sentence cards to one and the rotation to four places; keep the poem, the exit and the letters. Longer: a child who finishes reads the poem to a partner with a different voice for each stanza.

### Words and supports

Words: strong, soft, hammering, passage, echo. Frames: "The strong words are ... because ..." "Do you mean ...?" Pointing, a home language and acting out the hammer count as answers. Say the English back once. Read every card aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 16 rereads the later part of the bicycle story, pages 8 to 11, and describes how the work changes. If many children read the exit line one word at a time (error R), its Reread and question part opens with four minutes: clap each phrase of a line from the poem, then say it.
