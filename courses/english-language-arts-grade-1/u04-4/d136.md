# Day 136: The four parts of an opinion paragraph

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the model, the frame and the strips. Shuffle the plant corner strips, one set a pair. Name today's heard six and the catch-up group, and the children who gave only a likeness on day 135 (error S, s04-X135cv2). Keep the exit card face down.

### The day

#### 5 min. Opening

Say: "These pages are made up for practice. Page C: Our pond corner has frogs. The frogs sit on stones. Page D: Our pond corner has ducks. The ducks swim in the water." **Ask.** "Say one thing that is different between page C and page D." Pairs decide in 90 seconds and three pairs say it, 20 seconds each. Expect: frogs on stones, ducks in water. This is the repair for error S (s04-X135cv2): a child who says only that both are about a pond corner hears "What does page D tell that page C does not?" Then say: "Today we read a page that tells what a writer thinks."

#### 15 min. Read the model

Review: in unit 1 (day 7) a paragraph frame held a topic and a closing, and in unit 3 (day 94) the closing went back to the topic. Review: in kindergarten (day 15) an opinion said what you like, and on day 134 you said "I think the nest should ___ because ___." Review: a view is one side of a debate (day 132). New today: an opinion paragraph has four parts, and the writer takes a position, which is the side the writer chooses. Reread page 4 of A City Nest: Two Views, the take-the-nest-down view, pointing to its picture, 1 minute. Say: "This paragraph is made up for practice." Read The City Nest twice from the printed copy while children follow the picture, 2 minutes.

Worked example, 5 minutes. Shuffle the four model strips on the board and think aloud as you put them in order. "Hawks have a nest on a tall city building" tells what the whole paragraph is about, so it is the topic and goes first. "I think the nest should come down" tells what the writer chooses, so it is the opinion. "I think so because bits of the nest fall onto the sidewalk below" tells why, so it is the reason. "So the city nest should come down, to keep the sidewalk safe" goes back to the topic and says what to do, so it is the closing and goes last. Write the four names on the board in order. Children say each name back after you.

Then each child touches a box on the paragraph frame as you name its job, 4 minutes, one ask a minute.

1. **Ask.** "Touch the box where the writer says what they think." Expect: the opinion box.
2. **Ask.** "Touch the box where the writer tells why." Expect: the reason box.
3. **Ask.** "Touch the box that goes back to what it is about." Expect: the closing box.
4. **Ask.** "Touch the box that comes first." Expect: the topic box.

#### 15 min. Talk

Partners take two turns each time, then two children are heard, 40 seconds each, so the six heard are spread over the three asks. Pass is allowed. Use the turn card.

1. **Ask.** "Do you think the nest should stay or come down? Say: I think the nest should ___ because ___." Use the point-and-reason strips as the bank. Either side is right if the reason fits. Heard: two.
2. **Ask.** "Here is a paragraph that stops early: Hawks have a nest on a tall city building. I think the nest should stay. I think so because the young need it as they grow. Say a closing for it." Expect a sentence that returns to the nest, such as "So the nest should stay." Heard: two.
3. **Ask.** "What would the model be missing if we took away the third strip?" Expect: the reason, so it would not say why. Heard: two.

#### 12 min. Analyze the parts

Pairs lay the shuffled plant corner strips in order, saying each name as they lay it, 4 minutes: Our class has a plant by the window. I think we should water it on Monday. I think so because the soil is dry after the weekend. So we should water the plant on Monday. Three pairs show their order, 20 seconds each. Then each child draws one small picture in each box of the frame for the model, a building with a nest, a nest coming down, bits falling, a clear sidewalk, with one word label, 5 minutes. Finish with one ask, 1 minute. **Ask.** "Pip puts the closing second, right after the topic. Is Pip right?" Thumbs. Expect: no, the closing goes last.

#### 8 min. Closing

Review: the four kinds of sentence (unit 2, day 35, and day 133). Say the model's closing, then say it again with a shout: "So the city nest should come down, to keep the sidewalk safe!" **Ask.** "Which one is the exclamation? Hold up one finger for the first and two for the second." Expect: two. Then say: "Here is a new paragraph, made up for practice. Our class has a book basket. I think the basket should stay by the door. I think so because the books are easy to reach." **Ask.** "Say a closing for the book basket paragraph." Pairs say one, 90 seconds. Hear three pairs, 40 seconds each. Expect: "So the basket should stay by the door."

#### 5 min. Exit

Say: "This card is invented for practice." Read the card once from the card sheet and show its three pictures. All children mark a picture on the page. **Ask.** "Which picture shows what this writer wants?" ([[s04-X136a]]) While the rest finish, the heard six come to your table, 35 seconds each, with the card in view. **Ask.** "Which words give the reason?" ([[s04-X136b]]) **Ask.** "What does the ending do?" ([[s04-X136c]]) **Ask.** "What is this writer's topic?" ([[s04-X136d]]) Mark each: shown, shown with help (you reread the card once), not yet seen. A child who gives the topic words where the reason was asked is error C, for day 137's opening. A liking in place of a reason is error B.

#### 5 min. Oral sounds

Spoken only. Say tub and tube: which has the long vowel sound? Blend /p/ /l/ /ay/. Say the first, middle and last sound of stay. Segment tray.

#### 10 min. Map and spell

Cycle 22, lesson 112. Review: ay and ow say long vowel sounds (day 135). Chain on small boards, 20 seconds a word: stay, play, tray, away; then window, pillow, shadow. Say each, tap the sounds, write it. Review: a at the start of away says /uh/ (day 125). Then map one irregular word, 3 minutes: today. The first part says /tuh/, and day says the long a. Then read page 1 of Pat's Birthday together.

#### 10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation

Print "Hawks have a nest on a tall city building." as a copy, one letter form checked (the capital H), 5 minutes. Dictate "We stay away from the nest." whole, then a word at a time, 4 minutes.

#### 30 min. Rotation

The catch-up group first, 10 minutes: the six children the cycle record names; map ay and ow, blend, read, spell, reread. Then four groups of six at your table, 5 minutes each, with Pat's Birthday pages 1 to 9, first use, each child heard in turn for 40 seconds, so each group ends with 1 minute to spare. Page 10 waits until both, always and laugh are mapped. The other groups reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations.

#### 5 min. Organize and record

Fill the heard-six column, the cycle record and today's exit marks. Name day 137's opening repair.

### Other rooms

No printer: say the strips aloud and write the four names on the board. One child: put the four plant corner strips in order and answer the exit aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: write each sentence on a scrap of paper.

**Skip.** The sound chains.

- **10 min. Listen.** Read The City Nest aloud twice. **Say.** "Which sentence tells why?" Expect: the one with because.
- **10 min. Order.** Mix the four plant corner sentences. **Say.** "Put them in order: what it is about, what I think, why, then the ending." Expect: plant, water it, dry soil, water on Monday.
- **10 min. Words.** Print "stay" and "play."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X136a]] [[s04-X136b]] [[s04-X136c]] [[s04-X136d]] Read a short made-up opinion your child has not seen. Your child points to the picture of what the writer wants, then says the reason words, what the ending does and the topic.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the worked example, the ordering and the exit. Longer: a child who finishes draws a second reason for the model.

### Words and supports

Words: opinion, topic, reason, position, closing, because, stay, come down. Frame: "I think the nest should ___ because ___." Pointing, drawing, a scribe and a home language count; say the English back once.

### Tomorrow

The exit tells you who can say the topic, the opinion, the reason and the closing. A child who gave the topic words for the reason (error C, s04-X136b) goes to day 137's opening repair, and one who gave a liking (error B) hears the opinion said as a full sentence first. Day 137 drafts a nest opinion with the whole class.
