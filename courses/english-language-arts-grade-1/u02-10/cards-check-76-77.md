# Days 76 and 77 check cards (teacher only, unseen until the day)

Say each time: "These cards are made up for practice." Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the foot of every card. Plain black line drawings, no shading. Keep the cards covered; collect every card after each child. Day 76 uses the first set (children 1 to 15 of the hearing order), day 77 the second set (children 16 to 30). Each child reads one of three passages by rotation: children 1, 4, 7, 10, 13 read prose; 2, 5, 8, 11, 14 read the poem; 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 read the information. Every word below a child decodes uses only taught patterns (short vowels, sh, th, ch, wh, ck, qu, plural -s and -es, -ed, -ing, -er, -est, doubled finals, ou, final and starting clusters) and the mapped words the, a, is, has, this, look, like, and, said, see, says. No silent-e word.

## Day 76 reading passages

**Prose, "A Stump Job".** "Jed has a pick. A big stump is in the sand. Jed hacks at it. Dot yanks it. Crunch! The stump is out. "This job is quick," says Jed." Picture: two children and a stump in sand.

**Poem, "In the Shed".** "The pick goes chip, chip, chip. / The mop goes flip, flop, flip. / The drill goes zip, zip, zip. / Then the tools can rest and sit." Picture: three tools on hooks.

**Information, "Snips".** "Snips are a tool for tin. Snips have two jaws. A man grips the snips and clips. The jaws cut the tin. Snips help a man fix a tin lid." Picture: snips cutting a strip of tin.

## Day 76 words (cover until the ask)

- Digraph and short-vowel words: shack, chomp, thump, trash, chest.
- Words with endings: yanked, gulping, plucks, thicker, quickest.
- Mapped words: the, a, is, has, this, look, like, and, said, see.

## Day 76 oral sounds (three groups of five)

- Long or short vowel, children hold up the long card or the short card: tab, cake, fig, soap, wasp.
- Blend and say, one word to each child: blimp, brisk, snack, flock, grub.
- Say the sound asked: the first sound of chip; the middle sound of drum; the last sound of plug (the three words go round the group).
- Say each sound in the word with a finger tap: frisk (five sounds).

## Day 76 dictation (whole class, twice each)

plug; this; skunk. For skunk, say "Spell it the way it sounds." Print, never copy, from the board.

## Day 77 reading passages

**Prose, "The Tent".** "Tavi has a tent and a sack. A gust hits the tent. It flaps and flaps. Tavi grabs a stick and jabs it in. The tent is snug. "This is a fix," says Tavi." Picture: a child, a flapping tent, a stick.

**Poem, "Tools Nap".** "The pump goes pump, pump, pump. / The clamp goes snap, snap, snap. / The brush goes swish, swish, swish. / Then the tools nap in the shed." Picture: three tools on hooks.

**Information, "A Crank".** "A crank is a tool. A crank has a stub and a grip. A man grips the crank and spins it. The crank can lift a tub up. A crank helps a man lift a big tub." Picture: a crank lifting a tub.

## Day 77 words (cover until the ask)

- Digraph and short-vowel words: shrub, flash, crush, smash, snub.
- Words with endings: tucked, chomping, jabs, quicker, thickest.
- Mapped words: the, a, is, has, this, look, like, and, said, see.

## Day 77 oral sounds (three groups of five)

- Long or short vowel: yak, bean, rib, dime, vat.
- Blend and say: skunk, plunk, stunt, cramp, crust.
- Say the sound asked: the first sound of shop; the middle sound of crab; the last sound of fish.
- Say each sound in the word with a finger tap: scalp (five sounds).

## Day 77 dictation (whole class, twice each)

flush; look; stunt. For stunt, say "Spell it the way it sounds."

## Day 77 topic cards (whole class)

**Information read aloud once, "Pliers".** "Pliers are a tool. Pliers have two long handles and two short jaws. You squeeze the handles, and the jaws grip. Pliers can hold a nail still. Pliers can pull a bent wire straight. A person uses pliers to hold small things that fingers cannot hold well." Picture: pliers pulling a bent wire straight, handles and jaws labelled with one-word labels. The picture also shows a small red dot on one handle that the words never mention.

**Story read aloud once, "Tavi's Pedal", told by Tavi.** Page 1: "I am Tavi. My bicycle has a squeaky pedal. Squeak, squeak, squeak! I feel cross. It is a sunny morning in my yard." Page 2: "My uncle Anan walks over. He has a small tin of grease. "A squeaky part needs grease," says Uncle Anan. I dab grease on the pedal. I spin it. The squeak is gone." Page 3: "I smile. "Now I know," I say. "A squeaky part needs grease."" Pictures: page 1 Tavi frowning, hands over ears, bicycle on its side; page 2 Uncle Anan holding a small tin; page 3 Tavi smiling on the bicycle.

**Writing card, "Tongs".** A picture of kitchen tongs lifting a corn cob out of a pot, with the word "tongs" printed once. The child writes or draws and labels, or tells a scribe, a short piece about tongs: the tool, two facts, a closing.

## Notes for the teacher

The child gets the card face down, turns it, and the clock starts at the first ask. The pliers picture carries the small red dot on purpose: a picture fact the words never say. Tavi tells the whole story; Uncle Anan speaks only inside quotation marks.
