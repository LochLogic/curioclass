# Section 3.4 opener: comparing two books of one series (days 94 to 97)

## What the section does

For four days the class reads chapters 2 to 5 of book 2 and compares them with book 1: setting, plot and then theme. Children learn the difference between what happened and what it teaches, write a short narrative episode in the series world, and finish two story maps. On day 97 they also write a retained recount of each book with its message.

## How the days run

Each day is 90 minutes. Days 94 and 97 are partner-rereading days. Day 95 replaces the main writing with one episode, written alone in one sitting. Day 96 starts a second episode, the longer task of the section, and finishes it as the day's exit. Day 97 closes the maps and the recount.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Same author, same characters.** A series may keep its people and change its problem.
- **Setting, plot and theme are different things.** Setting is where and when. Plot is the events. Theme is what the events teach that could fit another story.
- **A single event is not a theme.** Ask: what does this show beyond itself?
- **An episode needs a situation.** Say who is where when the problem opens, put events in order, use time words that keep that order, and close the problem the episode opened.
- **Compare in the same row.** Give a likeness and a difference for each dimension, each with a located detail.

## The errors, in plain words

- **F:** Gives similarities without differences. Repair: Add a contrasting detail in each comparison dimension. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **G:** Treats a single event as a theme. Repair: Explain what the event suggests beyond itself. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **S:** Begins a narrative without an identifiable situation. Repair: Name who is where when the problem opens. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **U:** Uses a time word that reverses intended order. Repair: Place the two events on an arrow before choosing the link. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **V:** Ends by opening an unrelated problem. Repair: Close the consequence of the scene's existing action. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet, dated. Exits are unscored.
