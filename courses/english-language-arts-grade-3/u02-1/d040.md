# Day 40: Read the frog poems and ask how a frog's home shapes it

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, one set of frog poem copies for each child, the day 40 exit card and the record sheet. Draw the certainty line on the chart: less sure on the left, more sure on the right. Write the recording list: five names for today, then five for day 42. Pull the day 39 regroup list and the folders to hand back. Put the question on a strip, hidden.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*2 min.* Hand back the folders, the day 39 regroup list, the day 39 exit (s01-X39) and the day 39 repair practice (s01-P39-1, s01-P39-2). *8 min.* Review: -ness (day 31), -ly (day 32), -able (day 33). Say each word once and children write it on a slate: stillness, softly, breakable, [[s02-P40-4]]. Review: an irregular word has a part that does not sound the way it looks (grade 1). Map answer and word, [[s02-P40-5]].

#### 20 min. Independent reading and first recordings

Five children, 3 minutes each, swap included, 15 minutes in all. Review: recording (day 31). Each says a purpose, reads Under the Leaf, hears the replay and rereads one sentence, [[s02-P40-9]]. A copy of the poem is in every folder. The rest read from their logs. For the last 5 minutes everyone writes one log line. The four conferences move to day 41.

#### 15 min. Reading and reread

*4 min.* Read Pond Voices aloud twice; the second time children close their eyes. Show the strip: "How does where a frog lives shape how it looks and acts?" Post it. Say: "We will keep asking this." *2 min.* Read Under the Leaf once, aloud, for listening only; it is the poem the class will record. *2 min.* Read The Learned Fish and The Frog by Hilaire Belloc (1896, public domain). Say that polly-wog is an old word for a tadpole. *4 min.* Hard-part reread, in pairs. Review: a stanza is a group of lines (grade 2). Ask: "Which words make the quiet pond a chorus?" Pairs mark them on stanza one and two of Pond Voices, [[s02-P40-6]]. *3 min.* List the marked words on the chart: hushed shows the quiet before; peep, answers and sing along make the chorus.

#### 10 min. Word and sentence work

*3 min.* Review: literal and nonliteral, said exactly or meant another way (day 7). Model two lines from Pond Voices: "The reeds lean close" says it exactly; "wakes the water from its sleep" means another way, [[s02-K40-1]], [[s02-K40-2]]. Ask first, then answer: could water really sleep? *2 min.* Children sort four lines, [[s02-P40-1]]. *5 min.* New: a certainty line. Write on the chart: Mira heard a peep in the dark and ___ it was a frog. Try wondered, suspected and knew and place each on the line. Children then fill the three blanks on their page and tell a partner one choice, [[s02-P40-2]].

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: the discussion moves chart (day 1). *4 min.* Pairs, 2 minutes each, swap included: name a line from Pond Voices, then ask your partner for a detail about it, [[s02-P40-7]]. *6 min.* One demonstration group of five, about 65 seconds each: each child says one thing the partner's answer made clearer, [[s02-P40-8]]. Record these five on the record sheet. The other 25 watch with the moves chart. The five other groups are heard on later days.

#### 20 min. Writing

*4 min.* Say with a partner, in turn: choose one image from Pond Voices. Say what the words say exactly, what the poet means, and what it tells about the frog's home. *14 min.* Write three sentences in the folder, [[s02-P40-3]]. Use "The words ___ explain ___." Images come from Pond Voices only. *2 min.* Swap and read.

Check: the partner finds, in each set, the exact words, the plain meaning and the pond or reeds where the frog lives.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hear the six children the record shows least heard, 45 seconds each. Hand each one the card at their own turn and read it twice, [[s02-X40]]. Everyone else reads from their log. Unscored. Shown: says the rain made the frog go below and drummed compares the sound to a drum. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What is drummed like?" Not yet seen: says the frog plays a drum (error C) or adds a fact the card does not give (error A).

### Other rooms

One child: the adult is the partner for the talk, reads the poems aloud and asks for one line and one detail. Record where the child paused. No device: write the purpose and reread aloud, with no recording. Record any help.

### At home

**Swap.** No poem copy: say Pond Voices from memory.
**Skip.** The slate words.

- **10 min. Read.** Read Pond Voices aloud twice, once with eyes closed.
  **Say.** "Which words make the quiet pond a chorus?" Expect: peep, answers, sing along.
- **10 min. Think.** Find one line that is meant another way, [[s02-P40-1]].
  **Say.** "What does the poet mean?" Expect: a plain meaning, such as the peep ends the quiet.
- **10 min. Write.** Write what that line says exactly, what the poet means and what it tells about the frog's home, [[s02-P40-3]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s02-X40]] aloud, from the strip you hold.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the swap and read; keep the sort, the three sentences and the exit. Longer: a child finds a second image and says what it tells about the pond.

### Words and supports

Hushed, peep, answers, chorus, stillness. Frame: "The words ___ explain ___." Large print; the poems read aloud twice; a scribe for ideas, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 41 reads the long-tongue tale and asks how its events explain a feature. The opening spends 3 minutes repairing the commonest letter on today's exit, [[s02-X40]]. The folders and the poem copies stay in the folder.
