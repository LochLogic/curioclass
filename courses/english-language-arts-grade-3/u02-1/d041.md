# Day 41: Read the long-tongue tale and see how its events explain a feature

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page and a copy of the tale for each child and each pair. Write the day 41 exit card on the room copy. Draw the tale chart on paper with four rows: who tells it, the situation, three events joined by arrows, the ending. Mark five children for conferences on the record sheet.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*3 min.* Return and repair, on the commonest letter in day 40's exit (s02-X40), error C. Write "The reeds lean close" and "wakes the water from its sleep". Read each twice. Ask: what do the words say, and what does the poet mean? *7 min.* Review: -tion and -sion (grade 2). Say vacation, station and decision once each; children write them on slates, [[s02-P41-7]]. Review: an irregular word has a part that does not sound the way it looks (day 40). Map does, [[s02-P41-8]].

#### 20 min. Independent reading

Four conferences of 4 minutes (16 minutes) while the rest read from their logs. Then 4 minutes for one log line. Add the four to the heard-six column on the record sheet.

#### 15 min. Reading and reread

*2 min.* Review: The Lion and the Mouse (day 12). On a slate, write the first event, [[s02-P41-1]]. Unscored. *6 min.* Read Why the Frog Has a Long Tongue aloud once. As each word comes, show it with a gesture: startled, flexible, smirk. *7 min.* Hard-part reread, in pairs: "Which words show what changes Kikker's approach to the contest?" Pairs reread from "All day long" to the idea and point to the words, [[s02-K41-2]].

#### 10 min. Word and sentence work

*6 min.* On the tale chart, model the tale. Review: a narrator is the teller (grade 2). Ask: does Kikker tell it, or does someone outside the tale? Read the first paragraph and look for I or his, [[s02-K41-1]]. Then fill the situation, three events with arrows, and the ending. Review: time words (grade 2) and so and because (day 5). *4 min.* Children mark one time word in the tale, [[s02-P41-2]], and one word for effect, smirk, [[s02-P41-3]].

#### 10 min. Talk

*5 min.* Pairs, 2 minutes each, swap included: tell your partner what changes Kikker's approach, [[s02-P41-9]]; then say how your partner's answer changed your own, [[s02-P41-10]]. *5 min.* Six volunteers, 50 seconds each, tell the class. Use the moves chart. Anyone may pass. Hear the rest on later days.

#### 20 min. Writing

*6 min.* Each child makes a story map of the tale: the situation, three events with arrows, the ending, [[s02-P41-5]]. *2 min.* Judge two given sentences against the map; mark the one that matches the tale, [[s02-P41-4]]. *12 min.* Write two sentences: what the tale says about practice, and one event that shows it, [[s02-P41-6]]. The map and sentences stay in the folder. Judge with four look-fors: events in order, a cause linked to the result, a time word, a closing line. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hand out the exit card and read nothing aloud. Children write alone, [[s02-X41]]. Unscored. Shown: one sentence with because, the events in the card's order, the skin result after the cause. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What happened first?" Not yet seen: the result before its cause (error Y) or a fact with no events (error B).

### Other rooms

One child: the adult reads the tale aloud, then the child tells it with then. No chart paper: draw the map on one sheet. Record any help.

### At home

**Swap.** No copy of the tale: tell it from memory.
**Skip.** The slate words.

- **10 min. Tell.** Your child tells the tale in order, with the word then.
  **Say.** "What did Kikker do that made the difference?" Expect: he practiced his tongue.
- **10 min. Map.** Your child fills in the story map, [[s02-P41-5]].
- **10 min. Write.** Your child writes two sentences about practice, [[s02-P41-6]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s02-X41]] alone, from the strip you hold.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the talk to pairs only; keep the map, the two sentences and the exit. Longer: a child writes a third sentence about what Kikker did for the other frogs.

### Words and supports

Startled, flexible, smirk, practice, contest. Frame: "Kikker ___, so ___." Large print; the tale read aloud twice; a scribe for ideas, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 42 reads the colorful-frog tale and plans a tale of the child's own on a plan card. The opening spends 3 minutes repairing the commonest letter on today's exit, [[s02-X41]]. The map and sentences stay in the folder.
