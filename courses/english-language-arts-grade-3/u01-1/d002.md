# Day 2: What I know about long words and spelling

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print one spelling screen page for each child. Make the long-word reading card from the screen cards, and list your first 18 children from the record sheet for it. Cut the menu sort cards: lamp, slips (closed); she, so (open); date, home (silent e); pail, tray, peach, green (vowel team). Find the day 2 line of exit cards 1 to 4. Put slates out.

### The day

#### 10 min. Opening

Three minutes repair day 1's exit, and hand back the pointing answers and table rows (s01-P1-1, s01-P1-2, s01-P1-3 and s01-P1-4) with one comment each. If many children missed it, use the letter you saw most.

If error A (a fact the card does not give): read a new card aloud: "Omar put the red cup on the top shelf. The shelf is by the window." Children put a finger on the sentence and answer from it. **Ask.** Where is the cup? Expect: on the top shelf. **Ask.** What is in the cup? Expect: the card does not say.

If error D (a picture object named, no contribution): show a new picture of boots by a door and snow on the ground outside. Words: "Ana put on her boots." Fill two columns, the words say and the picture shows. Expect: the picture shows snow, so it is cold outside.

Seven minutes on slates, unscored. Review: in grade 2 you read vowel teams, two-syllable words and the prefixes un- and re-. Say each word, children write it: pail, grow (map the whole team first); picnic, paper (say the syllables, then write); unlock, redo (say the base, then say what the prefix does). Then children map friend from memory. Two new words for the wall: says (ay says /e/, s says /z/) and does (oe says /u/, s says /z/). Pin them under friend, heart, once and neighbors.

#### 5 min. Grammar

Review: in grade 2 an apostrophe and s shows that something belongs to one owner. Write "the girl's hat" and "the dog's bone". **Ask.** Who owns the hat? Expect: the girl. Say the owner, then add apostrophe s. Children write "the bird's nest" and "Sam's cap" on slates. Hold up and look; no scores.

#### 15 min. Partner rereading

Review: you learned partner rereading in grade 2, for expression. The feedback card is new in grade 3 and has three things: accurate, in phrases, expressive. Never talk about speed. Pairs reread pages 5 and 6 (151 words, about 1.3 minutes a pass). Each child reads the two pages twice, the partner listens, ticks one box and says one thing that went well and one next try. Do not stop a reader mid-sentence; say what you heard after. Walk and hear three pairs, 3 minutes each, and write them in the heard column. The other pairs finish by 12 minutes; then they choose from the tub.

#### 55 min. Screens

Say to the class: "Today I find out how you read and spell long words. It is not a grade. It tells me which group will help you most." Explain to yourself: a screen shows what a child does on these words today. It does not show what the child can learn, and it never decides who reads which book.

*First 15 minutes, whole class, written.* Say each of twelve words once, say it in a plain sentence of your own, then say it again; children write it on the screen page: rain, seed, float, napkin, tiger, cradle, baking, running, rerun, unfair, said, again. Then say once: "We were running to see the tiger again." Children write the sentence. Give no help and spell no word aloud. Collect the pages. After class mark each word right or wrong and the pattern missed (vowel team, syllable, ending rule, tricky word).

*Then 40 minutes.* One child at a time, you hear the long-word reading card, 2 minutes each, 18 children in list order: 36 minutes, 4 for changes. Note which syllable pieces a child blends and which a child guesses (error P). Do not read a word to a child. After about five seconds on one word, say "Go on". Everyone else works from the student page menu: read from the tub; sort the menu cards by syllable type; or draw and label a river library. Children return to the menu without asking you.

**Ask.** (to a child, once, at the end of the card) Which word was hardest? Expect: any word; write it down.

#### 5 min. Exit

Say once, slowly, as children write on the exit lines of their page: [[s01-X2]]. Unscored. This is the one exit where help is not given, because spelling is the evidence. Shown: the apostrophe sits before the s and sitting has two t. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "Whose cat is it?" Not yet seen: neither mark.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the twelve spelling lines on scrap paper. No slates: use scrap. No tub: use any books in the room. One projector: show only the story pages; keep the exit sentence unseen.

### At home

**Swap.** No slate: use scrap paper. No story pages: use any page of any book.
**Skip.** The screens and the card.

- **10 min. Read.** Your child reads one page of any book aloud. Listen all the way through before you say anything.
  **Say.** "Read it once more, like you are talking." Expect: the second reading sounds smoother.
- **10 min. Spell.** Choose three words from that page. Say each slowly; your child taps the sounds, writes it, covers it and checks it.
- **5 min. Who owns it?** Write "the girl's hat". 
  **Say.** "Who owns it?" Expect: the girl, so the apostrophe goes after girl.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Say [[s01-X2]] once, slowly. Your child writes it alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the menu to the tub and keep the screen and the card. Longer: children who finish the menu write three words from the story and the pattern in each.

### Words and supports

Vowel team, syllable, prefix, owner. Frames: "Who owns it? The ___'s ___." Access: exits may be read aloud except where reading or decoding is the evidence, which is this exit and the screens. A scribe for the sentence is not allowed on the screen; allow a pointer or large print. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 3 opens with three minutes on this exit. Error N is "bakers cat" with no apostrophe. Error O is "siting" without the double t. Day 3 is the oral-reading day: a first oral sample and a short writing sample.
