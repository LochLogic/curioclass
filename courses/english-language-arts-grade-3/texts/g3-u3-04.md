# How Series Books Are Made

A series is a group of books that belong together. The books in a series share some of the same characters. They often share a setting too. Each book still tells its own story, with its own beginning, middle and end.

I am the author of the Courtyard Club series, and I planned it with a few simple steps. First, I wrote notes about the characters. Ada likes to draw and wants things to be right. Ben likes to ask questions. I kept those notes in one folder. Every time I started a new chapter, I read them again so that Ada and Ben would act like themselves.

Second, I chose a setting. The books take place at one school, in one classroom and one small garden. I think this is the best choice. When readers know the place, they can notice small changes, such as a table moved to a new spot. Another reader might prefer a new place in every book. I do not think that is wrong, but it is not my choice.

Third, I gave each book a new plot. In the first book, Ada plans a route. In the second book, she plans seats. The problems are different, but the characters are the same. That is why a reader can compare the two books and say what is alike and what has changed.

Fourth, I wrote more than one draft. A draft is a first try. I kept every old draft in the same folder, and I think this is very useful. An old draft shows what I changed and why. Sometimes the old way was wrong, but looking at it helped me see the better way. I believe a writer should never throw a draft away too soon.

Last, I asked other people to read the chapters. Their questions showed me parts that were hard to follow. The narrator, who tells the story, also changed when I read it aloud. I made the narrator's opinions short, so readers could decide for themselves if they agreed.

Making a series takes patience. It takes notes, drafts, questions and many small checks. That is only my view, and you may have a different one. What do you think a series needs most?
