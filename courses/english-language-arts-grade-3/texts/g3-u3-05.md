## Teacher preview (sensitivity and difficulty)

The plans call the book sustained fantasy with older diction and danger, so preview before offering it, and keep it optional, never assessed. Chapter references come from a targeted check of the library transcription on 2026-11-20, not a full read; a person should read the book before recommending it.

- Danger and death in a fairy-tale voice: the house falls on the Wicked Witch of the East (I, II). The Tin Woodman chops the head off a wolf and the Scarecrow twists the necks of crows (XII). Black bees die against the Tin Woodman (XII). The Witch melts when Dorothy throws water on her (XII). Chapters XXI and XXII have a forest enemy the Lion must fight and armless Hammer-Heads who knock the travelers down.
- Fear and peril: the poppy field puts Dorothy, Toto and the Lion to sleep (VIII). The Winged Monkeys carry off the travelers, and the Witch keeps the Lion captive (XII to XIV).
- Hard ideas: the Winkies work under the Witch, and her wolves speak of making strangers slaves (XII); the Tin Woodman tells how he lost his body part by part (V); the Wizard turns out to be a humbug who has tricked everyone (XV, XVI).
- Munchkins, Winkies and Quadlings are invented fantasy peoples, not real communities.
- Reading level: long sentences and old words (garret, cyclone cellar, humbug); no measure on file. A child who finds it hard may read only the early chapters, or listen; do not push it.
