Teacher note. The characters, the Thursday book bus and the village are invented; the valley (Swaledale, in the Yorkshire Dales in England), its stone walls, field barns and sheep are real. Read whole on day 6, reread on 7 and 8, used on 9, 33 and 35. What the days need is in the story: Sam's wish (book two), his preparation (the bus card, the book in a bag, the closing time, packing the borrowed book: days 33 and the contract), his effort (the climb, the counting game: day 6 and 8), a cause and result (he arrives in time and another reader has just returned the book: day 6), and the picture mood (the low gray clouds, then the sunlight on the walls: day 7). Nonliteral phrases for day 7: the clouds sit like gray wool hats (page 3), his legs feel like jelly (page 3), a library that has grown wheels (page 5), stones that fit together like a puzzle (page 7). The exit strips for days 6 to 8 and 33 are separate invented practice strips; none is a sentence of this story. Children are not asked about their own travel to school or their own libraries.

## Text

[page 1]
Sam lives in Swaledale, a valley in the Yorkshire Dales in England. Stone walls climb the hills in long gray lines. White sheep graze between them, and old stone barns stand in the fields.
Sam's house sits high on a hill, far from the village. On Thursdays the book bus comes to the village green. It stays for one hour. Then it drives away, and it will not come back for a whole week.
Picture: A wide green valley with stone walls running up the hillsides. Sheep dot the slopes, and a stone barn sits in a field. High on the hill, a small farmhouse with a smoking chimney. Far below, a village with a green.

[page 2]
Today Sam has a plan. Last week he borrowed the first book in a story about two cousins who build a secret wall. He read it in four days. Now he must have the second book.
He checks the bus card pinned on the kitchen wall. Thursday, eleven o'clock to twelve o'clock. He wraps the first book in a bag to keep off the drizzle and zips it in his pack. Then he sets off.
Picture: Sam, a boy of about eight in a yellow raincoat, stands at a kitchen table. A card on the wall shows a bus and a clock. He slides a book in a plastic bag into his pack. Gray rain streaks the window.

[page 3]
The lane drops, and then it climbs. The hill is steep, and the wind pushes against Sam's chest. The clouds sit on the hilltops like gray wool hats. Soon his legs feel like jelly.
Sam stops beside a stone wall. His breath puffs out in a little cloud. He could turn back. The village is still so far away.
Picture: A tiny Sam in a yellow raincoat climbs a long, narrow lane between stone walls. The hill rises high above him and the clouds hang low. Sheep watch him from behind a wall.

[page 4]
Then Sam makes a game. "One wall," he says. He walks to it. "Two walls." He walks to the next. By the tenth wall, the road bends, and he hears an engine. Sam runs the last steps.
Below him, the village green opens up. The book bus is there. It is red and green, with bright windows and a sign that says LIBRARY.
Picture: Sam reaches the end of the lane. Below him is a village green with a red and green bus parked beside it. A sign on the bus reads LIBRARY. Sam's cheeks are pink and his hair is wet.

[page 5]
Inside, the bus looks like a library that has grown wheels. Shelves line both sides. A librarian called Mrs. Patel smiles at him.
"I am looking for book two," Sam says, trying to catch his breath.
Mrs. Patel looks at the screen. "I am sorry, Sam. It is out on loan." Sam's shoulders drop. A week is a long time to wait.
Just then, the door opens. A girl named Hannah comes in with a book in her hands. She has finished it, and it is book two.
Picture: Inside the bus, shelves full of books reach from floor to ceiling. Mrs. Patel, a woman with a purple scarf, looks at a screen. Sam stands in front of her, his pack still on. The door behind him opens, and a girl in a blue coat steps in.

[page 6]
Mrs. Patel stamps the book back in. "Now it is on the shelf again," she says, and she hands it to Sam.
Hannah grins. "It is good," she tells him. "You will want to read it slowly."
"Thank you," Sam says. He gives Mrs. Patel the first book. She checks it in and stamps the new one. Sam slides it into his pack and zips the bag shut. The clock says nearly twelve.
Picture: Mrs. Patel holds out a book to Sam over the counter. Hannah leans on a shelf, grinning. Sam reaches with both hands. A clock on the wall of the bus shows almost twelve.

[page 7]
The bus drives away, and Sam turns for home. This time the wind is at his back. The hill is still steep, but it feels shorter. The stone walls look different now. Each wall is built of stones that fit together like a puzzle.
At the top, Sam looks down at the village. The bus is a little red and green spot. He smiles.
Picture: Sam climbs the lane in the other direction, a pack on his back, with the village far below. The clouds are breaking, and a bar of sunlight lights the stone walls. A small red and green bus drives away along the valley road.

[page 8]
That night, by the lamp, Sam opens the new book. He reads the first chapter slowly, just as Hannah said. Then he finds the bus card and crosses off a day. Six more days until Thursday.
A week is long. But a good book makes it shorter.
Picture: Sam in bed under a patchwork quilt with an open book in the lamp's circle of light. On the wall, the bus card hangs beside a calendar with one day crossed off. Outside, the dark hills show the lines of the stone walls.
