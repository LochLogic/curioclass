## Text

Books on the Move

Some readers live far from a library building. For them, the library can come by bus, by boat, or in the mail. A book carrier is the vehicle or the person that brings books to readers. A carrier follows a route, which is the path it travels. Along the route it makes stops. At each stop, readers borrow books and return the ones they have finished.

By Road in England

Lincolnshire is a county in England with many farms and small villages. Its library service runs mobile libraries that visit country communities. A stop list tells readers the place and the time of each stop. In 2026, one route visits three villages on a Friday: South Witham, Corby Glen and Pickworth. The mobile library stays in South Witham from 11:15 until 12:00. It comes back to the same stops about every four weeks, so readers can plan ahead.

By Boat in Bangladesh

In the Chalan Beel region of Bangladesh, few roads reach the villages. Boats are the best way to travel all year. A group called Shidhulai Swanirvar Sangstha began in 1998 and sends boats to villages. Some of its boats are library boats. People can borrow books, read for pleasure and look up facts in reference books. The boats have flat bottoms, so they can cross shallow rivers and flooded land. Solar panels on the roofs make electricity for lights and computers.

By Bus and by Mail in New Mexico

New Mexico is a state in the United States. Its State Library lists three bookmobiles: West, Northeast and East. Readers who live 20 miles or more outside a city, far from a stop, can use Books by Mail instead. The books travel in canvas bags, and the library pays the postage both ways. To send books back, a reader flips the label over and leaves the bag for the postal carrier. A reader can keep up to ten books at a time, for six weeks.

The Same Job

A road, a river and a mail route are very different places. But each carrier does the same job. It brings books to readers who live far away. It also takes finished books back, so other readers can borrow them.
