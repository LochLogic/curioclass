# Water on Earth

## 1. Why Water Matters

Look at a picture of Earth from space. Much of it is blue. Water falls from clouds, fills rivers, and flows from taps. Every plant, animal, and person needs water to live.

But not all water is the same. Ocean water is salty, and people cannot drink it. Freshwater has very little salt. People can drink it, cook with it, wash with it, and use it to grow food.

This book asks: why does clean freshwater matter, and how can people protect it?

## 2. Where Water Is Found

Picture one hundred cups holding all the water on Earth. More than ninety-six of those cups would hold salty ocean water. Only about two and a half cups would hold freshwater.

Most of that freshwater is hard to reach. About seven of every ten cups of freshwater are frozen in ice caps and glaciers. About three of every ten are groundwater, which is water under the ground. Only about one cup in a hundred is surface water, found in lakes, rivers, and other places above ground.

So the freshwater people can easily reach is a small part of Earth's water.

## 3. How Water Moves

First, the sun warms the ocean, lakes, and rivers. Some water evaporates. That means it turns into a gas called water vapor and rises into the air. Next, high in the sky, the vapor cools and condenses into tiny drops. The drops gather into clouds. When the drops grow heavy, they fall as rain or snow. This is called precipitation.

Rain that lands on the ground runs downhill into streams and rivers. This is runoff. Some soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater. Rivers carry water to the ocean, and the cycle begins again.

## 4. How People Use Water

People use water at home, on farms, and in factories. At home we drink, cook, wash, and flush toilets. Farmers use water to grow crops. Factories use water, too.

A person in the United States uses about 80 to 100 gallons of water a day indoors. A large bucket holds about 5 gallons. So that is about 16 to 20 buckets a day. Toilets use the most water. Showers and baths come next.

**Sidebar: Gallons and buckets.** A gallon measures water. Five gallons fill one large bucket. Ten buckets hold fifty gallons.

## 5. Access to Clean Water

Access means being able to reach and use something. People have access to water when they can get enough clean water close to home.

Many communities have a tap or a well nearby. In other places, people walk a long way to reach water. Sometimes they also wait their turn. Walking and waiting take time that could be used for school, work, or play.

Water must also be clean. Water can look clear and still carry germs, and germs in dirty water can make people sick.

Communities improve access. They dig wells, build tanks, lay pipes, and repair pumps.

## 6. Demand for Water

Demand is how much water people want and need. Supply is how much water is available to use.

Demand grows when more people live in a place. A town that builds new homes needs more water for them.

Supply can shrink, too. When little rain falls, rivers and wells may hold less water. If demand is higher than supply, some people may not get enough. That is why communities plan how to share water.

## 7. Pollution

Pollution is something harmful that gets into water. Rain that runs over the ground picks up what it finds. It can carry litter, oil from roads, fertilizer from lawns, and waste from animals and people. The runoff carries these things into streams, lakes, and groundwater.

Polluted water can harm fish, plants, and people. Water for drinking must be cleaned first. A treatment plant is a place where workers clean water.

## 8. Protecting Water

People can protect water in many ways. They can conserve it, which means using less and wasting less. Turning off the tap while brushing teeth saves water. Fixing a leaky pipe helps, too.

People can keep water clean. Litter dropped on a street can wash into a drain and end up in a stream. A sign by a stream and a team that picks up litter both help.

Communities can also build and share. Some build wells, tanks, and dams to keep water for dry days. Others make a plan so that every household gets a turn.

Clean freshwater is a small part of Earth's water, and everyone needs it. People protect it when they conserve it, keep it clean, and share it.

## Words to know

- **access:** being able to reach and use something
- **conserve:** to use less and waste less
- **demand:** how much water people want and need
- **freshwater:** water with very little salt
- **groundwater:** water under the ground
- **pollution:** something harmful in water, air, or soil
- **source:** the place water comes from
- **supply:** the amount of water that is available
- **treatment plant:** a place where workers clean water for drinking
