# Public-domain animal poems: The Frog and The Learned Fish (Hilaire Belloc)

- **title:** The Frog; The Learned Fish
- **text_id:** g3-u2-02
- **author:** Hilaire Belloc (1870 to 1953), from The Bad Child's Book of Beasts (1896)
- **year:** 1896
- **kind:** literary
- **form:** comic animal verse, read aloud
- **word count:** 81 words in all (The Learned Fish 15, The Frog 66); verse, so no usable numeric measure
- **use:** paired, with Pond Voices
- **read on days:** 40, 51

Teacher note: older diction ("epithets", "justly sensitive", "likewise") and a joking voice ask the reader to separate what the speaker says literally from what is meant for fun. Read the poems aloud first. "Polly-wog" is an old name for a tadpole.

---

## The Learned Fish

This learned Fish has not sufficient brains
To go into the water when it rains.

## The Frog

Be kind and tender to the Frog,
And do not call him names,
As 'Slimy skin,' or 'Polly-wog,'
Or likewise 'Ugly James,'
Or 'Gap-a-grin,' or 'Toad-gone-wrong,'
Or 'Bill Bandy-knees':
The Frog is justly sensitive
To epithets like these.

No animal will more repay
A treatment kind and fair;
At least so lonely people say
Who keep a frog (and, by the way,
They are extremely rare).
