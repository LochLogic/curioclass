Teacher note. Read aloud on day 20 after the teacher reads the Bangladesh section of g3-u1-05. It is ours and not about the carriers. The ten note strips (below) are cut apart, sorted into two groups, and each group gets a heading (Built to travel; Built for reading). The paragraph shows a topic sentence, a definition of one word (library boat), link words (First, Second), and a closing sentence that says what the facts show. Possessives are taught on a separate line, not in this text.

## Text

A library boat is a boat that brings books to readers. In the Chalan Beel region of Bangladesh, few roads reach the villages, so boats are the best way to travel all year. First, the boats are built to travel. Their bottoms are flat, so they can cross shallow rivers and flooded land. Second, the boats are built for reading. Readers borrow books, read for pleasure and look up facts in reference books. Solar panels on the roofs make electricity for lights and computers. Library boats bring a library to places a road cannot reach.

Note strips (one fact each, under 40 words):
1. Few roads reach the villages.
2. Boats can travel all year.
3. The bottoms are flat.
4. Flat bottoms cross shallow rivers.
5. Flat bottoms cross flooded land.
6. Readers borrow books.
7. Readers read for pleasure.
8. Readers look up facts in reference books.
9. Solar panels are on the roofs.
10. The solar panels make electricity for lights and computers.

Groups: 1 to 5, Built to travel; 6 to 10, Built for reading. Strips 9 and 10 can be joined with also.
