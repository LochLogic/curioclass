Teacher note: both poems are built to be read aloud and recorded (RF.3.4, SL.3.5). Pond Voices personifies the reeds and the pond; Under the Leaf personifies the leaf and the rain. Each personification has a literal reading (the leaf shades the frog, the rain falls) and a nonliteral one (the leaf keeps a secret, the rain tiptoes). Each poem names a frog's home: a reed bed and pond, and the ground beneath a leaf. Neither poem uses a line from any reserved panel in the unit plan.

## Pond Voices

At dusk the pond is hushed and dim,
The reeds lean close, so still, so slim.
Then one small frog begins to peep
And wakes the water from its sleep.

Another answers, then a third,
Till every reed has found a word.
The quiet pond has found its song,
And all the night will sing along.

---

## Under the Leaf

A broad leaf bends above the ground
And keeps a secret, without a sound.
Beneath it waits a small brown frog,
As still as any mossy log.

The rain comes tiptoeing, tip by tip,
And slides in beads off the leaf's green lip.
The frog sits dry. The frog sits still,
The quietest thing on the whole green hill.

When clouds roll off and skies turn clear,
The frog hops out. The pond is near.
It leaps much farther than before
And lands in the wet beside the shore.
