Books on Horseback

A Library That Rode

Picture a library with no building. Its books ride in saddlebags, on a horse or a mule, along mountain paths. This library was real. It was in Kentucky, a state in the United States, in the 1930s.

A Hard Time

In the 1930s, many Americans had no work. The United States government began a program called the Works Progress Administration, or WPA. It paid people to work on public projects, such as building roads. One of the WPA projects in Kentucky was a library that traveled.

How It Worked

The people who carried the books were called carriers. Most were women. They went to the remote parts of the state by horse and by mule, bringing books to families in the mountains. Once a week, the carriers met at their library headquarters in Hindman, a town in Knott County. There they replenished their saddlebags with books. Then they rode out again. The WPA paid each carrier a small salary.

The Library Grew

By 1939, the state library office and the WPA were running 30 pack horse libraries in Kentucky. The pack horse libraries were one way Kentucky reached readers in country places.
