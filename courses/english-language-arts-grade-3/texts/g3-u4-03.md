# How a Town Cleans Its Water

## Why the water needs cleaning

Think of a town that takes its water from a river. Rain washes dirt, leaves, and tiny bits of soil into that river. The water may also carry germs. Nobody should drink it as it is. So workers at a treatment plant clean the water before it travels through pipes to homes and schools.

The workers follow steps, one after another. Each step does a different job. Most plants use the first four steps below, and then they add a last step to kill any germs that remain. Plants in different towns are not all the same.

## The steps

**Step 1: Coagulation.** First, workers add a small amount of a chemical to the water. This chemical makes the dirt and tiny particles stick to one another. Before this step, the particles stay spread out. After it, they begin to clump.

**Step 2: Flocculation.** Next, the water is stirred slowly and gently. Slow stirring helps the small clumps join into bigger, heavier clumps. These are called flocs, which look like soft flakes in the water.

**Step 3: Sedimentation.** Then the water rests in a large tank. Because the flocs are heavy, they sink to the bottom. The clearer water stays on top and moves on to the next step.

**Step 4: Filtration.** After that, the water flows down through filters made of layers such as sand, gravel, or charcoal. The filters catch the particles that are still left. They also take out germs and smells. The water that comes out is much clearer than the water that went in.

**Diagram: The steps in order.** Four tanks in a row, each labeled with its step number and name. Arrows lead from a river, through the four tanks, to a pipe labeled "to homes."

## Why the order matters

Each step prepares the water for the next one. The coagulation step makes clumps possible. The stirring step makes the clumps big enough to sink. The resting step removes the clumps, so the filters have less to catch. If workers skipped a step, the filters would have much more to catch.

## What I think

I think a treatment plant is one of the most important places in a town, even though few people ever see it. Clean water protects the whole community, so a town should look after its plant, check it often, and thank the people who work there.

## Words to know

- **coagulation:** the step where particles stick together
- **filter:** a layer of material that water passes through to catch particles
- **floc:** a soft clump of particles
- **sedimentation:** the step where heavy clumps sink to the bottom
- **treatment plant:** a place where workers clean water for drinking
