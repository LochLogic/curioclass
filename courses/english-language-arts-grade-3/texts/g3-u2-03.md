## Why the Frog Has a Long Tongue

Long, long ago, in a sparkling blue mountain lake, there lived a young frog named Kikker. His skin was smooth and lime green. His big black eyes had gold rims and bulged just a little. When Kikker was happy, his eyes shut into two straight gold lines, one on each side of his head. Kikker was clever and kind, and he was very, very tiny. He was no bigger than your thumbnail.

Every year the frogs in Kikker's lake held a fly-catching contest, and the frog who caught the most flies won. Kikker loved to watch the long-legged frogs practice. They sprang high into the air, opened their wide mouths and snapped them shut on a juicy fly. This year Kikker was finally old enough to enter. Oh, how he wanted to win!

"I must practice," Kikker thought. Just then a small fly buzzed past. Kikker hopped up as high as he could. He opened his small mouth and snapped it shut. He did not catch a fly. Instead he heard loud croaks of laughter.

"Do you need some stilts, Tiny?" one frog called.

"Maybe a ladder!" called another. "Look at those puny legs!"

Kikker dove deep under the water. His cheeks burned with embarrassment.

All day long Kikker tried to leap and catch flies. All day long he missed. "My little legs will never take me as high as the bigger frogs," he thought sadly. By evening his legs were sore and his mouth was parched from snapping it open and shut. He stretched his tongue out over the lake to take a cool drink.

Then, all at once, Kikker had an idea. It was a very clever idea! He swam to a quiet corner of the lake, still thinking about it. He knew just what he needed to practice now. And practice he did, day after day, working his muscles until he was strong and skilled.

At last the day of the contest came. Long-legged frogs hopped everywhere, leaping and turning in the air. Each frog seemed to jump higher than the one before. Two geese kept count of the flies each frog caught. Who would catch the most?

Over in a quiet corner sat Kikker, hardly moving, every muscle still.

"Did you give up, Kikker?" croaked Old Bullfrog, with a wide smirk on his face. "I don't blame you. You haven't a chance."

Just then Kikker's long, strong tongue shot out of his mouth, wrapped around a startled fly and pulled it in. He did it again, and again, and again. He caught more flies than all the other frogs combined, and he hardly moved a muscle. Of course Kikker won the contest. No frog had ever caught so many flies!

"Hey, Kikker!" called a long-legged frog from across the lake. "That looks much easier than what we are doing. Could you teach us?"

Kikker never held a grudge. He showed the other frogs the curling and rolling exercises that had made his tongue long, strong and flexible. Soon every frog in the lake was resting in the sun and catching flies with a flick of the tongue. And in the far corner of the lake sat Kikker, his eyes shut in two gold lines, with a big grin on his face.
