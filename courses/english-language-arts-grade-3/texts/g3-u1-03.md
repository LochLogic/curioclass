Teacher note. The characters and the library book are invented; the city (Cape Town, South Africa), Table Mountain and its cloud, and the summer south-easter wind are real. Read whole on day 10, reread on 11, used on 34 and 35. What the days need is in the story: the order of events (wind, supper reading, the knotted word, the skipped line, the tired eyes, the bookmark: day 10 recount); who speaks (Ama and Grandmother only, each line tagged: day 10 and 11, distinguish speakers); nonliteral phrases for day 11: the mountain wears a white tablecloth (page 1), a knot of letters (page 3), her laugh is as wide as the sky (page 3), a mountain is climbed one step at a time (page 5), like a little flag (page 6); shared learning (Grandmother and Ama each help the other: page 5); and a passage to rehearse for day 34 and 35 (the bookmark pages). Grandmother's reading is shown with dignity and as effort, never as shame. Do not ask any child about their own family's reading. The exit strips for days 10, 11 and 34 are separate invented practice strips; none is a sentence of this story.

## Text

[page 1]
In Cape Town, South Africa, a strong wind blows in the summer. People call it the south-easter. It pushes the clouds over Table Mountain until the mountain wears a white tablecloth.
Tonight the wind rattles Ama's window. But in the kitchen, the lamp is warm. Ama and Grandmother sit side by side at the table with a library book between them. Its cover is blue.
Picture: A small house at night at the foot of a tall flat-topped mountain wearing a thick white cloud. Trees bend in the wind. A window glows yellow. Inside, a girl and her grandmother sit at a table with a lamp.

[page 2]
Every evening after supper, they read. Ama reads aloud. Grandmother follows the words.
When Grandmother was young, the school was too far away, and her family needed her help at home. Now she is learning to read, a little more each night, at this table, beside Ama.
Picture: Close view of the kitchen table. Ama, about eight, with beaded braids, holds the blue book open. Grandmother, with silver hair and a green headscarf, leans close to read along. Two cups of tea steam beside them.

[page 3]
Ama finishes a page. "Your turn, Grandmother," she says.
Grandmother puts her finger under the first line. She reads slowly. Then she stops. "Ama, this word is a knot of letters."
"Cut it into pieces," says Ama. "Ad. Ven. Ture."
Grandmother says each piece. "Ad. Ven. Ture." Then she says it all together. "Adventure!"
Her laugh is as wide as the sky. Ama laughs too.
Picture: Grandmother's finger rests under a long word in the book. Ama points to the same word with her pencil. Both are smiling. The lamplight turns their faces gold.

[page 4]
Now it is Ama's turn. She reads fast, because she wants to find out what happens. "Mia climbed the hill with her wind caught it and it flew," she reads.
Grandmother tilts her head. "That does not sound right."
Ama stops. She reads the lines again, slowly. She had skipped one. "Mia climbed the hill with her kite. The wind caught it, and it flew."
"Yes!" says Grandmother. "Now I can see the kite."
Ama nods. Reading is not only saying the words. It is checking that they make sense.
Picture: Ama's pencil points at two lines of the book. Her eyebrows are scrunched. Grandmother's hand rests lightly on the page beside hers. Through the window behind them, the wind bends the trees.

[page 5]
After a while Ama puts her chin on her hand. "My eyes are tired," she says. "Reading is hard work."
Grandmother smiles. She looks out the window at the mountain in its white cloth. "A mountain is climbed one step at a time," she says.
"Then we are climbing," says Ama.
"Yes," says Grandmother. "You help me up the hard steps, and I help you up the others."
Picture: Ama rests her chin on her hand. Grandmother points out the window at the mountain, which shines pale in the dark. The lamp makes a warm circle on the table.

[page 6]
Grandmother takes a strip of bright paper from a drawer. She folds it once and slides it into the book. It sticks out like a little flag.
"Tomorrow we begin here," she says.
Ama closes the book, and the blue cover feels warm under her hands.
Picture: Grandmother slides a bright orange strip of paper into the closed book. Ama watches. The strip sticks out of the top like a little flag. Ama is smiling.

[page 7]
The wind still rattles the window, but the house feels quiet. Ama carries the lamp to the bedroom and Grandmother carries the book. Two readers, one lamp.
Tomorrow, the mountain will still wear its cloth. And tomorrow, the two of them will climb another page.
Picture: A hallway with a lamp swinging gently in Ama's hand. Grandmother walks behind her, holding the blue book to her chest. Their shadows stretch long on the wall.
