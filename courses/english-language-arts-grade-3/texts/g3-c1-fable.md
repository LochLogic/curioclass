Teacher note. Cold fable for RL.3.1 to RL.3.4, L.3.5a and RL.3.7. Message: look and try before you say no; help can come from a book. Details that carry it: Tobin calls books dull, the gate sticks, Tilly's book shows the latch trick, Tobin takes a book at the end. Nonliteral phrases: "His cheeks burned" (RL.3.4, meaning embarrassed) and "turned over a new leaf" (L.3.5a, a page in a book against starting fresh; neither phrase is taught). # The Goat Who Would Not Look

A box of books stood by the village well. Each morning, the children took one home.

Tobin the goat turned his head away. "Books are dull," he said. "I do not need them."

One day, the gate to the green hill stuck. Tobin pushed and pushed. It did not move.

Tilly the hen sat nearby with a book. "This book shows how a latch works," she said. "Slide a stick under it."

Tobin stood very still. Then he slid a stick under the latch. The gate swung open.

That evening, Tobin took a book from the box. His cheeks burned. "I will look first next time," he said. He had turned over a new leaf.
