## Text

Before Booker Learned to Read

Booker T. Washington was born in 1856 on a small tobacco farm in Virginia. In those years, many people in the southern United States were enslaved. Enslaved people were forced to work without pay, and they could not leave. In Virginia, it was against the law to teach enslaved people to read and write.

So Booker could not go to school. He once carried the books of a child from the family who held him to a schoolhouse, but he was not allowed to go inside.

In April 1865, Booker heard the Emancipation Proclamation read at the farm. Emancipation means being set free. After that, Booker and his family were free. Booker was nine years old.

Freedom changed what was possible. Booker's family walked hundreds of miles, from Virginia to West Virginia, to join his stepfather. Later, Booker wrote that new schools opened and people of every age wanted to learn. Even grandparents went to night school.

This is the world that Booker was in when he began to learn to read.
