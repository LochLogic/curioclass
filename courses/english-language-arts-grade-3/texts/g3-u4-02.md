# Water Paths in Kitui County

## Where is Kitui County?

Kitui County is in eastern Kenya, in Africa, about 150 kilometres east of the capital, Nairobi. Much of the land is dry. Rain comes in seasons, and many rivers hold water only after a storm.

## What a July 2025 report found

In July 2025, a team from Kenya's drought authority and its partners wrote a report about Kitui County. The long rains that year had been poor. The team asked how far people walked to reach water and what the trip was like.

Their answer was not one number. It depended on the area. The report grouped the land into zones, and two of them are shown below. A **round trip** is the walk to the water and the walk back home.

| Zone | Round trip to water (July 2025) | Usual round trip at that time of year | Waiting at the water point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marginal Mixed Farming | 5 to 7 kilometres | 3 to 5 kilometres | 40 to 50 minutes |
| Mixed Farming | 2 to 4 kilometres | 2 to 3 kilometres | 30 to 40 minutes |

These are averages for whole zones, measured at one time. They do not tell us how far one child walks today.

## More than distance

Distance is only part of the story. The report said that some rivers and ponds had started to dry up after the poor rains. Some boreholes, which are deep wells with pumps, had broken down. So more people used the sources that still worked, and waiting times grew.

Water also costs money in some places. At a water point, a 20-litre jerrycan cost 3 to 5 Kenyan shillings. A 20-litre jerrycan holds about as much as one large bucket.

## Water that stays

People in Kitui County are not waiting for rain to solve the problem. Many communities build sand dams. A sand dam is a low wall built across a seasonal river. When the rains come, the river carries sand and water, and the sand piles up behind the wall. The water sinks into the sand and stays underground. People reach it through a well.

One group, Kyeni Kya Wasya, in Kivou ward, built its first sand dam on the Kwa Isomba stream in 2021, with a well. They built a second on the Kangoo river in 2023. The group gave time, work, and money worth more than 400,000 Kenyan shillings. Today the group sells water to its neighbours, and it plans a water kiosk and a model farm.

## What this account shows

I believe this account shows two things. First, how far people walk matters, but so do waiting, broken equipment, and cost. Second, local work can change a water path. A sand dam turns a short rain into water for the dry weeks that follow.

Having water close by is not the same as knowing it is safe to drink. Dirty water can carry germs that make people sick. Water must be both close and clean.

## Words to know

- **borehole:** a deep, narrow well with a pump
- **round trip:** the walk to a place and the walk back
- **sand dam:** a low wall across a seasonal river that holds sand, and water in the sand
- **zone:** an area that shares some features, such as how people farm
