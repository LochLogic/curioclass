# Day 15: A photograph, a map and a main idea

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print Books on Horseback with Photograph 1, its caption and the Kentucky map, one for each pair. Draw the park map on the board: title "Pine Park", a key with a tree for a picnic spot, a square for a toilet block, a wave for the pond. Sort the day 14 work (s01-P14-1, s01-P14-2, s01-X14v2) for hand-back. Make the conference list of three children.

### The day

#### 10 min. Opening

*3 min. Repair and hand-back.* Repair the day 14 exit. Error A is a fact the section does not give ("The books come in boxes"). Error B is a topic given as the reason ("It is about mail"). Show a new strip: "The school van stops by the gate each Monday. A flag marks the stop." Ask: "When does the van stop?" Children point to the words that say, then answer: "Each Monday." Hand back the day 14 sorts, chart rows and exits with one comment each.

*7 min. Map title and key.* Review: you used a map title and key in grade 3, on day 1. Cover nothing. Ask: "What is the title? What does the tree mean? Where is the pond?" Children answer on slates. Expect: read the title first, then the key, then find the mark. Unscored.

#### 20 min. Skills

*8 min. Word check.* Say each word once and children write it on a slate: rebuild, dislike, unkind, replay, unfair, untie, disappear. Review: you learned un-, re- and dis- in grade 2. Then say three of them and ask what the prefix adds: re- in replay (again), un- in unkind (not), dis- in dislike (the opposite of). Read the base first, then add the prefix. Mark the children who miss two or more on the record sheet; they join a conference group. Regroup by error and reteach the missed pattern at the next skills block.

*12 min. Reading conferences.* Three different children from the list, 4 minutes each: one page read aloud (1 minute), one question answered from the words (1 minute 30 seconds), one next step in the log (1 minute 30 seconds). Write them in the heard-six column. The rest read from their logs. If a child guesses a word from its first letters (error P), say the chunks, blend, and check the sense.

#### 20 min. Model

*2 min. Read.* Say first: Kentucky is a state in the United States, and this happened in the 1930s. Read Books on Horseback aloud, about 231 words, children following with a finger. Name the hard time in one plain sentence: "In the 1930s, many Americans had no work." Say nothing more about it.

*16 min. The chart, row by row.* Review: in grade 2 you found a main idea and its details. New today: the chart. Put the chart on the board: section, topic, main idea, two details. Do How It Worked.

Review: in grade 2 you used a caption and a photograph together. First the photograph and its caption. Read [[s01-K15-1]] and show the steps with [[steps:s01-K15-1]]. Say: "A caption does not prove more than it says." Ask: "Does the caption say the carriers were happy?" Expect: no. Then the map. Read [[s01-K15-2]] and show the steps with [[steps:s01-K15-2]]. Say: "A map gives the place. Read the title, then the key."

Then the idea. Say the topic in a word or two: the carriers. Say the main idea as a whole sentence: carriers brought books to families far away. Use [[s01-K15-3]] and [[steps:s01-K15-3]] to choose two details that show it. Write them in the last column. Ask: "Which detail came from the words? Which came from the caption?" Link them with so.

#### 25 min. Hand work

*15 min. The chart.* Pairs fill their chart for A Library That Rode and A Hard Time with [[s01-P15-1]]. The main idea cells are filled on the board for them. Walk and look. If a pair writes a topic as a detail, ask: "Does that say something the section tells?"

*10 min. Main idea.* Each child judges two main ideas for the whole article with [[s01-P15-2]], picks the better card and says why. If a child picks the topic card (error B), ask: "Does it say what the whole article shows?" Judged after class with the look-fors in the item. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen on the record sheet.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs sit together. Each child takes 2 minutes to say the main idea and two details (4 minutes in all). Then 3 minutes for the partner to ask for a detail ("Which part tells that?"). Then 3 minutes: hear three volunteers at 1 minute each. Review: you used discussion moves in grade 3, on day 1. Ask: "What do the words, the photograph and the map each tell?" Anyone may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read The Library Grew aloud twice and point to the Kentucky map. Children write alone: [[s01-X15v2]]. Unscored. Shown: one whole sentence that fits the words and the map. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What do the words and the map each tell?" Not yet seen: only that it is about horses, or a claim the map does not support.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the caption and draw the map by hand on the board. No projector: keep the photograph on paper. No pairs: the adult is the partner for the chart and the talk.

### At home

**Swap.** No article: any short article with a picture and its caption.
**Skip.** The conferences and the talk.

- **10 min. Read.** Read the caption and the first section of Books on Horseback aloud.
  **Say.** "What does the photograph show?" Expect: only what the caption supports.
- **15 min. Read.** Read any book together.
- **5 min. Write.** Add a line to your reading log.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s01-X15v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the chart and the main idea; cut the word check to four words. Longer: a child writes what the map and the photograph each tell about Hindman, and one thing neither tells.

### Words and supports

Photograph, caption, map, key, headquarters, remote. Frame: "The main idea is ___. The details are ___ and ___." Describe the photograph aloud for a child who cannot see it, never the answer. Large print; a scribe for the chart, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 16 opens with 3 minutes on this exit. Error B is a topic given as the main idea ("It is about horses"): join two details to one complete claim with so. The day 15 exit goes back with one comment. Day 16 is a writing day; the children write a first paragraph from the two articles.
