# Section 1.6 opener: Booker T. Washington learns to read (days 26 to 29)

## What the section does

Children read a short context text and a retelling of how Booker T. Washington learned to read, with a few lines from his own book. They tell his own words from the retelling, work out what a new word means from its sentence, from a word part, from a root or from a dictionary entry, and tell a phrase meant literally from one meant another way. On day 28 they read a fresh panel and write an explanation. On day 29 they choose a reading strategy and write a goal.

## How the days run

Each day is 90 minutes. Day 26 opens with an eight-minute context bridge: enslaved people, freedom, and why a school was possible for him after 1865. Day 27 marks first-person lines. Day 28 holds no teaching. Day 29 repairs the explanation and sets a goal grounded in two records of the child's own work.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **The context comes first.** The bridge names slavery and freedom plainly and stays on the boy. It does not ask what slavery was like, and it does not ask for family stories.
- **An account is the person's own telling.** In this unit it means his own words, not a story told by someone else.
- **A word's meaning comes from its sentence.** A clue in the sentence, a word part or a root can help; a dictionary entry confirms.
- **A phrase can mean something other than its words.** Devour a book means read it eagerly.
- **A goal needs a move.** A goal and a move come from the child's own records.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A, B, W:** a fact the text does not give; a topic given as the point; the event not explained.
- **H, I, J:** facts not grouped, a link joining unrelated ideas, a closing on another topic.
- **AC, P:** a word part read the wrong way; a word guessed from its first letters.
- **X:** reading that races past the end marks.

## Marking

Same scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Day 28 is a check day, scored from the panel only. The cap panel is kept out of all teaching.
