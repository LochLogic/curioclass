# Reading group lines (day 28)

Five cards, one for each child in a group of five. Each is two sentences from the retelling, about 30 words. Each child reads the card twice; the group gives one comment on phrasing. Unscored. Day 35 supplies the unit fluency record.

1. Booker T. Washington was born enslaved in Virginia in 1856. Enslaved people were forced to work without pay, and they were not free to leave.
2. In April 1865, when Booker was a boy, he and the other enslaved people on his farm heard that they were free. Being set free from slavery is called emancipation.
3. After freedom, Booker's family moved to Malden, a town in West Virginia, where his stepfather had a job at a salt furnace. Booker went to work there too, while he was still a child.
4. He tried every way he could think of, and in a few weeks he knew most of the letters. When a school opened for children in Malden, Booker's stepfather still needed him at work.
5. Booker did not give up. He found a teacher for lessons in the evening, after the furnace work was done.

The sentences are the text of the retelling, word for word. A child who cannot read a card may echo it after the group.
