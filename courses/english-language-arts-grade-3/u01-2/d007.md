# Day 7: Words that are not meant exactly

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut the phrase cards and add two slips: "wraps the first book in a bag" and "white sheep graze between the walls". Print the dictionary page, one for each pair. Write on the board: "Phrase / What it means / The clue." Write the two sentence cards: "Ran up the steep hill." and "Sam climbed the hill he was tired he stopped." Find the day 7 line of exit cards 5 to 9. Sort the day 6 maps and lines and write a comment on each.

### The day

#### 10 min. Opening

Three minutes repair day 6's exit, and hand back the day 6 maps and two lines (s01-P6-1, s01-P6-2) with one comment each. If many children missed it, use the letter you saw most.

If error C (the action and result reversed): read a new strip: "The rain started. Mia put on her hood." Number the two events, draw an arrow from first to second and say: "The rain started, so Mia put on her hood." Children say it the wrong way round once and hear why it fails.

If error B (a trait with no link to the events): ask "What did the person do?" and add it to the sentence.

Then seven minutes on slates, unscored. Review: in grade 2 you read to the comma and used the sentence around a word as a clue. New today: words that mean about the same. (1) Write "After supper, Dad read us a story." Children read it aloud and pause at the comma. (2) Children write two words that mean about the same as small. (3) Write "The soup was scalding, so Lena blew on it." Children write what scalding means and underline the clue.

#### 20 min. Skills

*10 min. Partner rereading.* Review: in grade 2 you reread with a partner for expression; the feedback card is new in grade 3: accurate, in phrases, expressive. Pairs reread pages 3 and 4 (about 140 words, 1.2 minutes a pass). Each child reads twice and the partner ticks one box and says one thing that went well and one next try. Never say speed. Listen to two pairs, 3 minutes each, and write them in the heard-six column. In the four minutes left, hear the seventh oral sample from day 5 (1.5 minutes): an unseen passage, "Read it once, then tell me what happened", no word read to the child. A placement record, no grade.

*10 min. Sentences.* Review: in grade 1 you checked that a sentence tells who and what happened. Show the two sentence cards. Children fix "Ran up the steep hill." by adding who: "Sam ran up the steep hill." Then fix the run-on by splitting it into three sentences or joining with and and so. Pairs write both on slates.

#### 20 min. Model

Say: "Some words say exactly what they mean, and some are meant another way. The first kind is literal. The second is nonliteral." Put up the context and figurative chart on the board: Phrase, What it means, The clue.

Take the first phrase from page 3: legs like jelly (10 minutes in all for two phrases). Say the words exactly: "His legs are jelly." Ask whether legs can be jelly. Say what it means: tired and wobbly. Mark the clue: the long steep climb in the sentences before it. Show the steps with [[steps:s01-K7-1]]. Ask first and let children answer before you reveal each step. Then the second phrase, clouds like gray wool hats: say it exactly, say what it means (low, round clouds sitting on the hilltops), and mark the clue: the clouds sit on the hilltops. Leave the other two phrases for the pairs.

**Ask.** How do you know it is meant another way? Expect: legs cannot be jelly; the sentence about the climb says why.

Then the care family (5 minutes). Review: in grade 2 you used care in careful as a clue. Write care, careful, careless. Read careful from page 3 of the boat story ("Careful!" Lia calls). Say: "The ending -less takes the care away. Careless means without care." Test it in a sentence: "The careless cook left the gate open." Mark the clue: the gate was left open. Error F is "careless means with care"; test the meaning in the sentence and mark the clue.

Then one dictionary entry (3 minutes). Review: in grade 2 you looked a word up on a beginning dictionary page. Hold up the page and read the entry pack, which has two senses. Read the sentence "He zips the book in his pack." and choose the sense that fits: a bag carried on the back. Say: "Find the word, read every sense, choose the one that fits." Error G is a guess with no look-up.

Last, two minutes on a picture beside its words. Show the page 7 picture (sunlight on the walls). Read the words, "The hill is still steep, but it feels shorter." Fill one row: The words say ___. The picture shows ___. Say: the sunlight and breaking clouds add a lighter mood.

#### 25 min. Hand work

Pairs work at once. First 12 minutes: pairs sort the six cards into meant exactly and meant another way with [[s01-P7-1]], then say what the two new phrases mean and mark the clue in each: [[s01-P7-2]] and [[s01-P7-3]]. The clue strips on the back of each card are for checking. If a child says "A mountain grew" or reads a phrase as a real event (error E), put the literal and intended meanings side by side.

Then 5 minutes: each pair is given one word and its sentence: lane ("The lane drops, and then it climbs."), steep ("The hill is steep."), wall ("Sam stops beside a stone wall.") or pack ("Mina will pack her bag tonight."). Everyone looks up their own word at the same time with [[s01-P7-4]], writes the sense that fits, and checks the spelling against the entry.

Then 8 minutes: pairs compare the page 3 picture with its words in a words and picture table, with [[s01-P7-5]]. If a pair only names a pictured thing, such as a road (error D), ask what it adds to the words. Judged by you with the look-fors on each row; mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen on the record sheet.

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: in grade 2 you used the four-step chart (turn, pair, link, clarify); in grade 3 the chart adds a fifth move. Pairs talk all at once. Each child takes 2 minutes on one phrase and what its picture adds (4 minutes in all). Then the partner asks for a detail, 3 minutes. Anyone may pass. Then three volunteers share one sentence each, 1 minute each (3 minutes). Hear two pairs for your heard six.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read the day 7 card aloud twice and show its picture. Children write alone: [[s01-X7]]. Unscored. Shown: the phrase is not a real distance, and the picture adds something the words do not say. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What does the picture show that the words do not say?" Not yet seen: a real distance, or a pictured thing with no job.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the four phrases on the board and read the dictionary entry aloud. No projector: keep the chart on the board. No pairs: the adult is the partner.

### At home

**Swap.** No dictionary page: use any dictionary, or read a word's meaning aloud.
**Skip.** The oral sample, the sentence cards and the talk with a partner.

- **10 min. Read.** Read a page of any book aloud.
  **Say.** "Is there a phrase here that is not meant exactly?" Expect: a phrase with like, such as like a drum.
- **10 min. Phrases.** Answer [[s01-P7-2]] and [[s01-P7-3]].
- **10 min. Dictionary.** Look up one word in any dictionary, as in [[s01-P7-4]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s01-X7]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the care family to the word careless and keep the phrases, one look-up, the picture row and the exit. Longer: a child finds one more phrase in the boat story and sorts it.

### Words and supports

Literal, nonliteral, clue, entry, sense. Frames: "It means ___. The clue is ___." "The words say ___. The picture shows ___." Preview steep, lane, wall and pack with a picture for each. Large print; a spoken description of each picture for a child who cannot see it; a scribe for the sentences, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 8 opens with three minutes on this exit. Error D is a pictured thing named with no job ("The picture has children in a line"). Error E is a phrase read as a real event ("The line was a mile long, so she walked a mile"). Day 8 asks children to write a response to the bus story.
