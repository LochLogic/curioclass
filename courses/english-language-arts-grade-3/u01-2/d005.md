# Day 5: What Lia's actions show

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut the three back-words cards. Make one story map sheet for the board and one for each pair: fold in thirds; boxes for Who, Want, Where and when; a row of Event boxes joined by arrows; one wide Message box. Find the day 5 line of exit cards 5 to 9. List the seven children whose oral sample waits and your four conference children.

### The day

#### 10 min. Opening

Three minutes repair day 4's exit. If many children missed it, use error AD (a book picked by its cover or name, with no question). Read three new back-words cards aloud: "This book explains how clouds make rain." "A story about a fox who learns to swim." "How to grow tomatoes in pots." Say a question first: "I want to find out how a fox learns to swim." Children point to the card whose words match and read the words that told them. Do this once more with "How do clouds make rain?" Then write your own question for the third card.

Then seven minutes. Review: in grade 2 you said how a character responds: what the character wants and what the character does. Read two practice strips aloud, each made up for practice. "Pia's puppy hid under the bed. Pia lay on the floor and held out a treat." **Ask.** What does Pia want, and what does she do? Expect: she wants the puppy to come out; she lies down and holds out a treat. Then: "Raj's pot was empty. He asked the cook for a spoon and stirred." Pairs say what Raj wants and does. Unscored.

#### 20 min. Skills

*10 min. Oral samples.* Seven of your 30 children still wait for the oral sample from day 3. At 1.5 minutes each that is 10.5 minutes, and this slot has 10, so hear six now (9 minutes, one minute to change children) and hear the seventh on day 7. Use the same routine as day 3: an unseen passage, "Read it once, then tell me what happened", no word read to the child, a prompted reading recorded with its prompt. The rest read from their logs. A placement record, no grade.

*10 min. Words.* Review: in grade 2 you divided a two-syllable word between two middle consonants (picnic, paper) and mapped a tricky word before reading it. Bus-story words come first. Write village. Divide it between the two l letters: vil-lage. Say vil (a closed syllable), then lage, where age says /ij/: map it and underline age. Write valley: val-ley. Say val, then ley, where ey says /ee/. Children say each chunk, blend the word and say a sentence. Then children read the word, never guess it from its first letters (error P). Pin village and valley on the sound and spelling wall under water. Repair for error P happens in the conference slot, not here.

#### 20 min. Model

Review: in grade 2 you worked out the lesson of a fable from what happened in it. Today you do the same with a story: the message is what the actions and results show, and the topic is only what it is about. Say: "The topic of this story is a boat library. That is not its message."

Read the whole story aloud (502 words, about 4 minutes), showing each picture. Say first: "This story is made up. Only the city and the river are real." Say plainly that in Manaus, in Brazil, the river rises in the rainy months and covers some paths, and people lay boards and ride boats. Anyone may step out and come back.

Then build the story map on the board for pages 3 to 7 (16 minutes). Say: "A map has boxes for who, what they want, and the events." Fill them as you say them:

- **Who:** Lia.
- **Want:** to reach the boat and borrow something new (page 2: "I want to be ready").
- **Where and when:** Manaus, in the rainy months; water covers the path.
- **Events,** each in a box with an arrow: water covers the path and neighbors lay boards; Lia notices a loose board; she kneels and holds it still; her neighbors cross one by one; she reaches the boat and Dona Ivone says "Right on time"; on the way home the board stays still.

Say: "Her want is her motivation: the reason she acts." Write motivation beside Want. Then show the answer to the day's question with [[steps:s01-K5-1]]. Ask first and let children answer before you reveal each step.

**Ask.** What did Lia do on page 3, and what happened because of it? Expect: she noticed a loose board and held it still, so her neighbors could cross.

Then write the message in the wide box, from the events: "Lia saw a problem and fixed it, so everyone could cross." Say the link: "The events show that one person who notices a problem can make the way across for others." Cross out the wrong way once, about a book, not a child: "The story is about boats" names the topic, and the boxes do not say it.

#### 25 min. Hand work

Pairs work at once, each with their own sheet. First 15 minutes: pairs fill their own copy of the map for page 5, where Lia and Cami choose a book: Who, Want, three Event boxes and arrows. Use [[s01-P5-1]]. Walk and look at the Want and Event boxes. If a pair writes a fact the page does not give (error A), put a finger on the sentence and ask where it is.

Then 10 minutes: each child writes one message sentence with so for pages 3 and 5 together, using [[s01-P5-2]], and points to the sentence it came from. If a child writes only the topic, "It is about boats" (error B), ask: "What did Lia do, and what happened next?"

#### 10 min. Talk

Review: in grade 2 you used the four-step chart (turn, pair, link, clarify); in grade 3 the chart adds a fifth move. Pairs sit together, all talking at once. Each child takes 2 minutes to give their message sentence and the sentence it came from (4 minutes in all). Then the partner asks for a detail and links back, "You said ___; I would add ___," for 3 minutes. Anyone may pass. Then three volunteers share one sentence each, 1 minute each (3 minutes). Walk to your heard six. The maps and sentences go back to children on day 6.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read the day 5 card aloud twice. Children write one sentence alone: [[s01-X5]]. Unscored. Shown: what the action shows, with an event as proof, joined by so. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What did Lia do, and what happened because of it?" Not yet seen: only what the strip is about, or the result and the cause reversed.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the map boxes on scrap paper. No projector: build the map on the board and keep the exit card on paper. No pairs: the adult is the partner.

### At home

**Swap.** No story pages: use any story with a character who solves a problem.
**Skip.** The oral samples, the conference groups and the talk with a partner.

- **10 min. Read.** Read pages 3 and 4 of the boat story aloud to an adult.
  **Say.** "What did Lia do, and what happened because of it?" Expect: she held the loose board still, so the neighbors could cross.
- **15 min. Map.** Answer [[s01-P5-1]] on a sheet folded in thirds, then write one sentence for [[s01-P5-2]].
- **10 min. Read.** Read any book together; say one thing a character did.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s01-X5]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the oral samples to the six at 1.5 minutes and keep the map, the message sentence and the exit. Longer: a child maps page 7 and says what the last board shows about the way home.

### Words and supports

Message, motivation, event, topic. Frames: "Lia ___, so ___." "The events show ___." Preview river, boat, library, borrow, bus and hill before reading, with a picture for each. Large print; a spoken description of each picture for a child who cannot see it; a scribe for the sentences, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 6 opens with three minutes on this exit. Error B is a topic given as the message ("The strip is about books"). Error C is the cause and result reversed ("Lia held half, so her friend had too many books"). Day 6 reads the book-bus story whole and asks what Sam wants and does.
