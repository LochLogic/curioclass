# Day 48: Revise the problem and the response with speech and a comparison

## Teacher page

### Before class

Return the Kikker sentences from day 47 with a short note on each. Print the student page and draft pages for each child. Find the day 48 line of the exit cards. Take the day 45 word check cards and list the ten children not heard on days 45 and 46; eight are heard today and two on day 51. Mark three names on the heard-six column for the spoken exit ask.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*3 min.* Return and repair, on day 47's exit (s02-X47), error S (a line with no reason) and error Y (the friend and the call in the wrong order). Two openings on the board; children say which one tells where and why, [[s02-P48-1]]. *7 min.* Week 10: children write permission, invention and correction on slates, [[s02-P48-2]]. Review: -tion and -sion (day 41). Review: an irregular word has a part that does not sound the way it looks (day 40). Children mark that part of other, [[s02-P48-3]].

#### 20 min. Independent reading

Everyone reads from their logs, no teaching. The teacher hears eight of the ten children not yet heard for the word check begun on day 45, one at a time, about 2 minutes 15 seconds each with the changeover, no hint; the day 45 cards are used as printed, and the ending, the whole word and the sentence are scored apart on the record sheet. That is 18 minutes. Two children remain, heard on day 51. The last 2 minutes: each child adds one log line. If a child guesses a word from its picture (error J), say: blend it, check it in the sentence, reread.

#### 15 min. Reading and reread

*3 min.* Review: a word chosen for effect, such as smirk for Old Bullfrog (day 41). Children write one word for how Kikker looks, [[s02-P48-4]]. *7 min.* Review: hard-part reread (day 40). Pairs reread the lines about Drab, Ash and Grey in Why the Poison Dart Frog Is So Colorful and point to the words that change. **Ask.** "How does tiny change across the three frogs?" Expect: Drab was tiny, Ash was tinier and Grey was the tiniest, [[s02-K48-1]]. *5 min.* The class names the three words and says what each ending tells: how small each frog is next to the others.

#### 10 min. Word and sentence work

*5 min.* Review: -er and -est on a known base (grade 1): -er for two, -est for three or more. On the board: tiny, tinier, tiniest (y changes to i); bright, brighter, brightest. Review: an adjective tells about a thing and an adverb tells how an action is done (grade 2). New: a long adverb takes more and most: quickly, more quickly, most quickly. Say: ask what the word tells about, a thing or an action, then ask how many are compared. *5 min.* Children fill a three-row table on slates, [[s02-P48-5]], then mark the wrong word in [[s02-P48-6]] and choose the word that fits in [[s02-P48-7]] on the student page. Error N (a comparison with the wrong form) is watched here.

#### 10 min. Talk

Pairs, 2 minutes a child: read the problem in your tale; the partner says what the response shows, [[s02-P48-8]]. Then three volunteers read their problem and the class says what the response shows, 2 minutes each. Any child may pass.

#### 20 min. Writing

Each child revises the problem and the response in the draft: one line a character says, [[s02-P48-9]], and one description with a comparison word, [[s02-P48-10]]. Review: quotation marks with the comma inside (day 44). Watch for error U (speech with no marks or no comma): mark the spoken words first, then the marks and the comma. Watch for error P (an event with nothing said or felt): ask what the character says or feels. The draft stays in the folder with a dated note.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hand out the day 48 card and read it aloud twice; children write alone, [[s02-X48]]. While they start, hear three children from the heard-six column, one at a time, 45 seconds each, [[s02-X48b]]; read the same card to each. Unscored. Shown: a revised problem line that tells precisely how the two moved, with more and a form that fits, and a spoken line with its marks named. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "Two frogs, so which word?" Not yet seen: error N (a form for three used for two, or an invented form) or error U (no marks named).

### Other rooms

One child: the adult hears the word check and is the partner for the reread and the talk. No slate: write the table on paper. No printer: copy the exit card onto the board. Record any help.

### At home

**Swap.** No slate: write the words on paper. No tale copy: the child retells the tale from memory.
**Skip.** The word check and the partner talk.

- **10 min. Read.** Your child reads the revised problem aloud.
  **Say.** "What does the response show?" Expect: what the character does or feels, in the child's words.
- **20 min. Write.** Your child adds one line a character says, [[s02-P48-9]], and one description with a comparison word, [[s02-P48-10]]. Then the child does [[s02-P48-6]] and [[s02-P48-7]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s02-X48]] alone, then answers [[s02-X48b]] aloud.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut the volunteers; keep the revision and the exit. Longer: a child writes a comparison of three frogs in the tale.

### Words and supports

Compare, tiny, tinier, tiniest, more, most. Frame, printed on the student page: Of two, use -er or more. Of three or more, use -est or most. Large print; a scribe for ideas, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 49 opens with 3 minutes on this exit, [[s02-X48]]. Error N (a form for three used for two, or an invented form): match the comparison pattern to what is compared, then say it aloud. Error U on the line of speech is repaired in the margin. Day 49 revises the resolution and the time links, then tries compound and complex sentences. The draft pages stay in the folder.
