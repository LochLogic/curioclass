# Day 69: Type an introduction line and draft the introduction

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child. Write the typing list of thirty names in turn order: ten for the first 20 minutes, twenty for the writing part. Set up the device and the local page. Keep the typing card face down; no child sees it before their turn. Tally the day 68 strips and find the commonest letter.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*3 min.* Hand back the day 68 strips, s02-X68, s02-X68b, s02-X68c and the typed line, s02-X68d. Repair error AA (related facts left apart): sort six new facts into a body pile and a home pile, [[s02-P69-11]]. *7 min.* Week 14. Review: -ation on a base that loses its e (day 67). Write observation and protection, [[s02-P69-1]]. Then read a mixed list with -ment and -ous and name each base, [[s02-P69-2]]: argue loses its e before -ment.

#### 20 min. Rereading and grammar (device turns 1 to 10)

*5 min.* Grammar retrieval on slates. Review: the simple tenses (day 45). Write the past of climbs, [[s02-P69-3]]. *5 min.* Pairs reread the first paragraph of page 10, pausing at each full stop, [[s02-P69-4]]; no teacher listens today. *10 min.* At the one device, ten children take their one-minute turn in list order, the exit turn for E69. The teacher shows the card at the turn and records each child's typed line; the child presses every key. The other children keep rereading or reading from their logs. Watch for error AD: a child who watches has not typed.

#### 15 min. Reading and reread

*3 min.* Retrieve a topic sentence on slates: which sentence promises two groups, [[s02-P69-5]]. *2 min.* Read the Description paragraphs of the excerpt aloud (about 105 words) and the start of page 10. Do not define dorsal, reticulation or riverine. **Ask.** "Which groups does the introduction promise?" *6 min.* Hard-part reread, in pairs: point to the promise in the model topic sentence from day 68, [[s02-P69-6]]. Expect: body and home, in "the pond frog's body and its home." *4 min.* Class share.

#### 10 min. Word and sentence work

*4 min.* Show two introductions for the pond frog, one with the body facts together and the home facts together, and one with the groups scattered, [[s02-K69-1]]. Review: you used an introduction in unit 1 and a topic sentence on day 56. A new idea: the topic sentence promises two groups, and the next sentences keep each group together. *6 min.* Each child writes their essay introduction as three sentences on a slate to check, [[s02-P69-7]]: a topic sentence, a body sentence and a home sentence.

#### 10 min. Talk

*4 min.* Pairs, 2 minutes a child, [[s02-P69-8]]: read your introduction aloud; the partner says the two groups it promises. *6 min.* Three volunteers, 2 minutes each, read their introduction. Anyone may pass.

#### 20 min. Writing (device turns 11 to 30)

At the device, twenty children take their one-minute turn in list order, the exit turn for E69. Everyone else writes the essay introduction on paper from the three-box plan and marks the topic sentence and the two promised groups, [[s02-P69-9]]. A child waiting for a turn keeps writing. Watch for error AA: facts scattered between the groups.

#### 5 min. Exit

E69 was taken in each child's turn, [[s02-X69]]: type one line that names the frog and both details, then save it. Shown: a saved line that the child typed and that names the frog and both details. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What are the two details?" Not yet seen: one detail only (error AA) or a line watched, not typed (error AD). Now save the thirty lines and list them on the shared page. Children check that the paper introduction names the same two groups as their typed line, [[s02-P69-10]]. This check is not scored.

### Other rooms

One child: the adult sits beside the device and does not press keys. No device: each child writes the card line by hand on a card in a turn; record it as a paper stand-in, keyboarding not yet seen. No printer: write the student page items on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No log book: any animal book.
**Skip.** The typing, the partner reading and the slate sentences.

- **10 min. Read.** Your child reads the first paragraph of page 10 aloud.
  **Say.** "Which two groups does an introduction promise?" Expect: body and home.
- **20 min. Write.** Your child writes the essay introduction on paper and marks the topic sentence and the two groups, [[s02-P69-9]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child reads the introduction to you, [[s02-X69]]; you say the two groups you heard.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the typing turns, the paper introduction and the check; record the talk as not reached. Longer: write a second introduction that promises two different groups.

### Words and supports

Introduction, topic sentence, group, promise, type, save, observation, protection. Frame: "This page explains how the ___ and the ___ work together." Large print; a scribe for the introduction; the card read aloud once at the turn; record any help.

It plans linked body paragraphs and holds the first formal discussion in six groups of five, on the details that explain how the glass frog survives. The paper introduction stays in the folder, and the typed line stays on the shared page.
