# Day 68: Plan a three-paragraph page and type one line

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page and a three-box plan for each child. Tally the day 67 strips and find the commonest letter. Set up the one device with the local page, no account and no internet, and write the typing list: ten names. Set the model plan for the pond frog on the board. Pick the five names for today's practice clips.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*3 min.* Hand back the day 67 strips, s02-X67, s02-X67b and s02-X67c. Repair error X (a note in the wrong category): body is a part, habitat is a place, survival is how it stays safe. Sort three new notes by what each fact tells, [[s02-P68-11]]. *7 min.* Week 14. Review: introduce and conclude are bases you know. Spell introduction and conclusion, and mark the base in each, [[s02-P68-1]]. Conclusion ends in -sion, which also says /zhun/ (grade 2). Then map the irregular word different, [[s02-P68-2]].

#### 20 min. Independent reading

Five children make their practice clips of the poem with its display, 3 minutes each, one after another, [[s02-P68-3]]. Review: you recorded a poem on day 49. Each child listens back and names one thing to fix. The rest read from their logs. The last 5 minutes: each child writes a dated line, [[s02-P68-4]]. No conferences today.

#### 15 min. Reading and reread

*3 min.* Retrieve a paragraph group on slates: which two facts go together, [[s02-P68-5]]. *3 min.* Read page 17, Putting It Together, aloud, naming its three parts: the opening (the lines before the pond frog), the body (the four frogs) and the close (the last lines). **Ask.** "How does the model group body and habitat?" *6 min.* Hard-part reread, in pairs: find the sentence that promises two groups, and the sentence where a paragraph moves from home to body, [[s02-P68-6]]. Expect: "Each has a home, and each has a body that fits it," and "A pond frog has strong back legs" after the pond sentence. *3 min.* Class share.

#### 10 min. Word and sentence work

*6 min.* Model a topic sentence that promises two groups, [[s02-K68-1]], and a three-box plan for the pond frog on the board, [[s02-K68-2]]: each fact goes in one box. The plan has an opening, a body box, a home box and a closing; the body and home boxes will join into the middle paragraph. *4 min.* Each child writes a topic sentence for the glass frog on a slate, [[s02-P68-7]]. Watch for error S: a sentence too thin to open the page.

#### 10 min. Talk

*4 min.* Pairs, 2 minutes a child, [[s02-P68-9]]: tell your three-box plan and where a peer comment would improve it; the partner names the group they heard in each box. *6 min.* Three volunteers, 2 minutes each. Anyone may pass.

#### 20 min. Writing

*12 min.* Each child makes a three-box plan for the glass frog, [[s02-P68-8]], from the note card, and checks that each fact is in one box. Watch for error AA: related facts left apart. *Through the whole 20 minutes* the device runs at the front. Review: you typed at a shared keyboard in grade 2. First, the teacher types and saves one line (1 minute), and says each key pressed. Then ten children each take a supported one-minute turn, in the order on the list, and type the line from the typing card, [[s02-X68d]]; the child presses every key and the teacher only points. Children who have finished their plan check it and wait on the list for day 69. Watch for error AD: a child who watches a partner type has not typed.

**Shared shape.** The pond frog model and the glass frog practice share the shape of E68, a topic sentence that promises two groups. E68 uses a new case, the swamp frog, and neither the model nor the practice uses it.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hand out the strip to every child and read it aloud twice; children write alone, [[s02-X68]], [[s02-X68b]] and [[s02-X68c]]. The ten children who typed have also done [[s02-X68d]]. Shown: a topic sentence that names the frog and promises two groups; the two body notes; a closing that returns to the topic. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What two groups does the page promise?" Not yet seen: too thin to open the page (error S), body and home facts mixed (error AA), or a new unrelated topic in the close (error Q). Record on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

One child: the adult takes the partner role in the talk and sits beside the device. No device: the typing turn becomes a handwritten line on a card; record it as a paper stand-in, keyboarding not yet seen. No printer: rule the four boxes on a page.

### At home

**Swap.** No log book: any animal book.
**Skip.** The practice clip and the typing.

- **10 min. Read.** Your child rereads page 17 aloud.
  **Say.** "How does this page keep body and home apart?" Expect: it gives each frog its home, then its body.
- **20 min. Plan.** Your child makes the three-box plan for the glass frog, [[s02-P68-8]], and writes a topic sentence in the first box.
  **Say.** "Which two groups does your sentence promise?" Expect: body and home.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s02-X68]], [[s02-X68b]] and [[s02-X68c]] on paper, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the plan, the typing turns and the exit; record the talk as not reached. Longer: write a second topic sentence that promises two different groups.

### Words and supports

Plan, topic sentence, group, introduction, conclusion, body, home, keyboard, save. Frame: "This page explains how the ___ and the ___ work together." Large print; a scribe for the plan; the page read aloud again; the teacher points to keys only after the child has looked; record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 69 returns the day 68 strip, [[s02-X68]], [[s02-X68b]], [[s02-X68c]] and [[s02-X68d]], and repairs error AA first: sort the notes into body and home piles. It types and drafts an introduction that groups facts and states the frog topic, and every child takes a one-minute typing turn. The three-box plan stays in the folder, and the typed line stays on the shared page. The board and slate work, [[s02-P68-1]], [[s02-P68-2]], [[s02-P68-5]], [[s02-P68-7]], [[s02-P68-11]], [[s02-K68-1]] and [[s02-K68-2]], the spoken work, [[s02-P68-3]], [[s02-P68-6]] and [[s02-P68-9]], and the sheets, [[s02-P68-4]] and [[s02-P68-8]], stay in the folder.
