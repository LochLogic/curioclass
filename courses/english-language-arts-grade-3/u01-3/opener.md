# Section 1.3 opener: Grandmother, the Lion and the Crow (days 10 to 13)

## What the section does

Children read a story of a girl who learns to read beside her grandmother, recount its linked events with arrows, and use a context-and-figure chart for phrases that are not meant exactly. On day 12 they read The Lion and the Mouse, credited to Aesop and the 1919 edition, and explain its message. Day 13 is a Check day on a fresh tale, The Crow and the Pitcher.

## How the days run

Each day is 90 minutes. Day 10 recounts with a map; day 11 teaches phrases and a dictionary page; day 12 applies the work to a credited tale with one written explanation. Day 13 holds no teaching: silent skills, reading, six items, silent review and one exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A recount follows the arrows.** Events come in order, and each can cause the next.
- **A tale has a message.** It is shown by what a character does and what comes of it.
- **A picture can emphasize a feeling or a problem.** Ask what its size, color or lines add to the words.
- **A phrase is sometimes not meant exactly.** Say the literal and intended readings.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A to E:** a missing fact, a topic given as the message, a reversed action, a pictured thing named alone, a phrase read as a real event.
- **AC, F:** a word part read the wrong way, a word clue ignored.

## Marking

Same scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Repairs go to the next teaching lesson, never during the check.
