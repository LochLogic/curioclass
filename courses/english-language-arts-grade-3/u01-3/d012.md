# Day 12: The message of a tale

## Teacher page

### Before class

Find the day 12 line of exit cards 10 to 13. Print the tale page for each child: The Lion and the Mouse, credited "A fable credited to Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece, in Milo Winter's 1919 telling." Put a wall map where children can see Greece. Sort the day 11 phrase charts, look-ups and tables (s01-P11-1, s01-P11-2, s01-P11-3) for hand-back. Make the heard-six list.

### The day

#### 10 min. Opening

Three minutes repair day 11's exit, and hand back the day 11 charts, look-ups and tables with one comment each. If many children missed it, use error E (a phrase read as a real event). Write "The line was a tightrope." Say what the words say, then what is meant: a hard thing that needs care. Write them side by side.

Then seven minutes, unscored. Review: you learned tale in grade 1 and moral in grade 2. Ask: "Name two things a tale has." Expect: characters who talk and act, and a lesson at the end. Ask: "Where does a tale come from?" Point to Greece on the map: Aesop told fables in ancient Greece, and tales travel with the people who tell them. Ask children to say the credit line aloud: who told it, where, and which edition.

#### 20 min. Skills

*10 min. Partner rereading.* Review: in unit 1.2 you gave a partner feedback on the card. Pairs reread pages 4 and 5 of the grandmother story (about 145 words, 70 seconds a pass). Each child reads twice, 2 minutes 20 seconds, and the partner gives feedback, 30 seconds: one box ticked, one thing that went well, one next try. That is under 6 minutes for a pair. Never say speed. Listen to two pairs and write them in the heard-six column.

*10 min. Possessives.* Review: in unit 1.1 you learned the possessive with one owner. Say each, children write it on a slate: the bread of the baker (the baker's bread), the bowl of the dog (the dog's bowl), the shoe of the child (the child's shoe), the bag of the nurse (the nurse's bag). Ask: "Who owns it? Where does the apostrophe go?" Expect: after the owner, before the s.

#### 40 min. Hand work

*2 min. Reading.* Read The Lion and the Mouse aloud (193 words), children following with a finger. Say the credit first. Gloss two words in context: spare (do not hurt) and the net's ropes (the toils that hold him).

*13 min. The map.* Each child holds their own recount map sheet and fills it for the tale with [[s01-P12-1]]. Review: you built a recount with arrows on day 10. Walk and look. If a child writes a list (error K), ask: "What happened first? What came of it?"

*25 min. The message.* Each child writes with [[s01-P12-2]], from their own map, with the frame "The words ___ show ___." Put the writing frame card beside the page. Review: on day 8 you wrote what a character's actions show. Teach no new move. Look for four things: a message about what the Mouse's action shows, two events as proof, a link word such as so, and a closing line. If a child writes only "It is about a lion and a mouse" (error B), ask: "What did the Mouse do, and what came of it?" Judged after class with the look-fors in the item. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen on the record sheet. Day 13 is a Check day with no hand-back, so the maps and writing stay with the children and go back on day 14.

#### 15 min. Talk

Review: in grade 2 you used the four-step chart (turn, pair, link, clarify); in grade 3 the chart adds a fifth move. Pairs sit together. Each child takes 3 minutes to read their paragraph aloud and say the page of the tale each detail came from (6 minutes in all). Then 4 minutes for the partner to ask for a detail ("Which part of the tale shows that?") and to link: "I agree, and I would add ___." Then 5 volunteers at 1 minute each. Write two of them in the heard-six column. Anyone may pass.

#### 5 min. Exit

Read the day 12 tale aloud twice. Children write alone in 4 minutes: [[s01-X12]]. Unscored. Shown: says what happened and uses the goat's last words to explain the message. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What did the goats do? What did the last words say?" Not yet seen: only that it is about goats, or the result before the action.

### Other rooms

No printer: copy the tale by hand onto the board and read it aloud. No projector: keep the wall map on paper. No pairs: the adult reads the pages with the child and gives the feedback.

### At home

**Swap.** No tale page: any short fable or folktale, with its credit.
**Skip.** The partner rereading, the possessives and the talk.

- **10 min. Read.** Read The Lion and the Mouse aloud, or any short fable.
  **Say.** "What is the message, and which two events show it?" Expect: a message and two events from the tale.
- **15 min. Write.** Your child reads their paragraph aloud and adds one thing they left out. [[s01-P12-2]]
- **5 min. Read.** Read any book together.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s01-X12]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the map, the paragraph and the exit; cut the possessives to two phrases. Longer: a child writes a second paragraph on a fable from home and checks it against the four look-fors.

### Words and supports

Message, proof, link, ending, spare. Frame: "The words ___ show ___." Preview the tale with a spoken description of each moment. Large print; the tale read aloud for a child who cannot see the page; a scribe for the paragraph, noted. Record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 13 is a Check day with no repair slot. Error B is a topic given as the message ("It is about goats"). Error C is the action and its result reversed. Day 14 opens with five minutes on this exit and hands back the maps and paragraphs (s01-P12-1, s01-P12-2) with one comment each.
