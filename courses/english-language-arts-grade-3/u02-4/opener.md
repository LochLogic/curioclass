# Section 2.4 opener: a tale on your own (days 52 and 53)

## What the section does

Day 52 is a silent check: each child writes a fresh frog tale alone, then sets it beside the dated folder. Day 53 returns the tale with one written note and asks each child to make the one change the note names, in a margin version, not a copy. The same day the class opens the frog book and reads its first section for the gist.

## How the days run

Each day is 90 minutes. On day 52 children reread the two model tales, plan, and write for 28 minutes; the teacher answers nothing about content. On day 53 the teacher hands back the tale with a note, repairs the commonest letter on the board, and the class reads Frogs Live in Many Places and says its gist.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A whole tale holds together.** A situation, a change, an outcome that explains the feature.
- **One change at a time.** A note names one thing; copying the old words is not a change.
- **A gist is one sentence.** It says what the section is mostly about, not every detail.

## The errors, in plain words

- **S:** Writing leaves the intended explanation unclear. Repair: Add the missing explanation for its purpose. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **Q:** An ending introduces an unrelated topic. Repair: Close the established problem or explanation. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **B:** A topic replaces a complete central idea. Repair: Join two key events into a complete lesson statement. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.
- **A:** A fact is asserted without source support. Repair: Locate the exact source sentence. 5 minutes, next day's opening repair.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet, dated. Exits are unscored.
The day 52 tale and folder are scored on the end columns of the record sheet.
