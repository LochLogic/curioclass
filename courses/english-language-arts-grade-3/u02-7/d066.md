# Day 66: Use feedback to repair the paragraph and ask a focused question

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page for each child. Write one note on each day 64 final paragraph, and one on each day 65 sheet. Tally the error letters from the day 65 items, A50 to A60, and the strips, and find the commonest. Pick four names for conferences from the heard-six column. Set out the sheets, ready to hand back.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*3 min.* Return and repair day 65's exit (s02-X65 and s02-X65b), error Y (cause and result reversed): draw an arrow from the cause to the result in a new sentence, [[s02-P66-1]]. *7 min.* Week 13, regrouped by the errors in the day 64 edit, fewer words first: stopped, hoping and cried, [[s02-P66-2]]; then a longer base, enjoyment and tininess, [[s02-P66-3]]. Map the irregular word through again, [[s02-P66-4]].

#### 20 min. Independent reading

Children read from their logs. Hold four conferences of 4 minutes: the child tells one new fact and where the words are. Add the four to the heard-six column. The last 4 minutes: each child writes a dated line, [[s02-P66-12]].

#### 10 min. Feedback and return and repair

*3 min.* Hand back the day 64 final paragraph with its note and the day 65 sheets, s02-A50 to s02-A60, with their notes. Each child finds the one letter on the sheet. *4 min.* Repair the commonest letter at the board with a new case: A, a fact the panel does not give (point to the words); AA, facts in the wrong group (sort six cards into two piles); AB, a link word between ideas that do not belong (ask whether the second part belongs); D, a meaning that ignores its sentence (underline the clue); Z, a paragraph job named without checking (say each job). *3 min.* Groups by letter. The day 65 revision is made on day 67.

#### 20 min. Written repair

*12 min.* Each child repairs the day 64 final paragraph with the one change its note asks for, adding the missing reason why a body fact helps where the note says so, [[s02-P66-5]]. *8 min.* Show two questions about the glass frog: which is more focused, [[s02-K66-1]]. A focused question is about one frog and one detail. Each child then narrows a given broad question about tree frogs, [[s02-P66-6]]: circle the part that asks for everything and cross out parts until one detail is left. Watch for error W: a question that asks for everything.

#### 10 min. Reread

*4 min.* Pairs reread page 11. **Ask.** "Which detail explains usefulness?" Each pair points to the words that say why a body fact helps, [[s02-P66-7]]. Expect that a frog hard to see is less likely to be found by a hungry animal. *6 min.* Each child writes that detail as a brief note with its label, [[s02-P66-8]]. Watch for error A: a detail the page does not give.

#### 10 min. Word and sentence work

*4 min.* Review: you learned protect in grade 1, conclusion in grade 2 and also in grade 2. Say a sentence about a frog with protect, [[s02-K66-2]], and one about a paragraph with conclusion, [[s02-K66-3]]. *6 min.* Each child writes a sentence about a frog with also, [[s02-P66-9]], and one about their own paragraph with conclusion, [[s02-P66-10]].

#### 5 min. Talk

Expert groups of five, by angle: body, home or survival. 1 minute a child: read your narrowed question aloud. Then the group chooses the one with one detail and says why, [[s02-P66-11]].

#### 5 min. Exit

Hand out the strip to every child and read it aloud twice; children write alone, [[s02-X66c]]. While they write, call children to the table, 50 seconds each, about five: show the strip and ask [[s02-X66]], then open the book at the index and ask [[s02-X66bv2]]. Shown: a question about one frog and one detail, and the index word horned frog with page 13. Shown with help: right after one prompt, "What does the request leave out?" Not yet seen: a question that asks for everything (error W), or a page chosen at random (error V). The other children's evidence is the paper strip and the narrowed question, [[s02-P66-6]].

### Other rooms

One child: the adult takes the partner role and points to the words on page 11. No printer: write the student page items on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No log book: any animal book.
**Skip.** The expert group and the conferences.

- **10 min. Read.** Your child rereads page 11 aloud.
  **Say.** "Which detail tells why a body fact helps?" Expect: a frog that is hard to see is less likely to be found by a hungry animal.
- **20 min. Repair.** Your child fixes the paragraph, [[s02-P66-5]], narrows the tree frog question, [[s02-P66-6]], and writes the detail, [[s02-P66-8]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s02-X66]] and [[s02-X66bv2]] aloud and [[s02-X66c]] on paper, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the repair and the narrowed question; record the rest as not reached. Longer: write a research question about your own paragraph.

### Words and supports

Feedback, repair, focused, research question, index, protect, conclusion, also. Frames: "My question asks about one ___ and one ___." Large print; a scribe for the narrowing; the page read aloud again; record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 67 hears the exit, [[s02-X66]], [[s02-X66bv2]] and [[s02-X66c]], and repairs error W first: narrow the question to one frog and one detail. It opens with each child's narrowed question and the repaired paragraph, and the day 65 revision is made then. The paragraph and the narrowed question stay in the folder. The board and slate work, [[s02-P66-1]] to [[s02-P66-4]] and [[s02-K66-1]], the spoken work, [[s02-P66-7]], [[s02-P66-11]], [[s02-K66-2]] and [[s02-K66-3]], and the sheets, [[s02-P66-5]], [[s02-P66-6]], [[s02-P66-8]], [[s02-P66-9]], [[s02-P66-10]] and [[s02-P66-12]], stay in the folder.
