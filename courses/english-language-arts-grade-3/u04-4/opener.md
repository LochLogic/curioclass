# Section 4.4 opener: whose view is it? (days 133 to 136)

## What the section does

Four days teach children to tell whose words state a view: a character, the narrator, the writer or the reader. They also read how one step leads to the next in a short article, work out a phrase that means more than its words, and form irregular plurals. Day 135 is a check on a new passage, unscored; day 136 returns it.

## How the days run

Each day is 90 minutes. Day 133 reads a short story and an article and labels each judgment with its speaker. Day 134 follows steps and cause across two paragraphs and answers the writer with an opinion paragraph. Day 135 is silent: reading, items, review and an exit. Day 136 returns the passage and repairs it, then teaches plurals and a short opinion model.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Label whose words say it.** A character's wish is not the narrator's judgment, and neither is the reader's own view.
- **You may agree or disagree with a writer.** Keep the two views apart and give a reason from the text.
- **Some phrases mean more than their words.** Test the literal meaning and the other meaning against the surrounding sentences.
- **First, next and because show order and cause.** They tell how one sentence leads to the next.
- **Some plurals are irregular.** Say the singular and the plural together.

## The errors, in plain words

- **J:** Gives the reader's judgment to the writer. Repair: label each sentence writer or me.
- **K:** Reads a figure of speech as a physical action. Repair: test both meanings against the next sentence.
- **AM:** Gives a character's view to the narrator. Repair: write the speaker beside each judgment.
- **AU:** Says the opposite of a stated detail. Repair: reread the stating sentence.
- **R:** Adds a plain s to an irregular plural. Repair: say the singular and the irregular plural together.

## Marking

One scale holds all year: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark on the record sheet, dated. Exits are unscored and a check day stays unscored on the day.
