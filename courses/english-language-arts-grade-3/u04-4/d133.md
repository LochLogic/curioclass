# Day 133: Tell whose view it is: the character, the narrator, the writer or you

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, the label sheet, the story and the article part for each child. Return the day 132 exit slips with one comment each as children sit. Write on the board: character, narrator, writer, me. Open the record sheet at the heard-six column. Read the story once on your own: page 5 and the narrator's lines on pages 2, 5 and 9 matter most.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*4 min.* Children split three words into syllables, [[s04-P133-1]]. Review: you split long words into chunks from day 81 on; you met development on day 128, important on day 82 and community on day 127. *3 min.* They add -ing to three words, [[s04-P133-2]]. Review: you dropped a silent e and doubled a last letter on day 33, and carried the y on day 130. *3 min.* Say build and enough twice each, once inside a short sentence; children write them, [[s04-P133-3]]. Review: you met enough on day 102, with ough saying /uf/. New: build has ui saying /i/. Watch for a word guessed from its first letters.

#### 20 min. Skills block, log day

Children read their own book and write the log line, [[s04-P133-4]]. Four conferences of 4 minutes (16 minutes), each child saying what they read and one detail that fits, [[s04-P133-5]]. Two children read a log line aloud, 2 minutes each (4 minutes), [[s04-P133-6]]. Fill the heard-six column. Start with children not heard on day 130. Children not heard today come first on day 136. Review: you kept this log from day 4 on.

#### 5 min. Opening

*3 min.* Hand back the day 132 page with a tick on each written task. The commonest letter on the day 132 exit is likely error F: only a shared point, or only a difference. Show two cards on the board, "The pump fills a bucket" and "The tap fills a jug." Say the shared point first (both fill a container), then the difference (a pump, a tap). Children read the slip comment and mark one thing to fix. *2 min.* Show two one-line cards: Ben said, "My turn is next." and, from an article, "I think every school needs a tap." Say aloud that Ben is a character and the second line is the writer. Then the class says the three labels with a partner, [[s04-P133-7]]. Review: you told the character, the narrator and the reader apart on day 84, and you spotted an author's view by I think and I believe on day 82. New today: the label writer for the person who wrote an article, and the word viewpoint.

#### 20 min. Read and label

*8 min.* Read New Well, Shared Work whole and aloud once, as children follow on their copy. Do not stop to ask questions. *12 min.* Say: a character talks inside the story, in quotation marks; the narrator tells the story from outside; the writer wrote the article. A view is what someone thinks, not a plain fact. Model the first row aloud with a line from page 7, not from the rows below: Mr. Kioko says the sand holds a lot, but it is not endless. Then children fill the view label sheet. Row C, [[s04-P133-8]]. Row N, [[s04-P133-9]]. Row W, using the part What this account shows in the Kitui article, [[s04-P133-10]]. Walk the room; point at the page marks. Watch for the narrator's words put in the character's row, error AM.

#### 20 min. Write

*3 min.* Model one sentence aloud and on the board: "The character Mr. Kioko thinks the sand will not last for ever." Say who, then what they think. *14 min.* Children write three sentences, one for Mutheu [[s04-P133-11]], one for the narrator [[s04-P133-12]], one for the writer [[s04-P133-13]], then the Me row on the label sheet, [[s04-P133-14]]. Review: you wrote an opinion with because on day 98 and on day 132. A child who needs it may say the view aloud first, or draw it and label it. *3 min.* Children underline who thinks it in each sentence and fix one, [[s04-P133-15]].

#### 5 min. Partner talk

Each child tells a partner what they think about a village sharing one well, 2 minutes each and 1 to change over, [[s04-P133-16]]. Say: this is your own view, so say I think. Listen in on three pairs; no child is held up as the example of a mistake.

#### 5 min. Word in context

*3 min.* Read the sentence about a viewpoint. Children circle the words that tell what a viewpoint is, [[s04-P133-17]]. *2 min.* Read the entry on the board and check it against the circled words: a viewpoint is the way one person sees or thinks about something. Review: you used a sentence clue and then a glossary on day 18.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hand out the half slip to every child and read the card aloud twice; children write alone, [[s04-X133]]. Support is recorded. Shown: the narrator, and the words says sharing the buckets is kind. Shown with help: after one prompt, "Who tells the story, and what does the card say?" Not yet seen: Sam (error AM) or the writer (error AU). Record on the record sheet. Keep the label sheet in the folder.

### Other rooms

One child: the adult reads the story aloud, then asks "Who says that?" for three lines and "What do you think?" for one; the child fills the label sheet and answers the exit on paper. One projector: show the story one page at a time; children label on paper and partners talk in pairs.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed story: read any short story that has a teller, and any short article by a writer.
**Skip.** The long words and the word in context.

- **15 min. Read.** Read New Well, Shared Work aloud together.
  **Say.** "Who says that the plan was fair, Mutheu or the narrator?" Expect the narrator.
- **10 min. Label.** Your child fills the three rows of the label sheet, [[s04-P133-8]] to [[s04-P133-10]].
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s04-X133]] on paper, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the reading, the three rows and the exit; cut the long words and the partner talk. Longer: compare the narrator's view with the writer's view in two sentences.

### Words and supports

Character, narrator, writer, viewpoint, believe, fair. Frames: "The character thinks ___." "The narrator thinks ___." "The writer thinks ___." "I think ___ because ___." Three colour cards for character, narrator and writer; the story may be read aloud again for any child; large print for the card; a drawing may carry a view with a spoken sentence; record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 134 opens with the commonest slip on s04-X133, likely error AM: write the speaker beside each judgment. It reads How a Town Cleans Its Water, follows the steps and the words that join them, and has each child write a response to the writer. The label sheet goes in the folder.
