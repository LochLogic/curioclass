# Day 142: Draft a close and edit plurals

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, the plural strip and the look-for card of four for each child. Write thirst, cleanliness and health on the board. Return each child's day 141 draft with its comment; sort the drafts into at most four piles by the commonest error letter, L, M or N. Choose six children to confer with, starting with those not conferred with on day 141.

### The day

#### 10 min. Long words

*4 min.* Say protect, thirst and cleanliness twice each. Say the chunks: pro-tect, thirst, clean-li-ness. Children draw the lines, [[s04-P142-1]]. Review: you cut protect into two chunks on day 130. *3 min.* Say knew and sure twice each; knew has kn said /n/, sure has s said /sh/. Children write both, [[s04-P142-2]]. Review: you mapped knew in grade 1; sure is new. *3 min.* Say: -ed tells it happened. Children add -ed to wish and name, [[s04-P142-3]]. Review: you added -ed on days 125 and 138. Watch for the silent e kept, error x.

#### 20 min. Skills block, Monday

*5 min.* Say: an abstract noun names an idea you cannot touch; thirst, cleanliness and health are on the board. Children circle the abstract nouns in a short list, [[s04-P142-5]]. Review: you circled abstract nouns on day 139. Say man and men, foot and feet, goose and geese together. Children write the three plurals, [[s04-P142-4]]. Review: you said irregular plurals together on day 139. Watch for a plain s, error R.

*15 min.* Partners reread two paragraphs of the town text, Why the water needs cleaning and Why the order matters. Read, listen, card, swap: each child reads one paragraph for 2 minutes to the partner, [[s04-P142-6]], [[s04-P142-7]]; the listener ticks the feedback card for one minute, [[s04-P142-8]]; then they swap. That is 12 minutes of reading and ticking and 3 for changing over. Review: you used the feedback card and reread this text on day 134. Move round and listen; leave a written note for three children.

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand each child the day 141 draft with its comment, and give back the day 141 exit slip, s04-X141, with one comment; tick each task on the day 141 page. Read aloud the written move for the pile: for L add the choice, for M swap the liking for a source detail, for N name the relationship before the link word. Each child makes one change that answers the comment and marks it, [[s04-P142-9]]. Watch for a neat recopy, error T.

#### 5 min. Opening

Say: a close comes back to the opinion and says one reason again. Write on the board: does the close come back to the opinion? Children read two closes on the page and choose, [[s04-P142-10]]. Show the look-for card of four; the last row is the close. Review: you heard a close in the rain garden model on day 136.

#### 30 min. Draft (O04)

Children write the close of the water essay: two sentences, the first coming back to the opinion, the second giving one reason from a source. The close is kept in the folder as unscored drafting. They use one abstract noun from the board anywhere in the draft and underline it, [[s04-P142-12]]. Review: you used abstract nouns in a draft on day 139. Confer with six children for 4 minutes each (24 minutes) while the rest write; the talk is a note, not a mark. Watch for a liking in place of a close, error M, and for a close about something else, error O. Save the draft as G142.

#### 5 min. Plural edit

Children circle every plural noun in the draft and write its single word beside it, [[s04-P142-13]]. They check each pair against the plural strip, which shows the one and the more than one for man, woman, foot, child, mouse, goose, leaf, life, box, dish and class. Mark R beside a plain s on an irregular plural and AO beside a plain s where es is needed. Review: you edited a plural in your draft on day 139.

#### 5 min. Partner talk

Partners read the close aloud, 2 minutes each and 1 to change over, [[s04-P142-14]]. The listener says what the close comes back to. Each writer then ticks the conclusion row of the look-for card of four. Say: the planned question is, does the close come back to the opinion?

#### 5 min. Word in context

Read the sentence from the compelling question. Children write what protect means in it, [[s04-P142-15]]. Check the answers against the sentence. Review: you met protect, to keep safe, in grade 3 on day 66.

#### 5 min. Exit

Hand out the half slip to every child and read each card aloud twice; children write alone, [[s04-X142]]. Then they turn to the exit box on the page and write alone, [[s04-X142b]]. Support is recorded. Shown: a close that returns to the opinion in the draft on the card; both plurals written right. Shown with help: after one prompt, "What is the draft about?" or "Say the one and the more than one." Not yet seen: a close about something else, error O, or liking alone, error M; a plain s on an irregular plural, error R, or a plain s where es is needed, error AO. Record on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

One child: the adult hears the paragraphs read, listens to the close and asks, "What does it come back to?"; the child writes on paper and answers the exit alone. One projector: show the board words and the plural strip; children work on paper, in pairs.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed text: use any short page about water.
**Skip.** The long words.

- **15 min. Close.** Read your child's essay aloud, then the last sentence.
  **Say.** "Does the last sentence come back to your opinion?" Expect a yes with the opinion named. Your child picks the better close in [[s04-P142-10]].
- **10 min. Plurals.** Say man and men, foot and feet, goose and geese; your child writes them, [[s04-P142-4]].
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Your child answers [[s04-X142]] and [[s04-X142b]] on paper, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: keep the close, the plural edit and the exit; cut the long words and the word in context. Longer: write a third closing sentence that says what the reader can do.

### Words and supports

Close, opinion, reason, plural, abstract noun, protect. Frames: "My close comes back to ___." "One reason is ___." A printed look-for card; the plural strip; extra time; a drawing with a spoken close may stand in; record any help.

### Tomorrow

Day 143 returns the day 142 draft with a written move for error O, R or AO. Then children give and take one peer look-for, revise the essay once from a peer and once from the teacher, and publish it. The drafts go in the folder.
