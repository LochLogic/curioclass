# Day 79 sources: leaders, battles and the home front

Side 1 holds the sources. Side 2 is the outline map you mark on the exit ticket.

**Source A. Confederate States Congress, the conscription act, April 16, 1862.** Public domain. Public Laws of the
Confederate States, First Congress, First Session.

> [to cut: the clause placing white men aged 18 to 35 in military service for three years, about 50 words, checked
> against the scan]

Gloss: *conscription*, a law that makes people serve in the army.

**Source B. Robert E. Lee to Jefferson Davis, September 3, 1862.** Public domain. Official Records, series 1, vol.
19, part 2, p. 590.

> The present seems to be the most propitious time since the commencement of the war for the Confederate Army to
> enter Maryland.

Gloss: *propitious*, favourable.

**Source C. Ulysses S. Grant, Personal Memoirs, 1885, on the Vicksburg campaign.** Public domain. Written twenty
years after the events, while Grant was dying, to support his family.

> [to cut: Grant's account of moving below Vicksburg and cutting loose from his supply line, about 120 words,
> checked against the 1885 text]

**Source D. The Homestead Act, May 20, 1862.** Public domain (United States government work). 12 Stat. 392.

> [to cut and check: the clause entitling a head of a family, or a person aged twenty-one, to enter a quarter
> section of unappropriated public lands]

**Source E. The Pacific Railway Act, July 1, 1862, section 2.** Public domain (United States government work).
12 Stat. 489.

> The United States shall extinguish as rapidly as may be the Indian titles to all lands falling under the operation
> of this act.

Gloss: *extinguish the Indian titles*, end Native nations' legal ownership of the land.
