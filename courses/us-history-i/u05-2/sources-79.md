# Day 79 sources: leaders, battles and the home front

Read each source line first: who made it, when, and for whom. Write on your student page, not here.

**Source A. Confederate States Congress, "An Act to further provide for the public defence", approved by President
Jefferson Davis, April 16, 1862.** Public domain. Statutes at Large of the Confederate States, First Congress,
first session, chapter 31.

> the President be, and he is hereby authorized to call out and place in the military service of the Confederate
> States, for three years, unless the war shall have been sooner ended, all white men who are residents of the
> Confederate States, between the ages of eighteen and thirty-five years at the time the call or calls may be made,
> who are not legally exempted from military service.

Gloss: *call out*, order into the army; *legally exempted*, excused by law.

**Source B. Robert E. Lee to Jefferson Davis, September 3, 1862.** Public domain. Official Records, series 1, vol.
19, part 2, p. 590. A private letter to his president.

> The present seems to be the most propitious time since the commencement of the war ... to enter Maryland.

Gloss: *propitious*, favourable. Printed copies differ on one word where the dots stand.

**Source C. Ulysses S. Grant, Personal Memoirs, 1885, chapters 33 and 34, on the Vicksburg campaign of April and May
1863.** Public domain. Written twenty-two years after the events, while Grant was dying, to support his family.

> I felt a degree of relief scarcely ever equalled since. Vicksburg was not yet taken it is true, nor were its
> defenders demoralized by any of our previous moves. I was now in the enemy's country, with a vast river and the
> stronghold of Vicksburg between me and my base of supplies. But I was on dry ground on the same side of the river
> with the enemy. ... I therefore determined to move independently of Banks, cut loose from my base, destroy the
> rebel force in rear of Vicksburg and invest or capture the city.

Gloss: *base of supplies*, the depot his food and ammunition came from; *invest*, surround.

**Source D. The Homestead Act, May 20, 1862, section 1.** Public domain (United States government work). 12 Stat.
392.

> any person who is the head of a family, or who has arrived at the age of twenty-one years, and is a citizen of the
> United States, or who shall have filed his declaration of intention to become such, ... and who has never borne
> arms against the United States Government or given aid and comfort to its enemies, shall, from and after the first
> January, eighteen hundred and sixty-three, be entitled to enter one quarter section or a less quantity of
> unappropriated public lands

Gloss: *enter*, claim; *a quarter section*, 160 acres; *unappropriated public lands*, federal land not yet sold or
granted. The law does not say whose land it had been.

**Source E. The Pacific Railway Act, July 1, 1862, section 2.** Public domain (United States government work).
12 Stat. 489.

> The United States shall extinguish as rapidly as may be the Indian titles to all lands falling under the operation
> of this act and required for the said right of way and grants hereinafter made.

Gloss: *extinguish the Indian titles*, end Native nations' legal ownership of the land; *right of way*, the strip
the railroad runs on.

**Source F. Three battles: our facts card.** Ours, from National Park Service park pages (public domain). Losses
count soldiers killed, wounded, captured or missing on both sides.

| Battle | When | Who led | What happened at once | Losses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antietam, Maryland | September 17, 1862 | Lee; McClellan | Lee's first invasion of the North turned back | about 22,720 in one day |
| Gettysburg, Pennsylvania | July 1 to 3, 1863 | Lee; Meade | Lee's second invasion turned back | more than 51,000 in three days |
| Vicksburg, Mississippi | campaign to July 4, 1863 | Grant; Pemberton | The city and its army surrendered on July 4 after a 47-day siege | more than 48,000 in the whole campaign |

**Source G. Alexander Gardner, photograph, "President Lincoln on battle-field of Antietam", October 3, 1862.**
Public domain; Library of Congress, no known restrictions on publication. Published by Gardner in his Photographic
Sketch Book of the War, 1866.
