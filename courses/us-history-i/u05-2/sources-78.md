**Source B. Abraham Lincoln to Horace Greeley, August 22, 1862.** Public domain. A public letter, printed in the
newspapers. Collected Works, vol. 5, pp. 388-389.

> My paramount object in this struggle is to save the Union, and is not either to save or to destroy slavery. If I
> could save the Union without freeing any slave I would do it, and if I could save it by freeing all the slaves I
> would do it; and if I could save it by freeing some and leaving others alone I would also do that.

Gloss: *paramount*, most important.

**Source C. Abraham Lincoln, the Emancipation Proclamation, January 1, 1863.** Public domain (United States
government work). National Archives.

> [to cut: the sentence declaring persons held as slaves in areas in rebellion forever free, and the sentence on
> receiving freed men into the armed service, about 120 words, checked against the transcript]

Gloss: *in rebellion*, fighting against the United States on that date.

**Source D. General Benjamin Butler to General Winfield Scott, Fortress Monroe, Virginia, May 27, 1861.** Public
domain. Official Records, series 2, vol. 1; Freedmen and Southern Society Project transcription.

> [to cut: Butler's account of the people who escaped slavery to the fort and his decision to keep them, about 120

Gloss: *contraband*, enemy property that may be seized in war; the word Butler used.
