# Day 82: Test a memory against the record

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the source sheets and the exit-source slips; hold the slips. Mark day 80's organizers and exits. Day 81's s05-P81-1 stays in the essay folders with draft 1. Write feedback on draft 2 during days 82 and 83; it comes back in day 84's warm-up.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 80's four-source organizer (s05-P80-2) and day 80's exit, as day 80 promised, and day 79's organizer (s05-P79-2) with your comments, for the folder. Students answer **Ask.** [[s05-P82-1]] alone. Expect a limit tied to who made the source, when or why. If more than a third of day 80's exits showed error O, sort aloud what Source D on day 80's sheet shows (uniforms, rifles, a posed company) and cannot show (feelings, reasons for joining), then do the same for the cartoon. If exits showed error E, students add Cumming's nursing from day 80's Source B to their organizers. The practice and the organizer stay with students in their folders.

#### 12 min. Worked example

The Lost Cause is an account written after defeat: it said the Confederacy fought for states' rights and honour, not slavery. Edward Pollard's 1866 book gave it its name; historians since, Black historians among the first, have tested it against the record and found it false. We test it; we never balance it as one view of two.

Before Source B, say that its claim is racist and hateful and that we read it as evidence of why the Confederacy was made. You read it aloud, once, plainly; no student reads it. Read Sources A and B with their source lines: Davis wrote in 1881, sixteen years after defeat; Stephens spoke in March 1861 as vice president, and the printed text is a newspaper report. Students attempt **Ask.** [[s05-K82-1]], then reveal the steps:

[[steps:s05-K82-1]]

Davis's claim fails against Stephens's statement; Davis's book is still good evidence of memory-making. Earlier is not always truer: timing, office and purpose decide together. Last step: before reading any table, read its title, what each column counts, and what the order of its rows shows.

#### 15 min. Pairs: memory

Read Sources C and D aloud with their source lines. Pairs discuss **Ask.** [[s05-P82-2]], then each writes alone. Expect: Lee asks people to remember courage overwhelmed by numbers; Douglass asks them never to forget what each side fought for. For error H, opinions treated as equal, send pairs back to who wrote, when and why.

#### 3 min. Debrief

**Ask.** Who is left out of a reunion story about brave armies on both sides? Expect: the enslaved, and the Black soldiers of day 80. Name slavery plainly.

#### 15 min. Exit ticket

Hand out the exit-source slip. Read the claim card and Source E aloud, neutrally. Students answer [[s05-X82]] alone: what the claim asks people to forget, the test against Source E, their question, then Source F. Read Source F's title and columns without pointing to a pattern. A table cannot show any one person's motive. Accept dictated answers, scribed without hints. Collect tickets.

### Other rooms

Paper is the main path. Without a printer, copy Sources A to D on the board; write the slip's three parts on a separate covered sheet.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the sources onto paper; keep the slip's part on its own covered sheet.

**Skip.** The warm-up; you are the partner.

- **10 min. Test a claim.** Read Sources A and B. Talk through [[s05-K82-1]] before its steps.
  **Say.** "Why does the date matter?" Expect: Davis defends a lost cause later; Stephens says why the Confederacy was made, in 1861.
- **12 min. Memory.** Read Sources C and D. Talk about [[s05-P82-2]]; the child writes alone.
  **Say.** "What does each leave out?" Expect: Lee leaves out slavery; Douglass refuses to.
- **15 min. Exit ticket.** Hand over the slip. The child answers [[s05-X82]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: hear one pair in the debrief; keep the test, the question, Source F and the exit. Longer: set Lee's order beside Douglass's speech and say which better answers why the war was fought, and why.

### Words and supports

Lost Cause: a later account defending the Confederacy and denying slavery's place. Memory: how the past is remembered. Read source lines aloud, use the glosses, and allow spoken answers first; a language gap is not error H.

This exit comes back again on day 87, which returns to questioning historians' accounts. Day 83 turns to the war's consequences. Draft 2 comes back with feedback in day 84's warm-up.
