# Day 82 sources: testing a memory against the record

Source B holds a hateful claim. We read it because it tells us, in a founder's own words, why the Confederacy was
made. Keep Source F folded until the exit ticket asks for it.

**Source A (worked example). Jefferson Davis, The Rise and Fall of the Confederate Government, 1881, vol. 1, a
chapter headed "Slavery not the Cause, but an Incident".** Public domain. Written sixteen years after the Confederacy
he led was defeated.

> the existence of African servitude was in no wise the cause of the conflict, but only an incident

Gloss: *servitude*, slavery; *in no wise*, not at all; *incident*, a side matter.

**Source B. Alexander H. Stephens, Confederate vice president, speech at Savannah, Georgia, March 21, 1861.**
Public domain. Printed in Henry Cleveland, Alexander H. Stephens, in Public and Private, 1866.

> Our new government is founded upon exactly the opposite idea; its foundations are laid, its cornerstone rests,
> upon the great truth that the negro is not equal to the white man; that slavery, subordination to the superior
> race, is his natural and normal condition.

[to check against the 1866 text]

**Source C. Robert E. Lee, General Orders No. 9, April 10, 1865.** Public domain. His farewell to his army, the day
after its surrender.

> After four years of arduous service marked by unsurpassed courage and fortitude, the Army of Northern Virginia has
> been compelled to yield to overwhelming numbers and resources.

[to check against a scan]

**Source D. Frederick Douglass, address at Arlington National Cemetery, May 30, 1871.** Public domain. Frederick
Douglass Papers Project.

> [to cut: about 60 words ending with his line that we must never forget the difference between those who struck at
> the nation's life and those who struck to save it, those who fought for slavery and those who fought for liberty
> and justice]

**Source E (exit ticket). Constitution of the Confederate States, March 11, 1861, Article I, section 9, clause 4.**
Public domain.

> No bill of attainder, ex post facto law, or law denying or impairing the right of property in negro slaves shall
> be passed.

Gloss: *impairing*, weakening.

**The claim card (exit ticket).** A claim printed in many places long after the war: the Southern states left the
Union to defend states' rights, and slavery had little to do with it.
