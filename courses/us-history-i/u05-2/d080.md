# Day 80: Service and the limits of a source

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the listed pieces; fold Source F under on every sheet. Sort day 79's exit tickets by look-for. Keep the Component M organizers for day 89. Finish written feedback on essay 1 draft 1 for day 81.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 79's exit; read two answers to day 79's s05-P79-1 aloud and judge them together. Students answer **Ask.** [[s05-P80-1]] alone. Expect Vicksburg, then Gettysburg. If more than a third of the exits showed error D, use this warm-up to sort the three offices and put the battles in order from day 79's Source F. Error K goes to a small group during the pairs part: read "Indian titles" in day 79's Source E and name the nations. The worked example keeps its ten minutes.

#### 10 min. Worked example

Read Source A and its source line aloud. Students attempt **Ask.** [[s05-K80-1]] before the model:

[[steps:s05-K80-1]]

Douglass, who had escaped slavery himself, spoke in March 1863 to free Black men in the North, after the Proclamation opened the army to them. Point to the words that recruit. A call to serve cannot show who joined or how they were treated. Students fill the frame on side 1.

#### 20 min. Pairs: the four-source organizer

Read Sources B and C aloud with their glosses; show D and E with their descriptions. Content note: wounded men, and a veteran who lost a leg. Ask no one to share family stories. Cumming nursed Confederate wounded; the army she served fought for slavery. Source C is Tubman's own letter: she guided Colonel Montgomery's Black soldiers of the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers up the Combahee, and 756 people came away to freedom. She asks credit for "we colored people." United States Colored Troops was the army's name for Black regiments. Black soldiers were paid less than white soldiers until Congress made pay equal in 1864, and captured Black soldiers faced death or enslavement, as at Fort Pillow in 1864.

Pairs complete **Ask.** [[s05-P80-2]]; each student fills all four rows. Cumming's journal describes 1862 but was published in 1866: give both dates. The photograph shows a posed company, not feelings. The cartoon argues for the vote; it is not one man's record. Collect the organizers after the share.

#### 5 min. Share

**Ask.** Which of Sources B to E answers something Source A cannot show? Expect: Source C and Source D show Black men did serve; Source E asks what their service should earn. **Ask.** What can the photograph still not tell you? Expect: why they joined or what they felt. Two pairs read one limit each; the class sorts it into who made it, when, or why.

#### 10 min. Exit ticket

Students unfold Source F, a letter they have not seen. Read its source line and first sentence aloud, neutrally; it keeps Rice's spelling. Students answer [[s05-X80]] alone on the ticket. Accept dictated answers, scribed without hints. Collect tickets for day 82.

### Other rooms

Paper is the main path. Without a printer, copy the passages and the organizer onto the board, and read the image descriptions aloud. One projector can show the two images large.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the pages; look at the two images together on one screen. Keep Source F folded.

**Skip.** The warm-up and the share.

- **8 min. Example.** Read Source A together. The child tries [[s05-K80-1]]; follow its steps together.
  **Say.** "What service does Douglass ask for?" Expect: joining the army; a recruiting call does not prove who joined.
- **20 min. Sources.** Read Sources B and C aloud; look at D and E with their descriptions. The child fills in [[s05-P80-2]].
  **Say.** "What can the photograph not tell you?" Expect: what the soldiers felt or why they joined.
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Unfold Source F. The child answers [[s05-X80]] alone. Keep both pages for day 82.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one pair reports in the share; keep the model, all four organizer rows and the exit. Longer: set Source A's promise beside Source E's question: by 1865, what had service earned?

### Words and supports

Gloss franchise as the right to vote, and sourcing as asking who made it, when, for whom and why. Read in chunks. Frame: "This source shows ___, but it cannot show ___ because ___ (who made it, when, or for whom)." Take the frame away for the exit. Accept spoken reasons, so a language gap is not marked as error E.
