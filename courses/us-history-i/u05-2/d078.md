# Day 78: How emancipation changed the Union's war aims

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the listed pieces and fold Source E under so it stays covered. Have day 71's readiness R1 and R2 results ready, with the retrieval task from the grade 5 Civil War row. Keep day 77's essay 1 draft 1 for written feedback before day 81.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Hand back day 71's readiness R1 and R2. Students answer alone: **Ask.** [[s05-P78-1]]. The grade 5 retrieval task asked what students remember about emancipation; listen for error C, everyone freed at once, and error J, an aim that never changed. Hear two answers and contrast them: in 1861 the Union's stated aim was restoring the Union.

#### 10 min. Teaching first

Read Sources A and D aloud with their source lines and glosses; students follow on paper. Source A: in April 1861 Lincoln ordered a blockade, warships closing Southern ports to trade and supplies, to restore the Union. Source D: in May 1861 three men escaped slavery to Fortress Monroe. Later accounts name them as Frank Baker, Shepard Mallory and James Townsend. Their flight, and the many who followed, forced Union officers to decide whether to send people back. Butler's letter is his view, not theirs. "Contraband" was the army's word; we say people who escaped slavery. Students mark A and D for their timeline.

#### 10 min. Worked example

Read Source B aloud. Students attempt **Ask.** [[s05-K78-1]] before the model. Walk through the steps, pausing for students to mark the aim, the three choices and the closing sentence:

[[steps:s05-K78-1]]

The closing sentence matters: Lincoln separates his official duty from his personal wish that all men everywhere could be free. The letter was public; a month earlier he had read a draft order to his cabinet, so it shows his public position, not his whole plan. Students complete the frame on side 1.

#### 12 min. Pairs: the timeline of aims

Read Source C aloud first, then its gloss. **Ask.** [[s05-P78-2]]. One partner finds dates and evidence, the other aims and who pushed; switch halfway. Circulate: expect Sources A, D, B, C in order, with flight from slavery pressing policy.

#### 3 min. Debrief

**Ask.** What came between Sources B and C? Expect: in September 1862, after Antietam, Lincoln announced the preliminary proclamation, a warning that freedom would come on January 1 in areas still in rebellion. **Ask.** Why did the Proclamation stop where it did? Expect: Lincoln issued it as a war measure, so it could reach only areas fighting the Union, and he needed the loyal slave states of Kentucky, Maryland, Missouri and Delaware to stay. Tennessee was never named; parts of Louisiana and Virginia were excepted. Freedom on paper still needed armies and people's own flight.

#### 10 min. Exit ticket

Uncover Source E. Read its source line and passage aloud, neutrally; its first lines say Conkling's meeting was full of Union men who disliked the Proclamation. Students answer [[s05-X78]] alone on the ticket. Accept oral answers, scribed without hints. Collect tickets; timelines go in folders. Send the family note home.

### Other rooms

Paper is the main path. Without a printer, copy the passages and source lines on the board, keeping Source E covered. No projector is needed.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed pages: copy the questions and passages onto paper. Cover Source E.

**Skip.** The warm-up and the debrief.

- **10 min. Read.** Read Sources A and D together. Point to the dates and read the glosses.
- **10 min. Example.** Read Source B; the child tries [[s05-K78-1]], then you follow its steps together.
  **Say.** "What does this public letter show?" Expect: Lincoln's stated aim, not his whole thinking.
- **10 min. Timeline.** Read Source C; do [[s05-P78-2]] together, the child writing.
  **Say.** "Who pushed for change?" Expect: people escaping slavery, not Lincoln alone.
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Uncover and read Source E without explaining it. The child answers [[s05-X78]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: cut pair reports to one; keep all four items and the exit. Longer: partners add to the timeline what each source cannot show, and who made it.

### Words and supports

Use the sheet's glosses for blockade, paramount, in rebellion and contraband; emancipation means freeing people from slavery. Read in short chunks and accept dictated reasoning. A student who explains the limit aloud without the word proclamation has met it. Frame: "This source shows ___, but it cannot show ___ because ___ (who made it, when, or for whom)."
