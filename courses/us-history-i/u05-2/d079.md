# Day 79: Decisions, places and consequences

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the listed pieces. Fold Sources D, E and H under; hold the exit-source slips. Sort day 78's exit tickets by look-for, and count how many missed the place the Proclamation did not reach.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 78's practice, s05-P78-1 and s05-P78-2. Read the right order aloud, Sources A, D, B, C, with the aim beside each; students correct their own timelines, then put them in folders. An aim that never changes is error J. Return day 78's exit. Then students answer **Ask.** [[s05-P79-1]] alone. If more than a third of the exits missed the reach (error C), read day 78 Source C's gloss list now (loyal slave states, Tennessee, the excepted parishes and counties); otherwise a small group takes it. Victory alone (error L): that group rereads day 78's Source D, who came to Fortress Monroe and what Butler did.

#### 10 min. Teaching first

Three offices: Davis, Confederate president; Lee, commander of the Army of Northern Virginia; Grant, Union general in the West, later of all Union armies. Grant's military secretary from late 1864, Ely S. Parker, was a Seneca leader; at Appomattox he helped draft the surrender documents in his own hand. The Confederacy was founded to protect slavery, and its armies fought for that. Read Sources A and B aloud with their source lines. Davis signed the 1862 draft law to keep armies full; Lee told Davis the time was right to enter Maryland, and began crossing before Davis could reply.

Read the facts card, Source F, row by row. Antietam turned Lee back in September 1862 and gave Lincoln the moment for the preliminary proclamation. Gettysburg turned back a second invasion; Lee never invaded the North again. Battles cost thousands of lives; none alone ended the war.

Source G: a photograph of Lincoln and McClellan at Antietam, no dead shown. Read its description: Lincoln came to press McClellan to attack, and McClellan's slowness soon cost him his command. The picture shows a meeting, not what either man thought.

#### 10 min. Worked example

Read Source C aloud. Students read the Vicksburg row of Source F, a city on the Mississippi River, and attempt **Ask.** [[s05-K79-1]] before the model:

[[steps:s05-K79-1]]

Grant cut loose from his supply line; Vicksburg surrendered on July 4, 1863. Port Hudson surrendered on July 9, and only then did the Union hold the whole river, splitting the Confederacy. As Union armies moved through Mississippi, thousands of enslaved people fled to their lines. Students fill the frame on side 1.

#### 10 min. Alone: the organizer

Hand out the organizer half-sheet. Students answer **Ask.** [[s05-P79-2]] alone from Sources A, B, C and F. Each leader needs a decision and its result; each battle an effect at once and one that lasted. It is practice: walk the room, give no answers, and collect the organizers to write comments on. They come back in day 82's warm-up and are never scored.

#### 5 min. The 1862 land laws

Uncover Sources D, E and H; read their source lines, passages and glosses aloud. Federal power here means Congress deciding who would own western land. The Railway Act promised that the United States "shall extinguish" the titles: the nations still held the land in 1862. The Western Shoshone negotiated as a nation; the treaty has no article giving up their land. Teacher background, not taught: the US-Dakota War of 1862 and the Sand Creek massacre of 1864 happened during this war; unit 6.2 (Western expansion, Native nationhood) takes them up.

#### 10 min. Exit ticket

Hand out the exit-source slip. Read Source I's source line and passage aloud, neutrally, without explaining them. Students answer [[s05-X79]] alone. Accept dictated answers, scribed without hints. Collect tickets.

### Other rooms

Paper is the main path. Without a printer, copy the sources and the organizer's two tables onto the board; students draw the tables on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: copy the questions and passages onto paper. Cover Sources D, E and H; keep the slip for the exit.

**Skip.** The warm-up.

- **8 min. Read.** Read Sources A, B, F and G's description. Davis led the Confederate government, Lee its main army, Grant the Union army in the West.
- **8 min. Example.** Read Source C; the child tries [[s05-K79-1]], then follow its steps together.
  **Say.** "Why did the river matter?" Expect: holding it split the Confederacy.
- **8 min. Organizer.** The child fills in the organizer half-sheet alone; talk it over after.
- **5 min. Land laws.** Read Sources D, E and H with their glosses.
  **Say.** "Whose land was it?" Expect: nations Source H names.
- **10 min. Exit ticket.** Hand over the slip; read Source I without explaining it. The child answers [[s05-X79]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: read Source G's description only; keep the model, the organizer, the land laws and the exit. Longer: compare Lee's letter with what Antietam did to his plan.

### Words and supports

Gloss conscription as required military service and propitious as favourable; use the sheet's glosses for "Indian titles", stipulated and alternate section. Name nations, not "Indians" in general: Source H names the Western Shoshone, and its gloss the Cheyenne and Arapaho, whose land the line crossed. Say "Indian" only when reading the law's words; in our own voice say "Native nations' titles". The nations' own names, for the teacher: Newe (Shoshone), Tsistsistas and Hinono'ei (Cheyenne and Arapaho). Some students' families are citizens of these nations: ask no student to speak for a nation, and run no land-claim game. Read in chunks; describe places by the river and the states. An unfamiliar word is a language gap, not error D or error K: ask the question again in plain words before marking it.

### Tomorrow

Tick the exit's four look-fors. Flag error D (offices confused), error M (one battle ending the war), error N (battles with the same effect) and error K (empty land). Day 80's warm-up returns this exit and checks the s05-P79-1 answers aloud. If more than a third show error D, M or N, that warm-up sorts the offices and the battle dates; error K goes to a small group with Sources E and H. Day 80 documents the men and women who served, the United States Colored Troops among them. Write comments on the organizers; they come back on day 82.
