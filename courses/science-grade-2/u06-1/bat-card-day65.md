# Bat card (day 65)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

Unscored fresh practice, labelled "invented for practice". Pollen, nectar and receiver are named on the picture. There are no arrows and no explanation.

**Picture 1, the bat's head before:** a small bat hanging in front of a tall night flower, its long nose near the flower's middle. Its head hair is clean. Labels: "nectar", "pollen" (the yellow powder at the flower's middle).
**Picture 2, the bat's head after:** the same bat with its nose in the nectar spot. New yellow marks cling to the hairs of its head. The first flower has fewer yellow marks at its middle.
**Picture 3, a second flower of the same kind, before:** its middle holds a patch with no yellow marks. Label: "receiver".
**Picture 4, the second flower after the bat's visit:** the receiver now has yellow marks that match the marks on the bat's head, and fewer marks remain on the bat's head.
**Say:** "Draw the route. Label what moves, and say which part of the bat makes it possible."
**Key (teacher):** the pollen moves from the first flower to the bat's hairy head to the second flower's receiver; the hairy head makes it possible. The bat visits for nectar, which is food, not to help the plant (error C). The pollen is not a seed (error A). Nothing is built. Unscored.
