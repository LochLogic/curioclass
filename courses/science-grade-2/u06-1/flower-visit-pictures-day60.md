# Flower-visit pictures (day 60)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

Two pictures to show beside the sock. Where flowers are open, the teacher's own dated photographs of real flower visits may stand in, taken from a distance; never handle a live bee. Where they are not, use these drawn pictures. Neither picture shows an animal leaving one flower and arriving at another, and neither names a cause.

**Picture 1:** a round bumble bee with a fuzzy body standing on a wide, flat, daisy-like flower. Its head is pushed into the middle of the flower. Small yellow specks cling to its fuzzy back and legs. A label under the picture reads "bee on a flower".
**Picture 2:** a bumble bee hanging at the opening of a bell-shaped flower on a stem. Its head and front legs are inside the bell and a few yellow specks show on its back. A label reads "bee on a bell flower".
**Say:** "Here are two flowers with a bee on each. What do you see? What do you wonder?" Take ideas, accept none, and write the notices as questions on the question board.
**Key (teacher):** the bee goes for food, nectar or pollen. Yellow powder, pollen, rubs onto its body and may be left on the next flower it visits. Seeds and pollen differ. Do not explain on day 60 until the class has looked and sketched; the explanation is told after the lens turn.
