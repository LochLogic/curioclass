# Section 6.1 opener: models of animals that carry seeds and pollen

## What the section does

Six days, 60 to 65. Day 60 starts from a fuzzy sock with burrs on it, or a hook-and-loop stand-in said aloud to be a model, beside two pictures of bees at flowers. Children sketch two routes, one for a seed and one for yellow powder. Day 61 builds a sock model that carries a card seed along a mat and reads where it stops, to the whole centimeter. Day 62 tests a smooth seed beside a hooked one, then carries paper dots from one card flower to another. Days 63 and 64 are observed: each child builds and improves, alone, a model of a new animal's job. Day 65 returns the work, repairs by letter and closes both anchor questions.

## How the days run

Days 61 and 62 open with a 3 minute return and repair of the day before's exit. Read every stem aloud, one ask at a time, and say every picture is invented for practice. Six rotating children are heard each day, 15 to 20 seconds each, and the sheet names who. Mark with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Children read only short labels. Days 60 to 62 are never scored. Day 63 is feedback, day 64 is revision, and the retained day 64 work is scored on day 65 before class on ten components, all on the new case.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A model here does what the animal does.** In grade 1 children copied a part of nature. Now the model carries something from one place to another. The word model also meant a place on the unit 1 chart. Say which sense you mean.
- **Hooks catch loops.** A burr has tiny hooks. Fuzzy cloth is made of loops. Hook-and-loop squares do the same job, and are said aloud to be a model, not a real burr.
- **A beak can carry a seed.** Some birds hold a seed in the beak, fly, and may leave it in a new place. A beak holds by closing and lets go by opening, so hooks are not the only way a seed travels.
- **Pollen is not a seed.** Pollen is the yellow powder a flower makes. A bee visits for food, some powder rubs onto its body, and may be left on the next flower. The bee does not mean to help.
- **Not every seed sticks, and not every plant needs an animal.** A smooth seed falls from the sock. Neither job applies to every plant.
- **A model shows carrying, not growing.** Every model has this limit.

## Safety and set-up

Everyone wears fitted eye protection when handling model pieces. Keep pieces large and below faces, away from mouths and hair; tie hair back. Only an adult handles stored burrs or seed heads, enclosed in a seal; discard any that are mouldy. Check allergies beforehand. Use paper dots only: no chalk, loose dust, drink powders or real pollen, and no blowing. No live bees or other animals are ever handled. The adult folds and covers the ends of pipe cleaners, trials every set the day before, and swaps any set that fails. With no sock or hook-and-loop squares, use the pictures and cards; the record says which components could not be shown.
