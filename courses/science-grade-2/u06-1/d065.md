# Day 65: Repair, and answer both questions

## Teacher page

### Before class

Write each child's group on the feedback slip. Set a repair set at each group table. Pin the carry-arrow chart. Choose today's six.

### Safety and set-up

Eye protection on. Pieces below faces, away from mouths. Check pipe-cleaner ends; no bare wire. No live animals. Clean-up, 2 minutes: bag pieces, wipe tables, store models.

### The day

#### 5 min. Scored work returned

Each child gets their model and day 64's assessment record with one next step, read or heard (3 minutes). Six rotating children say theirs back, 20 seconds each (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes).

#### 12 min. Repair by letter groups

Cards are invented. Groups follow the day 63 and 64 records and the day 62 exit (error D). Each group is with you 2.5 minutes in turn while the rest work with a partner from the picture directions; 2 minutes to move (4 x 2.5 + 2). Group A: the route cards; label seed and powder apart, revise the labels. Group B: test whether the model moves a card between places, then repair it. Group C: trace a nectar visit and the powder left. Group D: smooth and hooked seeds on one cloth; limit the every-seed claim. If many made one letter, it takes the first 3 minutes with the whole class and each other group takes 2 (3 + 3 x 2 + 2). One child follows the steps with an adult. A child with no gap tells a partner what each model part stands for.

#### 9 min. Fresh practice

The bat card, invented for practice. Each child draws the route and labels what moves and which part makes it possible; nothing is built (6 minutes). Six rotating children say it, 30 seconds each (6 x 30 s = 3 minutes). Unscored. Expect: pollen goes from the first flower to the bat's head to the second flower's receiver; the bat visits for food.

#### 4 min. Consensus

The class dictates; you write, citing days. Aim for: hooks catch fibers and carry seeds away; a jay carries a seed in its beak; bees visit for food and powder that sticks may be left on the next flower; pollen is not a seed; not every plant needs an animal for either job; models show carrying, not growing.

**Ask.** How did the burrs get on the sock, and why do bees visit flower after flower? Expect: hooks caught the fuzz; bees go for food and powder may be left.

#### 3 min. Exit

Read [[s06-X65a]]; all touch (0.5 minute). Read [[s06-X65b]]; all touch (0.5). Six rotating children say why, 15 seconds each (6 x 15 s = 1.5 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Clean up

As in Safety and set-up.

### Marking

After class, 20 minutes: mark the exit and record each repair apart. Never rescore day 64.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw each card on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** Two drawn flowers for the cards.

**Skip.** The letter groups and the class consensus.

- **5 min. Note.** Read the written next step together.
- **10 min. Repair.** Redo the weakest piece of the jay model.
  **Say.** "What does this part stand for, and what does it do?" Expect: a part named for the beak, acorn or oak, and what it does.
- **10 min. Bat.** Draw the bat card's route and label what moves.
  **Say.** "Where does the yellow powder start, ride and stay?" Expect: first flower, the bat's head, the second flower.
- **5 min. Say it.** How did the burrs get on the sock, and why do bees visit flower after flower?
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X65a]] and [[s06-X65b]]. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one repair group. Never cut the exit. Longer: change one model part and predict.

### Words and supports

Hook, seed, pollen, nectar, food, receiver, carry. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing and drawing are accepted.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who moves the wings, not the patch (error B), swaps dots for seeds (error A) or says the moth goes to help (error C). Day 66 starts a new unit: surveying two schoolyard spots and a picture inventory of plants and animals.
