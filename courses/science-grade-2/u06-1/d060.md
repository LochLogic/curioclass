# Day 60: How did the burrs get on the sock?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 59 leaves no gap. Seal one dry seed head in a clear bag and tie it to a sock (no seed head: stick on three hook-and-loop squares; no pins). Check allergies. Set one lens a group. Pin the carry-arrow chart and a questions chart. Choose today's six.

### Safety and set-up

Only an adult handles the seed head, and it stays sealed. Eye protection on when the sock is handled. Plastic lenses only. No live bees, real pollen or chalk.

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook

Show the sock beside the two flower-visit pictures. Say the pictures are invented for practice and a stand-in is a model. Ask: "How did the burrs get all over my sock, and why do bees visit flower after flower?" Explain nothing.

**Ask.** What do you notice? Expect: any idea; pointing counts. Write each notice as a question on the class questions chart.

#### 4 min. Retrieval

Card 1: "Mark the covering that could catch a hook." Expect the fuzzy cloth. Card 2: five design-cycle cards out of order; "Show the pictures in order." Expect problem, sketch, build, test, compare. Two minutes a card. After a miss, show the copied-part picture from grade 1 and the unit 2 chart. Say: here a model copies what the animal does, not its parts.

#### 8 min. Look, then explain

Look aloud at the sock through a lens, never at the sun or a bright window (2). Each group has one lens: five children, 30 seconds each, while the rest look at the pictures (3 minutes). Pairs say how they think the burrs held on and why the bee visits; take three ideas (1). Then name what they found (2): hooks catch the loops of fuzzy fibers, so the seed rides. A bee visits a flower for food. Yellow powder, pollen, rubs onto its body and may be left on the next flower. Seeds and pollen are not the same.

#### 13 min. First model

On the carry-arrow chart draw one arrow: plant, animal, new place (3 minutes). Each child sketches both routes on the two-box sheet, placing plant, animal, seed and pollen (6 minutes). Partners point and explain, as in grade 1; the author redraws (2 minutes). The chart gets two arrows and the new questions go up (2 minutes).

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the cards are invented for practice. Read [[s06-X60a]] once; all touch a card (0.5 minute). Hand out the paper ticket, read [[s06-X60b]] once; all draw (1 minute). Six rotating children say where their arrow goes, 15 seconds each (6 x 15 s = 1.5 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Clean up

The adult bags the specimen; wipe tables; hands are washed at the next scheduled washing, said aloud.

### Other rooms

No sock: use squares or the pictures. No printer: draw two boxes on scrap paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No hand lens: look close. No bee pictures: draw a bee on a flower.

**Skip.** The lens turns, the chart and the partner talk.

- **5 min. Look.** Look at a fuzzy sock or scarf and a flower together. Wonder aloud.
  **Say.** "What do you notice? What do you wonder?" Expect: any idea; fuzz, yellow dust or a bee.
- **10 min. Tell.** Tell it: hooks catch fuzz, so a seed rides. A bee visits for food, and yellow powder may be left on the next flower.
- **15 min. Draw.** Draw both routes on the student page.
  **Say.** "Which path is the seed and which is the powder?" Expect: the seed rides the animal to a new place; the powder may be left on the next flower.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X60a]] and [[s06-X60b]]. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one lens turn a group. Never cut the two routes or the exit.

### Words and supports

Seed, pollen, hook, loop, carry, model, flower. Frame: "I notice ___. I wonder ___." Pointing is accepted.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who calls the powder a seed (error A). Day 61 begins with two route cards for error A, then builds a sock model that carries a card seed and reads where it stops, in whole centimeters.
