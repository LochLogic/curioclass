# Day 21: Watch the wind trials

## Teacher page

### Before class

The day before, run the fan on low over a spare damp-sand tray and log the wetness and fan place. Tape a fixed line and a 0 on each tray. Write the day 20 class gap on the board. Choose six readers, one a group.

### Safety and set-up

Only you run the fan, on low, fingers clear, cord dry. Sand stays damp and side-shielded; never dry it. Goggles on through clean-up; children stay behind the taped line. Stop if dust lifts. Wash hands.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back the day 20 exits, s02-X20a and s02-X20b. The more missed of error C and error E takes the five minutes; the other goes to a card at the table. Error C: write "The tall wall will win, it looks strongest." Draw invented bars: 6 squares by shape card 1, 3 by shape card 3.

**Ask.** Which barrier lost less? Expect: the row of sticks; the bars decide, not the look.

Error E: show the fair-plan card; **Ask.** What stays the same? Expect: tray, soil, wetness, fan place. Arrows straight through the shapes: draw one bending round a stick on shape card 3.

#### 3 min. Set up and safety

Goggles on. Show the taped line. Only you touch the fan; "stop" means everyone freezes. The windbreak uses the gap on the board.

#### 17 min. Wind trials

Six runs: bare, windbreak, wall, then the same three again. Lay a strip with its 0 at the fixed line, run the fan on low for a slow count of sixty, stop, and pencil the new sand edge on the strip. A reader from a different group each run reads the retreat to the whole centimeter, aloud; every group writes it down. A run takes about 2.5 minutes (6 x 2.5 = 15); 2 more smooth the sand. Measure the path width of the windbreak and wall once.

**Ask.** Why do we keep the tray, the sand wetness and the fan place the same? Expect: so only the barrier changes.

A difference of 1 centimeter is about the same; wind scatters more than water. Fallback cards: bare 9 and 8, windbreak 3 and 4, wall 2 and 6; path used, windbreak 3, wall 5.

#### 6 min. Compare

Pairs say one thing each barrier did well and poorly. Hear six (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes).

**Ask.** Which barrier did better against wind, and what did it do poorly? Expect (cards): both beat bare sand; the windbreak was steady, 3 and 4; the wall lost 2 then 6 and takes 5 of the path.

Add wind arrows to the class change model: solid where sand was seen to move, dotted where we think the wind went but did not measure.

#### 3 min. Clean-up

You sweep the sand into the lined bin. Goggles wiped and bagged. Hands washed, two waves, two sinks.

#### 3 min. Board

Answer "What protects against wind?" from the chart. Leave open: "Would sticks set closer protect more?"

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the cards are invented. Hand out the day 21 cards. Read [[s02-X21]] once; everyone points. Hear six (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

No fan or trays: use the wind trial cards. No printer: draw the bars on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No trays or fan: use the six invented numbers below. No ruler: count squares on squared paper.

**Skip.** The readers and the record sheet.

- **10 min. The records.** Read the bars: bare 9 and 8, windbreak 3 and 4, wall 2 and 6. Path used: windbreak 3, wall 5. Your child writes them on the page.
  **Say.** "Which barrier did better, and what did it do poorly?" Expect: the windbreak was steady; the wall was good once, then poor, and uses more path.
- **10 min. Fair.**
  **Say.** "Why keep the tray and the fan place the same?" Expect: so only the barrier changes.
- **6 min. Say it.** Your child finishes the sentence on the page.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Your child does [[s02-X21]], alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: four runs, bare and windbreak. Never cut the exit. Longer: draw where sand piles.

### Words and supports

Wind, windbreak, retreat, path width used, fair. Frame: "It did well at ___ and poorly at ___." A scribe is fine.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who picks the barrier that looks strongest (error C). Day 22 opens with error C in 5 minutes: match two barriers to their result bars. Then children turn today's numbers into bars on squared paper.
