# Day 25: Which screen for this problem?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Keep the wall covered and children seated apart. Print the packet and the student page. Set the record sheet with six columns, one for each item below. The day 23 and day 24 records are kept, not collected again. Papers are read before day 26: no reteach, no hints.

### Safety and set-up

Paper only. Nothing to wear, nothing to clean.

### The day

#### 5 min. Directions

Say the packet is invented for practice. Read the whole prompt neutrally, one step at a time, with a pause after each. Say the problem: two screens protect the same soil strip in equal wind trials, and we may lose at most 5 centimeters in each run. Step 1: what screen P does well, [[s02-E7]]. Step 2: what P does poorly, [[s02-E8]]. Step 3: what screen Q does well, [[s02-E9]]. Step 4: what Q does poorly, [[s02-E10]]. Step 5: mark the four runs, [[s02-E11]]. Step 6: choose and draw, [[s02-E12]]. Name neither screen as the winner.

#### 25 min. The packet

Each child works alone on their own paper. Steps 1 to 4 are a few words on a line; step 5 marks four runs; step 6 draws the four records against the line at 5 on blank model paper and says why. No group talks. You may read a line again on request, once. A scribe or an oral answer is allowed for any child whose plan says so, 30 seconds a step, six children at most (6 x 6 steps x 30 s = 18 minutes, while the others write). Mark six look-fors on one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Only "shown" counts a point; record help apart from independent work.

1. Names P's low first run, 1 centimeter, as its strength.
2. Names P's second run, 7, as over the limit of 5.
3. Names Q's equal runs, 4 and 4, as under the limit of 5.
4. Names Q's first run, 4, as more than the 1 that P lost.
5. Marks all four runs against the limit: P 1 under, P 7 over, Q 4 under, Q 4 under.
6. Chooses Q and draws the four records against the line at 5, with the reason that Q meets the limit in both runs.

#### 5 min. Check and collect

Check names on every paper and collect the packets and model papers. Read nothing aloud and score nothing in front of the class.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the two screens and their bars on the board and read them aloud; children write on plain paper. One adult: children work alone anyway.

### At home

**Swap.** No packet: draw two screens, P with bars 1 and 7 and Q with bars 4 and 4, and a line across them at 5.

**Skip.** The record sheet and the scribe.

- **25 min. The assessment.** Read the prompt aloud one step at a time and wait. Your child fills the four lines and does [[s02-E11]] and [[s02-E12]] alone. Give no hint.
- **10 min. Look together.**
  **Say.** "What does screen P do poorly?" Expect: in the second run it lost 7, over the limit of 5.
- **10 min. Choose again.**
  **Say.** "Which screen would you choose, and why?" Expect: Q, because both its runs, 4 and 4, are under 5.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: none; the check is not shortened. Longer: none; nothing is added.

### Words and supports

Screen, limit, over, under, strength, weakness. Frames: "It does well at ___." "It does poorly at ___." Read aloud, large print, a scribe, an oral answer or pointing all count.

### Tomorrow

Day 25 is not scored in class. Each child gets their day 23, 24 and 25 records back on day 26, each with one written next step. Error D, the screen that wins has no weakness, goes to the day 26 design group, with the day 25 papers. Day 26 repairs by letter group and retries only the missed part.

### Credit

Ours, all of it.
