# Day 22: Two limits, a second tray

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy the day 21 second-run numbers onto the board (cards: bare 8, windbreak 4, wall 6). Pin the day 19 water bars and limit line. Choose two different sixes, for Say it and the exit. Key: key-cards-exits-21-22.

### Safety and set-up

Paper only. Nothing is poured and no fan runs.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back the day 21 exits, s02-X21. Error C: write "The thick fence will win, it looks strongest." Draw invented bars beside two pictures: thick fence 7 squares, thin mesh 3.

**Ask.** Which lost less? Expect: the thin mesh; the bars decide, not the look.

Pairs find one barrier on the pinned water bars and say which bar decides.

#### 6 min. Strips become bars

Pairs draw the three second-run wind numbers as bars on squared paper, one square for each centimeter, beside the pinned water bars. Draw a limit line at 6 across them.

**Ask.** Which bars go past the line? Expect: bare, at 8; the windbreak and the wall stop at or under it.

#### 6 min. A second tray

Say the card is invented. Show the second tray card: the same soil, wetness and fan place, a reed screen (2 and 3, path 4) and a board panel (4 and 4, path 1). The limits are: lose at most 4 in a run, and use at most 3 of the path. Mark each run and the path aloud, one barrier at a time.

**Ask.** Does the choice carry over to this tray, and which barrier meets both limits? Expect: the board panel; the reed screen uses 4 of the path, over 3.

#### 12 min. Compare alone

Write on the board, invented: a field windbreak lost 2 then 3 and uses 7 of the path; a field wall lost 5 then 6 and uses 4. The limits are: lose at most 6 in a run, and use at most 5 of the path. Pairs mark each number at or under the limit, or over it, draw the bars and both limit lines, and finish the sentence on the page. Visit six pairs (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes).

**Ask.** Which meets both limits? Expect: the wall.

**Ask.** What does the windbreak do well? Expect: it loses least, 2 and 3; it fails the path limit.

#### 5 min. Pip's claim

Pip says, one line at a time: "The one that lost least is always best." Children test it against the limit lines. No child is the mistake.

**Ask.** Is the one that lost least always the best choice? Expect: no; the windbreak lost least but uses 7 of the path, over 5.

#### 3 min. Say it

Six children say "It helps when ___ and fails when ___", 30 seconds each (6 x 30 s = 3 minutes). Any child may pass.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the card is invented. Read [[s02-X22]] once; everyone points to the design that meets both limits and whispers the numbers to a partner. Hear six (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

No squared paper: rule squares on plain paper. No pinned bars: draw them on the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No squared paper: rule squares on plain paper.

**Skip.** The second tray card and the record sheet.

- **8 min. Bars.** Draw bars for 8, 4 and 6, one square for each centimeter, and a line at 6.
  **Say.** "Which bars go past the line?" Expect: the 8.
- **12 min. Two limits.** A windbreak lost 2 then 3 and uses 7 of the path; a wall lost 5 then 6 and uses 4. Lose at most 6; use at most 5.
  **Say.** "Which meets both limits?" Expect: the wall; the windbreak uses 7, over 5.
- **6 min. Say it.** Your child finishes the sentence on the page.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Your child does [[s02-X22]], alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one limit, then the other. Never cut the weakness or the exit. Longer: a third barrier on the same limits.

### Words and supports

Bar, limit, meets, helps, fails. Frame: "It helps when ___ and fails when ___." A scribe is fine.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who says the one that lost least has no weakness (error D). Days 23 to 25 are observation days with no reteach: read the slips before day 26, where error D joins the design group. Day 23 begins with directions for the first packet.
