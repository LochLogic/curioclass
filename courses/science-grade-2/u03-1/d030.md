# Day 30: How far does the water climb?

## Teacher page

### Before class

The day before, an adult fills each tray to its marked line from one equal cup, cuts equal strips and pieces with dull edges, and sets three equal cups a group. Groups 1, 3 and 5 take towel, fabric and foil for the hat test; groups 2, 4 and 6 take wool or its substitute, plastic and rubber. Choose Today's six.

### Safety and set-up

Splash goggles on for all. Pieces are new, fabric and paper only, foil edges dull. Rubber is nonlatex. Any child may use the substitute fabric for wool or rubber. Pour slowly; wipe spills at once.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 29's exit, error C takes this part. Show the repair card, one cup of water in three pictures: ice shaped like the cup, partly melted, flowing. **Ask.** Is it the same water in all three? Expect: yes; only how it holds its shape changed. Neighbours say what stayed the same.

#### 3 min. Plan

Ruler zero, whole centimeters and equal start are review: zero sits on the water line; one strip size, one tray line, one pour. Change only the material. Watch two things: how far the water climbs, and whether the paper tab under a hat piece stays dry, damp or wet. The tab stands for the doll. Climbing and keeping dry are different questions.

#### 4 min. Set the strips

Goggles on. Stand the six strips with their ends at the line; the climb starts now.

#### 11 min. Hat test

Each group tests three pieces on dry tabs. One child pours a cup slowly over each piece, three pours by three children; the other two lift the piece and mark the tab dry, damp or wet. The record sheet says dry or wet: write damp in the box when it is damp. Wipe spills at once.

#### 6 min. Read the strips

After about 10 minutes of climbing, one reader a group reads each strip to the whole centimeter; a strip wet to the top reads "top". For each material every group holds up a number card; write the number most groups show (1 centimeter apart is about the same). Do the same for each hat tab. If no strip climbs, say so aloud and use the invented strip and hat records, said to be invented, as if measured.

#### 3 min. Compare

Set the two lists side by side. **Ask.** The water climbed this strip. Did it keep the paper dry? Are those the same question? Expect: no; climb shows water going in, the tab shows what a hat does. Hear Today's six, 30 seconds each (6 x 30 s = 3 minutes).

#### 5 min. Dry tables and clean up

Two waves of three groups: one wave wipes tables and goggles while the other washes hands at two sinks (15 children, 20 seconds each, 2.5 minutes); then they swap.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the pictures are invented. Show the day 30 exit picture, read [[s03-X30]] once; everyone touches a strip. Hear Today's six, 20 seconds each (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

No trays: use the invented strip and hat records.

### At home

**Swap.** No ruler: a strip of paper marked in centimeters. No goggles: pour small amounts and stay seated.

**Skip.** The six groups and the class chart.

- **8 min. Strips.** Stand a paper towel strip and a plastic strip in a shallow dish of water, ends at a line.
- **10 min. Hat test.** Lay a piece of towel, then plastic, over a dry paper tab; pour a little water.
  **Say.** "Is the tab dry, damp or wet?" Expect: towel wet; plastic dry.
- **8 min. Read.** After 10 minutes, read each strip from the water line.
  **Say.** "How far did the water climb? Is that the same as keeping the tab dry?" Expect: no; they are different questions.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Ask [[s03-X30]] aloud, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: three strips and three pieces. Never cut the two readings or the exit. Longer: add a piece of newspaper.

### Words and supports

Water climb, centimeter, zero, dry, damp, wet. Frame: "The water climbed ___ centimeters." A scribe is fine.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who says no climb means the most water went in (error F). Day 31 opens with 5 minutes of return and repair for it, then the class draws the climbs as bars on squared paper from the class record chart.
