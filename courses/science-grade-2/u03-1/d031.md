# Day 31: Which material makes the hat?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy the day 30 class record chart where all can see it. Draw the choice model on the board: job, the property we tested, what the test showed, our choice. Draw the first bar's axes on the board with the bottom line as zero. Fold a day 31 folder for each pair. Choose Today's six.

### Safety and set-up

Paper only. Nothing is poured.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 30's exit, error F takes this part. Show the day 31 repair card, two pictured strips: on strip 1 a wet mark climbs inside; on strip 2 water pools outside with a dry strip above. Children trace where the water went. **Ask.** Which strip took water in? Expect: strip 1; on strip 2 the water stayed outside.

#### 3 min. Retrieval

Say the bar picture is invented. Show the apple and pear bars. **Ask.** Which bar is taller, and by how many squares? Expect: apples, 2 squares. A miss: count squares aloud on a second graph.

#### 12 min. Graph together

One square is one centimeter, and the bottom line is ruler zero (review). You draw the first bar from the class record chart and label it. Pairs draw the other five and label them. A strip wet to the top gets the full bar. Write dry, damp or wet beside each bar from the chart. Visit pairs.

#### 8 min. Choose

Pairs fill the choice model, copying words from the strips: the job is the rain hat; the property we tested; what the test showed; our choice. Add to the structure and function line: the shape fits the job, and so does the material. Show the tallest bar next to its wet tab.

**Ask.** The water climbed farthest here. Is that the best hat? Expect: no; the tab got wet, and a hat that takes water in is not the same as a hat that keeps the doll dry (error H). Any material whose tab stayed dry fits; pairs choose one.

#### 4 min. Question board

Post, as on the grade 1 board: Would a different job change our choice? Children name jobs; you write them. No answer today.

#### 3 min. Say it

Hear Today's six, 30 seconds each (6 x 30 s = 3 minutes): "I choose ___ because ___." The rest finish the student page frame by drawing or by scribe. Judge with three look-fors: uses the dry or wet result, not liking; names the property tested; says water climbing alone does not decide.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the pictures are invented. Read [[s03-X31a]] once; children draw two bars on the blank grid (1 minute). Show the day 31 exit picture, read [[s03-X31b]] once; everyone touches a hat and whispers what tells them to a partner. Hear Today's six, 15 seconds each (6 x 15 s = 1.5 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A slip in the drawing gets a note, no letter.

#### 2 min. Hand in

Graph, choice model, records and slips into the day 31 folder.

### Other rooms

No squared paper: rule squares on plain paper. No printer: draw the choice model on paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No squared paper: rule squares on plain paper.

**Skip.** The pairs and the question board (review from grade 1).

- **12 min. Bars.** Draw bars for 8, 5, 3, 0, 0 and 0 centimeters, one square each (invented numbers; or use your own).
  **Say.** "Which bar is tallest? Is that the best hat?" Expect: the 8; no, its tab was wet.
- **8 min. Choose.** Your child fills the choice model.
  **Say.** "Which would you use for a hat, and why?" Expect: one whose tab stayed dry.
- **5 min. Student page.** Your child finishes the frame.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Ask [[s03-X31a]] and [[s03-X31b]] aloud, alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: three bars, not six. Never cut the wet-tab question or the exit. Longer: sort the materials by the job they would suit.

### Words and supports

Bar, zero, climb, dry, damp, wet, job, choose. Frame: "I choose ___ for the hat because the paper stayed ___ and the water climbed ___." A scribe is fine.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who says the hat that takes in water is best (error H). Day 32 opens with 5 minutes of return and repair for it, then uses towel records to choose between materials for a spilled-water pad and says why the rain-hat choice may change.
