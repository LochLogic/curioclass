# Section 3.1 opener: what is a material like?

## What the section does

Five days, 27 to 31. A paper-doll hat goes soggy, and the class asks why some materials suit some jobs. Children feel, bend, press and shine a light through new samples, watch water in two conditions, and run equal tests of water travel and rain hats in whole centimeters. On day 31 they graph the hat results together on squared paper and choose a hat from what the tests showed. Every day ends with an exit, and the day 27 exit is practice only.

## How the days run

Days 28 to 31 open with five minutes of return and repair of the last exit, one error letter. Read every stem aloud, one ask at a time. Mark with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Children read only short labels. You prepare the sealed cups, the damp hat and the strip trays; children never open a sealed cup.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A property is something we can observe or test.** Colour, rough or smooth, hard or soft, bends or stays stiff, and lets light through or blocks it.
- **One property does not tell us another.** Two things the same colour can bend differently.
- **Ice is water that is solid.** The same water flows when it is warmer.
- **A fair test changes one thing.** Everything else, such as the place of the lamp, the line, the cup and the time, stays the same.
- **Taking in water and keeping a doll dry are different jobs.** The job decides which test result matters.
- **Length is the only number.** Whole centimeters from ruler zero; 1 centimeter apart is about the same.

## Safety and set-up

Samples are new, with blunt edges. No glass and no sharp metal in student hands; wool is swapped for another fabric if it irritates a child. Press gently with a blunt stick below faces. Never shine a light into eyes. Warm water is lukewarm and sealed by an adult, and ice is never tasted. Keep a cloth at hand and wipe spills at once.
