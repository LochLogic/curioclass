# Key: scenes-day72

Teacher only. Do not print for children.

- **Land and water.** Meadow: land and the ground. Stream edge: water and its banks. Marsh: shallow water and plants. Meadow has no water: land only is right. A usable method is any named order or two-pass way (left to right, top to bottom, plants first then animals) that draws each plant kind and each animal kind once.
- **Inventories.** Meadow: plants grass, clover; animals butterfly, ant. Stream edge: plants grass, willow; animals frog, snail. Marsh: plant reed; animals duck, frog. Each kind counts once.
- **Counts.** Meadow four kinds, stream edge four, marsh three. A graph with one picture for each kind shows 4, 4 and 3. Ten kinds for the meadow comes from counting all ten pictures (error A).
- **Comparing.** Meadow and stream edge are equal at four. They share grass. Only the meadow shows clover, a butterfly or an ant; only the stream edge shows willow, a frog or a snail. For alike, a shared kind, the same count or "both have plants and animals" counts; for different, one kind must be named. Equal counts do not mean identical kinds (error B). The marsh has the fewest.
- **A limit.** The pictures show only what is drawn. Yes, the marsh can have kinds this picture does not show; no snail in the marsh picture does not mean no snail could live there (error C).
- Scientific names earn nothing. Accept descriptive drawings and labels.
