# Wet and dry pair (days 71 and 73)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

For repairing error D, wet places always have more kinds. An invented pair, wet beside dry, with equal counts. It opens the anchor answer on day 71 for the whole class and is group D's case on day 73. The case is new and is not any exit. One child follows the same steps one to one with an adult.

**Card W, a rain garden.** Water in a shallow dip. Two identical irises, two identical dragonflies and one water strider. Three kinds.
**Card G, a gravel lot.** Dry gravel. Two identical thistles, two identical crickets and one pigeon. Three kinds.
**Say:** "One place is wet and one is dry. Circle the kinds on each card and count your circles. Which place has more kinds?"
