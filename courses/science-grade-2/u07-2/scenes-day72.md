# Three scenes for the day 72 record

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

Withheld until day 72 and printed only on that day, one set a child, nothing shared. Every kind is drawn whole and the same way in every scene, and told apart from the others by shape, not by colour alone. Nothing is hidden, cut off or unclear. Each scene shows its land, bank or water. These are constructed scenes, not survey results and not claims about all such places. The title of each scene is printed above it: Scene 1, the meadow; Scene 2, the stream edge; Scene 3, the marsh.

**Scene 1, the meadow, ten pictures, four kinds.** Dry open ground. Three identical grass tufts, two identical clover plants, two identical butterflies and three identical ants.
**Scene 2, the stream edge, nine pictures, four kinds.** A stream with a bank. Two grass tufts drawn exactly as in scene 1, two identical willow shrubs, two identical frogs and three identical snails.
**Scene 3, the marsh, seven pictures, three kinds.** Shallow water among plants. Three identical reed clumps, two identical ducks and two frogs drawn exactly as in scene 2.

## Key (teacher)

- **Land and water.** Meadow: land and the ground. Stream edge: water and its banks. Marsh: shallow water and plants. A usable method scans each scene in an order, for example left to right, and draws each plant kind and each animal kind once.
- **Inventories.** Meadow: plants grass, clover; animals butterfly, ant. Stream edge: plants grass, willow; animals frog, snail. Marsh: plant reed; animals duck, frog. Each kind counts once.
- **Counts.** Meadow four kinds, stream edge four, marsh three. A graph with one picture for each kind shows 4, 4 and 3. Ten kinds for the meadow comes from counting all ten pictures (error A).
- **Comparing.** Meadow and stream edge are equal at four. They share grass. Only the meadow shows clover; only the stream edge shows willow. Equal counts do not mean identical kinds (error B). The marsh has the fewest.
- **A limit.** The pictures show only what is drawn. Other kinds may be unseen, hidden or outside the picture; no snail in the marsh picture does not mean no snail could live there (error C).
- Scientific names earn nothing. Accept descriptive drawings and labels.
