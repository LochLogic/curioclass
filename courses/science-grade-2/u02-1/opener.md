# Section 2.1 opener: how can a place look the same and still change?

## What the section does

Five days, 11 to 15. The class meets the question "How can land look the same every day and still change?" and learns to tell a fast change from a slow one using two sources each time: a picture and the words that say when. On day 11 children notice a worn path beside grass. On day 12 they study four fast changes. On day 13 they test the same checks on a second place. On day 14 they study four slow changes. On day 15 they sort the path evidence and see what a tray can and cannot show. Nothing is scored in this section. Each day ends with one exit that you read and hand back.

## How the days run

Day 11 opens with a five minute hook. Days 12 to 15 open with five minutes of return and repair: hand back the last exit, show one letter most children made, and fix it once. Read each stem aloud, one ask at a time. Children point, draw or say a few words. Mark each exit with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Land changes, fast or slow.** A flood, a slide or an eruption can change land in a day. Wind, rain and feet can change it a little each season.
- **Look for the words that say when.** Words like "in one night" or "a little each season" are evidence. A liking or a story is not.
- **A change can be too slow to see on any one day.** Two pictures of the same place, older and later, show it.
- **Erosion** is wind or water wearing land away and carrying it off.
- **A tray is a model.** It shows how soil moves. It does not show how long a real change took.

We do not count years or give time numbers for landscape change, and we do not talk about moving plates or canyons.

## Safety

Printed cards only in this section. Choose calm pictures; no one is hurt in any card. If a child has lived through a flood or a slide, do not ask for personal accounts, and let any child pass.
