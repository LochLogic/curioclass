# Section 4.1 opener: the same pieces, and changes that return

## What the section does

Four days, 39 to 42. A castle of six blocks becomes a boat, and the class asks which changes can be undone. Children take apart and rebuild blocks, then rounded card shapes, and match the old pieces to the new whole. Then they look at water and chocolate that were warmed and cooled and decide, from a third look, whether the change went back. Every day ends with an exit. The egg, the change that stays, waits for section 4.2.

## How the days run

Days 40 to 42 open with five minutes of return and repair of the last exit, one error letter. Read every stem aloud, one ask at a time, and say that every picture is invented for practice. Mark with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Children read only short labels. You hear a rotating six each day and write who on the record sheet. Three class charts stay on the wall: the parts-and-whole sketch, the change-arrow model and the question board.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A new arrangement is not new pieces.** Match each old piece to its place in the new whole. Matching is counting by lines; no number is said.
- **A piece suits a job by its shape and its stiffness.** A flat piece makes a floor and a round one rolls. A stiff strip keeps its shape and a floppy strip droops.
- **An attempted return is a third look.** We change the temperature back and then look again. Waiting is not an attempted return.
- **A change back need not restore the outline.** Cooled chocolate can be hard in a new shape and still be hard again.
- **Water and chocolate come first.** They are the clear returns, and the egg is the contrast that follows.

## Safety and set-up

All heating is done by an adult before the children come in; children see photographs and sealed cool samples. The adult wears splash goggles at the warm-water bath, and observers stay behind a line. Children never operate the freezer. Check egg and milk allergies first, offer the photograph path to any child, and ask no one what is eaten at home. Nothing is eaten and nothing sealed is opened. Blocks stay on the table, card edges are rounded, spills are wiped at once, and hands are washed at the next scheduled washing.
