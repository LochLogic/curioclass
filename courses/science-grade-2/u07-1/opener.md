# Section 7.1 opener: observing and comparing kinds in different places

## What the section does

Four days, 66 to 69. On day 66 the class looks at two schoolyard spots from the door, guesses whether they hold the same living things, then goes out for five minutes at each spot and draws everything it sees. Day 67 sorts the drawings: repeats are circled, one picture is drawn for each kind, and a picture graph shows how many kinds each spot holds. Day 68 compares a pond card and two forest cards. Day 69 compares a desert card and a shore card, and shows that two places can hold the same number of kinds and still hold different ones.

## How the days run

Days 67 to 69 open with a short return and repair of the day before's exit. Read every stem aloud, one ask at a time, and say that every card is invented for practice. Six rotating children are heard each day, 15 to 20 seconds each, and the record sheet names who. Mark with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Children read only short labels. Days 66 to 69 are practice and are never scored. The five minutes at each spot are kept by the wall clock, so every group looks for the same time.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A habitat here is a place where living things find what they need.** Use no ecology words. A spot by the fence and a pond are both places to look.
- **A kind is a group that look alike.** Ten daisies are one kind. We count kinds, not individuals. A label such as "tall green blade plant" is fine if it tells two kinds apart. A young and an adult animal that look different may get two labels; say so aloud and never score it.
- **People count as animals but are not inventoried.** The children draw what lives at the spot, not each other.
- **Five minutes at one patch sees only some kinds.** Say this before the survey. A kind that was not seen is not a kind that is absent; it may have been hiding.
- **Equal counts can hide different kinds.** Desert and shore each show three kinds, and none are the same.
- **Wet is not always more.** One pond card has more kinds than one forest card, but the shore card has no more than the desert card. This is a pattern in these cards, never a ranking of habitats.
- **Watch only.** Nothing is touched, moved or collected, not even a stone.

## Safety and set-up

The day before the survey an adult checks the paths, the weather, stinging insects, harmful plants, allergies and access. Stop for unsafe conditions and use the matched photographs instead; the record says the photographs are weaker because framing limits what can be seen. Closed shoes, stay on the path, stay in sight, touch and collect nothing. Only adults inspect sites. Twelve rope loops about one metre across, six for each spot, serve one pair a group; thirty boards mean no waiting. Clean-up outdoors takes about four minutes: loops and boards collected, clothing checked for insects in pairs in the line, hands washed at two sinks. If the line runs over, the last children wash at the next scheduled washing, said aloud. A room with nothing needs only the photographs, pencils and paper.
