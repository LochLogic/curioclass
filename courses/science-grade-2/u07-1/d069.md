# Day 69: A desert and a shore

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print five sets of the desert and shore cards a group, a claim strip a child, and the four-habitat chart. Have the equal-count cards ready. Put the day's question on the question board: Can two places with the same count hold different kinds? Choose today's six. Key: key-desert-and-shore-cards-day69.

### Safety and set-up

Indoors; pencils only. No hazard. Count the cards back at the end.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Day 68's exit. Say the cards are invented for practice. Error B (3 minutes): show the two equal-count cards, feeder corner and vegetable bed, three kinds each; the child touches each kind on one card, then the other. Put "same number, so the same" beside "same number, different kinds." Error D (2 minutes): lay the second pond card (three kinds) beside forest A (four); touch a wet place with a small count and a drier place with a bigger one. If many made one letter, it takes the whole class; the rest begin their sets.

#### 10 min. Observe and record

Say the cards are invented for practice, not every desert or shore. Each child has the desert card and the shore card: five minutes a card. On a folded scrap, one row a card: circle repeats, draw one picture per kind, count the kinds. Reuse labels; agree new ones aloud.

#### 12 min. Compare

Four-habitat comparison (8 minutes): groups lay their graphs in the four rows, pond five kinds, forest A four, desert three, shore three. The class finds the shared kind (dragonfly, pond and forest A), the largest count (pond), and one wet place that is not the largest (shore). Say aloud: these are four invented cards, not a rule.

Equal counts (4 minutes): desert and shore each show three.

**Ask.** Both places have three. Are they the same kinds? Expect: no; touch each kind, none match.

#### 8 min. Revise the model

Add to the class sheet "equal counts can hide different kinds" and "wet is not always more". Each child writes or dictates a claim strip, "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___," in 5 minutes. Six rotating children read theirs, 20 seconds each (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes). Add "I only looked at these cards" aloud, then the class sheet (1 minute).

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the cards are invented for practice. Read [[s07-X69a]] once; all touch a card (0.5 minute). Read [[s07-X69b]] once; all touch a kind (0.5 minute). Six rotating children say why, 15 seconds each (6 x 15 s = 1.5 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Cards back into the sets and counted.

### Marking

After class, 10 minutes: read each strip for four things: each kind drawn once, labels that tell kinds apart, a shared or different kind named, a limit named. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice only.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the desert (2 tall cacti, a lizard, 3 stout beetles) and the shore (3 seaweed strands, 2 gulls, a sand worm).

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: compare two places you know, such as a yard and a pot plant.

**Skip.** The groups, the four-row chart and reading aloud.

- **5 min. Look back.** A pond shows three kinds and a forest four.
  **Say.** "Does a wet place always show more kinds?" Expect: no; this pond shows fewer.
- **10 min. Cards.** Draw one picture for each kind in each place; count.
- **8 min. Compare.**
  **Say.** "Both have three kinds. Are they the same kinds?" Expect: no; touch each kind, none match.
- **6 min. Strip.** Write or say "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___."
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** [[s07-X69a]] and [[s07-X69b]]. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one claim strip and the exit. Never cut the equal counts or the exit. Longer: a third card with three kinds.

### Words and supports

Equal counts (two places with the same number of kinds). Frame: "Both have ___. Only ___ has ___." Pointing and drawing are accepted.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who says the bed with more pictures shows more kinds (error A) and who says both show the same kinds (error B). Day 70 repairs both, then repeats the schoolyard surveys.
