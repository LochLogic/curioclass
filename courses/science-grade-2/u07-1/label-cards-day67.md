# Label cards (day 67)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

Eight cards, one living thing each, large enough to hold up. The teacher makes them the day before by copying eight different drawings from the class's own inventory sheets, one kind a card, and writes nothing on the front. If the class's sheets hold fewer than eight kinds, add stand-ins from this set until there are eight. The class agrees one describing label for each card, aloud, and the teacher writes it on the back. Labels are provisional: they tell two kinds apart and are never a name and never scored.

## Stand-in set

1. A tall green blade plant. Label: "tall green blade plant".
2. A low plant with a round yellow flower. Label: "round yellow flower".
3. A small black animal with six legs, walking. Label: "small black walker".
4. A flying insect with a striped body. Label: "striped flyer".
5. A fuzzy brown caterpillar on a stem. Label: "fuzzy brown crawler".
6. A gray bird in flight. Label: "gray fluttering bird".
7. A flat plant with one wide leaf. Label: "flat broad-leaf plant".
8. A green animal with long back legs, mid-hop. Label: "spotted hopper", drawn with dark spots.

**Say:** "Look at the card. What tells it apart from the one we held up before?" Six rotating children answer, 20 seconds each.
**Key (teacher):** any label that tells two cards apart is right, for example "tall green blade plant" and "flat broad-leaf plant". "Plant" alone does not tell two cards apart. A young and an adult animal that look different may get two labels; say so aloud and never score it.
