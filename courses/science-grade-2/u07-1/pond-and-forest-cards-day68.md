# Pond card, forest A and forest B (day 68)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

Three cards, five paper sets a group, one a child. Every kind is drawn the same way wherever it appears, and no living thing is hidden or cut off. The cards show land and water context. They are invented for practice, not survey results and not claims about every pond or forest. Kinds are described by shape, not by name.

**Pond card, nine pictures, five kinds.** Water with a muddy edge. Three identical lily-pad plants floating, two identical tall edge plants with narrow leaves, two identical fish, one dragonfly with long wings and one water beetle with a round shiny back and oar-like legs.
**Forest A, eight pictures, four kinds.** Dry ground under trees. Three identical trees, two identical ferns, one squirrel and two dragonflies. The dragonfly is drawn exactly as on the pond card.
**Forest B, four pictures, two kinds.** Dry ground under trees. Two identical trees, the same tree as in forest A, and two identical long green-black beetles with shiny backs, drawn differently from the pond's water beetle.

**Say:** "Circle the pictures that are the same kind. Draw one picture for each kind. Count your kinds."
