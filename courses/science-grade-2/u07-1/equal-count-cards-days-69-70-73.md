# Equal-count cards (days 69, 70 and 73)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

For repairing error B, equal counts of kinds do not mean the same kinds. Two cards with three kinds each and no kind in common. The cases are new and are not any exit or end item. One child follows the same steps one to one with an adult.

**Card P, a feeder corner.** Three identical sunflowers, two identical chickadees and one green caterpillar. Three kinds.
**Card Q, a vegetable bed.** Two identical cabbages, three identical slugs and one crow. Three kinds.
**Say:** "Each card shows three kinds. Touch each kind on card P, then each kind on card Q. Are they the same kinds?"
**Key (teacher):** three kinds each, and none are the same: sunflower, chickadee and caterpillar against cabbage, slug and crow. Both have three kinds; the kinds are different. The wrong claim, "they have the same number, so they are the same", is shown beside the right one.
