# Matched schoolyard photographs (days 66 and 70)

Drawing style: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words except where a card says so. Children in any picture are of different ages, skin tones and hair, shown as simple figures. Say aloud that every picture is invented for practice, and print "invented for practice" beneath each one.

The route for a site that is unsafe or frozen, or for a child with an allergy. Where the class can, the teacher takes its own photographs of the two spots from the path: the same distance, the same size, one pair a group, dated. Never take a photograph that needs a child to leave the path. Where it cannot, use these drawn stand-ins. Say aloud that a photograph is weaker than looking, because the frame hides some of the place, and write that on the record. Offer the photograph pair to any group that wants to compare it with the outdoor sheet, so the route is not only for the one child.

## Date 1 (day 66)

**Spot 1, a fence corner:** two identical broad-leaf weeds, one curly vine climbing the fence, one spider in a web and two identical flies. Label: "fence corner, first look".
**Spot 2, a planter box:** three identical orange flowers, one green caterpillar on a stem and one small brown moth. Label: "planter box, first look".

## Date 2 (day 70, held back until then)

**Spot 1, a fence corner:** two identical broad-leaf weeds, the curly vine, the spider in its web, one small yellow flower and one green hopping cricket. No flies. Label: "fence corner, second look".
**Spot 2, a planter box:** three identical orange flowers, the small brown moth and one slug on the soil. No caterpillar. Label: "planter box, second look".

**Say (day 66):** "Look at each photograph for one minute. Draw one picture for everything living that you see."
**Key (teacher):** fence corner, date 1: weed, vine, spider and fly, so four kinds. Planter box: orange flower, caterpillar, moth, so three kinds. Date 2: the fence corner shows weed, vine, spider, yellow flower and cricket, five kinds; the planter box shows orange flower, moth and slug, three kinds. Seen on both dates: at the fence corner the weed, the vine and the spider, and at the planter the orange flower and the moth. Seen on one date only: flies and the caterpillar on date 1; the yellow flower, the cricket and the slug on date 2. A kind seen on one date only may have been hiding or may have changed; "we cannot tell" is a finished answer.
