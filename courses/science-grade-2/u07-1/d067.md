# Day 67: How many kinds?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Copy eight different drawings from the class's own day 66 sheets onto eight cards, one kind a card, nothing written on the front. If the sheets hold fewer than eight kinds, add stand-ins from the printed set. Set the leaf pile flap card ready. Write the day's question on the question board: Does seeing more of one thing mean more kinds? Choose today's six.

### Safety and set-up

Indoors; pencils only. No hazard. Cards and sheets go into the group folder at the end.

### The day

#### 3 min. Return and repair

Day 66's exit (error C). Say the card is invented for practice. Show the leaf pile flap card, flap down: "Pip looked and drew no toad. What can Pip say?" Lift the flap. Put "there is no toad here" beside "Pip did not see a toad; one may be hiding." If many missed it, the whole class; otherwise a small group while the rest lay out their sheets.

#### 7 min. Agree labels

Hold up the eight cards one at a time, about 30 seconds each (4 minutes). The class agrees one describing label, such as "small black walker"; hands vote; write it on the back. A label is only words that tell two kinds apart, never a name. A young and an adult that look different may get two labels. Six rotating children say what tells two kinds apart, 20 seconds each (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes).

**Ask.** What tells this one apart from the one before? Expect: size, color, legs or wings.

#### 14 min. Record and graph

Model once on the class sheet: find a thing drawn more than once, circle all of them together, and say that is one kind; we count circles, not pictures. If no repeat was drawn, draw three alike on the board. Each child circles repeats on their own sheet (5 minutes), then draws one picture per kind on the template, one row a spot, plants above and animals below (9 minutes). One picture is one kind; say it aloud. People are not drawn.

#### 8 min. Compare

Groups lay two children's graphs side by side and find a shared kind and a different kind (5 minutes). Each of six groups reports one, 30 seconds each (6 x 30 s = 3 minutes). Frame: "Both have ___. Only ___ has ___." If a pair shares none, say so.

#### 4 min. Revise the model

Add a kinds row for each spot to the class sheet: one picture a kind from the groups' graphs. The class touches and counts each row together, as in kindergarten. Say the question on the board is answered only for these two spots.

#### 2 min. Exit

Say the picture is invented for practice. Read [[s07-X67]] once; all draw on their exit line (1.5 minutes) while six rotating children say how many kinds they drew and why, 15 seconds each (6 x 15 s = 1.5 minutes). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Clean-up

Cards and sheets into the group folder.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw the graph rows on folded paper. A child may point, draw or dictate.

### At home

**Swap.** Plain paper for the graph; cut-out pictures for cards.

**Skip.** The class sheet, groups and the vote.

- **3 min. Look back.** Pip drew no toad in a first look.
  **Say.** "Can Pip say there is no toad?" Expect: no, Pip did not see one; one may be hiding.
- **6 min. Labels.** Choose four drawings; give each words that tell it apart.
  **Say.** "What tells this one apart from that one?" Expect: size, color, legs or wings.
- **13 min. Graph.** Circle repeats, then draw one picture per kind, plants above, animals below, one row a spot.
  **Say.** "Are these the same kind? How many circles?" Expect: circle the same, count circles.
- **6 min. Compare.** Name a kind in both spots and a kind in one.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** [[s07-X67]]. Your child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one spot's graph and the exit. Never cut the exit. Longer: a third spot or a card.

### Words and supports

Kind (living things that look alike), describing label (words that tell one kind from another), repeat (the same kind drawn again). Frame: "Both have ___. Only ___ has ___." Pointing and drawing are accepted.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who counts every picture (error A: seven or four). Day 68 repairs it with a circle-and-count card, then compares a pond card and two forest cards.
