# Section 1.1 opener: where does the rain go?

## What the section does

Four days, 1 to 4. The class meets the question "After a rainstorm, where does the water on our schoolyard go?" and builds a first model of land and water. On day 1 children notice where rain water goes and sketch a route. On day 2 they pour water on a sand tray and draw what it does. On day 3 they build a class model from picture pieces and revise it from their pour records. On day 4 they draw a picture map of a corner of the school grounds and compare it with the tray. Nothing is scored in this section. Each day ends with one exit that you read and hand back.

## How the days run

Days 2 to 4 open with five minutes of return and repair: you hand back the last exit, show one letter most children made, and fix it once. Day 1 opens with ten minutes of hook and question board. Read each stem aloud, one ask at a time. Children draw, point or say a few words. Mark each exit with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Water goes down.** On a slope water runs to the lowest place it can reach and gathers there.
- **A model is a drawing that stands for the real thing.** It shows the shape of the land, the water and the way the water went. Arrows mean "the water went this way." We do not use arrows for anything else.
- **A key tells a reader what each mark means.** A dotted arrow means a route nobody saw.
- **A model is revised, not replaced.** Keep the old drawing beside the new one and date both.

## Safety

Splash goggles for the sand tray on day 2. Use clean damp sand, a small cup of tap water and a cloth. Tip sand into a lined bin, never a drain. Wipe the table and wash hands at clean-up. Hold the pour over the tray, not over the floor.
