# Day 5: Another place, the same pattern

## Teacher page

### Before class

Tape up the class model chart and rule a two-place chart beside it: our schoolyard on the left, the new place on the right. Have the day 2 pour record and the day 4 exits ready. Choose today's six. You speak for Pip, one line at a time. Key: key-cards-exits-5-7.

### Safety and set-up

Paper only. Nothing to wear, nothing to clean.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back the day 4 exit. Teach the one error most children made, error E first. Trace the low route with a finger on the day 2 pour record, then each child redraws the arrow on the exit card. Anyone with error D or error B gets a picture card at their table for the revise part.

#### 5 min. Look at a contrasting place

Show Stone Bay and Dry Ridge together and say both are invented for practice. Read each page aloud. Do [[s01-P5-1]] together: everyone points on your signal. Any child may keep their arm down. A bay is a part of the sea that curves into the land.

#### 6 min. Compare

The class model chart, with its day 3 revision, is the first place, our schoolyard. Point as children talk and fill the right column.

**Ask.** What is the same? Expect: water runs downhill and collects in a low place.

**Ask.** What is different? Expect: a bay, a lake, hills, no puddle on blacktop.

Pip says "every river goes to the ocean." Children do [[s01-P5-2]] against the Dry Ridge page. Expect: the stream ends in the lake, and there is no ocean on that page. On day 3 we saw a river end where the land is lowest. Say it plainly: not every river reaches the ocean; on Dry Ridge it ends in a lake.

#### 12 min. Revise your model

Each child changes their own model once, using Stone Bay or Dry Ridge, and does [[s01-P5-3]]. Then they say and write the frame [[s01-P5-4]]. Hear today's six, 30 seconds each, 3 minutes (6 x 30 s): "Where does the water go in your new place?" Scribe their words and mark who was heard. Picture cards go to error D and error B tables.

#### 4 min. Share the pattern

Pairs say their frame to each other, 20 seconds each. Add one row to the chart: water runs down and collects low; the shapes differ.

#### 3 min. Exit E5

Read the card once, as printed in cards-exits-5-7. Every child does [[s01-X5]]. Mark each: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Question board

Add "Where else is water?"

#### 2 min. Pack up

Models into folders, picture cards back, pencils in.

### Other rooms

No pictures: draw the two places on the board. One projector: show one place at a time.

### At home

**Swap.** No place cards: draw a bay with a stream and a lake with slopes round it. No chart: two boxes on a page.

**Skip.** The pairs, the chart and the rotating six.

- **5 min. Look.** Show Stone Bay and Dry Ridge, and say they are made up. Your child does [[s01-P5-1]].
  **Say.** "Where does Stone Bay's stream end?" Expect: in the bay, the low place.
- **6 min. Pip.** Your child does [[s01-P5-2]].
  **Say.** "Pip says every river goes to the ocean. Is that so on Dry Ridge?" Expect: no, the stream ends in the lake.
- **14 min. Change the model.** Your child does [[s01-P5-3]], then [[s01-P5-4]].
  **Say.** "What is the same as our yard, and what is different?" Expect: water runs down and collects low; the shapes differ.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Your child does [[s01-X5]], alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one place only, and skip the pairs. Never cut Pip's test, the arrow or the exit. Longer: draw a place you know where water collects.

### Words and supports

Bay: part of the sea that curves into the land. Frame: "Water runs ___ and collects ___; the shapes here are ___." Pointing, speaking or a scribe all count.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who draws the water alone with no land round it (error D), and who spreads the water over the meadow, which has no letter and is said in plain words. Day 6 opens with Return and repair of the day 5 exit, error D first. In its talk a child who spread the water over the meadow is asked which part of the meadow is lowest.
