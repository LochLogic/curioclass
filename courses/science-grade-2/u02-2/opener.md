# Section 2.2 opener: can we slow the loss?

## What the section does

Five days, 16 to 20. The class uses the worn path as the problem and tests ways to slow the soil loss in small trays. Day 16 plans a fair test: equal slopes, equal cups, and a paper strip that marks how far the edge moved back. Day 17 builds and tests a card wall and a stone row. Day 18 tests a fabric mat and plants. Day 19 draws the results as bars and reads them against a limit line, which is the most we allow. Day 20 plans wind tests with a stick windbreak and a card wall. Nothing is scored in this section; each day ends with one exit that you read and hand back.

## How the days run

Days 16 to 20 open with four or five minutes of return and repair: hand back the last exit, show one letter most children made, and fix it once. Read each stem aloud, one ask at a time. Children measure with paper strips first, then read the strip with a ruler to the whole centimeter. Teach zero and whole centimeters by modelling; do not use halves. Mark each exit with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A fair test starts equal.** Same slope, same soil, same wetness, same cup. Only the barrier changes.
- **Retreat** is how far the edge moved back, in whole centimeters.
- **A barrier has a strength and a weakness.** Say what it did well and what it did poorly.
- **A limit is the most we allow.** A bar that goes past the line fails that limit.
- **A plan can need two limits.** A barrier that loses little soil but fills the path fails the path limit.

## Safety

Splash goggles for pouring. Spilled water is wiped at once. Wash hands after handling soil or sand. Keep sticks blunt and below faces. Tip soil into a lined bin, never a drain. Wind trials are run by you in section 2.3, not by children.
