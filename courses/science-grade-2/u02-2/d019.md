# Day 19: Results become bars

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut a strip to the class bare number and one to the class wall number. Copy the class middle second-run numbers and the path widths from the chart onto the board. Choose two different sixes, for Retrieval and the exit. Key: key-cards-exits-16-20.

### Safety and set-up

Paper only. Nothing is poured.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back the day 18 exits. Error D: write "The mat lost the least, so it has no weakness." Point to the mat's path width used on the chart.

**Ask.** What does the mat do poorly? Expect: it takes a lot of the path.

Pairs pick another design and mark one thing it does poorly in its own record. An error C goes to a card at the table.

#### 5 min. Retrieval

Show the bar retrieval card: one square is one centimeter. Everyone points; hear six (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes).

**Ask.** Which bar is longer, and how many squares tall is it? Expect: gravel pile, 8.

For a miss, count one bar up one square at a time with a finger.

#### 8 min. The first two bars

Lay the bare strip on squared paper from a bottom line. Count the squares up; draw the bar; label it. Do the wall the same way. Pairs copy both bars onto their sheet's first two columns.

**Ask.** Which bar lost less? Expect: the shorter. Bars 1 square apart are about the same.

#### 10 min. Graph the results

Pairs draw the rocks, mat and plants bars from the day 17 and day 18 middle second-run numbers. Visit six pairs (6 x 20 s = 2 minutes).

**Ask.** Which lost the least? Expect: the shortest bar, from the chart.

No clean chart numbers: use the invented water records, said aloud to be invented (bare 10, wall 4, rocks 6, mat 3, plants 7).

#### 7 min. Limits and the tradeoff

A limit is the most we allow. Draw a line across the bars at 6. A bar that stops at the line is allowed. Lay a ruler along the line; bars that rise above it are over.

**Ask.** Which bars go past the line? Expect: the ones taller than 6.

Draw a second graph of path width used with its line at 4 (invented if needed: wall 8, rocks 3).

**Ask.** Which design would you choose, and what would you give up? Expect: a design that stops more water may use more path.

#### 3 min. Exit

Say the card is invented. Read [[s02-X19]] once; everyone draws on the exit card grid. Collect the cards and mark each from the page: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Close

On the board: what protects against water? Expect: barriers, mats and plants; each does well and poorly; the bars and the limit line help us choose. Pin the graph and limit lines.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw five columns on squared paper. No clean numbers: use the invented water records.

### At home

**Swap.** No sheet: squared paper. No numbers from days 17 and 18: use 10, 4, 6, 3 and 7, said to be invented.

**Skip.** The first two bars and the retrieval card.

- **10 min. Bars.** Draw a bar for each design, one square for each centimeter.
  **Say.** "Which bar lost the least?" Expect: the shortest.
- **12 min. The limit line.** Draw a line across the bars at 6.
  **Say.** "Which bars go past the line?" Expect: the ones taller than 6.
- **4 min. Choose.** Your child finishes the frame on the student page.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** Your child does [[s02-X19]], alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: three bars and one line. Never cut the limit line, the weakness or the exit. Longer: a third graph of another number.

### Words and supports

Bar, limit, line. Frame: "I chose ___ because ___ and it is poor at ___." A scribe is fine.

### Tomorrow

The exit shows who reads the wrong bar or ignores the line (error G). Day 20 opens with error G: lay a ruler along the limit line and read each bar as over or under. Then wind.
