# The Gatekeeper and the River

1. In the time when rivers could speak, Lina kept the gate above a valley of orchards. The villagers had chosen her because she could lift the heavy key and read the water marks on the gateposts. Each spring she polished the key until it reflected her face. This year rain fell in the distant hills for days. Below Lina's window the river rose, brown and restless, until it pressed against the closed gate.
2. Lina thought a stronger lock would keep the orchards dry. She added a second chain. Before nightfall, water had begun to spread across the empty meadow above the gate. The village had already moved its people and animals to the hill, but the orchard trees stood below. From her dry watchtower, Lina watched the water climb. She gripped the key so tightly that its teeth left marks in her palm.
3. "You have locked the road I used to take," said the river. Lina had expected a roar. Instead she heard the scrape of a tired voice. The river described an old channel across the empty meadow, away from the orchards. Lina found it on an older drawing beneath her new gate plan. She had mistaken the pale winding line for a crack in the paper.
4. When the water had fallen and officials declared the work area safe, Lina took the drawing to the villagers. She wanted to order them to dig. Instead she spread the paper where everyone could see it. The gardeners knew where roots held the soil. The miller knew where stones narrowed the old channel. Together they changed her drawing and arranged the work. When the next spring's water came, it spread into the meadow along the cleared channel instead of pressing against the orchard gate. This was one season's result, not a promise for every season.
5. Lina returned the gate key to the meeting room. She kept her place in the watchtower, but she no longer polished the key every morning. She studied the water marks and brought her drawings downstairs. When the river spoke again, it sounded neither gentle nor angry. It sounded like a voice she had finally made room to hear.
