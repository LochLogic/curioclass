# The Noticeboard

1. Before the town meeting, I wanted our old noticeboard back beside the river. My grandfather had painted its border. Last spring the flood had left the board face down in the mud; now it leaned, dry and splintered, against the meeting-room wall.
2. "Where would people read it?" asked Jo. I pointed to its old place on our drawing. Jo pointed to the path on the hill, where the replacement meeting room would stand. I traced the climb with my finger. My grandfather could walk there, but he would need somewhere to rest.
3. That afternoon, at my grandfather's table, I changed my drawing. The board stood beside the uphill path, with a bench below it. I kept the painted border. When I showed my grandfather, he ran one finger along the faded blue line. "Room for two on that bench?" he asked. I drew it wider.

## Teaching notes

On day 17, locate the board and the narrator in paragraph 1. Follow the change in plan through the question in paragraph 2. On day 19, compare the grandfather tracing the border and asking about the bench with the drawing that follows. On day 22, use that sequence to show how a detail can prepare a conclusion.
