# Section 1.7 opener. Pronouns, voice and peer readers

A subject acts or is described. Subjective, objective and possessive name the form used for the subject,
an object or possession. An intensive gives emphasis. In chapter 12, “Grandpa himself had drawn” retains a
subject when “himself” is removed. Reread the full sentence for its meaning. Review tense using an earlier
sentence and preserve deliberate changes in time. Objective in a summary means free of personal judgment.

A pronoun’s antecedent is who or what it points to. Use Kit's chapter 11 note and its surrounding paragraph
to identify the intended person. Person identifies the speaker, listener or someone spoken about. Number
distinguishes one from more than one here. A shift is a change. Start with separate lines for each problem.
Repair K by checking the pronoun’s role, L by removing the emphasis, M or W by naming the intended speaker
or owners, and N by writing the intended name. Keep anchor sections under 240 words.

Register is the way words fit a setting. A reference gives a form to check. Read both transcript lines
of Choosing words for a meeting, or have the teacher voice them with the same print in view. Explain how
conventional wording fits a formal note. For error O, discuss the audience and purpose with respect for
both speakers. A student can pass in public talk and use an invented sentence.

Before class, give each writer a dated private folder and the digital work log. Use school devices when
available to save an individual draft, post it for classmates, receive a comment and save a response.
Record the actual acts. Paper preserves language evidence. Schedule named school makeups for digital work.
Keep the day 27 language cards and teacher key apart from instruction. Give written cues before day 29.
Collect the revised story, earlier versions and reason for a change on day 30.
