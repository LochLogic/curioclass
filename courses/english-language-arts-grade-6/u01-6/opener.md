# Section 1.6 opener. From first draft to a revision plan

A transition is a cue that tells readers when or where the action moves. A time word, a place phrase and an
opening clause can each orient the reader. Compare the start of chapter 3 with the walk after the signing
in chapter 15. Use the first part of chapter 16 and the final drawing to locate tangible details and a
choice that produces the ending. Keep each selected anchor work segment to 300 words.

Review precise, specific enough to identify the intended subject or feature. A sensory detail tells what
someone can see, hear, smell, taste or feel. Review the writing process through the folder: plan, draft,
feedback, revision, edit. Review “revise,” change meaning or organization to help the reader.
Feedback is a reader's response that can guide a change. Tone is how the voice sounds about what is happening.

Use the revision sheet for separate teacher and reader comments. Give one located cue on each first draft
after day 22. Allow two minutes per draft outside class. On day 23 compare two changes to released material.
Review sentence-combining by keeping complete thoughts when joining clauses. Save N1 versions privately.

Error E calls for ordering two released events and pointing to the cue. For H, trace an outcome back to its
cause. For B, identify what evidence is still missing. For J, P or Q, carry one cue into the first draft
work on day 24. Keep P7 makeups during Peer feedback and the first five minutes of Try another approach.
Collect each original exit before anyone sees a partner’s answer.
