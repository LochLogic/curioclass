# Teacher-only answers, section 1.4

Teacher-only answer key. Keep separate from every child copy.

Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen; no points.

## s01-Pr11

Prompt: Match each retained detail to motion evidence or the warming account.

Expected response: Block motion is observed; slight warming comes from the account. Motion, sound and warming are destinations, not disappearing energy.

Look for: Identify record and account separately.; Keep motion beside sound.; No felt warmth does not prove no warming; no temperature was measured.

Expected wrong responses: All energy became sound (D); Energy disappears (C); No felt warmth proves no warming (E).

## s01-Po11

Prompt: Complete the car trial table, including the missing slower trial.

Expected response: Record approach speed and both motion changes honestly. Retain unreadable entries; mark fallback records invented.

Look for: Keep car, block, surface, block start and contact fixed.; Use observed approach speed, not force lines.; Repeat an unclear trial once if time allows; otherwise use C separately.

Expected wrong responses: My expected result is an observation (I).

## s01-Pn11

Prompt: Match one recorded observation to the class question it could help answer.

Expected response: A specific recorded motion observation is linked to a relevant question; it is evidence, not proof of every future result.

Look for: Point to an actual trial.; Connect to motion after contact or whether slower and faster effects differ.; Keep the note brief, without an integrated prediction and explanation.

Expected wrong responses: A prediction proves what happened (I).

## s01-X11

Prompt: Invented for practice. The same wagon repeatedly hits the same carton under matching conditions. Every slower contact moves the carton less than every faster contact. The wagon slows in every trial. Predict a slower repeat using this record as evidence.

Expected response: Expect the wagon to slow and the carton to move less than in faster contacts. Repeated controlled differences support less starting motion energy available for transfer at slower speed.

Look for: Predict wagon slowing and less carton motion than in faster contacts.; Support the prediction with the controlled repeated effects and the motion-energy link.

Expected wrong responses: The same wagon has equal starting energy at both speeds (A); The wagon loses energy because energy vanishes (C).

## s01-Pr12

Prompt: Choose an observable outcome for a car-speed question.

Expected response: How far the same block moves after contact.

Look for: Choose a measurable or observable collision effect.

Expected wrong responses: Which speed is best? (F); Same car means equal energy at both speeds (A); Energy vanishes (C).

## s01-Pa12

Prompt: Classify each correct overlay as a push on an object or an energy destination.

Expected response: Overlay A shows forces on the two objects at contact. Overlay B connects truck motion energy to block motion, remaining truck motion, sound and slight warming.

Look for: A: truck push arrow starts on block, points right; block push arrow starts on truck, points left.; B: arrows connect initial motion energy to named destinations, not travel paths.; No lengths or equal shares are inferred.

Expected wrong responses: A push arrow carries energy into air (J).

## s01-Ps12

Prompt: Sort the labels under push on object or energy destination.

Expected response: Push on object: truck pushes block right; block pushes truck left. Energy destination: block motion; remaining truck motion; sound; slight warming.

Look for: Keep the two object pushes together.; Keep the four energy destinations together.; A force is on an object; energy arrows show where energy goes.; Accept pointing or label letters; no paragraph drafting.

Expected wrong responses: Sound receives all energy (D).

## s01-X12

Prompt: Invented for practice. A bead rolls right into a still card. The bead slows but keeps moving; the card moves right. The model says energy vanished. Its push arrow points from the card into air. Correct this model, showing what each arrow means.

Expected response: Retain remaining bead motion energy. Show transfer to card motion, sound and slight warming. A contact-force arrow starts on the card, pointing right. Energy arrows connect initial motion energy to destinations.

Look for: Replace vanished energy with remaining bead motion and transfer destinations.; Put the contact push on the card, pointing right.; Label force arrows as pushes and energy arrows as transfers to destinations.

Expected wrong responses: Energy vanished as the bead slowed (C); The force arrow carries energy from the card into air (J).

## s01-Pr13

Prompt: Point to the object receiving the contact push in a correct truck overlay.

Expected response: The block receives the rightward contact push.

Look for: Locate the push on the receiving object.

Expected wrong responses: A force arrow carries energy into air (J); Energy vanishes (C).

## s01-K13

Prompt: Attach a supporting record detail to an answered anchor question.

Expected response: Controlled repeated greater cup motion supports more starting motion energy in the faster marble. Energy passes at contact, changing both motions; sound is observed and slight warming comes from the account.

Look for: Check actual repeated records before choosing an answer.; Link faster marble motion to more energy available to transfer, supported by greater controlled effects.; Keep remaining marble motion and receiving cup motion, sound and slight warming.; Label observation or account. Retain questions not settled by these sources; do not force a pattern from overlapping trials.

Expected wrong responses: Same marble means equal starting energy (A).

## s01-Pp13

Prompt: Invented for practice. A paper ball rolls toward a still paper tube. Give a conditional motion prediction for their single contact.

Expected response: If the ball reaches and strikes the tube, the tube may move and the ball may slow, stop or rebound. Energy can pass to tube motion.

Look for: State the contact condition.; Predict physically possible changes in both objects.; One receiver only; no chain or record-explanation packet.

Expected wrong responses: The ball slows because energy vanishes (C).

## s01-X13

Prompt: Invented for practice. The same orange hits the same bottle under matching conditions. Three faster contacts each move it farther and sound louder than three slower contacts. Now a second bottle stands in the first bottle's path. Predict the two-bottle chain if contact occurs, using the record to support your model.

Expected response: The first bottle can transfer energy to second-bottle motion if it reaches it. The first may slow, stop or rebound. Faster recorded effects support more starting motion energy, without guaranteeing second contact. Retain remaining motion, sound and slight warming.

Look for: Use the controlled single-contact record to support the conditional prediction.; Show a possible first-to-second bottle energy transfer if contact occurs.; Retain energy in continuing motion and sound and warming destinations; reaching the second bottle is uncertain.

Expected wrong responses: The same orange has equal initial motion energy at both speeds (A); The energy vanishes after contact (C); All energy becomes sound (D).
