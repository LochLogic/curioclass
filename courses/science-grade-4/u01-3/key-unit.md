# Teacher-only answers, section 1.3

Teacher-only answer key. Keep separate from every child copy.

Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen; no points.

## s01-Pr7

Prompt: Label what centimeters measure in the retained marble record.

Expected response: Centimeters measure cup travel distance, not energy.

Look for: Label the measured quantity as travel distance.

Expected wrong responses: The same marble always has equal motion energy. (A); The cup moved centimeters of energy. (B).

## s01-K7

Prompt: Invented for practice. A resting crate has equal opposite pushes. Describe how a stronger rightward push changes this situation.

Expected response: Equal opposite arrows cancel; no horizontal motion change. A stronger rightward push gives an unbalanced pair and rightward motion change.

Look for: Review: arrows start on the crate and point with each push.; Equal opposite pushes are balanced and cancel.; With a stronger rightward push, motion changes rightward.

Expected wrong responses: The push arrow leaves the crate and carries energy into air. (J).

## s01-Ke7

Prompt: Classify each supplied car-and-block arrow as a contact push or energy transfer.

Expected response: A contact-force arrow is a push on the block; an energy arrow connects initial car motion energy to receiving block motion.

Look for: Classify a push arrow on an object.; Classify an energy arrow linking motion-energy destinations.

Expected wrong responses: The force arrow carries energy away into the air. (J).

## s01-Pq7

Prompt: Sort the supplied car-and-block questions by testability.

Expected response: A needs an observable outcome. B is testable; underline how far the block moves.

Look for: A leaves best undefined.; B compares speeds and names block travel; keep car, block, surface, start and alignment fixed.

Expected wrong responses: A is ready because best tells us what to measure. (F).

## s01-Po7

Prompt: Record what both objects do during the collision.

Expected response: Predict possible block motion, then honestly record car and block changes, including unclear observations.

Look for: Predict before watching.; Record both motions after contact.; Mark unclear when speed or change cannot be distinguished.; Fallback C is invented, never measured.

Expected wrong responses: My prediction counts as something already observed. (I).

## s01-X7

Prompt: Invented for practice. A ball rolls right toward a still carton. A previous slower contact moved that carton slightly. This approach is faster; other conditions match. Draw a prediction model for this faster collision, supported by the slower-contact record.

Expected response: Predict ball slowing and carton gaining motion, plausibly more than before. Show energy passing into carton motion. A rightward contact-force arrow starts on the carton.

Look for: Use the slower-contact record to support a defensible two-object prediction.; Show transfer from ball motion into carton motion, retaining remaining ball motion if it continues.; Place the contact push on the carton, pointing right.

Expected wrong responses: The slowing ball loses energy because it disappears. (C); The contact push arrow leaves the carton for the air. (J).

## s01-Pr8

Prompt: Choose an observable outcome for comparing car speeds.

Expected response: How far the same block moves after contact.

Look for: Name an observable outcome under matching conditions.

Expected wrong responses: Which speed is best needs no further detail. (F); The car slows because energy disappears. (C); The push on the block starts in the air. (J).

## s01-P8

Prompt: Complete the chronological collision record for both objects.

Expected response: Six honest event notes identify approach speed, both motion changes and unclear or changed conditions.

Look for: Use same car, block, surface, start and alignment.; Observe actual approach speed.; Note both motions separately.; Keep unclear entries; separate invented fallback events.

Expected wrong responses: Record the prediction as the observed result. (I).

## s01-Pu8

Prompt: Mark an observation that supports or challenges your earlier spoken prediction.

Expected response: A recorded motion change that genuinely supports or challenges the earlier prediction.

Look for: Select an actual observation while retaining the original prediction.

Expected wrong responses: Erase the observation to match the prediction. (I).

## s01-X8

Prompt: Your prediction said the can would keep moving unchanged. New pictures show it slowing and the cork starting to move. Revise your prediction and energy model using these observations.

Expected response: A repeat can slow the can and start cork motion. Energy passes from can motion to cork motion. Retain motion energy in the still-moving can; both observations support revision.

Look for: Revise the supplied prediction using both observations.; Connect can motion energy to cork motion energy at contact.; Retain motion energy in the can that continues moving.

Expected wrong responses: The can slows because its energy disappears. (C); The pictured slowing is only a prediction, not an observation. (I).

## s01-Pr9

Prompt: Mark an observed motion change in a retained car trial.

Expected response: A motion change actually present in the chosen record.

Look for: Mark recorded rather than predicted motion.

Expected wrong responses: The next roll is already observed because I predicted it. (I); The car slowing means its energy disappeared. (C).

## s01-K9

Prompt: Invented for practice. A practice listener reports an unclear contact sound. Show how to enter this event without calling it quiet.

Expected response: Add one event tick; label approach speed unknown and sound unclear. Leave motion unknown unless supplied.

Look for: Tick means event, not loudness.; Write unclear sound and unknown for unsupplied speed or motion.; Never invent readings.

Expected wrong responses: My expected quiet sound counts as observed sound. (I).

## s01-P9

Prompt: Complete the chronological listening log, keeping unclear reports visible.

Expected response: Six chronological entries with honest speed, sound and both motion observations; unclear retained, no numerical sound levels.

Look for: Same safe listening place.; One tick per event, separate from quiet/loud/unclear report.; Both motions; invented C separate from live records.

Expected wrong responses: Sound receives all energy, so block motion needs no record. (D).

## s01-Pc9

Prompt: Explain aloud why listening alone cannot show every energy destination.

Expected response: Block motion is another destination. Sound and motion observations together support transfer; sound is not an energy meter.

Look for: Use a recorded event.; Include block motion and sound.; Review: same car faster has more motion energy; unclear sound cannot establish a pattern.

Expected wrong responses: All energy became sound. (D).
