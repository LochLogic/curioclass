# Section 3.3: Agreeing on a code

Days 48 to 50 research communication before design, then generate multiple complete rules. Need means who needs what, so that; criterion means a way to check the need is met; limit means the most we allow. State and follow rules explicitly; no unstated number-pattern inference. Devices can encode voice as ordered digits and decode received digits to sound. Shared meanings and order matter; digits do not guarantee perfect reception. No light-wave model.

Read the original account in digits-account.md, at most 150 words. Its source identifiers point to checked entries in sources.csv. Use the printed account if outside access is unavailable. Children keep statement/location notes. Three-cell teaching strips preserve square pictures for assessment. Design tables use three messages, preserving E49's two-message drawings.

Day 48 research: six receivers, thirty seconds each in quiet and conversation, six minutes; four minutes to record. Fix positions and gentle output. Waiting children keep notes. Teach from actual observations; no shouting to force a reading. Day 50 kit access: six two-minute visits plus three setup minutes. Children keep individual proposals before team selection. Teacher handles batteries; aim steady light at a card away from eyes. No rapid flashing, lasers, mirrors or sun viewing. Gentle drum taps only; stop for discomfort. Cleanup five minutes: kit two, filing three.

Print source-notes.md, three-cell-strips.md, proposals.md, peer-response.md, assembly-check.md and reserved exits. Day 49 returns E48 E five minutes and D in a separate five-minute criteria move. Day 50 repairs commonest E49 E/F in five minutes, other gaps through written prompts during proposals. Day 51 returns E50 and source/proposal feedback in ten minutes. Paper-only systems remain untested until actual trials.

Every day lasts 45 minutes. Each child keeps individual paper evidence. Read directions neutrally; a scribe may record the child's own reasoning. Public talk is optional. Hear a rotating six at 45 seconds each within five minutes. Partner turns take 45 seconds each, not thirty serial turns. All papers, including those of children not heard, receive written component feedback on the named hand-back day. Many means more than ten of thirty.

Teach from observations before naming explanations. Label and announce every supplied record: invented for practice. Keep actual observations with collector, date and conditions; mark unclear readings. Retry once inside the existing observation slot, then use the named fallback separately. No simulation or paper record proves practical performance.

Before class, print only that day's student pages, its reserved exit sheet, and the teacher-only key. Keep future exits and portfolio transfer cases sealed. Never project the teacher-only key. Packets use 14 point type and at most 350 student words a day, including questions. Exits use half sheets. Day 50 and day 54 have two judged exit rows; other exits each ask one task with at most two parts.

Use the formative record's three categories. Preserve originals and second-color revisions. Assessment points go only on assessment-record.md. No public grading or child used as a mistake example.
