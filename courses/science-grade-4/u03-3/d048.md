### The day

#### 5 min. Design retrieval

Review the question board's communication question. Review grade 3 need: who needs what, so that; criterion: a way to check the need is met; limit: the most we allow. Review its design cycle: define, sketch, build, test, compare. Read the invented basin-helper problem; this is a supply station, not a water investigation. **Ask.** [[s03-P48-design]]. Keep the checkable reception result separate from permitted materials. Children write alone.

#### 10 min. Read and locate

Review source: a card or page that tells you something. Review unit 2 statement/location notes. Children read the account silently; then read it aloud. **Ask.** [[s03-P48-source]]. Each child records a claim and paragraph, not an unsupported guess. Explain encode, decode, digit and code key using the account. A code key gives agreed meanings/order, rather than graph quantities. Source rows U3-images, U3-patterns and U3-design support this original account.

#### 10 min. Research observations

Review grade 1 message, signal, sender and receiver: intended information, traveling sound/light, and the two ends. Review evidence as observations supporting a claim. Six volunteers receive, three near and three far; others observe. Each has thirty seconds in quiet and thirty with conversational background: six minutes. Send two separated gentle taps per trial; record detected counts before revealing two. Never raise output. Stop for discomfort. Everyone completes [[s03-P48-research]] in four recording minutes, including positions, date and collector. Retry one unclear trial within its slot; otherwise mark unclear. If unavailable, use research-fallback.md, announced as invented for practice. Children answer [[s03-P48-fallback]] from the supplied quiet/conversation detections. Keep this record separate from actual readings. No claim of actual kit performance follows fallback evidence.

#### 10 min. Trace a known rule

Children do [[s03-P48-encode]], then [[s03-P48-decode]], before revealing their worked steps privately prepared in the key. [[steps:s03-P48-encode]] [[steps:s03-P48-decode]]

**Ask.** [[s03-P48-voice]]. Expect the ordered encode/send/receive/decode chain, not a wave-motion arrow. Partners each have one 45-second optional turn, then write alone. If error E drops agreement, point back to the stated rule. Collect papers; reserve individual source/research feedback for day 51.

**From sound and pictures to digits**

1. A picture can be represented by a sequence of digits. A sender and receiver agree what each digit means. They also agree where to start and which way to read. The receiver uses those rules to rebuild the picture.

2. Devices can also change voice into ordered digits. Another device can receive those digits and turn them into sound. Encoding means changing information into a code. Decoding means using its rule to recover information.

3. A shared rule helps preserve a message across distance. It does not guarantee that every signal arrives correctly. We must test proposed systems and keep misses as well as successes.
