# Section 3.1: Observing waves

Days 42 to 44 develop models of qualitative wave patterns using the tub and marked cork. Distinguish height from spacing and local movement from energy travel. Rest position is the undisturbed level; amplitude is displacement from rest, not peak-to-trough distance. Wavelength is matching-peak spacing. Never measure either numerically. A cork can drift; observe whether it follows each crest rather than promising perfect stillness.

Review energy-arrow meanings from unit 2, then apply them to waves on day 42. Day 43 separates vibration producing sound, shown with a secured ruler, from sound making another surface vibrate, shown only through receiving-drum records. Readiness diagnoses retrieval gaps in grade 1 days 31 and 32; reteach a missing causal direction explicitly. The signal retrieval agrees a LOOK meaning and checks reception.

One adult operates the tub. Six two-minute group views take twelve minutes. Day 42 has three additional recording minutes. Day 43 has five setup/label minutes and three recording minutes. Day 44 rope is an adult demonstration. Adult wears goggles; observers remain outside splash/recoil reach. Adult fills/empties water and secures the ruler. Use nonlatex equipment; no food, soil or plants. Stop for damage, recoil or uncontrolled spills. Five cleanup minutes: adult stores, wipes goggles and washes three; dry observers file two. No class sink queue.

Print observation-sheet.md, water-fallback.md, receiving-drum.md, wave-strips.md and daily reserved exits. No kit: use W labeled invented; record missing firsthand evidence. Error A confuses material with a traveling crest. Error B ties height to spacing. Day 44 repairs E43 five minutes per letter; E42 joins the local-motion move without extra minutes. E44 goes to day 45.

Every day lasts 45 minutes. Each child keeps individual paper evidence. Read directions neutrally; a scribe may record the child's own reasoning. Public talk is optional. Hear a rotating six at 45 seconds each within five minutes. Partner turns take 45 seconds each, not thirty serial turns. All papers, including those of children not heard, receive written component feedback on the named hand-back day. Many means more than ten of thirty.

Teach from observations before naming explanations. Label and announce every supplied record: invented for practice. Keep actual observations with collector, date and conditions; mark unclear readings. Retry once inside the existing observation slot, then use the named fallback separately. No simulation or paper record proves practical performance.

Before class, print only that day's student pages, its reserved exit sheet, and the teacher-only key. Keep future exits and portfolio transfer cases sealed. Never project the teacher-only key. Packets use 14 point type and at most 350 student words a day, including questions. Exits use half sheets. Day 50 has one square exit; its boundary diagnostic occurs during Assemble. Day 54 has one grid-rule exit; the pond model occurs during Anchor consensus.

Use the formative record's three categories. Preserve originals and second-color revisions. Assessment points go only on ../u03-1/assessment-record.md. No public grading or child used as a mistake example.
