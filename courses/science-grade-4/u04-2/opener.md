# Section 4.2: Explain land-changing processes

Day 61 compares initial, thawed and control clay; a short trial may show no change. Clay is an analogue, not rock. Adult gently shakes a secured wet jar of rounded soft pieces 100 times; compare an unshaken matched jar. Count within the demonstration, not as child turns. If no visible change occurs, preserve that finding and use fallback-records.md separately. Never promise new chips. Day 62 reviews graph scales, then graphs actual class readings and uses causal arrows. The exit asks for six held flow results plus a counterexample; its process question is an Exit box.

Day 63 has five minutes of paper comparison because the simulation is not built. Claim-cards.md is the complete route. It rehearses matched results and limits; the exit instead audits a confounded pebble claim. Day 64 uses separate invented process-cards.md and dated sketches; no wind blowing. The four brief Exit box records and rainfall-test exit total five minutes, with process answers kept to short labels. Day 65 uses plant-sheet.md and the same six four-minute turn pattern as day 60. Compare rooted turf with bare sand, keeping other conditions matched as closely as possible. Turf also changes surface structure; do not claim roots alone are isolated. Only handlers touch plants. Each child reads actual paired distances. Waiting students work on their own evidence notes. Return day 64 notes during Explain; any needed five-minute repair replaces waiting-paper work, without taking apparatus turns. Day 66 revises the canyon using dated evidence, then works the separate process-cards-66.md records.

Return each exit the next day, with day 60 work at day 61 Compare controls. Day 61 practical notes return day 62. Days 65 and 66 practice get written feedback, then seven and eight minutes respectively of day 70 Feedback revision. Error A needs breakage in place; B a visible change or honest failed trial; C controlled comparisons; D organism effects; E rainfall and life. More than ten errors triggers one five-minute selected move in the named receiving part, replacing its rehearsal. Never take the day 59 response or assessment minutes.

Every day lasts 45 minutes. Follow the minute table below. Read printed directions neutrally; students may use a scribe for their own reasoning. Partner turns take 45 seconds each, then partners swap. Public talk is optional. Hear six children at 45 seconds each inside five minutes, not thirty serial turns. Every child supplies paper evidence; return all papers on the recorded day, including those not heard. Use record.md with one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record an error letter only when expressed, never for missing work. Preserve originals and later revisions.

Before class, print only today's pieces and reserved exit, plus teacher-key.md for yourself. The answer key stays out of student view. Exits use half sheets. Exit box rows on days 62, 64 and 65 stay on the student page. No key is printed on a student card. Print at 14 point, at most 350 student words per day including items; an assessment record can be read aloud neutrally. Keep future exit and assessment envelopes closed.

Use remembered terms exactly: erosion, slow change, bar graph, fossil, layer, older, younger, undisturbed, question board and consensus. Review their meanings briefly on the planned retrieval days. Source notes use the earlier two-column routine: source statement and source location. Introduce weathering as breaking material down; carrying alone does not establish new breakage. Practices, Earth ideas and cause/effect or patterns stay joined in each explanation. An instrument reads whole centimeters, never finer invented precision. Earth is very old; these records establish relative history, not numerical ages. People bring different ideas. In science class we look at what the evidence shows.

No child reports a home experience, illness or disaster. Use supplied generic events. Science explanations rest on observations, not personal belief statements. A real photo's lack of clarity is a limitation. Retry a reading once inside its existing slot; then mark it unresolved. Say aloud that any printed fallback is invented for practice. It never supplies firsthand observation or measurement credit. There is no dependence on a visitor, local trip or unfinished tool.

Safety and set-up: the adult checks actual sand, clay and plant ingredients and allergies before use. Nothing is tasted. Keep sand damp; never blow it. Use clean untreated materials, covered tables, a catch bucket and spill cloths. All present wear suitable splash protection from setup through cleanup on wet or fragment days. The adult pours, resets trays, operates the freezer and shakes closed jars. Inspect lids and non-glass containers; stop for leakage or damage. Freezing uses plastic, loose covers and headspace. Settle runoff; keep solids out of sinks.

Only two active handlers touch wet apparatus; others observe and record from dry positions. Five cleanup minutes: adult and any second handler wash with soap and water, clean eye protection and contain solids while dry observers file papers. No thirty-child sink queue is assumed. Clay days use six groups of five, large counted tokens and blunt slicers, cutting away from hands. Begin handwashing during each group's final work minute, staggering pairs; the final five cleanup minutes finish remaining handlers and store clay. If washing or protection cannot fit safely, use the printed model route and record missing practical evidence.
