## C-E40-origin

Answer: Crude oil comes from ancient living remains changed underground over millions of years. Refining separates useful products from extracted oil; it does not replenish deposits. Oil is nonrenewable on human timescales.

Look for: Look for natural origin, refinery distinction and renewal timescale. One category per component.

Wrong: A refinery is the natural origin of crude oil. Error I.

Wrong: Oil deposits refill each year. Error J.

## C-E40-use

Answer: Crude oil is extracted, refined into diesel and used in an engine to move a truck. Its need is moving goods. Source A supplies origin and refining; the invented record supplies truck use.

Look for: Look for natural resource, refinery/engine technology and energy meeting the transport need.

Wrong: Only the made truck is needed, with no natural resource. Error x.

## C-E40-effects

Answer: Drilling can clear vegetation, changing land habitat. Transport spills contaminate soil or water. Source B supplies effects; Source A connects the oil supply to transport fuel. Possible effects, not every well or ship.

Look for: Look for extraction and transport causal links joining Source A use with Source B effects.

Wrong: Extracting and moving oil never affect surroundings. Error K.

## C-E40-change

Answer: Source B supports reduced spill risk, not guaranteed prevention. Drawings leave drilling unchanged, so vegetation disturbance remains possible. Source A still requires extracted oil for fuel. Ship walls do not make oil renewable.

Look for: Look for changed spill risk, remaining effect and both sources with before/after comparison.

Wrong: Two ship walls remove every effect. Error L.
