# Section 6.4: Arguments about interacting structures

Days 109 to 112.

## Science and evidence

Arguments use visible-scale parts and evidence for survival, growth, behavior and reproduction. Organ lists do not show interactions. Intake connects with digestion; lungs exchange gases, heart circulates blood, brain guides action. Bird eggs and nonhuman mammal womb/milk-gland cards introduce reproduction. A fertile egg can develop, but not every egg does. Use schematic separated organs, not claims of exact anatomical placement. No student-body comparisons.

## Expected ideas and response

F excludes internal structures; G gives reproduction to petals alone; H overclaims rootless evidence. F/G receive ten minutes on 109/110. F/G/H receive fifteen on 111. F/H receive ten on 112; H/F return on 113.

## Before class and equipment

Print animal-parts.md, reproduction-cards.md and evidence-locators.md. Paper and pencils only. Teach each route separately. Day 110 models an oral familiar-plant argument; later practice adds locators to valid single claims. Held goat, plant Q, bird F and paired weak claims remain unseen until their exit.

## Fallback and record

Source tags separate observed and supplied evidence. If a specimen record is absent, use the clearly marked supplied Plant P. No visitor, local page, photograph or live animal is needed.

## Participation and access

Read labels and directions aloud without supplying the answer. Offer enlarged diagrams, tactile arrows, a scribe or home-language explanation. Public talk is optional. No home, pet, illness or body disclosures. Pairs take simultaneous forty-five-second turns. Teacher hears a rotating six, forty-five seconds each, within five minutes during work. Everyone else submits the paper record; the next named hand-back returns it. Use record.md to note who was heard. Initial ideas are descriptive. Other formative responses use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keep first models and revise in a second color. Do not rank children.
