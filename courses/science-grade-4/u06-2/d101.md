# Day 101: Signals can gain meaning

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read teacher-only keys. Set six groups of five, action cards and tally sheets. Reserve the hidden-card exit. Use gentle claps only; no blindfolds. Everyone may point while seated or observe. Check comfortable sound levels and use supplied Signal S if hearing the cues is inaccessible. Keep actual and supplied evidence distinct.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Return day 100's tickets, catch records, s06-P100, s06-P100-repair and s06-P100-order. If many day 100 tickets show error D, use [[s06-P101-repair]] with separate role cards. Review day 99's receiving/processing distinction. Briefly define gentle claps and equal pointing/observation choices.

#### 5 min. Signal meanings

Review signal from unit 3: a shared meaning must be learned. One gentle clap means point to square; two mean circle. Introduce remembered meaning: the brain can use a meaning learned earlier. **Ask.** [[s06-P101-meaning]]. Expect ears receiving sound and brain processing its learned meaning, not a new sense organ. Explain no storage mechanism.

#### 18 min. Equal rounds

Six groups work simultaneously from the teacher's common cues, avoiding competing sounds. Each group agrees one action per opportunity; all children may point. Use signal-record.md: demonstrate one opportunity, then early cues one, two, one, two, two claps. Each child copies [[s06-P101]] counts. Practice both meanings; later cues are two, one, two, one, one claps. Five opportunities in each round; count correct actions in ones, no reaction times or child rankings.

Groups check totals against ticks. Retain unchanged or lower success. If a tally cannot be recovered, mark unclear and use S, printed and read aloud as invented for practice: early two of five, later four of five. Never replace valid non-improvement.

#### 10 min. Read counts

Individuals use [[s06-P101-compare]]. Practice counts compare actions; they cannot directly show brains or prove memory alone caused a change. Review day 99's system-model; add learned meaning beside processing. No delayed visual cue in practice. Partners take two simultaneous 45-second turns. Review grade 3's rotating-six routine: hear six at 45 seconds each, 4.5 minutes concurrently; record names. Public talk may be passed; all submit paper.

#### 2 min. Cleanup

Collect group cards/tallies. Each child keeps the copied count record on the page for collection.

#### 5 min. Exit E101

Reveal [[s06-X101]] for the first time. Read neutrally; individuals explain on paper. The vanished visual card is reserved for this turn. Look for incoming visual information and processing using remembered meaning. Error D skips processing; error E denies remembered signals. Collect every page and ticket. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen only.

### Other rooms

No audible cues: use invented Signal S and discuss its equal opportunities. No printer: copy cards and records; reserve the exit. No device needed.

### At home

**Swap.** Copy action cards and prompts. Use invented Signal S if gentle claps are unsuitable.

**Skip.** Group agreement and speaking rotation.

- **5 min. Return.** Do [[s06-P101-repair]].
- **7 min. Meanings.** Agree the clap meanings; ask [[s06-P101-meaning]].
- **13 min. Rounds.** Adult gives the printed cue lists. Child records [[s06-P101]], then answers [[s06-P101-compare]].
  **Say.** "Must the later count be larger?" Expect no; keep the actual count. Explain remembered meaning as something the brain can use.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal [[s06-X101]]. Child explains alone; keep that first answer.

### Shorter or longer

Shorten repeated practice, keeping equal rounds, remembered meaning and exit. Extend by describing another possible reason for changed counts.

### Words and supports

Remembered: learned earlier and usable now. Review brain and response. Read records aloud; allow enlarged cards, faithful scribing and home-language explanations. Keep an individual response.
