# Section 6.2: Information guides action

Days 99 to 103.

## Science and evidence

Modeling connects sensory information, brain processing and guided action as interacting system roles. Light travels to eyes; information arrows represent what happens inside an animal. Remembered meaning can guide action after a signal stops. Counts show actions, not processing seen directly. Owl hearing and some snakes' heat sensing broaden the input. Do not teach receptor or memory-storage mechanisms.

## Expected ideas and response

D omits processing; E omits remembered meaning. Day 99 receives ten minutes of A/B repair before its brain link. D returns on 100/101; D/E receive ten minutes on 102 to 104. Return written records for every child.

## Before class and equipment

Print role-cards.md, catch-record.md, signal-record.md, animal-inputs.md and flag-actions.md. Catch: adult plus one consenting handler per group, two impact goggles, soft ruler, mat and clear enclosure. Faces outside, hands below rim. Six three-minute visits include cleaning and drying. Others record without ranking children. Gentle claps, seated pointing and observation are equal choices.

## Fallback and record

Pretest containment and hygiene; failure or absent consent uses Catch C. Unreadable signal records use Signal S; valid non-improvement stays. Both are printed and announced invented for practice.

## Participation and access

Read labels and directions aloud without supplying the answer. Offer enlarged diagrams, tactile arrows, a scribe or home-language explanation. Public talk is optional. No home, pet, illness or body disclosures. Pairs take simultaneous forty-five-second turns. Teacher hears a rotating six, forty-five seconds each, within five minutes during work. Everyone else submits the paper record; the next named hand-back returns it. Use record.md to note who was heard. Initial ideas are descriptive. Other formative responses use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keep first models and revise in a second color. Do not rank children.
