# Day 100: What can a catch show?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Read today's teacher-only keys. Pretest containment, mat and hygiene. Adult and consenting child wear impact goggles; faces stay outside, hands below the enclosure rim through side openings. Others sit beyond the boundary. Stop for discomfort or escape from containment. If protection, consent or cleaning/drying cannot fit three minutes, use invented Catch C. Keep the gate-click exit private.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Return day 99's tickets, s06-P99, s06-P99-repair, s06-K99-retrieval, s06-K99-link and role notes. If many day 99 tickets show error D, use [[s06-P100-repair]] with separate role and input cards. Review day 99: receiving differs from processing. Children annotate one role, not another complete animal pathway.

#### 18 min. Catch observation

Six groups have three minutes each at the protected station, including goggle fitting, suitable wiping and drying. One consenting handler per group works with the adult. All thirty record [[s06-P100]] on the catch sheet. Release a soft ruler over the mat; no hitting. Tally catches/misses in ones, not distance or time. Keep names off group records; never rank bodies or ability.

If a reading is unclear, record unclear. Use Catch C if a usable record cannot be obtained safely. Read aloud: "Catch C is invented for practice." Its six releases include four catches and two misses; ruler movement precedes hand movement. Keep actual observations separate; supplied counts are not measurements.

#### 9 min. Information order

Partners point to their visible-event notes. Review day 99's system-model. **Ask.** [[s06-P100-order]]. Expect visible movement before the hand response; information processing is inferred, not seen. Do not claim every catch succeeds. If many treat counts as pictures of brains, separate the observed and inferred labels again. No flawed-model evaluation in practice.

#### 5 min. Record explanation

Individuals complete their observation/inference notes. Review grade 3's rotating-six routine: hear six at 45 seconds each, 4.5 minutes concurrently; record names. Others submit paper; public talk may be passed. Review second-color revision, retaining original counts. Record with shown, shown with help, not yet seen only.

#### 3 min. Cleanup

Adult stores ruler, mat, enclosure and cleaned, dry goggles. Children finish anonymous group codes and keep records separate from supplied C.

#### 5 min. Exit E100

Reveal [[s06-X100]] for independent paper responses. This gate-click case and flawed-model critique are first seen now. Look for the brain link and the limit of event-order evidence. Error D assigns direct control to ears. Collect catch sheets, page and tickets; never require a harmful test to establish the brain's role.

### Other rooms

No protected kit: use invented Catch C. No printer: copy its record and the prompts, keeping the exit hidden. No device needed.

### At home

**Swap.** Use invented Catch C instead of a catch apparatus. Copy the record without a printer.

**Skip.** Group visits, handling and speaking rotation.

- **5 min. Return.** Read feedback and do [[s06-P100-repair]].
- **12 min. Record.** Adult reads C as invented. Child completes [[s06-P100]] on the catch sheet.
- **8 min. Explain.** Do [[s06-P100-order]].
  **Say.** "Which events were visible?" Expect moving ruler then moving hand. Brain processing is inferred. Separate what the record shows from the model's explanation.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Reveal [[s06-X100]]. Child answers alone. Keep the first response.

### Shorter or longer

Use C if station visits cannot fit. Retain observed/inferred work and exit. Extend by identifying something catch counts leave unknown.

### Words and supports

Review brain, information and response. Observed: directly seen. Inferred: explained using evidence. Read records aloud; offer enlarged boxes and faithful scribing. Observation is equal participation.
