# Teacher-only answers, section 1.1

Teacher-only answer key. Keep separate from every child copy.

Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen; no points.

## s01-P1

Prompt: Invented for practice. A toy passes equally spaced marks A, B and C at steady beats. Mark its next place.

Expected response: One equal gap beyond C.

Look for: Mark the next equally spaced position.

Expected wrong responses: The next mark stays at C. (x).

## s01-K1

Prompt: Watch the marble and cup. Describe one change you notice.

Expected response: A visible cup movement or a sound difference.

Look for: Describe something actually observed; no energy vocabulary required.

Expected wrong responses: My guess proves every result. (I).

## s01-Pq1

Prompt: Add one testable question about the observed rolls to our question board.

Expected response: Will repeating the two speeds change how far the same cup moves?

Look for: Question proposes an observable repeat with the same apparatus.

Expected wrong responses: I observed what tomorrow will do. (I).

## s01-X1

Prompt: Invented for practice. The same train hits the same tower pieces on the same surface. Faster contact scatters more pieces. The pictures show two train speeds. Draw a possible explanation for the tower's different changes.

Expected response: A tentative drawing connects train motion to the different tower changes; energy vocabulary is unnecessary.

Look for: Drawing plausibly connects train motion to displaced tower pieces.; Treat the connection as an idea to test, not proof of future results.

Expected wrong responses: My guess proves every future result. (I).

## s01-P2

Prompt: A child says, "The next cup roll will go farther." Label this as observed or expected.

Expected response: Expected; that future trial has not happened.

Look for: Label expected.; Distinguish future prediction from observation.

Expected wrong responses: Observed; the child said it. (I).

## s01-K2

Prompt: Two setups use the same marble and surface. Setup A changes speed and cup size. Setup B changes only speed. Cross out the extra change.

Expected response: Cup size in A; B isolates speed.

Look for: Cross out cup size.; Either speed or cup size could cause the result in A.

Expected wrong responses: Changing both makes it fairer. (G).

## s01-Pr2

Prompt: Record any extra change in the repeated marble comparisons.

Expected response: Identify an actual extra change, or record none when conditions match.

Look for: Check object, target, surface, target start and contact arrangement.

Expected wrong responses: Different cups prove speed matters. (G).

## s01-X2

Prompt: A ring rolls into a lid. Plan a fair test of faster and slower ring rolls. Use a plan table: change, keep, watch.

Expected response: Change ring speed; keep ring, lid, surface, target start and contact arrangement fixed. Repeat both speeds and observe lid motion.

Look for: Change speed only.; Keep the objects and contact conditions fixed.; Observe lid motion in repeated trials at both speeds.

Expected wrong responses: Use a different lid for each speed. (G).

## s01-P3

Prompt: A slower roll uses one cup. A faster roll uses a larger cup. Cross out the change that hides the speed effect.

Expected response: Cross out larger cup; keep the receiver unchanged.

Look for: Identify cup change.; Retain the same cup for both speeds.

Expected wrong responses: Keep changing cups. (G).

## s01-K3

Prompt: Invented for practice. A pencil starts at ruler mark 2 and ends at 7 centimeters. Count its travel intervals.

Expected response: Five one-centimeter intervals; travel is 5 centimeters.

Look for: Count intervals 2 to 3, 3 to 4, 4 to 5, 5 to 6 and 6 to 7.; Record 5 centimeters, not endpoint 7.

Expected wrong responses: The pencil travels 7 centimeters. (H).

## s01-Pp3

Prompt: Invented for practice. A ribbon is 5 centimeters long. Place one mark for its length on a labeled line plot.

Expected response: One mark above 5, axis labeled length in centimeters.

Look for: Use equal whole-centimeter intervals.; Place one mark at 5; label length in centimeters.

Expected wrong responses: The axis measures energy in centimeters. (B).

## s01-Pr3

Prompt: Complete the cup trial table, keeping unreadable trials visible.

Expected response: Six trial rows with units and honest unclear readings.

Look for: Each row has speed and trial label.; Nearest whole-centimeter mark; less than one centimeter is not zero.; Repeat an unclear reading once; keep both entries.

Expected wrong responses: Erase the awkward result and invent a matching one. (x).

## s01-X3

Prompt: The washer starts at 6 centimeters and stops at 15 centimeters. Another trial moves it 9 centimeters. Record both travel distances with units.

Expected response: 9 centimeters; 9 centimeters.

Look for: First travel: 15 minus 6 equals 9 centimeters.; Second travel: the supplied 9 centimeters.

Expected wrong responses: First travel is 15 centimeters. (H); The washer has 9 centimeters of energy. (B).

## s01-P4

Prompt: Review the pencil record from day 3. Count the spaces from 2 to 7 centimeters. Label what the ruler measures.

Expected response: 5 centimeters of distance.

Look for: Count five intervals.; Label distance in centimeters.

Expected wrong responses: The endpoint 7 is the travel. (H); Centimeters measure energy. (B).

## s01-Pr4

Prompt: Add one cup-table entry to the class line plot.

Expected response: Match the row to its mark; describe observed cup motion.

Look for: Match distance and speed series.; Keep equal intervals and speed labels.; Describe an effect, never energy units.

Expected wrong responses: The cup moved centimeters of energy. (B).

## s01-K4

Prompt: What might repeated cup movement tell us about the moving marble?

Expected response: If controlled repeats separate consistently, greater effects support more motion energy in the faster marble.

Look for: Check actual records first.; Repeated greater controlled effects support more motion energy.; Overlap calls for further trials, not altered records.

Expected wrong responses: The same marble has equal energy at both speeds. (A).

## s01-X4

Prompt: Invented for practice. One disk dents matching fresh clay pads. Other conditions stay fixed. Dent depths, in millimeters: slower 1, 1, 2; faster 4, 5, 4. What does this dent record suggest about the disk's motion energy at each speed?

Expected response: The faster disk has more motion energy. Every faster dent is deeper than every slower dent under matching conditions. Dent depth indicates an effect, not energy units.

Look for: Infer greater motion energy at faster speed.; Use the repeated greater deformation under matching conditions as evidence.

Expected wrong responses: The same disk has equal energy at both speeds. (A); Dent depth measures energy in millimeters. (B).
