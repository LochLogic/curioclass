# Day 28: The missing part

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, tickets and key. Have day 27's tickets and Lee's sums beside you, look-fors ticked, and
the facts group's names. Envelopes of pieces stay in the cupboard today.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: equal names

Whole class, in their heads ([[s04-W28-1]]). Side 1 face down; write one blank at a time on the board; hands up
when ready. After (c), students turn the sheet over and write the three answers.
The facts group (readiness item 4) does [[s04-W28-2]] on side 2 alone first, then joins for (c).

**Ask.** After (a): "Is 4/8 a new number, or a new name for 1/2?" Expect: a new name; both sit at the same point
on the line.

#### 3 min. Day 27's tickets and written task

Hand back Lee's sums, look-fors ticked. Whole class for what more than a third showed; otherwise the students named, at your
table, while the rest read side 1.

- **If day 27's tickets showed many C** (5/10, the 2 and the 3 put on top): write "3 1/5 = 4/5: the 3 goes on
  top" beside a sketch of three whole strips in fifths and one fifth more, 5/5 + 5/5 + 5/5 + 1/5 = 16/5. Ask
  how many strips 5/5 fills. A "yes" on Lee's sum is C too.
- **13/10 (one whole counted):** those students sketch two whole strips in tenths with a partner and count.

#### 8 min. Teaching first: count up from the part you have

Whole class; students fill the frames on side 1. No pieces: say the parts in words.

**Example 1** ([[s04-K28-1]]).

**Ask.** "Before we count: what size will the missing part be?" Expect: eighths; joining eighths cannot make
another size.

[[steps:s04-K28-1]]

**Ask.** After step 3: "Is there a faster way than counting one at a time?" Expect: 3 and 7 make 10, so 7
eighths: the tops are a fact.

**Example 2** ([[s04-K28-2]]).

**Ask.** "What is the first step here, and why?" Expect: write 1 as twelfths, 12/12, so all three numbers count
the same parts. Take "count up from 4" and ask: count up to what?

[[steps:s04-K28-2]]

#### 6 min. Fix it: Max and Kit

Pairs, two minutes, then the class. "Fix it", side 1 ([[s04-F28-1]], [[s04-F28-2]]).

**Ask.** "Max wrote 7/0. How big is one zeroth?" Expect: there is no such part; the parts are tenths, so the
missing part is tenths. [[steps:s04-F28-1]]

**Ask.** "Kit's 1 went on top. What is 1 in eighths?" Expect: 8/8, so count from 3 to 8. [[steps:s04-F28-2]]

If many still take the bottoms away: say the sum in words, "four tenths and how many tenths make eleven
tenths?"

#### 18 min. Make it true

Alone, then pairs. "Make it true": set A on side 1 ([[s04-P28-1]]), then set B on side 2 ([[s04-P28-2]]). Under set B each
student writes the first step for (c) in a sentence. Then partners compare: did you start the same way? Where
answers differ, each sketches a strip for that one in the box on side 2. Then the display
shows both keys.

Check: Is the bottom of each missing part the same as the others? Do your part and the given part join to
make the sum?

Watch for a 0 on the bottom, such as 4/0 or 3/0 on set A (error A), and 4/4 on set B (a), the 1 put on top with the 3 (error C), and 77/100 on set A (c), the two numbers added. Ask as
you walk: "How many fourths fill one whole?" For A (d), 8/8 and 1 are both right. For B (c), 2 is 6/3 first.
For A (c) and B (d), count on by tens, 20/100, 30/100 ... 50/100, then 7 more, or use the fact 20 + 37 = 57.

If many miss set B: write 1 = 4/4, 1 = 10/10, 2 = 6/3 on the board and have them say each aloud.

#### 8 min. True or false

Pairs ([[s04-P28-3]]). Sort each sum without working every one out. Then the class.

**Ask.** "Which could you tell at a glance? How?" Expect: (b), the bottom changed to twelfths, but sixths
joined stay sixths; (a) and (d), the tops add to the bottom, so the sum is one whole.

Check: (a) true, (b) false, (c) false, (d) true, (e) true, (f) false. Fix any in pen.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "When the whole was 1, what did you do first? Did your partner start the same way?" Expect: wrote 1 as
the same parts as the other number, 1 = 4/4 or 1 = 10/10. Some counted up, some thought of a fact: both work.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s04-X28]]). No pieces; a sketch is allowed. Record under E28.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** The examples, the fixes, both sets and the sort on the board; notebooks; the sketch in the
  margin.
- **No projector.** Write each step on the board as you say it; read the keys aloud for the partner check.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No pieces needed today: draw a strip and split it when you disagree.

**Skip.** The warm-up, day 27's tickets and the challenge.

- **8 min. Two examples.** [[s04-K28-1]], then [[s04-K28-2]]: read each step; the child says the size first.

    **Say.** "What is 1 in twelfths?" Expect: 12/12, so 8 more than 4/12.

- **4 min. Fix it.** [[s04-F28-1]].

    **Say.** "What size are the parts?" Expect: tenths, so the answer is 7/10, not 7/0.

- **4 min. Fix it 2.** [[s04-F28-2]].

    **Say.** "What is 1 in eighths?" Expect: 8/8, so 5/8.

- **8 min. Make it true.** [[s04-P28-1]], then [[s04-P28-2]]. The child says the first step aloud.
- **6 min. True or false.** [[s04-P28-3]].

    **Say.** "Which can you tell at a glance?" Expect: (b): sixths joined stay sixths.

- **7 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s04-X28]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop True or false (e) and (f) and the Pull it together Ask; never the fixes, set B or the exit.
Longer: the challenge ([[s04-C28-1]]). The search ends: the two blanks make 7/8, the smaller one can only be
1/8, 2/8 or 3/8, and past that the same parts come back in a new order (error D).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, whole, missing part. Frame, on side 2: "My first step for (c): ____." Say it as "I wrote __ as __ because __."
Access: a student may sketch every item; a helper may write the sentence as the student says it.

### Tomorrow

On (b), 3/6 (error C, 1 not seen as 6/6): if many, day 29 opens with 1 = 5/10 + __ answered 6/10, "the 1 goes
on top with the 5", beside 1 = 10/10, counted up from 5 tenths: 5/10. On (a) or (c), 3/0 or 7/0 (error A, the
bottoms taken away too) is met in day 29's teaching; if many, point at it there. Day 29
brings the line back for adding.
