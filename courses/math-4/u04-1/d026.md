# Day 26: One amount, more than one way

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, strip sheets, tickets and key. Students cut the eighths and twelfths strips into pieces for
the day 25 envelope, as morning work (or hand out pre-cut). Have day 25's tickets and the readiness marks (R1 to
R4) beside you.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: facts within 20

Alone ([[s04-W26-1]]). Students who missed readiness item 4 sit with you and say how they found each fact ("9 and
1 make 10, and 5 more"); they keep the facts warm-ups through day 31. At 4 minutes, show the answers.

Check: Is yours the same? Fix it in pen.

#### 5 min. Readiness gaps and day 25's tickets

Whole class for what more than a third showed; otherwise the students named, at your table, while the rest check
their envelopes, thirds to twelfths.

- **If day 25's tickets showed many B** (five 1/5s, or "less, it is a fraction"), or readiness item 1: show "There
  is no 4/3: 3/3 is all of it" beside third-pieces counted aloud past 1 on line 1, thirds marked by eye.
- **If many A** (a sum = 6/30): show "1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 + 1/10 = 4/40" beside four tenth-pieces, said as "four
  tenths".
- **Five 1/6s:** those students read 6/5 aloud as "six fifths" and lay six fifth-pieces with a partner.
- **Readiness item 2:** four fourth-pieces under each of two whole strips, counted. **Item 3:** unit 3's fold,
  one third into two sixths.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: one amount, ringed two ways

Whole class. You lay pieces under the camera; students lay theirs and ring Example 1's two strips. Read every
fraction as words.

**Example 1** ([[s04-K26-1]]). [[steps:s04-K26-1]]

**Ask.** After step 3: "Is 2/6 + 3/6 the same way as 1/6 + 1/6 + 3/6? How do you know?" Expect: no; the rings
hold 2 and 3 pieces, then 1, 1 and 3.

#### 20 min. Recipe Scaler: building rounds, or the same on paper

When it exists and its building rounds open by themselves: alone, Party kitchen, Building, from Grandpa Lou's
round ([[s04-T26-1]]); each second way needs its "because" sentence. **Stop at 20 minutes.**

Until then, on paper. Alone, 15 minutes: "Building rounds", side 1 ([[s04-T26-2]], [[s04-T26-3]], [[s04-T26-4]],
[[s04-T26-6]]), one piece a scoop. Lay the pieces, write the unit fractions, then ring a second way and write it.
Then 5 minutes, partners swap pages.

Check: Do both ways make the same amount? Are the rings a new set of sizes?

Watch for a second way that is the first written again (error D) and two fourth-pieces written 2/8 (error
A). Ask as you walk: "How many pieces in each ring?" Cornmeal has no second way: it waits for the end. Rounds 5 and 7 to 9 wait for
the tool: 5/8 is the fix, and wholes are day 27's.

#### 4 min. Fix it: a new order

Pairs, one minute, then the class. "Fix it", side 2 ([[s04-F26-1]]).

**Ask.** "Is 3/8 + 2/8 a new way if you already wrote 2/8 + 3/8?" Expect: no; the same two rings, 2 pieces and
3, in a new order. [[steps:s04-F26-1]]

If many still count a swap: ring five eighth-pieces under the camera in sizes not used yet.

#### 10 min. Three ways each, on the line

Alone, pieces on the line page. "Three ways", side 2 ([[s04-P26-1]], [[s04-P26-2]]): 6/10 on line 4, then the
strip; 9/12 on line 5. Every way is an equation.

Watch for 2/5 + 4/5 for 6/10 (error A) and a two-part way that swaps the first (error D). Ask: "What size is
each piece in this ring?" If many split the bottom: say each ring in words, "two tenths"; the pieces are still
tenths.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Cornmeal, 2/3, had only one way. Can 1/10 be taken apart into other tenths?" Expect: no; 1/10 is one
piece, and a smaller piece is not a tenth. 2/10 has one way; from 3/10 up there are more.

**Ask.** "Why does each of your ways make 6/10?" Expect: each groups the same six tenth-pieces; grouping adds
none, takes none away and does not change their size. Take "the tops add to 6", then ask why the bottom stays 10.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s04-X26]]). Read it aloud once. Pieces stay in the envelope. A tick needs two different sums
that make 7/12 and a strip with 7 parts shaded and ringed. Record under E26.

### Other rooms

- **No strip sheet.** Fold scrap strips of one length: eighths, in half three times; twelfths, in thirds, then
  in half twice.
- **No printer.** The rounds, the fix and the two amounts on the board; notebooks and hand-ruled strips.

### At home

**Swap.** No pieces: cut paper strips of one length into sixths, eighths and tenths. No line page: lay the pieces
in a row. No device: the Building rounds on side 1 are the job. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the reteach, the tool, two of the four rounds, 9/12 and the challenge.

- **6 min. The example.** [[s04-K26-1]]: you lay five sixth-pieces; the child groups them two ways, rings each
  way on a strip and writes each equation.

    **Say.** "Is 2/6 + 3/6 the same way as 1/6 + 1/6 + 3/6?" Expect: no; the groups are 2 and 3 pieces, then 1,
    1 and 3.

- **8 min. Two rounds.** [[s04-T26-4]], then [[s04-T26-6]]: one piece a scoop; the sum, then a second way.
- **5 min. Fix it.** [[s04-F26-1]].

    **Say.** "Is 3/8 + 2/8 a new way if you already wrote 2/8 + 3/8?" Expect: no; the same two groups in a new
    order.

- **8 min. Three ways.** [[s04-P26-1]]: three different sums of tenths, then the strip.
- **7 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s04-X26]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop cornmeal, 9/12's four-part way and the challenge; never the fix, 6/10 or the exit. Longer: the
challenge ([[s04-C26-1]]). The search ends: write each way smallest part first, no part smaller than the one
before; the first part stops at 3/10 for two parts, 2/10 for three.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, equation, unit fraction. Frame, to take or leave: "__ = __ + __, and also __ = __ + __ +
__." Access: a different coloured pencil for each ring; a helper rings as the student points.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, a split bottom such as 3/6 + 4/6 (error A): day 27 opens on A's wrong example, said in words
beside its pieces. The same two parts swapped (error D): day 27 opens on D's wrong example beside rings of new
sizes. A sum making 6/12 or 8/12: those students recount with twelfth-pieces and a partner. Pieces stay in
envelopes; day 27 lays whole strips under them.
