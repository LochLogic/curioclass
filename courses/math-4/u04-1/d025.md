# Day 25: A fraction as a sum of pieces

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, line pages, tickets, key and family note. As morning work, students cut the thirds,
fourths, fifths, sixths and tenths strips into single pieces; the whole, eighths, twelfths and blank strips go in
the envelope uncut. Have day 24's assessments and the readiness marks beside you. Seat those who stopped at 4/4 on
readiness item 1 near the camera.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: count by halves

Whole class, aloud. Count together by halves from one half to eight halves ([[s04-W25-1]]); write each count on
the board as it is said. Then, alone, students write the counts that are whole numbers.

**Ask.** "Which counts are whole numbers? Why every second one?" Expect: 2/2, 4/2, 6/2, 8/2 are 1, 2, 3, 4; two
halves make each new whole. Then: "Did the count stop at 1?" Expect: no, it went on to 8/2.

#### 5 min. Day 24's items 2 and 5 back

Alone, in pen. Hand back day 24's assessments, look-fors ticked; students fix items 2 and 5 beside the first
try. Agreed with Rosa (unit 3's D): lay two sixth-pieces beside the twelfths strip, still whole, and count four
twelfths. Part size alone (unit 3's J): say each as "how many parts, of what size". Item 5 with unit 3's D or G: one line from 0 to 1, both
points, read the sign aloud.

#### 15 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. You lay pieces under the camera; students lay their own in a row on the desk and fill the frames on
side 1. The line only counts and marks the end. Read every fraction as words: "three fifths", never "three over five".

**Example 1** ([[s04-K25-1]]). [[steps:s04-K25-1]]

**Ask.** After step 4: "How many pieces, and how big is each one?" Expect: three, each one fifth.

**Example 2** ([[s04-K25-2v2]]). Pairs pool their fourth-pieces: one envelope holds only four. [[steps:s04-K25-2v2]]

**Ask.** After step 5: "Is 7/4 more or less than 1? How do you know?" Expect: more; four fourths make 1, and
three more are past it.

#### 14 min. Board paths

Pairs, pieces pooled. "Board paths", side 2 ([[s04-P25-1]], [[s04-P25-2]], [[s04-P25-3]]). For each path,
students lay one piece a board in a row, write one unit fraction a piece, then answer more or less than 1 with a
reason. Then they mark the path's end on the line page: line 3 (sixths) for paths 1 and 3; line 1 for path 2,
after marking thirds by eye, two marks in each whole.

Watch for a sum with the bottoms added (5/15 on path 2: error A) and a count that stops at 6/6 on path 3 (error
B). Ask as you walk: "How does your row of pieces show the sum? Where is 1 on your row?"

#### 8 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then the class. "Fix it", side 2 ([[s04-F25-1]],
[[s04-F25-2v2]]). The display shows the wrong picture; the right one comes after the Ask.

**Ask.** After step 1: "Bo's row is 4 tenth-pieces. Is 4/40 that long?" Expect: no; 4/40 is 4 tiny parts. [[steps:s04-F25-1]]

**Ask.** After step 1: "Ada says 3/3 is all of it. Lay a fourth third-piece. Where does it end?" Expect: past 1,
at 4/3. [[steps:s04-F25-2v2]]

If many still add the bottoms: say each sum in words, "four tenths", and lay the row again. If many still stop at
1: lay third-pieces in a row past one whole strip, then count thirds aloud on line 1, marked by eye.

#### 6 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Five thirds, path 2: how many pieces, and how big is each one?" Expect: five pieces, each one third; it
is past 1 because three thirds make 1. Take "five pieces" first, then the size.

**Ask.** "In 5/3, which number tells the size of a piece, and which counts them?" Expect: the 3 names the size,
thirds; the 5 counts the pieces.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s04-X25]]). Pieces stay in the envelope. A tick needs the sum and "more"
with a reason: five fifths make 1 and one fifth more (or past 1 on a line); "the top is bigger" is not one. Record under E25 on the record sheet. Send the family note home.

### Other rooms

- **No strip sheet.** Rule strips 30 cm long: thirds every 10 cm, fourths every 7.5 cm, fifths every 6 cm,
  sixths every 5 cm, tenths every 3 cm. Cut them.
- **No printer.** Write the three paths and the two fixes on the board; students answer in notebooks and draw a
  line from 0 to 2 by hand.

### At home

**Swap.** No pieces: cut paper strips of one length into thirds, fourths, fifths and sixths, two strips of each. No
line page: draw a line from 0 to 2. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, day 24's hand-back, the tenths fix and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** [[s04-K25-1]], then [[s04-K25-2v2]]: you lay the pieces, the child lays them too,
  writes one unit fraction for each piece and fills the frame.

    **Say.** "How many pieces, and how big is each one?" Expect: seven, each one fourth.

- **12 min. Board paths.** [[s04-P25-1]], [[s04-P25-2]], [[s04-P25-3]]: one piece a board, then the sum, then
  more or less than 1.

    **Say.** "Where is 1 on your row?" Expect: after six sixths on path 3; one more sixth is past it.

- **6 min. Fix it.** [[s04-F25-2v2]]: the child lays a fourth third-piece and says where it ends.
- **7 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s04-X25]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop path 1 and the challenge. Never drop the fixes, path 3 or the exit. Longer: the challenge
([[s04-C25-1]]). Then: how many different paths can Ren make from the two kinds of boards? A new order is not a new
way. The search ends at two: one or two long boards; three would pass 7/6. A long board is three sixth-pieces
pushed together.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: unit fraction, sum, equation, whole, each on a labelled piece. Frame, to take or leave: "__ is
__ pieces of size __, so __ = __ + __ + ..." Access: wide pre-cut pieces; a helper lays pieces as the student
points.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, five 1/5s or "less, it is a fraction" (error B): day 26 shows "There is no 6/4: 4/4 is all of it"
beside fourth-pieces in a row past a whole strip, in its first five minutes. A sum = 6/30 (error A): day 26
shows "1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = 3/15" beside three fifth-pieces. Five 1/6s (top and bottom read the other way): in
the same minutes, those students read 6/5 aloud as "six fifths" and lay six fifth-pieces with a partner. Readiness gaps go to day 26, as the section
opener says. Pieces stay in envelopes for day 26's rings.
