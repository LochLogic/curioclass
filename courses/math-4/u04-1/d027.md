# Day 27: Wholes are parts too

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, tickets and key. Students take three blank whole strips from the envelope. Have day 26's
tickets and the facts group's names beside you.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: groups of twelfths

Whole class, in their heads ([[s04-W27-1]]). Write one sum at a time on the board; hands up when ready; write each answer
and one student's way on the board. The facts group (readiness item 4) does [[s04-W27-2]] alone first, then
joins for (c).

**Ask.** After (c): "Which sums made a whole? How do you know?" Expect: (b), 12/12, twelve twelfths cover one
strip; (c), 18/12 is one whole, 12/12, and 6/12 more.

#### 3 min. Day 26's tickets

Whole class for what more than a third showed; otherwise the students named, at your table, while the rest lay
out their whole strips and sixth-pieces.

- **If day 26's tickets showed many A** (3/6 + 4/6 for 7/12): lay seven twelfth-pieces, ring 3 and 4, and write
  "7/12 = 3/6 + 4/6?" beside them. The class says each ring: "three twelfths and four twelfths". The rings hold
  twelfths, so the bottom stays 12.
- **If many D** (the same two parts swapped): show "4/10 + 3/10 and 3/10 + 4/10" beside seven tenth-pieces
  ringed 1, 2 and 4: a new way needs new ring sizes.
- **A sum making 6/12 or 8/12:** those students recount seven twelfth-pieces with a partner.

#### 15 min. Teaching first: whole strips under the pieces

Whole class. You lay strips and pieces under the camera; students lay theirs and fill the frames on side 1. Pairs
pool pieces for the 6/5 test and Example 3; threes for Example 2. Read every fraction as words.

**Example 1** ([[s04-K27-1]]). [[steps:s04-K27-1]]

**Ask.** "Why can we write 1 as 6/6 but not as 6/5?" Students lay six fifth-pieces on a whole strip to test it.
Expect: six sixths cover the strip exactly; five fifths already cover it, so the sixth fifth-piece runs past the
end: 6/5 is more than 1.

Readiness item 2 (2 written 2/4): those students, pairs pooled, cover a second strip: 2 = 12/6.

**Example 2** ([[s04-K27-2]]). [[steps:s04-K27-2]]

**Ask.** After step 4: "On line 9, how many sixths from 0 to 1? From 0 to 2?" Expect: six; twelve. Each space is
one sixth, so 13/6 is one space past 2.

**Example 3** ([[s04-K27-3]]). This one starts from the fraction. [[steps:s04-K27-3]]

**Ask.** After step 3: "Rui wrote 10/6 = 6/6 + 4/6. Bea wrote 10/6 = 1 + 4/6. Who is right?" Expect: both;
6/6 and 1 cover the same whole strip. Take "Rui, the bottoms match" and ask what 6/6 covers.

#### 6 min. Fix it: the whole on top

Pairs, two minutes, then the class. "Fix it", side 1 ([[s04-F27-1]]).

**Ask.** "How many whole strips does Jo's 3/3 cover? How many does 2 1/3 need?" Expect: one; two, and a third
more. [[steps:s04-F27-1]]

If many still put the whole number on top: write the whole as a sum first, 2 = 1 + 1 = 3/3 + 3/3, lay a whole
strip of thirds for each 1, then count the thirds.

#### 12 min. Wholes and parts, then the line

Alone. "Wholes and parts", side 2 ([[s04-P27-1]], [[s04-P27-2]], [[s04-P27-3]], [[s04-P27-4]]). Question 2
with pieces on a whole strip, in pairs; question 3 with strips, in threes, then on line 9; question 4 on line 7
only, halves marked by eye; question 5 from the fraction.

Watch for 2 2/6 written 4/6 (error C), a sum with one 6/6 where two are needed (one whole counted), and a
bottom that grows (error A). Ask as you walk: "How many sixths fill one whole?" For 5 (a), 1 + 6/4 is right: a
true sum whose parts still hold a whole's worth.

If many miss 3 1/2 on the line: count halves aloud from 0, two to each whole, then write 2/2 under each 1.

#### 8 min. Written task: Lee's sum

Alone ([[s04-W27]]). Read Lee's claim aloud once. Pieces and strips may be used; the drawing goes in the box.
Collect; judge after class with the three look-fors and record under W27, one tick a look-for.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "10/4 had two right answers, 1 + 6/4 and 2 + 2/4. Which fills every whole it can? How do you know?"
Expect: 2 + 2/4; 6/4 still holds four fourths, a whole, and 2/4 cannot fill one.

**Ask.** "Why does the bottom stay 6 when a whole is written as 6/6?" Expect: the whole is cut into sixths, so
we count sixth-pieces; counting does not change their size. Sketch a strip in tenths, parts roughly equal, and
write 10/10 on it: the exit's strips are drawn this way.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s04-X27]]). Read it aloud once. Pieces stay in the envelope; strips are drawn. A tick needs
10/10 + 10/10 + 3/10, then 23/10, and two whole strips in tenths with 3 tenths more; roughly equal tenths, or a
strip labelled 10/10, counts. Record under E27.

### Other rooms

- **No blank whole strips.** Cut scrap paper to the length of six sixth-pieces laid end to end.
- **No printer.** The examples, the fix and the four questions on the board; notebooks; lines from 0 to 5
  ruled by hand, sixths and halves marked by eye.

### At home

**Swap.** No pieces: draw three strips of one length, each split into as many equal boxes as the item's bottom number (6, 3 or 4), and shade instead of
laying pieces. No line page: draw a line from 0 to 3. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, day 26's tickets, Example 3, questions 4 and 5, and the challenge.

- **8 min. Wholes as sixths.** [[s04-K27-1]], then [[s04-K27-2]]: the child lays or shades the pieces and writes
  each sum.

    **Say.** "Why can we write 1 as 6/6 but not as 6/5?" Expect: six sixths fill one strip exactly; six
    fifths go past it.

- **5 min. Fix it.** [[s04-F27-1]].

    **Say.** "How many whole strips does 3/3 fill? How many does 2 1/3 need?" Expect: one; two and a third
    more, so 3/3 + 3/3 + 1/3 = 7/3.

- **8 min. Two on your own.** [[s04-P27-1]], then [[s04-P27-2]].
- **7 min. Lee's sum.** [[s04-W27]]: the child draws, then says why.
- **7 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s04-X27]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop question 4 and the second Ask in Pull it together; never the fix, question 3, Lee's sum or the
exit. Longer: the challenge ([[s04-C27-1v2]]). The search ends: each whole uses 4 fourths, and a third whole
needs 12/4, more than 9/4.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: whole, sum, equation, unit fraction. Frame, to take or leave, on side 1: "One whole is __
pieces of size __, so 1 = __." Access: tape the whole strip to the desk so pieces do not slide; a helper lays
pieces as the student points.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, 5/10 (error C, the 2 and the 3 put on top): day 28 opens with C's wrong example, three
minutes. 13/10 (one whole counted): those students sketch two whole strips in tenths with a partner and count. Lee's sums
go back in day 28's first minutes, look-fors ticked; a "yes" there is C too. Pieces are put away after today:
day 28 sketches only.
