# Day 75: Ungroup more than once, then check by adding

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print line. Look at readiness items 3 and 4 (R3, R4) and decide: whole class, or a small
group; only one branch below runs. Mark the E74 slips and sort them: F (smaller digit from
the larger) and G (tens left as they were). Put the place name cards up and the display page on the projector. Write the warm-up
on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one ungrouping

Alone, on grid paper: [[s09-P75-11v2]], [[s09-P75-12v2]], then [[s09-P75-13]] if there is time. Read the answers aloud.
If only a few missed readiness items 3 and 4, do the reteach below with them now.

#### 5 min. Readiness reteach, or E74 back

Hand back the day 74 slips.

**If many missed readiness items 3 and 4:** write 734 − 258 in expanded form, rewrite 700 + 30 + 4 as 600 + 120
+ 14, and subtract each place; then the same in columns. Then say 402 as 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones before
writing. Name E74's error inside the worked difference that follows.

**If not, and day 74's slips showed many F** (3,336: the smaller digit taken from the larger): F's wrong example
again, 5,842 − 1,718 shown as 4,136 beside 4,124. Ask "can I take 8 from 2?" in every column, then ungroup.

**If many G** (3,334, or "8 tens and 13 ones": the 1 written, nothing traded): G's wrong example again, 9,372 −
4,156 shown as 5,226 beside 5,216, in expanded form beside the algorithm. Ask what the 7 tens became.

**If both:** F first; then G inside the worked difference, asking at each ungrouping what the place became.

If neither: one student reads their E74 difference, the place they ungrouped and what it became.

#### 7 min. Teaching first: three ungroupings

Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go. No expanded form today; the place names stay up.

**Example** ([[s09-K75-1]]). Estimate, subtract, check by adding.
[[steps:s09-K75-1]]

Say the point once: find every ungrouping first, write the new top number, then subtract. The estimate catches
a big mistake, and adding back catches a small one. Either order is right if the add-back works.

#### 14 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, problems 1 to 3 ([[s09-P75-1]], [[s09-P75-2]], [[s09-P75-3]]), on the column sheet; add back on its back.
Each: estimate, subtract, add back. Any close estimate is right; it only has to catch a big mistake.
Walk with one question: "Can you take that digit from the one above it?" Watch for:

- 5,335, 45,345 or 97,422: the smaller digit taken from the larger (error F).
- 5,275, 34,665 or 83,388: an ungrouping written but the place not made smaller (error H, or G if nothing was
  traded). Ask them to add back; it will not reach the top number.
- 8,156 on problem 3: 8,358 lined up from the left (error B). Ask for the estimate first.

At 12 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own and fix in pen. If many miss problem 2,
redo its tens and hundreds together on the board.

Check: Did your check by adding come back to the top number?

#### 13 min. Set 2: place names down

Take the place name cards down. Alone, problems 4 to 6 ([[s09-P75-4]], [[s09-P75-5]], [[s09-P75-6]]), on grid
paper, adding back under each difference. Same routine.

Watch for 513,533 or 531,333 (F) and an answer too big by exactly 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 or 100,000 (H). At 11
minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own.

Check: Is each difference near its estimate? Does adding back give the top number?

#### 6 min. Fix it: Ty and Nia

Side 2, problems 7 and 8 ([[s09-F75-1]], [[s09-F75-2]]). One minute alone on each, then the class; the display
shows each working beside the right one.

Ty (error B): ask for the estimate before any column. About 41,000 says 6,466 cannot be right; then line up the
ones. Nia (error H): add back first. 92,415 is not 82,415, so a place went wrong; find the place that was
ungrouped but not made smaller: the 8 ten thousands should be 7.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

**Ask.** "How do you know 52,918 − 4,375 is not 9,168?" Expect: "about 53,000 − 4,000 is about 49,000".

**Ask.** "Why does adding back prove the difference?" Expect: the difference and the number taken away make the
top number again.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s09-X75]]). Place names stay down. Collect at the door;
write results on the record sheet under E75.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** The example and six problems on the board, one at a time; columns on lined paper turned
  sideways, the place names written at the top of set 1 only.
- **One child at home.** Do the example together, then problems 1, 2 and 4. The child says "can I take it?"
  aloud in every column, and adds back after each one.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: in set 2 do problems 4 and 6 only. Never drop the estimate, the adding back, or the fixes. Longer: the
challenge ([[s09-C75-1]]): adding back finds the missing digit, so the check becomes the method.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: difference, ungroup, estimate, check, and the place names to hundred thousands. Frame on the display and side 1: "1 __ is 10 __." Large-square grid paper and
typed columns for a student who cannot write small digits.

### Tomorrow

On the slip, 336,964, 337,864, 346,864 or 436,864 (error H: an ungrouped place not made smaller) or 463,144
(error F: the smaller digit taken from the larger): day 76's first three minutes show H's wrong example again,
ungrouping everything first (H), or the slip's own difference, "can I take 2 from 6?" in every column (F). If
neither, one student reads their estimate and difference. A slip
with no estimate counts as half; say so when it goes back. Then day 76 teaches subtracting when the top number
has zeros.
