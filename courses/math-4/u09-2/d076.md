# Day 76: Subtracting across zeros

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print line. Mark the E75 tickets and sort them: H (one ungrouping not taken away), F
(smaller digit from the larger). From the readiness check, list who
missed item 4. Write the warm-up on the board. Put the display page on the projector. Put out grid paper or
the blank column sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: differences and tens

Alone, on grid paper: [[s09-P76-11]], [[s09-P76-12]], [[s09-P76-13v2]], [[s09-P76-14]]. The two differences
are checked by adding; the two products are read aloud. Watch for a smaller digit taken from a larger one (F): ask "can I take 8 from
1?"

#### 3 min. E75 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 75 tickets.

**If day 75's tickets showed many H** (such as 336,964): H's wrong example, 82,415 − 36,278 = 56,137 beside
46,137; ungroup everything first, then subtract.

**If many F** (463,144): the ticket's own difference on the board, "can I take 2 from 6?" asked in every column,
then ungroup.

Otherwise, one student reads their estimate and difference. A ticket with no estimate counts as half; say so as
it goes back.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: zeros in the top number

Expanded form comes back today, for zeros only. Students who missed readiness item 4 sit near you
for both examples. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go. Place names stay over the columns.

**Example 1** ([[s09-K76-1v2]]).
[[steps:s09-K76-1v2]]

Say why this is honest: 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones and 4 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones are the same number. One
hundred became ten tens, and one ten became ten ones. Nothing was added or lost. A zero between keeps 9: it took
ten and passed one on. The place that needed ones keeps all 10.

**Example 2** ([[s09-K76-2]]).
[[steps:s09-K76-2]]

The habit for the day: rename the whole top number in one line before any column is subtracted. Adding the
answer to the number taken away is a full check: subtracting asks "what joins 2,347 to make 6,005?", so the
two parts must add back to the whole.

#### 5 min. Fix it: Kai across zeros

Side 1 ([[s09-F76-1]]). Two minutes alone: what went wrong, and Kai's add-back. Then the class does the right
working on the display. Kai's check is the clue: 3,276 + 1,836 is 5,112, not 5,002. Kai turned 5,002 into 4
thousands, 10 hundreds, 10 tens, 12 ones, and that is 5,112.

#### 20 min. Six differences across zeros

Alone, on grid paper, side 2 ([[s09-P76-1]], [[s09-P76-2]], [[s09-P76-3]], [[s09-P76-4]], [[s09-P76-5]],
[[s09-P76-6]]). For each: rename the top number in one line, subtract, then add to check. Everyone finishes 1
to 4; 5 and 6 are for those who are ready.

Walk with one question: "What did that zero become, and why?" Watch for:

- 356, 5,436, 18,648, 33,664, 64,927 or 258,756: each zero made 10 and left 10 (error I). Say the rename aloud:
  "9,004 is 8 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 14 ones."
- 32,653, 53,816 or 248,655: every zero made 9, even the place that needed ones (error I). Ask: which place
  needed the ones?
- 454, 6,674, 22,462, 47,346, 146,183 or 351,344: smaller digit taken from the larger (error F). Ask "can I
  take 7 from 3?"
- An adding check that does not come back to the top number, left as it is. The check found a mistake; find it.

At 12 minutes, the display shows the answers; students check their own and fix in pen. If many wrote 10 over a
zero, rename Example 2 again with the class, in words before digits.

Check: Add your answer to the number taken away. Do you get the top number?

#### 7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 ([[s09-W76v2]]). Two sentences; a student may dictate them to you. Collect them: they are judged
after class. Read the look-fors on the key.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

**Ask.** "In 500 − 263, why does the tens zero become 9 but the ones zero become 10?" Expect: "the tens gave one
away"; "the ones were the place that needed it". Take a marks-only answer first ("you cross out and write 9"),
then one that says what was ungrouped. Show Example 1's rename beside 400 + 90 + 10 = 500.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s09-X76]]). Collect at the door; write results on
the record sheet under E76.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Examples and the six differences on the board; all work on grid paper or lined paper turned
  sideways.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. Grid paper: lined paper turned sideways.

**Skip.** The warm-up, problems 5 and 6, the written task and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** [[s09-K76-1v2]], then [[s09-K76-2]]. Say the renamed top number together before
  writing: "6,005 is 5 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones."

    **Say.** "In 500 − 263, why does the tens zero become 9 but the ones zero become 10?" Expect: the tens
    passed one on; the ones were the place that needed it.

- **5 min. Fix it.** [[s09-F76-1]]. The child adds Kai's answer back: 3,276 + 1,836 is 5,112, not 5,002.
- **15 min. Four differences.** [[s09-P76-1]], [[s09-P76-2]], [[s09-P76-3]], [[s09-P76-4]]: rename the top
  number in one line, subtract, then add to check. If the check misses, find the mistake together.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s09-X76]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: problems 1 to 4. Never drop the rename, the adding check or the fix.
Longer: the challenge ([[s09-C76-1]]): add to find the start, then subtract across zeros to check.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: ungroup, rename, difference, check, zero, and the place names. Frames, printed on the student page: "1 __ is 10 __." "__ renamed: __ thousands, __ hundreds, __ tens,
__ ones."
