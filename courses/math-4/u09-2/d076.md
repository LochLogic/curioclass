# Day 76: Subtracting across zeros

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, the exit slips and the key. Mark the E75 slips and sort them: H (one ungrouping not
taken away), F (smaller digit from the larger), B (lined up from the left). From the readiness check, list who
missed item 4. Write the warm-up on the board. Put the display page on the projector. Put out grid paper or
the blank column sheet.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: differences and tens

Alone, on grid paper: [[s09-P76-11]], [[s09-P76-12]], [[s09-P76-13]], [[s09-P76-14]]. Each difference is
checked by adding, as on day 75. Watch for a smaller digit taken from a larger one (F): ask "can I take 8 from
1?"

#### 3 min. E75 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 75 slips.

**If day 75's slips showed many H** (one ungrouping not taken away, such as 336,964) **or many F** (463,144):
put H's wrong example on the board, 82,415 − 36,278 = 56,137 beside 46,137. Ungroup everything first, write
the whole new top number, then subtract.

**If many B** (lined up from the left): put 45,206 − 3,874 = 6,466 beside its estimate, about 41,000. Say both
numbers aloud first.

Otherwise, one student reads their estimate and their adding check.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: zeros in the top number

Whole class. Expanded form comes back today, for zeros only. Students who missed readiness item 4 sit near you
for both examples. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go. Place names stay over the columns.

**Example 1** ([[s09-K76-1]]).
[[steps:s09-K76-1]]

Say why this is honest: 4 hundreds, 0 tens, 2 ones and 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones are the same number. One
hundred became ten tens, and one ten became ten ones. Nothing was added or lost. That is why the zero becomes 9,
not 10: it took one hundred's ten tens and passed one ten on.

**Example 2** ([[s09-K76-2]]).
[[steps:s09-K76-2]]

The habit for the day: rename the whole top number in one line before any column is subtracted. Adding the
answer to the number taken away is a full check: subtracting asks "what joins 2,347 to make 6,005?", so the
two parts must add back to the whole.

#### 20 min. Six differences across zeros

Alone, on grid paper, side 2. Problem 1 has one zero, 2 has two, 3 and 4 have three, 5 starts at 100,000, and 6 has six digits.
For each: rename the top number in one line, subtract, then add to check. Everyone finishes 1 to 4.

Walk with one question: "What did that zero become, and why?" Watch for:

- 356, 5,436 or 18,648: each zero made 10 and left 10 (error I). Say the rename aloud: "9,004 is 8 thousands, 9
  hundreds, 9 tens, 14 ones."
- 454, 6,674 or 22,462: smaller digit taken from the larger (error F). Ask "can I take 7 from 3?"
- An adding check that does not come back to the top number, left as it is. The check found a mistake; find it.

At 10 minutes, partners compare problems 1 to 3. If many wrote 10 over a zero, stop: rename Example 2 again
with the class, in words before digits.

Check: Add your answer to the number taken away. Do you get the top number?

#### 5 min. Fix it: Kai across zeros

Side 1 ([[s09-F76-1]]). Two minutes alone, then pairs, then the class: the display shows Kai's working beside
the right working. Kai's check is the clue: 3,276 + 1,836 is 5,112, not 5,002. Kai turned 5,002 into 4
thousands, 10 hundreds, 10 tens, 12 ones, and that is 5,112. If many still write 10s: rename 5,002 together, in
words.

#### 7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 ([[s09-W76]]). Two sentences; a student may dictate them to you. Collect them: they are judged
after class. Read the look-fors on the key.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Why do the two zeros in 6,005 turn into 9s and not 10s?" Expect: "each one passes one on"; "one
thousand went to ten hundreds, then one hundred went to ten tens"; "if they were 10s the number would be
bigger". Take a marks-only answer first ("you cross out and write 9"), then one that says what was ungrouped.
Show Example 2's rename beside 5,000 + 900 + 90 + 15 = 6,005.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s09-X76]]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on
the record sheet under E76.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Examples and the six differences on the board; all work on grid paper or lined paper turned
  sideways.
- **One child at home.** Say each renamed top number together before writing: "6,005 is 5 thousands, 9
  hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones." Then subtract and add back. Four differences are enough if they are right.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: problems 1 to 4, and the pair comparison drops. Never drop the rename, the adding check or the fix.
Longer: the challenge ([[s09-C76-1]]): add to find the start, then subtract across zeros to check.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: ungroup, rename, difference, check, zero, and the place names. Families may say "borrow"; all
are welcome. Frames, printed on the student page: "1 __ is 10 __." "__ is __ thousands, __ hundreds, __ tens, __
ones."

### Tomorrow

On the slip, 23,776 (error I: each zero left as 10) or 37,324 (error F: smaller digit from the larger), or no
check: on day 77, a check day, the marked slips and the written tasks go back when pencils go down. A student
who made I or F joins that group in the respond half. Day 77 starts with the mixed set, then the end check.
