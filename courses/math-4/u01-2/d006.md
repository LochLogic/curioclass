# Day 6: Show what I know

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Open the display; show the answers only at the marking. Write the three group
tasks on a covered part of the board, and, until unit 2's readiness sheet exists, its three items. Take down any chart that lists primes.

### The day

#### 30 min. End-of-unit assessment

Keep the day 5 tickets and written tasks until pencils go down. Alone, unaided: no tiles, no tool, no chart. A
student below the facts mark keeps the 9 by 9 table. Read each item aloud once. At 30 minutes, pencils down; not
reached is not wrong.

#### 20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

**Mark, 5 minutes.** Pencils away; hand back the day 5 tickets and written tasks; show the answers to 1 to 4.
Students tick or circle each in pen. You judge 5 and 6 after class.

**Groups, 15 minutes.** Hands up sets the groups; a student in two joins the earlier one. Each group fixes its
items in pen. Tasks are on the display.

- A to C (missed item 1): search 30 in order, saying "yes, with __" or "no" for 1 to 6, and stop at the turn. Cross out any turned pair. Test 3 and 4 on 42 and 56 from ten times. Then fix item 1.
- D and E (missed item 2 or 3): sort 1, 3, 9, 18, 27 and 54 into "factor of 9" and "multiple of 9", each said with its equation. 9 goes in both: 1 × 9 = 9 says it. Fence 58 between two products of 4, then of 6, starting from ten times. Then fix items 2 and 3.
- F and G (missed item 4, or the day 5 ticket or written task): search 39, 57 and 97 in order to the turn. Call a number prime only when the search finds nothing. Anyone who called 1 prime or 2 composite counts the factors of 1, 2, 3 and 4. Then fix item 4 and the ticket.

Students who missed nothing do [[s01-C6-1]] on the 1 to 100 chart. Collect the checks.

#### 10 min. Unit 2's readiness check

Alone, on its sheet; until the sheet exists, students write answers only: item 1, four numbers; item 3, the even
numbers. Read each item aloud once.

1. "Write the next four numbers: 6, 12, 18, __, __, __, __."
2. "4 × 3 = 12, 4 × 5 = 20, 4 × 7 = 28. Is 4 times a number always even? Write why, or draw."
3. "Circle the even numbers: 3, 8, 11, 14, 15, 17, 20."

Collect them.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: mark from the answers below.

**Skip.** The groups and the challenge.

- **30 min. End-of-unit assessment.** Items 1 to 6, alone, with no coins or chart; a child who has used the 9 by
  9 table keeps it. Read each item aloud once. Not reached is not wrong.
- **12 min. Go over it, one to one.** Mark items 1 to 4 together. The child fixes each missed one in pen beside
  the first try: [[s01-E1]], search 80 in order from 1 to the turn; [[s01-E2]], read each pair both ways;
  [[s01-E3]], start from 4 × 10 and 6 × 10; [[s01-E4v2]], a pair for each composite.

    **Say.** For [[s01-E6]]: "Why can Maya stop?" Expect: 7 goes with 5, which she has; every pair after is
    one she has, turned.

    Read [[s01-E5v2]] and item 6 after, against the answers.

- **3 min. Ready for unit 2.** Ask aloud: the next four numbers after 6, 12, 18; is 4 times a number always even,
  and why; which of 3, 8, 11, 14, 15, 17, 20 are even. Write down what the child says.
