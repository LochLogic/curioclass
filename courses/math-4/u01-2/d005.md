# Day 5: Prime or composite? Prove it.

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Mark and sort the E4 tickets: D (the words swapped), E (no 4s past 40, or
"even" with no product). Put a 9 by 9 table on the desk of each student under 32 right on either
facts check.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: E4 back

Alone, then whole class. Hand back the day 4 tickets and the marked facts check sheets. Students under the mark join
the D or E work, 9 by 9 table out; it stays out from today.

**If day 4's tickets showed many D** (factor and multiple swapped): say [[s01-W5-1]] both ways, pointing to each
number. Pairs say [[s01-W5-2]] and [[s01-W5-3]] both ways.

**If many E** (the 4s stopped at 40, or "even" with no product): fence [[s01-W5-4]] together from ten times.
Students who said no on 92 sit near you for the sort.

If both: D, then the fence aloud. If neither: [[s01-W5-5]], alone, then the answers on the display; students
under the mark instead copy each missed fact, with its answer, on the back of their facts check sheet.

#### 8 min. Task: only one rectangle

Pairs, grid paper, sketches only ([[s01-P5-1]], side 1). One partner takes 1 to 10, the other 11 to 20; then they
trade and check. Ask: "Is that a new rectangle, or one you have, turned?" Watch for 9 or 15 on the list (ask for 3
rows) and 4, 9 or 16 (a square is a rectangle too). At 7 minutes, list on the board: 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17,
19.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: prime, composite, and 1

Whole class. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go. Each number on the board list but 1 makes one long row
only: exactly two factors, 1 and itself. That is **prime**. More than two factors, so more than one rectangle:
**composite**. One pair other than 1 × itself proves it.

**Ask.** "Is 1 prime? How many factors does it have?" Expect "yes, one rectangle" first; then "one factor, just
1". Prime means exactly two factors, so 1 is neither prime nor composite.

**Example 1** ([[s01-K5-1]]). No sketch past 20: search in order, as in section 1.1 (frame: 2 to 5).
[[steps:s01-K5-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s01-K5-2]]). A number that looks prime.
[[steps:s01-K5-2]]

"Composite" needs a proof pair; "prime" needs a search to the turn that finds none. Odd, or "not
in my tables", proves nothing.

#### 4 min. Sort, set 1 (Egg Carton Factory, paper path)

When it exists, Egg Carton Factory's Rush shift 2 (cards 2a and 2b) and card 4c take these 15 minutes, word and
pair written before each placing; card 2c (two end-of-unit assessment numbers) is left out. Until then, this paper path.

Alone, side 1: [[s01-P5-2]]. A composite needs its pair; a prime, the last number tried. At 3 minutes, the
check screen; students fix in pen.

Check: Does each composite have a pair other than 1 × itself?

#### 3 min. Sort, set 2 (Egg Carton Factory, paper path)

Alone: [[s01-P5-3]]. Then the check screen. If many put 1 in Prime, count its factors aloud: one.

Check: Is 1 in Neither? Does 69 have its pair?

#### 4 min. Sort, set 3 (Egg Carton Factory, paper path)

Alone: [[s01-P5-4]]. Then the check screen. If many put 2 in Composite, write its factors: 1 and 2, exactly two.

Check: Is 2 in Prime? Does 63 have its pair?

#### 4 min. One decade (Egg Carton Factory, paper path)

Alone: [[s01-P5-5]]. Watch for 45 or 49 ringed: odd is not a proof. Then the check screen.

Check: Are only 41, 43 and 47 ringed? Do 45 and 49 have pairs?

#### 6 min. The 1 to 30 chart

Alone, on the chart sheet ([[s01-P5-6]]). Up to 30 the stop comes by 5 (6 × 6 = 36). Watch for 1 ringed or 2
not ringed (G), and 9, 15, 21, 25 or 27 ringed (F). At 4 minutes, the check screen.

Check: Is 1 unringed and 2 ringed? Does each odd composite have its pair?

**Ask.** "Every even number after 2 is composite. Why?" Expect: "it has a pair with 2"; "an even number of
squares makes 2 rows". So 2 is the only even prime. Then students write the answer on the chart.

#### 5 min. Fix it: Kai and Pia

Side 2 ([[s01-F5-1]], [[s01-F5-2]]). Two minutes alone, one in pairs, then the class: the display shows each
working beside the right one. If many still call 87 prime, search it aloud: 2, no; 3, yes, with 29.

#### 6 min. Written task: Ben

Alone, side 2 ([[s01-W5]]); it may be dictated. Collect it; read it after class against the key's look-fors.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s01-X5v2]]). Read it aloud once if asked; the 9 by 9 tables stay out. Collect at the door;
record results under E5 on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** The sets and the decade on the board, one at a time; a 1 to 30 chart ruled by hand.
- **One projector.** A student who says "composite" names the pair before the check screen.
- **One child at home.** "Take 13 beans. Make a rectangle that is not one long row." It cannot be done: prime.
  Then 15 beans.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 3 and the decade. Never drop a proof, the even-number question, the fixes or the written task.
Longer: the challenge ([[s01-C5-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: prime (one long row), composite (more than one rectangle), factor pair, proof pair. Students say
each with its picture; a home language is welcome. Frames on the display: "__
is composite, because __ × __ = __." "__ is prime: I tried __ to __ and found no pair."

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, 77 called prime (error F), 59 or 73 called composite with no pair (a guess), a prime with no number
tried (x, "no search"), or 1 × 77 as the proof: these join day 6's F and G group. Day 6 opens with the end-of-unit assessment.
E5's tickets and today's written task go back at pencils down.
