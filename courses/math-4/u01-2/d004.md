# Day 4: Is it a multiple?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the facts check part 2 sheets, the exit slips and the key. Mark the E3 slips
and day 3's written tasks and sort them: B (no 7 × 14), E ("98 is even"), no turn. Put the display page on the
projector. Have 9 by 9 tables ready.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 2

Alone, on the facts check sheet: the 36 quotients, no clock shown. Collect at 5 minutes; an item not reached is not wrong. Mark
after class. A 9 by 9 table is on hand today for any student who asks.

#### 3 min. E3 and the written task back

Whole class. Hand back the E3 slips and day 3's written tasks.

**If day 3's slips showed many B** (no 7 × 14): test 7 on 98 from ten times. 7 × 10 = 70; 28 more is 7 × 4.

**If many E** ("98 is even"): even tells you about 2, not 6. Today's Fix it has that mistake.

**If many written tasks named no turn** (error C or A): read one that does, "at 8 × 8 the pairs turn round".

Otherwise one student reads a task that names the turn. Students with B sit near you for Teaching first.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: one fact, three ways; then past ten times

Whole class. Students fill the side 1 frames as you go. The display shows each step on its own screen.

**Example 1** ([[s01-K4-1]]). [[steps:s01-K4-1]]

Say "multiple" beside "multiply": a multiple is what multiplying makes.

**Example 2** ([[s01-K4-2v2]]). [[steps:s01-K4-2v2]]

Draw the jumps on the board as the steps come: one long jump to 60, then six short ones. The display cannot draw
a number line yet, so the board carries it. The point: the 6s do not stop at 60.

#### 10 min. Egg Carton Factory, Fill Line: four orders on paper

**When Fill Line exists:** students write yes or no on side 1, then run the order in that carton size. Until it
exists, this paper path does the job in the same 10 minutes.

Alone, side 1 ([[s01-T4-1]], [[s01-T4-2]], [[s01-T4-3]], [[s01-T4-4]]). Yes or no first, before any drawing.
Then the jumps on a number line: one long jump for ten cartons, then one jump a carton. Landing on the order means
yes; passing it means the last carton is short. Each yes gets both sentences.

After each order, one student says where the last jump landed; draw it on the board. Watch for "no" on 84 (the 7s
stop at 70) and "yes" on 58 (it is even): both error E.

#### 15 min. Is it a multiple? Fence it

Alone, side 2 ([[s01-P4-1]], [[s01-P4-2]], [[s01-P4-3]], [[s01-P4-4]]). Two products of the number tested, one at
or below, one at or above, from ten times past the tables. Then yes or no. Walk with one question: "Which two
products is it between?" Watch for:

- "Yes" on 86 (ends in 6) or 62 (even): error E. Ask for the products first.
- "No" on 88 or 69, the table stopped short: error E. Ask "What is 4 × 20?"

At 11 minutes, partners check against the display; a mismatch gets a question mark and is redone together. If
many miss 86, fence it on the board.

Check: Is your number on a product, or between two? On a product means yes.

#### 8 min. Fix it: Kai, Ivy and Ravi

Side 2 ([[s01-F4-1]], [[s01-F4-2]], [[s01-F4-3]]). A minute alone on each, then the class; the display shows each
wrong working beside the right one.

Kai listed factors: they make 6; multiples are made from 6. Ivy went by the last digit. That works for 2, 5 and 10
because every ten is a multiple of them; ten is not a multiple of 4 or 6. Ravi stopped at the table.

If many still swap the words (error D): sort 1, 3, 9, 18, 27 and 54 into "factor of 9" and
"multiple of 9", each with its equation.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "21 is a multiple of 7. What else does 3 × 7 = 21 tell you?" Expect: "21 is a multiple of 3"; "3 and 7
are factors of 21"; "3 rows of 7 make 21". One fact, read both ways.

**Ask.** "How do you tell if a big number is a multiple of 6?" Expect: from 6 × 10, count on, or fence it. Not
the last digit.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s01-X4a]], [[s01-X4b]]). Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; record under E4 on
the record sheet.

### Other rooms

- **No devices, or the tool not built.** The paper path above is the whole job.
- **No printer.** Orders and questions on the board; number lines in notebooks.
- **One projector.** With Fill Line, you run it; the class writes yes or no first.
- **One child at home.** 30 beans: rows of 4 with none left? Rows of 6? Both sentences for each yes.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the first three fence questions; students check their own. Never drop yes or no first, the fixes or the
two sentences. Longer: the challenge ([[s01-C4-1]]): every multiple in a stretch, so students count on.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: multiple, factor, product, multiply. Students may say the sentences in a home language first.
Frames on the display: "__ is a multiple of __, because __ × __ = __." "__ is a factor of __."

### Tomorrow

On the slip, "55 is a factor of 5", or the blanks swapped (error D): day 5's warm-up says one equation both ways,
then two more like it. On 92, "no, the 4s stop at 40", or "yes, it is even" with no product (error E): day 5's
warm-up fences one number past the tables. If neither, day 5's warm-up is one factor-pair list from unit 1.1.
Mark the facts check tonight: students under 32 of 36 on either part do their fact print in day 5's warm-up and
keep a 9 by 9 table from then on.
