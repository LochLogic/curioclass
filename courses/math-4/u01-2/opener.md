# Unit 1.2: Multiples, prime and composite (days 4 to 6)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.OA.B.4, its last three parts: "Recognize that a whole number is a multiple of each of its factors.
Determine whether a given whole number in the range 1–100 is a multiple of a given one-digit number. Determine
whether a given whole number in the range 1–100 is prime or composite." Unit 1.1 taught the first part, finding
every factor pair.

**Factor and multiple are one fact read two ways.** 3 × 7 = 21 says 3 and 7 are factors of 21, and 21 is a
multiple of 3 and a multiple of 7. The factors are the rectangle's sides; the multiple is the whole rectangle. So a number is a multiple of each of its factors: that is the second part of the standard, and
students should say it from one equation, both ways, on day 4.

**A multiple is where counting by a number lands.** The multiples of 7 are 7, 14, 21 and on, without end. To
decide whether a number is a multiple of 7, count by 7s (or build from a product you know) and see whether you
land on it. 84 is a multiple of 7: 7 × 10 = 70, and 14 more is 7 × 2, so 7 × 12 = 84. A number between two
products is fenced: it lands on neither, so it leaves a leftover and is not a multiple. Reading what the
leftover means is stretch 13; here a leftover only means "no".

**Why the last digit works for some numbers and not others.** Every multiple of 10 is a multiple of 2 and of 5,
so a number's tens never change whether it is a multiple of 2 or 5: only its last digit does. 10 is not a
multiple of 4 or of 6, so for 4 and 6 the last digit settles nothing. 46 ends in 6 and is not a multiple of 6.
No divisibility rules are taught as rules; the fence does the work.

**Prime means exactly two factors.** A prime number has exactly one rectangle, one long row: its only factor
pair is 1 × itself. A composite number has more. 1 has one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite. 2 has
two factors, so it is prime, and every even number after 2 is composite, because it has the pair 2 × half of
it. To show a number is composite, give a factor pair that is not 1 × itself: that pair is the proof. To show
a number is prime, search in order and find nothing. For any number up to 100, testing 2, 3, 5 and 7 is
enough: a composite number under 100 has a factor below 10 (10 × 10 is 100), and 4, 6, 8 and 9 are already
covered by 2 and 3.

**Limits.** Whole numbers 1 to 100. "A multiple of" is tested against one-digit numbers. No common multiples,
no greatest common factor, no prime factorization (grade 6).

## The sketch, the number line and the chart

The rectangle comes back as a sketch only: on day 4, a multiple of 7 is rows of 7 with none left over; on day
5, a prime's only rectangle is one long row. Each is tied to its sentences ("84 = 12 × 7, so 84 is a multiple of
7 and 7 is a factor of 84"; "composite, because __ × __ = __").

- **Day 4.** Jumps on a number line: a size, and an order to fill. Yes or no is written before the jumps are
  drawn.
- **Day 5.** Sort cards, three sets of three numbers, a proof pair on each composite before it is placed. Then
  the 1 to 30 chart (chart-30.md): every prime ringed, a proof pair under each odd composite, and what is true
  of every even number after 2.

## Why this model

Jumps on a line make "multiple" something you can see land, and a short jump at the end makes "not a multiple"
just as visible. Egg Carton Factory's Fill Line packs a count into groups one after another and shows the last
group full or short; it is the planned tool for day 4, and Rush shift 2 (cards 2a and 2b) with card 4c is
planned for day 5. Card 2c is left out: it holds numbers the checks use. The tool is not live, so both days run
their paper path in the same minutes. The chart makes prime a result: a number is ringed only after the search
finds no pair.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

Unit 1.1's opener explains A to C; they can return in day 5's warm-up and day 6's groups.

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D | Swap factor and multiple: "the multiples of 6 are 1, 2, 3, 6" | The two words are not yet tied to one equation | Sort 1, 3, 9, 18, 27 and 54 into "factor of 9" and "multiple of 9"; say each with its equation |
| E | Judge "a multiple of" by the last digit or by even and odd, or stop at ten times | A rule that works for 2, 5 and 10 is stretched; the tables are taken for all the multiples | Fence four numbers between two products of the number tested, starting from ten times where needed |
| F | Call a number prime because it is odd, or not in the tables | Prime is a look, not the result of a search | Test 2, 3, 5 and 7 in order on 39, 57 and 95; call a number prime only after the search finds nothing |
| G | Call 1 prime, or 2 composite because it is even | Prime is "few factors", not exactly two | Write the factors of 1, 2, 3 and 4 and count them: one, two, two, three |

The day each wrong example is shown: D and E on day 4, F and G on day 5. Each day's teacher page names the wrong
answer that signals each error on that day's exit.

## Supplies for the unit

- Per student: plain paper for number lines (day 4); a set of paper sort cards (day 5); the 1 to 30 chart
  (day 5); a pencil.
- Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board; a 1 to 100 chart for the sieve on day 6.
- A room with nothing: lines and charts ruled by hand, and cards cut from scrap paper.

## Words

Multiple, factor, prime, composite, each with its picture. "Multiple" and "multiply" are said side by side.
Frames: "__ is a multiple of __, because __ × __ = __." "__ is composite, because __ × __ = __." "__ is prime,
because its only factor pair is 1 × __."

## The end of the stretch

Day 6: the end check, 30 minutes, unaided; items 2 to 5 are this unit's. Then the respond half, by error group
(missed item 1: A to C; items 2 or 3: D and E; item 4: F and G; nothing missed: the sieve on a 1 to 100 chart).
E5's slips and day 5's written task go back when pencils go down, and their errors join the F and G group. The
last ten minutes are stretch 2's readiness check. Items 5 and 6 are judged after class and handed back on day 7.
Everything goes on stretch 1's record sheet (u01-1/record.md).
