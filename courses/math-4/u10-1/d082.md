# Day 82: Sentences into equations

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets, about 6 strip sheets and the key. Put the display page on the
projector. Have day 81's exit tickets sorted: one sentence only, roles swapped (error C), 'more than' or strips with no
copies (error A), and the rest.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Alone, on the board: [[s10-P82-17]], [[s10-P82-18]], [[s10-P82-19]], [[s10-P82-20]], [[s10-P82-21]],
[[s10-P82-22]], [[s10-P82-23]]. Answers on the display at 4 minutes.

#### 3 min. Day 81's exit back

Whole class. Hand back day 81's tickets. **If day 81's tickets showed many C or many with one sentence only:** draw
a 7-strip with four copies under it and read 28 = 4 × 7 both ways. Point to the longer strip: "Which amount is
bigger? It stands alone on one side." Then show C's wrong example, 3 = 4 × 12, beside 12 = 4 × 3. Tickets with
one copy too many (error D): count the copies aloud with those students. Otherwise one student reads both sentences
for their strip, and the class names the bigger number each time.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: three sentences into equations

Whole class. Students write each equation on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on
its own screen. Draw a strip only if a sentence is hard.

**Sentence 1** ([[s10-K82-1]]). [[steps:s10-K82-1]]

**Sentence 2** ([[s10-K82-2]]). [[steps:s10-K82-2]]

**Sentence 3** ([[s10-K82-3]]). [[steps:s10-K82-3]]

Say the rule once and leave it on the board: the bigger amount stands alone; the times and the smaller amount
are multiplied. Either side of the equals sign, and either order of the two factors, is fine. If time runs
short, take sentence 2 in one screen: read it, write 10 × 8 = 80, point to the 80.

#### 7 min. Sentence set 1

Alone. Either reading of an equation is right; early finishers write the second one too: [[s10-P82-1]], [[s10-P82-2]], [[s10-P82-3]],
[[s10-P82-4]]. Go first to the students day 81 marked A; draw a strip with each on their first measured
sentence.

About 4 minutes, then the check screen; read each answer from the key. Students tick a right first try and fix
a wrong one in the second color, comparing only with their own last set; no counts aloud.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

Watch for 45 = 5 + 9 (error A); 9 = 5 × 45 (error C); and on a measured sentence, 50 × 10 (error B). If many miss one item,
draw its strip on the board before set 2: one strip for the smaller amount, copies for the bigger.

#### 7 min. Sentence set 2

Alone, the same way: [[s10-P82-5]], [[s10-P82-6]], [[s10-P82-7]], [[s10-P82-8]]. Its first sentence starts
with the times, like sentence 2.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

#### 7 min. Sentence set 3

Alone: [[s10-P82-9]], [[s10-P82-10v2]], [[s10-P82-11]], [[s10-P82-12]]. Two equations are ten times a
two-digit number. Walk to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

#### 7 min. Sentence set 4

Alone: [[s10-P82-13]], [[s10-P82-14]], [[s10-P82-15]], [[s10-P82-16]]. The section's target (ours, untested) is
at least 7 of the 8 in sets 3 and 4, right on the first try. No time target.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it? Does each sentence start with it?

If many are still under 3 of 4 in set 4, the strip sheet comes out for the written task.

#### 9 min. Written task: is Jo right?

Alone, side 2 ([[s10-W82v2]]). Read after class with the tickets. Look-fors, each ticked alone: [[steps:s10-W82v2]]

Watch for a 3-strip and a 5-strip side by side with 3 + 5 = 8 (error A): ask "how many copies of 3 are there?"

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "A ten is how many times as much as a one?" Expect: 10 times; 10 = 10 × 1. Then tie it to sentence 2:
80 is 10 times as many as 8, and each ten is 10 times a one.

**Ask.** "In every equation today, where did the bigger number go?" Expect: alone on one side of the equals sign.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, no strip sheet, read aloud once ([[s10-X82a]], [[s10-X82b]], [[s10-X82c]]). Write the results on the
record sheet under E82.

### Other rooms

  line under each set for the count. The exit: read the three aloud; students write on the back of their lined paper.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4's last two items and the second Ask. Never drop the checks between sets, the written task or
the exit. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C82-1]]), on lined paper; then three sentences of
their own with ten times a two-digit number, swapped with a partner.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, times as long as, times as tall as, equation. Frames on the
display: "__ is __ times as many as __." "__ = __ × __". The frame from day 80 stays on the wall over its strip picture.
