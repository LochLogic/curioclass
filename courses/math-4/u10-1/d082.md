# Day 82: Sentences into equations

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips, the strip sheets and the key. Put the display page on the
projector. Have day 81's exit slips sorted: one sentence only, roles swapped (error C), and the rest.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Alone, on the board: one difference with the standard algorithm, and six facts from the 6, 7 and 8 tables, two
of them a one-digit number times a multiple of 10.

#### 3 min. Day 81's exit back

Whole class. Hand back day 81's slips. **If day 81's slips showed many C or many with one sentence only:** draw
the C example again, "12 is 4 times as many as 3" written as 3 = 4 × 12, beside 12 = 4 × 3. Point to the longer
strip first: "Which amount is bigger? It stands alone on one side." Otherwise one student reads both sentences
for their strip, and the class names the bigger number each time.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: three sentences into equations

Whole class. Students write each equation on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on
its own screen. Draw a strip only if a sentence is hard.

**Sentence 1** ([[s10-K82-1]]). [[steps:s10-K82-1]]

**Sentence 2** ([[s10-K82-2]]). [[steps:s10-K82-2]]

**Sentence 3** ([[s10-K82-3]]). [[steps:s10-K82-3]]

Say the rule once and leave it on the board: the bigger amount stands alone; the times and the smaller amount
are multiplied. Either side of the equals sign is fine.

#### 7 min. Sentence set 1

Alone. Each set mixes both ways, things counted and things measured. For an equation either reading is right;
a student who finishes early writes the second one too: [[s10-P82-1]], [[s10-P82-2]], [[s10-P82-3]],
[[s10-P82-4]].

About 4 minutes to write, then the check screen, and you read each answer from the key. Students tick a right
first try and fix a wrong one in the second colour. A student compares only with their own last set; no counts
aloud.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

Watch for an addition, 5 + 9 or 45 = 5 + 9 (error A); the bigger number inside the multiplication, 9 = 5 × 45
(error C); and on a measured sentence, the two big numbers multiplied, 50 × 10 (error B). If many miss one item,
draw its strip on the board before set 2: one strip for the smaller amount, copies for the bigger.

#### 7 min. Sentence set 2

Alone, the same way: [[s10-P82-5]], [[s10-P82-6]], [[s10-P82-7]], [[s10-P82-8]]. Its first sentence starts
with the times, like sentence 2.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

#### 7 min. Sentence set 3

Alone: [[s10-P82-9]], [[s10-P82-10]], [[s10-P82-11]], [[s10-P82-12]]. Two equations are ten times a
two-digit number. Walk to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

#### 7 min. Sentence set 4

Alone: [[s10-P82-13]], [[s10-P82-14]], [[s10-P82-15]], [[s10-P82-16]]. The unit's target (ours, untested) is
at least 5 of 6 right on the first try by today: read it from sets 3 and 4 together. No time target.

Check: Is the bigger number alone? Does the multiplication make it?

If many are still under 3 of 4 in set 4, the strip sheet comes out for the written task.

#### 9 min. Written task: is Jo right?

Alone, side 2 ([[s10-W82]]). The picture is a strip picture; the strip sheet is there for anyone who wants it.
You read these after class with the slips. Look-fors, each ticked alone: [[steps:s10-W82]]

Watch for a 3-strip and a 5-strip side by side with 3 + 5 = 8 (error A): ask "how many copies of 3 are there?"

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "A ten is how many times as much as a one?" Expect: 10 times; 10 = 10 × 1. Then tie it to sentence 2:
80 is 10 times as many as 8, and each ten is 10 times a one.

**Ask.** "In every equation today, where did the bigger number go?" Expect: alone on one side of the equals sign.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, no strip sheet, read aloud once ([[s10-X82a]], [[s10-X82b]], [[s10-X82c]]). Write the results on the
record sheet under E82.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write each set of four on the board as it starts; students write on lined paper and draw a
  line under each set for the count.
- **One child at home.** Say each sentence aloud and have the child write the equation, then turn it round.
  Ten right is enough: stop at ten if all ten are right, and skip the count.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4's last two items and the second Ask. Never drop the checks between sets, the written task or
the exit. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C82-1]]), on lined paper; then three sentences of
their own with ten times a two-digit number, swapped with a partner.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, times as long as, times as tall as, equation. Frames on the
display: "__ is __ times as many as __." "__ = __ × __". The frame from day 80 stays on the wall over its strip picture.
