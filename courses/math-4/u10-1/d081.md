# Day 81: One equation, two comparisons

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the strip sheets, the exit slips and the key. Count readiness item 1 from day 79 and sort day 80's slips by error. Put the display page on the
projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: two divisions and a difference

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s10-P81-1]], [[s10-P81-2]], [[s10-P81-3]]). Answers on the screen at 4 minutes. 72 or 56
for the first is adding or taking away, not a missing factor.

Students who missed readiness item 1, if they are not many, skip the warm-up and sit with you: deal 30 counters
into 5 rows, then write 5 × ? = 30 and read it as "5 rows of what make 30?".

#### 5 min. Reteach, or day 80's error

**If many missed readiness item 1** (more than a third): the whole class does the counters, as above, with you
dealing on the board.

**Otherwise,** show day 80's commonest error on its slips. A (7, or "5 more"): "8 times as many as 3 is 11", a
3-strip and a strip of 3 and 8, beside eight copies of the 3-strip, 24. D (12, or six copies): "4 times as many as
3" drawn as a 3-strip with four more after it, 15, beside four copies, 12. Count the copies aloud, the first strip
as copy one.

**If both are needed,** the counters go here and day 80's error is talked through in the notice task.

#### 8 min. Notice: 3 strips of 4 and 4 strips of 3

Pairs. Each partner cuts a rectangle 3 squares by 4 from grid paper: one cuts it into 3 strips of 4, the other
into 4 strips of 3. They lay their strips side by side.

**Ask.** "What do you notice about 3 strips of 4 and 4 strips of 3?" Expect, and show in this order: "both are 12
squares"; "the 3 and the 4 swap jobs"; "one is 3 copies, the other is 4 copies". Take a sentence with "copies" last.

If day 80's error rides here, a pair whose strips show it counts its copies aloud.

#### 3 min. Tie the strips to 12 = 3 × 4

Whole class. Write 12 = 3 × 4 on the board. Students fill the two frames under Example 1 as you go.

**Example 1** ([[s10-K81-1]]). [[steps:s10-K81-1]]

#### 8 min. Growth Spurt, Read it both ways: on paper

Growth Spurt is not built. When it exists: "Read it both ways", three cards, 8 minutes, stopped at the third card.
Until it exists, the same 8 minutes run on side 1's equation cards.

**Example 2** ([[s10-K81-2]]), whole class, read aloud as the model: [[steps:s10-K81-2]]

Then alone, cards 1 and 2 ([[s10-P81-4]], [[s10-P81-5]]). At 6 minutes, read the four sentences aloud from the
key; students check their own and fix in pen.

Check: Listen to each sentence. Is the number that stands alone the bigger amount?

Watch for one sentence only, and for the smaller amount first (C). Ask: "Which number stands alone? Is it the
biggest?"

#### 15 min. Six equations

Alone, side 2 ([[s10-P81-6]], [[s10-P81-7]], [[s10-P81-8]], [[s10-P81-9]], [[s10-P81-10]], [[s10-P81-11]]). For
each: both sentences, then one strip picture on the strip sheet, either way round. Read the first two aloud first: the
product sits on either side.

Walk and ask: "Where is the 9 in your strips? Where is the 2?" Watch for a strip picture of two strips with no
copies, or a 9-strip beside a 2-strip (A), and for sentences that say "more". At 12 minutes, partners check
one equation: both sentences against the strips.

Check: Do both sentences match the strips? Is the bigger amount first?

If many write one sentence only, stop and read 12 = 3 × 4 both ways again from the cut strips.

#### 6 min. Fix it: Ben's equation

[[s10-F81-1]]. One minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class. The display shows Ben's working beside the
right working. Point to the longer strip first and ask
"Which amount is bigger?": the bigger amount stands alone. Students write what went wrong and the right equation.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Why does 12 = 3 × 4 tell us two things?" Expect: "12 is 3 fours and 4 threes"; "you can cut 12 squares
both ways". Show the strips first, then the sentences.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone. The exit slip ([[s10-X81]]). Collect at 5 minutes.

### Other rooms

- **No devices, or the tool not yet built.** The 8 minutes are the equation cards on side 1, as written, checked
  against the key read aloud.
- **No scissors.** Pairs draw the two rectangles and ring each strip in pencil.
- **One child at home.** Twelve coins in 3 rows of 4; the child reads it both ways, then makes 4 rows of 3.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check and the challenge, and cut the six equations to four (the first four). Never drop
the second sentence or the fix. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C81-1]]), then the extension ([[s10-C81-2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, comparison, equation, factor, product, copy. The frame stays on the wall over
a strip picture: "__ is __ times as many as __." A student may say each sentence in a home language first.

### Tomorrow

Day 82 opens with these slips. Many with one sentence only, or "7 is 4 times as many as 28" (error C): day 82
shows C's wrong example again, pointing to the longer strip first. Otherwise one student reads both sentences for
their strip. "28 is 4 more than 7", or a 4-strip beside a 7-strip with no copies (error A): note the names and
draw a strip with them on day 82 when a sentence is hard. Results go on the record sheet under E81.
