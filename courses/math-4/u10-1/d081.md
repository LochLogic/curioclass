# Day 81: One equation, two comparisons

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the strip sheets, the exit tickets and the key. Count readiness item 1 from
day 79 and sort day 80's tickets by error. Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: two divisions and a difference

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s10-P81-1]], [[s10-P81-2]], [[s10-P81-3]]). Answers on the screen at 4 minutes. 72 or 56
for the first is adding or taking away, not a missing factor.

Students who missed readiness item 1, if they are not many, skip the warm-up and sit with you: in pairs, deal 15
counters into 3 rows, then 24 into 3 rows, as in the section opener, and write 3 × ? = 15 and 3 × ? = 24.

#### 5 min. Reteach, or day 80's error

**If many missed readiness item 1** (more than a third): the warm-up and these 5 minutes are the reteach, whole
class, as above; the warm-up sums move to day 82's warm-up.

**Otherwise,** show day 80's commonest error beside the right one. A: "8 times as many as 3 is 11" beside eight
copies of the 3-strip, 24. D: a 3-strip with four more after it, 15, beside four copies, 12. Count the copies
aloud, the first strip as copy one.

**If both are needed,** the counters go here and day 80's error is talked through in the notice task.

#### 8 min. Notice: 3 strips of 4 and 4 strips of 3

Pairs. Each partner cuts a rectangle 3 squares by 4 from the grid paper: one cuts it into 3 strips of 4, the
other into 4 strips of 3. Each also cuts one extra strip of their size, "the one copy", and lays it above their row.

**Ask.** "What do you notice about 3 strips of 4 and 4 strips of 3?" Expect, and show in this order: "both are 12
squares"; "the 3 and the 4 swap jobs"; "one is 3 copies, the other is 4 copies". Take a sentence with "copies" last.

If day 80's error rides here, a pair counts its copies aloud.

#### 3 min. Tie the strips to 12 = 3 × 4

Whole class. Write 12 = 3 × 4 on the board. Say: "Equal groups is one amount. A comparison is two amounts:
the 12 and one 4. The 12 is measured in 4s." Students fill the two frames under Example 1 as you go.

**Example 1** ([[s10-K81-1]]). [[steps:s10-K81-1]]

#### 8 min. Growth Spurt, Read it both ways: on paper

When Growth Spurt exists: "Read it both ways", three cards, stopped at the third. Until then, side 1's equation
cards.

**Example 2** ([[s10-K81-2]]), whole class, read aloud as the model: [[steps:s10-K81-2]]

Then alone, cards 1 and 2 ([[s10-P81-4]], [[s10-P81-5]]). At 6 minutes, read the four sentences aloud from the
key; students check their own and fix in pen.

Check: Does each sentence start with the bigger amount?

Watch for one sentence only, and for the smaller amount first (C). Ask: "Which number stands alone? Is it the
biggest?"

#### 15 min. Six equations

Alone, side 2 ([[s10-P81-6]], [[s10-P81-7]], [[s10-P81-8]], [[s10-P81-9]], [[s10-P81-10]], [[s10-P81-11]]). For
each: both sentences, then one strip picture on the strip sheet, either way round, the equation on its letter line. Read equations 1 and 2 aloud first: the product can be on either side of the equals
sign. Early finishers: the challenge at the foot of side 2.

Walk and ask: "Where is the 9 in your strips? Where is the 2?" Watch for a 9-strip beside a 2-strip with no
copies (A), and for "more". At 12 minutes, partners check one
equation against the strips; then show the answers screen.

Check: Do both sentences match the strips? Is the bigger amount first?

If many write one sentence only, stop and read 12 = 3 × 4 both ways again from the cut strips.

#### 6 min. Fix it: Ben's sentence

[[s10-F81-2]], side 2. Pairs talk one minute, then the class. The display shows Ben's working beside the
right working. Point to the longer strip first and ask
"Which amount is bigger?": the bigger amount comes first. Students write what went wrong and both sentences.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Why does 12 = 3 × 4 tell us two things?" Expect: "you can cut 12 squares both ways"; last, "12 is 3
times as many as 4, and 4 times as many as 3, because the 4 and the 3 can swap jobs". Show the strips first.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The exit ticket ([[s10-X81]]). Collect at 5 minutes.

### Other rooms

- **No devices, or no tool yet.** Side 1's equation cards, as written.
- **No scissors.** Pairs draw the two rectangles and ring each strip in pencil.

### At home

**Swap.** No grid paper or scissors: 12 coins or paper squares. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the cutting, half the six equations, and the challenge.

- **8 min. Coins both ways.** Lay 12 coins in 3 rows of 4, then in 4 rows of 3. Then [[s10-K81-1]] together.

    **Say.** "What do you notice about 3 rows of 4 and 4 rows of 3?" Expect: both are 12; the 3 and the 4 swap
    jobs.

- **7 min. Read it both ways.** Read [[s10-K81-2]] aloud together. The child does [[s10-P81-4]] and
  [[s10-P81-5]]; check from the key that each sentence starts with the bigger number.
- **10 min. Three equations.** [[s10-P81-6]], [[s10-P81-7]] and [[s10-P81-9]]: both sentences, then one strip
  picture each.
- **5 min. Fix it.** [[s10-F81-2]]: the child writes what went wrong and both sentences.

    **Say.** "Which amount is bigger? Where does it go?" Expect: 12; first in the sentence.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s10-X81]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check and cut the six equations to four (the first four). Never drop
the second sentence or the fix. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C81-1]]), then the extension ([[s10-C81-2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, comparison, equation, factor, product, copy. The frame stays on the wall over
a strip picture: "__ is __ times as many as __." A student may say each sentence in a home language first.

### Tomorrow

Day 82 opens with these tickets. Many with one sentence only, or "7 is 4 times as many as 28" (error C): day 82
shows C's wrong example again, pointing to the longer strip first. Otherwise one student reads both sentences for
their strip. "28 is 4 more than 7", or a 4-strip beside a 7-strip with no copies (error A): note the names and
draw a strip with them on day 82 when a sentence is hard. A 7-strip and 4 more
copies, 35 (error D): count the copies aloud with them. Results go on the record sheet under E81.
