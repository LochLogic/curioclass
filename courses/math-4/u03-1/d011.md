# Day 11: The strip on the line

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the line pages, the exit tickets and the key. Sort day 9's readiness checks
by item and day 10's tickets by error. Draw a line from 0 to 1 on the board, as long as the large strip, and
mark it in fourths. Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: two sums and a fact

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s03-P11-1]], [[s03-P11-2]], [[s03-P11-3]]). Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes;
students mark their own.

Students who missed a readiness item, if they are not many, skip the warm-up and sit with you: item 1 or 4,
lay the one-fourth strip from 0 five times, counting "one fourth, two fourths..."; item 2, fold a half strip
again and count the shaded fourths; item 3, a thirds strip beside an eighths strip.

#### 5 min. Reteach, or day 10's error

**If many missed a readiness item** (more than a third): the warm-up and these 5 minutes are that item's fix,
whole class; drop the warm-up sums.

**Otherwise, if day 10's tickets showed many A** (2/6 or 4/3): show "1/3 = 1/6" beside the two strips; fold
thirds into sixths again and count the shaded parts aloud. **Many B** (4/5): a 1/3 strip beside a 2/4 strip.
Adding 1 to both numbers changed the length.

**If neither,** everyone does item 1's fix: four fourths land on 1.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: the strip on the line

Whole class. Hold the large strip against the board line, its left end at 0. Students lay their own 1/4 strip
on line 1 of the line page and copy each move. The display shows each step on its own screen.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K11-1]]). [[steps:s03-K11-1]]

Every time: the point is where the strip ends; count parts, not marks.

#### 10 min. Cut and rename, up to 1

Alone, side 1 ([[s03-P11-4]], [[s03-P11-5]], [[s03-P11-6]]), 7 minutes, one line each on the line page: mark
it with the matching strip, then fold to cut each part in 2.

Watch for one part too many, 5/8 or 9/10 (error C: the mark at 0 counted), and only the denominator changed,
2/8 or 4/10 (error A). Ask: "Where does your strip end? How many parts from 0?"

Then 3 minutes in pairs: partners swap and lay a strip on each point. A mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Does your strip end at the point? Did you count parts, not marks?

#### 5 min. Fix it: Ana's count

[[s03-F11-1]], side 1. One minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class. The display shows Ana's working
beside the right working. Ask: "Where would 6/6 be?" Lay the strip from 0 again: the first part ends at the
first mark after 0.

If many still count marks, every student lays the sixths strip on the line and touches each part, saying
"one sixth, two sixths..."

#### 5 min. Past 1: the second whole

Whole class. Lay a second strip after the first on the board: the line now runs to 2. Students fill the frame
under Example 2.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K11-2]]). [[steps:s03-K11-2]]

#### 10 min. Past 1, alone

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P11-7]], [[s03-P11-8]]), on the line page's 0 to 2 lines. Early finishers: the
challenge.

Watch for 7/10 and 14/20 for the first (error A, then error K: the whole line taken as the whole), and 8/8
for the point at 2 (error K). Ask: "Where is 1 on your line? How many parts make one whole?"

#### 5 min. Fix it: Max's line

[[s03-F11-2]], side 2. Pairs, one minute, then the class. The display shows Max's working beside the right
working. Cover the line past 1 with a sheet of paper: four parts make 1. Uncover it and count on past 1:
"five fourths."

If many said 8/8 for the point at 2, cover that line the same way.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" Expect, in this
order: "there are more parts"; "each part is smaller"; last, "the point stayed in the same place, so it is the
same amount with a new name". Students finish the frame on side 2.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The exit ticket ([[s03-X11]]). The line is printed on the ticket. Collect at 5 minutes.

### Other rooms

- **No line page yet.** Until the line page exists, students lay a whole strip along a line of lined paper and
  mark 0 and 1 at its ends; for 0 to 2, two strips end to end.
- **No strip sheet yet.** Cut strips 10 inches long from scrap paper: rule fifths every 2 inches, tenths every
  inch; fold the rest.

### At home

**Swap.** No strip sheet: cut strips from plain paper, all the same length, and fold them. No line page: lay a
strip along a ruled line and mark 0 and 1 at its ends. No projector: point at the page.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the readiness fix, questions 6 and 9, and the challenge.

- **8 min. The strip on the line.** Read [[s03-K11-1]] together; fold and mark as it says.

    **Say.** "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" Expect: more
    parts, each smaller; the point stayed put.

- **7 min. Up to 1.** The child does [[s03-P11-4]] and [[s03-P11-5]], counting parts from 0 aloud.
- **5 min. Fix Ana's count.** [[s03-F11-1]].

    **Say.** "Where would 6/6 be?" Expect: at 1, so her point is 5/6.

- **8 min. Past 1.** Read [[s03-K11-2]] together with two strips end to end, then the child does
  [[s03-P11-7]].
- **4 min. Fix Max's line.** [[s03-F11-2]]. Cover the line past 1 with your hand.

    **Say.** "How many fourths make 1?" Expect: four, so the point is 5/4.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X11]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop questions 6 and 9 and the partner check (students lay their own strip on each point). Never
drop the two fixes or the planned question. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C11-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: number line, point, equivalent, numerator, denominator. Read fractions as words: "three
halves", not "three over two". Frame on side 2 and on the display: "Each part was cut into __, so there are
__ times as many parts and each part is smaller. The length is the same, so __ = __." A cut may be ruled, not
folded.

### Tomorrow

Day 12 opens its task with these tickets. Many 5/8 or 7/8 (error C, marks counted): day 12 shows a line where
0 is counted as "one", and the class lays the strip from 0 again. Many 3/8 (error A): fold fourths into eighths
and count the shaded parts aloud before the task.
"Yes, it moved": lay a 1/4 strip on the line before and after the cut. Results go on the record sheet under
E11.
