# Day 11: The strip on the line

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the number lines sheets, the exit tickets and the key. Check every envelope
holds the fourths, fifths, eighths and tenths strips. Sort day 9's readiness checks by item and day 10's tickets
by error. Fold day 10's plain long strip in fourths. Draw a line from 0 to 2 on the board, each whole as long as
a long strip; mark 0, 1 and 2. Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: a sum, a difference and a fact

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s03-P11-1]], [[s03-P11-2]], [[s03-P11-3]]). Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes;
students mark their own. Students whose day 10 ticket said 4/6 with no cuts drawn draw the cuts on it first.

A few who missed a readiness item sit with you instead: item 1 or 4, lay the fourths strip on line 1, count
four fourths to 1, then slide it past the end, "five fourths"; item 2, fold a half strip again; item 3, a thirds
strip beside an eighths strip.

#### 5 min. Reteach, or day 10's error

**If many missed a readiness item** (more than a third): the warm-up and these 5 minutes are that item's fix,
whole class. Day 10's error then goes into Ana's fix, one minute.

**Otherwise, if day 10's tickets showed many A** (2/6 or 4/3): show "1/3 = 1/6" beside the two strips; fold
thirds into sixths again and count the shaded parts aloud. **Many B** (4/5): lay the 2/3 strip beside the 4/5
strip. Adding 2 to both numbers changed the length.

**If neither,** everyone does item 1's fix: four fourths land on 1.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: the strip on the line

Whole class. Hold the long fourths strip on the board line from 0; fold it again for eighths. Students lay their
strips on line 1 of the number lines sheet and copy each move.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K11-1]]). [[steps:s03-K11-1]]

Every time: the point is where the strip ends; count parts, not marks.

#### 10 min. Cut and rename, up to 1

Alone, side 1 ([[s03-P11-4]], [[s03-P11-5]], [[s03-P11-6]] for early finishers), 8 minutes. Mark the line with
the matching strip, then lay the eighths or tenths strip on it and mark its parts.

Watch for one part too many, 5/8, 9/10, or 6/5 and 11/10 (error C: the mark at 0 counted), and only the
denominator changed, 2/8 or 4/10 (error A). Ask: "Where does your strip end? How many parts from 0?"

Then 2 minutes in pairs: partners swap and lay a strip on each point. A mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Does your strip end at the point? Did you count parts, not marks?

#### 5 min. Fix it: Ana's count

[[s03-F11-1v2]], her line printed. Pairs, one minute, then the class. Ask: "Where would 6/6 be?" Lay the strip from 0 again: the first part ends
at the first mark after 0.

If many still count marks, every student lays the sixths strip on line 1 and touches each part, saying "one sixth,
two sixths..."

#### 5 min. Past 1: the second whole

Whole class. Lay a second long strip after the first on the board line: it runs to 2. Students use line 4, printed
in halves, and fill the frame under Example 2.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K11-2]]). [[steps:s03-K11-2]]

#### 10 min. Past 1, alone

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P11-7]], [[s03-P11-8]]), on lines 5 and 6: to cut each printed part in 2, mark its middle.
Early finishers: the challenge.

Watch for 7/10 and 14/20 for the first (error A, then error K: the whole line taken as the whole), and 8/8
for the point at 2 (error K). Ask: "Where is 1 on your line? How many parts make one whole?"

#### 5 min. Fix it: Max's line

[[s03-F11-2]], his line printed. Pairs, one minute, then the class. Cover the line past 1 with a sheet of paper: four parts make 1. Uncover it and count on past 1:
"five fourths."

If many said 8/8 for the point at 2, cover line 6 the same way.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" Expect, in this
order: "there are more parts"; "each part is smaller"; last, "the point stayed in the same place, so it is the
same amount with a new name". Students finish the frame on side 2.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The exit ticket ([[s03-X11v2]]). Both lines are printed on the ticket. Collect at 5 minutes.

### Other rooms

- **No number lines sheet.** Lay a whole strip along lined paper and mark 0 and 1 at its ends; for 0 to 2,
  rule 0, 1 and 2 evenly and measure the parts.
- **No strip sheet.** Cut strips 10 inches long from scrap paper: rule fifths every 2 inches, tenths every
  inch; fold the rest.

### At home

**Swap.** No strip sheet: cut strips of one length from plain paper and fold them; for fifths and tenths, rule a
10 inch strip every 2 inches, then every inch. No number lines sheet: lay a strip along a ruled line and mark 0
and 1 at its ends. No projector: point at the page.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the readiness fix, questions 6 and 9, and the challenge.

- **8 min. The strip on the line.** Read [[s03-K11-1]] together; lay and mark as it says.

    **Say.** "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" Expect: more
    parts, each smaller; the point stayed put.

- **7 min. Up to 1.** The child does [[s03-P11-4]] and [[s03-P11-5]], counting parts from 0 aloud.
- **5 min. Fix Ana's count.** [[s03-F11-1v2]].

    **Say.** "Where would 6/6 be?" Expect: at 1, so her point is 5/6.

- **8 min. Past 1.** Read [[s03-K11-2]] together on line 4, then the child does [[s03-P11-7]].
- **4 min. Fix Max's line.** [[s03-F11-2]]. Cover the line past 1 with your hand.

    **Say.** "How many fourths make 1?" Expect: four, so the point is 5/4.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X11v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop questions 6 and 9 and the partner check (students lay their own strip on each point). Never
drop the two fixes or the planned question. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C11-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: number line, point, equivalent, numerator, denominator. The frame is on side 2 and the display.
A cut may be ruled, not folded.

### Tomorrow

Day 12 opens its task with these tickets. Many 7/8 (error C, marks counted): day 12 shows a line where 0 is
counted as "one", and the class lays the strip from 0 again. Many 3/8 (error A): fold fourths into eighths and
count the shaded parts aloud. "Yes, it moved": lay the fourths strip on the line before and after the cut. Many
8/10 on part (b) (error K): cover the line past 1, count the parts in one whole, then count on. Results go on the
record sheet under E11.
