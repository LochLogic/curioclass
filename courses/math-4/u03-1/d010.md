# Day 10: One amount, more than one name

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Print the strip sheets one-sided ahead: students
cut out the strips into their envelopes before the lesson (day 9's last minutes or morning work), or hand them out
pre-cut. Cut three long strips of one length; mark one in halves and one in thirds, and shade 1/2 and 1/3. Put the
display page on the projector. Have day 9's readiness results beside you.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: add, subtract, two facts

Alone, then whole class: [[s03-W10-1]], [[s03-W10-2]], [[s03-W10-3]], [[s03-W10-4]].

**If the readiness check showed gaps:** nothing moves today. Gaps on items 1, 3 and 4 go to day 11's warm-up
and first five minutes. Item 2 (1/2 as fourths) is today's work: seat those students where they can see your
strips, and look at their folds first.

#### 15 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. You fold the long strips, held up high; students fold a blank strip from page 2 of the sheet along
with you and fill the frames on side 1. The display shows each step on its own screen. Read every fraction as
words: "one half", "two fourths", never "one over two".

**Example 1** ([[s03-K10-1]]). [[steps:s03-K10-1]]

Say: 1/2 and 2/4 name the same amount. Fractions like that are equivalent. Put up the card.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K10-2]]). [[steps:s03-K10-2]]

Say the reason in full once: when every part is cut into 2, there are twice as many parts in the whole and twice
as many in the shaded piece, each half the size, so the length does not change. Do not give a rule about
multiplying; section 3.2 writes it.

#### 18 min. Fold and record

Alone, then pairs. Side 1, "Fold and record" ([[s03-P10-1]], [[s03-P10-2v2]], [[s03-P10-3v2]]), 14 minutes.
Shade blank strips only; printed strips are laid, never shaded. On items 2 and 3, shade and
draw each cut on the strip printed on the page, then write the name. Fifths and sixths are too small to fold:
mark each middle in pencil, then check with the tenths or twelfths strip. Item 2 is the
first time two shaded parts are cut: count the shaded fifths, then the shaded tenths, aloud. Then 4 minutes in
pairs: partners lay two strips side by side and check that the shaded lengths match.

Check: Lay the strips together. Does the shaded part reach the same place?

Watch for a new name with only one number changed (error A: 1/6 on the first, 2/10 or 4/5 on the second, 5/12 or
10/6 on the third), and for the same number added to both (error B: 5/6, 7/10, 11/12). Ask as you walk: "How many
parts are in the whole now? How many are shaded?" If many make A, fold thirds into sixths again with the class and
count the shaded parts aloud.

#### 12 min. Fix two wrong answers

"Fix it", side 2 ([[s03-F10-1]], [[s03-F10-2]]). Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then the class.
The display shows each wrong picture beside the right one. Lu cut the shaded third too, so it is 2 parts: 1/6
beside 1/3 is plainly shorter. Mo recounted the shaded parts but not the whole: 6/6 is the whole strip, and only
half is shaded.

If many still keep one number, every student lays their thirds strip on the sixths strip and counts aloud: "one
sixth, two sixths".

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class, at your long halves strip.

**Ask.** "1/2 and 4/8 have different numbers. How can they be the same amount?" Expect: "the parts are smaller,
but there are more of them"; "the shaded part is the same length". Take the length answer first, then the parts.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X10]]). Read it aloud once. Strips stay in the envelope; the ticket's strip is drawn on.
A tick needs the cuts drawn (6 parts, 4 shaded) as well as 4/6. Record results on the record sheet under E10.
Collect the envelopes for day 11.

### Other rooms

- **No strip sheet.** Students cut eight strips of one length from scrap paper. Halves, fourths and eighths: fold
  in 2, again, again. Thirds: roll the strip into an S and press flat. Sixths and twelfths: fold the thirds in 2,
  then again. Fifths and tenths: rule a 10 inch strip every 2 inches, then every inch. Mark each fold with a
  pencil line.
- **No printer.** Write the three "Fold and record" questions and the two fixes on the board; students answer in
  their notebooks.

### At home

**Swap.** No strip sheet: cut strips of one length from plain paper. Fold one in 2, again and again for halves,
fourths and eighths. Roll one into an S and press flat for thirds; fold thirds in 2 for sixths. No projector:
point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, Fold and record 2, and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** [[s03-K10-1]]: you fold a strip, the child folds one too and fills the frame. Then
  [[s03-K10-2]] the same way.

    **Say.** "Lay the folded strip on one not folded. Did the shaded part change length?" Expect: no; more
    parts, each smaller.

- **8 min. Fold and record.** [[s03-P10-1]], then [[s03-P10-3v2]]. The child folds, draws the cuts, then writes.
- **8 min. Fix two.** [[s03-F10-1]], then [[s03-F10-2]].

    **Say.** "Lu cut the shaded third too. How many parts are shaded now?" Expect: 2, so 1/3 = 2/6.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X10]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop Fold and record 3 and the challenge. Never drop the fixes or the strips; every new name is folded
before it is written today. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C10-1]]). The search ends when all four strips are laid.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: equivalent, numerator, denominator, each on a labelled strip. Frames, to take or leave: "Each
part was cut into __. There are __ times as many parts." "Each part is smaller. The length is the same, so __ =
__." Access: pre-cut, wide strips; a fold may be ruled with a pencil line instead.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, 2/6 or 4/3 (error A, one number changed): day 11 shows Lu's 1/3 = 1/6 beside the right strips in
its reteach minutes, and the class folds thirds into sixths again, counting the shaded parts aloud. 4/5 (error B,
2 added to both): day 11 lays the 2/3 strip beside the 4/5 strip, which is plainly longer. 4/6 with no cuts drawn:
these students draw their cuts first in day 11's warm-up. Bring back the envelopes: day 11 lays the strips on the
number line.
