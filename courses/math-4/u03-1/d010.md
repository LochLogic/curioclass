# Day 10: One amount, more than one name

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the strip sheets, the exit tickets and the key. Until the strip sheet exists,
cut six paper strips of one length per student. Cut your large strips and shade 1/2 and 1/3. Put the display page
on the projector. Have day 9's readiness results beside you, with the names of students who missed item 2.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: add, subtract, two facts

Alone, then whole class: [[s03-W10-1]], [[s03-W10-2]], [[s03-W10-3]], [[s03-W10-4]]. Meanwhile, students who
have the strip sheet cut out the strips and put them in the envelope.

**If the readiness check showed gaps:** nothing moves today. Gaps on items 1, 3 and 4 go to day 11's warm-up
and first five minutes. Item 2 (1/2 as fourths) is today's work: seat those students where they can see your
strips, and look at their folds first.

#### 15 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. You fold the large strips, held up high; students fold their own along with you and fill the two
frames on side 1. The display shows each step on its own screen. Read every fraction as words: "one half", "two
fourths", never "one over two".

**Example 1** ([[s03-K10-1]]). [[steps:s03-K10-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s03-K10-2]]). [[steps:s03-K10-2]]

Say the reason in full once: when every part is cut into 2, there are twice as many parts in the whole and twice
as many in the shaded piece, each half the size, so the length does not change. Do not give a rule about
multiplying; section 3.2 writes it.

#### 20 min. Fold and record

Alone, then pairs. Side 1, "Fold and record" ([[s03-P10-1]], [[s03-P10-2]], [[s03-P10-3]]), 14 minutes. Each
student folds or lays strips and writes the new name. Then 6 minutes in pairs: partners lay their two strips side
by side and check that the shaded lengths match.

Check: Lay the strips together. Does the shaded part reach the same place?

Watch for a new name with only one number changed (error A: 1/6 on the first, 5/12 or 10/6 on the third), and
for the same number added to both (error B: 5/6 for the first, 11/12 for the
third). Ask as you walk: "How many parts are in the whole now? How many are shaded?" If many make A, fold thirds
into sixths again with the class and count the shaded parts aloud.

#### 10 min. Fix two wrong answers

"Fix it", side 2 ([[s03-F10-1]], [[s03-F10-2]]). Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then the class.
The display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Lu cut the shaded third too, so it is 2 parts: lay
1/6 beside 1/3, and 1/6 is plainly shorter. Mo recounted the shaded parts but not the whole: 6/6 is the whole
strip, and only half is shaded.

If many still keep one number, every student lays their thirds strip on the sixths strip and counts aloud: "one
sixth, two sixths".

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class, at your large halves strip.

**Ask.** "1/2 and 4/8 have different numbers. How can they be the same amount?" Expect: "the parts are smaller,
but there are more of them"; "the shaded part is the same length". Take the length answer first, then the parts.

**Ask.** "Lu wrote 1/3 = 1/6. How can you tell without counting?" Expect: lay the strips together; 1/6 is shorter.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X10]]). Read it aloud once. Strips stay in the envelope; the ticket's strip is drawn.
Record results on the record sheet under E10. Collect the envelopes for day 11.

### Other rooms

- **No strip sheet.** Students cut six strips of one length from scrap paper. Halves, fourths and eighths: fold
  in 2, again, again. Thirds: roll the strip into an S and press flat. Sixths and twelfths: fold the thirds in 2,
  then again. Mark each fold with a pencil line.
- **No printer.** Write the three "Fold and record" questions and the two fixes on the board; students answer in
  their notebooks.

### At home

**Swap.** No strip sheet: cut six strips of one length from plain paper. Fold one in 2, again and again for
halves, fourths and eighths. Roll one into an S and press flat for thirds; fold thirds in 2 for sixths, again
for twelfths. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, Fold and record 2, and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** [[s03-K10-1]]: you fold a strip, the child folds one too and fills the frame. Then
  [[s03-K10-2]] the same way.

    **Say.** "Lay the folded strip on one not folded. Did the shaded part change length?" Expect: no; more
    parts, each smaller.

- **8 min. Fold and record.** [[s03-P10-1]], then [[s03-P10-3]]. The child folds, then writes.
- **8 min. Fix two.** [[s03-F10-1]], then [[s03-F10-2]].

    **Say.** "Lu cut the shaded third too. How many parts are shaded now?" Expect: 2, so 1/3 = 2/6.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X10]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop Fold and record 2 and the challenge. Never drop the fixes or the strips; every new name is folded
before it is written today. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C10-1]]). The search ends when all four strips are laid.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: equivalent, numerator, denominator, each on a labelled strip. Frames, to take or leave: "Each
part was cut into __. There are __ times as many parts." "Each part is smaller. The length is the same, so __ =
__." Access: pre-cut, wide strips; a fold may be ruled with a pencil line instead.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, 2/6 or 4/3 (error A, one number changed): day 11 shows Lu's 1/3 = 1/6 beside the right strips in
its reteach minutes, and the class folds thirds into sixths again, counting the shaded parts aloud. 4/5 (error B,
2 added to both): day 11 lays 1/3 beside the added answer 2/4, which is plainly longer. Bring back the envelopes: day 11 lays the strips on the
number line.
