# Day 12: The same amount, or not? Prove it

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Put the display page on the projector. Sort day
11's exit tickets: 7/8 (error C), 3/8 (error A), "yes, it moved", 8/10 on part (b) (error K), and the rest. Strip
envelopes stay in desks, closed for the exit.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: multiples

Alone, on the board: [[s03-P12-1]], [[s03-P12-2]], [[s03-P12-3]]. Answers on the display at 4 minutes. Leave
the fives up: the question uses them (5 × 2 = 10).

#### 8 min. The question: one half as tenths, as thirds

**If day 11's tickets showed many C** (7/8): on a board line in fourths, count 0 as "one", then lay a fourths
strip from 0; the point is the strip's end. **Many A** (3/8): fold fourths into eighths; count the shaded parts
aloud. **"Yes, it moved"**: lay the fourths strip on the line, cut each fourth in 2, lay it again; its end stays.
**Many K** (8/10): cover the line past 1, count one whole's parts, then count on. Any of these takes the pairs' 2
minutes and 1 from the class talk.

Alone, 3 minutes, on the line stack, side 1 ([[s03-P12-4]]); a strip only to check. Then pairs, 2 minutes,
then the class.

**Ask.** "Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" Expect, in this order: "yes, 5/10: the
1/2 point is on a tenths mark"; "no: 1/2 falls between 1/3 and 2/3". Take the thirds answer from a student who
used the lines. Listen for 2/3 "because you add one to each" (error B): leave it on the board for Jo's claim.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Students mark the line stack and fill the frames on side 1 as you go. In Example 1 they draw 2 marks
inside each fourth on the fourths line before looking at the twelfths line, the check. The display shows each step
on its own screen.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K12-1]]). [[steps:s03-K12-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s03-K12-2v2]]). [[steps:s03-K12-2v2]]

Tie both to the question: each half cut into 5 makes 1/2 = 5/10; no thirds mark sits at 1/2. Say every new name
with its cut.

#### 12 min. Same or not? Four pairs

Alone, side 1 (pair 1) and side 2 (pairs 2 to 4), 8 minutes ([[s03-P12-5]], [[s03-P12-6]], [[s03-P12-8]],
[[s03-P12-7]]): cut the top line first, then mark and circle. Pair 1: mark the middle of each sixth, then 10/12;
the twelfths line checks the cuts. Then 4 minutes in pairs: partners swap and lay a pencil straight down from one
point. A mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Did you count from 0? Does the pencil touch both points?

Watch for "the same" on pairs 2 or 3 (error B: the same number added to the top and the bottom), and "not the
same, it has bigger numbers" on pairs 1 or 4 (error D). If many show B, mark 3/10 and 5/12 on the board line:
adding moved the point. If many show D, lay two strips side by side: more parts, each smaller. On pair 4, "not
the same, nothing times 6 is 10" is not D: no cut joins sixths and tenths, but both are 1/2 (the halves line).
Day 16 meets this again.

#### 5 min. Jo's claim

Whole class. Read it aloud ([[s03-F12-1]]); the display shows Jo's working. Show the right working after the
class has answered.

**Ask.** "Jo says 3/4 = 5/6 because she added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" Expect: no, the
points do not line up; 5/6 is farther from 0. Then say it once: adding moves the point; cutting each part does
not. Students write what went wrong.

#### 6 min. Kai's claim: a talk

Read it aloud ([[s03-F12-2]]); the display shows Kai's working. Pairs, 2 minutes, then the class; show the right
working after the class has answered.

**Ask.** "Is Kai right? Prove it with the fifths and tenths lines." Expect: no; each fifth cut into 2 makes
tenths, twice as many parts, each half the size: one point. Students mark the middle of each fifth on the
stack's fifths line and check on the tenths line; you mark the display's (or the board). Then they write.

#### 9 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-W12]]). There is no eighths line: cut each fourth in 2 on the fourths line. Read after class
with the tickets; write "One point?" beside any 6/8 called more. Look-fors, each ticked alone: [[steps:s03-W12]]

Watch for "6/8 is more, 6 and 8 are bigger" (error D): ask "how long is one eighth? one fourth?"

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, envelopes closed, read aloud once ([[s03-X12v2]]). Students mark and answer on the ticket; collect pages
and tickets together. A mark for 4/5 past 8/12 and short of 1 is "as well as
you can". Write the results on the record sheet under E12.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Draw the stack on the board, every line 0 to 1 one length; students write on lined paper. For
  the exit, each rules a 12 centimeter line and marks every centimeter: twelfths.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No strips: fold a paper strip to check.

**Skip.** The warm-up, pairs 3 and 4, and the challenge.

    **Say.** "Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" Expect: 5/10, yes; no third is at the
    1/2 point.

- **10 min. Two examples.** Read [[s03-K12-1]] together: mark 3/4, draw 2 marks in each fourth, count 9
  twelfths, check on the twelfths line. Then [[s03-K12-2v2]].
- **5 min. Two pairs.** [[s03-P12-5]] and [[s03-P12-6]]. Check each by laying a pencil straight down from one
  point.
- **6 min. Jo and Kai.** [[s03-F12-1]], then [[s03-F12-2]].

    **Say.** "Jo added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" Expect: no; 5/6 is farther from 0.

- **6 min. Written task.** [[s03-W12]]. The child may draw.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X12v2]] alone on the ticket's line, strips away.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop pair 4 and the partner check. Never drop Jo's claim, the
written task or the exit. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C12-1v2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: equivalent, numerator, denominator, multiple. The frames are on side 1 and the display. Jo's
and Kai's claims are read aloud; the line stack prints large for students who need it.

back at day 13's start; on one marked "One point?" (error D), day 13 says "Watch Example 1". D returns on day 15.
