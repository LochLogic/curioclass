# Day 12: The same amount, or not? Prove it

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Put the display page on the projector. Sort day
11's exit tickets: 5/8 or 7/8 (error C), 3/8 (error A), "yes, it moved", and the rest. Strip envelopes stay closed
in desks.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: multiples

Alone, on the board: [[s03-P12-1]], [[s03-P12-2]], [[s03-P12-3]]. Answers on the display at 4 minutes. Leave
the fives up: Example 2 uses them.

#### 8 min. The question: one half as tenths, as thirds

**If day 11's tickets showed many C** (5/8 or 7/8): first show a line in fourths with 0 counted as "one", then
lay a fourths strip from 0 again; the point is the strip's end, not a tick. **If day 11's tickets showed many A**
(3/8): show "1/3 = 1/6" beside two strips, 1/6 plainly shorter, and count the shaded parts aloud. Either takes 3
minutes from the pairs' talk below. Students who wrote "yes, it moved" sit near you.

Alone, 3 minutes, on the line stack, side 1 ([[s03-P12-4]]); a strip only to check. Then pairs, 2 minutes,
then the class.

**Ask.** "Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" Expect, in this order: "yes, 5/10: the
1/2 point is on a tenths mark"; "no: 1/2 falls between 1/3 and 2/3". Take the thirds answer from a student who
used the lines. Listen for 2/3 "because you add one to each" (error B): leave it on the board for Jo's claim.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Students mark the line stack and fill the frames on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on
its own screen; hold the long strip up for each cut.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K12-1]]). [[steps:s03-K12-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s03-K12-2]]). [[steps:s03-K12-2]]

Tie both to the question: each half cut into 5 makes tenths, so 1/2 = 5/10. No thirds mark sits at 1/2, and the
lines show it. Say every new name with its cut: "each part was cut into 3".

#### 12 min. Same or not? Four pairs

Alone, side 2, 8 minutes ([[s03-P12-5]], [[s03-P12-6]], [[s03-P12-7]], [[s03-P12-8]]): mark both fractions on
their two lines, then circle. Then 4 minutes in pairs: partners swap and check each pair by laying a pencil
straight down from one point. A mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer.

Check: Did you count from 0? Does the pencil touch both points?

Watch for "the same" on pairs 2 or 4 (error B: the same number added to the top and the bottom), and "not the
same, it has bigger numbers" on pairs 1 or 3 (error D). If many show B, mark 3/10 and 5/12 on one line: adding
moved the point. If many show D, lay two strips side by side: more parts, each smaller.

#### 5 min. Jo's claim

Whole class. Read it aloud ([[s03-F12-1]]); the display shows Jo's working beside the right working.

**Ask.** "Jo says 3/4 = 5/6 because she added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" Expect: no, the
points do not line up; 5/6 is farther from 0. Then say it once: adding moves the point; cutting each part does
not. Students write what went wrong.

#### 6 min. Kai's claim: a talk

Read it aloud ([[s03-F12-2]]). Pairs, 2 minutes, then the class.

**Ask.** "Is Kai right? Prove it with the fifths and tenths lines." Expect: no; each fifth cut into 2 makes
tenths, twice as many parts, each half the size, so 2/5 and 4/10 are one point. Hold the long strip in fifths
up and mark each fifth in half. Students write what went wrong.

#### 9 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-W12]]). Students may draw on the line stack. Read after class with the tickets. Look-fors,
each ticked alone: [[steps:s03-W12]]

Watch for "6/8 is more, 6 and 8 are bigger" (error D): ask "how long is one eighth? one fourth?"

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, envelopes closed, read aloud once ([[s03-X12]]). Students mark the line printed on the ticket and
answer there; collect pages and tickets together. A mark for 4/5 past 8/12 and short of 1 is "as well as
you can". Write the results on the record sheet under E12.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Draw the stack on the board, every line 0 to 1 the same length; students judge from it and
  write on lined paper. For the exit, each student rules a line 12 centimeters long and marks every centimeter:
  twelfths.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: read each step from the key and point at side 1. No strips: fold a paper strip to check.

**Skip.** The warm-up, pairs 3 and 4, and the challenge.

- **5 min. The question.** [[s03-P12-4]], on the line stack.

    **Say.** "Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" Expect: 5/10, yes; no third is at the
    1/2 point.

- **10 min. Two examples.** Read [[s03-K12-1]] with its steps from the key; the child marks the lines and fills
  the frame. Then [[s03-K12-2]].
- **5 min. Two pairs.** [[s03-P12-5]] and [[s03-P12-6]]. Check each by laying a pencil straight down from one
  point.
- **6 min. Jo and Kai.** [[s03-F12-1]], then [[s03-F12-2]].

    **Say.** "Jo added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" Expect: no; 5/6 is farther from 0.

- **6 min. Written task.** [[s03-W12]]. The child may draw.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X12]] alone on the ticket's line, strips away.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop pair 4 and the partner check (students check their own with a pencil). Never drop Jo's claim, the
written task or the exit. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C12-1]]), with a strip folded in eighths.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: equivalent, numerator, denominator, multiple. Frames on the display: "Each part was cut into __,
so there are __ times as many parts and each part is smaller. The length is the same, so __ = __." "__ and __ are
not the same amount: the points do not line up." Jo's and Kai's claims are read aloud; the line stack prints
large for students who need it.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, "yes, the same amount" (error B): day 13 gives Jo's claim 5 of its 15 teaching minutes, then
2/5 = 4/7. A name for 2/3 that keeps one number, 2/12 (error A): day 13's fix beside the cut line is for those
students. Hand the written tasks back at day 13's start; on any that said 6/8 is more (error D), write "One
point?" first, since D returns on day 15.
