# Day 90: Find the missing side

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides and the facts check part 2 sheet. Open Community Plot Planner on each device
in Lot planner. Put the display page on the projector and grid paper under the camera.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 2

The 36 quotients from stretch 1, untimed. Students under 32 of 36 on day 89 keep their 9 by 9 table out today.
Mark the sheets after class.

**If the readiness check showed gaps** (more than a third missed item 1 or 2): the warm-up and five more minutes
go to the reteach in the unit opener; the facts check moves to day 91, and the two "On your own" questions move to
day 91's warm-up.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Students fill the two frames on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you.

**Example 1** ([[s11-K90-1]]). Sketch a rectangle: 7 ft on a short side, a question mark on a long side, 63
square feet inside. Say: "Area is how many squares cover it. They come in rows of 7. How many rows of 7 make 63?
I write 7 × ? = 63." Under the camera, shade a row of 7 and count by sevens to 63, a row at a time: 9 rows. "7
times 9 is 63. The bed is 9 feet long. Check: 7 × 9 = 63." The missing side is a missing factor.

**Example 2** ([[s11-K90-2]]). Sketch a rectangle: 12 ft on both long sides ("opposite sides of a rectangle
match"), a question mark on both short sides. Say: "The fence goes all the way round: 12 + ? + 12 + ? = 38. The
long sides use 24 feet. 38 − 24 = 14 feet are left for both short sides, so each is 7. Check: 12 + 7 + 12 + 7 =
38." Then: "Yesterday we wrote 2 × (l + w). Length plus width is half the fence: 19, and 19 − 12 = 7. The same."

Write the four-side equation first, every time: it stops errors B and D.

#### 20 min. Community Plot Planner: the five notes

Alone, in Lot planner. Each family note gives one number and one side; the student finds the missing side, writes
the equation, then fills the plot's tag with the measure the note did not give. Students copy each equation into
the Plot notes table on side 2 ([[s11-T90-1]] to [[s11-T90-5]]).

Watch for a tag marked "counted" or a plot "built with squares" (the pieces came back), and a missing side that
is the note's two numbers added or taken away (54 or 42 for the Lee family: error C). Ask as you walk: "Which
number is fixed? Which side is missing? What equation says that?"

**Stop at 20 minutes**, lot finished or not. The notes, equations and tags are the job; placing plots is the reward.

#### 15 min. On paper: fix two, then two alone

"Fix it", side 1 ([[s11-F90-1]], [[s11-F90-2]]), 6 minutes, each on the display beside the right working. Jo
subtracted: area is rows times columns, so ask "5 times what is 35?". Max took off one long side and stopped: a
fence has two long sides and two short ones.

"On your own", side 2 ([[s11-P90-1]], [[s11-P90-2]]), 6 minutes, alone, no grid. Then 3 minutes: partners swap
and check each answer by putting it back in the equation ("Does 3 × 9 give 27?"). A mismatch gets a question
mark, not the answer.

If many miss the area question: rows of 3 on grid paper until 27 squares are used. If many miss the fence
question: fill in 13 + __ + 13 + __ = 42 together.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

**Ask.** "For the garden bed we never took anything away. For the pen we took away and then halved. Why?"
Expect: "area is rows times columns, so it is what times 7"; "the fence has two of each side". Show the first
answer, then the second, then connect them: both began with an equation for this rectangle, its numbers and one
unknown.

**Ask.** "How can you check a missing side with no one to tell you?" Expect: put it back in the equation.

#### 5 min. Exit: On your own in Community Plot Planner, or the slip

With devices: "On your own" from the tool's header, four items, no hints. Students show the end screen; write
area and perimeter results on the record sheet under E90. No devices, or the tool not yet built: the exit slip
([[s11-X90a]], [[s11-X90b]]).

### Other rooms

- **No devices, or the tool not yet built.** The 20 minutes become the paper path: students solve the five notes
  on side 2, shading rows on grid paper for the area notes and sketching the fence with four labelled sides.
- **One projector.** You run the tool; the class writes each equation on side 2 before you type it.
- **One child at home.** Build the first "On your own" rectangle from 27 paper squares in rows of 3, then write
  the equation.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check (students check their own) and the challenge. Never drop the fixes or the written
equations, and never give the grid back for "On your own". Longer: the challenge ([[s11-C90-1]]), then every
rectangle with area 36 and whole-number sides, with each perimeter.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square foot, equation. Frames on the display: "The missing side
is __ because __ × __ = __." "The two short sides are __ feet together, so each one is __ feet."

### Tomorrow

Many answering 40 or 50 on the poster (error C): day 91 opens with Jo's wrong answer again. Many answering 29 or
12 on the frame (error D): it opens with Max's. Students with a tag marked "counted" sit near you for day 91's
first ten minutes.
