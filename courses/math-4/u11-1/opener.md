# Section 11.1: Area and perimeter formulas (days 89 to 93)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.MD.A.3: "Apply the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real world and mathematical
problems." Students met both measures in grade 3 by counting: area as the unit squares that cover a shape,
arranged in rows and columns (3.MD.C.7), and perimeter as the length of the edge (3.MD.D.8). This section turns
each count into a formula and then uses the formula backwards.

**Area is rows times columns.** A rectangle 6 feet by 8 feet holds 6 rows of 8 one-foot squares, so its area is
6 × 8 = 48 square feet. A = l × w says the same thing for every rectangle. The unit is a square (a square foot,
a square meter), because what is counted is squares.

**Perimeter is the four sides added.** P = l + w + l + w. Because there are two of each side, the same sum is
2 × l + 2 × w, and by the distributive property 2 × l + 2 × w = 2 × (l + w). In words: length plus width is
half the perimeter. Students should see this on a drawn rectangle (the edge cut into two equal halves, each
one length and one width) before they see it in symbols. The unit is a length (feet, meters), because what is
measured is a line.

**A missing side is a missing number in an equation.** If a floor covers 63 square feet and is 7 feet wide,
the equation is 7 × ? = 63, so the missing side is a missing factor: 9 feet. This is the unknown-factor work of
3.OA.A.4 and the factor pairs of 4.OA.B.4. If a fence is 38 feet all the way around and the pen is 12 feet
long, the equation is 12 + ? + 12 + ? = 38: the two long sides use 24 feet, the 14 feet left make two short
sides, so each is 7 feet. Students write the equation for this rectangle, with its numbers and one unknown,
and solve it. They do not recall a formula and fill it in. That is what "apply" means here (Progressions for
the Common Core State Standards, Geometric Measurement).

**Bigger and harder numbers.** Day 91 uses sides of two and three digits (find l + w, then add the sum to
itself) and sides that are fractions with like denominators (1 5/6 + 2 2/6 + 1 5/6 + 2 2/6). Day 92 asks
students to decide which measure a situation needs, with no cue word. A cue word is any word that names the
inside or the edge (covers, fills, around, edge, side, border of, whole floor); the situation (fence, tape,
paint, trim, wire) is not a cue.

**Limits.** Rectangles only. Areas stay within 100, or a one-digit number times a multiple of 10. A side found
from a perimeter comes from an even perimeter of 100 or less. Larger areas come back in units 12 to 14.

## Why this model

Grid paper and tiles on day 89 make both counts visible: the squares inside, a finger along the edge. On day 90
the squares go away. Community Plot Planner gives each student a lot and five family notes ("we need 48 square
feet; our plot is 6 feet wide"), so the student has to write the equation before the plot can be built; the
squares fade as the student works. Where the tool is not built or there are no devices, the same notes are
done on paper with grid paper and sketches. From day 91 a rectangle is a sketch with labelled sides, which is
what students will meet on every later page.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Use the area formula for perimeter, or the other way round | The words have no pictures yet | Sort ten situations by drawing what is measured: the inside or the edge |
| B | Add one length and one width and call it the perimeter | They do not see the edge going all the way round | Trace and name all four sides; write l + w + l + w |
| C | Find a side from an area by subtracting, adding or multiplying the two numbers given | They do not see a missing factor | Rows of the known side on grid paper until the area is used up |
| D | From a perimeter, take off one side only, or forget to halve | They count one pair of sides | Fill in 14 + __ + 14 + __ = 44 together |
| E | Write "40 feet" for an area | Squares and lengths are not told apart | Eight answers with no units: write feet or square feet on each |
| F | Count the squares along the edge, not the unit sides (12 for a 5 by 3 rectangle) | The edge is not yet seen as unit lengths | Tick and number each unit side along the edge, then count the ticks |

Eight answers with no units (error E): the fence round a yard, 64; the carpet on a bedroom floor, 120; the ribbon
round a card, 30; the paint on a wall, 96; the tape round a rug, 26; the grass in a park, 900; the rope round a
play area, 80; the tiles on a hall floor, 54. Students write feet or square feet (yards for the park) on each and
say why.

Each day's teacher page names the wrong answer that signals each error on that day's exit.

## The reteach (readiness gaps)

The readiness check is the last ten minutes of day 88. If more than a third of the class missed item 1 (area
of a 6 by 7 rectangle) or item 2 (perimeter of a 9 by 4 rectangle), day 90's warm-up becomes this reteach, and
the facts check moves to day 91.

1. Each student draws a 5 by 3 rectangle on grid paper.
2. Count the squares inside together, row by row: "5, 10, 15. Three rows of 5 is 15 squares." Write 3 × 5 = 15.
3. Trace the edge with a finger, naming each side: "5, 3, 5, 3." Write 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16.
4. Ask: "Which number tells how much paper the rectangle covers? Which tells how far an ant walks around it?"
5. Students do a 4 by 6 rectangle alone, then compare with a partner.

For item 3 (8 × ? = 48) gaps: fact help goes into the warm-ups only, not into the lesson.

## Supplies for the section

- Per student: grid paper (about six sheets over the section; large squares for students who need them), about
  15 tiles or paper squares (day 89), a pencil.
- Per class: the projector for the display pages, a board, one string loop 36 units long (day 92's pens).
- Devices for day 90 if Community Plot Planner is built; otherwise grid paper.
- A room with nothing: hand-ruled grids and squares cut from paper do every job.

## Words

Area, perimeter, length, width, square unit (square foot, square meter), formula, equation. Teach each on a
labelled rectangle. "Covers" and "goes around" help at first; by day 92 problems use neither.

## How the section is checked

Readiness check: day 88, four items, marked before day 89. Exits: one each on days 89 to 92, written on the
record sheet by error letter. End-of-unit assessment: day 93, seven items, 30 minutes, unaided, no grid; items 5 and 6 are
judged by the teacher with look-fors and answered in day 94's first five minutes. Area returns with larger sides
in units 12 to 14; without that return the standard is only half taught.
