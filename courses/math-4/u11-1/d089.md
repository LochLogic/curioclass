# Day 89: Both measures of one rectangle

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the facts check part 1 sheet, the exit slips and the key. Count 15 tiles or
paper squares into a cup for each student. Have stretch 10's end checks ready to hand back, items 7 and 8 marked.
Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 1

Alone. The 36 products from stretch 1, untimed. Collect at 5 minutes; an item not reached is left blank, not
marked wrong. Mark the sheets after class. Students under 32 of 36 keep their 9 by 9 table out from day 90.

#### 25 min. Stretch 10's judged items, then teaching first: one rectangle

**First 5 minutes: stretch 10's items 7 and 8.** Hand back the end checks. Show one answer that meets each
item's look-fors on the board (Nia's beads, Ravi's estimate). Students fix their own in pen beside the first
try. Collect them for the record sheet.

**Then 20 minutes: build one rectangle** ([[s11-K89-1]]). Whole class; each student builds along with tiles on
grid paper and fills the frame on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows the figure, then each step.

[[steps:s11-K89-1]]

Say as you point: area **covers** the inside, perimeter **goes around** the edge. Leave A = l × w,
P = l + w + l + w and P = 2 × (l + w) on the board.

**For you, not for the class.** l + w + l + w regroups as (l + w) + (l + w): the same sum twice, so
2 × (l + w). It is also two lengths and two widths, 2 × l + 2 × w. That 2 × l + 2 × w = 2 × (l + w) is the
distributive property: doubling a sum is doubling each part and adding. Students need not name it. They show it
on the tiles: two long sides and two short sides, or one long side and one short side, twice.

Watch for a student who counts the corner tiles instead of unit sides on the edge (they get 12, not 16). Have
them run a finger along each side and say each unit side aloud.

#### 15 min. On paper: three rectangles

"Three rectangles", side 1 ([[s11-P89-1]], [[s11-P89-2]], [[s11-P89-3]]). Alone. Students draw each one on grid
paper, shade the inside, trace the edge, then write both equations with the units. For each perimeter they
write the four sides first, then the 2 × form. Tiles stay out.

Watch for a perimeter that is one length and one width (8, 14 or 13: error B) and answers in the
wrong rows (error A). Ask: "Did you count the squares or the edge? Which one did the question ask for?" If many
give one side pair: trace all four sides together and write l + w + l + w. If many swap them: sort the three
drawings' numbers into "covers" and "goes around" on the board.

**If the readiness check showed no gap** (fewer than a third missed item 1, and fewer than a third item 2),
this part is one rectangle ([[s11-P89-1]]), 5 minutes. Then "Missing side" on side 2 ([[s11-P89-4]]), alone,
checked by multiplying back, 5 minutes. Then pairs start the planned question below on their own rectangle, 5
minutes; the class finishes it in "Pull it together".

#### 5 min. On paper: fix two

"Fix it", side 2 ([[s11-F89-1]], [[s11-F89-2]]). Pairs for one minute on Ava's, then the class. The display
shows each wrong working beside the right one. Ava added the four sides: that is the edge, not the inside. Ben
stopped half way round.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Is 2 × (l + w) always the same as l + w + l + w? How do you know?" Expect, and show in this order:
"It worked for all three of mine" (true for those three; ask "and one we have not drawn?"); "you go one length
and one width, then the same again" (trace the tile rectangle to the half-way corner and stop); "it is two
lengths and two widths either way". Close on the second: half way round is l + w, and the other half matches.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, the exit slip ([[s11-X89]]). Students write both equations and the units. Record on the record sheet
under E89.

### Other rooms

- **No tiles.** Students shade 15 squares on grid paper instead; the build is the same.
- **One projector.** You build on the projector or the board; the class builds at their desks first, then
  checks against yours.
- **One child at home.** Measure a real table or rug to the nearest foot and write both measures, then do side 1.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the third rectangle and the pairs minute on Ava's. Never drop the four-side equation before the 2 ×
form, or the fixes. Longer: "Missing side" ([[s11-P89-4]]), then the challenge ([[s11-C89-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square unit, formula, equation, each on a labelled rectangle.
Frames on the display: "The area is __ square units because __ rows of __ cover it." "The perimeter is __ units
because __ + __ + __ + __ = __." Access: large-square grid paper; tiles for touch.

### Tomorrow

On the slip, 11 for the perimeter (error B): day 90's first worked example starts with a finger traced round
all four sides. 22 for the area and 24 for the perimeter (error A): day 90 opens its teaching with Ava's rug again,
two minutes. Units swapped, "24 meters" (error E): note it; day 91 teaches it. Students who counted corner tiles, or
wrote neither equation, sit near you for day 90's worked examples.
