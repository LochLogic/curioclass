# Day 89: Both measures of one rectangle

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the facts check part 1 sheet, the exit tickets and the key. Count 15 tiles or
paper squares into a cup for each student. Have unit 10's end-of-unit assessments ready to hand back, items 7 and 8 marked.
Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 1

Alone. The 36 products from unit 1, untimed. Collect at 5 minutes; an item not reached is left blank, not
marked wrong. Mark the sheets after class. Students under 32 of 36 keep their 9 by 9 table out from day 90.

#### 5 min. Day 88's items 7 and 8

Hand back day 88's end-of-unit assessments. Show a "meets" answer for each. Item 7: "b = 20 + 4 × 20.
4 × 20 = 80. 20 + 80 = 100 beads." Item 8: "18 is about 20, and 7 × 7 is about 50, so about 70. 175 is far too
big. s = 67." Students fix their own in pen beside the first try. Collect them for the record sheet. Many 80 on 7 (F): underline the question and ask "is 80 what it
asked?" Many 175 on 8 (H): write Ravi's steps as two equations.

#### 20 min. Teaching first: one rectangle

**12 minutes together: build one rectangle** ([[s11-K89-1]]). Each student builds along with tiles on grid paper
and fills the frame on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. In the box on side 1, they sketch the 5 by 3 and label
its sides. The display shows the figure, then each step.

[[steps:s11-K89-1]]

Say as you point: area **covers** the inside, perimeter **goes around** the edge. Leave A = l × w,
P = l + w + l + w and P = 2 × (l + w) on the board.

**For you:** 2 × l + 2 × w = 2 × (l + w) is the distributive property (section opener); students show it on the
tiles, not by name.

Watch for a student who counts the squares along the edge instead of the unit sides (12, not 16: a corner square
has two sides on the edge). Have them mark each unit side with a tick and count the ticks.

**8 minutes in pairs.** "Make any other rectangle from your 15 tiles. Each of you writes A = and both forms of P;
swap and check that both forms give the same number."

#### 15 min. On paper: three rectangles

"Three rectangles", side 1 ([[s11-P89-1]], [[s11-P89-2]], [[s11-P89-3]]). Alone. Students draw each one on grid
paper, shade the inside, trace the edge, then write both equations with the units. For each perimeter they
write the four sides first, then the 2 × form. Tiles stay on the desk only to check the 6 by 2.

Watch for a perimeter that is one length and one width (8, 14 or 13: error B), answers in the
wrong rows (error A) and edge squares counted (24 or 22: error F). Ask: "Did you count the squares or the edge? Which one did the question ask for?" If many
give one side pair: trace all four sides together and write l + w + l + w. If many swap them: sort the three
drawings' numbers into "covers" and "goes around" on the board.

**If the readiness check showed no gap** (fewer than a third missed item 1 and fewer than a third missed item 2),
this part is one rectangle ([[s11-P89-1]]), 5 minutes. Then "Missing side" on side 2 ([[s11-P89-4]]), alone,
checked by multiplying back, 5 minutes. Then pairs start the planned question below on their own rectangle, 5
minutes; the class finishes it in "Pull it together".

#### 5 min. On paper: fix two

"Fix it", side 2 ([[s11-F89-1]], [[s11-F89-2]]). Pairs for one minute on Ava's, then the class. The display
shows each wrong working beside the right one. Ava added the four sides: that is the edge, not the inside. Ben
stopped halfway around.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Is 2 × (l + w) always the same as l + w + l + w? How do you know?" Expect, and show in this order:
"It worked for every pair's" (true for those; ask "and one we have not drawn?"); "you go one length
and one width, then the same again" (trace the tile rectangle to the halfway corner and stop); "it is two
lengths and two widths either way". Close on the second: halfway around is l + w, and the other half matches.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, the exit ticket ([[s11-X89]]). Students write both equations and the units. Record on the record sheet
under E89.

### Other rooms

- **No tiles.** Students shade 15 squares on grid paper instead; the build is the same.
- **One projector.** You build at the front; the class builds first, then checks against yours.
- **One child at home.** Measure a real table or rug to the nearest foot and write both measures, then do side 1.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the third rectangle and the pairs minute on Ava's. Never drop the four-side equation before the 2 ×
form, or the fixes. Longer: "Missing side" ([[s11-P89-4]]), then the challenge ([[s11-C89-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square unit, formula, equation, each on a labelled rectangle.
Frames on the display: "The area is __ square units because __ rows of __ cover it." "The perimeter is __ units
because __ + __ + __ + __ = __." Access: large-square grid paper; tiles for touch.
