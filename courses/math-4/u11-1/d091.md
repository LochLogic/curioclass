# Day 91: Both formulas, big sides and fraction sides

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print what the header lists (the facts check part 2 only if day 90 ran the
reteach). Put the display page on the projector. Put the grid paper away:
from today a rectangle is a sketch with labelled sides.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: four sums

Alone, top of side 1: two multi-digit sums and two fraction sums ([[s11-P91-1]], [[s11-P91-2]], [[s11-P91-3]],
[[s11-P91-4]]). Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes. Denominators added (4 6/10 for the third) is a fraction
slip. Those students, and those day 90 flagged ("counted", or shading square by square), sit near you for the examples. If
day 88's item 8 showed many with no estimate: write "about __" before each sum.

**If day 90 ran the reteach:** the facts check part 2 is the warm-up instead, untimed, collected at 5 minutes.
The four sums are dropped; the examples carry the same moves.

**If many made error C or D on day 90's exit:** in "Fix it", take fix 3 (C) or fix 4 (D) first, whole class,
with one minute's turn-and-talk. If day 90 sent A here (day 89's ticket), Ava's rug is the warm-up. The facts
check, when it runs, has the whole warm-up.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Students fill the two frames on side 1 as you go. No grid: each sketch is a rectangle with all four
sides labelled. The display shows each figure, then each step on its own screen.

**Example 1** ([[s11-K91-1]]). [[steps:s11-K91-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s11-K91-2]]). [[steps:s11-K91-2]] Accept 16 12/4, 18 4/4 or 76/4.

Say it twice: length plus width is halfway around, so add it to itself.

#### 12 min. Fix it: five wrong answers

Side 2 ([[s11-F91-1]], [[s11-F91-2]], [[s11-F91-3]], [[s11-F91-4]], [[s11-F91-5]]), one for each error A to E. Pairs, about 2 minutes each: sketch, say what went wrong, write the right working. Then
the display shows the wrong working beside the right one.

Lia found the edge for a floor (A): ask "does the floor cover, or go around?" Ben stopped halfway around (B).
Kai subtracted from an area (C): "8 times what is 72?" Rosa did not halve (D). Nia's number is right and her
unit is wrong (E): "is 150 feet a line or 150 squares?"

#### 22 min. The generator sheet

Alone for 18 minutes, then 4 minutes checking in pairs. The sheet (sheet-d091) holds twelve mixed problems with no
pictures. Every answer is a sketch with labelled sides, an equation and a unit.
Most students finish 8 to 10 in 18 minutes. That is fine: the pairs check what is done,
and the rest go home or into tomorrow's spare minutes.

At 18 minutes, partners swap and compare; where they disagree, both redo it. Then put the sheet's key on the
display, equations and all. Students mark each answer with a tick, or a question mark and the right working.

Watch for: half-size perimeters (B); swapped measures (A); no unit, or feet for an area (E); a side from an
area by taking away (C); a fence side not halved (D); a lost regrouped ten; denominators added. Ask: "Does this cover or go around? So which unit?"

If many make B: write 274 + 189 + 274 + 189 on the board beside 463 + 463 and show they match. If many make E:
the eight answers with no units from the section opener, each written as feet or square feet.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "When the sides are big, why add length plus width first?" Expect: "fewer numbers to add"; "the other
half is the same". Show the first, then the second, then connect: 2 × (l + w) means the sum twice.

**Ask.** "With no picture, how do you know whether to write feet or square feet?" Expect: area covers, so it
counts squares; perimeter goes around, so it is a length.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, three questions ([[s11-X91a]], [[s11-X91b]], [[s11-X91c]]); students may sketch. On the record sheet
under E91: a tick if all three are right; otherwise the letter of each error, and a dot for a number slip.

### Other rooms

- **The sheet not printed.** Write the six problems from the sheet page's "Without a printer" list on the board;
  12 minutes alone, then the key on the board.
- **One projector.** The display page runs the examples and the fixes; the class writes before each step shows.
- **One child at home.** Measure a door and a table top to the nearest foot with a tape measure or a ruler; find both
  measures of each, with units. Then do the sheet together.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the pairs' check on the sheet (students check their own against the key) and the second Ask. Never
drop the fixes, the sketches or the units, and never hand back the grid. Longer: the challenge on the board
([[s11-C91-1]]), then the extension ([[s11-C91-2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: perimeter, area, square foot, mixed number, length, width. Frames on the display: "Length plus
width is __, so the perimeter is __ + __ = __." "It covers, so the unit is square __." "It goes around, so the unit
is __." A student who needs touch traces the four sides of the sketch with a finger while saying each number.
