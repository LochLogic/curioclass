# Day 92: Area or perimeter?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips and the key. Have the 36-foot string loop and grid paper out.
Put the display page on the projector. Write the two warm-up problems on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: two perimeters

Alone, on the back of the grid paper. [[s11-P92-1]] and [[s11-P92-2]]. Students add the length and the width,
then add the sum to itself, as on day 91. Go over both at 5 minutes. A sum not doubled (393, or 4 2/5) is error B:
trace the four sides on the board.

#### 20 min. The dog pen, then teaching first

**10 minutes: the task, in pairs** ([[s11-K92-1]]). Read the task aloud once. Two volunteers hold the string loop
as a long thin pen, then a square one: same string, which dog has more room? Pairs sketch pens on grid paper and
fill the table on side 1: length, width, length plus width, room inside. Push for whole feet only. Watch for pairs
who change the fence while changing the pen (a 10 by 10 pen): count its sides with them.

Close it whole class, from the pairs' tables, smallest pen first:
[[steps:s11-K92-1]]

**Ask.** "Every pen used 36 feet of fence. What is the same about the length plus the width of each? Why?"
Expect: "they all make 18"; then "the fence is two lengths and two widths, so one of each is half". Take the
first, then press for the second.

**Ask.** "Two rectangles have the same perimeter. Must they have the same area?" Expect "no": point at 1 by 17
(17 square feet) and 9 by 9 (81).

**10 minutes: two worked examples, whole class.** Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go.

**Example 1, times as long** ([[s11-K92-2]]). [[steps:s11-K92-2]]

"2 times as long" is 2 × 7, not 7 + 2: the same as stretch 10's comparing. A child who writes 9 is adding.

**Example 2, two steps** ([[s11-K92-3]]). [[steps:s11-K92-3]]

Say the question in each step: "What is this number, the inside or the edge?"

#### 20 min. Six problems by hand

Alone, side 2. Read each problem aloud once, all six at the start, then students work. For each, students
write "area" or "perimeter" first, sketch on the grid paper, then the answer with its unit. None of the six says "covers" or
"around": the story tells which. Problems 3 and 5 are "times as long".
[[s11-P92-3]] [[s11-P92-4]] [[s11-P92-5]] [[s11-P92-6]] [[s11-P92-7]] [[s11-P92-8]]

After 15 minutes, pairs swap and check each answer against the story: "Is your number the inside or the edge?"
A mismatch gets a question mark.

Watch for: a "times as long" length found by adding (8 for the bench, 11 for the flag); the wrong measure (30
tiles, 126 feet of tape, 162 inches of trim: error A); "54 feet" of tiles (error E); stopping at the side (7 for
the dog run). If many choose the wrong measure: each pair sketches problem 1 and shades what the story asks
for, the inside or the edge. If many add for "times as long": draw 2 bars of 9 for the flag.

#### 5 min. Written task

Alone, the box at the foot of side 2: [[s11-P92-9]]. Students may answer aloud to you instead (a scribe is
allowed). Collect them; read after class.

Four look-fors, each ticked alone: (1) says what is measured, the inside or the edge; (2) names the measure;
(3) the unit matches, feet for perimeter; (4) the reason comes from the situation, not from one word.

Meets (ours, untested): "Perimeter. The tape makes the lines of the rectangle. It does not fill the floor. 14 + 9
+ 14 + 9 = 46 feet." Does not meet: "Area, because it says floor. 14 × 9 = 126 square feet." That answer
rests on one word and measures the inside. A child who writes "perimeter because it is tape" has look-fors 2 and
3 only: ask "what does the tape do?"

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Which problem was hardest to decide? What in the story told you?" Expect the dog run or the flag.
Show a problem where the answer came from the story first, then one from a word. Connect them: ask "inside or
edge?" before any number.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper: [[s11-X92]]. Read it aloud once. Write the results on the record sheet under E92.

### Other rooms

- **No string loop.** Draw a long thin pen and a square one on the board with 36 squares of edge each.
- **One projector.** As in the defaults; the class writes each answer on side 2 before you show it.
- **One child at home.** Make the 36-foot pens from a 36-inch string on a table, with paper squares for the room
  inside. Then do problems 1 to 3 together and 4 to 6 alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: stop the task's table at 6 pens and close it from there; drop the partner check (students check their
own). Never drop the written task or the reading aloud. Longer: the challenge ([[s11-C92-1]]), on the back of the
grid paper.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square foot, times as long. Frames on the display: "This
problem needs __ because the story asks about the __." "The length is __ times __, so it is __ feet."

### Tomorrow

Day 93 is the end check. On the slip, a length of 7 (so 28 and 22) is adding for "times as long": those students
go to stretch 10's comparing help in the respond half. Swapped measures (32 for the floor, 48 for the
string) is error A; "48 yards" is error E: both groups take their error's "if many" action. Hand back the written
tasks at the door, each with its look-fors ticked; no class time is taken.
