# Day 92: Area or perimeter?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print what the header lists. Have the 36-foot string loop and grid paper out, the display on the projector, and
the two warm-up problems on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: two perimeters

Alone, on the back of the grid paper. [[s11-P92-1]] and [[s11-P92-2]]. Students add the length and the width,
then add the sum to itself, as on day 91. Go over both at 5 minutes. A sum not doubled (393, 4 2/5 or 3 7/5) is
error B: trace the four sides on the board. If day 88's item 8 showed many with no estimate: write "about __"
before each sum.

**If many made B on day 91's exit** (383 or 5 5/10): Ben's wrong answer from day 91's display first, one minute.
**E** (240 feet): the opener's eight answers with no units replace [[s11-P92-2]]. **A** (92 for the hall): open
the dog pen with Lia's wrong answer: "Does the floor cover, or go around?"

#### 20 min. The dog pen, then teaching first

**10 minutes: the task, in pairs** ([[s11-K92-1]]). Read the task aloud once. Four volunteers hold the corners of
the string loop as a long thin pen, then a square one: same string, which dog has more room? Pairs sketch two pens
on the grid side, the long thin one and the square, and fill the rest of the table on side 1 by numbers alone:
length + width must be 18. Watch for pairs who change the fence while changing the pen (a 10 by
10 pen): count its sides with them.

**Ask.** "Every pen used 36 feet of fence. What is the same about the length plus the width of each? Why?"
Expect: "they all make 18"; then "the fence is two lengths and two widths, so one of each is half". Take the
first, then press for the second.

**Ask.** "Two rectangles have the same perimeter. Must they have the same area?" Expect "no": point at 1 by 17
(17 square feet) and 9 by 9 (81).

Ask first, from the pairs' tables. Then close it, smallest pen first:
[[steps:s11-K92-1]]

**10 minutes: two worked examples, whole class.** Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go.

**Example 1, times as long** ([[s11-K92-2]]). [[steps:s11-K92-2]]

"2 times as long" is 2 × 7, not 7 + 2: the same as unit 10's comparing. A child who writes 9 is adding.

**Example 2, two steps** ([[s11-K92-3]]). [[steps:s11-K92-3]]

In each step ask: "Is this number the inside or the edge?"

#### 20 min. Six problems by hand

Alone, side 2. Read all six aloud once at the start. For each, students
write "area" or "perimeter" first, sketch on the back of the grid paper (a plain rectangle with its sides
labelled; do not count squares), then the answer with its unit. None of the six says "covers" or
"around": the story tells which.
[[s11-P92-3]] [[s11-P92-4]] [[s11-P92-5]] [[s11-P92-6]] [[s11-P92-7]] [[s11-P92-8]]

After 15 minutes, pairs swap and check each answer against the story.

Check: Is your number the inside or the edge? Is the unit right? If not, put a question mark.

Watch for: a "times as long" length found by adding (8 for the bench, 11 for the flag); the wrong measure (30
tiles, 126 feet of tape, 162 inches of trim: error A); "54 feet" of tiles (error E); stopping at the side (7 for
the dog yard). If many choose the wrong measure: each pair sketches problem 1 and shades what the story asks
for, the inside or the edge. If many add for "times as long": draw 2 bars of 9 for the flag.

#### 5 min. Written task

Alone, the box at the foot of side 2: [[s11-P92-9]]. Students may answer aloud to you instead (a scribe is
allowed). Collect the student pages with the exit tickets at the end; read after class.

Tick each look-for on the key alone; the key gives samples that meet, partly meet and do not (ours, untested). A
child who writes "tape, lines, 46 feet" with no full sentence has look-fors 1 to 3: that is language, not math.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Which problem was hardest to decide? What in the story told you?" Expect the dog yard or the flag.
Show one decided from the story, then one from a word; connect them: "inside or
edge?" before any number.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper: [[s11-X92]]. Read it aloud once. Write the results on the record sheet under E92.

### Other rooms

- **No string loop.** Draw a 17 by 1 pen and a 9 by 9 pen on the board, each with 36 feet of edge.

### At home

**Swap.** String loop: a 36-inch string on a table, an inch for each foot. Grid paper: plain paper for
sketches.

**Skip.** The warm-up, problems 1, 4 and 6, and the challenge.

- **10 min. The dog pen.** Read [[s11-K92-1]] aloud. Make a long thin pen with the string, then a square one, and
  fill the table on side 1 together.

    **Say.** "Every pen used 36 feet of fence. What is the same about length plus width?" Expect: 18 every
    time, half of 36. Then: "Same fence, same room?" Expect: no; 9 by 9 is the most.

- **8 min. Two examples.** [[s11-K92-2]], then [[s11-K92-3]]. In each step ask: "Inside or edge?"
- **10 min. Three problems.** [[s11-P92-4]], [[s11-P92-5]] and [[s11-P92-7]], alone, area or perimeter first.
- **5 min. Tell me.** [[s11-P92-9]]. The child may say it while you write it down.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Read [[s11-X92]] aloud once; the child answers alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: stop the task's table at 6 pens and close it from there. Never drop the written task or the reading aloud. Longer: the challenge ([[s11-C92-1]]), on the back of the
grid paper.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: area, perimeter, length, width, square foot, times as long. Frames on the display: "This
problem needs __ because the story asks about the __." "The length is __ times __, so it is __ feet."
