# Day 2: Search in order

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Sort the marked E1 slips: C (one rectangle counted twice), a short last row,
A (only 2 by 5). From the readiness sheets, list who missed item 2, or both item 3 and E1. Cut a 2 by 5 and a 4
by 7 rectangle from grid paper. Write the warm-up on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: add and subtract

Alone, then whole class: [[s01-W2-1]], [[s01-W2-2]], [[s01-W2-3]], [[s01-W2-4]].

Students who missed readiness item 2, or missed both item 3 and E1, if fewer than a third, sit with you instead.
Item 2: three rows of 8 dots, read as 3 × 8 = 24 and 24 ÷ 3 = 8. Item 3: build 14 with paper squares in every
rectangle; a turned one is the same rectangle.

**If more than a third missed item 2 or 3**, the next 5 minutes are that reteach for the whole class, and E1's
error is taken inside the worked search.

#### 5 min. E1 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 1 slips.

**If day 1's slips showed many C** (2 by 5 and 5 by 2 counted as two): cut a 2 by 5 rectangle from grid paper
and turn it. "Did it change? One rectangle, two names."

**If many drew a short last row** for 3 rows: build 10 in 3 rows with tiles. Rows of 3 leave 1 over; rows of 4
need 12. The last row is short, so 3 rows do not fit.

If neither: one student shows both rectangles. Students who drew only 2 by 5 (A) sit near you for Teaching
first: the search in order answers them.

#### 8 min. Teaching first: one search, 24

Whole class. Students fill the table on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. Pairs build each row count with 24
tiles. The display shows each step on its own screen.

**Example** ([[s01-K2-1]]).
[[steps:s01-K2-1]]

Why stop: once rows × rows is more than the number, any new fit would need fewer in each row than the rows, and
those were tried. Show it with the tiles: 5 rows of 5 would need 25.

#### 20 min. Egg Carton Factory, Pack Station: on paper

Alone, on the rows table page; grid paper to check. When it exists: Pack Station, the same table filled beside
the cartons, stopped at 20 minutes. Until then, the paper path: [[s01-T2-1]], [[s01-T2-2]], [[s01-T2-3]],
[[s01-T2-4]].

For each number of rows from 1 up, students write "fits, with __" or "no" before checking it by a fact or by
shading rows on grid paper. They draw a line under the last pair. After each table, the display shows its key.

Check: Does every row match? Is your line under the same pair? Fix a row in pen.

Walk with one question: "How many rows did you just try? What comes next?" Watch for:

- A turned pair written again, such as 6 × 5 on 30 (error C). Point to the line.
- A table that starts at 2 rows, so no 1 × 36 (error A). "Start with 1 row."
- A row skipped because the fact is not one they know, such as 2 × 18 (error B). Shade it on grid paper. No
  shortcut today; that is day 3.
- 49: a student who stops at 6 rows. 7 rows of 7 fits. 60: stop at 8 rows.

If many miss the same row of 36, shade it together, then go on.

#### 7 min. Fix it: Mia and Kai

Side 2 ([[s01-F2-1]], [[s01-F2-2v2]]). Three minutes alone, one in pairs, three for the class: the display shows
each wrong working beside the right one. Mia used facts she knew, in no order, so she could not know she was
done. Kai kept going past 4 × 7. Turn the cut 4 by 7 rectangle.

#### 6 min. On your own: 40

Alone, side 2, no tiles ([[s01-P2-1]]); grid paper only to check a row. Four minutes, then partners swap and check.

Check: Does your partner start at 1 row? Is every pair there once? Is there a line under the last pair?

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "You found 6 × 6 for 36. Why is every fit after that one you already have?" Expect: "7 rows would need
fewer than 6 in each row, and I already tried those"; "they are the ones before, turned"; "7 × 7 = 49 is past 36". Hold up
the cut 4 by 7 rectangle and turn it.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s01-X2]]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the
record sheet under E2.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Students rule their own rows table: "rows" and "fits?". The fixes and 40 on the board.
- **No tiles.** Squares cut from grid paper.
- **One projector.** For each row count the class writes "fits, with __" or "no" before you show it.
- **One child at home.** 24 coins, rows from 1 up; at 6 rows ask "Have we made this before?"

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop 49 from the rows tables and the partner check (students check their own). Never drop the search
in order, the fixes or the stop. Longer: the challenge ([[s01-C2-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: factor, factor pair, row, product. Anchor chart: the table for 24 with its line. Students
say each row aloud with a partner, in a home language if they wish.

### Tomorrow

On the slip, 10 × 5 as a fourth pair (error C): day 3's E2 back, 2 minutes after its facts check, crosses out
the turned pairs in one list together. "I just know", or no reason: day 3 reads one reason that names the stop.
No 1 × 50 (A) or no 2 × 25 (B): day 3's search from 1, testing from ten times, answers them. Students who listed
fewer than three pairs sit near you for day 3's Teaching first.
