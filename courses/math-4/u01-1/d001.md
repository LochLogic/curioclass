# Day 1: Every rectangle for a number

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Cut a quarter sheet of grid paper for each ticket. Count 24 tiles into a bag
for each pair. Partners are desk neighbours. Put one bag on each pair's desk, closed. Put the display page on the projector. Write
"rows" and "in each row" on the board with a small 2 by 3 sketch of tiles beside them, the words labelled.

### The day

#### 15 min. Readiness sheet

Alone, on paper, no tiles. Say the sheet shows what they bring, not a test; a blank is fine. Give each student a
sheet of grid paper for item 3; names on both; collect them together. Read each item aloud once. Mark it after
class and write R1 to R3 on the record sheet. Nothing today waits on it.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: the rectangles for 12

Whole class. Say once: tiles stay flat on the desk and are only for building. Build at the front; pairs build
along. Students fill side 1 as you go, not ahead. The display shows each step on its own screen.

[[steps:s01-K1-3]]

Now name the words. 3 and 4 are **factors** of 12: they multiply to make it. Together they are a **factor
pair**. 12 is the **product**. Point to the 3 rows, the 4 in each row, and the 12 tiles as you say each word.
Students fill the Words lines at the top of side 2.

Turn the 3 by 4 rectangle a quarter turn so it stands as 4 rows of 3.

**Ask.** "Is a 3 by 4 rectangle a different rectangle from a 4 by 3? Is it a different factor pair?" Expect
first: "yes, it has 4 rows now". Then: "no, it is the same tiles, just turned"; "4 × 3 and 3 × 4 are the same
pair". Turn it back and forth until the class agrees, two minutes at most: one rectangle, one factor pair, written
either way. The search in order is day 2's.

#### 15 min. Rectangles for 18 and 14

Pairs, then alone. Side 2, [[s01-P1-1]] and [[s01-P1-2v2]]. Pairs build each rectangle with tiles; then each student draws it
on grid paper and writes the multiplication inside. Each student writes every multiplication on side 2.

Walk with one question: "Are all your rows full?" Watch for:

- A shape with a short last row: not a rectangle. Ask the pair to count each row aloud.
- 2 by 9 and 9 by 2 drawn as two (error C). Have them lay one drawing on the other and turn it.
- No 1 by 18 or 1 by 14 (error A). Ask "Can all the tiles go in one row?"

If many build only one or two rectangles, build a third for 18 at the front. Then pairs go on.

The student page carries the questions; the display shows the answers in the last minute. Each pair crosses out
any drawing that is another one turned.

Check: Count your rectangles. A turned one is the same rectangle.

#### 15 min. Rectangles for 16 and 15

Pairs, then alone, as before. Side 2, [[s01-P1-3]] and [[s01-P1-4]]. 16 makes a square, 4 by 4. If a pair says a square is
not a rectangle, ask: "Are all its rows full? Are its corners square?" A square is a rectangle with all sides the
same. 15 makes only two rectangles; let pairs argue whether they have missed one, and leave it open.

Pairs who finish try the challenge ([[s01-C1-1]]).

Last minute: the display shows the answers; each pair checks their own, as before.

Check: Count your rectangles. A square counts. A turned one is the same rectangle.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s01-X1v2]]). Each student draws on the quarter sheet of grid paper and writes the 3 rows
answer on the ticket. Read it aloud once. No tiles. Collect the ticket with its quarter sheet at the door, names on
both; write results on the record sheet under E1. Send the family note home.

### Other rooms

- **When the start-of-year check exists.** It takes the first 30 minutes in place of the readiness sheet; the 16
  and 15 part is dropped.
- **No tiles.** Pairs cut squares from grid paper, 24 a pair, in the first minute of Teaching first.
- **No printer.** The readiness items and the four numbers go on the board. Students draw on grid paper or ruled
  squares, and write the multiplications under each drawing.

### At home

**Swap.** Tiles: 24 coins or paper squares. Grid paper: squares ruled on plain paper. No projector: point at
side 1.

**Skip.** The readiness sheet, 15 and the challenge.

- **10 min. Build 12.** Together, build [[s01-K1-1]], then [[s01-K1-2]] and [[s01-K1-3]] with 12 coins; the
  child fills each line on side 1, then the Words line: 3 and 4 are factors, 12 is the product.

    **Say.** Turn the 3 by 4 a quarter turn: "Is this a new rectangle?" Expect: no, the same one turned; 3 × 4
    and 4 × 3 are one factor pair.

- **20 min. 18, 14 and 16.** [[s01-P1-1]], [[s01-P1-2v2]], then [[s01-P1-3]], alone: build each, draw it on the
  squared paper with its multiplication inside, then write them on side 2. Ask: "Are all your rows full?"

    **Say.** "Can all the coins go in one row?" Expect: yes, 1 by 18 counts. A square counts too.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s01-X1v2]] alone, drawing on squared paper, no coins. Read it
  aloud once.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop problem 4, never the drawing on grid paper or the multiplication inside each rectangle. Longer:
the challenge ([[s01-C1-1]]); it needs the square from 16.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: rectangle, row, factor, factor pair, product. Point to a built rectangle for each. A student who
cannot move tiles directs a partner. Retell in a home language if a student wishes.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket: 2 by 5 and 5 by 2 drawn as two (error C), a shape with a short last row for the 3 rows question,
or no 1 by 10 (error A). Day 2's second five minutes, after the warm-up, answer the ticket if no whole-class
readiness reteach is needed: for C, a rectangle cut from grid paper is turned; for a short last row, the exit's 10 is built in 3 rows
and the last row is looked at. The readiness sheet, marked tonight, says whether day 2 opens instead with
the reteach on item 2 or item 3.
