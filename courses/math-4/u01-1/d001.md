# Day 1: Every rectangle for a number

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the readiness sheet, the student page both sides, the exit slips and the key. Cut a quarter sheet of grid
paper for each slip. Count 24 tiles into a bag for each pair. Put the display page on the projector. Write
"rows" and "in each row" on the board with a small 2 by 3 sketch of tiles beside them, the words labelled.

### The day

#### 15 min. Readiness sheet

Alone, on paper, no tiles. It is the first day of the year: say the sheet is to see what they bring, not a test,
and that a blank is fine. Read each item aloud once. Collect it; mark it after class. Nothing today waits on it.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: the rectangles for 12

Whole class. Build with tiles at the front; pairs build along with their own tiles. Students fill the frames on
side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on its own screen.

Now name the words. 3 and 4 are **factors** of 12: they multiply to make it. Together they are a **factor
pair**. 12 is the **product**. Point to the 3 rows, the 4 in each row, and the 12 tiles as you say each word.

Turn the 3 by 4 rectangle a quarter turn so it stands as 4 rows of 3.

**Ask.** "Is a 3 by 4 rectangle a different rectangle from a 4 by 3? Is it a different factor pair?" Expect
first: "yes, it has 4 rows now". Then: "no, it is the same tiles, just turned"; "4 × 3 and 3 × 4 are the same
pair". Turn it back and forth until the class agrees: one rectangle, one factor pair, written either way.

Do not show how to find them all or when to stop. That is day 2.

#### 15 min. Rectangles for 18 and 14

Pairs, then alone. Side 2, [[s01-P1-1]] and [[s01-P1-2v2]]. Pairs build each rectangle with tiles; then each student draws it
on grid paper and writes the multiplication inside. Each student writes every multiplication on side 2.

Walk with one question: "Are all your rows full?" Watch for:

- A shape with a short last row: not a rectangle. Ask the pair to count each row aloud.
- 2 by 9 and 9 by 2 drawn as two (error C). Have them lay one drawing on the other and turn it.
- No 1 by 18 or 1 by 14 (error A). Ask "Can all the tiles go in one row?" Say no more; the order of the search
  is day 2's.

If many build only one or two rectangles, stop and ask the class for a third rectangle for 18; build it at the
front. Then pairs go on.

Last minute: the display shows the answers. Each pair crosses out any drawing that is another one turned.

Check: Count your rectangles. A turned one is the same rectangle.

#### 15 min. Rectangles for 16 and 15

Pairs, then alone, as before. Side 2, [[s01-P1-3]] and [[s01-P1-4]]. 16 makes a square, 4 by 4. If a pair says a square is
not a rectangle, ask: "Are all its rows full? Are its corners square?" A square is a rectangle with all sides the
same. 15 makes only two rectangles; let pairs argue whether they have missed one, and leave it open.

Pairs who finish try the challenge ([[s01-C1-1]]).

Last minute: the display shows the answers; each pair checks their own, as before.

Check: Count your rectangles. A square counts. A turned one is the same rectangle.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s01-X1v2]]). Each student draws on the quarter sheet of grid paper and writes the 3 rows
answer on the slip. Read it aloud once. No tiles. Collect at the door; write results on the record sheet under E1.

### Other rooms

- **When the start-of-year check exists.** It takes the first 30 minutes in place of the readiness sheet, and
  the 16 and 15 part is dropped. Teaching first, the 18 and 14 part and the exit stay as written.
- **No tiles.** Pairs cut squares from grid paper, 24 a pair, in the first minute of Teaching first.
- **No printer.** The readiness items and the four numbers go on the board. Students draw on grid paper or ruled
  squares, and write the multiplications under each drawing.
- **One child at home.** Use 24 coins or paper squares. Build 12 together, then let the child find the
  rectangles for 18 alone. Ask the turn question and wait.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop problem 4, never the drawing on grid paper or the multiplication inside each rectangle. Longer:
the challenge ([[s01-C1-1]]); it needs the square from 16.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: rectangle, row, factor, factor pair, product. Point to a built rectangle for each. A student who
cannot move tiles directs a partner. Retell in a home language if a student wishes. Frame on the display:
"__ and __ are a factor pair of __, because __ × __ = __."

### Tomorrow

On the slip: 2 by 5 and 5 by 2 drawn as two (error C), a shape with a short last row for the 3 rows question,
or no 1 by 10 (error A). Day 2's first five minutes answer the slip if no whole-class readiness reteach is
needed: for C, a rectangle cut from grid paper is turned; for a short last row, the exit's 10 is built in 3 rows
and the last row is looked at. The readiness sheet, marked tonight, says whether day 2 opens instead with
the reteach on item 2 or item 3. Day 2 teaches the search in order.
