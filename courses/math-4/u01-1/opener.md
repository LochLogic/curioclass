# Unit 1.1: Rectangles and factor pairs (days 1 to 3)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.OA.B.4, the first of its four parts: "Find all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1–100."
Unit 1.2 teaches the other three (multiples, and prime and composite).

**A factor pair is a rectangle.** In grade 3, students found area by counting squares in rows and columns
(3.MD.C.7). Turn that round: take 12 squares and ask which rectangles they make. 1 row of 12, 2 rows of 6, 3
rows of 4. Each rectangle is a multiplication, 3 × 4 = 12, and its sides are a factor pair of 12. Some
counts of rows do not work: 12 squares in 5 rows leave a short last row, so 5 is not a factor of 12. A factor
is a number of rows that comes out equal, with none left over.

**A turned rectangle is the same pair.** A 3 by 4 rectangle turned on its side is a 4 by 3 rectangle. It is
the same rectangle and the same factor pair, so 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 are listed once. This is the commutative
property students met in grade 3 (3.OA.B.5), now used to decide when a list is finished.

**The search in order, and the turn.** To find every pair, try 1 row, 2 rows, 3 rows and on, in order, and
write "fits, with __" or "no" for each. For 36: 1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12, 4 × 9, 5 no, 6 × 6. At 6 the two
factors meet. Any number of rows past 6 would need a partner smaller than 6, and every number below 6 has
already been tried. So the search stops there. For a number that is not a square, the stop comes where the
next try is already bigger than its partner, or where a pair already found comes back turned. That is why the
search for any number up to 100 never needs more than 10 tries: 10 × 10 is 100. Students say why they stopped;
they are not given a rule to recite.

**Pairs outside the times tables.** The tables stop at 9 × 9 (or at ten times). The search does not. 3 is a
factor of 42 because 3 × 14 = 42, and 14 is in no table students learned. Students find it by building from
ten times: 3 × 10 = 30, 12 more is 3 × 4, so 3 × 14 = 42. This is division as a missing factor (3.OA.B.6):
"3 rows of how many make 42?"

**Limits.** Whole numbers 1 to 100. No factor trees, no greatest common factor (grade 6). The Progressions say
students "need not be fluent" in finding all pairs, so nothing in the unit is done for speed.

## The rows table

The model the unit carries. A table with three columns: rows, "fits, with __", or no. Each number of rows from
1 is tried in order; each fit is written as an equation; the turn is marked with a star.

- **Day 1.** Square tiles, then grid paper. Every rectangle for 12 under the camera, then pairs build 18 and
  20. An equation inside each rectangle.
- **Day 2.** The rows table on paper (rows-table.md), checked by the fact or on grid paper. Every list ends at
  the turn.
- **Day 3.** Lists only. No tiles, no grid. The table is drawn only by a student who is stuck.

The rows table page holds eight tables, enough for days 2 and 3: one a student, both sides.

## Why this model

Tiles make "a factor" something a hand does: deal the squares into rows and look at the last row. The rows
table keeps the same question ("how many rows, and does it fit?") with the tiles gone, and it puts the tries in
order, which is what lets a student say the list is finished. Egg Carton Factory's Pack Station deals a count
into any number of rows and shows what is left over; it is the planned tool for day 2, and its cards called
Every shape are planned for day 3. The tool is not live, so both days run their paper path in the same minutes:
the rows table, with each fit checked by the fact or on grid paper.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

Unit 1.2 meets errors D to G; its opener explains them. E appears once here, on E3's second part.

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | List some pairs and stop; often leave out 1 × the number | There is no order to the search | Search 30 in order as a class: for 1 to 6 rows, say "yes, with __" or "no" |
| B | Find only pairs from the times tables: no 3 × 14 for 42 | A factor is "a fact I know", not "a number of rows that fits" | Test 3 and 4 on 42, 45, 56 and 60, starting from ten times |
| C | Count a turned pair as new, or keep searching past the turn | 4 × 9 and 9 × 4 are not yet seen as one rectangle | Cut a 4 by 9 rectangle from grid paper, turn it, name it both ways; cross out the turned pairs in two lists |

The day each wrong example is shown: A and C on day 2, B on day 3. Each day's teacher page names the wrong
answer that signals each error on that day's exit.

## The reteach (readiness items 2 and 3)

Readiness items are marked after day 1 (readiness.md, or the start-of-year check once it exists). If more than a
third of the class missed item 2 or item 3, day 2's warm-up and first five minutes are this reteach for the whole
class. Otherwise run it with the students who missed it during day 2's warm-up, and the five minutes go to E1.

**Item 2, division as a missing factor.**

1. Draw 3 rows of 8 dots on the board.
2. Ask: "How many rows? How many in each row? How many dots?" Write 3 × 8 = 24.
3. Cover one row's count with your hand: "3 rows of how many make 24?" Write 3 × ? = 24, then 24 ÷ 3 = 8.
4. Pairs draw 4 rows of 7 dots and write both equations, the multiplication first.

**Item 3, every rectangle for an area.** Only for students who also missed E1, since day 1 built rectangles
with tiles. Each student takes 14 paper squares and builds every rectangle, one at a time, writing its
equation. Turn each one: "Is this a new rectangle, or the same one turned?"

Item 1 (facts) has no reteach slot: it is the first reading for the facts checks on days 3 and 4.

## Supplies for the unit

- Per pair: 24 square tiles or paper squares (day 1, and the reteach on day 2).
- Per student: grid paper, the rows table page, the day 3 practice sheet (practice-d003.md), a pencil.
- Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board; a 9 by 9 multiplication table for any student who
  asks on day 3.
- A room with nothing: squares cut from paper, and tables ruled by hand.

## Words

Factor, factor pair, row, column, product, rectangle, each taught on a labelled rectangle. Frame: "__ and __
are a factor pair of __, because __ × __ = __." A student may say a pair in a home language first, then in the
frame.

## How the stretch is checked

Readiness: three items, day 1 (readiness.md), marked after class. Exits: one each on days 1 to 5, on paper.
Day 2's exit and the written tasks of days 3 and 5 are judged with look-fors. End check: day 6, six items,
unaided; items 1 and 6 are this unit's. All of it goes on one record sheet for the stretch (record.md), by
error letter. The family note (family.md) goes home on day 1.
