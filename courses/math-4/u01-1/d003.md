# Day 3: Factor pairs with no tiles

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Mark the E2 slips and sort them: a turned pair listed as new (C), no reason
given, fewer than three pairs, and the rest. Put 9 by 9 tables where students can reach them. Seat students who
listed fewer than three pairs on E2 near you for Teaching first.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: facts check, part 1

Alone, on the facts check sheet: the 36 products, untimed, no clock shown. Collect at 5 minutes; not reached is
not wrong. After class, write the number right under F1 on the record sheet. A 9 by 9 table stays on hand all
lesson for anyone who asks.

#### 2 min. E2 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 2 slips.

**If day 2's slips showed many C** (10 × 5 listed as a fourth pair): write 1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10, 10 × 5 on
the board and cross out the turned pair together. "10 × 5 is the 5 by 10 rectangle turned."

**If many gave no reason:** read one slip whose reason names the turn: "8 × 8 is past 50, so any new pair would
be one I have, turned."

If neither: say that today's search answers a missing 2 × 25 (B) or 1 × 50 (A).

#### 9 min. Teaching first: 48 with no tiles, then 81

Whole class, lists only: no tiles or grid from today. Students fill the example lines on side 1 as you go. The display
shows each step; write the list on the board as it grows.

**Example** ([[s01-K3-1]]).
[[steps:s01-K3-1]]

Point to 3 × 16 and 4 × 12: not in the tables, both found from ten times. A factor fits with none left; it
need not be a fact you know. Leave "start from ten times" on the board. If what is left is still past your table,
take another ten.

**Example 2** ([[s01-K3-2]]).
[[steps:s01-K3-2]]

#### 10 min. Every shape on paper: 72 and 90 (Egg Carton Factory, when it exists)

Alone, side 1 ([[s01-T3-1]], [[s01-T3-2]]). Everyone does 72; 90 for those who finish. For each number of rows
from 1 up, students write "fits, with __" or "no", mark the turn and list the pairs. At 5 minutes the display
shows 72's list; students tick or fix in pen. At 10 minutes, 90's list.

Watch for 72 with no 3 × 24 or 4 × 18, and 90 with no 3 × 30, 5 × 18 or 6 × 15 (error B): ask "Count 3 by tens:
30, 60. How far is 60 from 72?" Watch for 9 × 8 or 10 × 9 added (error C): ask "which pair is that, turned?"

When it exists: cards 4a and 4b (Every shape), stopped at 10 minutes. Students write "fits" or "no" on paper
before each tap of Pack, since Pack does the dividing.

#### 7 min. Practice set 1: 63, then 44

Alone, on the practice sheet ([[s01-P3-2]], then [[s01-P3-1]] for those who finish). About 5 minutes, then the
check screen; students tick a right list and fix a wrong one in pen.

Check: Did you start at 1? Did you test the numbers not in your tables from ten times? Did you stop when rows ×
rows passed the number?

If many miss 4 × 11 or 3 × 21, test 3 on 63 together: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 more is 3 × 1, so 3 × 21 = 63.

#### 8 min. Practice set 2: 75, then 66

Alone, the same way ([[s01-P3-3]], then [[s01-P3-4]]). Walk first to the students with the fewest ticks in set 1.

Check: Did you start at 1? Did you test the numbers not in your tables from ten times? Did you stop when rows ×
rows passed the number?

Watch for 75 with no 3 × 25, and 66 with no 3 × 22 (error B).

#### 5 min. Fix it: Rosa's list

Side 2 ([[s01-F3-1]]). Two minutes alone, one minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class: the display
shows Rosa's working beside the right working. If many still skip 3: test 3 and 4 on 45 and 56 from ten times.

#### 6 min. Written task: when to stop

Alone, side 2 ([[s01-W3]]). It may be dictated. Collect it; read it after class against the look-fors:
[[steps:s01-W3]]

It goes back on day 4.

#### 3 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "How can 3 be a factor of 42 when 42 is not in the 3 times table you learned?" Expect: "3 × 14 = 42";
"start from ten times: 3 × 10 = 30, then 12 more"; "a factor fits with none left, even if it is not a fact I
know".

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s01-X3a]], [[s01-X3b]]). Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door;
write results on the record sheet under E3.

### Other rooms

- **No devices, or the tool not yet built.** The paper path above, in the same 10 minutes.
- **No printer.** Write the numbers on the board; students list in notebooks, rows down the margin.
- **One projector.** Once the tool exists, you tap Pack; the class writes "fits" or "no" first. Everyone writes
  the whole list, then the class checks it.
- **One child at home.** Search 48 together aloud; the child then does 63 and 75 alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop 66 from set 2. Never drop the fix, the written task or the test from ten times.
Longer: the challenge ([[s01-C3-1]]), then the unit's extension: "Which numbers to 50 have an odd number of
factors? Why?"

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: factor, factor pair, product, turn. Frame on the display: "__ and __ are a
factor pair of __, because __ × __ = __." A student may say a list aloud before writing it.

### Tomorrow

On the slip, no 7 × 14 (error B): day 4 tests 7 on 98 from ten times. "Yes, 98 is even" (error E): day 4 says
so and points ahead to its wrong example. No 1 × 98: write "start at 1" on the slip. Written tasks that stop at
64, or name no turn: day 4 reads one that names the turn. Then day 4 reads 3 × 7 = 21 three ways.
