# Day 13: Cut each part, multiply both numbers

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Mark and sort the E12 tickets: "yes, the same
amount" is error B. Put the display page on the projector. Draw a long line from 0 to 1 on the board, marked in
fifths, with a point at 2/5. It stays up all lesson.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: multiples and factor pairs

Alone, side 1 ([[s03-W13-1]], [[s03-W13-2]]). Hand back the E12 tickets and day 12's written tasks as they work.
On a written task that called 6/8 more (error D), say: "Watch Example 1: one point gets two names." Return to it
after step 5. At 4 minutes, the answers on the display; students fix in pen. Today these products count parts.

Check: Do the multiples run 7 to 42? Does 18 have three pairs?

#### 15 min. Teaching first: the rule under the line

Whole class. Students draw the cuts and fill the frames on side 1 as you go. You cut the board line; the display
shows each step on its own screen.

**If day 12's tickets showed many B** (2/3 and 4/5 called the same amount): read Tam's claim ([[s03-F13-3]]),
3 minutes. Mark 4/7 on the board line, a little past the 1/2 point. Two points: adding moved the point. Then
Example 1 and Example 2 as written. Take the 3 minutes from Cuts on printed lines: drop problem 5 there.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K13-1]]). [[steps:s03-K13-1]]

Point at each number as you say it. The 2 in front is the cut. The cut is the number in front, not the top
number: here both happen to be 2. The 5 and the 10 count the parts in one whole.
The 2 and the 4 count the parts from 0 to the point.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K13-2v2]]). Past 1, the whole is still 0 to 1. [[steps:s03-K13-2v2]]

**Ask.** "What did the cut change, and what did it not change?" Expect: "the numbers: more parts, each one
smaller", then "the point". Take "more parts" first and "the point" last.

#### 15 min. Cuts on printed lines

Alone, 12 minutes ([[s03-P13-1]], [[s03-P13-2]], [[s03-P13-3]]). On each line: the cuts, the old name above, the
new name below, the rule under it. Then 3 minutes in pairs: partners check each new name by counting on the line,
the parts from 0 to the point and the parts in one whole. A mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer.

Check: Are the new names 3/6, 5/10, 10/6 and 10/8? Is the cut in front, on the top and on the bottom?

On 0 to 2 lines, students may mark only the cuts they need. Watch for the top left alone, 1/6, 5/6
or 5/8 (error A): "Were the parts up to the point cut too?" For 7/5 or 7/6
(error B): "Where on the line do you see adding?" For 10/12 or 10/16 (error K): cover the line past 1 and count
one whole. If many write A: count the parts up to the point aloud on the board line, before the fix.

#### 6 min. Fix it: 2/5 = 2/10

Side 2 ([[s03-F13-1]]). Two minutes alone, one in pairs, then the class. The display shows Ria's working beside
the right working. Ria counted the new parts in the whole and forgot the parts up to the point. On the board line,
2/10 is another point, nearer 0. If many keep the 2: count the parts up to the point aloud, one by one.

#### 9 min. Is it twice as much?

Pairs, then alone.

**Ask.** "We multiplied both numbers by 2. Is 4/10 twice as much as 2/5?" One minute in pairs, then the class.
Expect "yes, it is times 2" first, then "no, it is the same point". Let the board line settle it: one point, so one
amount. Twice as many parts, each half as big.

Then the fix for Sam, side 2 ([[s03-F13-2v2]]), 3 minutes alone: his claim is about 1/2 and 3/6, on the line
from problem 3. The display shows his working beside the right one. If many still write "3 times" (error F): mark
1/2 and 3/6 on one line; one point.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "In 3/2 = (5 × 3)/(5 × 2), which number tells the cut? Which numbers count parts?" Expect: "the 5 is
the cut"; "10 is the parts in one whole, 15 the parts up to the point". The rule is the cut, written down.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X13v2]]), read aloud once. Collect at the door; results go on the record sheet under E13.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Rule each line on the board with its marks and point; students copy it with a ruler, then cut.
  The key is read aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No ruler: the edge of a book.

**Skip.** The warm-up, problem 5 and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** Read [[s03-K13-1]] together; the child draws the cuts and fills the frame. Then
  [[s03-K13-2v2]].

    **Say.** "What did the cut change, and what did it not change?" Expect: more parts, each smaller; the point
    stayed.

- **10 min. Cuts.** [[s03-P13-1]], then [[s03-P13-2]]: the cuts, both names, the rule.

    **Say.** "Count the parts from 0 to the point, then in one whole." Expect: 10 and 6, so 10/6.

- **8 min. Fix two.** [[s03-F13-1]], then [[s03-F13-2v2]].

    **Say.** "We multiplied both numbers by 2. Is 4/10 twice as much as 2/5?" Expect: no, it is the same point.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X13v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop problem 5 and the pairs check (students count their own). Never drop the rule under each line,
the fix for 2/5 = 2/10 or the twice question. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C13-1]]), then the extension
([[s03-C13-2]]), for the few who are ready: hundredths come on day 15.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: multiply, equal, cut, parts in one whole, each shown on a cut line. A student may say the cut in
a home language first. Frame on the display: "Each part was cut into __, so I multiply both numbers by __."
Lines may be printed large; a student may say the cuts and you draw them.
