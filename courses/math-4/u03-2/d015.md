# Day 15: Equal fractions with no drawing

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print what the header lists. Mark the E14 tickets and sort them: 4/3 or 20/10 (A), 1/9 (B), and the rest. Put
the display page on the projector. Rule a long line from 0 to 1 on the board, cut into tenths, with room above
it to write.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: four facts

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s03-P15-1]], [[s03-P15-2]], [[s03-P15-3]], [[s03-P15-4]]). Read the answers aloud at 4
minutes. Hand back the E14 tickets.

**If day 14's tickets showed many A** (4/3, or 20/10): the facts are dropped. Write "2/5 = 2/10" on the board
beside the cut line, where the point is at 4/10. Count the parts in the point aloud, then fix it together.

**If day 14's tickets showed many B** (1/9: 3 taken from both): the facts are dropped. Mark 4/12 and 1/9 on one
line: taking away moves the point, grouping does not. Students who wrote 1/9 sit near you for the sets.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: one tenth cut into 10

Whole class, at the board line. Students fill the Example 1 frame on side 1.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K15-1]]). [[steps:s03-K15-1]]

This is the last line given. From here a student may sketch one to check, but the work is written.

#### 4 min. Example 2: no drawing

Whole class. Students fill the Example 2 frame.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K15-2]]). [[steps:s03-K15-2]]

Leave on the board: "Find the number that multiplies. Do the same to the other number."

#### 7 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

Side 1 ([[s03-F15-1]], [[s03-F15-2]]). Two minutes alone on each, then one minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then
the class. The display shows each wrong working beside the right one.

Kim read 50 and 100 as whole numbers (error D). On the board line, cut one tenth into 10: more parts, each
smaller. Five tenths hold 50 hundredths, and the point is at 1/2. Nia kept the top number (error A). Ask "how
many hundredths are in the one tenth now?"

If many still say 50/100 is more: cut a second tenth into 10 and count to 20 hundredths, then 50.

#### 24 min. Missing numbers: four sets of three

Alone, side 2. Each set is three blanks. After each set the display shows its key; students tick a right first
try and fix a miss in pen. They count their ticks at the end. The count stays with them. No clock.

Set 1 cuts the parts ([[s03-P15-5]], [[s03-P15-6]], [[s03-P15-7]]). Set 2 groups them ([[s03-P15-8]],
[[s03-P15-9]], [[s03-P15-10]]). Set 3 is tenths and hundredths ([[s03-P15-11]], [[s03-P15-12]],
[[s03-P15-13]]). Set 4 mixes all three ([[s03-P15-14]], [[s03-P15-15]], [[s03-P15-16]]). About 6 minutes a set,
checking included.

Check: Did you find the number that multiplies? Did you do the same to both numbers? Fix a miss in pen.

Watch for a blank that copies the given number (error A: 7 for 7/5, 13 for 13/10). Ask "how many parts is each
one cut into?" Watch for the same number added or taken away (error B: 12 for 7/5, 11 for 16/10). Ask "what
times 5 is 10?"

If many make A in set 1: write the multiplier above the equal sign for set 2. If many make B: mark 7/5 and 12/10
on the board line; the points are not the same.

#### 6 min. Names for one fraction

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P15-17]]), then the class reads the row aloud.

**Ask.** "What repeats in the names for 7/2?" Expect: "the bottom goes up 2 each time and the top goes up 7";
"each top is 7 times as many as the halves". Show the first, then the second. If no one sees 350: half of 100
is 50, and 7 × 50 = 350.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "With no line, how do you find the missing number?" Expect: "find what one number was multiplied by,
then multiply the other"; "if the numbers got smaller, divide both by the same number". Then: "What does that
number mean on a line?" Expect: each part was cut into that many, or grouped by that many.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X15]]). No drawing given. Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; write results
on the record sheet under E15.

### Other rooms

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X15]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4 and the second Ask. Never drop the fixes or the check after each set, and never give a line
for the sets. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C15-1]]), then the extension on the board ([[s03-C15-2]]): the list is
the stop, since only bottoms that are 4 times a whole number work.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: tenth, hundredth, equal, multiply, divide, group. Frames on the display: "Each part was cut
into __, so I multiply both numbers by __." "I grouped the parts by __, so I divide both numbers by __." A
student who wants a line may sketch one on the back; the answer is still written.
