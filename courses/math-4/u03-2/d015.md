# Day 15: Equal fractions with no drawing

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print what the header lists. Sort the marked E14 tickets: 4/3 or 20/10 (A), 10/90 (B), 3 or no group size;
hand them back at the door. Display page on the projector, open at Example 1. Rule a long line from 0 to 2 on the
board in tenths, with room above it.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: four facts

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s03-P15-1v2]], [[s03-P15-2v2]], [[s03-P15-3v2]], [[s03-P15-4v2]]). Read the answers
aloud at 4 minutes.

**If day 14's tickets showed many A** (4/3, or 20/10): the facts are dropped. Write "20/100 = 20/10" beside the
board line. Count: the 20 hundredths reach 2/10; 20/10 is 2 wholes, far past the point. Then 4/12 = 4/3: 4/3 is
past 1, 4/12 is not.

**If day 14's tickets showed many B** (10/90: 10 taken from both): the facts are dropped. Draw 4/8 and 2/6 on two
strips, one above the other: taking away moves the point, grouping does not. Students who wrote 10/90 sit near
you for the sets.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: each tenth cut into 10

Students fill the Example 1 frame. Cut the first tenth into 10 on the
board and count them; the display shows every tenth cut.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K15-1]]). [[steps:s03-K15-1]]

From here a student may sketch a line to check; the work is written.

#### 4 min. Example 2: no drawing

Whole class. Students fill the Example 2 frame.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K15-2]]). [[steps:s03-K15-2]]

Leave on the board: "Find the number that connects the two. Do the same to the other number."

#### 9 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

Side 1 ([[s03-F15-1]], [[s03-F15-2]]). Two minutes alone on each, a minute in pairs, then the class. The display shows each wrong working beside the right one.

Kim read 50 and 100 as whole numbers (error D). Yesterday we grouped 50 hundredths into halves; today cut the
other way. On the board line, cut one tenth into 10: more parts, each
smaller. Five tenths hold 50 hundredths, and the point is at 1/2. Nia kept the top number (error A). Ask "how
many hundredths are in the one tenth now?"

If many still say 50/100 is more: cut a second tenth into 10 and count to 20 hundredths, then 50.

#### 22 min. Missing numbers: four sets of three

Alone, side 2. Each set is three blanks. After each set the display shows its key; students tick a right first
try and fix a miss in pen. The count of ticks stays with them. No clock. About 5
minutes a set, checking included.

Set 1 cuts the parts ([[s03-P15-5]], [[s03-P15-6]], [[s03-P15-7]]).

Check: The connecting number on both? Fix a miss in pen.

Set 2 groups them ([[s03-P15-8]], [[s03-P15-9]], [[s03-P15-10]]). Before it, ask the students whose E14 (a) had 3
or no group size: "How many in each group?"

Check: The connecting number on both? Fix a miss in pen.

Set 3 is tenths and hundredths ([[s03-P15-11]], [[s03-P15-12]], [[s03-P15-13]]).

Check: The connecting number on both? Fix a miss in pen.

Set 4 mixes all three ([[s03-P15-14]], [[s03-P15-15]], [[s03-P15-16]]).

Check: The connecting number on both? Fix a miss in pen.

Watch for a blank that copies the given number (error A: 7 for 7/5, 13 for 13/10). Ask "how many parts is each
one cut into?" Watch for the same number added or taken away (error B: 12 for 7/5, 11 for 16/10). Ask "what
times 5 is 10?"

If many make A in set 1: write the multiplier above the equal sign for set 2. If many make B: mark 7/5 and 12/10
on the board line: 7/5 is 14/10, two tenths past 12/10.

#### 6 min. Names for one fraction

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P15-17]]), then the class reads the row aloud.

**Ask.** "What repeats in the names for 7/2?" Expect: "the bottom goes up 2 each time and the top goes up 7";
"each top is 7 times as many as the halves". Show the first, then the second. If a student says "so adding works": only adding 7
on top and 2 below, one more 7/2 each time. Adding the same number to both moves the point (error B). If no one sees 350: half of 100
is 50, and 7 × 50 = 350.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "With no line, how do you find the missing number?" Expect: "find what one number was multiplied by,
then multiply the other"; "if the numbers got smaller, divide both by the same number". Then: "What does that
number mean on a line?" Expect: each part was cut into that many, or grouped by that many.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X15v2]]). Read it aloud once if asked. Collect at the door; write results
on the record sheet under E15.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write each set on the board; students work in notebooks, one set at a time.
- **A room with nothing.** Read each set's key aloud after the set.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 4 and the pull-together's second question. Never drop the fixes or the check after each set, and never give a line
for the sets. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C15-1]]), then the extension on the board ([[s03-C15-2v2]]): the list is
the stop, since only bottoms that are 4 times a whole number work (8, 12, 100).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: tenth, hundredth, equal, multiply, divide, group. Frames on the display: "Each part was cut
into __, so I multiply both numbers by __." "I grouped the parts by __, so I divide both numbers by __." A
student who wants a line may sketch one on the back; the answer is still written.

99 or 14 (error B): day 16 opens with 20/12 = 11/3: 11/3 is more than 3, 20/12 less than 2. A student whose ticket
shows A or B, or who made A or B in two sets, sits near you on day 16.
