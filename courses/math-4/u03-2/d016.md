# Day 16: Equal or not?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Display page on the projector. A long paper
strip and a marker for a line from 0 to 2.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: factor blanks

Alone, in notebooks, untimed: the three blanks on the display ([[s03-P16-1]]), the groupings the sort needs.
At 5 minutes one student reads the answers.

**If day 15's tickets showed many A** (20, 9 or 4: one number kept): the warm-up becomes "9/10 = 9/100" beside a
line from 0 to 1 in tenths. Cut the first tenth into 10: 9 hundredths sit inside the first tenth, far from 9/10.
The blanks move to the sort.

**If day 15's tickets showed many B** (11, 99 or 14): the warm-up becomes "20/12 = 11/3". 11/3 is more than 3;
20/12 is less than 2: adding moved the point. With both, B first; A takes 2 minutes of the sort.

Seat near you for the sort the students day 15 flagged (A or B in two sets) and any whose ticket showed A or B.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: Lena's claim

Whole class. Read Lena's claim aloud ([[s03-F16-1]]); students follow on side 1.

**Ask.** "What would you show Lena?" Take answers before showing the fix. Expect first: "6/4 is 3/2 and 9/6 is
3/2"; then "on a line from 0 to 2, both land halfway between 1 and 2". If no one finds a third name, ask:
"Can you give each one a name with smaller numbers?"

[[steps:s03-F16-1]]

After the talk, students fill 6/4 = __, 9/6 = __, both are __. Error E: a student looks for one multiplier,
finds none, and stops. The fix is the third name.

#### 8 min. Worked example: circle every equal fraction

Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go.

**Example** ([[s03-K16-1]]). [[steps:s03-K16-1]]

Say: "Check every one. Some equal names have bigger numbers, some have smaller." Watch for a student
who skips 11/5 (the rule used one way only), circles 24/12 (B) or 22/5 (A), or says 220/100 is more because its
numbers are bigger (D).

#### 22 min. Equal or not: eight pairs in three short sets

Alone, sides 1 and 2. Each set is marked at once against the display; students tick each pair right on the first try
and write the count. No clock. Two minutes are left for a branch or a reteach.

Set 1 ([[s03-P16-3]], [[s03-P16-4]], [[s03-P16-5]]), 6 minutes, then 1 to mark.

Check: Right on the first try? Tick it. If not, fix it beside your first try.

Set 2 ([[s03-P16-6]], [[s03-P16-7]], [[s03-P16-8]]), 6 minutes, then 1 to mark.

Check: Right on the first try? Tick it. Then add your ticks so far.

Set 3 ([[s03-P16-10]], [[s03-P16-9]]), 5 minutes, then 1 to mark.

Check: Right on the first try? Tick it. Write your count of 8.

Walk and ask: "Can you group both to smaller numbers? If the names differ, can you put them in the same parts?"
Pairs 2, 5, 6 and 7 need a third name. Watch for "equal" on 2/4 and 4/6 (B) or 9/4 and 9/8 (A), "not equal" on
7/10 and 70/100 (D) or a third-name pair (E). A sketch may explain, never find, the answer.

If many miss a third-name pair: do 14/4 and 21/6 together, Lena's way. If many call 15/10 and 20/12 equal: put
both in sixths, 9/6 and 10/6. If many stop at 6/4 and 9/6 on pair 7: group again.

#### 10 min. Written task: Mo's 2s

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-W16]]). Read it aloud once; a sketch is allowed. Collect it with the exit tickets.
Look for: each third cut into 2; twice as many parts in the whole and in the fraction; the point did not move.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Lena looked for one number to multiply by. What do you do when there is none?" Expect: "give both a
name with smaller numbers, then compare the names."

**Ask.** "How do you know two fractions are not equal?" Expect first: "in the same parts the counts differ: 3/6
and 4/6"; then "only one number changed."

#### 5 min. Exit: the ticket

Alone ([[s03-X16v2]]), read aloud once. Results go under E16 on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

- **No projector.** Write each set's answers on a covered part of the board and uncover them to mark. Lena's
  claim and the example go on the board, one step at a time.
- **No printer.** Write the eight pairs on the board, one set at a time. Students write "equal" or "not equal"
  and the names they found in notebooks. The written task goes on the board.
- **A room with nothing.** Lines ruled by hand; the key read aloud.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the page, and mark each set from the answers in this view.

**Skip.** The warm-up, set 3, the challenge and the count of ticks.

- **5 min. Lena's claim.** Read [[s03-F16-1]] together.

    **Say.** "What would you show Lena?" Expect: 6/4 is 3/2 and 9/6 is 3/2, so they are equal.

- **8 min. The example.** Read [[s03-K16-1]] together. The child checks each fraction aloud, then circles.
- **12 min. Equal or not.** [[s03-P16-3]], [[s03-P16-4]] and [[s03-P16-5]]. Mark these three, then do
  [[s03-P16-6]], [[s03-P16-7]] and [[s03-P16-8]].

    **Say.** When the child writes "not equal": "Can you group both to smaller numbers? If the names differ, can
    you put them in the same parts?" Expect: for 14/4 and 21/6, both are 7/2, so equal.

- **7 min. Mo's 2s.** The child says an answer to [[s03-W16]], then writes it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X16v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: set 3 becomes pair 7 alone, and the pull-together takes one question. Never drop Lena's claim, the
third-name pairs or the written task. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C16-2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: equal, group, numerator, denominator. Frames on the display: "I grouped the __ in __s, so __ =
__." "Both are __, so they are equal." "In the same parts they are __ and __, so they are not equal." Lena and the written
task are read aloud.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket: circles a name with the same number added (error B); circles a name with one number changed
(error A); leaves out an equal name (the rule used one way only); "not equal" in (b) (error E). If many show one,
day 17's teaching opens with it: A, "1/3 = 1/6" beside two strips; B, Jo's 3/4 = 5/6 on one line; E, Lena's 6/4
and 9/6 again, then in the same parts; a left-out name, one written with both numbers divided. Judge the written
tasks after class, each look-for alone; they go back in day 17's first five minutes with the "meets" answer. "10/6
is 2 times as big" (error F): day 17 marks 5/3 and 10/6 on one line, one point.
