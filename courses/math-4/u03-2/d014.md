# Day 14: Group the parts

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Mark the E13 tickets and sort them by error: A
(7/12, one number changed), B (10/7, 3 added to both), F ("3 times as big"). Fold a long strip in twelfths and
shade 8. Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: factors and multiples

Alone, then whole class: [[s03-W14-1]], [[s03-W14-2]], [[s03-W14-3]]. Read the answers aloud; students tick their
own. Say once: today a group size has to fit two numbers, so these facts come back.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: twelfths in twos, then sixths in twos

Whole class. Hand back the E13 tickets as you start.

**If day 13's tickets showed many A** (7/12): before Example 1, put "2/5 = 2/10" beside the line cut in tenths,
the point at 4/10. Count the shaded parts aloud: 4, not 2.

**If many B** (10/7): mark 2/5 and 4/7 on one line. Adding moved the point; cutting did not.

**If many F** ("3 times as big"): "4/10 is twice 2/5". Mark both on one line: one point.

A branch takes 2 minutes; take them from "On your own" (drop problem 3).

**Example 1** ([[s03-K14-1]]). Hold up the strip. Students circle the groups on the strip on side 1 and fill the
frame as you go. The display shows each step on its own screen.
[[steps:s03-K14-1]]

The number we divide by is the size of each group. It is the same on top and bottom, because the shaded parts
and the whole are grouped the same way.

#### 8 min. Example 2: hundredths in tens

Whole class. Hundredths are new: each tenth holds 10 hundredths, and day 15 shows the cut. Students circle on side
1 as you go.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K14-2]]). [[steps:s03-K14-2]]

Stop at the halves step. **Ask.** "How did we know each group needs 5 tenths?" Expect: halves means the whole is 2
equal groups, and 10 tenths in 2 groups is 5 each. This is the step the exit needs: from the new name, find the
group size.

Watch for 50/10: the groups in the whole were counted, the shaded ones were not (error A).

#### 10 min. Fix it: Lu and Max

Side 1 ([[s03-F14-1]], [[s03-F14-2]]). Two minutes alone, two minutes of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class:
the display shows each wrong working beside the right one.

Lu took 2 from both (error B). Mark 4/8 and 2/6 on one strip: taking away moved the point. Max kept the top
number (error A): count the shaded groups aloud, "one ten, two tens ... seven tens". 70/10 is 7 wholes, far past 1.

If many still subtract: grouping never takes parts away, it only circles them. If many keep a number: count the
shaded groups first, then the groups in the whole.

#### 14 min. On your own, then a partner check

Side 2, problems 1 to 3 ([[s03-P14-1]], [[s03-P14-2]], [[s03-P14-3]]). Ten minutes alone: circle the groups, write
the new name and the equation with ÷. Then four minutes in pairs.

Check: Does the shaded part end at the same point? Is every group the same size?

A mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer. Watch for 4/5 or 6/4 (top kept, A) and 2/8 or 4/6 (subtracted,
B). If many miss problem 3, do its fifths step together on the board: 10 tenths in 5 groups is 2 each.

#### 8 min. Which ones can be grouped?

Pairs, then whole class. Side 2 ([[s03-P14-4]]). Pairs try group sizes from 2 upward for each fraction. A group
cannot hold more parts than the shaded count, so the search stops at the top number.

**Ask.** "8/12 has a name with smaller numbers. Does 3/8? How can you tell?" Take "no, I tried" first, then the
reason. Expect: no; groups of 2 do not fit 3, and groups of 3 do not fit 8; a group size must fit both numbers,
and 3 and 8 share only 1. Link it to the warm-up: 8 has the factors 1, 2, 4 and 8.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Yesterday we cut each part into 2. Today we grouped parts in twos. What happened to the two numbers each
time?" Expect: cutting multiplies both by the same number; grouping divides both by the same number; the point
never moved.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X14]]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Write results under E14 on the record sheet;
until the record sheet exists, on a class list.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Draw each strip on the board; students rule strips of 12, 10 and 8 boxes on plain paper and
  shade them before circling.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the strips on side 1. No long strip: fold a paper strip in half, in half again,
then in three, and shade 8 parts.

**Skip.** The warm-up, problem 2 and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** [[s03-K14-1]], then [[s03-K14-2]]. The child circles the groups on the strip and fills
  the frame.

    **Say.** "To make halves, how many tenths go in each group?" Expect: 5, because 10 tenths in 2 groups is 5
    each.

- **8 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F14-1]], then [[s03-F14-2]].

    **Say.** "Lu took 2 from both. Is 2/6 the same amount as 4/8?" Expect: no, it is less; group the eighths in
    twos to get 2/4.

- **6 min. On your own.** [[s03-P14-1]] and [[s03-P14-3]]. The shaded part must end at the same point.
- **6 min. Which ones?** [[s03-P14-4]].

    **Say.** "Does 3/8 have a name with smaller numbers?" Expect: no; groups of 2 do not fit 3, and groups of 3 do
    not fit 8.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X14]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop problem 3 and the partner check (students check their own). Never drop the fixes or the halves step
of Example 2. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C14-1]]): the search ends when all five sixths are checked.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: group, equal groups, divide, equal, hundredth. Frames on the display: "I put the parts in groups
of __, so I divide both numbers by __." "To make __, the whole makes __ groups of __."

The section opener, when it exists, holds the math of grouping at an adult's level; until then this page holds
all the day needs.
