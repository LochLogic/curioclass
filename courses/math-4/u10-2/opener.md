# Unit 10.2: Comparison word problems: times as many or more than? (days 83 to 86)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.OA.A.2: "Multiply or divide to solve word problems involving multiplicative comparison, e.g., by
using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem, distinguishing
multiplicative comparison from additive comparison."

**The unknown can sit in three places** (the standards' glossary, Table 2). One story shows all three:

- **The larger amount.** "Lia has 7 marbles. Max has 8 times as many. How many does Max have?" m = 8 × 7, so 56.
  Multiply.
- **The smaller amount.** "Max has 56 marbles. That is 8 times as many as Lia has. How many does Lia have?"
  8 × l = 56, or l = 56 ÷ 8, so 7. Divide.
- **The number of times.** "Max has 56 marbles and Lia has 7. Max has how many times as many as Lia?" n × 7 = 56,
  or n = 56 ÷ 7, so 8. Divide.

**The word "times" does not tell you to multiply.** It is in all three stories, and two of them are solved by
dividing. A student who multiplies because the story says "times" (error B) gets 448 for Lia's marbles, more than
Max has. The question that catches it is the one asked every day of this unit: "Who has more?" The answer must fit
the longer strip.

**Two kinds of comparison.** "More than" is additive: the longer strip is the shorter one with a piece on its end.
"Times as many" is multiplicative: the longer strip is copies of the shorter. The same number does different work
in each. On day 83 the class watches the gap grow: with 8 to start, "3 more" gives 11 and "3 times as many" gives
24; with 1 to start, "3 more" (4) beats "3 times as many" (3). Students should see this in strips and numbers, not
memorise it.

**The letter.** From day 84 the letter goes on the unknown part of the strip before the equation is written, and
the student says what it stands for. 8 × l = 56 and l = 56 ÷ 8 say the same thing; either is right
(unknown-factor equations, 3.OA.A.4).

**"Times more than"** (error E, a first idea, not an error). Some people read "3 times more than 4" as 12 and
some as 16. Day 83 talks about it for five minutes; it is never asked or marked. Our problems say "times as many"
and "times as much".

**Limits.** One step. Dividing stays inside the one-digit facts; multiplying may also be a one-digit number times
a multiple of 10, or 10 times a one- or two-digit number. Whole numbers, no remainders.

## The routine

Write it on a card for each student, or once on the wall:

1. Read the story. Say it back in your own words.
2. Who has more? Draw two strips: the longer one for them.
3. Put a letter on the part you do not know.
4. Write the equation.
5. Solve it.
6. Answer in a sentence.
7. Read the story again with your answer in it. Does it fit?

## Why this model

The strip picture from unit 10.1 already holds both kinds of comparison, so a student draws first and decides
the operation from the picture, not from a word. Two strips one above the other, with the extra piece marked, is
also the picture for readiness item 2, so the reteach and day 83 start in the same place. Growth Spurt would add
two things print cannot: on day 83 (Growing, with the comparison switch) many cases seen in minutes, and on day 86
(Which one?) feedback on each strip. While it is a spec only, day 83 runs a table on the board and day 86 runs
sort cards, in the same minutes; each day's teacher page gives that paper path in full.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Read "times as many" as "more than" | Comparing still means adding on, as in grade 2 | Build or draw both, "more than" and "times as many"; say which is which |
| B | Multiply because the story says "times", when the smaller amount or the number of times is unknown | A key word is followed in place of the story | Before any numbers: "Who has more?" Mark the longer strip; the answer must fit it |
| C | Swap the roles: say the smaller amount is so many times as many as the larger | They lost track of which number is the multiplier | Point to the longer strip first; the bigger amount stands alone in the equation |
| E | Say "3 times more than 4" could be 12 or 16 | The phrase is unclear in everyday English | A talk, not a correction. Not marked |

Error D (copies added on top, unit 10.1) can come back on day 83; count the copies aloud, starting with the
first strip as copy one. The day each wrong example is shown: A and E on day 83, B on day 84, C on day 85. Each
day's teacher page names the wrong answer that signals each error on that day's exit.

## The reteach (readiness item 2)

Readiness item 2 (shells, "how many more") is the additive comparison this unit sets beside "times as many". If
more than a third of the class missed it, day 83's warm-up and first five minutes are this reteach for the whole
class. Otherwise run it with the students who missed it during day 83's warm-up, and the five minutes go to day
82's exit.

1. Draw two strips, one above the other, lined up at the left: Rosa's 13 shells on top, Ben's 9 under it.
2. Mark the part of Rosa's strip that sticks out past Ben's. Ask: "What does this piece show?" (How many more
   Rosa has.)
3. Write 9 + ? = 13, then 13 − 9 = 4. Answer in a sentence: "Rosa has 4 more shells than Ben."
4. Pairs draw 12 and 7 the same way, mark the piece, write both equations and answer in a sentence.

That picture, two strips and the piece that sticks out, is where day 83's teaching starts.

## Supplies for the unit

- Per student: strip sheets (about two a day on days 83 to 85) or paper strips; a ruler; a routine card.
- Per pair: one set of day 86's sort cards, printed from that day's file.
- Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board.
- Devices for days 83 and 86 if Growth Spurt is built; otherwise the paper paths.

## Words

As many as, as much as, more than, how many times, equation, letter, unknown. Each goes on the wall over a strip
picture. "That is 3 times as many as ..." is taught as a pattern on day 84. Short sentences, one fact in each, no
idioms. Students retell each story before drawing, in a home language if they wish. Read problems aloud, twice
if asked.

## How the unit is checked

Exits on days 83 to 86, on paper, written on the stretch record sheet by error letter (courses/math-4/u10-1/
record.md). Day 86's exit has two answers; a tick means both. Day 86's written task is judged by the teacher with
look-fors on that day's key. End check items 3 to 6 on day 88 are this unit's.
