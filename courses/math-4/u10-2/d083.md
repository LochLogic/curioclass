# Day 83: More than, or times as many?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the strip sheets, the exit tickets and the key. Have day 79's readiness results and the E82 tickets
in hand. Draw the Bo table on the board: "Bo has", "Cy has 3 more", "Cy has 3 times as many", four empty rows.
Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Alone. Two sentences from section 10.1 turned into equations ([[s10-W83-1]], [[s10-W83-2]]), and times tables.

**If more than a third missed readiness item 2:** the warm-up and the next part are the opener's reteach for the
whole class (10 minutes). E82's A example goes beside the strips in Teaching first; for many B, ask "who has
more?" about Kai's rope before the Fix it. Fewer: those students do it with you during the warm-up, marking the part
of the longer strip that sticks out.

#### 5 min. Readiness item 2, or day 82's exit back

Whole class. If many of day 82's tickets added, 2 + 7 = 9 (error A): A's wrong example
again, "8 times as many as 3 is 11", beside eight copies of the 3-strip. If many wrote 16 + 8 = 24 or 16 × 2 = 32
for the ropes (errors D and B): reread Kai's sentence and ask "who has more?" before any numbers. If neither, one student reads an
equation; the class says its sentence. Tickets with roles swapped (error C) go back with "Which is bigger?"
written on them; written tasks go back too, and 3 + 5 = 8 joins A's example.

#### 8 min. Teaching first: one story, told two ways

Whole class. Students draw in the boxes on side 1 as you go. Draw both strips on the board, one under the
other, and leave them up.

**Example 1** ([[s10-K83-1]]). [[steps:s10-K83-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s10-K83-2]]). [[steps:s10-K83-2]]

**Ask.** "In which story does the 2 tell you how many copies?" Expect: "the times story; in the more story the 2
is a short piece". The piece that sticks out is the readiness picture: that is what "more than" looks like.

#### 15 min. Growth Spurt, Growing: the Bo table on paper

**When Growing exists:** set both modes to 3, drag the bean to 1, 2,
4 and 10 cm, flip the switch at each, and students write both heights in the side 1 table; stop after the fourth
row. The Ask runs as below. Until then, the Bo table does the same job.

Alone, then whole class. Students write each row on side 1 before you fill it in, in this order:

- [[s10-T83-1]]
- [[s10-T83-5]]
- [[s10-T83-2]]
- [[s10-T83-4]]

For each row one student says an answer as a strip: "a piece of 3", or "3 copies".

Watch for the same number in both columns (error A), and for 8, 16 or 40 in the times column, Bo's strip then three
more copies (error D): count the copies aloud, Bo's strip as copy one.

**Ask.** "If Bo had 10 stamps, which gives Cy more: 3 more, or 3 times as many? What if Bo had 1?" Expect: "times
as many, 30 beats 13"; "at 1, 3 more wins". Then: "Which column grows faster?" The 3 more column keeps its gap of
3; the times column pulls away.

**Ask.** "Between which two rows does 3 times as many take the lead?" Expect: "between 1 and 2".

#### 12 min. Four pairs, each drawn both ways

Pairs. Side 2 ([[s10-P83-1]], [[s10-P83-2]], [[s10-P83-3]], [[s10-P83-4]]). Each partner draws all three strips for
every story on the strip sheet, then they compare. The strip comes before the number.

Ask as you walk: "Is the small number a piece, or a number of copies?" If many give both the same number: back to Ada and
Eli; point to the piece, then the copies.

#### 5 min. A phrase we will not use

A talk, not a correction (error E, a first idea). Say once, aloud: "Some people say '3 times more
than 4 dollars'. Is that 12 dollars, or 16?" Write only 12 and 16 on the board, never the phrase. Let both be
argued. "People mean different things, so we say
'times as many' or 'times as much'." If anyone wrote 16 for Bo's 4 stamps, this is
why: 16 is 3 times as many and the 4 again. "3 times as many as 4" is only ever 12. Nothing is marked.

#### 5 min. Fix it

Alone 2 minutes, then whole class ([[s10-F83-1]]). The display shows Lu's working beside the right working. The
10 is how many copies of Bo's strip, not a piece to add. If many miss it: draw "8 more than 3" beside "8 times as
many as 3".

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The ticket ([[s10-X83]]), read aloud once. Results go on the record sheet under E83.

### Other rooms

- **One projector.** Once the tool exists, you drag the bean; the class writes the next height on paper first.
- **One child at home.** Dried beans or buttons: you set out 4; the child sets out 3 more, then 3 times as many.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: pairs 1 and 3 only. Never cut the strips, the talk or the fix. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C83-1]]),
then the extension ([[s10-C83-2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards, each with its strip picture: more than, times as many, times as much, copies. A student may say
a comparison in a home language first. Frames on the display: "__ is __ more than __." "__ is __ times as many as
__."

for one story and pick the one the words say. Di 12, a 4-strip and then two more (error D): day 84 counts the
