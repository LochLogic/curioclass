# Day 84: Which amount is missing?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips, the blank strip sheets and the key. Mark the E83 slips and
sort them by error: A (both children the same, or swapped), D (an extra copy for Di). Put the display page on
the projector. Write the routine from side 1 on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: missing factors

Alone, then whole class. Division facts for the 6s, 7s and 8s, each written as a missing factor (8 × ? = ...),
and one sentence from unit 10.1 turned into an equation.

#### 3 min. E83 back

Whole class. Hand back the day 83 slips.

**If day 83's slips showed many A** (both children given the same number, or the two swapped): draw both strips
for Ty's cars, "2 more" and "2 times as many", and ask which one the words for Di say.

**If many D** (Di 12, an extra copy): count Di's copies aloud, starting with the first strip as copy one: "one,
two. Two times as many is two copies."

If neither: one student shows their Mo and Di strips and says which is which.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: one story, the unknown moved

Whole class. Students fill the two frames under the printed strips on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on
its own screen; draw the strips on the board as each step comes, and point to the routine.

**Example 1** ([[s10-K84-1]]). The larger amount is missing.
[[steps:s10-K84-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s10-K84-2]]). The same story, and now the smaller amount is missing.
[[steps:s10-K84-2]]

Put the two strip pictures side by side: the same strips, only the letter moved. Letter on the long strip:
multiply. Letter on the short strip: a missing factor, so divide. Teach the pattern "That is 8 times as much
as...": the number before "that is" belongs to the longer strip.

#### 25 min. Six problems: strips, letter, equation, sentence

Alone, side 2. The six problems are mixed: 1, 3 and 5 have the larger amount missing; 2, 4 and 6 the smaller. Each gets two strips on the
strip sheet with the letter on the unknown, an equation, and an answer sentence. "Who has more?" comes first: mark
the longer strip before any number.

Walk with one question: "Where is your letter: on the long strip or the short one?" Watch for:

- 252, 225 or 288: multiplied because the story says "times" (error B). Ask "who has more?" The answer must fit
  the shorter strip.
- 13, 17 or 57, or a difference such as 36: added or subtracted (error A). Ask for the strips first.
- 45, 80 or 400: one copy too many (error D). Count the copies aloud.

At 12 minutes, pairs compare the first three answers by rereading each story with the answer in it. If many
miss problem 2, stop and redraw Example 2 together; then they go on.

#### 7 min. Fix it: Lu's shells

Side 1 ([[s10-F84-1]]). Two minutes alone, two minutes of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class: the display
shows Lu's working beside the right working. Draw Jo's strip as 2 copies of Max's. Max's strip is the short one,
so 24 cannot be right. If many still multiply: before any numbers, "who has more?", and mark the longer strip.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "The problem says 'times'. Do we multiply? How do you know?" Expect: "not always"; "when the bigger
amount is missing we multiply, when the smaller is missing we divide"; "look at where the letter is on the
strips". Show Example 1's strips, then Example 2's; the word "times" is in both, and the letter is not in the same
place.

**Ask.** "How can you tell your answer fits?" Expect: reread the story with it; the bigger amount is on the longer
strip.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s10-X84]]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the
record sheet under E84.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** The six problems on the board, one at a time; strips on grid paper.
- **One child at home.** Tell the story with the child's own things: "You have 4 cars. I have 3 times as many."
  Then keep the story and hide a different number. Ask "who has more?" before any working.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: do problems 1 to 4 (two of each kind) and drop the pair comparison. Never drop the strips, the fix or the
answer sentence. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C84-1]]): the comparison comes before the number, so the strips must
be drawn before anything else.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, unknown, equation, letter. Students retell each problem,
in a home language if they wish, before drawing. Frames on the display: "That is __ times as many as __." "__
has more, so the longer strip is __." "The letter is on the __ strip, so I __."
