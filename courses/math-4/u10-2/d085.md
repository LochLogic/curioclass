# Day 85: How many times as many?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, the exit tickets and the key. Cut a few strip sheets into strips. Sort day 84's tickets:
right, 147, and 14 or 28. Write the warm-up problem on the board. Put the display page on the projector. Have one
index card per pair.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one grade 3 two-step problem

Alone, in notebooks, untimed: the crayon problem on the board ([[s10-P85-1]]). Pattern Painter (grade 3) may stand in when it exists. Watch for 24, the crayons taken (error F), and 376, worked left to right (error H).
At 5 minutes one student reads the equation and the class checks: 3 × 8 = 24, 50 − 24 = 26.

#### 3 min. Day 84's tickets back

Whole class, tickets handed back. Students whose ticket had no letter or no strips sit near you through the
teaching.

**If day 84's tickets showed many 147** (multiplied, error B) **or 14 or 28** (error A): before any numbers, ask "Who
has more, the big tank or the small one?" Draw the small tank's strip, then the big tank's as seven copies of it.
The answer must fit the short strip: 3 fish.

Otherwise, one student with a right ticket draws the two tank strips and says the equation.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: the number of times unknown

Whole class. Yesterday the routine found a missing amount. Today both amounts are given and the number of times
is missing. Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go.

**Example** ([[s10-K85-1v2]]). [[steps:s10-K85-1v2]]

The word says times; the strips say divide.

#### 18 min. Six problems: the unknown in all three places

Alone, then pairs for the last 3 minutes ([[s10-P85-2]], [[s10-P85-4]], [[s10-P85-6]], [[s10-P85-5]],
[[s10-P85-3]], [[s10-P85-7]]). Problems 1 to 3 are one of each kind; everyone finishes those. 4 to 6 as time
allows; problem 6 is drawn for all in the discussion.

Ask "Who has more?" before you look at any numbers. Watch for 252 or 648 on problems 2 and 4 (error B), a
difference such as 36 or 40 (error A), 64 or 99, one copy too many (error D): count the copies aloud, and a
sentence that gives the times to the smaller amount (error C). If many miss problems 2 and 4, stop and redraw the whale: the long strip is 72, so the dolphin's must be shorter.

At 15 minutes partners check each other's.
Check: Read the story again with your answer in it. Does it fit the strips?

#### 7 min. Discussion: two pictures, one equation

Whole class. Draw problem 6 ([[s10-P85-7]]) twice on the board. Left: the shed's 9-strip, and under it five
9-strips, every copy shown, bracketed and labelled 45. Right: the shed's 9-strip, and under it one long strip labelled 45, with "5 times"
over it. Write n × 9 = 45 under both, then n = 5.

**Ask.** "Where is the 5 in each picture? The 9? The 45?" Expect: "five boxes on the left"; "the label on the
right"; "the 9 is the shed".

**Ask.** "Which picture would you draw for 9 times? Why?" Expect: the long strip; nine copies take a while.

#### 8 min. Pairs write a problem

Pairs. Each pair writes one "how many times as many" problem on a card, with numbers from the times tables, and
solves it on the back. Pairs swap, solve with strips and an equation, then check against the back. A question
that gives the times away ("Ana has 3 times as many") is a larger-amount problem: ask them to hide the times.

#### 5 min. Fix it: who has more?

Pairs for one minute, then the class ([[s10-F85-1v2]]). Ask "Who has more? Whose strip is long?" The left picture is Ali's sentence drawn: Raj's strip as 8 copies
of Ann's 24. Raj has only 3 pens. Ann has more, so Ann has 8 times as many. The bigger amount stands alone in 24 = 8 × 3. If many still swap: everyone says problem
3's answer as a full sentence and points to the long strip.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "The question said 'how many times'. Did we multiply? How did you know?" Expect: "we divided, because
both amounts were given"; "we counted copies of the short strip". Take the first, then the second.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The exit ticket ([[s10-X85v2]]). Write the result on the record sheet under E85.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** The problems on the board two at a time; strips in notebooks.
- **One projector.** One problem at a time; the class holds up the equation before you show it.
- **Strips to move.** A student who cannot draw the copies lays paper strips.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No index card: any scrap of paper. Coins stand in for paper strips.

**Skip.** The warm-up, problems 4 and 5, and the challenge.

- **6 min. Count the groups.** [[s10-K85-1v2]]: lay out 4 coins, then 36; the child counts the groups of 4 and
  fills the frame on side 1.
- **12 min. Three problems.** [[s10-P85-2]], [[s10-P85-4]], [[s10-P85-6]]. Ask "Who has more?" before any
  number.

    **Say.** "Read the story again with your answer in it. Does it fit?" Expect: for the jars, 6 × 8 = 48, so
    6 times fits.

- **5 min. Two pictures.** Draw [[s10-P85-7]] twice: five 9-strips, then one long strip marked "5 times".
- **7 min. Fix it, then write one.** [[s10-F85-1v2]]. Then the child writes a "how many times as many" problem
  for you to solve.

    **Say.** "Who has more? Whose strip is long?" Expect: Ann; Ann has 8 times as many pens as Raj.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s10-X85v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the pairs part becomes 4 minutes, each pair solving its own card. Never drop the discussion, the fix or
the sentence answers. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C85-1]]), then a problem whose answer is "10 times".

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: times as many as, times as much as, times as long as, how many times. Frames on the display: "__
has __ times as many __ as __." "The long strip is __ copies of the short strip." Students retell each story
before drawing, in a home language if they wish.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, "Jon read 4 times as many" (error C): day 86 opens with it, says the answer as a full sentence and
tests it on the strips. 15 (error A) or 100 (error B): those students sit near you for day 86's sort, where each
card asks for the strip before the kind.
