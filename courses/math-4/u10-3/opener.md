# Section 10.3: Two-step problems: a letter for the unknown, an estimate to check (days 87 to 88)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.OA.A.3 (this unit's share): "Solve multistep word problems posed with whole numbers and having
whole-number answers using the four operations ... Represent these problems using equations with a letter
standing for the unknown quantity. Assess the reasonableness of answers using mental computation and estimation
strategies including rounding." Here that means two steps and no remainders. More steps, larger numbers and
remainders come in units 13 and 14.

**Two equations, or one.** "Ella saves 9 dollars. Her brother saves 4 times as much. How much do they save
together?" A student may write the steps as two equations, with the letter in the second: b = 4 × 9, so b = 36;
then s = 9 + 36, so s = 45. Or as one: s = 9 + 4 × 9. Both are right. Writing two first and then joining them is
how the section teaches it.

**The letter stands for the thing asked.** Before any numbers, the student says what the letter is, in words:
"s is all the dollars they save." A letter that stands for nothing named (error G) means the equation was written
after the answer, as decoration, not as the plan.

**Why 10 + 3 × 3 is 19, not 39.** When an equation has no parentheses, multiplying and dividing are done before
adding and subtracting. Grade 3 used this rule (3.OA.D.8) but it is often not firm, so working left to right
(error H) is common. Every problem in this section is chosen so that one equation needs no parentheses: the
multiplying or dividing step comes first. Parentheses as something to learn are grade 5.

**A good-enough estimate.** Round the story's numbers to easy ones and work with those: 9 is about 10, so about
10 + 4 × 10 = 50. The answer 45 is near 50, so it makes sense. An estimate only needs to be close enough to catch
an answer that is double, or ten times too big, or the answer to one step only (36 here). It is not a second exact
answer, and "45 is about 50" with no working from the story is not an estimate (4.NBT.A.3, rounding).

**Limits.** Two steps, whole-number answers, no remainders. The multiplying or dividing step is a one-digit fact
or a one-digit number times a multiple of 10. The adding or subtracting step stays within 1,000.

**One teaching day.** This section has day 87 to teach and day 88 to check. Three verbs in one period hold only
because grade 3 taught two-step problems with a letter and an estimate, the letter equation has been in daily use
since day 84, and unit 14 teaches the standard in full. Expect day 87 to feel full; cut practice problems, not
the estimate.

## Why this model

Paper does both key moments well. Two short equations written first, then joined into one for the same story,
show that one equation can hold both steps. Three finished answers, one far too big, judged by estimate before
anyone computes, show what an estimate is for. The strip picture is drawn when a step is a comparison; otherwise
a quick sketch of the story, with the letter written on it before the equation. Growth Spurt's Orders part has the
right shape but uses numbers outside this unit's rule, so the section runs on print.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | Stop after one step | They solved the part that was easy to see | Underline the question; after solving, read it again and ask "Is that what I found?" |
| G | Give an answer with no equation, or a letter that stands for nothing named | The equation is seen as extra, not as the plan | Say what the letter stands for, in words, before any numbers are written |
| H | Work a two-step equation left to right: 10 + 3 × 3 = 39 | Order of operations from grade 3 is not firm | Write the two steps as two equations for a week, then join them |

All three wrong examples are shown on day 87. Errors A and B can come back in a comparison step; the section 10.2
opener has their actions.

## The reteach (readiness item 3)

Readiness item 3 (Tom's books) is a grade 3 two-step problem with a letter. If more than a third of the class
missed it, day 87's warm-up and first five minutes are this reteach for the whole class. Otherwise run it with the
students who missed it during day 87's warm-up, and the five minutes go to day 86's exit. Days 85 and 86 have
already put one grade 3 two-step problem in everyone's warm-up.

1. Tell the story: "Rosa had 50 dollars. She bought 3 games for 8 dollars each. How much does she have left?"
2. Ask: "What do we need to find first?" (What the games cost.) Write g = 3 × 8, so g = 24.
3. Then: "Now what?" Write m = 50 − 24, so m = 26. Say what m is: the dollars Rosa has left.
4. Join them: m = 50 − 3 × 8. Multiply first: 50 − 24 = 26. Beside it, work it left to right: 50 − 3 = 47, then
   47 × 8 = 376. Ask: "Can Rosa have more money than she started with?"
5. Pairs: "Max had 70 dollars. He bought 6 tickets for 8 dollars each." Two equations, then one, each letter named.

## Day 88

The end-of-unit assessment, 30 minutes, unaided; strip pictures may be drawn. Then the respond half as in the course defaults:
mark 5 minutes, then groups by error, 15 minutes. A and D rebuild one comparison with strips; B and C sort three
problems by which amount is unknown; F to H do this section's "if many" action, joined by the students whose day 87
tickets, handed back when pencils go down, showed F, G or H. A student in two groups goes to the earlier one. Then the last
ten minutes are unit 11's readiness check. Items 7 and 8 are judged after class and handed back on day 89.

## Supplies for the section

- Per student: lined paper; strip sheets for any comparison step; a routine card (section 10.2).
- Per class: the projector for the display pages; a board.
- A room with nothing: problems on the board, one at a time.

## Words

In all, left, each, estimate, about, equation, letter. "Left" and "in all" are shown on a sketch of the story,
not taught as signals for an operation. Students retell the story and the question before writing. Read problems
aloud, twice if asked; two equations are always allowed.

## How the section is checked

Day 87's exit is judged by the teacher, with three look-fors (one each for parts f, g and h) and sample answers on
that day's key; a tick on the record sheet means all three. Day 87's written task is judged the same way. Unit
assessment items 7 and 8,
 on day 88, are this section's, each marked with three look-fors. Everything goes on the
