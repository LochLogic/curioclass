# Day 87: Two steps, one equation, one estimate

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets, a few strip sheets and the key. From the day 79 readiness
results, list who missed item 3. Sort the day 86 tickets by error. Put the display page on the projector; write
the warm-up on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: sentences, facts, one difference

Alone. Write as equations [[s10-P87-10]] and [[s10-P87-11]]; the 7s and 8s facts aloud with a partner; then
[[s10-P87-12]] with the standard algorithm.

**If the readiness check showed gaps on item 3** (many missed it): the warm-up and the next five minutes go to
the section opener's reteach, whole class: each step as its own equation, then joined. The day 86 tickets go back with the E87 tickets on
day 88: melon 96 joins the B and C group, melon 20 or squash 96 the A and D group. If fewer missed it, they do the reteach with you as a small group during the warm-up.

#### 5 min. Day 86's exit back

Whole class. **If day 86's tickets showed many B** (melon 96) **or many A** (melon 20, or squash 96; error A): redraw
the pumpkin as 4 copies of the melon, then the squash as the pumpkin plus a piece of 4. Otherwise one student shows the melon strip. Hand back the two-stories task; read the meets sample aloud
and ask which words make the 2 mean copies. Both stories one kind (error A) join the redraw.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: one story, two equations, then one

Whole class. Students fill the example frames on side 1 as you go. Draw the strips on the
board (the display shows the same picture): one strip for Ben, four copies for Amy, 135 marked off, p on the rest.

**Example** ([[s10-K87-1]]). [[steps:s10-K87-1]]

Two short equations are a right answer too. A good estimate uses easy numbers from the story, not the answer, and only needs to catch
an answer double or ten times off.

**Ask.** "Could the answer be more than 1,000? How do you know without working it out?" Expect: "the book has
only 280 pages". Then "What does your letter stand for?"

#### 17 min. Two steps, six problems

Alone ([[s10-P87-1]], [[s10-P87-2v2]], [[s10-P87-3]], [[s10-P87-4]], [[s10-P87-5]], [[s10-P87-6]]). For each:
name the letter in words, one equation, solve, estimate. A strip only when stuck. Most will finish four; 5 and 6 are
for those who finish. Visit first the students who drew most strips on day 86. In the last 2 minutes the display
shows the answers.

Check: Compare with yours. A mismatch gets a question mark, not a fix.

Watch for the first step only (300 stamps, 7 feet, 42 hens: error F), a letter with no words (G), and 463 − 7 =
456 written first on problem 6 (H). On problem 2 ask "who is taller, the tower or the fence?" (615 is error B). Ask as you walk:
"Is that the answer to the question that was asked?"

If many stop at one step: underline the question together and reread it after solving. If many skip the
estimate: problem 4's aloud, 400 − 290.

#### 5 min. Judge it by estimate

Whole class. Show one finished answer at a time ([[s10-P87-7]], [[s10-P87-8]], [[s10-P87-9]]). Read each aloud as it
shows. Students estimate in their heads and write "makes sense" or "far off" on side 2 before anyone computes.

**Ask.** "How did you know 847 was wrong before working it out?" Expect: "5 days of 80 is 400, and 47 is about
50, so about 450".

#### 6 min. Fix three

Pairs for a minute each, then the class; the display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Students
write what went wrong on side 2.

- [[s10-F87-1]] (F). **Ask.** "Is 80 the answer to the question that was asked?" Then "Which part of your
  equation is Omar's money?" Expect: 2 × 40.
- [[s10-F87-2]] (G). Say what s stands for before writing it.
- [[s10-F87-3]] (H). Multiply before adding: 10 + 9, not 13 × 3.

#### 5 min. Written task: Mia's estimate

Alone, 3 minutes, side 2 ([[s10-W87]]), read aloud once. Then 2 minutes, whole class: two students read theirs;
the class says whether each says the two numbers disagree and what to check. Pages are not collected.

#### 7 min. Exit ticket

Alone, read aloud once ([[s10-X87v3]]). Judged after class, one look-for each for parts f, g and
h, marked on the record sheet under E87.

### Other rooms

- **No projector.** Write each judged answer and each wrong working on the board, one at a time.
- **One projector.** One problem on the screen at a time; the class holds up equations.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: read the answers from the key.

**Skip.** The warm-up, problems 2, 3 and 5, the three to judge, and two of the fixes.

- **8 min. One story, one equation.** [[s10-K87-1]] together: Ben's strip, 4 copies for Amy, 135 marked off,
  p on the rest.

    **Say.** "Could the answer be more than 1,000? How do you know?" Expect: the book has only 280 pages.

- **12 min. Three problems.** [[s10-P87-1]], [[s10-P87-4]], [[s10-P87-6]]. Before each, the child tells the
  story back, says what the letter stands for and guesses about how many. Then one equation, solve, estimate.
- **5 min. Fix one.** Read it aloud: Lena has 40 dollars and Omar has 2 times as much; Pia says they have 80
  dollars together.

    **Say.** "Is 80 the answer to the question that was asked?" Expect: no, that is Omar alone; together
    40 + 2 × 40 = 120.

- **4 min. Mia's estimate.** [[s10-W87]], written alone, then read aloud to you.

    **Say.** "What should Mia do next?" Expect: 12 and 120 are far apart, so she checks her working and her
    estimate again.

- **7 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s10-X87v3]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: do problems 1, 2, 4 and 6 only, and talk through one fix instead of three. Never drop the estimates,
the judge-it round or the named letter. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C87-1]]), judged by its look-fors.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: estimate, about, in all, left, each, equation. Show "left" and "in all" on a sketch, not as
signals for an operation. Students retell the story and question first, in a home language if they wish. Frames:
"My letter __ stands for __." "My estimate is about __, so __ makes sense."

### Tomorrow

Day 88 is the end-of-unit assessment. Hand the E87 tickets back when day 88's marking starts, after pencils go
down; students showing these signs join the F to H group in the respond half: 90 (error F); a working that
starts 158 − 3 = 155 (error H); 248 (added the 90); 68 with no letter, or a letter never named (error G); no estimate, or "68 is about 70" with nothing from the story
(part h).
