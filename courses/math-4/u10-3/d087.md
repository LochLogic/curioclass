# Day 87: Two steps, one equation, one estimate

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips, a few strip sheets and the key. From the day 79 readiness
results, list who missed item 3. Sort the day 86 slips by error. Put the display page on the projector; write
the warm-up on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: sentences, facts, one difference

Alone. Write as equations "45 is 5 times as many as 9" and "6 times as many as 8 is 48"; the 7s and 8s facts
aloud with a partner; then 503 − 268 with the standard algorithm.

**If the readiness check showed gaps on item 3** (many missed it): the warm-up and the next five minutes go to
the unit opener's reteach, whole class: each step as its own equation, then joined. The day 86 slips go to day
88's groups. If fewer missed it, they do the reteach with you as a small group during the warm-up.

#### 5 min. Day 86's exit back

Whole class. **If day 86's slips showed many B** (melon 96) **or many A** (melon 20, or squash 24): redraw the
pumpkin as 4 copies of the melon, then the squash as the melon plus a piece of 4. Ask "who has more?" first.
Otherwise one student shows the melon strip and says the two steps: today's idea.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: one story, two equations, then one

Whole class. Students fill the example frames on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. Draw the strips on the
board (the display shows the same strip picture): one strip for Ben, four copies for Amy, 135 marked off, p on the rest.

**Example** ([[s10-K87-1]]). [[steps:s10-K87-1]]

Two short equations are a right answer too. Today's problems all multiply or divide first, so one equation
needs no parentheses. A good estimate uses easy numbers from the story, not the answer, and only needs to catch
an answer double or ten times off.

**Ask.** "Could the answer be more than 1,000? How do you know without working it out?" Expect: "the book has
only 280 pages". Then "What does your letter stand for?"

#### 17 min. Two steps, six problems

Alone ([[s10-P87-1]], [[s10-P87-2v2]], [[s10-P87-3]], [[s10-P87-4]], [[s10-P87-5]], [[s10-P87-6]]). For each:
name the letter in words, one equation, solve, estimate. A strip only when stuck. In the last 2 minutes the
display shows the answers; a mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer.

Watch for the first step only (300 stamps, 7 feet, 42 hens: error F), a letter with no words (G), and 18,240
on problem 6 (H). On problem 2 ask "who is taller, the tower or the fence?" (615 is error B). Ask as you walk:
"Is that the answer to the question that was asked?"

If many stop at one step: underline the question together and reread it after solving. If many skip the
estimate: problem 4's aloud, 400 − 290.

#### 5 min. Judge it by estimate

Whole class. Show one finished answer at a time ([[s10-P87-7]], [[s10-P87-8]], [[s10-P87-9]]). Students
estimate in their heads and write "makes sense" or "far off" on side 2 before anyone computes.

**Ask.** "How did you know 847 was wrong before working it out?" Expect: "5 days of 80 is 400, and 47 is about
50, so about 450". Ivy's 69 shows an answer can be far too small too.

#### 6 min. Fix three

Pairs for a minute each, then the class; the display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Students
write what went wrong on side 2.

- [[s10-F87-1]] (F). **Ask.** "Is 80 the answer to the question that was asked?" Then "Which part of your
  equation is Omar's money?" Expect: 2 × 40.
- [[s10-F87-2]] (G). The number is right; the plan is missing. Say what s stands for before writing it.
- [[s10-F87-3]] (H). Multiply before adding: 10 + 9, not 13 × 3.

#### 5 min. Written task: Mia's estimate

Alone, side 2 ([[s10-W87]]), read aloud once. Collect the pages; judge it after class by its look-fors.

#### 7 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, read aloud once, twice if asked ([[s10-X87v2]]). Judged after class, one look-for each for parts f, g and
h, marked on the record sheet under E87.

### Other rooms

- **No devices.** Nothing changes: today runs on paper.
- **One projector.** One problem on the screen at a time; the class holds up equations. Put two different right
  ones side by side: how does each match the story?
- **One child at home.** Problems 1, 4 and 6 are enough. Before each, the child tells the story back, names the
  letter and guesses "about how many"; compare the guess with the answer.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: do problems 1, 2, 4 and 6 only, and talk through one fix instead of three. Never drop the estimates,
the judge-it round or the named letter. Longer: the challenge ([[s10-C87-1]]), judged by its look-fors.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: estimate, about, in all, left, each, equation. Show "left" and "in all" on a sketch, not as
signals for an operation. Students retell the story and question first, in a home language if they wish. Frames:
"My letter __ stands for __." "My estimate is about __, so __ makes sense." Problems are read aloud on request.

### Tomorrow

Day 88 is the end check. Hand the E87 slips back at the door; students showing these signs join the F to H
group in the respond half: 90 (error F); 4,650, worked left to right as (158 − 3) × 30 (error H); 248 (added the
90); 68 with no letter, or a letter never named (G); no estimate, or "68 is about 70" with nothing from the story
(part h).
