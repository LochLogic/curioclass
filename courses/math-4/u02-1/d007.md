# Day 7: Follow the rule, then notice more

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Put the display page on the projector. Write the two tapes on a covered part
of the board: "Start at 1. Add 3. 1, 4, 7" and "Start at 3. Add 5. 3, 8, 13". Beside them, covered, day 6's two
"meets" answers: "99 = 9 × 11, so 99 is not prime" and "Maya may stop: 7 goes with 5, and she has 5 × 7". Have
the readiness results and day 6's marked assessments beside you.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: factor pairs and multiples

Alone: [[s02-W7-1]], [[s02-W7-2]], [[s02-W7-3]]. At 4 minutes, show the answers.

Check: Is yours the same? Fix it in pen.

**If the readiness check showed gaps:** the reteach is day 8's warm-up and first five minutes. Today, a miss on
item 3 gets counters to keep; a miss on item 1, watch on the first jump; examples on item 2, ask first in the Lena
Ask.

#### 5 min. Unit 1's items 5 and 6 back

Alone. Hand back the day 6 assessments. Uncover the "meets" answers to items 5 and 6 on the board. Students fix
their own in pen beside the first try. Collect them again for the record.

#### 12 min. Teaching first: start at 3, add 4, with dot pairs

Whole class. You draw dots on the board; pairs build each number with counters. Students fill the frame for
Example 1 on side 1.

**Example 1** ([[s02-K7-1]]). [[steps:s02-K7-1]]

**Ask.** After step 4: "The rule says add 4. Did it say anything about odd numbers? So why is every number odd?"
Expect: "no"; then "3 is odd, and 4 is two pairs".

Then the wrong example, Fix it 1 ([[s02-F7-1]]), whole class. [[steps:s02-F7-1]] The start is a number in the
list, not a number in the rule. If many wrote 4 first in the frame: five rules on the board (start 5, add 2; start 7,
add 10; start 4, add 6; start 10, add 3; start 6, add 4), circle each start, write it first, then jump.

#### 10 min. Rule Machine, on paper: two tapes

Alone, on the recording page, patterns 1 and 2 ([[s02-T7-1]], [[s02-T7-2]]). When it exists: Rule Machine,
number tapes 1 and 5, practise with feedback. Until then, uncover tape 1 on the board. Students copy the start,
the rule and the three numbers, write the next five, mark one feature and write why. At 5 minutes show the full list; each
checks their own numbers in pen. Then tape 5 the same way.

Watch for the rule given as the feature or the reason (error C) and more numbers as the reason (error E): the
next fixes take both. On tape 5, a student who saw only odd and even is right; ask "what do the ones digits
do?"

**Stop at 10 minutes.**

#### 23 min. By hand: two fixes, then two rules

**Fix it, 8 minutes.** Side 2 ([[s02-F7-2]], [[s02-F7-3]]). Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then
the class.

For Theo: [[steps:s02-F7-2]] If many named the rule as the feature: cover the rule on the board and list what is
true of the numbers themselves.

For Lena: **Ask.** "Lena showed me three more numbers. How do you know about the ones she has not written?"
Expect: "a jump of 3 is a pair and one more, so it turns odd to even"; "every jump adds 3" alone is the rule: ask
"and what does that do to the one left over?" If many give numbers: dot pairs for one jump, then finish "It must
keep going because __" together.

[[steps:s02-F7-3]]

**On your own, 12 minutes.** Side 2 ([[s02-P7-1]], [[s02-P7-2]]), alone, on the recording page, patterns 3 and 4.
Eight numbers each before anything is written about them. Watch for 3, 6, 9 on the first (error A), and for 3, 5,
7, 9 on the second (added 2): ask "what does multiply by 2 do to 3?" Past 100, double by adding the number to
itself: 96 + 96. "Take turns" on pattern 4, from 3 and 6 alone, is error D: ask "and 12?"

**Partner check, 3 minutes.** Partners swap recording pages. Each reads the other's reason and asks "does it say
what one jump does to the feature?" A reason that is only more numbers, or the rule, gets a question mark.

Check: Does the reason say what one jump does to the feature?

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s02-X7v2]]). Read it aloud once. A tick needs the six numbers, a feature that is not the rule,
and a reason that says what one jump of 6 does to that feature. Collect the tickets and the recording pages; both
come back on day 8. Record under E7. Send the family note home.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write the tapes and the two rules on the board; students rule eight boxes in a notebook.

### At home

**Swap.** No counters: coins or dried beans, laid in pairs. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, items 5 and 6, tape 5, and the challenge.

- **10 min. The example.** Read [[s02-K7-1]] together, with coins in pairs for 3 and 7. The child fills the frame.
  Then [[s02-F7-1]].

    **Say.** "The rule says add 4. Did it say anything about odd numbers? So why is every number odd?" Expect: no;
    3 is odd, and 4 is two pairs, so the one left over stays alone.

- **7 min. Tape 1.** [[s02-T7-1]]: write 1, 4, 7 on the recording page; the child writes the next five, marks one
  feature and writes why.
- **6 min. Fix two.** [[s02-F7-2]], then [[s02-F7-3]].

    **Say.** "Lena showed three more numbers. How do you know about the ones not written?" Expect: a jump of 3
    is a pair and one more, so it turns odd to even.

- **10 min. On your own.** [[s02-P7-1]], then [[s02-P7-2]]: eight numbers first, then a feature and why.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s02-X7v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop tape 5 and the challenge. Never drop the two fixes or the reasons; every pattern today ends with
"why will it keep going". Longer: the challenge ([[s02-C7-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: rule, pattern, term, odd, even, take turns. Say "feature" as "something true about the numbers".
Frames, on side 1: "I notice __." "It will keep going because __." Access: large counters; a student who cannot
draw directs a partner; reasons may be said aloud to you or a scribe.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, 6 or 35 first (error A): these students build step 1 first on day 8. "It adds 6" or "because it
adds 6" (error C), "take turns" (error D), or more numbers as the reason (error E): day 8's first five minutes go
to these tickets, unless many need the readiness reteach: list what is true of 29, 35, 41, then dot pairs for one
jump of 6. Day 8 uses side 2 of the recording page, and its wrong example D sits on today's 3, 6, 12, 24.
