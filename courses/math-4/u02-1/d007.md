# Day 7: Follow the rule, then notice more

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Put the display page on the projector. Write the two tapes on a covered part
of the board: "Start at 1. Add 3. 1, 4, 7" and "Start at 3. Add 5. 3, 8, 13". Have the readiness results on unit
2's record sheet beside you, and day 6's marked items 5 and 6 ready to hand back.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: factor pairs and multiples

Alone, then the answers on the display: [[s02-W7-1]], [[s02-W7-2]], [[s02-W7-3]]. Students below the facts mark
keep the 9 by 9 table out.

**If the readiness check showed gaps:** nothing moves today; the reteach is day 8's warm-up and first five
minutes. Students who missed item 3 get counters now and keep them. Students who missed item 1 are the ones to
watch on the first jump. Students who gave examples on item 2 get the second planned question.

#### 5 min. Unit 1's items 5 and 6 back

Alone. Hand back the day 6 assessments. Show the "meets" answer of item 5, then of item 6, from the key. Students
fix their own in pen beside the first try. Collect them again for the record.

#### 12 min. Teaching first: start at 3, add 4, with dot pairs

Whole class. You draw dots under the camera; pairs build each number with counters. Students fill the frame for
Example 1 on side 1.

**Example 1** ([[s02-K7-1]]). [[steps:s02-K7-1]]

**Ask** (after step 4). "The rule says add 4. Did it say anything about odd numbers? So how did they get there?"
Expect: "no"; then "3 is odd, and 4 is two pairs". Take "no" first, then the pairs.

Then the wrong example, Fix it 1 ([[s02-F7-1]]), whole class. [[steps:s02-F7-1]] The start is a number in the
list, not a number in the rule. If many wrote 4 first in the frame: five rules on the board, circle each start,
write it first, then jump.

#### 10 min. Rule Machine, on paper: two tapes

Alone, on the recording page, patterns 1 and 2 ([[s02-T7-1]], [[s02-T7-2]]). When it exists: Rule Machine,
number tapes 1 and 5, practise with feedback. Until then, uncover tape 1 on the board. Students copy the start, the rule and
the three numbers, write the next five, mark one feature and write why. At 5 minutes show the full list; each
checks their own numbers in pen. Then tape 5 the same way.

Watch for the rule given as the feature (error C) and more numbers as the reason (error E): the next fixes
take both. On tape 5, a student who saw only odd and even is right; ask "what do the ones digits
do?"

**Stop at 10 minutes.**

#### 23 min. By hand: two fixes, then two rules

**Fix it, 8 minutes.** Side 2 ([[s02-F7-2]], [[s02-F7-3]]). Pairs, two minutes each to say what went wrong, then
the class. The display shows each working beside the right one.

For Theo: [[steps:s02-F7-2]] If many named the rule as the feature: cover the rule on the board and list what is
true of the numbers themselves.

For Lena: [[steps:s02-F7-3]]

**Ask.** "Lena showed me three more numbers. How do you know about the ones she has not written?" Expect: "every
jump adds 3, a pair and one more"; take any answer about the jump, not about more numbers. If many give numbers:
dot pairs for one jump, then finish "It must keep going because __" together.

**On your own, 12 minutes.** Side 2 ([[s02-P7-1]], [[s02-P7-2]]), alone, on the recording page, patterns 3 and 4.
Counters stay on the desk. Eight numbers each before anything is written about them. Watch for 3, 6, 9 on the
first (error A), and for 3, 5, 7, 9 on the second: that student added 2. Ask "what does multiply by 2 do to 3?"

**Partner check, 3 minutes.** Partners swap recording pages. Each reads the other's reason and asks "does it say
what one jump does?" A reason that is only more numbers gets a question mark.

Check: Does the reason say what every jump does?

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s02-X7v2]]). Read it aloud once. A tick needs the six numbers, a feature that is not the rule,
and a reason about the jump. Record results on the record sheet under E7. Send the family note home.

### Other rooms

- **No counters.** Dots drawn in pairs, two rows, in pencil; a short row shows the one left over.
- **No printer.** Write the tapes and the two rules on the board; students rule eight boxes in a notebook.

### At home

**Swap.** No counters: coins or dried beans, laid in pairs. No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, items 5 and 6, tape 5, and the challenge.

- **10 min. The example.** Read [[s02-K7-1]] together, with coins in pairs for 3 and 7. The child fills the frame.
  Then [[s02-F7-1]].

    **Say.** "The rule says add 4. Did it say anything about odd numbers? So how did they get there?" Expect: no;
    3 is odd, and 4 is two pairs, so the one left over stays alone.

- **7 min. Tape 1.** [[s02-T7-1]]: write 1, 4, 7 on the recording page; the child writes the next five, marks one
  feature and writes why.
- **6 min. Fix two.** [[s02-F7-2]], then [[s02-F7-3]].

    **Say.** "Lena showed three more numbers. How do you know about the ones not written?" Expect: every jump
    adds 3, a pair and one more.

- **10 min. On your own.** [[s02-P7-1]], then [[s02-P7-2]]: eight numbers first, then a feature and why.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s02-X7v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop tape 5 and the challenge. Never drop the two fixes or the reasons; every pattern today ends with
"why will it keep going". Longer: the challenge ([[s02-C7-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: rule, pattern, term, odd, even, take turns. Say "feature" as "something true about the numbers".
Frames, on side 1: "I notice __." "It will keep going because __." Access: large counters; a student who cannot
draw directs a partner; reasons may be said aloud to you or a scribe.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, 6 or 35 first (error A): these students build step 1 first on day 8 and write its count before
any jump. "It adds 6" (error C), or more numbers as the reason, such as 65 or "it did it for 59" (error E): day 8's
first five minutes, when the readiness reteach is not needed by many, go to these tickets: cover the rule and
list what is true of 29, 35, 41, then dot pairs for one jump of 6. Keep the recording pages: day 8 uses side 2,
and its wrong example D sits on today's doubling list, 3, 6, 12, 24.
