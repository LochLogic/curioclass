# Section 2.1: Follow the rule, then notice more (days 7 to 9)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.OA.C.5: "Generate a number or shape pattern that follows a given rule. Identify apparent features of
the pattern that were not explicit in the rule itself." Students are given a start and a rule, write the terms,
and look for something true of the numbers that the rule never said. Then they explain, informally, why it will
keep going. The rule is always given: grade 4 never asks students to find it, and the Progressions say the
standards "do not require students to infer or guess the underlying rule".

**Why odd plus even is odd.** An odd number is pairs and one left over. Add an even number, which is all pairs,
and the one left over is still alone: odd. Add an odd number and the two leftovers make a new pair: even. So
"start at 1, add 3" gives odd, even, odd, even for ever, and "start at 13, add 6" stays odd for ever. The reason
is one jump, not the list.

**Why adding 5 makes the ones digits take turns.** Two jumps of 5 are one jump of 10, and adding 10 leaves the
ones digit alone. So the ones digit comes back every second term: 6, 11, 16, 21 go 6, 1, 6, 1.

**Why three more examples are not a reason.** Two more terms say nothing about the hundredth. A reason says why every jump does the same thing. This is the line between part (c) and part (d), and
error E lives on it.

**A shape pattern is a number pattern.** Count each step's squares or sticks and write the counts as a list. A
later step then comes from the first count and the number of jumps: stick squares go 4, 7, 10, so the 10th is 4
and 9 more threes, 31. Drawing all ten is error B.

**Limits.** Rules add or multiply by a whole number. Terms stay under 1,000, and the farthest step asked is the
10th. No rule written with a letter, no two patterns compared, no "100th shape" (unit 13).

## Dots, squares and the list

- **Day 7.** Dot pairs under the camera, then on paper: each term drawn as pairs and one left over, tied that day
  to the sentence "odd plus even is odd". The list is numbered, and each jump is drawn as an arrow between
  neighbours.
- **Day 8.** Paper squares and sticks in pairs: build steps 1 to 3, write each count under its step, then find
  the 10th from the list.

**The tool minutes, on paper.** When it exists: Rule Machine, number tapes 1 and 5 (day 7) and the shape tapes
(day 8). Until then, write the tape on the board: day 7, "start at 1, add 3" and "start at 3, add 5"; day 8,
two of "start at 3 tiles, add 2", "start at 1 tile, add 3", "start at 4 tiles, add 4" and "start at 2 tiles, add 5".
Give three terms or three steps. Students write the next five in a frame on the recording page, mark one feature
and write why. Then show the full list; each checks their own.

## Why this model

Dot pairs show even and odd in the hand: the one left over can be pointed at. Squares and sticks make a shape
step into a count, and the numbered list carries both. Rule Machine (spec only) would give feedback on terms,
but noticing and explaining are judged by a teacher. The section runs on print.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Leave out the start, or start at the number in the rule: "start at 3, add 4" written 4, 8, 12 | The start is not seen as the first term | Five rules on the board: circle each start, write it first, then jump |
| B | Draw or count every step to reach a later one | The count is not yet tied to the jump | Say step 10 in words first: "the first shape and how many jumps?" |
| C | Give the rule back as the feature: "it adds 3" | "Notice" is heard as "say what you did" | Cover the rule; list what is true of the numbers themselves |
| D | Name a feature from two or three terms | A glance, not a check of the list | Write eight terms before saying anything |
| E | Give more terms as the reason | An example is taken for a reason | Dot pairs for one jump; finish "It must keep going because __" |

Wrong examples: A, C and E on day 7; B and D on day 8. Each day's teacher page names the wrong answer that
signals each error on its exit ticket. A, C, D and E rest on our memory of classrooms; B is from the open course.

## The reteach (readiness items 1 to 3)

The readiness check (readiness.md) is given on day 6 and marked before day 7. If more than a third of the class
missed an item, day 8's warm-up and first five minutes are its reteach for the whole class. Otherwise run it with
the students who missed it during day 8's warm-up, and the five minutes go to day 7's exit ticket.

1. **Items 1 and 2, jumping and a reason.** Skip count by 6 on the 1 to 100 chart, circling each landing. Ask
   "what is true of every number you landed on?" Then four rows of dots, put in pairs: none is left over, so 4
   times a number is even.
2. **Item 3, even and odd.** Counters put in pairs: none left over is even, one left over is odd. Sort 8, 11, 14
   and 15 this way.

## Supplies for the section

- Per pair: about 30 counters or drawn dots, about 30 paper squares, about 20 sticks (toothpicks, straws or pencil
  marks).
- Per student: the recording page (recording-page.md), printed on both sides, for days 7 and 8; a 1 to 100 chart
  (the unit 1 chart, chart-100.md: its crossing-out line is day 6's, so cover it or ignore it); a pencil.
- Per class: the projector for the display pages and the tape lists; a board.
- A room with nothing: dots drawn in pairs, squares torn from scrap paper, sticks drawn as pencil marks.

## Words

Rule, pattern, term, step, odd, even, take turns, each on a card on the wall. "Feature" is hard: say "something
true about the numbers". Frames, to take or leave: "I notice \_\_\_." "It will keep going because \_\_\_."

## How the unit is checked

Readiness check, day 6 (readiness.md); exit tickets on days 7 and 8, on paper; day 8's written task, judged with
two look-fors on its key; the end-of-unit assessment on day 9, where items 3 and 5 are judged with look-fors. All
of it goes on one record sheet for the unit (record.md), by error letter. The family note (family.md) goes home on
day 7.
