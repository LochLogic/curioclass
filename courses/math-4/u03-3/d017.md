# Day 17: Half of what?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Cut your two strips, 10 inches and 6 inches.
Cut about 7 sheets into long strips and 5 into short ones. Draw a line from 0
to 2 on the board with 1/2 and 1 marked, long enough to write under. Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: a name for 1/2

Alone: [[s03-P17-1]]. Hand back day 16's written task as they work, and read one "meets" answer aloud. If many
said 10/6 is 2 times as big (error F), mark 5/3 and 10/6 on the board line: one point. Then ask
for other names for 1/2 and write them on the board: 2/4, 3/6, 4/8, 5/10, 6/12, 50/100. Leave them up all day.
A student who wrote 7 added 5 to both numbers (error B): ask "6 is how many times 1?"

#### 5 min. Question aloud: two pizzas

Whole class. **Ask.** "Is half of a small pizza equal to half of a large one?" Listen; do not settle it yet. Note
who says "the same, both are a half" and who says "the big half is more food".

#### 14 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Hand out the strips first; students fold their own as you fold yours, and fill the frames on side 1.
The display shows each step on its own screen.

**If day 16's tickets showed many A, B or E:** that error's wrong example from the error register opens with its
"if many" action ("1/3 = 1/6" beside two strips for A; Jo's 3/4 = 5/6 on one line for B; Lena's 6/4
and 9/6 each given a name with smaller numbers for E). Many who left out 4/3: write 8/6 = 4/3 with both numbers
divided by 2. Either takes its 3 minutes from the sort's partner check.

**Ask.** After step 3, pairs, 1 minute: "Half of this strip and half of that one: are they the same length? Are
they both one half?" Expect: not the same length; both are 1/2, each of its own strip. Push for "half of what?"

**Example 1** ([[s03-K17-1]]).
[[steps:s03-K17-1]]

Come back to the pizzas: the big half is more food, and both are 1/2. "1/2" says how the whole is cut, not how
much stuff. On the number line the whole is always the length from 0 to 1, so it never changes: any two fractions
on one line can be compared.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K17-2]]). On the board line.
[[steps:s03-K17-2]]

Say: "1/2 is a benchmark: a point we compare to." Name the two ways to set a fraction against 1/2: half of its
bottom number, or a name for 1/2 in its own parts (the warm-up list). Say aloud that 5 and 8 being bigger than 2
and 5 is not the reason.

#### 10 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

"Fix it", side 1 ([[s03-F17-1]], [[s03-F17-2]]). One minute in pairs on each, then the class; the display shows
their working first and the right working after the pair minute.

Rio (error H) is right that there is more food and wrong that 1/2 > 1/2. If many agree with Rio: fold two
strips of different length again and ask "half of what?" Kim (error D) read 4 and 10 as whole numbers. Point to
5/10 under 1/2 on the line: 4/10 is one tenth to its left. If many agree with Kim: students lay fifths and tenths
from their envelopes side by side; more parts, each smaller.

#### 9 min. Sort ten fractions

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P17-2]]), 6 minutes; students may mark them on the printed line. Then 3 minutes in pairs:
partners settle any fraction they placed differently by naming its half. Walk the room for 30/100 or 2/12 under
"more than" (error D): ask "what is 1/2 in hundredths?" Watch 7/5 and 3/3; both are past 1/2 on the line.

Check: Less than 1/2: 1/10, 2/12, 30/100. Equal: 4/8, 3/6. More: 7/12, 70/100, 6/8, 7/5, 3/3.

#### 8 min. On your own: compare across 1/2

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P17-3]], [[s03-P17-4]]). Each is two comparisons with 1/2, then the answer. In both, the
fraction with bigger numbers is the smaller one: > in the first or < in the second compared the numbers (error
D). Send the student to the warm-up list for a name for 1/2.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Rio and Kim were both wrong. What do you check before you compare two fractions?" Expect: "that they
are of the same whole", then "where each one is beside 1/2".

**Ask.** "How can you tell a fraction is less than 1/2 without a picture?" Expect: "the top is less than half
the bottom"; "find 1/2 in its own parts".

#### 5 min. Exit: the ticket

Alone. Read part (a) aloud once ([[s03-X17v2]]). Collect them; the results go on the record
sheet under E17.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write the sort and the two comparisons on the board; students write in notebooks and rule
  their own line from 0 to 2.

### At home

**Swap.** Strips: cut a 10-inch and a 6-inch strip from any paper. No projector: point at the page.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the pizza talk, On your own 2 and the challenge.

- **6 min. Two strips.** Work [[s03-K17-1]] with your strips; the child folds and lays the halves together.

    **Say.** "Half of this strip and half of that one: the same length? Both one half?" Expect: not the same
    length; both are 1/2, each of its own strip.

- **8 min. Example 2.** Read [[s03-K17-2]] together on the printed line; the child fills the frame.
- **8 min. Fix two.** [[s03-F17-1]], then [[s03-F17-2]].

    **Say.** "Rio says this 1/2 is greater. Half of what?" Expect: each is half of its own pizza, so 1/2 = 1/2.

- **8 min. Sort and compare.** [[s03-P17-2]], then [[s03-P17-3]]. The child says where each is beside 1/2.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X17v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check on the sort and the challenge. Never
drop the strips, the two fixes or the written comparisons with 1/2. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C17-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: half, the same whole, benchmark, more than, less than. Frame on the display: "__ is less than 1/2 and __ is more than 1/2, so __ < __." Wide strips and a large-print
line for those who need them; a scribe or an oral answer for part (a).

### Tomorrow

again, folded and laid together, and "half of what?". In (b), 3/8 > 2/3 (error D): day 18 opens with "4/10 is
more than 1/2" beside the line where 1/2 is also 5/10.
