# Day 17: Half of what?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Cut your two strips, 10 inches and 6 inches,
and two strips of different length for each student. Draw a line from 0 to 2 on the board with 1/2 and 1 marked,
long enough to write under. Mark day 16's written task. Put the display page on the projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: a name for 1/2

Alone: [[s03-P17-1]]. Hand back day 16's written task as they work, and read one "meets" answer aloud. Then ask
for other names for 1/2 and write them on the board: 2/4, 3/6, 4/8, 5/10, 6/12, 50/100. Leave them up all day.
A student who wrote 7 added 5 to both numbers (error B): ask "6 is how many times 1?"

#### 5 min. Question aloud: two pizzas

Whole class. **Ask.** "Is half of a small pizza equal to half of a large one?" Listen; do not settle it yet. Note
who says "the same, both are a half" and who says "the big half is more food". Both are partly right, and
Teaching first settles it.

#### 12 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its own screen.

**If day 16's tickets showed many A, B or E:** that error's wrong example from the error register opens with its
"if many" action, 3 minutes ("1/3 = 1/6" beside two strips for A; Jo's 3/4 = 5/6 on one line for B; Lena's 6/4
and 9/6 each given a name with smaller numbers for E). Example 1 then runs without the talk.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K17-1]]). Hold up the two strips, fold each, lay the halves together.
[[steps:s03-K17-1]]

Come back to the pizzas: the big half is more food, and both are 1/2. "1/2" says how the whole is cut, not how
much stuff. On the number line the whole is always the length from 0 to 1, so it never changes: any two fractions
on one line can be compared.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K17-2]]). On the board line.
[[steps:s03-K17-2]]

Name the two ways to set a fraction against 1/2: half of its bottom number, or a name for 1/2 in its own parts
(the warm-up list). Say aloud that 5 and 8 being bigger than 2 and 5 is not the reason.

#### 6 min. Fold and lay together

Pairs. Each student folds their two strips in half and lays the halves together.

**Ask.** "Half of this strip and half of that one: are they the same length? Are they both one half?" Expect: not
the same length; both are 1/2, each of its own strip. Push for "half of what?" in every answer.

#### 8 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

"Fix it", side 1 ([[s03-F17-1]], [[s03-F17-2]]). One minute of turn-and-talk in pairs on each, then the class.
The display shows each wrong working beside the right one.

Rio (error H) is right that there is more food and wrong that 1/2 > 1/2. If many agree with Rio: fold two
strips of different length again and ask "half of what?" Kim (error D) read 4 and 10 as whole numbers. Point to
5/10 under 1/2 on the line: 4/10 is one tenth to its left. If many agree with Kim: two strips side by side, one in
fifths and one in tenths; more parts, each smaller.

#### 9 min. Sort ten fractions

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P17-2]]), 6 minutes; students may sketch a line. Then 3 minutes in pairs: partners compare
sorts and settle any fraction they placed differently by naming its half. Walk the room for 30/100 or 2/12 under
"more than" (error D): ask "what is 1/2 in hundredths?" Watch 7/5 and 3/3; both are past 1/2 on the line.

Check: Less than 1/2: 1/10, 2/12, 30/100. Equal: 4/8, 3/6. More: 7/12, 70/100, 6/8, 7/5, 3/3.

#### 6 min. On your own: compare across 1/2

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P17-3]], [[s03-P17-4]]). Each is written as two comparisons with 1/2, then the answer, in
the frame. In both, the fraction with bigger numbers is the smaller one: a student who writes > for the first or
< for the second compared the numbers (error D). Send them to the warm-up list for a name for 1/2.

#### 4 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Rio and Kim were both wrong. What do you check before you compare two fractions?" Expect: "that they
are of the same whole", then "where each one is beside 1/2".

**Ask.** "How can you tell a fraction is less than 1/2 without a picture?" Expect: "the top is less than half
the bottom"; "find 1/2 in its own parts".

#### 5 min. Exit: the ticket

Alone. Read part (a) aloud once ([[s03-X17]]). Collect them.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write the sort and the two comparisons on the board; students write in notebooks and rule
  their own line from 0 to 2.
- **One projector, no display page.** Draw each step on the board line; each student writes "less than 1/2",
  "equal" or "more than 1/2" on paper before you mark the point.
- **A room with nothing.** Strips torn from scrap paper; a line ruled by hand with 1/2 and 1 marked.
- **Record sheet.** Until the record sheet exists, write E17 results on a class list, (a) and (b) apart.

### At home

**Swap.** Strips: cut a 10-inch and a 6-inch strip from any paper. No projector: point at the page. The line:
draw it from 0 to 2 on paper, with 1/2 and 1 marked.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the pizza talk, On your own 2 and the challenge.

- **6 min. Two strips.** Work [[s03-K17-1]] with your strips; the child folds and lays the halves together.

    **Say.** "Half of this strip and half of that one: the same length? Both one half?" Expect: not the same
    length; both are 1/2, each of its own strip.

- **8 min. Example 2.** Read [[s03-K17-2]] together on your line; the child fills the frame.
- **8 min. Fix two.** [[s03-F17-1]], then [[s03-F17-2]].

    **Say.** "Rio says this 1/2 is greater. Half of what?" Expect: each is half of its own pizza, so 1/2 = 1/2.

- **8 min. Sort and compare.** [[s03-P17-2]], then [[s03-P17-3]]. The child says where each is beside 1/2.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X17]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check on the sort (students check against the Check screen) and the challenge. Never
drop the strips, the two fixes or the written comparisons with 1/2. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C17-1]]), then:
each student writes one fraction less than 1/2 and one more, in different parts, and a partner writes the
comparison under the line.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: half, the same whole, benchmark (1/2 is a benchmark: a point we compare to), more than, less
than. Frame on the display: "__ is less than 1/2 and __ is more than 1/2, so __ < __." Wide strips for students
who need them; a large-print line. Read the ticket's story aloud once; a scribe or an oral answer for part (a).

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, "yes" in (a) (error H): day 18 opens with the two strips again, folded and laid together, and
"half of what?". In (b), 3/8 > 1/2 or 3/8 > 2/3 (error D): day 18 opens with "4/10 is more than 1/2" beside the
line where 1/2 is also 5/10. Day 18 then moves to 1 on the same line, so a student who still compares the numbers
as whole numbers meets D again there.
