# Day 18: Closer to 1

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Sort day 17's tickets by error. Draw a long
line from 0 to 2 on the board, with 1/2 and 1 marked and room under it to write. Put the display page on the
projector.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one equal fraction

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s03-P18-1]]). Then each student rules the line in the box under it: 6 inches, 0 at the
left end, 1 at 3 inches, 2 at 6 inches, 1/2 at an inch and a half. Show the answer at 4 minutes. 7 is one number
kept (A); 16 is 9 added to both (B).

**If day 17's tickets showed many H** (yes in part a): before Example 1, fold two strips of different length in
half and lay the halves together. Ask "half of what?" Write under the board line: "On one line, 1 is one length."

**If day 17's tickets showed many D** (3/8 > 1/2): before Example 1, write "4/10 is more than 1/2, because 4 and
10 are bigger" beside the board line, where 1/2 is also 5/10. With both, H goes first and D waits for Each side of 1.
Either branch takes its 3 minutes from the partner check.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: 6/5 and 5/6

Whole class, at the board line. Students mark both on their own line and fill the frame under Example 1.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K18-1]]). [[steps:s03-K18-1]]

#### 45 min. By hand: each side of 1, then one part short

**Each side of 1, 8 minutes.** Alone, side 1, Each side of 1 ([[s03-P18-2]], [[s03-P18-3]], [[s03-P18-4]],
[[s03-P18-5]]). Then 3 minutes in pairs: swap pages; a mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer.

Check: Is the top number less than, equal to, or more than the bottom number?

Watch for the sign that follows the bigger numbers (D): questions 1, 2 and 4 are built so D gives the wrong sign.
If many make it, two strips side by side: more parts, each smaller. Then say each fraction as "how many parts,
of what size".

**Example 2, 7 minutes.** Whole class. Students mark 4/5 and 5/6 on their line, shade each missing part, and fill
the frame.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K18-2]]). [[steps:s03-K18-2]]

**The planned question, 3 minutes.** Pairs, one minute, then the class.

**Ask.** "5/6 and 7/8 are each one part short of 1. Are they equal?" Expect, in this order: "yes, each is one
part short"; "no, an eighth is smaller, so 7/8 is closer to 1". Take the yes first and write it up as Rio's claim.

**Fix it, 8 minutes.** Side 2 ([[s03-F18-1]], [[s03-F18-2]]). For each, one minute of turn-and-talk in pairs,
then the class. The display shows each wrong working beside the right one. Rio counted the missing parts and not
their size (I): shade both missing parts on the board line, 1/6 is longer than 1/8. Ivy looked at the size of
the parts and not how many (J): say each as "how many parts, of what size", then 2/3 = 4/6 on the line.

**One part short, 6 minutes.** Alone, side 2, One part short ([[s03-P18-6]], [[s03-P18-7]]). Each needs the
sign and the missing part of each. Watch for = (I) and for the sign turned by part size (J). If many miss the first,
mark 2/3 and 3/4 on the board line and shade both missing parts together.

**Dee's claim, 10 minutes.** Alone, side 2 ([[s03-W18]]). Students mark both on their line and write. Walk and
read; a scribe or an oral answer is fine. Ask: "What is each one missing? Which missing part is longer?" Collect
these with the tickets; they are judged against the look-fors on the key and handed back in day 19's first five
minutes.

**Pull it together, 3 minutes.** Whole class.

**Ask.** "How can you tell a fraction is more than 1 without the line?" Expect: the top number is more than the
bottom number.

**Ask.** "Two fractions are each one part short of 1. How do you tell which is closer?" Expect: the one missing
the smaller part; more parts in the whole means each part is smaller.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, no line given ([[s03-X18]]). Read it aloud once. Collect at 5 minutes.

### Other rooms

- **No rulers.** Fold a paper strip in half twice for 0, 1/2, 1, 3/2 and 2, and lay it under the box.
- **No projector.** Write each fix on the board, the wrong working on the left and the right working beside it.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No ruler: a strip of paper folded in half twice.

**Skip.** The warm-up, Each side of 1 questions 2 and 4, One part short 4, and the challenge.

- **8 min. Two examples.** The child rules the line. Read [[s03-K18-1]] together, then [[s03-K18-2]]; the child
  marks each and fills the frames.

    **Say.** "Which one is missing the bigger part, 4/5 or 5/6?" Expect: 4/5; a fifth is bigger than a sixth.

- **8 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F18-1]], then [[s03-F18-2]]. The child writes what went wrong.

    **Say.** "5/6 and 7/8 are each one part short of 1. Are they equal?" Expect: no; an eighth is smaller, so 7/8
    is closer to 1.

- **7 min. Each side of 1.** [[s03-P18-2]] and [[s03-P18-4]], then [[s03-P18-6]].
- **7 min. Dee's claim.** [[s03-W18]]: the child marks both and writes why.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X18]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check (students check their own against the display) and the challenge. Never drop
the fixes or Dee's claim. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C18-1]]), then the extension ([[s03-C18-2]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: benchmark, the same whole, closer to, one part short. Frames on the display: "__ is missing
1/__. __ is missing 1/__. A __ is smaller, so __ is closer to 1." "__ is less than 1 and __ is more than 1, so
__ < __." Large-print line on request.

### Tomorrow

Day 19 opens with these tickets. Many with > in part a (error D): day 19 shows "7/12 is more than 2/3, because
7 and 12 are bigger" beside both on one line, and asks whether each is less or more than 1 first. Many with = in
part b (error I): Rio's 5/6 = 7/8 again, both missing parts shaded. Many with > in part b, "eighths are bigger"
(error J): Ivy's 2/3 and 5/6, said as "how many parts, of what size". Dee's claim goes back in day 19's first five
minutes. Results go on the record sheet under E18.
