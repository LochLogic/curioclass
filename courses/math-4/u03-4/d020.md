# Day 20: Rename both fractions

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Mark the E19 tickets and sort them by error: D
(the bigger numbers called the bigger fraction), A (one number changed, as 1/2 = 1/12), G (a right renaming, the
sign turned round). Put the display page on the projector. Draw the line from Example 1 large on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: common multiples and one benchmark

Alone, side 1: [[s03-W20-1]], then [[s03-W20-2]] on the printed line from 0 to 2. Go over both at 4 minutes: 18
and 36 are in both lists; 2/5 is under 1/2 and 7/8 is over it.

#### 5 min. Task: can both be cut into the same parts?

Hand back the E19 tickets. If many missed E19, two minutes of this part go to its error first:

**If day 19's tickets showed many D** (a > in (a) or a < in (b)): day 19's wrong example again, "7/12 is more
than 2/3, because 7 and 12 are bigger", beside both on the board line: 8/12 sits right of 7/12.

**If many A** (1/2 = 1/12): cut one half on the board line into 6 parts and count them: 1/2 = 6/12.

**If many G**: read E19's two answers aloud as sentences, "5 twelfths is less than 6 twelfths". The open side
faces the greater number.

Then pairs, using the line under Example 1. **Ask.** "Can 2/3 and 4/5 be put in the same parts? Which parts?"
Expect, in this order: "no, 3 does not go into 5" (yesterday only one fraction changed; today both may); "cut
each third into 5"; "fifteenths, because thirds and fifths both fit into 15". Note any pair that says 2/15 and
4/15 for Teaching first.

#### 10 min. Teaching first: rename both

Whole class. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go, not ahead of you. The display shows each step on its
own screen; point to the board line as each cut is made.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K20-1]]). [[steps:s03-K20-1]]

Then the task's wrong example (error A): "2/3 = 2/15 and 4/5 = 4/15, so 2/3 < 4/5." The sign is right; the names
are wrong. On the board line, cut one third into 5 and count the parts in 2/3: 10, not 2. A right sign from wrong
names is still wrong.

**Example 2** ([[s03-K20-2]]). [[steps:s03-K20-2]]

Both times the fraction with the bigger numbers was greater. Say so, and say it tells us nothing: only the
renamed parts settle it.

#### 8 min. Fix it: Ivy's working

Side 2 ([[s03-F20-1]]). Two minutes alone, two minutes of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class: the display
shows Ivy's working beside the right working. **Ask.** "Ivy's sign is right. Is her working right?" Expect: no;
4/30 is a tiny piece near 0, and 4/5 is near 1; each fifth cut into 6 gives 24 thirtieths.

If many still change one number: count the parts in 4/5 aloud on the board, "one fifth is 6 thirtieths, two
fifths are 12", up to 24.

#### 22 min. On your own: four pairs, then a partner check

Alone, side 2, no line given, 17 minutes. For each pair: list multiples of both denominators until one number is
in both lists, rename both, write the sign. Then check with 1/2 or 1 where that settles it. Do not say which
pairs those are; let students find them.

Walk with one question: "What size are the parts now, and how many of each?" Watch for:

- a sign that follows the bigger numbers, as 5/8 > 4/5 or 2/3 < 3/10 (error D). Ask how many fortieths each is.
- one number changed, as 5/40 and 4/40 (error A). Ask where 5/40 would sit on a line: near 0.
- a list that runs long: the two denominators multiplied always work (8 × 5 = 40). Any number in both lists
  works.

Then 5 minutes in pairs: partners swap and check each renamed fraction by multiplying back ("5 × 5 = 25 and 8 ×
5 = 40, so 5/8 = 25/40"). A mismatch gets a question mark, not the answer. If many miss pair 2, list the
multiples of 8 and 5 together on the board.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Is 15 the only number of parts that works for thirds and fifths?" Expect: no; 30 and 45 work too; any
number in both lists; 3 × 5 always works. Show 2/3 = 20/30 and 4/5 = 24/30: the same sign.

**Ask.** "When did 1/2 or 1 save you the renaming?" Expect: when one fraction is under it and one is over (pairs
3 and 4).

#### 5 min. Exit: the ticket

Alone: the exit ticket ([[s03-X20]]), no line given. Write results on the record sheet under E20.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Copy the warm-up and the pairs from the board. Students rule their own lines: 15 centimetres
  with a mark every centimetre for Example 1 (each mark is a fifteenth), 12 centimetres with a mark every half
  centimetre for Example 2 (twenty-fourths). Thirds and fifths, or thirds and eighths, are then counted off.
  The key is read aloud for the partner check.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1.

**Skip.** The warm-up, On your own 4 and 5, and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** Read [[s03-K20-1]] together; the child fills the frame under the line. Then
  [[s03-K20-2]].

    **Say.** "Can 2/3 and 4/5 be put in the same parts? Which parts?" Expect: fifteenths; cut each third into
    5 and each fifth into 3.

- **8 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F20-1]].

    **Say.** "Ivy's sign is right. Is her working right?" Expect: no; 4/5 is 24/30, not 4/30.

- **12 min. On your own.** [[s03-P20-1]], then [[s03-P20-2]]. The child lists multiples first, then checks
  the second pair with 1/2.

    **Say.** "Is 15 the only number of parts that works for thirds and fifths?" Expect: no; 30 works too, or
    any number in both lists.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X20]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the partner check (students check with 1/2 or 1 alone) and the second Ask. Never drop the fix-it
or the listed multiples. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C20-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: multiple, common multiple, common denominator, greater than, less than. Read every sign as words.
Frame on the display: "__ and __ are both __ths now, and __ is more than __, so __ > __."

### Tomorrow

3/20 and 7/20 (error A): day 21's warm-up asks the missing number in one equal fraction, and those
students count the parts on a line first. 15/20 and 14/20 with the sign turned round (error G): read the
comparison aloud as a sentence before day 21's warm-up.
