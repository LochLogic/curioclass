# Day 23: Pick a way and prove it

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print what the header lists. Stack the pairs' keys and cut out the eight card rows (s03-P23-2 to s03-P23-9,
together in the table): that strip is the card key, and the rest stays hidden. Put the display page on the
projector. Have day 22's written tasks ready to hand back, look-fors ticked.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one benchmark

Alone, no line given. Hand back day 22's written tasks as students start. [[s03-P23-1]] Students write the sign
and one sentence. Go over it at 5 minutes: half of 100 is 50, half of 12 is 6. A student who wrote > read 45
and 100 as big numbers (error D).

**If day 22's tickets showed many D** ((a) <, (b) > or (c) <): before the example, two minutes from the cards.
Hold up a strip cut in tenths and one in twelfths, and say four pairs aloud as "parts of size": "7 parts of size
one twelfth". **If many G** (signs turned round): read six comparisons from day 22 aloud as sentences; the open
side faces the greater number. Both: D first, G in the stories' pull-together.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: one pair, three ways

Whole class. Students fill the three lines of the frame on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its
own screen; sketch one line from 0 to 1 on the board for the first way.

**Example** ([[s03-K23-1]]). [[steps:s03-K23-1]]

Say it plainly: every way gives the same sign, so pick the one that is quickest for this pair, then prove it.

#### 25 min. Cards, with a partner holding the key

Pairs. Fraction Wall Duel may take this slot once it is built and played; until it exists, the day runs on the
eight cards on side 1 ([[s03-P23-2]], [[s03-P23-3]], [[s03-P23-4]], [[s03-P23-5]], [[s03-P23-6]], [[s03-P23-7]],
[[s03-P23-8]], [[s03-P23-9]]). Partner A takes card 1 and says the sign and the way before writing anything;
then writes the proof in the row. Partner B holds the card key and says only "yes" or "try again". Swap for
card 2, and so on.

Two cards are traps (the book and the milk): the two fractions are of different wholes, so the answer is
"cannot tell". Read both aloud to the class at the start. Warn pairs that a trap is only a trap when the story
names two different things.

Watch for:

- A > on 8/12 and 4/5, > on 9/8 and 5/4, < on 1/5 and 2/12 or < on 2/8 and 3/12: bigger numbers read as a bigger
  fraction (error D). Ask "how many parts, and of what size?"
- An = on 6/8 and 8/10, "each is two parts short" (error I): ask which missing piece is longer, 2/8 or 2/10.
- "Jay" or "Mo" (error H): ask "half of what?"
- 1/2 tried on 8/12 and 4/5, both over: send them to same parts or same count.

If many miss the traps, hold two strips of different length and fold each in half. Stop at 25 minutes; collect
the card keys.

#### 20 min. Two stories, the written task, the planned question

**Stories, 8 minutes, alone** ([[s03-P23-10]], [[s03-P23-11]]). Read both aloud once. The ribbons are past 1;
the sandwiches are different wholes. Then 2 minutes: partners compare answers, and each mismatch gets a
question mark.

Check: Max's ribbon is longer, 13/10 < 5/3. Noor cannot be sure: the sandwiches are different wholes.

Watch for "Ivy's" (D) and "Max's" with 13/10 > 5/3 written (G). For Noor, a "yes" is H; a "no" with no reason
gets "why not?".

**Written task, 6 minutes, alone**, the box at the foot of side 2: [[s03-W23]]. A student may answer aloud to
you instead (a scribe is allowed). Collect the pages at the end; read after class against the key's look-fors
and samples.

**Pull it together, 4 minutes, whole class.**

**Ask.** "Which way did you pick for 6/8 and 8/10, and why not another?" Expect: same parts or same count,
"1/2 can't settle it: both are over". Take one of each, then: "For 1/5 and 2/12?" Expect same count, one rename.

**Ask.** "When does 'cannot tell' fit?" Expect: only when the wholes are different things.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper: [[s03-X23]]. Read it aloud once. Write the results on the record sheet under E23.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write the eight cards on the board, numbered; pairs work in notebooks, and the partner reads
  the key from your copy, card by card.
- **One projector.** One card at a time on the screen. Every student writes the sign and the reason, then holds
  up a sign card, or "cannot compare" for a trap.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the page. The partner with the key is you.

**Skip.** The warm-up, cards 1, 7 and 8, and the extension.

- **8 min. One pair, three ways.** Read [[s03-K23-1]] together; the child fills the three lines.
- **12 min. Cards.** [[s03-P23-3]], [[s03-P23-4]], [[s03-P23-5]], [[s03-P23-6]] and [[s03-P23-7]]. The child says
  the sign and the way first, then writes the proof. You say only "yes" or "try again".

    **Say.** "Which way did you pick for this pair, and why not another?" Expect: for 6/8 and 8/10, same parts
    or same count, because both are over 1/2.

- **8 min. Two stories.** [[s03-P23-10]], then [[s03-P23-11]].

    **Say.** "Can Noor be sure?" Expect: no, the sandwiches are different sizes.

- **6 min. Prove it.** [[s03-W23]]. The child may say it while you write.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X23]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop cards 1 and 8 and the partner swap after the stories. Never drop the traps, the written task or
the proof on each card. Longer: "Find a fraction between 2/3 and 3/4. Prove it.", on the back of side 2.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, common denominator, common numerator, same whole. Each card sits
beside a marked line. Frame on the display: "__ and __ are both __ths now, and __ is more than __, so __ > __."
Read every sign aloud as words. A student may place sign cards instead of writing a sign, and may say a proof
aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 24 is the end-of-unit assessment; mark E23 tonight. "Ben" is error D. Ana's answer with 2/5 < 3/8 written
is error G. "Cannot tell" takes the same-whole rule too far: the trail is one whole. Hand back the E23 tickets
and the written tasks, look-fors ticked, when pencils go down on day 24, so their D, G and "cannot tell" join the
respond groups.
