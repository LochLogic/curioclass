# Day 23: Pick a way and prove it

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print what the header lists. On the covered part of the board, write the eight cards' signs, one proof each,
from the key: that is the card key. Put the display page on the projector. Have day 22's written tasks ready to
hand back, look-fors ticked, with one line on each.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one benchmark

Alone, top of side 1. Hand back day 22's written tasks as students start. [[s03-P23-1v2]]
Go over it at 5 minutes: half of 100 is 50, half of 12 is 6. A student
who wrote > read 47 and 100 as big numbers (error D).

**If day 22's tickets showed many D** ((a) <, (b) > or (c) <): before the example, two minutes from the cards
(stop them at 23 minutes). Hold up the tenths and twelfths strips and say four pairs aloud as parts of a size: 4
tenths and 4 twelfths, 3 sixths and 3 eighths, 2 fourths and 2 fifths, 5 eighths and 5 twelfths. **If many J**
((c) >, "bigger pieces"): say "how many parts, of what size" for 4/10 and 40/100. **If many G** (signs turned
round): read six comparisons from day 22 aloud as sentences: 7/10 < 4/5, 3/8 > 3/10, 5/12 < 3/4, 3/10 > 25/100,
7/6 < 5/4, 7/8 < 9/10. Both: D first, G in the stories' pull-together.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: one pair, three ways

Whole class. Students fill the frame on side 1 as you go. The 1/2 way is said, not drawn.

**Example** ([[s03-K23-1]]). [[steps:s03-K23-1]]

Every way gives the same sign: pick the quickest, then prove it. One more way, for the written task: how far
each fraction is from 1.

#### 25 min. Cards, with a partner holding the key

Pairs. Until Fraction Wall Duel exists, the eight cards, six on side 1 and two on side 2
([[s03-P23-2]] to [[s03-P23-9v2]]). Partner A says the sign and the way first, then writes the proof in the row.
B checks the sign, then the proof: are the new names equal to the old ones, and does the sign follow? Another
complete way is right too. A right sign with no proof is "try again". Swap for card 2, and so on; on your
partner's cards, write the sign only. Not all eight are expected.

Two cards are traps; do not say which. A story is a trap only when the two fractions are of wholes that may not
be the same size. Two ribbons measured in meters are not a trap: both are parts of one meter.

Watch for:

- A > on 8/12 and 4/5, > on 9/8 and 8/6, < on 1/4 and 2/12 or < on 2/8 and 3/12: bigger numbers read as a bigger
  fraction (error D). Ask "how many parts, and of what size?"
- An = on 6/8 and 8/10, "each is two parts short" (error I): ask which missing piece is longer, 2/8 or 2/10.
- "Jay" or "Mo" (error H): ask "half of what?"
- 1/2 tried on 8/12 and 4/5, both over: send them to same parts or same count.

If many miss the traps, hold two strips of different length and fold each in half. At 25 minutes, uncover the card key; pairs check every card against it.

#### 20 min. Two stories, the written task, the planned question

**Stories, 10 minutes: 8 alone, then 2 with a partner** ([[s03-P23-10]], [[s03-P23-11]]). Read both aloud
once. (For you: the ribbons are past 1; the sandwiches are different wholes. Do not say so.) Then partners compare; a mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Max's ribbon is longer, 13/10 < 5/3. Noor cannot be sure: the sandwiches are different wholes.

Watch for "Ivy's" (D), "Max's" with 13/10 > 5/3 written (G), and "yes" for Noor (H).

**Written task, 6 minutes, alone**, the box at the foot of side 2: [[s03-W23]]. A scribe is allowed. Collect the
pages; read them after class against the look-fors.

**Pull it together, 4 minutes, whole class.**

**Ask.** "Which way did you pick for 6/8 and 8/10, and why not another?" Expect: same parts or same count,
"1/2 can't settle it: both are over". Take one of each, then: "For 1/4 and 2/12?" Expect same count, one rename.

**Ask.** "When does 'cannot tell' fit?" Expect: only when the two fractions are of wholes that may be different
sizes; a meter, one trail or one ribbon is the same whole.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper: [[s03-X23]]. No line given; a sketch is allowed. Read it aloud once. Write the results on the record sheet under E23.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Write the eight cards on the board, numbered; pairs work in notebooks.
- **One projector.** One card at a time on the screen. Every student writes the sign and the reason, then holds
  up a sign card, or writes "cannot tell" on the back of the = card.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the page. The partner with the key is you.

**Skip.** The warm-up, cards 1, 7 and 8, and the extension.

- **8 min. One pair, three ways.** Read [[s03-K23-1]] together; the child fills the three lines.
- **12 min. Cards.** [[s03-P23-3]], [[s03-P23-4]], [[s03-P23-5]], [[s03-P23-6v2]] and [[s03-P23-7]]. The child says
  the sign and the way first, then writes the proof. You say only "yes" or "try again".

    **Say.** "Which way did you pick for this pair, and why not another?" Expect: for 6/8 and 8/10, same parts
    or same count, because both are over 1/2.

- **8 min. Two stories.** [[s03-P23-10]], then [[s03-P23-11]].

    **Say.** "Can Noor be sure?" Expect: no, the sandwiches are different sizes.

- **6 min. Prove it.** [[s03-W23]]. The child may say it while you write.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X23]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop cards 1 and 8 and the partner swap after the stories. Never drop the traps, the written task or
the proof on each card. Longer: "Find a fraction between 2/3 and 3/4. Prove it.", on the back of side 2.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, common denominator, common numerator, same whole. Frame on the display: "__ and __ are both __ths now, and __ is more than __, so __ > __."
Read signs aloud as words. A student may place sign cards or say a proof aloud.

### Tomorrow

Day 24 is the end-of-unit assessment; mark E23 tonight. "Ben" is error D. Ana's answer with 2/5 < 3/8 written
is error G. "Cannot tell" takes the same-whole rule too far: the trail is one whole; write ct in the E23 cell. Hand back the E23 tickets
and the written tasks, look-fors ticked, when pencils go down on day 24, so their D, G and "cannot tell" join the
respond groups.
