# Section 3.4: Comparing with a common denominator or numerator (days 19 to 24)

## The math, for the adult

Standard 4.NF.A.2: "Compare two fractions with different numerators and different denominators, e.g., by creating
common denominators or numerators, or by comparing to a benchmark fraction such as 1/2. Recognize that comparisons
are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. Record the results of comparisons with symbols >,
=, or <, and justify the conclusions, e.g., by using a visual fraction model." This section's share: creating
common denominators or numerators, recording with the signs, and on day 23 choosing a way and justifying it.
Benchmarks and the same whole were section 3.3. Every story on days 19 to 22 says the whole is the same; day 23
mixes in stories where it is not, so students must check.

**Same size parts: compare the counts.** 2/3 and 7/12 cannot be compared by their numbers, because a third and a
twelfth are different sizes. Cut each third into 4 and 2/3 = 8/12. Now both are counts of twelfths, and 8 twelfths
is more than 7: 2/3 = 8/12 and 8/12 > 7/12, so 2/3 > 7/12. Day 19 starts with pairs where one denominator is a
multiple of the other, so only one fraction is renamed.

**When neither fits the other.** For 2/3 and 4/5, thirds and fifths both cut evenly into fifteenths, because 15 is
a multiple of 3 and of 5 (unit 1's common multiples): 10/15 and 12/15. The product of the two denominators always
works; any common multiple will do, and no least common denominator is asked.

**Same count: compare the size of the parts.** 1/3 and 1/8: one part each, and a third is bigger, so 1/3 > 1/8.
The same holds for any equal count: 2/5 and 2/8 are both 2 parts, and fifths are bigger. So a pair can also be
compared by making the numerators the same: 1/3 = 2/6, and 2/6 > 2/8 because sixths are bigger than eighths. This
is the move students find hardest to say, because the larger denominator now means the smaller fraction (day 21).

**Choosing a way.** By day 23 students have three ways: a benchmark (section 3.3), the same parts, the same count.
The point of day 23 is to look at the pair before choosing a way, and to prove the answer, not
to follow one method every time.

**The sign.** > and < are read as words every time: "is greater than", "is less than". The open side faces the
greater number. A student who reasons rightly and writes the sign the wrong way round (error G) has not yet
read the sign as a sentence.

**Limits.** Two fractions at a time; ordering three or more is not taught.

## The line, then none

- **Day 19.** Both fractions on one printed line, the sign written beside it: the drawing tied to the written
  comparison, pairs past 1 too.
- **Days 20 and 21.** Printed lines for the first pairs, then none. Each answer is checked against 1/2 or 1 where
  that settles it.
- **Day 22.** No line is given, and the line from 0 to 2 with 1/2 and 1 marked is withdrawn too. A student may
  sketch one to justify.
- **Day 23.** No line. Paper cards, with trap cards, a partner checking and a card key on the board; then two
  story problems.

Days 19 to 21 print their lines on the student page.

## Why this model

Counts can be compared only when the parts are the same size: marking both points on one line after renaming
shows the sign is right, and a renaming that moved a point shows at once. Withdrawing the line on day 22 makes the
written comparison carry the proof, with a sketch always allowed. Day 23 asks for a choice among ways, which a tool
could check on every try; Fraction Wall Duel is a light tool for now (spec only), so day 23 runs on paper cards
with a partner checking. Days 20 and 22 would suit a light practice set with a line shown after each try;
it does not exist, so they run on paper with a key.

## The errors to expect, in plain words

| | What students do | What it shows | What to do when many do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Rename the denominator only: "2/3 = 2/15 and 4/5 = 4/15, so 2/3 < 4/5" | They change the parts and forget to recount them | Cut one third into 5 on a line and count the parts in 2/3 |
| D | Think bigger numbers make a bigger fraction: "7/12 is more than 2/3", or "3/12 is more than 3/10, because 12 is more than 10" | They read the numerals as whole numbers | A tenths strip beside a twelfths strip; then 4/10 and 4/12, 7/10 and 7/12 said aloud as "parts of size" |
| G | Reason rightly and write the sign the wrong way round: "2/5 = 4/10, and 3/10 is less, so 3/10 > 2/5" | The signs are not yet read as words | Read six comparisons aloud as sentences; the open side faces the greater number |

The day each wrong example is shown: D on days 19 and 21, A on day 20 (a right answer from wrong names, so it is
easy to miss), G on day 22. On day 23 watch for "cannot tell" on a story where the whole is the same: the same-whole rule from section 3.3
taken too far.

## Readiness item 3, asked again

Day 19's warm-up asks readiness item 3 again with new numbers, on paper, two minutes: 3/8 or 5/8; 1/4 or 1/6.
Read it beside the record sheet's R3. A student who missed both times sits with you for the first worked pair.

## Day 24

The end-of-unit assessment, 30 minutes, unaided; a sketched line is allowed. Then the respond half: 5 minutes of
marking against the key, each wrong answer with its error letter, then 15 minutes in groups by letter, each doing
its "if many" action from the opener that owns it (A to D and K, section 3.1; E and F, 3.2; H, I and J, 3.3; G,
and D on a comparing item, this one). The E23 tickets and written tasks go back when pencils go down, and their
errors join the groups. The last ten minutes are unit 4's readiness check. Items 3.1b and 3.3a are judged after
class and answered in day 25's second five minutes, after the warm-up.

## Supplies for the section

- Per student: paper practice sets with their keys (days 20 and 22); sign
  cards, >, = and <, written by each student on three scraps of card on day 19 and kept; a pencil.
- Per pair, day 23: the paper cards, trap cards included; the card key goes on a covered part of the board.
- Per class: the projector and a board (no document camera: draw each line on the board after students hold up
  a sign card).

## Words

Greater than, less than, common denominator, common numerator, the same size, the same count, each beside a marked
line. The signs are read as words every time. Frame, to take or leave: "__ and __ are both __ths now, and __ is more
than __, so __ > __." For day 21: "__ and __ both have __ parts. __ are longer, so __ __ __."

## How the section is checked

Exits on days 19 to 23, on paper, written on the unit record sheet by error letter (courses/math-4/u03-1/
record.md). Day 22's short written item and day 23's written task are judged with three look-fors on their keys;
day 22's goes back on day 23, day 23's when pencils go down on day 24. End-of-unit assessment item 3.4a is this
section's; all seven items are given on day 24.
