# Day 22: Pick a way, then compare

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, the exit tickets and the key. Sort day 21's tickets: right, a sign turned to "bigger
numbers" (D), and 2/3 = 6/3 (A). Those who wrote a sign with no renamed fractions sit near you for the
first ten minutes. Put the display page on the projector. Strip envelopes on desks.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one equal fraction, one comparison

Alone, untimed, side 1 ([[s03-P22-1]], [[s03-P22-2]]). No line is printed from today; a student may sketch
one. Watch for 11 in the first blank: 6 added to both (error B). Ask
"what was each sixth cut into?" At 5 minutes one student reads both answers aloud.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: one pair, two ways

Whole class. Students fill the example frame on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its own
screen.

**If day 21's tickets showed many D** (5/12 > 5/8, or 2/3 < 6/10): first show day 21's fix-it again, "3/12 is
more than 3/10, because 12 is more than 10". Hold up one strip in tenths and one in twelfths: more parts, each
smaller. Say four pairs aloud as parts of a size: 4 tenths and 4 twelfths, 3 sixths and 3 eighths, 2 fourths and 2
fifths, 5 eighths and 5 twelfths. Take the 3 minutes from set C.

**If day 21's tickets showed many A** (2/3 = 6/3): fold a strip in thirds, then each third into 3. Count the
shaded parts aloud: 6 of 9, so 2/3 = 6/9, not 6/3. Take the 3 minutes from set C.

**Example** ([[s03-K22-1]]). [[steps:s03-K22-1]]

Say it plainly: both ways always work. Pick the one with less to do, then check the sign.

#### 4 min. Fix it: the sign turned round

Pairs for one minute, then the class ([[s03-F22-1]]).
Rio's thinking was right; only the sign was wrong (error G). The class reads Rio's sign aloud as words.

If many cannot say what went wrong: read these aloud as sentences, then turn each sign: 6/10 > 3/8, 3/5 < 6/8,
3/10 < 2/5, 1/3 > 1/8, 2/5 > 4/12, 3/4 > 7/10.

#### 8 min. Set A

Alone, side 1 ([[s03-P22-3]], [[s03-P22-4]], [[s03-P22-5v2]]). No clock: a student who finishes early checks with 1/2 or 1. At 6 minutes show set A's
answers; students mark their own in red and fix any wrong sign.

Check: Read each sign aloud as words. Does the open side face the greater number?

Watch for these:

- 7/10 > 4/5, 16/10 > 8/5 or 3/8 < 3/10: bigger numbers read as a bigger fraction (error D). Ask "how big is each
  part, and how many are there?"
- A right sign, no renaming, "bigger pieces" (error J): ask "how many parts?"
- No renamed fractions and no "same count" written. Ask "which way did you pick?"

If many miss set A for D: hold up a strip in tenths beside one in twelfths, and say set A's pairs aloud as parts
of a size. If many miss for G: the class reads set A's three answers aloud before set B.

#### 9 min. Set B

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P22-6]], [[s03-P22-7]], [[s03-P22-8]]). At 7 minutes show set B's answers; mark and fix in red.

Check: Read each sign aloud. Does it face the greater number?

#### 11 min. Set C

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P22-9v2]], [[s03-P22-10v2]], [[s03-P22-11]], [[s03-P22-12]]). Two pairs are equal or past 1.
At 9 minutes show set C's answers; mark and fix in red. Watch for 35/40 and 36/40 written right, then 7/8 > 9/10:
the sign turned round (error G). Ask the student to read it aloud.

Check: Read each sign aloud. Does it face the greater number?

#### 8 min. Two ways, and which was quicker

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-W22]]). Look-fors: both renamings right, both ways giving 1/4 > 2/10, a reason for the
choice. Either way may be the quicker, if the reason fits. Collect the pages at the end of the day and read this
item after class.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Which pair today was quickest with the same count? Which needed the same size parts?" Expect: 3/8 and
3/10, or 3/6 and 3/4, already have the same count; 3/4 and 6/10 needed one cut to get there; 7/8 and 9/10 needed
fortieths. Take the same-count answer first.

**Ask.** "How can 1/2 or 1 tell you your sign is wrong?" Expect: 5/12 is under a half and 3/4 is over, so 5/12
must be less; 7/8 and 9/10 are each one part short of 1, and a tenth is the smaller gap.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. No line given; a sketch is allowed ([[s03-X22]]). Write the results on the record sheet under E22.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Copy the pairs on the board one set at a time; students work in notebooks. Read the key aloud
  after each set.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No red pen: any other colour.

**Skip.** The warm-up, set C and the challenge.

- **8 min. One pair, two ways.** Read [[s03-K22-1]] together; the child fills the frame.
- **5 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F22-1]].

    **Say.** "Read Rio's sign aloud as words. Is that what Rio found?" Expect: no; 3/10 is less, so 3/10 < 2/5.

- **12 min. Two short sets.** Set A ([[s03-P22-3]], [[s03-P22-4]], [[s03-P22-5v2]]), then set B ([[s03-P22-6]],
  [[s03-P22-7]], [[s03-P22-8]]): the child writes the renamed fractions and the sign. Mark each set together
  against the answers before the next.

    **Say.** "Read each sign aloud. Does the open side face the greater number?" Expect: yes, for each.

- **5 min. Two ways.** [[s03-W22]]. Ask which way was quicker, and why.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X22]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the third and fourth pairs of set C; keep 7/8 and 9/10 and both planned questions. Never drop the
fix-it, the marking after each set or the written item. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C22-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, equal to, common denominator, common numerator. Read every sign as
words. Frames on the display: "__ and __ are both __ths now. __ is more than __, so __ > __." and "Both have
__ parts. __ths are bigger, so __ > __." Read it again in the pair's order. A student may place a sign card or say a comparison aloud.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, (a) <, (b) > or (c) < with no renaming or wrong renaming (error D): day 23's warm-up holds up a
tenths strip and a twelfths strip and says four pairs aloud as parts of a size. Right renamed fractions with a sign
turned round (error G): day 23 reads six comparisons from day 22 aloud as sentences. (c) > with "bigger pieces"
(error J): day 23 says "how many parts, of what size" for both fractions in the warm-up. Hand the written item
back in day 23's first minutes, look-fors ticked.
