# Day 22: Pick a way, then compare

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page, the exit tickets and the key. Sort day 21's tickets: right, a sign turned to "bigger
numbers" (D), and 2/3 = 6/3 (A). Those who wrote a sign with no renamed fractions sit near you for the
first ten minutes. Put the display page on the projector. Write the warm-up on the board.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: one equal fraction, one comparison

Alone, untimed, side 1 ([[s03-P22-1]], [[s03-P22-2]]). No number line is printed from today, here or on the
pairs; a student may sketch one to explain. Watch for 11 in the first blank: 6 added to both (error B). Ask
"what was each sixth cut into?" At 5 minutes one student reads both answers aloud, signs as words.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: one pair, two ways

Whole class. Students fill the example frame on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its own
screen.

**If day 21's tickets showed many D** (5/12 > 5/8, or 2/3 < 6/10): first show day 21's wrong example, "4/12 is
more than 4/10, because 12 is more than 10". Hold up one strip in tenths and one in twelfths: more parts, each
smaller. Say four pairs aloud as "parts of size": 4 twelfths, 4 tenths. Take the 3 minutes from the pairs.

**If day 21's tickets showed many A** (2/3 = 6/3): fold a strip in thirds, then each third into 3. Count the
shaded parts aloud: 6 of 9, so 2/3 = 6/9, not 6/3. Take the 3 minutes from the pairs.

**Example** ([[s03-K22-1]]). [[steps:s03-K22-1]]

Say it plainly: both ways always work. Pick the one with less to do, then check the sign.

#### 6 min. Fix it: the sign turned round

Pairs for one minute, then the class ([[s03-F22-1]]). The display shows Rio's working beside the right working.
Rio's thinking was right; only the sign was wrong (error G). Have the class read Rio's sign aloud as words. The
open side of the sign faces the greater number.

If many cannot say what went wrong: read six comparisons aloud as sentences, then turn each sign to face the
greater number.

#### 26 min. Ten pairs in three short sets

Alone. Set A is pairs 1 to 3 on side 1 ([[s03-P22-3]], [[s03-P22-4]], [[s03-P22-5]]); set B is pairs 4 to 6
([[s03-P22-6]], [[s03-P22-7]], [[s03-P22-8]]); set C is pairs 7 to 10 ([[s03-P22-9v2]], [[s03-P22-10]],
[[s03-P22-11]], [[s03-P22-12]]). Students write the fractions they renamed beside each pair, then the sign. Three
pairs are equal or past 1. No clock: a student who finishes a set early checks it with 1/2 or 1 while waiting.

After each set, show its answers on the display. Students mark their own with a red pen and fix any wrong sign
before starting the next set. Most students finish set A in about 6 minutes, B in 8 and C in 10.

Check: Read each sign aloud as words. Does the open side face the greater number?

Walk the room. Watch for these:

- 7/10 > 4/5, 8/6 > 4/3, 3/6 > 3/4 or 9/12 > 6/8: bigger numbers read as a bigger fraction (error D). Ask "how
  big is each part, and how many are there?"
- 35/40 and 36/40 written right, then 7/8 > 9/10: the sign turned round (error G). Ask the student to read it
  aloud.
- No renamed fractions written, even where the sign is right. Ask "which way did you pick?"

If many miss set A for D: stop, hold up a strip in tenths beside one in twelfths, and say set A's pairs aloud as
parts of a size. If many miss for G: the class reads set A's three answers aloud before set B.

#### 8 min. Two ways, and which was quicker

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-W22]]). Look-fors: both renamings right, both ways giving 1/4 > 2/10, a reason for the
choice. Either way may be the quicker, if the reason fits. Collect the pages at the end of the day and read this
item after class.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Which pair today was quickest with the same count? Which needed the same size parts?" Expect: 3/8 and
3/10, or 3/6 and 3/4, already have the same count; 5/8 and 7/12 need twenty-fourths. Take the same-count answer
first.

**Ask.** "How can 1/2 or 1 tell you your sign is wrong?" Expect: 5/12 is under a half and 3/4 is over, so 5/12
must be less; 7/8 and 9/10 are each one part short of 1, and a tenth is the smaller gap.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. No line given ([[s03-X22]]). Write the results on the record sheet under E22.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Copy the pairs on the board one set at a time; students work in notebooks. Read the key aloud
  after each set.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No red pen: any other colour.

**Skip.** The warm-up, set C and the challenge.

- **8 min. One pair, two ways.** Read [[s03-K22-1]] together; the child fills the frame.
- **5 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F22-1]].

    **Say.** "Read Rio's sign aloud as words. Is that what Rio found?" Expect: no; 3/10 is less, so 3/10 < 2/5.

- **12 min. Two short sets.** Set A ([[s03-P22-3]], [[s03-P22-4]], [[s03-P22-5]]), then set B ([[s03-P22-6]],
  [[s03-P22-7]], [[s03-P22-8]]): the child writes the renamed fractions and the sign. Mark each set together
  against the answers before the next.

    **Say.** "Read each sign aloud. Does the open side face the greater number?" Expect: yes, for each.

- **5 min. Two ways.** [[s03-W22]]. Ask which way was quicker, and why.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X22]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set C's last two pairs and the second planned question. Never drop the fix-it, the marking after
each set or the written item. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C22-1]]), then "Find a fraction between 3/5 and 2/3.
Prove it."

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, equal to, common denominator, common numerator. Read every sign as
words, every time. Frame on the display and side 1: "__ and __ are both __ths now, and __ is more than __, so __
> __." A student may place a sign card or say a comparison aloud.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, (a) 2/3 < 5/8, (b) 6/12 > 3/4 or (c) 4/10 < 40/100 with no renaming, or with wrong renaming
(error D): day 23 opens its teaching with day 22's set A said aloud as parts of a size. Right renamed fractions
with a sign turned round (error G): day 23 reads three of the ticket's comparisons aloud as words before the
teaching. Hand the written item back in day 23's first minutes, with one line on each.
