# Day 24: Show what I know

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print list. Open the display; show the answers only at the marking. Keep the day 23
tickets and written tasks until pencils go down. Cover the Word Wall's comparing cards. Put strips and plain paper out.

### The day

#### 30 min. End-of-unit assessment

Alone, unaided: no strips, cards or printed lines; a sketched line is allowed. Read each item aloud once, signs
as words. At 30 minutes, pencils down; not reached is not wrong.

#### 20 min. Respond: mark, then fix in groups

**Mark, 5 minutes.** Pencils away; hand back the day 23 tickets and written tasks; show the answers to 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7.
Students tick or circle each in pen and match each wrong answer to the group list. You judge 2 and 5 after class.

**Groups, 15 minutes.** Hands up sets the groups; a student in two joins the earlier one. Each group fixes its
items in pen beside the first try. Tasks are on the display.

- A, C and K (item 1: 4/6, 7/6, 9/6 or 8/12; item 3: 6/100; item 4: 7, 5 or 11): fold a thirds strip into
  sixths, counting the shaded parts aloud. On a line from 0, count one whole's parts, then on past 1.
- B and E (item 1: 6/5; item 3: 8/12, 3/5 left out, or "no"; item 4: 13, 11 or 101): mark your answer and the
  given fraction on one line. Adding moves the point; cutting does not. For (ii), name 10/4 and 15/6 with
  smaller numbers.
- D and G (item 7, or a day 23 ticket saying Ben or a turned sign): a tenths strip beside a twelfths strip: more
  parts, each smaller. Say each pair as "parts of size", then read it as a sentence; the open side faces the
  greater number.
- H (item 6 "yes", or a day 23 "cannot tell"): fold two strips of different length in half; lay the halves
  together. "Half of what?" Is the trail one whole?

Students who missed nothing do [[s03-C24-1]], then [[s03-C24-2]], then check a group's fixes. Collect the
checks.

#### 10 min. Unit 4's readiness check

Alone; read each item aloud once. Until it exists, pairs read each other's items 2 and 5 instead, asking:

**Ask.** "How was each fifth cut, and what happened to the parts?" Expect: each into 2; smaller parts, the same length.

**Ask.** "Where is each fraction from 1/2, and how do you know?" Expect: 48 is under half of 100; 3 is over
half of 5.

### Other rooms

- **No projector.** Answers and group tasks on a covered part of the board; item 1's line drawn there too.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: mark from the key. No strip envelope: fold paper strips.

**Skip.** The groups, the challenges, and unit 4's readiness check (give it the next morning).

- **30 min. End-of-unit assessment.** Items 1 to 7, alone, with no strips. Read each item aloud once. Not
  reached is not wrong.
- **15 min. Go over it, one to one.** Mark items 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 together from the key; judge 2 and 5 from its
  look-fors. Fix each missed one in pen beside the first try: [[s03-E1]] by folding a thirds strip into sixths
  and counting the parts from 0; [[s03-E4]] by asking how many parts each part was cut into, top and bottom alike.

    **Say.** For [[s03-E7]]: "Read each answer aloud as a sentence. Does the open side face the greater
    fraction?" Expect: 5/6 is greater than 7/10; 3/10 is less than 1/3; 2/6 equals 4/12.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: groups ten minutes, never the check, marking or readiness check. Longer: [[s03-C24-2]] for anyone done.

### Words and supports

The defaults hold; sign cards may be placed instead of written.
