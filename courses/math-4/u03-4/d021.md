# Day 21: Same count, different parts

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Mark the E20 tickets to hand back and sort them by error: A
(3/20 and 7/20), D (3/4 < 7/10), G (sign turned round). Put the display page on the projector. Mark two strips 60 cm long:
one every 6 cm, one every 5 cm.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: an equal fraction and a benchmark

Alone, side 1: [[s03-W21-1]] and [[s03-W21-2]], on the tenths line. Then hand back the E20 tickets.

**If day 20's tickets showed many A** (3/20 and 7/20, one number kept): before Example 2, draw a line from 0 to 1
in fourths, cut each fourth into 5, and count: 3/4 = 15/20, not 3/20. Do the same for 7/10: each
tenth into 2, 14/20. Take the minute from Pull it together.

**If many D** (3/4 < 7/10): hold up the two strips, tenths over twelfths, and say four pairs aloud as "parts of
size": 1 tenth and 1 twelfth, 2 tenths and 2 twelfths, 5 tenths and 5 twelfths, 9 tenths and 9 twelfths. Take
the 2 minutes from Pull it together.

**If many G** (the sign turned round): read six comparisons aloud as sentences; the open side faces the greater
number. Take the minutes from Pull it together.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: same count, different parts

Whole class. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go. The display shows each step on its own screen.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K21-1]]). Hold the two strips up, ends together; students lay their own two strips together.
[[steps:s03-K21-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s03-K21-2]]). [[steps:s03-K21-2]]

#### 12 min. Two ways, one pair

Whole class, then pairs, with the lines still up.

**Ask.** "Both have 4 parts. Which parts are longer, tenths or twelfths?" Expect: tenths, because the same whole is
cut into fewer parts. Take "4 and 4 are the same, so they are equal" first, then point at the two lines: the counts
match, the lengths do not.

Students fill the frame for Example 3.

**Example 3** ([[s03-K21-3]]). [[steps:s03-K21-3]]

**Ask.** "Which way is quicker for this pair?" One minute of turn-and-talk in pairs, then the class. Expect: same
numerators, because only 1/3 needed renaming, by 2. Then: "When would the other way be quicker?" Expect: when one
denominator fits into the other, as 3 into 12. Do not settle on one way for every pair.

#### 6 min. Fix it: Mia's claim

Side 1 ([[s03-F21-1]]). Two minutes alone, two in pairs, then the class: the display shows Mia's working beside the
right working (error D). If many still agree with Mia: on the two lines, count 3 tenths from 0 and 3 twelfths from 0, and compare where each stops.

#### 20 min. On your own: six pairs

Alone, side 2 ([[s03-P21-1]] to [[s03-P21-6]]). Pairs 1 and 2 already have the same numerators; 3 and 4 are quicker with the numerators the same: one
fraction renamed; 5 is equal; for 6
the student says which way was quicker. A student may sketch a line in the box.

Walk with one question: "How many parts, and of what size?" Watch for:

- < on 1 or > on 2: the bigger denominator read as the bigger fraction (error D). Point to the two lines on side 1.
- 2/5 for 1/5 on 3, or 8/6 for 4/6 on 4: one number changed (error A). Ask what each fifth was cut into.
- The right renaming, then the sign turned round (G): ask them to read it aloud as a sentence.
- On 6, "thirds are bigger pieces" (error J): ask "how many parts, of what size?" for each.

At 14 minutes, show the answers on the display. Partners swap pages, read each comparison aloud as a sentence ("4
fifths is greater than 4 sixths"), and tick or fix it against the screen. If many miss 3 or 4, redo Example 2
together with 1/4 and 3/10.

Check: Does the sign match? Did you write the renamed fraction?

#### 7 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Pair 1 had the same numerators. Pair 6 was quicker in twelfths. How do you choose?" Expect: "look for a
denominator or a numerator that fits the other"; "either way gives the same sign".

**Ask.** "Why is 4 tenths more than 4 twelfths, when 12 is more than 10?" Expect: twelfths are shorter, because the
same whole is cut into more parts.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s03-X21v2]]). Read it aloud once, signs as words. Collect at the door; write results on the record
sheet under E21.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Draw the two lines on the board, the same length, one in tenths and one in twelfths; students
  copy the pairs and rule their own two lines of one length.
- **One projector.** Show one pair at a time; each student writes the renamed fractions and holds up a sign card
  before the line is shown.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at the two printed lines on side 1. No paper strips: the two printed lines.

**Skip.** The warm-up, pairs 5 and 6, and the challenge.

- **8 min. Same count.** [[s03-K21-1]], then [[s03-K21-2]]; the child fills the frames.

    **Say.** "Both have 4 parts. Which parts are longer, tenths or twelfths?" Expect: tenths; the same whole is cut
    into fewer parts.

- **8 min. Two ways.** [[s03-K21-3]]: the child renames 1/3 as sixths, then both as twenty-fourths.

    **Say.** "Which way was quicker?" Expect: sixths; only one fraction was renamed.

- **6 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F21-1]]. The child counts 3 parts from 0 on each line.
- **10 min. On your own.** [[s03-P21-1]], [[s03-P21-2]], [[s03-P21-3v2]] and [[s03-P21-4]]. The child reads each answer to you as a sentence.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X21v2]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop pair 5 and the partner reading (students read their own aloud quietly). Never drop the fix or the
renamed fractions written beside each sign. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C21-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: numerator, denominator, greater than, less than, common numerator. Read every sign as words. Frame
on the display: "__ and __ both have __ parts. __ are longer than __, so __ > __."
