# Day 19: Rename one, then compare

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit tickets and the key. Put the display page on the projector. Cut a
thirds strip and a twelfths strip of one length from card, big enough for the room to see. Strip envelopes from
section 3.1 go on desks.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: a common multiple and one half

Alone, top of side 1 ([[s03-P19-1]], [[s03-P19-2v2]]). Hand back the E18 tickets and day 18's written task (Dee's
claim) as they work. Read the answers aloud at 4 minutes. Say "12 is 3 times 4": Example 1 needs it. For the
second, each fraction goes on its own side of the 1/2 mark on the printed line. A ">" there is error D.

**If day 18's tickets showed many D** (9/10 > 7/6): the D students sit near you, and in Example 1 give Kai's
claim a full minute of turn-and-talk before step 2. **If many I** (7/8 = 11/12, "each is one part short"): after
Example 2, do Pair 1 together: 3/4 and 7/8 are each one part short, yet 6/8 < 7/8. **If many J** ("eighths are bigger, so 7/8 > 11/12"): in both examples, say each fraction as "how many
parts, of what size" before you rename.

#### 2 min. Two pairs from grade 3

Alone, side 1 ([[s03-P19-3]], [[s03-P19-4]]): readiness item 3 again, with new numbers. Hands up for each
answer. A child who circles 1/6 (error D) lays a fourths strip beside a sixths strip: more parts, each smaller.
One who circles 1/6 and missed R3 on the record sheet sits near you for Example 1; write a small 3 in their E19
cell.

#### 15 min. Teaching first: two worked examples

Whole class. Students fill the frames on side 1 as you go and cut and mark on the printed lines. The display
shows each step on its own screen. At step 4 of Example 1, hold the large thirds strip on the twelfths strip.

**Example 1** ([[s03-K19-1]]). Stop on Kai's claim.

**Ask.** After step 1: "Why can't we just compare the 2 and the 7 in 2/3 and 7/12?" Expect, in this order: "they
are different parts"; "a third is bigger than a twelfth"; "a third is 4 twelfths, so 2 thirds is 8 twelfths".
Finish on the last: it names the size.

[[steps:s03-K19-1]]

**Example 2** ([[s03-K19-2]]). [[steps:s03-K19-2]]

Write it the long way: 2/3 = 8/12 and 8/12 > 7/12, so 2/3 > 7/12.

#### 8 min. Fix it: two wrong answers

Side 2 ([[s03-F19-1]], [[s03-F19-2]]). Pairs for 5 minutes, then the class.

Rae read 9 and 12 as whole numbers (error D): ask "what size are the parts?", then cut each sixth into 2. Ivy
changed the bottom number only (error A): ask "how many tenths are in one fifth?" and count them on a line.

If many back Rae: lay the sixths strip on the twelfths strip; one sixth covers 2 twelfths.
Then say Pairs 1 and 2 aloud as "parts of size": 3 parts of size one fourth, 7 parts of size one eighth. If many
back Ivy: fold a fifths strip into tenths and count the shaded parts aloud.

#### 20 min. Four more pairs

Alone for 15 minutes, side 2 ([[s03-P19-5]], [[s03-P19-6v2]], [[s03-P19-7]], [[s03-P19-8]]): mark the fraction to
rename first, then cut, rename and mark the other; if the new name is not on the first point, the name is wrong.
Write it the long way, then the sign. Each line is printed in the parts of the fraction to rename. Strips are
allowed. Pair 4 is the one to leave if time runs short.

Then 5 minutes in pairs: partners swap and read each comparison aloud. A mismatch gets a question mark.

Check: Read it aloud. Is the greater one to the right on the line? Does the open side face it?

Watch for renaming the wrong fraction (fourths from eighths), a name with one number changed (A), and the right
renaming with the sign turned round (error G, first met today): have the child read it aloud. Pair 3 is equal,
one point with two names. Pair 4 runs past 1.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "How did you choose which fraction to rename?" Expect: "the one whose bottom number fits into the
other's"; "8 is 4 times 2, so fourths cut into eighths".

**Ask.** "Pair 3 came out equal. What does that look like on the line?" Expect: one point with two names.

Last minute: each student writes >, = and < on three scraps of card, initials on the back, into the strip
envelope. They come back on day 23.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone. The ticket ([[s03-X19]]), read aloud once. Collect it; results go under E19 on the record sheet.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** Copy the pairs on the board; students rule a line for each.
- **No strips.** Fold paper strips of one length in fourths, eighths and twelfths.

### At home

**Swap.** No projector: point at side 1. No strip envelope: fold one paper strip in fourths and one in eighths.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the grade 3 pairs, Pairs 2 and 4, and the challenge.

- **10 min. Two examples.** Read [[s03-K19-1]] together; the child cuts each third on the line into 4 and fills
  the frame. Then [[s03-K19-2]], with the folded strips.

    **Say.** "Why can't we just compare the 2 and the 7 in 2/3 and 7/12?" Expect: thirds and twelfths are different sizes; 2
    thirds is 8 twelfths.

- **8 min. Fix it.** [[s03-F19-1]], then [[s03-F19-2]]. The child says what went wrong, then does it right.
- **12 min. Two pairs.** [[s03-P19-5]] and [[s03-P19-7]]: rename one, mark both, write the sign.

    **Say.** "Read it to me as a sentence. Which point is on the right?" Expect: "three fourths is less than
    seven eighths"; 7/8 is on the right.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s03-X19]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop the second **Ask.** in "Pull it together" and Pair 4. Never drop the fixes, the printed lines or
the comparison written the long way. Longer: the challenge ([[s03-C19-1]]).

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: greater than, less than, equal to, twelfths, rename, each beside a marked line. Read every sign
as words. Frame, said aloud in the pair check: "__ and __ are both __ths now. __ is more than __, so __ is greater
than __." Access: a sign card (>, =, <) may be placed instead of written, and a comparison may be said aloud.

### Tomorrow

On the ticket, (a) > or (b) < with no renaming (error D): day 20's task shows day 19's wrong example again, 7/12
beside 8/12 on a twelfths line. 1/2 = 1/12, or 3/2 = 3/8 (error A): day 20 cuts the twelfths line at its half,
1/2 = 6/12. The right renaming with the sign turned round (error G): day 20 reads E19's answers aloud as
sentences, in the task.
