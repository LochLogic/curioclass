# Day 73: Line them up, carry, and check

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print everything on the print line. Mark the E72 tickets and sort
them: A (a carry lost), D (a two-digit sum in one column), C ("a 1", or "one ten"). Count who missed
readiness items 1 and 2. Write place names over six columns on the board, hundred thousands to ones, and the
warm-up.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: a sum and three facts

Alone: [[s09-P73-11]], [[s09-P73-12]], [[s09-P73-13]], [[s09-P73-14v2]]; the display shows the answers at 4 minutes.
If only a few missed readiness items 1 or 2, sit with them now: 467 + 285 in expanded form on a place value chart, then in columns;
then 306,042 read by its comma groups.

Check: the four answers are on the screen.

#### 5 min. Readiness reteach, or E72 back

Whole class. **If many missed readiness items 1 and 2:** the reteach above, with everyone; the E72 tickets go
back when pencils go down, a circle on any lost carry, and the C line below still runs. Otherwise hand back the
day 72 tickets now.

**If day 72's tickets showed many A or D** (a carry lost, or "613134"): put day 72's fix (3,746 + 2,538) back up,
shown as 6,274 and as "512714", beside 6,284. Say each carry by its value: "14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones."

**If many C** ("a 1", or "one ten"): students name every carry by its value, aloud, all day.

If neither: one student says what each small 1 in
their E72 sum is worth. Students whose E72 lost every carry sit near you for set 1.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: different lengths

Whole class. No expanded form today. Students fill side 1 as you go.

**Example** ([[s09-K73-1]]).
[[steps:s09-K73-1]]

Say both numbers first: 947 has no thousands. Then say what the estimate cannot do: 7,222, a lost small 1, is
close to 7,200 too. The estimate catches a wrong place; the columns catch a lost 1.

#### 8 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, side 2, on the column sheet: [[s09-P73-1]], [[s09-P73-2]], [[s09-P73-3]]. Walk with one question:
"One what?" at any small 1. Watch for:

- 13,257 on problem 1: lined up from the left (error B). Ask for both numbers aloud.
- 13,131 on problem 3: the last small 1 lost (error E). Ask: does the estimate pass 100,000?
- A sum short by 10, 100 or 1,000: a carry lost (error A). Ask what that column made.

After each set, the display shows its answers; students mark their own in pen and count the right-first-try
sums.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Did you write every small 1?

Take the place names off the board now.

#### 7 min. Set 2: place names down

Alone, on grid paper with no place names: [[s09-P73-4]], [[s09-P73-5]], [[s09-P73-6]]. Problem 5 puts the shorter number first. If many
lined up from the left in set 1, say problem 4's numbers aloud together first.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Count your right-first-try sums.

#### 7 min. Set 3: one more set

Alone, on the grid paper: [[s09-P73-7]], [[s09-P73-8]], [[s09-P73-9]]; problem 8 is for fast finishers.
Problem 9 carries in five columns in a row. If many miss it, work it together, one column at a time.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Compare your count with set 1's count, not a friend's.

#### 6 min. Fix it: Kai and Mia

Turn back to side 1 ([[s09-F73-1v2]], [[s09-F73-2]]). Two minutes alone on Kai's; then the display shows Kai's
working beside the right working. The same for Mia's. Each estimate does the catching: about 5,700 is far from
14,388, and 965 has no thousands; about 112,000 passes 100,000. If many miss Mia's: "11 ten thousands is 1 hundred thousand and 1
ten thousand," said aloud.

#### 7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 ([[s09-W73]]). A student may say it first, then write. Collect the student pages at the door;
read the task tonight against its look-fors on the key.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

**Ask.** "Without adding, how do you know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" Expect: "947 is under 1,000, so the sum
is under 8,000"; "lined up from the left, the 9 goes under the thousands, but 947 has no thousands".

**Ask.** "Problem 9 carried in five columns in a row. What happened the same way every time?" Expect: "ten of
one place made one of the next"; "ten hundreds made a thousand, ten thousands made a ten thousand". If no one
says the first, put two of the columns side by side.

#### 5 min. Exit ticket

Alone, on paper ([[s09-X73]]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the
record sheet under E73. The estimate is half the question.

### Other rooms

### At home

**Skip.** The warm-up, set 3, the written task and the challenge.

    **Say.** "Without adding, how do you know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" Expect: 947 is under 1,000, so the
    sum is under 8,000.

- **10 min. Set 1.** [[s09-P73-1]], [[s09-P73-2]], [[s09-P73-3]], with the place names written over the columns.
  The child says an estimate aloud before each sum, then checks the sum against it.
- **10 min. Set 2.** [[s09-P73-4]], [[s09-P73-5]], [[s09-P73-6]], with no place names.
- **6 min. Fix it.** [[s09-F73-1v2]], then [[s09-F73-2]].

    **Say.** "Mia got 12,322. Should the sum pass 100,000?" Expect: yes, about 112,000, so a small 1 was
    lost; 112,322.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** The child answers [[s09-X73]] alone. The estimate is part of the answer.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 3 and the second Ask; never the estimates, the fixes or the exit. Longer: the challenge,
[[s09-C73-1]], on grid paper: three numbers, so a column may carry 2.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, estimate, regroup, and the place names to hundred thousands.
Frame: "__ ones is __ ten and __ ones." Large-square grid paper or typed columns for a student who cannot
write small digits.

estimate. If many in sets 1 and 2 lost the last small 1 into a new place (13,131, error E), day 74 shows E's
instead. On a ticket short by 10, 100 or 1,000, or with no estimate, circle the place before it goes back. The written task goes back with the E73 tickets on day 74.
