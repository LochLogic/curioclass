# Day 73: Line them up, carry, and check

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the student page both sides, the exit slips, the column sheets and the key. Mark the E72 slips and sort
them: A (a carry lost), D (a two-digit sum in one column), C (the small 1 called "a 1"). Count who missed
readiness items 1 and 2. Write place names over four columns on the board, and the warm-up.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up: a sum and three facts

Alone, then whole class: [[s09-P73-11]], [[s09-P73-12]], [[s09-P73-13]], [[s09-P73-14]]. If only a few missed
readiness items 1 or 2, sit with them now: 467 + 285 in expanded form on a place value chart, then in columns;
then 306,042 read by its comma groups.

#### 5 min. Readiness reteach, or E72 back

Whole class. **If many missed readiness items 1 and 2:** the reteach above, with everyone; the E72 slips go
back at the door. Otherwise hand back the day 72 slips now.

**If day 72's slips showed many A or D** (a carry lost, or "613712"): put day 72's fix back up, 3,746 + 2,538
shown as 6,274 and as "512714", beside 6,284. Say each carry by its value: "14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones."

**If many C** ("a 1", or "one hundred"): students name every carry by its value, aloud, all day.

If both: talk E72's error through inside today's worked sum. If neither: one student says what each small 1 in
their E72 sum is worth. Students whose E72 lost every carry sit near you for set 1.

#### 5 min. Teaching first: different lengths

Whole class. Expanded form is gone from today. Students fill side 1's estimate and columns as you go, not
ahead of you.

**Example** ([[s09-K73-1]]).
[[steps:s09-K73-1]]

Saying both numbers first lets students hear that 947 has no thousands.

#### 8 min. Set 1: place names up

Alone, side 2, on the column sheet: [[s09-P73-1]], [[s09-P73-2]], [[s09-P73-3]]. An estimate first, every time. Walk with one question:
"One what?" at any small 1. Watch for:

- 13,257 on problem 1: lined up from the left (error B). Ask for both numbers aloud.
- 13,131 on problem 3: the last small 1 lost (error E). Ask: does the estimate pass 100,000?
- A sum short by 10, 100 or 1,000: a carry lost (error A). Ask what that column made.

Then the display shows set 1's answers. Students mark their own in pen and count the right-first-try sums.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Fix any column in pen.

Take the place names off the board now.

#### 7 min. Set 2: place names down

Alone, on grid paper with no place names: [[s09-P73-4]], [[s09-P73-5]], [[s09-P73-6]]. Problem 5 puts the shorter number first. If many
lined up from the left in set 1, say problem 4's numbers aloud together first.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Count your right-first-try sums.

#### 7 min. Set 3: one more set

Alone: [[s09-P73-7]], [[s09-P73-8]], [[s09-P73-9]]. Problem 9 carries in five columns in a row. If many miss it, work it together, one
column at a time, before the check.

Check: Is your sum close to your estimate? Compare your count with set 1's count, not a friend's.

#### 6 min. Fix it: Kai and Mia

Side 1 ([[s09-F73-1]], [[s09-F73-2]]). Two minutes alone on Kai's, an estimate first; then the display shows
Kai's working beside the right working. The same for Mia's. Each estimate does the catching: about 7,200 is far
from 15,755, and about 112,000 passes 100,000. If many miss Mia's: "11 ten thousands is 1 hundred thousand and 1
ten thousand," said aloud.

#### 7 min. Written task

Alone, side 2 ([[s09-W73]]). A student may say it first, then write. Collect the student pages at the door;
read the task tonight against its look-fors on the key.

#### 5 min. Pull it together

Whole class.

**Ask.** "Without adding, how do you know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" Expect: "947 is under 1,000, so the sum
is under 8,000"; "Kai put the 9 under the thousands, but 947 has no thousands". Show the estimate answer first.

**Ask.** "Why do expanded form and the standard algorithm give the same sum?" Expect: "the columns are the
places"; "the small 1 is the ten we traded, written short". If no one says the second, show day 72's two methods side by side.

#### 5 min. Exit: the slip

Alone, on paper ([[s09-X73]]). Read it aloud once if a student asks. Collect at the door; write results on the
record sheet under E73. The estimate is half the question.

### Other rooms

- **No printer.** The sums on the board, a set at a time; lined paper turned sideways, place names for set 1
  only.
- **One child at home.** Six of the nine sums, an estimate before each, said aloud. Once they are right, stop
  explaining; keep the count.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: drop set 3 and the second Ask; never the estimates, the fixes or the exit. Longer: the challenge,
[[s09-C73-1]]: three numbers, so a column may carry 2.

### Words and supports

Word Wall cards: sum, estimate, regroup, and the place names to hundred thousands. "Carry" is welcome too.
Frame: "__ ones is __ ten and __ ones." Large-square grid paper or typed columns for a student who cannot
write small digits.

### Tomorrow

On the slip, 819,627 (error B: lined up from the left): day 74's E73 back shows B's wrong example beside its
estimate. If many in sets 1 and 2 lost the last small 1 into a new place (13,131, error E), day 74 shows E's
instead. On a slip short by 10, 100 or 1,000, or with no estimate, circle the place before it goes back. The written task goes back with the E73 slips on day 74.
