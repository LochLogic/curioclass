# Section 5.3 opener: a nation of immigrants, and who it leaves out

## What the section does

Five days, 63 to 67, of 35 minutes. The unit asks: why do people leave home for a new place? Day 63 teaches the phrase "a nation of immigrants": many families here have a story of coming from another country, at many times, and families arrive today. Day 64 teaches the honest limits of the phrase: Native nations were here first and are not immigrants, and Africans who were enslaved did not choose to come. Day 65 turns to Ellis Island and perspective: what newcomers said about arriving, and two dates, the move and the telling. Day 66 tells Angel Island plainly: a law kept most Chinese people out, and some newcomers were held for weeks and months. Day 67 applies the work: what a newcomer long ago and a newcomer told more recently each said about arriving, alike and different.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a repair, then teaching, a part for pairs, the rotating six heard at the teacher's table, an exit ticket and a question-board turn. The teacher reads every card aloud. Children read labels of one to three words. No child is asked about their own family, home, money, religion or how they came here. Quiet drawing is open to any child.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **An immigrant comes from another country to live.** A visitor goes home. A person born here is neither. One family is an example; the phrase is about many families.
- **The phrase describes some arrivals and not others.** Everyone belongs; the question is how people arrived. Native nations are named by their own names and are living nations today. Being forced west was removal, a decision of the United States government, and it does not make the Cherokee people immigrants.
- **Perspective.** What a person hoped, wanted or felt, in their own telling, with who made it and when it was told. How a person traveled is a different question.
- **Two dates.** The move and the telling are two dates. Anna arrived in 1920 and told it in 1991. Smith's account was printed in 1798, which is when it was written down.
- **Alike and different.** A newcomer long ago beside one told more recently. It is not a claim about all newcomers.
- **Angel Island.** A law, the Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882, kept most Chinese people out. Chinese newcomers were held on Angel Island longer than others. Being held was not a choice the traveler made.

## The errors, in plain words

Pip says each wrong idea once and the right idea is said beside it: a visitor or a person born here is an immigrant (P, day 63); they chose because they got on a ship (C, day 63); the arrow runs the other way (I, day 63); the Cherokee became immigrants when they went west (D, day 64); everyone came here because they chose to (J, day 64); the Cherokee are gone (M, day 64); Anna came by boat, so that is how she felt (E, days 65 to 67); everybody felt the same about moving (L, days 65 to 67); Lin chose to be held (N, day 66).

## What stays held back

The exit cards for days 63 to 67 (the Wei Card, the Keetoowah Card with its Removal Caption, the Cookie Card with the Newcomer Card, the Lin Card and the two Arrival Voices paragraphs) are printed only on their days. The Antin cards, the Example Cards and the Present Card are held for the day 70 packet.
