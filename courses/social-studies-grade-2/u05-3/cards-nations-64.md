# Nation Cards, day 64, and the Homeland and Removal Map

Real nations, each a short summary in our words of that nation's own page, with a credit line and a date read. The teacher reads each once. Each nation is named by its own name and is here today. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## Eastern Band card

Credit: "Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians, official website, and National Park Service, Great Smoky Mountains National Park, read November 2026. Summary by Curio Class."
**Card:** "The Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians says it is a sovereign nation, with its own laws, elections and government. Its homeland is in the Southern Appalachian region, in western North Carolina. About 11,000 members live on the Qualla Boundary. Some Cherokee people stayed and rebuilt in their North Carolina homeland."

## Chickasaw card

Credit: "Chickasaw Nation, Our Nation, History page, read November 2026. Summary by Curio Class."
**Card:** "The Chickasaw Nation's roots are in what is now Mississippi, Kentucky, Alabama and Tennessee. A treaty in 1837 called for the Chickasaw to move to Indian Territory. The Chickasaw moved during the Great Removal, on the Trail of Tears. The nation formed its own government in 1856 and wrote a new constitution in 1983. Its capital is Tishomingo, Oklahoma."

## Homeland and Removal Map (ours)

**Picture:** The Southeast and the middle of North America as a plain outline, with a coast only. Two shaded homelands, one in the Southern Appalachian mountains and one in the area of Mississippi and Alabama. An arrow west from each to Oklahoma. A star at the Qualla Boundary in North Carolina and a star at Tishomingo in Oklahoma. A line at the top: "As of November 2026."

## Teaching words (8 minutes)

"An immigrant comes from another country to live. A nation that was here first did not immigrate. Being forced west was removal, a decision of the United States government." Pip says D, then M.

## The limits of the phrase (7 minutes)

Anna's card (she chose to come) beside the Smith Life card (brought by force). "The phrase describes ___; it does not describe ___." Everyone belongs; this is about how they arrived. Pip says J.
