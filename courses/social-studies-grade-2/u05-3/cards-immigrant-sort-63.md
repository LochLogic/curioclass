# Six sorting cards, day 63

Every invented card prints "invented for practice". The teacher says aloud that Kit, Lark, Moss, Wren, Finn and the family on the last card are made up. One actor in each. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## Two real examples (taught, 8 minutes)

- **Anna** (the Anna Arrival card from day 58, with its credit line): she came from Europe to North America in 1920.
- **Catherine** (credit line: "Catherine Hannon English, interview EI-116a, 17 November 1991, Ellis Island Oral History Collection, National Park Service. Summary by Curio Class."): "Catherine came from Poland to New York in 1920 when she was ten." Poland is in Europe, so the arrow runs Europe to North America like Anna's.

Each gets the three questions and an arrow on the continent map. The teacher says: "Both cards are from Europe. Families came from every continent. Our invented cards show other continents."

## The words (6 minutes)

Immigrant: a person who comes from another country to live. Visitor: a person who comes and goes home. Born here: a person who was born in this country. The phrase "a nation of immigrants": many families here have a story of coming from another country, at many times, and families arrive today. One family is an example; the phrase is about many.

## Six sorting cards (pairs, 9 minutes)

Sort under immigrant, visitor and born here.
1. **Kit**, from Africa, comes to study and stays to live.
2. **Lark**, from South America, arrived last year to live.
3. **Moss**, from Europe, came long ago to live.
4. **Wren**, from Asia, visits for a week and goes home.
5. **Finn** was born here.
6. **A family** who arrived last month to live. (This card shows "still".)

The six are heard (about 4.5 minutes), one ask each.

Immigrant: Kit, Lark, Moss, the family. Visitor: Wren. Born here: Finn. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
