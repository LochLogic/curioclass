# Day 12: North America, labeled from a word bank

## Teacher page

### Before class

Choose today's six: group 2. Count day 10's misses (error G) and day 11's (error K). Key: key-exits-9-12.md.

### The day

#### 9 min. Return and repair

Two parts: error G, 5 minutes; error K, 4. Teach the one more children missed to the class; a small group takes the other in Pairs work.

Error G (days 9 and 10): lay the globe beside a flat card. Trace Australia on each with a finger. **Ask.** What changed? Expect: the shape or the size. Say: "Any flat map changes something."

Error K (day 11): two job cards (made up), a ship holding its course and two sizes to compare. Pairs match each to a card. Say: "This map suits the job because ___." Expect: Mercator for the ship, Peters for sizes. The ship's straight line is not always the shortest way.

#### 8 min. Model: North America

Show the model outline (made up): a title, a key and a rose. Key: a wavy line is a river; triangles are a mountain range; ripples are an ocean or a lake. Review: in section 1.1 a key told us what each symbol stands for and a rose showed north.

Model, pointing at the key each time: the United States, Canada and Mexico at three country arrows; the Pacific Ocean on the ripples; the Rocky Mountains on the triangles (read 2026-11-20).

Review: continent, country. Pip says: "North America is a country." Beside it: "North America is a continent with several countries." Say: this map shows some countries and waters, not every one. Native nations live across North America today; the map does not say the land was empty.

#### 12 min. Pairs work

Pairs hold Pip's labeled outline (made up). One country arrow has the whole continent's name; the river is written on the triangles. Minutes 0 to 5: a small group takes the other repair at the table.

Minutes 0 to 8: pairs use the key to say what each label should be, and write the right word from the bank for one (s01-P12-1). It stays in the folder.

Minutes 8 to 11: the six, 30 seconds each: answer (s01-X12b), pointing at the key. A child may pass or point.

Three looks: each label sits at the feature it names; each is a fitting kind; no country label covers the whole continent.

#### 6 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 write on a fresh outline (s01-X12): five labels, one country, one ocean, the Great Lakes, one river and one range. A label modeled today may be chosen. Write 5 minutes; collect and tick the record sheet 1. A continent written at the country arrow is error H; a river on the triangles is error A.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No sheet: draw a rough outline and four arrows.

**Skip.** The repairs.

- **8 min. The key.** Draw a wavy line, triangles and ripples.
  **Say.** "What does each one stand for?" Expect: a river, a mountain range, an ocean or lake.
- **8 min. A bank.** Write six words on scraps: Canada, Pacific Ocean, Rocky Mountains, Brazil, North America, Mississippi River.
  **Say.** "Which are a country? A range?" Expect: Canada and Brazil; the Rocky Mountains.
- **9 min. Fix it.** [[s01-P12-1]] Your child finds the wrong label and writes the right one.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X12]] Your child writes alone.
  **Say.** [[s01-X12b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: three labels. Longer: a child writes a label for a river that is not in the bank.

### Words and supports

Words: continent, country, ocean, river, mountains, key, rose, bank. Frame: "The key says ___ stands for ___." Pointing counts; the teacher writes a word a child points to.

### Tomorrow

Day 13 opens with five minutes on error H, a continent written as a country: a country card beside a continent card, and children say which holds the other.
