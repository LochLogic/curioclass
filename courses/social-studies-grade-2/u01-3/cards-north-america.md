# North America, labelled from a word bank (day 12)

Say first: "We drew this outline for our class from the real shape of the land." Plain black line drawing on white.

## The model outline (the teacher's worked example)

**Picture:** A simple outline of North America with a title, a key and a compass rose. The key: a wavy line is a river; triangles are a mountain range; ripples are an ocean or a lake. The three countries are shown by thin borders only, with no names printed. The model labels from the word bank: the United States, Canada, Mexico, the Pacific Ocean and the Rocky Mountains.

**Word bank (fourteen words):** Canada, United States, Mexico, Pacific Ocean, Atlantic Ocean, Arctic Ocean, Great Lakes, Mississippi River, St. Lawrence River, Rocky Mountains, Appalachian Mountains, North America, Amazon River, Brazil.

## Pip's labelled outline (day 12 pairs work)

**Picture:** The same outline, key and rose, with five labels written by Pip. One country arrow carries "North America" (the whole continent, not a country). A river label, "Mississippi River", is written on the triangles (the mountain range) and the wavy line carries no label. Say first: "This is made up for practice." Pairs say what the key says, then write the right bank word for one wrong label.

## The facts behind the labels (teacher page; each read 2026-11-20)

North America is a continent with several countries, bordered by the Arctic Ocean, the Atlantic Ocean and the Pacific Ocean. The Great Lakes lie along the Canada and United States border and drain by the St. Lawrence River to the Atlantic. The Mississippi River flows from Minnesota to the Gulf of Mexico. The Rocky Mountains run from Canada into the United States; the Appalachian Mountains run through eastern North America. Amazon River and Brazil are in the bank on purpose and are not in North America.

## For the teacher

Pip says North America is a country; beside it, North America is a continent with several countries. The map shows selected countries and waters, not every country. Native nations live across North America today; this map shows countries and waters only and does not say the land was empty. No one is asked to recall these from memory: the word bank is always on the page.
