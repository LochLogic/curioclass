# Day 10: The Mercator map

## Teacher page

### Before class

Choose today's six: group 5. Set the globe beside the Mercator card and the opened shell. Cut Greenland and Africa from the Peters card, so each keeps its true size and bends its shape. Key: key-exits-9-12.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up and repair

Error Q (from day 9, s01-X9b): lay the intact globe beside the separated shell strips. Children point to which has gaps. Say: "The globe stays whole; the paper model opens."

#### 14 min. The Mercator map

Review: on day 1 this was Card 1. Its name is the Mercator map. You say Mercator; children say "the map that keeps small shapes." Name the equator: the line round the middle of the globe, the dotted line on the card.

Small shapes near us look right. Lands far from the equator are drawn too big (USGS, read 2026-11-22). Lay the Greenland cut-out on the drawn Greenland near the top. Say: "true size, bent shape."

**Ask.** What do you see? Expect: the paper is much smaller than the drawing.

Lay the Africa cut-out on the drawn Africa near the middle: it nearly fits.

**Ask.** Which is bigger on the globe, Greenland or Africa? Expect: Africa, much bigger. Say: "On this map Greenland looks as big as Africa; on the globe Africa is much bigger."

Pip says: "A flat map keeps everything the globe does." Beside it: "Any flat map changes something; this one stretches lands far from the middle."

#### 14 min. Pairs work

Minutes 0 to 4: pairs lay both cut-outs on their Mercator card and say what each does.

Minutes 4 to 8: the island pairs card (made up): Moss Island near the bottom edge looks as wide as Tern Island near the middle. **Ask.** Where could you look to find out which island is really bigger? Expect: the globe, or the true-size cut-outs (s01-P10-1). It stays in the folder.

Minutes 7 to 8: circulate: pairs say "true size, bent shape"; no pair trusts the card alone.

Minutes 8 to 12: the six, 40 seconds each: mark the exit card (s01-X10), then answer (s01-X10b) and (s01-X10c). A child may pass or point.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 mark the card. Read aloud: "Does the drawing prove the top lake is bigger? Circle yes or no." Mark 1 minute; collect and tick 1. Yes is error G.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No cut-outs or globe: use any printed world map and a globe picture.

**Skip.** The island card.

- **8 min. Two lands.** Find Greenland and Africa on a globe or picture.
  **Say.** "Which is bigger on the globe?" Expect: Africa, much bigger.
- **8 min. Stretched.** Draw two circles of one size, one near the middle of a page and one near the top; stretch the top one wide.
  **Say.** "Which one was stretched?" Expect: the top one.
- **9 min. Look.** [[s01-P10-1]] Your child says where to look to find which island is really bigger.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X10]] Your child marks the card alone.
  **Say.** [[s01-X10b]] Expect: it stretches lands far from the middle. Then [[s01-X10c]]: it keeps small shapes right.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one cut-out. Longer: a child draws a map stretched toward the top and says what it keeps.

### Words and supports

Words: Mercator, equator, stretch, true size, bent shape. Frame: "Far from the middle, lands look too ___." Pointing counts.
