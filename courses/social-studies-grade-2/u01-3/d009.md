# Day 9: A round Earth on flat paper

## Teacher page

### Before class

Choose today's six: group 4. Set the globe beside World Projection Card 1. Print the paper shell. Count day 8's misses (errors E, J, M, F, L). Key: key-exits-9-12.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Day 8's exit (the last day of section 1.2) gives the most common error. Teach it to the class; a few children go to the table in Pairs work. Errors E, J, M: sort three credit cards again. Errors F, L: tick the proof on the caption repair card.

#### 11 min. Hook and shell

Review: in kindergarten you found land and water on a globe and a flat map, and said each "stands for" the Earth. Show the globe beside Card 1.

**Ask.** Can this round skin lie flat? Expect: not as it is. New word: projection, a way to draw the round Earth on flat paper.

Tear the printed paper shell along its line and lay it flat: gaps open between strips, or the paper must stretch. If you have a fruit peel, peel it whole yourself and lay it flat: it splits. No child handles it.

**Ask.** What would we have to do to a round skin to make it lie flat? Expect: stretch it, fold it or open it.

Pip, holding the opened shell, says: "The Earth gets gaps when you open it." Beside it: "The globe stays whole; the paper model opens." Say: "Any flat map changes something."

#### 14 min. Pairs work

A paper cup a pair. Press it flat with your palms; say what happens (it wrinkles, folds or tears); say the frame "To lie flat, the skin must ___." (s01-P9-1). It stays in the folder.

Minutes 0 to 4: a few children from the day 8 repair take that error at the table.

Minutes 4 to 10: circulate with two looks: each pair says what the cup did; the frame holds a verb, not "stay."

Minutes 10 to 13: the six, 30 seconds each: mark the exit card (s01-X9), then answer (s01-X9b). A child may pass or point.

#### 2 min. Question board

Add: "Can a map ever show the world just as it is?" Write the class's first answer in its own words; it is not final.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 mark the card. Read aloud: "Circle the one that can lie flat with no gap and no stretch." Mark 1 minute; collect 1; tick the record sheet 1. The balloon or bowl circled is error G; "Earth gets holes" is error Q.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No cup: lay a sheet of paper over a ball and press it flat.

**Skip.** The question about the world.

- **8 min. A round skin.** Press a sheet of paper over a ball.
  **Say.** "What happens?" Expect: it folds, wrinkles or tears.
- **8 min. The sentence.** [[s01-P9-1]] Your child finishes the sentence.
- **9 min. A map.** Look at a globe or a flat world map together.
  **Say.** "Can a map show the world just as it is?" Expect: any first idea; it will change.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X9]] Your child marks the card alone.
  **Say.** [[s01-X9b]] Expect: stretch, fold or open.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: tear the shell and skip the peel. Longer: a pair presses a paper ball flat, then a cup, and compares.

### Words and supports

Words: round, flat, stretch, fold, tear, globe, projection. Frame: "To lie flat, the skin must ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Day 10 teaches the Mercator map. If many gave error Q ("Earth gets holes"), it opens with an intact globe beside separated strips; error G goes to day 12.
