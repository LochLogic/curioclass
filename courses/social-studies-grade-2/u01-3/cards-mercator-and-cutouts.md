# The Mercator card, the cut-outs and the island pairs card (days 10 and 11)

Say first: "We drew these for our class from the real shapes of the lands." The Mercator and Peters cards are the World Projection Cards of day 1, now named. Coastlines are from Natural Earth, public domain.

## The cut-outs (day 10, and day 11)

Cut the land shapes from the Peters card, which keeps sizes, so each paper keeps its true size but its shape is bent: Greenland and Africa. Say aloud: "true size, bent shape." Lay Greenland on the Mercator card, where it looks about as big as Africa; lay it on the Peters card, where it fits. Africa is much bigger than Greenland on the globe (children say much bigger; they see it by laying the cut-outs on the globe, no number).

## Pairs card: two invented islands

**Picture:** A small Mercator-style card with a compass rose and two islands, both made up. Near the bottom edge, Moss Island looks as wide as Tern Island, which is drawn near the middle. The teacher's note says Tern Island is much larger in truth.

**Pairs ask:** "Where could you look to find out which island is really bigger?" Answer: the globe, which keeps true sizes (s01-P10-1). The pairs do not circle yes or no on a card: the exit asks that on a new card.

## For the teacher

Name the equator as the line round the middle of the globe. Keep Mercator and Peters as names the teacher says; children say the map that keeps small shapes and the map that keeps sizes. Pip says the Mercator map is useless; beside it, it keeps small shapes and lets a ship hold one compass direction, but it is the wrong map for comparing sizes. A line of one compass direction is not always the shortest route.
