# Day 11: The Peters map and choosing a map

## Teacher page

### Before class

Choose today's six: group 1. Day 10 taught the Mercator map. Set the globe between the Mercator card and the Peters card. Key: key-exits-9-12.md.

### The day

#### 3 min. Warm-up

**Ask.** Which card draws Greenland too big? Expect: the Mercator card. **Ask.** What is true on the globe? Expect: Africa is much bigger.

#### 12 min. The Peters map

Card 2 is the Peters map. You say Peters; children say "the map that keeps sizes." Lay the Greenland cut-out on it: it fits. So does Africa.

Review: in kindergarten you said "Both ___, but ___" of two maps. Say: "Both show the whole world, but the Peters map keeps sizes and stretches shapes, and the Mercator map keeps small shapes and draws lands far from the middle too big." Children say it.

Say: on the Mercator map a ship holding one compass direction draws a straight line, so it suits sailing a course.

Read the credit under each card with the three-question card: "Drawn by Curio Class, 2026, made in a school office. Coastlines from Natural Earth, public domain." **Ask.** Who made it? Expect: Curio Class; Natural Earth is the data, not the maker. When and where: 2026, a school office.

#### 7 min. Claim, evidence, because

Review: in grade 1 you learned claim, evidence, because; evidence is what we saw or read that shows it. Model, with the frame: "I think the Peters map is best for comparing sizes because Greenland's cut-out fits it and the sizes match the globe."

#### 8 min. Pairs work

Partners try a different job: a ship holds one compass direction. Each says "I think the ___ map is best for ___ because ___." (s01-P11-1). It stays in the folder.

Minutes 0 to 4: partners say the frame; circulate with two looks: a map named; a reason about the job, not the looks.

Minutes 4 to 7: the six, 30 seconds each: mark the exit card (s01-X11), then answer (s01-X11b). A child may pass or point.

#### 2 min. Question board

**Ask.** Can a map show the world just as it is? Expect the class's revised answer: no flat map keeps everything; the best map depends on the job. Write it in their words.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 mark the card. Read aloud: "Circle the map that keeps the shapes of small places near us right." Mark 1 minute; collect 1; tick the record sheet 1. The other card circled because the first is useless is error K.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw two world outlines, one with the top lands stretched wide.

**Skip.** The credit lines.

- **8 min. Both.** Look at the two cards.
  **Say.** "Both show the whole world, but ___." Expect: one keeps sizes, one keeps small shapes.
- **8 min. Who made it.** Read a credit line together.
  **Say.** "Who made it? When? Where?" Expect: the maker, the year and the place in the line.
- **9 min. A job.** [[s01-P11-1]] Your child says which map suits a ship holding one direction, and why.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X11]] Your child marks the card alone.
  **Say.** [[s01-X11b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip the credit reading. Longer: a child says what the Peters card bends that the Mercator card keeps.

### Words and supports

Words: Peters, Mercator, size, shape, claim, evidence. Frames: "Both ___, but ___." "I think the ___ map is best for ___ because ___." Pointing counts.
