# Section 6.2 opener: a telling as a source, what people bring, and how a place and its people change

## What the section does

Three days, 76 to 78, of 35 to 37 minutes. Day 76 reads the visitor's account of day 74 as a source: what it says, what it cannot tell, who knew it firsthand, and where its dates go on a strip, with an "I do not know when" box for a date nobody gave. Day 77 teaches what people bring when they move: in a bag and in a person, and what a song or a words class shows. Day 78 teaches that a move changes the newcomers and the place, and that newcomers, long-time residents and Native nations each add something. The Posadas Card comes back on day 78, a Cherokee Nation card (Language Today) and the local nation's own page are the worked examples, and then the class's own town is the community. The unit's one shared goal comes from the cards, never from a child's family.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a short repair, then teaching, a part for pairs or tables where the rotating six are heard (about 45 seconds each), an exit ticket and a question-board turn. The teacher reads every card aloud. Children read labels. Real tellings are summaries in our words with one credit line. Everything else is invented, printed "invented for practice", and said aloud to be made up. No child reports on home, family, money, religion or how they came here, and any child may pass.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A memory shows what one teller said and leaves the rest unknown.** The source says one thing and cannot tell another: how everyone felt, what neighbors said. A date nobody gave goes in the box, not between years.
- **A copy is not a second teller.** Who knew it firsthand?
- **Culture moves in people, not only in bags.** A song, a rhythm or a words class travels with a person and by teaching.
- **Change goes both ways.** The newcomers change and so does the place. A card that does not say a place changed says "the card does not tell".
- **Native nations are living governments** with their own languages and schools, named by their own names, in the present tense, never an immigrant group. Belonging never depends on adding a tradition.

## The errors, in plain words

Pip says each wrong idea once with the right one beside it: "the note has no year so I will say 2015" (C); "two sheets say it, so it is true twice" (E); "the note says 2014, so she liked it here" (I); Pip puts 2021 before 2016 (J); "only things in a bag move" (A); "only the new family changes" (F); "everyone in the town does it the same way" (G); "the new family's songs are better" (K); "the nation was here long ago" (L); "only the new people have culture" (M).

## What stays held back

The exit cards of days 76 to 78 print only on their days. The local nation's page is printed by the teacher from the nation's own site.
