# Language Today card (the Cherokee Nation, worked example of day 78)

Real nation, named by its own name, in the present tense. Summary in our words from the Nation's own pages. Credit line printed under the card: "Cherokee Nation, Tahlequah, Oklahoma, pages on learning the Cherokee language and on the Nation's government. Summary by Curio Class, not an exact quotation." Nothing sacred and no language exercise is copied. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## The card (read aloud)

"The Cherokee Nation governs itself today. It is led by a Principal Chief. The Nation runs the Cherokee Immersion School in Tahlequah, from pre-K to 8th grade."

**Picture:** A map of the United States with one marked area labeled "Cherokee Nation" and a school building labeled "Cherokee Immersion School".
**Job of the card:** shows what a long-time community adds. Pip says L: "The nation was here long ago." The teacher says beside it: "The Nation's own page says it teaches its language today."

## The local nation (teacher-supplied)

The teacher finds the nation whose homeland this town is, through that nation's own government site, and prints its education or language page with the title and link before day 78. The class reads two or three sentences, named by the nation's own name, in the present tense. If no free page is found, read one sentence that the nation is a living government with its own language and schools, linked to its site. Never substitute another nation's work for the local one.
