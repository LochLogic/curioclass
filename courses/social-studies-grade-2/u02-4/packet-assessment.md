# Day 28 packet (kept back until day 28)

Read each item once, neutrally, and no more. One copy of each card for each child, laid flat, one pencil each. Do not teach, hint or confirm. No answer map is visible. Every card prints "invented for practice" in small letters, except World Map B, which uses the world outline.

## Card 1: World Map B

**Picture:** The world, Atlantic in the middle, a north arrow top left. Seven shapes lettered AA, BB, CC, DD, EE, FF, GG. Six numbered ocean shapes, 1 to 6. Marked dots: four in Asia and nearby, each a different sign: a star in the south of Asia, a square in the far north of Asia, a cross on the east coast of Asia and a triangle in the west of Asia; four in the Americas and Europe, each a different sign: a circle in the north of North America, a moon in the south of North America, a diamond in South America and a bar in Europe.
**Word bank 1:** North America, South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia, Antarctica, Egypt.
**Word bank 2:** Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern.

## Card 2: Coast and Ridge

**Picture:** An invented place. The sea on the west edge. A ridge running north to south. Tall forest on the west slope with rain falling. Scattered shrubs in the east valley, no rain. No thermometers. People on the wet slope: a potter at a wheel and a bus driver. A farmer in the dry valley with a hat.
**Caption, observations only:** A sea lies to the west. A ridge runs north to south. Over many years the western slopes have been wet and the eastern valley dry. Today rain is falling on the western slope.
**Credit line:** Drawing by Curio Class.

## Card 3: Change Pair B

**Picture:** Two pairs of panels, each pair with a small locator map above it.
Pair 1, a locator of Europe: Lake Fen, 1990 panel, a lake of four blue squares. 2020 panel, two blue squares. Printed explanation under the pair: Less rain reached the lake.
Pair 2, a locator of Asia: Tamarind Street, 2000 panel, shops and a road. 2020 panel, the same shops and road and a tram on the road.

## The six items, read aloud

**Item 1 (7 minutes).** "On World Map B, write the letter of each continent beside its name." "Circle the dot where the Himalayas lie." "Circle the dot where the Great Lakes lie."
**Item 2 (5 minutes).** "Write the number of each ocean shape beside its name." "Circle yes or no. Is the water of the Indian Ocean joined to the water of the Atlantic Ocean?" "Circle one picture that shows why oceans matter for a livable Earth: a fish home, a cloud raining over the sea, a family on a beach holiday or a sailing race."
**Item 3 (5 minutes).** On Coast and Ridge: "Circle the sentence that tells what usually happens on the west slope over many years: Rain falls on the west slope now. The west slope is wet year after year." Spoken, a third of the class at a time: "Using where the sea and the ridge lie, say why rain falls on the west slope today." "Using where the ridge lies, say why the east valley is drier." Paper: "Circle the plants that grow in the east valley: tall forest or scattered shrubs." "Circle yes or no. Does the card say the east valley is hot?"
**Item 4 (5 minutes).** Spoken, a third at a time: "Say one thing people on the wet slope might need because rain falls there often." "Say one thing a farmer in the dry valley might need because little rain falls there." Paper: "Circle what the places on the wet slope share: much rain, the same job or the same family." "Circle yes or no. Do all the people on the wet slope do the same work?" "Circle where a hiker needs the warmer coat: the ridge top or the foot of the slope."
**Item 5 (4 minutes).** On Change Pair B: "Circle the continent for the lake locator: Europe, Asia or Australia." "Circle the continent for the street locator: Asia, Africa or Europe." "Circle what changed at the lake between 1990 and 2020: the lake is smaller or the lake is bigger." "Circle what is new on the street in 2020." "Draw a box round one thing that stayed the same on the street." "Circle yes or no. Does the 2000 picture of the street show the street today?" "Circle whether the tram is a physical change or a cultural change: physical or cultural." "Circle whether the smaller lake is a physical change or a cultural change: physical or cultural." Spoken, a third at a time: "What printed words tell why the lake is smaller?"
**Item 6 (4 minutes).** "Circle the place you would choose to live in: the wet slope or the dry valley." "Say a reason for your choice that the card shows." Then record it in the frame: I choose ______ because ______. The ______ shows ______, made by ______. "Write the name of the source that shows your reason." "Write who made the Coast and Ridge card."
