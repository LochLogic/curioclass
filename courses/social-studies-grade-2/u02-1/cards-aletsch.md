# Aletsch sheet (days 17 and 24)

## Page 1: the two images

**Picture:** Two satellite images of Aletsch Glacier, in the Alps of Europe, side by side. Left: 2 September 1984. Right: 6 August 2024. Print the two images from the NASA Earth Observatory page named in sources.csv; the images are federal, credit printed with them.
**Credit line, read aloud:** NASA Earth Observatory images by Michala Garrison, using United States Geological Survey Landsat data. The two dates are a month apart, so children compare ice, not snow.

## Page 2: the reason card (day 24)

**Picture:** A plain card with a drawing of a warm sun over a glacier and a stack of snow beside it.
**Label:** Summers in the Alps have grown warmer. Warm summers melt more ice than winter snow adds.

## Key (teacher)

The glacier's end moved back more than 1,300 metres between the two images (NASA page). The cause on the reason card rests on the Swiss Glacier Bulletin 2025, page 10: the faster retreat of Alpine glaciers is mainly due to rising summer air temperatures. Tell it at grade level without alarm. The images are printed from the source and not described here; read them with a person before use.
