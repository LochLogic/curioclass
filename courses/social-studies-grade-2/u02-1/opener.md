# Section 2.1 opener: where on Earth

## What the section does

Four days, 17 to 20, of about 40 minutes. The unit asks one question to the end: where on Earth would you choose to live, and why? These four days answer the first part, where are these places? On day 17 the class names the seven continents on a flat card and on the globe and says where each lies by compass word. On day 18 it places four features of North and South America, on day 19 eight features of the other five continents, and on day 20 the five oceans and why oceans matter. Children mark a card each day; about six are heard at the globe each day, so everyone is heard about once a week. First placements are practice.

## How the days run

Day 17: a short repair from unit 1, a hook, grade 1 retrieval, the seven continents, pairs with name tags, the exit. Day 18: a repair, four features of the Americas, a practice map, the exit. Day 19: a warm-up repair, eight features, who made the picture, a practice map, the exit. Day 20: a warm-up repair, the five oceans, four Ocean Cards on why oceans matter, a practice map, the exit.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A continent is a very large land area.** Countries lie inside a continent. Africa is a continent with many countries.
- **Seven continents and five oceans.** The Southern Ocean is the water round Antarctica. All five oceans are one joined salt water; a flat map cuts the Pacific at its two edges, and the globe shows it whole.
- **A feature is placed by a compass word,** in the west of South America, not as a dot and not by any grid.
- **Oceans matter** because living things make their homes there, ships cross them, sea water rises and falls again as rain, and most of the planet's water is in the ocean.
- **Every continent holds cities, farms, homes and wild places.** No one page stands for a whole continent. Antarctica is ice and research stations.

## The errors, in plain words

Pip the puppet says each wrong idea once and the right idea is said beside it. Africa is a country (A). The Nile is in South America, or the Andes sit on the wrong side (J). The Pacific stops at the paper edge (B). Oceans matter only for holidays (C). The oceans are separate pools (L). There are four oceans (M). A land placed on the wrong side, or north and south swapped, has no letter: we say not yet seen and repair with the north arrow.

## Supplies for the section

A class globe (or a ball with the continents drawn on it), the World Outline Cards, the Feature Atlas pages and key, the Ocean Cards, the globe prompt cards, exit cards, practice maps, name tags, a record sheet and a question board. No child is asked about a home, a trip or a family.
