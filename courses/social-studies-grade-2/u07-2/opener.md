# Section 7.2 opener: gathering facts on land and water, resources and work, people today

## What the section does

Three days, 85 to 87, of 35 minutes. Each child gathers two facts a day from their own country's printed packet (map, Land and Work page, People Today page): land and water on day 85, resources and how people earn a living on day 86, people today on day 87. Each fact goes on the note page in the child's own words or a drawing, with a source sticker (the page name, Curio Class, and the office whose records the page used). Written feedback on source choices is returned on day 89 for revision.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a short repair, then a review of a model already taught (the map key and region on 85, resource and opportunity cost on 86, corroboration and "Both ___, but ___" on 87) with invented Reedmark and Oakfield, then gathering in country groups of one to four reading in pairs, an exit ticket and a question-board turn. The teacher reads aloud for at most six children a day (about a minute each) and hears the rotating six. Nothing is taught as new.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Read a symbol against its key.** A symbol means what the key says, never what it looks like.
- **Resource and product.** A resource is what comes from the land or water (reeds, wood); a product is what is made from it (a basket, a chair).
- **Two pages from one set are one teller.** A copied caption is not a second witness. Two people who each saw the same thing and agree make that part more trustworthy.
- **Some and many, not everyone.** One page about one town supports "many families go" and never "everyone does".
- **Every fact has its sticker.** A fact with no source named is H.

## The errors, in plain words

Pip says: the star means a hill because stars are bright (G, day 85); my report is only the animals (C, day 85); a basket is a resource (I, day 86); everyone in Oakfield goes to the market (E, day 87); I just wrote it, where it came from does not matter (H, day 85).

## What stays held back

The exit cards of days 85 to 87 print only on their days. The packets are planned only, not yet written: each of the sixteen needs its own checked government sources before it is printed. A class cannot gather real facts until its packet exists.
