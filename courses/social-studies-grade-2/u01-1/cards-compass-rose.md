# Compass rose maps (day 3, with the day 4 and day 5 repair cards)

Two invented maps. Say first: "We made these maps for our class. The places are made up." Each prints "invented for practice". Plain black line drawing on white, labels of one to three words.

## Map 1: Birch Camp (the teacher's worked example)

**Picture:** A camp map with the compass rose drawn pointing down the page, the arrow marked N toward the bottom edge. A cabin is drawn at the top, a fire ring in the middle and a well at the bottom, in one column. A key box: a house shape is a cabin; a ring is a fire ring; a circle with a cross is a well. Make a second copy to turn upside down.

**Teacher asks:** "Start at the fire ring and walk north. Where do you get to?" The well, because the rose points down. Then: "Which way is south?" Up, the opposite way from north. Turn the page: the rose turns with the page.

## Map 2: Quarry Hill (pairs work)

**Picture:** A hill map with a rose in the corner, arrow marked N pointing to the left edge. A gravel pit, a bench and a pond are drawn in a row from left to right. With north to the left, the gravel pit is north of the bench and the pond is south of it; nothing is east or west of the bench on this map. Each pair also gets a paper rose on a fastener (see blank-paper-rose-and-signs.md): turn it until its N arrow matches the map's.

**Pairs say:** "North is ___. The ___ is north of the ___." Then one partner asks "What is south of the bench?" and the other answers.

## Cards for the day 4 warm-up (error B repair)

Two fresh cards, each with its own rose and two places. Card 1: a bakery at the left and a post office at the right, the rose pointing right. Card 2: a kite shop at the top and a fountain at the bottom, the rose pointing down. Pairs turn the card on the desk until its rose points up, then answer: "Which place is north of the other?" Card 1: the post office is north of the bakery. Card 2: the fountain is north of the kite shop.

## For the teacher

Four directions only, no northeast. Set the wall signs by compass if the room allows; a compass needle points in the general direction of north (USGS, read 2026-11-20), and a room with steel can spoil it, so if the needle misbehaves, name one wall north and say aloud that we named it. A drawn friend or a toy says the wrong idea, never a child.
