# World Projection Cards (day 1, and again on days 9 to 12 and 14)

Two world cards and a globe. Say first, on day 1: "We drew these cards for our class from the real shapes of the lands." The coastlines come from a public domain dataset, Natural Earth (terms read 2026-11-20: public domain, free to use, credit not required). Each card carries a credit line. Plain black line drawing on white, thick coastlines, no shading, no color needed, no printed names of countries.

On day 1 the cards are called Card 1 and Card 2. They are named Mercator and Peters on days 10 and 11, not before.

## Card 1: the Mercator map

**Picture:** A rectangle with the whole world's coastlines drawn on it, centered on the Atlantic Ocean. Lands near the top and the bottom edges are stretched wide, so the island at the top left looks very large. A dotted line runs round the middle (the equator).

**Credit line (printed under the card):** Drawn by Curio Class, 2026, made in a school office. Coastlines from Natural Earth, public domain.

## Card 2: the Peters map

**Picture:** A rectangle with the same coastlines, centered on the Atlantic Ocean, in the equal-area form known as Gall-Peters. Lands look tall and narrow near the middle and flat and wide near the top and the bottom. The same dotted line runs round the middle.

**Credit line:** Drawn by Curio Class, 2026, made in a school office. Coastlines from Natural Earth, public domain.

## For the teacher

Day 1 asks: "Which would help us compare how big lands are?" Each child puts a counter in one of two cups, or passes; read the totals as numbers and do not say which is right. Use "Card 1" and "Card 2". A globe, or a printed globe net with a soft paper sphere, stays beside both cards on every day they appear. The credit lines are read with the three questions on day 11: who made it, when, where. Keep Natural Earth as the data, never as the maker. The exit cards for days 11 and 15 use differently centered cards and a different credit line.
