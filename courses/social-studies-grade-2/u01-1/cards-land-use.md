# Maps of people and the land (day 5)

Three invented maps. Say first: "We made these maps for our class. The places are made up; nobody is hurt in any of them." Each prints "invented for practice". Each has all four parts: a title, a key, a compass rose and a scale bar. Plain black line drawing on white, labels of one to three words.

## Map 1: Alder Farm (the teacher's worked example)

**Picture:** A farm map with a field, a stream, a barn and a row of trees. A ditch is drawn from the stream to the field. The key: a dashed line is a ditch; a wave is a stream; a square is a barn. A rose and a scale bar of equal boxes, one box is ten steps.

**Teacher says:** "People ___ the ___ so ___": people dug a ditch from the stream so water reaches the field.

## Map 2: Riverbend Park (pairs work, with a written label)

**Picture:** A park beside a river that bends. A walking path runs along the bank. A mowed field is drawn beside the path, and a small pond is drawn inside a low ring of earth. The key: a dotted line is a path; a circle with a wave is a pond; a grid is a field. A rose and a scale bar.

**Written label (day 5, item s01-P5-1):** write one word beside one place that people made or changed, such as path or pond, or draw what they did there. The card stays in the folder.

## Map 3: Lowfield (pairs work)

**Picture:** A flat field beside a creek. A raised bank is drawn beside the creek as a thick line with small marks, and a row of small houses sits farther from the water. The key: a thick line with marks is a raised bank; a wave is a creek; a small square is a house. No one is hurt; the made-up town is dry.

## Warm-up repair cards (item 4, land and water)

A fresh paper map of a hillside with a stream running down it. Pairs trace the stream with one color and the land beside it with another, then find a second example on Riverbend Park.

## For the teacher

Ask each pair for one way people use or change the land or water and which map part told them. Pip says "There is a creek"; beside it, "People built a raised bank beside the creek."
