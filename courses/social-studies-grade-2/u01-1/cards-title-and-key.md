# Title and key maps (day 2)

Two invented maps. Say first: "We made these maps for our class. The places are made up." Each prints "invented for practice". Plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, labels of one to three words, the key in its own box.

## Map 1: Lantern Lane Market (the teacher's worked example)

**Picture:** A small market street seen from above. Along it, stalls are drawn as stars and a pond is drawn as a wave. A key box in a corner: a star is a stall; a wave is a pond. A title strip at the top reads Lantern Lane Market. Make three copies of the same picture: the whole map, the map with the title strip hidden by a paper flap, and the map with the key box hidden by a flap.

**Teacher says:** "The title tells me this map shows a market. The key tells me a star is a stall."

## Map 2: Harvest Hill (pairs work, one copy for each pair)

**Picture:** A hill farm stand. A barn is drawn as a triangle, a haystack as a circle, a gate as a square and a water trough as a short wavy line. Three loose title cards sit beside the map: Harvest Hill, Pond Day, My Cat. Three symbols are cut out for matching: triangle, circle, wavy line. A key box lists the three words: barn, haystack, trough.

**Pairs choose** the title that fits the map and match three symbols to their key words.

## For the teacher

Trace from a symbol to the key and back, aloud, with your finger on day 2; pairs do it with no pointing on day 5. Pip says the title tells what the star means; show the right idea beside it. The answer to a symbol is always on this map's key, never from memory of another map. The day 2 repair for error N (three job cards) is in cards-job-cards.md.
