# Day 1: Which map shows the world best, and best for what?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Set the globe, the two cards, the counters and two cups on the teacher table, one counter for each child. Draw a strip on chart paper. Choose today's six: group 1 on the record sheet. Key: key-exits-1-2.md.

### The day

#### 15 min. Hook and question

Say: "We made these cards for our class; they are drawn from real coastlines." Show the globe, Card 1 and Card 2. Review: in kindergarten you put two world maps beside a globe; in grade 1 you learned a map is a flat drawing seen from above.

**Ask.** What does each one show? Expect: lands and water; the globe is round, the cards are flat. List what each card shows.

**Ask.** Which looks more like the globe to you? Each child drops a counter in cup 1 or cup 2 on the way back to the mat, or passes (30 children, 5 seconds each, 3 minutes). Read the totals as numbers; do not say which is right.

Post the big question: "Which map shows the world best, and best for what?" Review: in grade 1 a supporting question is a smaller question that helps answer the big one, and the question board holds both.

**Ask.** What do we want to find out? Write each answer on the board. If no one asks, say: "I wonder if any map can show the world just as it is."

Say: "A map is made for a job: to show where a place is, what is in it, or how things compare."

#### 3 min. Retrieval

**Ask.** What smaller question could help us choose a useful map? Expect: "What does the map show?" Hear four volunteers; anyone may pass.

#### 14 min. Start-of-year check

Not scored: pointing, drawing or an aid is an answer. Say: "We made these pictures; the places are made up, except the old map of Boulder, made in 1904 by the U.S. Geological Survey." Review: in grade 1 you learned map, key, symbol, land, water and north.

For 8 minutes read items 1 to 5 (s01-R1 to s01-R5) once each, one at a time. Note the support each child used.

For the last 6 minutes children look back over their page while the six come to your table, one at a time, about a minute each: the child marks the exit card (s01-X1), then answers (s01-X1b) and (s01-X1c). A child may pass or point.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 mark the exit card; read aloud: "Circle the map that helps you find the path to the lookout tower." Mark 1 minute; collect and tick the record sheet 1; read the board once. Review: in grade 1 you marked an exit ticket. A world map circled for size or color is error N.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No globe: use a ball. No cards: draw two flat world maps. No cups: two bowls.

**Skip.** The class vote and the start-of-year page.

- **10 min. Two maps.** Show a ball and a flat map of the world.
  **Say.** "Which would help us compare how big lands are?" Expect: any choice; do not say which is right.
- **10 min. Questions.** Say "Which map shows the world best, and best for what?" Write what your child wonders.
  **Say.** "What do you want to find out?" Expect: any question.
- **10 min. Map for a job.** Your child draws one map and says what job it is for.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]] Your child marks the day's card alone.
  **Say.** "Why does that map help?" [[s01-X1b]] Expect: a reason about the job, not size or color. Then [[s01-X1c]]: ask one smaller question for a new job.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: hear two volunteers in retrieval and read items 1, 2 and 4 now. Longer: children draw a map for a job.

### Words and supports

Words: map, globe, question, board. Frame: "This map helps because ...". Pointing, drawing and home-language answers count first.

### Tomorrow

Day 2 teaches the title and the key. If many chose by size or color (error N), or missed item 1, it repairs the more common for the class and a small group takes the other.
