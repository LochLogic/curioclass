# Day 4: Scale in whole units

## Teacher page

### Before class

Cut six paper strips for each pair, each as long as one box of the scale bar. Choose today's six: group 4 on the record sheet. Key: key-exits-3-5.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up and repair

Error B (s01-X3) is the one repair. Review: in grade 1 you learned north is where the direction indicator points, and on day 3 you met the compass rose. Pip says, "North is the top of the page." Beside it: "North is where the rose points." Pairs get a card (a bakery and a post office, or a kite shop and a fountain), turn it until its rose points up, and say which place is north of the other. Hear two pairs a card.

#### 12 min. Scale in whole units

Say: "We made these maps; the places are made up." Show Sunny Street. New word: scale, how far one length on the map stands for on the ground, counted in whole units. Review: in grade 1 math you measured by laying equal units end to end.

Point to the scale bar: a row of equal boxes, and one box is one block. Lay a strip as long as one box on the bar. Board frame: "Each scale length stands for ___." Children say it: "one block."

**Ask.** A walker went three blocks from the bakery. Lay strips along the path to where she stopped. How many strips? Expect: three.

Show Cove Road: one box is five steps. Count by fives as you lay a strip.

**Ask.** A walker went 15 steps from the dock sign. How many lengths is that? Expect: three; five, ten, fifteen.

Pip says, "Three lengths means three paces, whatever the label." Beside it: "Each length stands for what the label says; three lengths of five paces is fifteen paces."

#### 15 min. Pairs work

Six strips and two cards a pair (made up). On Card 1 lay strips along both paths, say which path is longer, then which is eight blocks. On Card 2 a walker goes 15 steps from the swing: lay strips, count by fives and stop where she stops. Partners take turns laying strips.

Minutes 0 to 4: the small group takes any child still counting marks, not units.

Minutes 5 to 11: circulate; listen for "stands for", not only a count.

Minutes 11 to 14: the six, 30 seconds each: mark the exit card (s01-X4), answer (s01-X4b). A child may pass or point.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 mark the card. Read aloud: "Circle how far the path is: 6, 9 or 18 blocks." Mark 1 minute; collect 1; add today's answer to the board 1. A strip is not a ruler. Circling 6 or 9 is error C.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No strips: fold paper to one box's length.

**Skip.** The warm-up, the pairs and the second card.

- **8 min. One block.** Draw a path four boxes long, one box a block. Lay a strip along it.
  **Say.** "Each scale length stands for ___. A walker goes three blocks: how many strips?" Expect: one block; three.
- **8 min. Fives.** Make one box five steps, the path four boxes.
  **Say.** "A walker goes 15 steps: how many lengths?" Expect: three, counted 5, 10, 15.
- **9 min. Two paths.** Draw one path five boxes and one four, a box being two blocks. Ask which is longer, and which is eight blocks.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X4]] Your child marks the card alone.
  **Say.** "What does the scale tell us?" [[s01-X4b]] Expect: how far each map length stands for on the ground.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: skip Cove Road's second count and hear four pairs. Longer: pairs say why one bar could mean blocks on one map and steps on another.

### Words and supports

Words: scale, block, step, distance. Frame: "Each scale length stands for ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Day 5 applies all four parts. If many gave error C, it opens with strips laid on a fresh path; a small group takes item 4, land and water. Day 4's strips come back on day 5.
