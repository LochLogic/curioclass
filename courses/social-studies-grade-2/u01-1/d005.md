# Day 5: Maps of people and the land

## Teacher page

### Before class

Choose today's six: group 5 on the record sheet. Key: key-exits-3-5.md.

### The day

#### 8 min. Return and repair

Day 4's marks give two routes: item 4 (s01-R4) and error C (s01-X4). Teach the more common to the class; a small group takes the other in Pairs work.

Item 4: review: in kindergarten you found land and water on a globe and a flat map. On the hillside card pairs trace the stream in one color and the land beside it in another, then find a second stream or pond on Riverbend Park.

Error C: pairs lay strips along paths A and B on the repair map and count the ground units aloud; one box is two paces. Which path is eight paces?

#### 7 min. Model: Alder Farm Map

Say: "We made this map; the farm is made up." Read its four parts aloud with the four-part card: the title, the key (a dashed line is a ditch, a wave is a stream, a square is a barn), the rose, the scale bar (one box is ten steps).

Review: in grade 1 you learned some things on a map were made by people and some were already there.

**Ask.** How did people change the land or water here? Expect: they dug a ditch from the stream so water reaches the field. The key told us the dashed line is a ditch.

Board frame: "People ___ the ___ so ___."

#### 14 min. Pairs work

Riverbend Park and Lowfield, one of each a pair (made up; nobody is hurt in Lowfield). Pairs read each map's four parts without your pointing, say one way people use or change land or water, and say which part told them. Each pair writes one word on Riverbend Park (s01-P5-1) or draws it; it stays in the folder.

Minutes 0 to 4: the small group takes the repair not taught to the class.

Minutes 5 to 9: circulate with three looks: the child points to a mapped feature; says what people do there; names the part that told them.

Minutes 10 to 14: the six, 40 seconds each: mark the exit card (s01-X5), answer (s01-X5b) and (s01-X5c). A child may pass or point.

#### 3 min. Question board

Review: in grade 1 you kept a question board.

**Ask.** What do map parts tell us? Expect: the title tells what the map shows; the key what symbols mean; the rose directions; the scale how far. Add the class's answer in its own words.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

The other 24 mark the card. Read aloud: "Circle two places where people use or change the land or water." Mark 1 minute; collect 1; tick the record sheet 1. Circling the bare hill, the water or the rock, or naming a feature with no action, is error D.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed maps: draw each one. No strips: fold paper.

**Skip.** The repair and the pairs.

- **7 min. A farm.** Draw a field, a stream and a barn, a dashed ditch from stream to field, a key, a rose and a scale bar.
  **Say.** "How did people change the land or water here?" Expect: they dug a ditch so water reaches the field.
- **8 min. A park.** Draw a path, a mowed field and a pond in a low ring of earth.
  **Say.** "What did people make here, and which part told you?" Expect: the path and the pond; the key.
- **10 min. One word.** [[s01-P5-1]] Your child writes one word beside a place people made, or draws it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X5]] Your child marks the card alone.
  **Say.** "How do people use it?" [[s01-X5b]] Expect: what people do there, not only what is there. Then ask which part told them, [[s01-X5c]]: the key.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: read only Riverbend Park. Longer: pairs draw a made-up map that shows people using water.

### Words and supports

Words: use, change, land, water. Frame: "People ___ the ___ so ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Day 6 starts section 1.2: who made a map, when and where. If many gave error D, it opens with cards pairing an action with its pictured feature, and it hands back today's marks.
