# Repair and fresh task cards, day 47

Every card prints "invented for practice" and "Made by Curio Class" in small letters. Plain black line drawing on white. Never from the check packet. Read every card aloud once.

## Group 1: maps (errors A to E)

**Bench Row Map (repair):** A small map with the title Bench Row. A key: a square is a bench, a circle is a tree. A scale: one bar length is four paces. A bench and a tree two bar lengths apart. A corner note: Drawn by Curio Class for practice, winter 2026. Pairs trace a mark to the key and back.
**Rook Lane Map (fresh task):** A map of a market lane. A key: a square is a stall, a ring is a bench. A scale: one bar length is three paces. The gate and the bench are five bar lengths apart.

## Group 2: names (errors F to H)

**Sorting cards (repair):** Six name cards: Asia, Pacific, Africa, Indian, Antarctica, Arctic. Pairs sort into land and water. Then Pip says: The Atlantic is a continent.

## Group 3: maker and date (errors I, J)

**Corner note (repair):** The Bench Row Map corner note, read for who and when. Pairs point to each.

## Group 4: a claim with a source (errors K to M)

**Mossy Row Card (fresh task):** A made-up street of small cottages. A shady porch with a bench. A rain barrel by a gate. A tall hedge. **Caption, read aloud:** Mossy Row is a street made up for practice. Made by Curio Class.
**Pip's claim (repair):** I choose Mossy Row because it is nice. Pairs fix it with a picture and the card named.

## Written move strips (one for each group)

Group 1: trace one mark from the map to its key; walk the bar length; say how far. Group 2: sort the six names; say an ocean is water. Group 3: point to the maker and the date. Group 4: fix Pip's claim with the frame: I choose ___ because ___. The ___ shows ___, made by ___.

## Key (teacher)

Bench Row: eight paces. Rook Lane: fifteen paces. Sorting: Asia, Africa and Antarctica are land; Pacific, Indian and Arctic are water. Corner note: Curio Class, winter 2026. Mossy Row: the second claim names its source. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
