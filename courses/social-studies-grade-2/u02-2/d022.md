# Day 22: How land and water shape the weather

## Teacher page

### Before class

Today's six: the sixth six on the record sheet. Set the paper arrow and the four cards of places. Hand back day 21's cards (s02-X21) and sentences (s02-P21-2). Say each place is invented for practice, except the two real deserts. Key: key-exits-21-23.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up and repair

Error D from day 21 (s02-X21, s02-X21b): Pip labels the six cards again, two wrong; say agree or fix, with a reason.

#### 12 min. Four places

Say sea and ridge by compass word.
- **Granite Peak** (invented for practice): thermometers show 5 at the top and 15 at the foot. Pip says: "High places are warmer because they are closer to the Sun." Beside it: "The card shows 5 at the top and 15 at the foot; higher is colder." **Ask.** Which is colder, the top or the foot? Expect: the top. Say only that the ground warms the air near it.
- **Kelp Bay and Gray Ridge** (invented for practice): the sea lies east of the ridge. Move the paper arrow up the east side: wet air from the sea goes up the hill, cools, and the water falls as rain. Over many years the east side is wet and has forest; the west side is drier with scattered shrubs. Pip says: "Every coast is rainy." Beside it: "Not every place beside the sea is rainy; the far side of the ridge is drier."
- **Tide River** (invented for practice), near the equator, the line round the middle of the globe: warm and often rainy. **Ask.** Is it often warm or cold on low land near the equator? Expect: warm (s02-X22e). Review: you met the equator on day 10.
- **Two deserts:** the Sahara in Africa is hot and dry; Antarctica is a cold desert, dry. Pip says: "Every desert is hot." Beside it: "A desert is dry; some are hot and some are cold."

#### 4 min. Pairs work

Pairs match three picture cards to "cold up high", "wet on the sea side of a ridge" and "dry", and say one "because" ([[s02-P22-2]]).

#### 12 min. Supported work

About 9 minutes: the six come to the globe one at a time, 85 seconds each, for (s02-X22b), one of (s02-X22c), (s02-X22d) or (s02-X22e), dealt, and three globe locations from the prompt cards ([[s02-P22-1]]). Everyone else revises the map from the cue only. This block has about 3 minutes of slack.

#### 2 min. Question board

Revise the class's answer.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

Read aloud: "Circle the town where a worker needs the warmer coat" (s02-X22). Mark 2 minutes on Birch Ridge. The six were heard in supported work. Low Birch circled for "closer to the Sun" is error O; every desert hot is error K; every coast rainy is error E. Cold or warm swapped near the equator has no letter: record not yet seen.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No globe: use a ball or a world map.

**Skip.** The cue sheet.

- **10 min. Find it.** Point to a feature your adult names.
  **Say.** "Where does it lie?" Expect: in the ___ of ___.
- **15 min. Match them.** [[s02-P22-2]] Match three paper picture cards to cold, wet or dry.
  **Say.** "Why is it colder up high?" Expect: the air is colder higher up.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s02-X22]] Your child marks the card alone.
  **Say.** [[s02-X22b]] [[s02-X22c]] [[s02-X22d]] [[s02-X22e]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: two of the four places. Longer: a child says what would change if the sea lay on the other side of the ridge.

### Words and supports

Words: ridge, coast, desert, equator, higher, colder, dry, wet. Frame: "It is ___ because ___." Pointing counts.
