# Four places (day 22)

Every card prints "invented for practice", except the two real deserts, which print their names and our credit. Plain black line drawing on white. Credit line: Drawing by Curio Class.

## Granite Peak (cold higher up; error O)

**Picture:** A tall mountain with a village at the foot and a hut at the top. A thermometer at the top reads 5. A thermometer at the foot reads 15.
**Label:** On Granite Peak the top is colder than the foot.

## Kelp Bay and Gray Ridge (the sea and a ridge; error E)

**Picture:** A bay of sea on the east side of the card, a long ridge running north to south, forest on the east side of the ridge, and a few scattered shrubs on the west side. An arrow rises from the sea up the east side.
**Label:** The sea lies east of the ridge. Over many years the east side has been wet. The west side has been drier.

## Near the equator (warm and often rainy)

**Picture:** A river town in the warm band round the middle of the globe: tall broad-leaved trees, a covered market, boats on a river, and rain falling.
**Label:** Near the equator the Sun shines straight down, so it is warm and often rainy. (A place called Tide River, invented for practice.)

## Two deserts (error K)

**Picture 1:** A sand desert with dunes and one caravan of travelers. **Label:** The Sahara in Africa is a hot desert. It is dry.
**Picture 2:** A white plain of ice with a research station. **Label:** Antarctica is a cold desert. It is dry.
**Say:** A desert is dry. Some are hot and some are cold.

## Key (teacher)

Higher is colder because the ground warms the air near it, so the air gets colder with height (Penn State meteorology page). Rain falls on the windward side when moist air rises and cools over a ridge, and the far side is drier (NASA Oregon rain shadow page); the sea in this card lies east, the day 28 card has its sea on the west. Rain is common near the equator because direct sunlight drives more evaporation (NASA rainfall page). A desert has very little rain and can be hot or cold (National Geographic).
