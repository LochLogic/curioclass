# Section 1.2 opener: who made it, and what can we check

## What the section does

Three days, 6 to 8, of about 35 minutes. Day 6 teaches the three questions on a card: who made this source, when, and where. Children answer them from the source's own words. The class reads an old map of Boulder, Colorado, made by a federal survey in 1904, and a photograph card made by Curio Class. Day 7 teaches fact and opinion: a fact is something we can check; an opinion is what someone thinks. Day 8 applies both to a captioned photograph and a second old map, a map of Pickaway County, Ohio, printed in 1858. Nothing about the Vermont map or the Willow Garden caption appears here; they wait for day 15.

## How the days run

Day 6: a repair for day 5, the three questions, pairs sorting six credit cards, and the exit. Day 7: a warm-up on a fresh card, fact and opinion, pairs placing tokens, and the exit. Day 8: two repairs, source two cards in pairs, the question board, and the exit. Each exit has a mark and a spoken part from the day's rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Three different places can sit on one source.** Where it was made (origin), what it shows, and who keeps it. Children keep them apart.
- **A maker can be a person, a firm or a group.** A library or archive keeps a map; it did not make it.
- **"The record does not say" is a full answer.** The Boulder margin does not print where the map was made. Nothing is guessed.
- **An old map is evidence of what its maker knew and chose,** not a photograph of the place.
- **A fact is checkable;** it may be checked on this source or by looking somewhere else. **An opinion** tells what someone thinks. A source with an opinion in it can still be used for what can be checked.
- The bridge question, asked of maps and photographs with the teacher modeling, is "What is this map for?"

## The errors, in plain words

- **E: the keeper is taken for the maker.** Pip says the library made this map.
- **J: the place shown is taken for the place made.** Pip says it was made in Boulder because it shows Boulder.
- **M: a missing credit is guessed.** Pip names a surveyor the margin never names.
- **F: everything printed is taken for fact.**
- **L: one opinion throws out the whole source.**
- **D: naming a feature, not an action,** returns from day 5.

Pip voices every wrong idea. No child's work is the example.

## Supplies for the section

Six credit cards for each pair, a three-question card, photograph cards, token sets, a printed transcript of each old map's margin, and the exit cards. A credit is always read as printed. Every photograph shows no child.

## A room with nothing

Copy a credit line onto paper in large print. Fold a card with three questions. A photograph is a drawing with a printed credit line. Two scraps of paper serve as tokens.
