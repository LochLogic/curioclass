# Section 4.2 opener: why countries trade, and one claim about it

## What the section does

Six days, 52 to 57, of 34 to 36 minutes. The unit asks: why do countries need each other? Day 52 teaches four reasons people in one country buy from another (a resource missing, a skill, something different, a lower price), that work and knowledge cross borders too, and the words import, export, maker, carrier, seller and user. Day 53 traces goods on a world map: where made, where sold, in or out. Day 54 teaches that a choice gives up another thing (opportunity cost), that countries can share a rule and stay two countries, and revises day 51's four-row chart. Day 55 answers the question with a short claim that names a card and its maker. Day 56 is the end-of-unit assessment on a packet held back until its day. Day 57 responds: small groups meet the teacher, then try a fresh task.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a repair, then a short teaching part, a part for the children to work with, the rotating six heard at the teacher's table, a question-board turn and a one-picture exit. Day 56 is a check: the packet is read aloud once, nothing is taught and there is no repair. The teacher reads every card, caption, story and prompt aloud. Children read labels of one to three words.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Countries trade because they differ in what they have and can do.** A resource a place lacks, a skill, a different product or a lower price are all reasons. Countries can trade things both make. Trading does not mean a country is poor or weak; both sides choose.
- **A service crosses a border as work or knowledge.** A person's work can be bought from another country.
- **Import and export depend on the viewpoint.** A good that comes into one country goes out of another. The arrow runs from where it was made to where it is sold. "Sold here" is not "made here".
- **A maker, a carrier, a seller and a user** are four different people along the arrow. Using a good links the user to the people who made and carried it.
- **Opportunity cost is the best other thing we gave up when we chose,** not everything we wanted.
- **An agreement between countries is a shared rule.** Two countries that share a rule stay two countries.
- **A claim names its reason, its card and the card's maker.** A liking is not a source. A missing maker is written "the card does not say".

## The errors, in plain words

Pip says each wrong idea once and the right idea is said beside it: a country trades only if it makes nothing (F, day 52); only things can be traded (G, day 52); sold here means made here (H, day 53); the shop made it all (I, day 53); an import goes out of the buyer's country (O, day 53); a place name explains the link (R, day 53); opportunity cost is everything I want (J, day 54); an agreement makes two countries one (L, day 54); I think it because I like it (K, day 55 and 57); the government has a ferry, with no purpose (N, day 57).

## Supplies for the section

The trade story cards, the loaf and haircut cards, the arrow and chain strip, the model labels, the printed world map, the choice chart cards, the shipping story, the claim cards, Trade Stories A, the trade claim strip, the repair cards, the exit cards, the day 56 packet (kept back until day 56), the fresh task cards, the group move strip, the self-check strip, the record sheet and the question board. The teacher says aloud that every trade story and label is a made-up model. Where a real country is named, the card says it is a model, not a record of real shipments or of what a country is known for. No brand name appears. No child is asked what is in their home or what their family buys.
