# Cards for day 62

Refugee Card and the reasons cards. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## Refugee Card (invented for practice)

**Words (read aloud):** "Refugee: a person who had to leave their country because staying was not safe." Credit: open reference page, read November 2026; weak source.
**Refugee Card, Bel (invented for practice):** "Fighting reaches Bel's town. It is not safe to stay. Bel's family goes to another country to be safe."
**Picture:** A small town, a road, a fence between two countries (outline only), a family walking away from the town. No people in danger are pictured.
The class fills a blank chain strip with no example in view, then answers "Could they have stayed?" No one is asked whether they or their family are refugees. Pip says C.

## Which reason is better supported? (three cards, each with two possible reasons)

- **Anna:** "to be with family" or "to see a big city". The Anna Arrival card tells that her mother and her aunt met her.
- **Smith:** "men took him and made him work for them" or "Smith wanted to travel". The Smith Life card tells that men took him by force and he did not choose it.
- **The Cherokee:** "the government decided" or "the people wanted to see the west". The Cherokee Today card tells that the United States government decided.

Each pair points to the supporting line: "The ___ card says ___." The six are heard, one ask each.

## Tracing

Each pair has the world map (blank-world-map-62) and traces three moves with a pencil: "from ___ to ___, by ___, because ___." Anna, Europe to North America, by ship; Smith, Africa to the Americas, by ship; the Cherokee, east to west on one continent, so an arrow that does not cross an ocean. Pip says I: Pip draws the Anna arrow backward and the class turns it around.

## Key (teacher sheet only)

Reasons: to be with family; men took him and made him work; the government decided. Trace: Anna Europe to North America; Smith Africa to the Americas; Cherokee from the Southeast to the west on North America. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
