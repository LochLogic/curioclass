# Smith Life

Real person, told in three short paragraphs in our words, once, calmly, with no picture of capture and no picture of chains. Credit line printed under the card: "Venture Smith, A Narrative of the Life and Adventures of Venture, 1798, as told by himself. Summary by Curio Class." The teacher reads it aloud and no child acts it out. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## The three questions of the card

Who made it: Venture Smith told his own life; it was written down and printed in New London, Connecticut. When: printed in 1798. Where: told in Connecticut. "Printed in 1798" is when it was written down, not when he was taken.

## The three paragraphs (read aloud, 9 minutes)

**One.** "Venture Smith was a child in Africa. Men took him by force from his home and his family. He was brought across the ocean to the Americas."
**Two.** "In the Americas, other people said he belonged to them. They are called enslavers. Smith was an enslaved person for about twenty-five years."
**Three.** "Smith worked and saved. He bought his own freedom. After that he worked more, to buy freedom for his wife and his children."

**Picture:** A boy's face and an outline of Africa. A small ship outline on the ocean (a ship only, no people on it). A man counting coins at a table. All plain, no scenes of harm.
**Say, to end:** "Smith's life went on after he was free." The later-life paragraph is held for the day 60 exit.

## Whose wish (4 minutes)

On the chain strip: "Because the men wanted to hold him, they took him from his home. Afterward he was enslaved." Two columns: "What the enslavers did" (took him, held him) and "What Smith wanted" (to be free, to look after his family). The card does not say more about what he hoped on the day he was taken.

## The lives people built (4 minutes)

"Enslaved people were people, with families and with skills. Smith worked, saved, and later bought freedom for his wife and children. Other sources tell about other lives. This card tells only Smith's." The class does not rank hardships. Teacher note: more about the lives enslaved people built, such as family, songs, stories and resistance, belongs in later work from sources not read for this unit.
