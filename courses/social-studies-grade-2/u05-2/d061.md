# Day 61: The Cherokee people, then and now

## Teacher page

### Before class

Name today's six, the fourth six on the record sheet. Say aloud: the Cherokee Nation, the United Keetoowah Band and the Eastern Band are real nations; every other child and family is made up. No acting out; any child may draw the map instead. Key: key-exits-60-62.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 60's sort (s05-P60-4) and exit ticket (s05-X60), marked. If many missed error C on day 60's spoken exit s05-X60b (Smith chose it), set a trip a family plans beside a family told they cannot stay: [[s05-P61-1]] Say: a ship or a car is how they traveled; the question is whether they could have stayed. Error K (s05-X60) goes to the small group below.

#### 3 min. Retrieval, grade 1

You learned in grade 1 that a nation is a people with their own government, and you heard the Cherokee Nation's own pages. A reminder, not a test. In pairs: [[s05-P61-3]]

#### 9 min. Cherokee Today

Read the card once, with its credit line, ending on rebuilding and today. Show the map: the homeland in the Southeast, an arrow west. Say "estimated" where the card does: in the summer of 1838, an estimated 4,000 of an estimated 16,000 Cherokee people died. Show no picture of suffering. Each of the other nations named is its own nation. The Seminole Card is the exit and stays out of today's teaching.

#### 4 min. Who decided

**Ask.** [[s05-P61-4]] Half-fill the strip: "In the summer of 1838 soldiers came to the homes in the ___. The families were made to go ___. Afterward families grieved and ___." Pip says error C: "They walked west, so they chose." Say beside it: the government decided; the Cherokee people did not choose to leave.

#### 4 min. Past and today

Two bins: "forced out in the 1830s" and "governs itself today". The Cherokee Nation goes in both. [[s05-P61-6]] Pip says error M: "The Cherokee are gone." Say beside it: three Cherokee governments govern themselves today, as of November 2026.

#### 7 min. Pairs work

Each pair finishes the half-filled strip and each child fills the last box: [[s05-P61-5]] Pairs then place the three name cards on the card map and write O or N: [[s05-P61-7]] Hear four of the six, 45 seconds each with the swap, 3 minutes, for s05-X61b; then a small group of at most 6 takes 4 minutes on error K: Ming's card, a finger on the line. [[s05-P61-2]]

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

Children mark [[s05-X61]]. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. While the rest mark, hear the other two of the six, 45 seconds each: [[s05-X61b]].

### Other rooms

Draw the map on the board; write names on slips.

### At home

**Swap.** No bins: two sheets of paper.

**Skip.** The groups and the six.

- **10 min. Cherokee Today.** Read the card once, calmly. **Say.** "Who decided?" Expect: the United States government.
- **10 min. Today.** Your child fills the last box and the O or N. [[s05-P61-5]] [[s05-P61-7]] **Say.** "Is the Cherokee Nation gone?" Expect: no, it governs itself today.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X61]] [[s05-X61b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the strip only. Longer: why each nation has its own name.

### Words and supports

Words: nation, government, forced, homeland, rebuilt, today. Frame: "Because the government ___, they ___. Afterward ___." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Day 62 asks refugees today and traces moves on a map. Error C shows as "chosen" circled; error M as "the Seminole Nation is gone". Day 62 repairs error M first and sends error C to its small group.
