# Section 5.2 opener: who could stay, and who could not

## What the section does

Three days, 60 to 62, of 35 minutes. The unit asks: why do people leave home for a new place? Day 60 names a forced move plainly: Venture Smith was captured as a child in Africa, taken across the ocean to the Americas and enslaved, and he later bought his own freedom. Day 61 names another forced move plainly: the United States government forced the Cherokee Nation and other Native nations from their homelands in the Southeast in the 1830s, and three Cherokee governments govern themselves today. Day 62 applies the chain and the test question to a refugee card and a world map: children pick the better-supported reason for each move studied and trace the moves from where people left to where they arrived.

## How the days run

Each day opens with a repair, then teaching, a part for pairs, the rotating six heard at the teacher's table, an exit ticket and a question-board turn. The teacher reads every card aloud, once and calmly. There is no role play or acting of capture, removal or flight; any child may draw instead of sorting, and may leave to a quiet drawing task at any time. No image of chains is shown. Children read labels of one to three words.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Who decided, and could they have stayed?** On every day. What the enslavers wanted and what Smith wanted are different, and the card says who decided. Enslaved people are people with families and skills.
- **Say "enslaved people" and "enslavers".** Never "slaves" or "owners". Smith's birthplace is given only as Africa.
- **Native nations are living nations.** The Cherokee Nation, the United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians in Oklahoma and the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians are three Cherokee governments today. The same nation is "forced out in the 1830s" and "governs itself today". Removal was a decision of the United States government, and the Cherokee people did not choose to leave.
- **Refugee.** A person who had to leave their country because staying was not safe. Refugees are people, not a nationality. No child is asked whether they or their family are refugees. The wording of the definition rests on one open reference page and a government page that supports its plain core (source rows 62A and 62B).
- **Hard history is not ranked.** No "worst".

## The errors, in plain words

Pip says each wrong idea once and the right idea is said beside it: arrival caused the departure (B, day 60); he rode a ship, so he chose (C, days 60 to 62); Smith liked sailing, I think, with no card (K, day 60); the Cherokee are gone (M, day 61); they went to see the place (A, day 62); the arrow runs from where they arrived (I, day 62).

## What stays held back

Smith's later life (day 60 exit), the Seminole Card (day 61 exit) and the Route Card (day 62 exit) are printed only on their days.
