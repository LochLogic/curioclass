# Cherokee Today and map

Real nations, told in our words. Credit line printed under the card: "Cherokee Nation, Remember the Removal pages; National Park Service, Trail of Tears National Historic Trail; United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians in Oklahoma; Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians; read November 2026. Summary by Curio Class." The teacher reads it once, ending on rebuilding and today. No acting out and no image of suffering. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## The card (read aloud, 9 minutes)

"Cherokee people lived in the Southeast, in homelands in the mountains and valleys. The United States government decided that Cherokee people must leave. In the summer of 1838, soldiers rounded up Cherokee families and forced them from their homes. They were made to go west. The Cherokee Nation says an estimated 4,000 of an estimated 16,000 people died. Families grieved and they rebuilt. The Muscogee (Creek), Choctaw and Chickasaw nations were forced out too, and each is its own nation. As of November 2026, three Cherokee governments govern themselves: the Cherokee Nation, with its capital in Tahlequah, Oklahoma; the United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians, in Oklahoma; and the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians, in North Carolina."

**Map:** The Southeast and the middle of North America as a plain outline. A shaded homeland in the Southeast, an arrow west to Oklahoma, a star at the Cherokee Nation's capital, and a second small shape in the Southeast mountains for the Eastern Band. No border lines drawn beyond a coast. 

## Who decided (4 minutes)

"The United States government decided. The Cherokee people did not choose to leave their homeland." Strip, half filled: "In the summer of 1838 soldiers came to the homes in the ___. The families were made to go ___. Afterward families grieved and ___." Pip says C.

## Past and today (4 minutes)

Two bins, "forced out in the 1830s" and "governs itself today". The Cherokee Nation and the United Keetoowah Band each go in both. The Eastern Band goes in "governs itself today" only: its people stayed in their North Carolina homeland. Pip says M. Each nation is its own nation with its own name; day 64 reads two nations' own pages.

## Teacher note

The fifth nation named in the federal trail pages is held for the day 61 exit card. "Chosen" is never said of removal. The pages say "estimated" and the card says "estimated".

## Pairs work (7 minutes)

Each pair finishes the half-filled strip: "In the summer of 1838 soldiers came to the homes in the Southeast. The families were made to go west. Afterward families grieved and rebuilt." Then places three name cards on a card map (Cherokee Nation, United Keetoowah Band, Eastern Band) in their homes today. The six are heard.

Cherokee Nation: Oklahoma (capital Tahlequah). United Keetoowah Band: Oklahoma. Eastern Band: North Carolina. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
