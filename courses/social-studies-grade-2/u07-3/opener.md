# Section 7.3 opener: from facts to a claim, the report, and the child's own digital entry

## What the section does

Three days, 88 to 90, of 35 minutes. Day 88 teaches the step from a list of facts to a claim: one answer to the big question, two facts from named sources, and a "because" that links them. The model is Japan, using only the three facts taught on day 82 and one reason said aloud as thinking. Day 89 drafts the report (the map with its key, a fact and sticker in each of three boxes, and the claim card) and the first fifteen children take a one-minute turn at the shared device. Day 90 finishes the report, practices presenting it in pairs, and the other fifteen take their turn. Completed claim examples come off the wall on day 89; only the blank frame stays.

## How the days run

Day 88 has pairs and the rotating six. Days 89 and 90 run a digital rotation (one adult, one offline device, no account, no network, no upload): each child directs their own entry and checks it; nobody types. The teacher writes a short note card for each child after class on 89 and 90. No six are heard on 89 and 90; the paper mark and the draft are the evidence.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A list of facts is not an argument.** A claim answers the big question and a "because" ties two facts to it. Different claims can fit the same evidence and none is ranked.
- **A fact needs its label.** The label names the page and who made it.
- **A digital version is the child's own.** Watching another child's turn is not a turn (F). A child with no device turn is recorded as not yet seen.
- **A listener's question helps.** "Which page says that?" asks for the source.

## The errors, in plain words

Pip says: here are my facts (D, day 88); where a fact came from does not matter, I just wrote it (H, day 89); I watched Mo's screen so I did digital (F, day 90).

## What stays held back

The exit cards of days 88 to 90 print only on their days. The display is paper first: a prepared offline page with a claim box, two fact boxes and a label slot. Without a device, keep all paper work and record digital as not yet seen.
