# Section 1.4 opener: making our own map, and showing what we know

## What the section does

Four days, 13 to 16, of 35, 35, 33 and 30 minutes. On day 13 the class plans a map of a familiar place, our classroom or a school part the class names, never anyone's home. The teacher paces the long wall once while the class counts aloud, the class picks a scale length in whole paces, and each child counts two kinds of features and draws a picture graph with one mark for each feature, then drafts the map. Drafts are practice. On day 14 each child gets a written cue, revises the map, counts fresh features, draws a new graph, writes a credit line, and makes a closing claim. Day 15 is the end-of-unit assessment: the packet is read neutrally once, children answer alone, and the teacher scores the revised map and graph after class. Day 16 responds: small groups re-teach what day 15 showed, then try a fresh task.

## How the days run

Day 13: a repair, the question What should our map show, the wall pace, count and graph, the draft, the exit. Day 14: return the drafts with cues, working time, the closing claim, what our map leaves out, the exit. Day 15: set-up, twenty minutes of independent items, five minutes to hear children not yet heard, collect. Day 16: return findings, targeted teaching, a fresh task, review with a self-check strip.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A map keeps what was seen.** Features sit where they are in the room. Decoration is not a map.
- **A graph shows counts.** One mark stands for one counted feature; the graph must match the counts.
- **A map needs four working parts:** a title, a key that matches its pictures, a compass rose set by the wall called north, and a scale counted in whole paces.
- **Drafts are practice; only the revision is scored.** The written cue names one thing a child can act on: a count that does not match, a feature in the wrong place, or a missing part.
- **Our own map has a maker, a date and a place,** so its credit line reads "Made by ___ in our classroom, 2026".
- **The best map depends on the job,** and every map leaves something out.

## The errors, in plain words

- **H:** a country is the whole continent (repaired on day 13).
- **I:** the features can be in any order (repaired on day 14).
- **O:** a graph can show any counts (the day 14 recount is the repair).
- **P:** a map needs no working parts (repaired on day 16).
- **N, C, E and J** return in the day 16 fresh tasks.

Pip voices every wrong idea.

## Supplies for the section

Pencils, paper, a taped line for the teacher's pace, counters, the sheets for the count and graph, the familiar place map sheet, feedback slips, the purpose cards, the packet (kept back until day 15), response pages, a self-check strip for each child, and the record sheet with the rotation and the four component groups. No device is needed.

## A room with nothing

Blank paper, a pencil, a taped line on the floor, and counters for the graph.
