# Music Here map and Posadas Card

Real person, told as a summary in our words. Credit line printed under the card: "Artemio Posadas, National Heritage Fellow; interview translated by the interpreter Russell Rodriguez, and biography, National Endowment for the Arts. Summary by Curio Class, not an exact quotation." The teacher reads the card once on day 72 (the first three sentences) and says it is one real man's telling, not everyone's. Day 78 adds the fourth line. No photograph and no recording are reproduced. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written). Plain black line drawing on white.

## The card (read aloud)

"Artemio Posadas grew up in San Luis Potosi, Mexico. At a fandango in Mexico, there is music and dancing from evening to dawn. In California, local laws limit all-night public events, so he organizes shorter fandangos at homes and garages."

**Day 78 line:** "The National Endowment for the Arts says his arrival helped traditional Mexican music grow in California."

**Picture:** Two locator maps side by side, one with Mexico marked and one with California marked, and a curved arrow from the first to the second. A speech bubble marked "Posadas remembers". The labels are "Mexico" and "California" only.
**Hook question (asked and left open):** "Can music travel without fitting in a suitcase?"
**Teacher note:** The card is one man's telling through an interpreter. It says what changed for his gatherings (shorter, at homes and garages) and, on the day 78 line, that the place changed too. It does not say how he or anyone felt. A fandango is a gathering with music and dancing; say it plainly and do not turn it into a costume or tasting activity.
