# Day 73: Four questions for a teller

## Teacher page

### Before class

Pick the repair card for day 72's most missed letter. Hang the four-box card empty. Say that Ms. Bell is made up. Ask no child about their own move; any may pass. Before day 74, write a mark and a picture cue by each question on the sheets. Key: key-exits-72-75.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Return and repair

Hand back day 72's pages (s06-P72-1) with a mark. Teach the letter most missed on s06-X72 and s06-X72b with its repair card: error A, five pictures sorted "I can hold it" or "I cannot" (both are culture); error G, two families keep the Harvest Supper, say one difference; error K, two dishes, say what differs and why neither is best.

#### 4 min. Retrieval, grade 1's calendar and sources

Review: grade 1's order of years and kinds of source. **Ask.** "Which comes first, 2011 or 2016?" Expect 2011. **Ask.** "Would a photograph or a person's memory help us ask about a move? What might each show?" Expect: a photograph shows how a place looked; a memory, what one person remembers. A miss goes to day 74's repair.

#### 9 min. Four questions, a model

A teller is a person who tells (review, unit 5). An interview is asking a teller questions to learn about a move. Ms. Bell, the crossing guard (made up): ask "Why did you move?" and she says "It was time." Not enough to learn from. Fill the four-box card with her lines, each question ending "this town": where (a lake town), how (by train), why (to work near the school crossing), when (2011). Pip says error B: "One question is enough: Where are you from?" Say beside it: "Four questions: where, how, why and when." A teller may say "I would rather not say"; that is a full answer.

#### 10 min. Pairs write

Each child fills the interview sheet [[s06-P73-1]]: four frames, "you" in each blank, and one question of their own about the move. Partners check each is about the move. Hear the rotating six at your table, about 45 seconds each: [[s06-X73b]]. Mark by the scale. In the last 3 minutes, up to six children who missed day 72's retrieval join you with two neighbor cards. Collect the sheets.

#### 4 min. What is still missing

Read one line of Ms. Bell's again: asked "Where are you from?", she says "A lake town." **Ask.** [[s06-P73-2]] Hear three answers; the class points to the empty boxes on the four-box card.

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

Read [[s06-X73]] aloud once; each child circles. [[s06-X73b]] was heard in the pairs work.

### Other rooms

No printer: write the four frames on the board; children copy them on paper. No four-box card: draw four boxes.

### At home

**Swap.** No printed card: draw four boxes labeled where, how, why, when.

**Skip.** The group and the six.

- **5 min. Look back.** **Say.** "Which comes first, 2011 or 2016?" Expect: 2011.
- **10 min. Four questions.** Read Ms. Bell's lines: a lake town, by train, to work near the school crossing, 2011. **Say.** "Ms. Bell says by train. Which box is that?" Expect: how.
- **10 min. Write.** [[s06-P73-1]] Write the four questions and one of your own.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s06-X73]] [[s06-X73b]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: write where and when only. Longer: who could answer the child's own question?

### Words and supports

Words: interview, teller, question, pass. Frame: "My question is ___."

### Tomorrow

The exit shows error B (a how or why card circled for when, or a topic missed) and error D (a question not about the move). Day 74 (Application) returns the sheets and repairs the letter most missed, then a visiting adult tells a safe move and children ask a follow-up and write only what is said.
