# Country Cards A: Japan and Saudi Arabia, days 51, 52 and 54

Each card names the country by its own name, in the present tense where it lives on, and shows examples, never everyone. No religion line is printed. No officeholder is named. Credit line on each card, read aloud: "Made by Curio Class, 2026, from named public pages." Plain line drawing on white.

## Japan card

**History:** In 1868 a city named Edo was renamed Tokyo. (Source 51B.) **Picture:** a city gate with a sign changing from one name to the other.
**Culture:** Bunraku is a puppet theater that is still performed today. It is one thing some people do. (Source 51C.) **Picture:** a puppeteer behind a puppet.
**Language:** Japanese. (Source 51A.) **Picture:** a speech bubble.
**Government:** Elected representatives choose the prime minister, who leads the government. The emperor has a role set by the constitution and does not govern. (Sources 50A, 50B, 50C.) **Picture:** voters, then representatives, then a prime minister; the emperor in a side box.

## Saudi Arabia card

**History:** The kingdom took the name Saudi Arabia in 1932. (Source 51F.) **Picture:** a page with a new name written on it.
**Culture:** Al-Sadu is a traditional weaving craft with wool and hair on a wooden loom. It is one thing some people do. (Source 51G.) **Picture:** a hand weaving on a wooden loom.
**Language:** Arabic. (Sources 51D, 51E.) **Picture:** a speech bubble.
**Government:** The leader's job passes within the ruling family and is not decided by a popular vote. (Source 51E.) **Picture:** a ruling family and an arrow to the leader.

## Said aloud

The three questions of unit 1 asked of each card: "Who made it? Curio Class. When? 2026. Where made? In our class, from named public pages." The teacher fills the history row for both. Pip says E on day 51: "Everyone in Japan does puppet theater." The right idea is said beside it: "The card shows one thing some people do; it does not say everyone."

## Key (teacher)

The four rows are history, culture, language and government for each country. No ranking of countries or peoples. A neutral reply to a child's question about belief: "People in every country follow many beliefs, and a card does not say what one person believes." The 1727 date and the Diriyah line of the plan are cut: no readable page supported them.
