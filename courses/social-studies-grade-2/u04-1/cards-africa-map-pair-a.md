# Africa Map Pair A, days 48 and 49

One printed pair for each pair of children and one for the wall. Plain line drawing on white. Credit line printed small on each map: "Made by Curio Class, 2026, redrawn from Natural Earth map data (public domain)". Maps are redrawn by Curio Class in 2026 from Natural Earth map data (public domain). Production draws no border that anyone disputes as settled: a disputed edge is left as a plain edge of the shading with no line and no label. The art is described here and has not been drawn or looked at yet (planned only, not yet written).

## Map A: physical

**Picture:** The continent of Africa. Title: "Africa, land and water". The Atlantic Ocean and the Indian Ocean in blue. A wide tan area across the north, a green area in the middle and the south, small triangles for mountain ranges, a wavy blue line for the longest river. No country lines. Key: tan stands for desert, green for grassland and forest, a triangle for a mountain, a wavy line for a river, blue for water. The Sahara is labelled across the tan area. Compass rose in a corner.
**Caption, read aloud:** Africa is a continent. This map shows its land and water.

## Map B: political

**Picture:** The same outline. Title: "Africa, countries". Each country in its own flat shade, the shades different from the shades of map A. Dashed lines between countries. Only one country is named: Egypt, in the northeast. Key: a dashed line is a border, a shade is one country, a star is a capital. Compass rose in a corner.
**Caption, read aloud:** Africa is a continent. More than 50 countries are in it. Egypt is one of them.

## Said aloud

"A physical map shows land and water. A political map shows countries and their borders. Each map has its own key." The teacher reads each key and each title; a child is never asked to count the countries. On day 49 the teacher points to the Sahara on map A and shows that it reaches across several country lines on map B: "A physical region can cross country borders." Pip says on day 48: "Africa is a country" (A), then "the map with more colours shows the borders" (B). The right idea is said beside each.

## Key (teacher)

The Sahara is found by the tan on map A, by its key. Egypt is found by its dashed borders on map B, by its key. Facts rest on sources 48A, 48B and 48C (Africa holds more than 50 countries; the Sahara crosses several countries and Egypt holds part of it).
