# How-chosen chart and strips, days 50 and 51

Two strips on a wall chart: **chosen by voting** and **not chosen by voting (the job passes in the family)**. Cards are stuck under a strip. Plain black line drawing on white. Invented countries print "invented for practice".

**Kelda (invented):** ballots, a tally strip, the most marks wins. Under voting.
**Orrin (invented):** the leader's job passes to a family member. Under family.
**The Navajo Nation, today:** it elects its President and Vice President, who are chosen together by voters every four years. (Review from grade 1; our own words, source 50D and 50E.) Under voting.
**Japan, today:** voters elect representatives. The representatives choose the prime minister, who leads the government. The emperor has a role set by the constitution and does not govern. (Our own words, sources 50A, 50B and 50C.) Under voting for the representatives; the emperor's box is marked "role set by the constitution".

## Said aloud

"Grade 1's example is one way, not the only way." Pip says D: "Every leader is chosen by voting." Then M: "Japan's emperor runs the government." The right idea is said beside each. Both ways are met as facts, with no judgment of either.

## Key (teacher)

Kelda and the Navajo Nation under voting; Orrin under family; Japan's prime minister chosen by elected representatives, and the emperor not governing. A card shows one country's way and is not a picture of a faith or a people.
