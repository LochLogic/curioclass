# Day 48: One continent, two maps

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 47 closed unit 3; its record sheet says what to carry. Start a clean question board. Print the cards for each pair and set out the object with its label (the model label if it has none). Pick today's six, the children least heard. Say aloud: Kelda, Tarn and every made-up place are invented. Key: key-exits-48-51.md.

### The day

#### 5 min. Hook

Show the wall copy of the Africa pair and read the object's label aloud: "Pencil cup. Made in Portugal." Write "date not on the label" beside it. Ask "Why do countries need each other?" Write four first answers as said, 20 seconds each. Hand back day 47's exit tickets (sc1-X47, sc1-X47c), 1 minute.

#### 5 min. Retrieval from grade 1

Review from grade 1: rules, voting, elected leaders. Partner talk, 1 minute. **Ask.** [[s04-P48-1]] Expect: to be safe, fair or help everyone learn. Hear four, 15 seconds each. **Ask.** [[s04-P48-2]] Expect: Room 3's captain. Scan pairs for 2 minutes; note each miss for day 49.

#### 8 min. Two maps of one place

Review: continent, a very large land area; a map key. New: physical map, political map, border, independent country (one that governs itself). Read Map A's key. **Ask.** [[s04-P48-3]] Expect: desert; find the Sahara by it. Read Map B's key. **Ask.** [[s04-P48-4]] Expect: a border; find Egypt by its borders. Say: "A physical map shows land and water. A political map shows countries and their borders." Lay the paper Africa outline holding country outlines: "Africa is a continent with more than 50 independent countries" (Source 48A); nobody counts them. Pip says: "Africa is a country." **Ask.** [[s04-F48-1]] Expect: no; Egypt is a country in it. Pip says: "The map with more colours shows the borders." Say beside it: read the key.

#### 10 min. Pairs work

Hand out the Tanzania Crop, 1 minute. For 8 minutes one partner draws the triangle from the physical key and says what it stands for ([[s04-P48-5]]), the other the star from the political key ([[s04-P48-6]]); then swap. First hear two children not heard on day 45 (s03-X45b, c, e or g), 1.5 minutes. Then the six, 45 seconds each plus 15 to call the next, 6 minutes: three hear [[s04-X48c]], three hear [[s04-X48d]] on the Nigeria Crop. Last minute: collect.

#### 4 min. Question board

Partner talk, 1 minute. **Ask.** [[s04-P48-7]] Expect: no; it holds many countries. Hear four, 30 seconds each. Add "What makes a country?"

#### 3 min. Exit ticket

Children mark [[s04-X48]] and [[s04-X48b]]; read each ask once. Mark later: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

### Other rooms

No printer: draw each map on the board; children answer on lined paper.

### At home

**Swap.** No maps: draw two maps of one yard, one with a pond and a hill, one with a fence.

**Skip.** The pairs and the six.

- **10 min. Two maps.** **Say.** "What does tan stand for in the key?" Expect: desert. [[s04-P48-3]]
- **10 min. The key.** **Say.** "What does the dashed line stand for?" Expect: a border. [[s04-P48-4]] Your child then draws a triangle and a star ([[s04-P48-5]] [[s04-P48-6]]).
- **10 min. Talk.** **Say.** "Is a continent a country?" Expect: no. [[s04-P48-7]]
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s04-X48]] [[s04-X48b]] [[s04-X48c]] [[s04-X48d]]

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: one symbol each in pairs work. Longer: a child draws a made-up place and its two keys.

### Words and supports

Words: physical map, political map, border, independent country, continent, key. Frame: "Africa is a continent. ___ is a country." Pointing counts.

### Tomorrow

Error A shows as a box round the whole crop, or "a country" for Europe; error B as a circled dashed line, or a pointer to the map with more colors. If more than a third made error A or B, day 49 opens with one repair; fewer go to a small group. Day 49 asks what makes a country.
