# Day 3: What an account claims, implies and cannot prove

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 2's exit goes back today with errors G and O marked. Print the excerpt and fold the last two sentences of paragraph II under on each copy; they are the exit. Hang the three-column chart on the wall. Have the background card ready to read.

### The day

#### 5 min. Warm-up

Return day 2's exit. Count errors G and O; the more common one takes the warm-up. For G, students do [[s01-P3-1]]: tag the fact each clause carries, then recombine without losing one. For O, rehearse day 2's sorting task again with the answers covered. If many (more than a third) missed day 2's exit, say what the rewrite lost aloud before pairs begin.

**Ask.** Which fact did your version put first? Expect: any of the three, with all three kept.

#### 7 min. Background and words

Read the background card aloud, about a minute. Say the Salem trials, the witchcraft accusations and the hangings plainly and move on; nothing is staged and anyone may step out and return. Then four words, one sentence each, read aloud; pairs say what they think each means. Sentences: The pious family prayed before every meal. The clerk was indicted in court, and the charge was read aloud. Her testimony was the only account of what happened that night. Children said the spectre in the old mill was only the moon on a sheet.

**Ask.** What does the sentence tell you? Expect: prayed for pious; the charge read aloud for indicted; the only account for testimony; was only the moon on a sheet for spectre. Hand out the other four word cards.

#### 12 min. Read the excerpt

Read the excerpt aloud once (5 minutes). Then silent rereading with the S and Q key (7 minutes). The last two sentences of paragraph II stay folded under and are not marked or discussed. Mather defended the court and printed this in 1693, so read it as a source with an interest, not as news.

**Ask.** Who is speaking, and who did the seeing? Expect: Mather is reporting; the witnesses saw, or said they saw.

#### 5 min. Retrieval

Students answer [[s01-R5]] alone, from Mather's indictment paragraph. A miss: model a neutral one-sentence summary that holds the reporting verbs apart from Mather's own claim.

**Ask.** Which words report what someone said? Expect: testifi'd, did testifie; Mather's own claim is that the Witchcraft was evident.

#### 10 min. Teaching first and worked example

Introduce the three columns on the wall: what the account claims, what it implies, what it cannot prove. The third column is the point of the day. Students try [[s01-K3-1]] first, then model on the board with [[steps:s01-K3-1]], thinking aloud: Who is reporting, and what did they see?

**Ask.** Why can the account not prove that Bishop did it? Expect: only the witness reports the Shape; the account gives the report, not the act or its cause. Spectral evidence means a victim's report that a person's spirit tormented her; no one else could see it, so it could not be tested.

#### 6 min. Alone, then pairs

Alone, students do [[s01-P3-2]] on the chart; pairs compare the third column.

**Ask.** What would settle it? Expect: independent witnesses, or an act anyone could see.

#### 5 min. Exit

[[s01-X3]], alone, on paper, with the folded sentences opened.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, read the excerpt aloud twice and students copy the three headings into the notebook.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: draw three columns on notebook paper.

**Skip.** The background card and the pairs.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Do [[s01-P3-1]] together.
  **Say.** "What fact does each clause carry?" Expect: they kept walking; the traveller urged speed; his arguments seemed to rise in Brown's own mind.
- **6 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K3-1]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "What did the witness report, and what can't it prove?" Expect: a Shape did it; it cannot prove Bishop did.
- **18 min. Read.** Read the excerpt aloud twice, folding the last two sentences under. Then do [[s01-P3-2]].
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Unfold the page and answer [[s01-X3]] alone.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: do [[s01-P3-2]] as a spoken pair task and keep the written chart for the exit. Longer: add a fourth row to the chart naming what Mather assumes before the testimony begins.

### Words and supports

Pious: devoted to religion. Indicted: formally charged. Testimony: what a witness says under oath. Spectre: a ghost, or in 1692 a shape said to be a person's spirit. Implies: suggests without saying. Cannot prove: needs more evidence than the account gives. Frame: The account says __, which implies __, but it cannot show __. Access: large print; the excerpt read aloud once more; the chart filled with a partner; a scribe for the exit. Glossary except on the exit. The student page stays in the folder.

### Tomorrow

Return [[s01-X3]] in day 4's warm-up, error A: a witness's report taken to prove the cause. If many (more than a third) missed it, sort four sentences into reported, implied and unproved. Day 4 turns to word meanings and the 1828 dictionary.

### Credit

Ours. Text: Cotton Mather, The Wonders of the Invisible World (1693), the trial of Bridget Bishop, sections I and II, public domain; the rights line is on the printed excerpt.
