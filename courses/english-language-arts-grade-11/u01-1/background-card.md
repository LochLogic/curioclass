# Salem in 1692: a short background

Teacher: read this aloud before the Mather excerpt, about a minute. It is ours, not Mather's. Say the trials, the accusations and the hangings plainly and move on; nothing is acted out.

In 1692, in and near Salem in Massachusetts, many people were accused of witchcraft. A special court, called a court of Oyer and Terminer, heard the cases. Some of the accusers said that a person's spirit, a "shape" or "spectre," had come to hurt them while the person was somewhere else. This kind of claim is called spectral evidence, and the court at first accepted it. Bridget Bishop was tried on June 2, 1692, and was hanged on June 10, the first person executed that year. Cotton Mather, a minister in Boston, wrote an account of several trials the next year. He was not the judge. He wrote to persuade readers that the court had done right. Our excerpt is his report of her trial.
