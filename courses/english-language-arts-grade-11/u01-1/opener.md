# Section 1.1 opener: what a story shows and what it only implies

## What the section does

Five days. Day 1 opens the question Young Goodman Brown leaves open and asks what the story shows directly and what stays a guess. Day 2 completes the story and builds two defensible readings of Brown's distrust, then rewrites a sentence without dropping a fact. Day 3 reads the first two sections of Cotton Mather's 1693 account of Bridget Bishop's trial and separates what an account claims, implies and cannot prove. Day 4 checks word meanings by inference, family and a printed 1828 entry. Day 5 holds a first seminar and ends with a written synthesis of two positions.

## How the days run

Fifty minutes a day, inside DEFAULTS.md: at most 750 words of teacher talk, at most 450 words of student reading in the room, the at-home reading at most 170 words, and a repair slot of at most 5 minutes in a warm-up. Each exit is on paper and teacher-judged against its look-fors on the record sheet.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **An open question is a claim the text supports two ways.** The staff "might almost be seen to twist" is a thing the story says and does not settle.
- **A report is not proof.** Mather reports what witnesses said, and a confident account can still leave the cause unproved.
- **A word is checked, not trusted.** Infer from the sentence, name the part of speech and a related form, read the entry, then verify.
- **A seminar needs preparation.** Each student arrives with a passage, and the group agrees roles and a goal first.

## The errors, in plain words

- **O:** one reading treated as proved. Sort statements into stated and left open.
- **A:** a confident source taken to prove its cause. Sort reported, implied and unproved.
- **G:** a sentence shortened to a different meaning. Tag each clause, then recombine.
- **C, E, F:** a meaning kept from memory with no clue and no entry read. Copy one named line and reread.
- **H:** copying a supplied list. Choose a word from your own book.
- **I, J, K, L:** an opinion with no prepared passage, a group run by one voice, repetition, and a list of positions with no relation.

## Content notes

The Salem trials, the accusations of witchcraft and the executions are named plainly and never acted out. Brown's forest scene, a witch-meeting, is read for what it says; nothing in it is dramatized. The story uses the period word "Indian" for Native people (paragraphs 9, 18, 43, 51, 53 and 57); read it as printed in 1835, say once that it is of its time, and do not discuss it as a fact about Native people. A student may pass on any question.
