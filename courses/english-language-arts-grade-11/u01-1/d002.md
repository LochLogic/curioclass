# Day 2: Two readings of one open question

## Teacher page

### Before class

Day 1's exit goes back today with error O marked where it occurs. Print the Two readings page and the card. Content note: the witch-meeting and the period word "Indian" (paragraphs 43 to 57) are read as printed in 1835. Hang a blank arc strip with five boxes. Do not mark or flag the paragraph that begins "The next morning"; it is today's exit.

### The day

#### 4 min. Warm-up

Return day 1's exit. Put the wrong statement beside the right one on the board: "The staff moved, so the traveller is the devil" and "The story says the staff seemed to move; it does not say it did." Then students sort [[s01-P2-1]] alone, one statement a row.

**Ask.** Which words of the paragraph made you choose? Expect: bore the likeness of a great black snake for the first statement; might almost be seen to twist for the second, and the narrator's own explanation of an ocular deception for the third. If many (more than a third) missed day 1's exit, rehearse one more statement together; otherwise confer during the reading.

#### 6 min. Retrieval

Alone, students write a claim, one short phrase from the text and a because sentence about the parting talk between Faith and Brown on the card [[s01-R3]]; partners swap. A miss: rebuild the because sentence on the board and the student rewrites it.

Check: the claim answers the question, the phrase is exact, and the because sentence joins them.

**Ask.** Does your because sentence join the phrase to the claim? Expect: it says what the phrase shows and why that supports the claim; a phrase left alone does not.

#### 16 min. Read the second half

Paragraphs 40 to 49 were the read-ahead. Silent reading covers paragraphs 50 to 58 (860 words, about 6 minutes), marking S and Q. Then read paragraphs 59 to 73 aloud to the end (1,410 words, about 9 minutes) while students follow. The paragraph that begins "The next morning" is read with the rest but not marked or discussed. A student who missed the read-ahead reads paragraphs 40 to 49 tonight.

**Ask.** Where did the story turn for you? Expect: Brown sitting on the stump, the pink ribbon, or the next morning.

#### 8 min. Teaching first and worked example

Ask the supporting question: Why does Brown keep walking with the traveller? Students try [[s01-K2-1]] first. Then model with [[steps:s01-K2-1]]. Choose two phrases to make two defensible readings: for a real meeting, a pink ribbon that can be held; for a dream, the question of whether he fell asleep and only dreamed. Neither reading is the answer, and the card holds both.

Begin the wall strip: students name the five places where the story turns.

#### 8 min. Syntax

Pairs answer [[s01-R4]] first (2 minutes). Then the long sentence, 6 minutes: [[s01-P2-2]] with the sentence guide. Pairs name the main verb, which comes late, then tag each fact a clause carries and recombine it two ways.

**Ask.** Which fact does your version put first, and what does that make a reader notice? Expect: starting with the minister puts his power first; starting with Brown's paling puts his fear first.

#### 3 min. Notes

Students fill the Two readings page: for each reading, what the text states and what the reading only adds. It is used again on day 5.

#### 5 min. Exit

[[s01-X2]], alone, on paper.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, copy the card and the page into the notebook from the board.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: write the three lines of the card in the notebook.

**Skip.** The wall strip and the partner swap.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Sort [[s01-P2-1]] together.
  **Say.** "Which words of the paragraph say it?" Expect: carved like a great black snake is stated; whether it really moved is left open.
- **6 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K2-1]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "Which reading does your phrase fit, a real meeting or a dream?" Expect: it fits a dream, and a tempter who knows him, so neither is proved.
- **20 min. Read.** Read paragraphs 40 to 73 aloud together, marking S and Q.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Answer [[s01-X2]] alone, on paper.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: take the syntax part with [[s01-R4]] only and do [[s01-P2-2]] tomorrow; keep the notes. Longer: students write the because sentence for the second reading too.

### Words and supports

Claim: what you say is true. Evidence: a short exact phrase from the text. Reasoning: why the phrase supports the claim. Recombine: put the same facts in a new order. Frame: The text says __, so I think __, but it does not show __. Glossary is allowed except on the exit. The student page stays in the folder. Access: large print; the end of the story may be read aloud; a scribe for the exit; two-column pages may be filled with a partner.

### Tomorrow

Return [[s01-X2]] in day 3's warm-up, errors G and O; take the more common one. Error G: a rewrite that cuts the stern and sad look or the missing greeting and calls the meaning the same; error O: one reading stated as the true one. If many (more than a third) missed it, tag the fact each clause carries in a three-clause sentence, then recombine it without losing one. Day 3 turns to Mather's account of a trial.
