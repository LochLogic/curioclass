# Day 1: What the story says and what it leaves open

## Teacher page

### Before class

Print the story and mark the stop line after paragraph 39, the one that ends "go after her?". Paragraphs 40 to 49 ("You will think better of this" to "beheld a pink ribbon") go home as the read-ahead. Do not mark the paragraph that begins "It was now deep dusk"; it is the exit. Put copies of The Red Badge of Courage on a table.

### The day

#### 8 min. Hook

Read the first five paragraphs aloud. Students fill the Faith page: what Faith says, what Brown makes of it. Content note, 2 of the 8 minutes: say plainly that the Salem trials of the 1690s began with witchcraft accusations and ended in executions, that the story has a witch-meeting and the period word "Indian" (paragraphs 9 and 18), which is of its time, that nothing is staged here, and that anyone may step out and return.

**Ask.** What does Faith say, and what does Brown make of it? Expect: she says a lone woman is troubled by dreams and asks him to stay this night of all nights; he hears a doubt of him ("dost thou doubt me already") and goes. Then write the unit question on the board: What do these stories refuse to settle, and how do we know?

#### 6 min. Retrieval

Students answer [[s01-R1]] and [[s01-R2]] alone, in the same sitting. Note on the record sheet what each student needed, not a score.

**Ask.** Does the speech say it, or do you guess it? Expect: afeard of herself sometimes supports anxiety; that she knows his errand is a guess. A miss is repaired inside the 6 minutes, at most 4: model finding one clause in Faith's second speech, then the student retries on a different sentence of paragraph 3.

#### 5 min. Words before reading

Read one sentence for each word aloud; pairs say what each word means from its sentence. No dictionary yet. Sentences: A melancholy tune drifted from the empty hall. She had scruples about taking the money, so she asked first. The two towns signed a covenant to share the river. After the service the neighbors stayed for communion, passing bread and a cup from hand to hand.

**Ask.** Which words helped? Expect: drifted from the empty hall for melancholy; so she asked first for scruples. Hand out the word cards; the other four words come on day 3.

#### 20 min. Read the first half

Hand out the S and Q key: S is what the story says, Q is what it leaves open. Read paragraphs 1 to 12 aloud (588 words, 4 minutes), pausing after paragraph 7 for [[s01-K1-1]]. Students try first on the student page; then model on the board with [[steps:s01-K1-1]].

Then silent reading of paragraphs 13 to 39 (1,417 words), marking S or Q beside any note with the words that made them choose (about 12 minutes). Students read the paragraph that begins "It was now deep dusk" with the rest but do not mark it, and no one discusses it. A student who has not reached the line finishes at home; the day does not wait.

**Ask.** Which mark was hardest? Expect: Brown's own guess, as in "Methought", feels like fact and is Q.

#### 5 min. Set-up: shelf and home reading

Open the independent shelf; The Red Badge of Courage is the checked option. Each student writes a nightly target of 20 to 30 minutes; shared reading comes first.

#### 6 min. Exit

[[s01-X1]], alone, on paper. Collect it; read it after class against the key.

### Other rooms

No devices are needed. Without a printer, read the story aloud in two sittings and students note S and Q by paragraph number.

### At home

**Swap.** No printer: read the story aloud together and keep S and Q notes on paper.

**Skip.** The class hook and the readiness look.

- **5 min. Hook.** Read Faith's first speech aloud, then answer [[s01-R1]].
  **Say.** "Which words say it, and which part is a guess?" Expect: afeard of herself sometimes is stated; that she knows his errand is a guess.
- **6 min. Example.** Try [[s01-K1-1]], then read its answer together.
  **Say.** "What does the paragraph say, and what does Brown only guess?" Expect: his heart smote him is stated; the dream warning is his guess.
- **20 min. Read.** Read paragraphs 1 to 39 to the stop line, marking S and Q. Paragraphs 40 to 49 are the evening read-ahead.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** Answer [[s01-X1]] alone, on paper.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: the teacher reads paragraphs 13 to 18 aloud as well, and the silent reading starts at paragraph 19. Longer: students who finish early mark where Brown's guess and the narrator's statement part in paragraph 8.

### Words and supports

The week 1 card glosses melancholy, scruples, covenant and communion. Frame: The text says __, so I think __, but it does not show __. Access: large print; the story read aloud once more; a scribe for the exit; a bilingual glossary except on the exit. Record each support a student used on the record sheet.

### Tomorrow

Return [[s01-X1]] in day 2's warm-up, error O: one reading treated as proved. If many (more than a third) missed it, open day 2 with the wrong example beside the right. Day 2 starts from the read-ahead (paragraphs 40 to 49), finishes the story and builds two defensible readings.
