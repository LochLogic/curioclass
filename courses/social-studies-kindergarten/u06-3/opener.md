# Section 6.3 opener: our class store, why people work, and the check

## What the section does

Days 57 to 61 finish the unit "Work and trade". Day 57 sets up a class store: six stations, a map with a key, price tags of one to five tokens. Day 58 is store day: buy, sell and trade with paper tokens, choose between two wants, and save a token if you want. Day 59 answers *Why do people work?* with "I think ..., because ..." from the sources of the unit. Day 60 is an observation task with fresh cards and no help. Day 61 re-teaches the parts the record shows and ends with a brief fresh try. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes. No child reads. Prices are dots with a numeral; tokens are paper and worth one each.

## How the days run

Day 57 reopens the F and G repair and the day 26 map and key, then the teacher sells a drawing for two tokens and a paper-folding service for three, roles swap, and tables place price tags and shelf symbols. Day 58 reopens E and F, gives each child five tokens in a named envelope, and runs the store while the teacher hears the day's ten. Day 59 begins with a saved token bought, reopens D and K, hears the next ten, and closes the wonder wall. Day 60 is the observation task: two minutes of directions, five blocks of three minutes, no completed example visible, and a quiet close. Day 61 returns the record by part, privately, and runs three groups for Work, Choice and Exchange.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A buyer pays; a seller is paid. A price is what the buyer gives. A shelf symbol stands for a shelf on the map. Choosing between two wants is reasoned by naming the limit (five tokens, two items that cost three each) and a reason; saving is a good answer. A claim ("I think people work to help others") needs a reason from a source ("because Work Around Us shows ..."). Helping, caring and earning each support a claim. How much money someone has never tells how hard they worked.

## Care

Tokens are paper and worth one each; there are no coins or bills and no real money is used. A child may say no to any trade or sale and may pass. No result is said aloud. The record is kept by part, not by child, and is never read aloud. No child is asked what they have or what their family buys; the store runs on tokens every child gets. Invented workers only.

## The errors, in plain words

- E: "Buying and selling are the same." Pip pays tokens and says he is the seller. Right: the one who pays is the buyer; the one paid is the seller.
- H: "People work because they work." Right: people work to help others, because a source shows a worker helping.
- L: how much money someone has does not tell how hard they worked. Used only as an adult-read invented scene.

A puppet says the wrong idea. A child's answer is never the example.

## Supplies

A large store map, shelf symbols, a key box, price tags with dots and numerals, 150 paper tokens, named envelopes, six station sheets, Our Store Record, source cards for Work Around Us, Jobs Book and Our Store Record, and crayons. A room with nothing: draw the map on scrap paper, tear tokens from scrap, and write dots on slips for prices.

## Exit tickets

Exits for days 57 and 58 are new cases printed "invented for practice" and never used in teaching or in the store. Day 59 has one stem. The marking scale is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
