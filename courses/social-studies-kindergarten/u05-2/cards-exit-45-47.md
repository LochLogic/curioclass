# Exit cards 45 to 47

New picture cards for the exits of days 45, 46 and 47, printed "invented for practice" along the bottom edge. Plain line drawings, thick outlines, no writing except that line. The teacher says "this is a pretend school" and reads every label and prompt aloud. Never used in teaching (rule 4). The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## Day 45

**Mark card:** a new close view of a flag on a pretend school's pole: thirteen stripes and a block of stars. Each child puts one token on the part that stands for one state today. **Spoken, rotating group:** a puppet says "blue on the flag stands for fairness because the old flag law says so." "Does the old flag law say that?"

## Day 46

**Mark card:** a pretend museum's old brass plate on a door, with a round seal on it, the Great Seal of the United States, showing a bird, a shield and a banner. Each child circles the bird on the seal. **Spoken 1, rotating group:** "Why can this bird stand for the United States?" **Spoken 2, same six:** "What could we ask our book about this bird?"

## Day 47

**Mark card:** three new pictures of a pretend class taking turns on a swing. In one the line moves along and each child in it gets a turn, and a child who stands quietly while the others say the flag words waits and gets a turn too. In one the children who say the flag words get turns and the others sit on the bench. In one the class must say the flag words together first, and then the turns begin. The teacher describes each aloud. Each child circles the picture that is fair for everyone. **Spoken, rotating group:** a puppet holds up the anthem page and says it does not know where the song came from. "How did our national anthem get its words?"

## Key (teacher)

45: a token on a star (one state today; fifty stars), not a stripe; the old law gave no reason for the colors, and people say what colors mean. 46: circle the eagle; leaders picked the bald eagle for the Great Seal, a bird that lives in North America; a question the page can answer, such as "Who picked it?", with the child pointing to the page. 47: the picture where every child gets a turn, including the child standing quietly; Francis Scott Key saw the big flag still flying after a battle and wrote words about it, and a law later chose it.
