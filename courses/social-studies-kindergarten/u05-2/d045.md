# Day 45: The flag, and how we know

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang page 1 and set the two bins side by side. Pick today's six.

### The day

#### 2 min. Reopen

Post: "What do symbols tell us, and how do we know?" Review: symbol, from unit 3. A national symbol stands for our country.

#### 4 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 44's exit (error H), whole class: the thank-you wall beside the quiet flag; Veterans Day thanks all who served, Memorial Day remembers those who died serving. Otherwise a small group.

#### 7 min. Teaching first

Read page 1; its maker is us, in 2026. Review: "Who made it, and why?" from unit 3. The flag stands for our country: thirteen stripes for the first thirteen states, and one star for each state, fifty today. Sources: a reference article, the 1777 resolution and the 1818 act.

Sort two statements. "Long, long ago, in 1777, a law said thirteen stripes, red and white" goes in the old paper bin: the record says. "People say red means being brave" goes in the speech bubble bin: people say it means. The old law gave no reason for the colors; say only red.

Model: "My question for the page is: what does a star stand for?" Pledge begun: a promise of loyalty to the country the flag stands for. We explain the words; nobody recites, and saying is a choice.

#### 10 min. Supported work

Say: "This is a pretend class." Pairs place the two statements in the bins, then put a token on a star and a stripe of the practice flag and say what each stands for. Hear the six, 60 seconds each: "What does a star stand for?" Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Exit

Every child puts a token on the part of the exit flag that stands for one state today ([[s05-X45a]]). Moss says blue stands for fairness because the old flag law says so. Ask the six, "Does the old flag law say that?" ([[s05-X45b]])

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No tokens: use buttons. No flag print: draw thirteen stripes and a block of stars.

**Skip.** The bins.

- **5 min. The flag.** Draw a flag. Say the stripes are the first thirteen states and each star is one state.
  **Say.** "What does a star stand for? A stripe?" Expect: a state; one of the first states.
- **5 min. Two papers.** Draw an old paper and a speech bubble.
  **Say.** "Which holds 'a law in 1777 said thirteen stripes'?" Expect: the old paper.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X45a]] [[s05-X45b]] Ask the one that fits; wait.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: sort or flag. Longer: a second question.

### Words and supports

Flag, stripe, star, pledge. A point counts.

### Tomorrow

If many chose a stripe (error C) or said the law gave a color's meaning (error J), day 46 repairs it. Day 46: the bald eagle.
