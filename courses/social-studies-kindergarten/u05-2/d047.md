# Day 47: The anthem, and fair for everyone

## Teacher page

### Before class

Hang pages 3 and 4 and the day 46 eagle page. Pick today's six. Nobody sings or recites.

### The day

#### 5 min. Reopen and repair

Point to the eagle page.

**Ask.** Who picked the eagle for the seal? Expect: leaders.

If many missed day 46's exit, repair the one most missed, four minutes. Error B: a flower beside the eagle; ask the page what each stands for. Error D: trace the eagle to the old paper. Error I: Moss guesses, then asks the page.

#### 8 min. Teaching first

Read page 3; its maker is us, in 2026. In 1814 Francis Scott Key watched a battle near a fort, saw the big flag still flying and wrote words about it. Much later, in 1931, a law made the song our national anthem: the song a law chose for the country. Source: the National Park Service.

Read page 4: liberty and justice for all means freedom and fair treatment for everyone. Review: fair, from unit 1. Saying the Pledge is a choice; a child who stands quietly is respected. Learning the words and saying them differ (error E).

Moss says, one at a time: "The song everybody cheered at the game is our anthem." A law chose it (error K). "Only children who say the words may use the art table." Fair is for everyone (error L). "I can say it fast, so I know what it means." Say it in your own words (error N).

#### 10 min. Supported work

Say: "This is a pretend class." Pairs answer a page question, such as "Who wrote the words?", and say what fair for everyone means. Hear the six, 60 seconds each: "A puppet says its favorite tune is our national anthem. Does liking a song make it the anthem?" ([[s05-X47b]]). Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 2 min. Exit

Describe each of the three exit pictures aloud. Every child circles the picture that is fair for everyone ([[s05-X47a]]).

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No book: draw a fort at sunrise with a big flag.

**Skip.** The pairs.

- **5 min. The song.** Say: in 1814 Key saw the flag still flying and wrote words; in 1931 a law made the song our anthem. Nobody sings.
  **Say.** "What made it the anthem?" Expect: a law.
- **5 min. Fair.** Draw children sharing one box of paints.
  **Say.** "What does fair for everyone mean?" Expect: everyone is treated well.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X47a]] [[s05-X47b]] Ask the one that fits; wait.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: anthem or Pledge page. Longer: a second question.

### Words and supports

Anthem, law, fair, choice. A point counts.

### Tomorrow

If many gave error K, E, L or N, day 48 repairs the one most missed. Day 48 begins traditions.
