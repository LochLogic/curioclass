# Fresh cards for the day 9 check

Three sets of pictures the class has never seen. Keep them in an envelope until day 9: no day before it shows or names them. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of every card, and say before each: "We made this card. It is pretend, not about anyone here." Plain black line drawings, no brand names. A sign is a picture sign; you read its notice aloud in one flat voice and say nothing about why it is there. Describe only what is drawn ("a cart stands in the doorway"). A neutral prompt ("Tell me more") is allowed; supplying the reason is not.

## School Doorway

**Picture, top:** The front of a pretend school. Beside the open door a picture sign shows a doorway with a small cart crossed by a line. **Notice (you read it):** "Keep this doorway clear."

**Picture, left panel:** A wheeled book trolley stands across the doorway. A line of children with backpacks waits outside, and one child at the front is pointing at the gap beside the trolley, which is too narrow to pass.

**Picture, right panel:** The same door with the trolley pushed against the hall wall. The line of children walks in, one after another.

## Garden Path

**Picture, top:** A pretend public garden. A picture sign on a post at the start of the path shows a shoe on a stone path. **Notice (you read it):** "Stay on the path."

**Picture, left panel:** A garden bed beside the path with neat rows of tiny green seedlings, each standing up, and a watering can at its end. **Picture, right panel:** A second bed of the same size. A line of footprints crosses it, and the seedlings along the line lie flat and bent.

## Playground-ball question cards

**Question card:** A large question mark above a playground ball. **You read:** "Where should our playground balls live? Do you want them in a net bag by the door, or in a bin in the hall?"

**Choice card 1:** A net bag hangs on a hook beside the classroom door, full of balls. A child reaches up and takes one ball easily.

**Choice card 2:** A low open bin stands against the hall wall, full of balls. A child bends and takes one ball easily.

Each child chooses freely. No view is assigned, and a classmate may choose the same or the other.

## Key (teacher)

- **School Doorway, why the rule exists (item 3):** the trolley blocks the doorway so the line cannot get in; clear, everyone can enter. Pointing to the two panels counts. Not enough: "Because the teacher says so."
- **School Doorway, the rule in school (item 4):** names the doing and the effect: keep the doorway clear so people can get in.
- **Garden Path, why the rule exists (item 3):** walking across the bed flattens the seedlings; the path keeps the plants safe. Not enough: "It is a rule" or "grown-ups said."
- **If a rule does not help (item 4):** tell the class and the teacher and try a change together. Not enough: "I would change it myself."
- **Playground balls (item 6):** the child says own choice and the partner's, and what is alike (both can reach the balls) or what differs (door or hall). Agreement with an accurate comparison counts. Ranking a classmate ("Jo is wrong") does not.
- **Compelling question (item 7):** a claim, one detail from a picture, and because: "The path keeps the plants safe, so the garden rule helps." Listening or fairness may be defended instead. Not enough: "It is good because it is good."
- **Why-question (item 8):** a partner asks "Why do you think that?" or "Why do you think that rule helps?" and the speaker explains. Not enough: a question about a color or a count.
