# Section 1.3 opener: talking and giving reasons

## What the section does

Five days, 6 to 10. Day 6: three talk rules for the class circle (listen to the speaker, wait for a turn, answer what was said). Day 7: two points of view about one class question, side by side, without deciding who is right. Day 8: the spoken "I think ..., because ..." and the partner's "Why do you think that?" Day 9: the observation task, in groups of six, that is this unit's end-of-unit assessment. Day 10: small groups re-teach what the observation showed, then each child gets a brief fresh try.

## How the days run

Twenty minutes a day (30 on the longer route). Days 6 to 8: two-minute reopen, a short model, supported work, a two-minute exit. Day 9: two minutes to start, fifteen for five groups of six, three for a wonder wall look back. Day 10: two to reconnect, ten for at most four groups, eight to finish children not yet heard. Notes before day 9 are practice; score only the later attempts.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **Listening means answering what was said**, not sitting still. A child using a communication aid, signing or moving is listening. Eye contact and stillness are not look-fors.
- **Two views can both be fine.** "We both want the paintings seen, but I like the window wall and my friend likes the door wall" compares ideas. It never ranks people.
- **The because is the point of a claim.** Picture details limit it: "because it is best" is not a reason. Neutral prompts ("Why do you think that?") are allowed without supplying content.
- **A pass is an answer.** A child may pass in the circle and show the evidence privately. An unheard response is "not yet observed", never a failure.

## The errors, in plain words

- **C:** listening means sitting perfectly still. Model a response to meaning while sitting or standing.
- **I:** a reply that ignores the idea. Match each reply to the idea heard before exchanging turns.
- **D:** "We disagree, so one person is bad." Place two reasons side by side and name agreement or difference without ranking people.
- **E:** "It is best because it is best." Point to the pictured result and say "because" together.
- **F and L:** no why-question, or an answer that repeats the claim. Model asking, then answering with "because".

## Supplies for the section

Paper, crayons, tape, chart paper for the talk-rule poster, a smooth stone for a talking piece, Our Class Charter from day 5, and printed pictures: three talk-rule pictures, four idea cards with eight replies, a question card with two choices and two reasons and column headings, the teaching card, four exit cards, and the withheld assessment pictures (not shown before day 9). The record sheet keeps practice and scored attempts apart.

## A room with nothing

Draw each card on scrap paper. The talk-rule poster is chart paper with three pictures. A stone or a block serves as the talking piece. Pairs, not a screen, hold the cards; a shared screen may show them but each child marks their own paper.
