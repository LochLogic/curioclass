# Exit cards, days 6 to 8 and 10

Day 9 has no exit card. Each card is marked by every child (circle, point or draw a line) and a spoken part is heard from the day's group of six. Print "invented for practice" in small letters at the bottom of each. None reuses a picture from the talk-rule pictures, the reply cards, the two-view cards or the teaching card.

## Day 6 exit card: answering what was said

**Picture:** Three small pictures in a row, each in a circle. (a) Two children: one points at a row of scarves on a low hook; the second points at the same scarves with a smile. (b) A child sits perfectly still, arms folded, looking straight ahead, a small blank bubble above. (c) A child points at a toy truck, with the first child's scarf bubble behind.

**Mark:** Point to the picture that shows answering what was said, not changing the subject. The key is (a). (b) is the still-sitting picture, which shows C; (c) changes the subject. **Spoken:** the teacher reads "I want the scarves hung lower so we can reach them." "Show how to answer that idea after your turn comes."

## Day 7 exit card: two puppets and their reasons

**Picture:** A left column of two puppets, Pia (round head) and Tov (a tall head with a star on top). A right column of two reason pictures, in a different order: a quiet room with one child listening; a table with paper and crayons ready. Space for a line from each puppet to its reason.

**Mark:** Draw a line from each puppet to its reason. Pia wants drawing first because the paper is ready; Tov wants listening first because the room is quiet. **Spoken:** "How are their ideas alike or different?"

## Day 8 exit card: the sand table

**Picture:** Two children reaching for one blue bucket at a sand table. Beside the table a turn card shows two hands passing a bucket. Below: three rule pictures in circles. (a) The turn card (the key). (b) The bucket on a high shelf. (c) Two hands pulling at one bucket from opposite sides.

**Mark:** Circle the rule picture that helps. **Spoken:** "Which rule helps this group? Why do you think so?" Then a partner asks a why-question and the child answers.

## Day 10 exit card: the museum

**Picture:** A small pretend museum with glass cases. A cluster of visitors of many ages looks at a clay pot in a case. Beside it, a sign picture shows a hand hovering over a pot, crossed by a line. Below: three pictures in circles. (a) A whole clay pot in its case (the key). (b) A pair of shoes. (c) The museum door.

**Mark:** Circle what the rule protects. **Spoken:** "Why this rule?" Answer: others can still see the objects whole. This is the fresh try; recheck other missed parts with a new two-view or claim card.
