# Section 3.1 opener: words that say where

## What the section does

Days 20 and 21 open the unit "Where things are", whose question is *How can we show someone where things are?* Day 20 teaches the position words near, far, left, right, behind, in front of, between and next to with real objects. Day 21 uses the same words on the playground and on a flat picture map of the classroom. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and nothing is read by children.

## How the days run

Day 20 opens with a puppet who asks where the crayons are. You first point and say "over there", then say where they are with words: beside the paper. You post the unit question and scribe three wonders on cards without names. You move a toy car, a box, a basket and a toy child one thing at a time, and then add a second object so that "between" can be seen. Pairs then place small objects from a tray and say where each one is. Day 21 begins with a short game, then shows where things are on the playground from the Our Place Record photograph (or on the playground itself if the class is already outside), and on the classroom picture map held the same way up for everyone. Each pair puts a toy person on the map and says where two things are. You scribe one sentence under each child's map: "The ___ is ___ the ___, from here."

## The ideas, at an adult's level

Left and right depend on whose side you stand on. A toy that is left of a box for a child on this side is right of it for a child on the other side, and neither child is wrong, so the class says the viewpoint every time ("from here", or "with the page this way up"). Between always needs two things. Near and far compare two places and are not measured. Many five-year-olds swap left and right; the words are practiced in use, never tested for speed, and a slip is noted and not marked down. Nobody is asked where anything is at home, and nobody is the example of a mistake: a puppet or a teacher card says every wrong idea.

## The errors, in plain words

- A: "Left is the way my hand points." Turn the viewer or the card and name the new relation with "from here".
- J: "Between needs only one thing." Put one object beside a toy child, ask what is between, then add a second object.
- K: "Left and right are swapped." Turn the card the same way up for everyone and name left and right on the page before the place.

## Supplies

A puppet (two folded-paper puppets are enough), a toy car, a box, a basket, a toy child, a tray of small objects for each pair, wonder cards, the unit question card, the Our Place Record photograph of the playground, a classroom picture map for each pair, a toy person, exit tokens, crayons. Every piece can be felt: a child who cannot see touches each object and the hand-placed relation. Large print and high contrast on every card.

## A room with nothing

Two lunch boxes, a toy and a lid; chalk lines on the floor; a hand-drawn room picture on scrap paper with a mark at the top edge.

## Exit tickets

Both are new cases printed "invented for practice" and never used in teaching. Every child marks a picture; the spoken "why" is heard from a rotating group of six each day, and the rest are recorded not yet heard.
