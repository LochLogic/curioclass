# School Garden (day 29, the fresh source)

Fresh source for items 1, 3 and 7. Withheld from teaching and from every exit (rule 4): no day before day 29 and no exit card shows it, and its symbols are used on no other page. Printed large, one copy for the room and one for each table; the map faces the same way for everyone. An invented place, not a photograph of our school. The teacher says so aloud: "The illustrator made this place up. It is not our school." The illustrated view is turned face down while children answer the "Use the key" questions (items 1 and 3), so the key and not the picture tells which symbol is which; turn it up afterward.

**The overhead map.** A page with a thick border, a sun picture at the top edge so everyone holds it the same way. From the page's left: a school, a pond in the middle, a garden at the right. A bench sits between the pond and the garden. Two small circles (trees) sit just above the bench, toward the top of the page. A path curves around the pond from the school to the garden, so people go from one to the other without going through the water. Each place is a plain symbol: a triangle for the school, an oval for the pond, a square for the bench, a star for the garden, a small circle for each tree. Use outlines and patterns, not color alone.

**The illustrated view.** The same place drawn as a scene, seen from the front, with the school at the left, the pond in the middle, the bench and then the garden at the right. Two trees stand behind the bench. The path curves around the pond.

**The boxed key.** A box at the page's edge with one row for each symbol: the symbol on the left and a small picture of the real thing on the right, with a one-word label under each picture: school, pond, bench, garden, tree. The key is on the page. The teacher reads a key word only when a child points to that key entry, and never says which map symbol matches it.

**Use (day 29):** items 1, 3 and 7. Item 1: "Use the key. Where is the school compared with the pond? Where is the bench compared with the pond and the garden?" Item 3 (the fresh-source half): "Use the key to show two places. How does the pond affect the path?" Item 7: "How can we show someone where things are? Use a detail from a named source to explain your answer."

## Key (teacher)

The school is left of the pond, with the page this way up; the bench is between the pond and the garden. Two places from the key: for example the triangle is the school and the star is the garden. The path bends around the pond so people can go from school to garden without going through the water. "The school is right of the pond" is error K; "the bench is between" with no second place is error J; "the triangle is a real school building" is error C; "the star means pond" is error H, because the key assigns the star to the garden; "maps help because maps help" is error G.
