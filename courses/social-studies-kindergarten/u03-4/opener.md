# Section 3.4 opener: addresses, finding our school, and the check

## What the section does

Days 27 to 30 answer "How does an address help locate a school?" and close the unit. Day 27 teaches the parts of an address with two real public buildings and then the school's own address: number and street, town or city, state, country. The class's state and country are taught on days 27 and 28, so later items ask the child to name all five parts. Day 28 finds a town on a state map and a school on that town's street map, and children redraw their day 26 map from written feedback. Day 29 is an observation task with a place they have not seen, School Garden, and fresh maps. Day 30 is the respond day: small groups re-teach what the observation showed, then a brief fresh try. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and nothing is read by children; every strip and label is read aloud.

## How the days run

Day 27 sets the address strips into rings, country largest, for the Massachusetts State House (24 Beacon Street, Boston, Massachusetts, United States) and the Washington State Legislative Building (416 Sid Snyder Avenue Southwest, Olympia, Washington, United States), then pairs do the school's own address. The school is the shared case. Day 28: four minutes to redraw the map from the feedback card, then six minutes in table groups to find the school's town on a state map and the school on the town's street map, with help recorded. A few minutes on our state in the United States is an extension that is never assessed and is dropped if time is short. Day 29 has four parts: you introduce School Garden, children give individual picture responses to items 1, 2, 5 and 6 with no partner answers, you hear the explanations for items 2 and 3 (the rest of the class quietly adds one picture to the class key card), and you close the wonder wall. Day 30 sets four tables named by task and never as the children who missed (Words, Key, Map, Address), with pairs working on the claim and because, then fresh tries for each child's missed part and the closing claims heard with a named source.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

An address nests: a number on a street, in a town, in a state, in a country. A state map and a street map show one place at two sizes. Some children have no stable home address or are uneasy about it, so the school is the example for everyone and no home address is asked, written or compared. Practice addresses are invented and labeled so. The two real buildings are public, and their addresses come from official pages read on the date in sources.csv. Recheck every local fact before printing. Day 29 sorts each record cell as independent, content-prompted or not yet observed; a child right only on the taught crop is content-prompted. Silence is not an error. A child who communicates with symbols, pointing or an aid is given the time and judged on geography alone, apart from English, speech, handwriting, vision and motor performance.

## The errors, in plain words

- E: "Town, state and country are the same." Mixed-up labels beside correctly nested cards; the child names street, town, state and country.
- F: "The state's name tells where the school is." Use the town dot, then the street.
- G: "It helps because it helps." Name a detail from the source.
- On day 30 the other letters are repaired with the contrasts written in sections 3.1 to 3.3: Words (A, J, K), Key (B, C, H, I), Map (D, L).

## Supplies

Address strips (read aloud), floor hoops or string rings, the school's own address on strips, state and street maps (original and simplified; see the maps piece), response rows, School Garden, the record sheet, tokens, crayons. Raised or string rings, large-print strips, a tactile dot for the town, an adult placing pieces as the child directs. Collect every response before showing an answer.

## A room with nothing

Chalk or string rings, hand-lettered strips, a hand-drawn street map.

## Exit tickets

E27, E28 and E30 are new cases printed "invented for practice", never used in teaching, never a place to visit. Day 29 has no exit ticket: its seven items are the evidence. The spoken part is heard from a rotating group of six, except on day 29 and day 30 as set in the section plan.
