# Response rows (day 29)

Fresh cards for the individual picture responses on day 29, items 1, 2, 5 and 6, plus the picture for item 3. These are the end items' own cards (rule 4): no other page in the unit shows any of them. Every place, name and address is invented, and each card is printed "invented for practice, not a place to visit" along the bottom edge. Large print, thick outlines, no brands. Each child answers alone by pointing or marking; no partner answers. Collect every response before showing an answer. Silence is not an error, and pointing, an aid or symbols count the same as speech.

## Row 1: School Garden

Uses the School Garden page (school-garden.md). The teacher reads the key only when a child points to a key entry. **Spoken:** "Use the key. Where is the school compared with the pond? Where is the bench compared with the pond and the garden?"

## Row 2: two world pictures

A second small desk globe turned to its Africa side, beside a flat world map with Africa in the middle; land patterned, water plain; neither used on days 22 or 23. A child with help also uses the Two World Maps cards from section 3.2. **Spoken:** "Show land and water on both. Tell one way they are alike. What can you see on the flat map that the globe does not show at the same time?" Then, on Two World Maps: "What is in the middle of each? What does each help us see together?"

## Row 3: the near place

One card, a plain scene of a place near a school: a crossing, a bench and a small shop, drawn with large simple shapes. The child points to two places on it and says what is near what. The teacher then asks the child to say or point to where the same kind of place is near our school. **Spoken:** "Show two places on this card. What is near the school where you learn? Tell what the pictures show."

## Row 3b: picture graph and key

A bounded table patch with three buttons and no beads, plus a blank two-row graph card. After the child makes the graph, the School Garden key is used to name two places. **Spoken:** "Make a graph with one picture for each thing counted. Use the key to show two places in the garden."

## Row 4: a revised drawing

Each child's day 28 map or model, with the feedback card beside it. **Spoken:** "What did you change? Show me." The revised graph counts are checked against the table patch as in row 3b.

## Row 5: the address strips

Five strips in a mixed order (not the order of the rings), the same for every child, large print: **Ohio** | **Meadow Lane** | **United States** | **9** | **Clearwater**. The teacher touches and reads one strip at a time, rereads on request. **Spoken:** "Name the number, the street, the town, the state and the country."

## Row 6: our own state and street

The child's school's own state map with the town dot, and the street map of the town with the school on its street (made as in maps-state-and-street.md, but cropped or scaled differently). **Spoken:** "Which state is this outline? Show the town. Now show the school. Which street helps you?" A child still learning the state's name may point to its name card as you say it: shown with help (shown, shown with help, not yet seen).

## Row 7: the closing claim

No card. **Spoken:** "How can we show someone where things are? Use a detail from a named source to explain your answer." The named source can be School Garden, a map or a card from the unit.

## Key (teacher)

Row 1: the school is left of the pond with the page this way up; the bench is between the pond and the garden. Row 2: the globe and the flat map both show Earth's land and water, and the flat map has been spread out from the round one; "they are two different Earths" is error B and "every map shows only a small place" is error I. Row 3: any two places named with their neighbor; the key is not a lookup for what is near our school. Row 3b: three button pictures in the button row, a labeled bead row with no marks (error L); the key's two places named with their symbols (error H). Row 4: changes keyed to the feedback (error D), the map matched to the real place (error C). Row 5: 9 the number, Meadow Lane the street, Clearwater the town, Ohio the state, United States the country; "Clearwater is the country" is error E; so is "Meadow Lane is the town." Row 6: our state's outline named or shown with help; the town dot, then the school on the street map; pointing to the whole state is error F. Row 7: a detail from a named source; "it helps because it helps" is error G.
