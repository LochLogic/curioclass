# Sharing the Glue

A picture read-aloud for day 3. Twelve pages. Say first: "We made this picture for our class. The dolls are pretend, and this is a made-up story." Nothing in it points at a child in the room, and nothing bad happens to a person: what changes is the glue stick. Read the words under each page and let children look.

Drawing style for all pages: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words. Mimi and Bo are cloth dolls with yarn hair and button eyes: Mimi has two braids, Bo has a short tuft. Both have a simple smile. The classroom table holds two sheets of paper, each with a bare brown tree trunk drawn in crayon, a pile of paper leaves and one glue stick with a cap.

## Page 1

**Picture:** Mimi and Bo sit at a table. Each has a paper with a bare tree. A small pile of paper leaves is between them. One glue stick stands upright in the middle, with its cap beside it.

**Read:** Mimi and Bo made leaf pictures. They had one glue stick.

## Page 2

**Picture:** Mimi holds the glue stick and gluing leaves quickly onto her tree. Bo sits with folded hands, looking at the glue stick.

**Read:** Mimi glued and glued. Bo's leaves waited.

## Page 3

**Picture:** Close view of the glue stick lying on the table with its cap off, a little way from it. The top looks rough and crumbly.

**Read:** The cap was off. The glue stick began to dry.

## Page 4

**Picture:** The glue stick has rolled off the table and lies on the floor under it. A dotted line shows the path across the floor, and a small footprint outline stands next to the stick.

**Read:** The glue stick rolled onto the floor. Now a foot could step on it and slip.

## Page 5

**Picture:** The two papers side by side. Mimi's tree is full of leaves. Bo's tree is bare. The glue stick on the floor is dry and cracked.

**Read:** Mimi's tree had leaves. Bo's tree had none. The glue stick was dry. Nobody could use it. Mimi had not meant any harm. There was no rule yet.

## Page 6

**Picture:** Mimi and Bo look at each other. A thought bubble between them holds a question mark and a picture of two hands.

**Read:** Mimi and Bo thought, "What could help us both?"

## Page 7

**Picture:** A rule card in a thick frame, drawn as two pictures: two arrows passing the glue stick between two hands, and a glue stick with its cap on.

**Read:** Their rule: take turns, and put the cap on.

## Page 8

**Picture:** A new glue stick, cap on the table. Mimi glues one leaf, then holds the glue stick out to Bo.

**Read:** Mimi glued one leaf. Then she passed the glue to Bo.

## Page 9

**Picture:** Bo gluing a leaf while Mimi waits with her paper, smiling. Her paper has a few leaves already.

**Read:** Bo glued one leaf. Mimi waited, and soon it was her turn again.

## Page 10

**Picture:** The cap clicked back on the glue stick, which now stands in a cup in the middle of the table. A hand is pressing the cap.

**Read:** At the end the cap went on. The glue stick went back in its cup.

## Page 11

**Picture:** Two full trees side by side, one on each paper. Mimi and Bo smile at their pictures. The glue stick stands in its cup.

**Read:** Two trees, both with leaves. The glue was still good for tomorrow.

## Page 12

**Picture:** Two small panels. Left: the table with no rule, a bare tree, a glue stick on the floor. Right: the table with the rule, two full trees, the glue stick in its cup. A large question mark hangs above both.

**Read:** Without a rule. With a rule. Why do we have this rule?

## For the teacher

Model the question and an answer, then children echo both: "Why do we have this rule?" Answer in one of three ways, with a finger on the picture: "to be safe" (page 4: the glue stick off the floor), "to be fair" (page 8 and 9: a turn each), "to help everyone learn" (page 11: both finish). No one is asked who was wrong; the rule fixed what happened to the glue and the pictures.
