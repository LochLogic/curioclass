# Section 1.2 opener: why we have rules

## What the section does

Three days, 3 to 5. Day 3: a picture story (Sharing the Glue) shows what happens to a shared thing with a rule and without one. Day 4: three rules outside school, a crosswalk, the library and a playground sign, with who made each and why. Day 5: the class proposes rules for its own room with a reason for each. That list is Our Class Charter. The teacher models the claim frame "I think we need this rule, because ..." for the first time.

## How the days run

Twenty minutes a day (30 on the longer route): reopen two minutes, six minutes of modelling, ten minutes of supported work in pairs, exit two minutes. A rotating group of six is heard on the spoken "why" each day. One ten-minute bridge from the start-of-year check may run in supported work, never two on a day.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A rule is a shared agreement with a reason.** It is not an adult's command. Children hear "Why do we have this rule?" and an answer, then echo both.
- **Three reasons are enough:** safe, fair, helps everyone learn. Here "fair" means everyone gets a turn or a share.
- **A consequence happens to the shared thing.** The glue dries, the plants get crushed. Nobody is punished, no child is singled out and no one acts out breaking a rule.
- **A rule can change, through the people who share it.** The class and the teacher try a change together. Rules about water, roads or equipment are explained, not renegotiated.
- **The charter is real; the story and exit cards are invented** and printed "invented for practice".

## The errors, in plain words

- **B:** "It is a rule because grown-ups say so." Act the rule with and without it and say who benefits.
- **G:** "I only follow it when reminded." Rehearse one easy rule with a picture cue, then fade the reminder.
- **E:** "The rule is good because it is good." Point to the pictured result and say "because" together.
- **F:** A child names a fact when asked to ask why. Model a why-question, then children echo it.
- **H:** "Each person chooses whether a shared rule applies." Contrast a private choice with a rule for everyone.
- **J:** "I can change a shared rule alone." Model proposing a change and agreeing it with those affected.

## Supplies for the section

Paper, crayons, one glue stick, two folded-paper dolls, tape for a crosswalk line on the floor, chart paper, and printed pictures: Sharing the Glue (12 pages), matching cards (6), rule-maker cards (3 and a places strip), claim-frame cards, three exit cards. You prepare three candidate-rule pictures from the room before day 5.

## A room with nothing

Draw the cards on scrap paper. Two folded-paper dolls and a glue stick tell the story. Mark a crosswalk on the floor with tape or a drawn line. Write the charter on the back of a poster; a picture and the children's words beside each rule are enough.
