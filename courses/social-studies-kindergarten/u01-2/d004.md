# Day 4: Who made the rule?

## Teacher page

### Before class

Envelope the rule-maker cards and places strip, one set a pair. Keep the wonder wall in sight. Choose today's six, not yet heard on a spoken exit, and the bridge group from day 1's s01-R1.

### The day

#### 2 min. Reopen

Hold up the take-turns card from day 3. Ask what it protects: a turn for each. If many gave error B on day 3's exit (s01-X3, "because the teacher says so"), set "the teacher says so" beside "wet paintings on the rack keep clothes clean" and point to the second.

#### 6 min. Rule-maker cards

Before each card, say its maker and place. "People who look after the road made this. It is for the street." Show the crosswalk, then the library's quiet-voices sign (the librarian), then the playground sign (park staff).

Model "Who made it? What is it for?" with an answer: "Road workers made it. It keeps people safe." Children echo both.

**Ask.** Who made the library sign? What is it for? Expect: the librarian; so everyone can read and think. Pointing counts.

#### 10 min. Supported work

Pairs sort each card to its maker and to a reason: safe, fair, or helps everyone. Crosswalk is safe, library helps everyone, playground is safe and fair.

Children point to one place on the places strip where a rule helps: street corner, library door or park gate. They do not tell about home.

For error H, hold up two things: "Which crayon is mine to choose. The crosswalk is for everyone."

Each child draws a rule from the cards and who made it. Meet the s01-R1 group: show a toy and say "I wonder why it is here"; then "It is a toy." Which one asks? Add a new wonder to the wall.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

Every child circles on the exit card (s01-X4); read aloud: "Circle the person who made the rule." Ask the six and mark the list: "Why was this gate closed, and what does the rule do for children?" Right: it keeps children out of the car lane; key only the reason. "Because grown-ups said so" is error B; "children can open it if they want" is error H.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw a crosswalk, a book shelf and a slide. No exit card: draw a gate and a car.

**Skip.** The reopen and the sort.

- **5 min. Cards.** Draw a crosswalk. Say who made it: the road workers.
  **Say.** "Who made it? What is it for?" Expect: road workers; to be safe.
- **5 min. Places.** Draw the library and a slide. Ask who made each rule.
- **5 min. Draw.** Your child draws one rule and who made it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X4]] Draw a gate across a car lane. Your child circles the helper who closed it.
  **Say.** "Why is the gate closed?" Expect: so children stay out of the car lane.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: use two cards. Longer: find a second place with a rule.

### Words and supports

Maker: the person who made it. Frame: "Who made it? What is it for?" A pointed card or aid is an answer.

### Tomorrow

Day 5 reopens with s01-X4: for error B, one picture and one sentence. Then the class proposes rules for its own room, with a reason for each. Before day 5, draw three candidate-rule pictures from your room.
