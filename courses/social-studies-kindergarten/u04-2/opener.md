# Section 4.2 opener: old photographs, objects and what changed

## What the section does

Days 37 to 40 finish the unit "Long ago and today", whose question is *Which change from long ago made the biggest difference to everyday work?* Day 37 shows one old photograph with its record read aloud and teaches the words photograph, object and clue. Day 38 compares four pairs of our own drawings of one farm and today, and the class says what changed and what stayed the same. Day 39 is the check: each child orders fresh picture cards, places events with time words, and answers the compelling question with a change, a named photograph, a reason and one thing that stayed the same. Day 40 re-teaches what the check showed. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and no child reads.

## How the days run

Day 37 begins with a short hand-back, then you show a large print of one federal photograph and read aloud only its maker, place and date. Children say "I see ...", then "I think ..., because I see ...". The record is what makes a photograph old, not its gray color. You also show drawings and a cardboard stand-in made by us (a washboard stand-in, a washing machine, a slate and a tablet) and fill in a two-column chart, then and now, with a row for what stayed. Day 38 has four pairs of drawings of one farm and today, each captioned "one farm, an example"; partners say "In this picture ..., but ...; both ...". Each child also revises the day 36 time-word log. Day 39 is a quiet sitting: every response is collected before any answer is shown. Day 40 has four small groups named by task, then each child makes one fresh try.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A photograph is a picture taken at the time; a drawing is made later; an object is a thing. A source is old because its record gives a date. One photograph shows one place and moment, so it cannot speak for everyone: say "the record does not say" when it does not. A tool changed, but its purpose often stayed. Handwashing, planting and sewing continue today. The compelling question is answered by a bounded choice with a reason, never by calling a time better or worse. Nobody brings a family photograph, and the photographs show particular people: read only the record's own words and do not describe anyone by how they look.

## The errors, in plain words

- C: "Gray means long ago." Set a new gray drawing beside a dated photograph and read each date.
- D: "Every old way of working has disappeared." Set a changed tool beside its continuing purpose.
- E: "It changed most because it was best." Name the pictured detail first.
- F: "A picture tells us everything about everyone." Separate "I see it" from "I guess it about everyone".
- G: "Everybody long ago lived badly." Compare only the pictured work.

## Supplies

Large prints with the records read aloud, a ridged cardboard stand-in with no sharp edges, blank charts, paper, crayons, tokens, the class calendar and the label cards. A child who cannot see gets a neutral spoken description of each picture and tactile outlines. Demonstrate dry; no child works any appliance.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn then and now pairs, a drawn two-column chart, and the teacher's own sketch of an old photograph with a written date.

## Exit tickets

Every exit is a new case, labeled invented for practice or credited to its record, and never used in teaching. Every child marks a picture; the spoken part is heard from a rotating group of six each day.
