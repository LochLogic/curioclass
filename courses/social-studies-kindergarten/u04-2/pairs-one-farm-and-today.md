# Four then and now pairs: one farm and today (day 38)

Eight original drawings, plain line, thick outlines, in four pairs. Each pair is captioned on its sheet **"one farm, an example"** and printed "drawing by Curio Class". The then drawings rest on the National Park Service page about the Richmond Hamilton Farm (source 38A): what the family did there each day, in our own words. The now drawings are today's ways, for comparison. They show one farm and today and are examples, not every farm or every place.

## Pair 1: water

Then: a person carrying two buckets of water. Now: a tap over a sink with a cup under it. Shared purpose: both help us get water.

## Pair 2: food

Then: a person tending a garden row beside a corn patch and a barrel of preserved food. Now: a food shop shelf with a basket. Shared purpose: both help us get food. Growing food still continues today: a garden row stays in the now picture's corner.

## Pair 3: clothing

Then: a person at a loom beside a spinning wheel. Now: a clothing shop rack. Shared purpose: both help us get clothes. Making clothes still continues today: a hand sewing a button appears in the now picture.

## Pair 4: travel

Then: a horse pulling a wagon on a farm road. Now: a bus on a street. Shared purpose: both help people and things go places. The caption says only that a horse or a mule pulled a wagon on this one farm.

## How it is used

Two minutes a pair on a blank chart row; partners say "In this picture ..., but ...; both ...". Then each child says to a partner a rehearsal claim, "I think ___ made a difference because ..."; it guides instruction and is not scored. Wren says "it was best because it was best" (error E); the class fixes it aloud.

## Key (teacher)

Water: buckets carried then, tap now; both for water. Food: garden then, shop now; growing continues. Clothing: loom then, shop now; making continues. Travel: wagon then, bus now; both for going places. No then picture is called bad (error G).
