# Section 6.1 opener: work, and what workers know

## What the section does

Days 52 and 53 open the unit "Work and trade", whose question is *Why do people work?* Each day is 20 to 30 minutes. Day 52 teaches that work is something a person does that helps someone, inside a home or outside it, and that work counts whether or not anyone pays. Day 53 teaches what one worker must know, be able to do and use, and ends with a two-picture look at work then and now. No child reads. Everything is a picture card or a page read aloud, and a child answers by pointing, circling, sorting, drawing or speaking.

## How the days run

On day 52 the teacher sets out paper while a picture shows a grown-up cooking in a pretend home, and one row of Our Jobs Chart from unit 2 is read. You read three pictures from Work Around Us, then each table sorts seven work cards into two hoops, home and not home, and draws one work done at school. On day 53 you reopen the pretend cafe cook beside the home cook, read the unit 4 washing chart once, read the Jobs Book page about Davi who drives a bus, and set the head, hand and tool icons beside his picture. Pairs do the same for Mina, who grows beans, and look at two school helpers. Two drawings of one pretend farm end the day. Every day ends with a picture mark every child makes and a spoken ask heard from a rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

Work is effort that helps someone or makes something people need. Being paid is one way work is recognized; it is not what makes it work. A person who cooks at home, cares for a small child or sets the table for supper is working. A person may also be paid for work inside a home, such as painting a wall. Where the work happens and whether it is paid are two separate facts. A worker has knowledge (what they keep in mind), a skill (what they can do because they learned it) and tools (what they use). A tool is never the whole job.

## Care

Invented workers only, and the teacher says they are made up. No child is asked who works in their home, what their family does for work, or what anyone is paid. Every kind of work is named with respect; none is called small. A real helper's photograph or visit is optional, with their agreement, and never needed. A child may pass.

## The errors, in plain words

- A: "Unpaid work does not count." Puppet Pip says cooking at home is not work. Right: cooking helps people eat, so it is work, paid or not.
- I: "Home work is always unpaid." Pip says work in a home is never paid. Right: a painter paints inside walls and is paid; a grown-up sets their own table and is not.
- B: "The tool tells all the worker needs." Pip says the driver needs a bus and nothing else. Right: the driver also knows the way and can stop gently.

A puppet or a teacher card says the wrong idea. A child's answer is never the example.

## Supplies

Large picture cards, two hoops (or two chalk circles), paper rows, crayons, three icon cards (head, hand, tool), the farm strip, tokens, Our Jobs Chart and the unit 4 washing chart. Large print and high contrast on every card; raised-line cards for a child who cannot see, with each picture described.

## A room with nothing

Scrap-paper cards, a chalk circle for the home, a hand-drawn icon strip, and the pages read aloud with one sketch each.

## Exit tickets

Every exit is a new case printed "invented for practice" and never used in teaching. Every child marks a picture; the spoken part is heard from a rotating six each day, and the rest are recorded not yet heard. The marking scale is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
