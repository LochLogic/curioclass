# Day 50 hearing cards (reserved)

Cards withheld until day 50, kept in their own folder and never shown before it. The teacher reads every prompt aloud and supplies nothing. The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. An unheard part is recorded not yet observed.

## The museum-label scene (E1 and E2)

A new drawing: a glass case in a pretend museum holding two framed drawings of people in old-fashioned clothes, and a label card read aloud: "Presidents' Day." Printed "invented for practice." Another holiday taught in the unit may be used instead, with an unseen scene and a plain label.

## The tradition cards (E11 and E12), two cards, each with its maker named aloud

- A new drawing of people gathered round a spinning-top game, from the Reform Judaism Hanukkah page, which describes the dreidel game.
- A new drawing of people watching someone make very large writing with a brush, from the Japanese American National Museum's blog, which names a large-scale calligraphy performance at its 2023 festival.

Neither card stands for all Jewish or all Japanese American families. No child plays or acts them. Heard on day 49 first; these cards only for a child not yet heard.

## The claim (E13)

The wonder wall, Our Holiday Cards, the sort bins and the cards above are the sources. The teacher scribes the child's "I think ___, because ___" beside a picture the child chooses.

## Key (teacher)

E1: Washington and Lincoln, who were presidents (named, not only the word presidents); the law names the day Washington's Birthday. E2: it shows thanks and respect and we learn what they did. E11: both show people gathering for a special occasion. E12: a game in one, big writing to watch in the other. E13: a claim, a named picture or page detail, and a reason joining them.
