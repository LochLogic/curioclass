# Day 51 fresh tries

A changed picture or question for each missed part, with the same content. Printed "invented for practice." The teacher reads every prompt aloud. The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. An unheard part from day 50 is recorded not yet observed until heard.

## Holiday (A, H, G)

A pretend town hall notice board with two notices: a folded flag labeled Memorial Day and a poster of children's handprints around the word Thank you, labeled Veterans Day. Mark: put a token on the notice that remembers people who died while serving. Spoken: a puppet says we remember Juneteenth because the people were the best; with the Juneteenth card ("the day in 1865 when enslaved people in Texas learned they were free"), "What could we say, using the card?"

## Book evidence (B, I) and symbols (C, D, E, J, K, L, N)

A new page of the flag in Symbols We Ask About, with the puppet guessing; a flag with one stripe and one star marked, asked as all the stripes or all the stars; a puppet says the old flag law said white means being pure; a new picture of a pretend stamp showing the seal's bird, and a puppet says eagles have sharp eyes so one became our bird; a puppet says the song everybody sings at a pretend fair is our anthem; a pretend class poster of children learning the Pledge words and one child who chooses to stand quietly, and a puppet says only children who say the words may be line leader; the phrase "one Nation, indivisible" read aloud and asked about in the child's own words, and a puppet says it can say it fast so it knows what it means.

## Tradition (F, M, O)

In pretend Lake Town, neighbors gather every year to fly kites. In pretend Farm Town, neighbors gather every year to make jam. "What is the same in both towns?"

## Key (teacher)

The folded flag, Memorial Day; the Juneteenth card's detail, not praise. The stars: one for each state, fifty today; the stripes: thirteen, like the first states. The old law gave no reason for the colors; people say what white means. Leaders picked the eagle for the Great Seal. A law chose the anthem. Fair treatment is for everyone, and saying the Pledge is a choice. One country together, not split apart. Neighbors gather every year in both towns; kites and jam differ.
