# Section 5.3 opener: traditions, the observation and the respond day

## What the section does

Days 48 to 51 finish the unit "Days we remember". Day 48 teaches how two real celebrations are alike and different, through food and gathering, with the supporting question *How are traditions alike and different?* Day 49 applies it to two more picture cards on a same-and-different chart (music, and new food pictures), and the point is that people do things differently for reasons that matter to them, where a page states a reason. Day 50 is the observation: each child is heard on holidays, symbols and traditions and closes the wonder wall with an "I think ___, because ___". Day 51 re-teaches in small groups what the hearing showed and tries once more. Days 48 and 49 are 30 minutes; day 50 is 20 to 30 minutes. No child reads.

## How the days run

Day 48: you read two pages of "Two Celebrations" (Hanukkah latkes from the Reform Judaism page; a rice-cake pounding from a Japanese American museum's blog), say the maker of each page aloud, and model "Both ___, but ___" and a because. Pairs put tokens on a blank same-and-different chart. Day 49: a koto player and people singing at Hanukkah go on the chart. Pairs fill a chart for two invented food pictures. You hear revised explanations of symbols, holidays and traditions. Day 50: ten children at a time are heard, about 30 seconds each, on new cards and pages. Day 51: four small groups, then fresh tries on changed pictures.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A tradition is something people do again, often with others, for a special day. Alike and different are the two halves of a comparison; one difference alone is not a comparison (error O). Real traditions are shown only from their own community's pages, with the maker named, and never stand for all families (error F). No tradition is strange, wrong or better (error M). Say a reason only where the page states one; otherwise say "the page does not say". People greet in many ways and no one greeting is universal. Nobody tastes, lights, plays, dances, sings along or prays; religious traditions are described, never practiced.

## Care

No child is asked to share a tradition or say what their family does; families who do not celebrate a holiday are respected. River Town, Hill Town, Pond Town and Ridge Town are invented for practice and stand for no real group. Before day 48 look for a publicly documented celebration of your school's own area, from its community's own published page, and swap it for one teaching example or add it; no family disclosure, guest or outside contact. If you find none, the two pages stand.

## The errors, in plain words

- F: "All families celebrate alike." Replace "all" with the page's actual reach: some families.
- M: "People who do it differently are wrong." Put each tradition beside its reason from the page, without ranking.
- O: "One difference is a complete comparison." Add a supported shared feature beside the difference.

A puppet says the wrong idea. A child's answer is never the example.

## Supplies

"Two Celebrations" pages, a blank two-column chart, tokens, the invented town cards, the record sheet, the withheld cards and photograph for day 50 (kept in their own folder), wonder wall cards and crayons. Raised-line pictures and spoken descriptions for a child who cannot see.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn cards on scrap paper, a chart drawn on the board, and the pages read aloud with one sketch each.

## Exit tickets

Days 48 and 49 each end with new cards printed "invented for practice" or credited to their makers, never used in teaching. Day 50 has no exit; its items are the evidence. Day 51's fresh tries use changed pictures. The marking scale is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
