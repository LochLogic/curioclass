# The Lion and the Mouse

Day 16 folktale, twelve pages. A very old story told by many people, credited to Aesop. Source: "The Lion and the Mouse" in Aesop for Children (1919), https://read.gov/aesop/007.html (rights note https://read.gov/aesop/about.html; the original book is in the public domain in the United States); This retelling is in our own words and the drawings are new. The old moral is kept: "A kindness is never wasted." The lion's paw moment is told calmly, and children do not act it out. The lion is strong, but strong is not the same as in charge: do not teach him as a rightful leader.

Art: plain line drawings, thick outlines, no writing. The mouse is a small gray mouse with a round ear and a little scarf; the lion is large with a soft mane. Faces are calm, never snarling.

**Page 1.** Text: "This is a very old story. Many people have told it. Now we tell it."
Picture: a grassy place with tall trees, a tiny mouse at the base of a tree and a lion asleep in the shade beyond.

**Page 2.** Text: "A little mouse ran across the grass. She was playing and did not see where she was going."
Picture: the mouse running, ears back, tail up. The lion's paw lies across her path.

**Page 3.** Text: "She ran right over the lion's nose! The lion woke up with a big yawn."
Picture: the mouse on the lion's snout, startled. The lion's eyes opening.

**Page 4.** Text: "The lion put his big paw down. He held the mouse."
Picture: the lion's large paw gently pinning the mouse to the ground. The mouse's eyes are wide. (Read slowly and calmly, then pause.)

**Page 5.** Text: "'Please let me go,' said the mouse. 'I am sorry. Someday I will help you. I promise.'"
Picture: the mouse looking up, paws together.

**Page 6.** Text: "The lion laughed. 'How could a little mouse help a big lion?' But he lifted his paw, and she ran free."
Picture: the lion's paw lifted, the mouse scampering off. The lion chuckling.

**Page 7.** Text: "Days passed. The lion walked in the forest and caught his feet in a net of ropes. He pulled, but he could not get free."
Picture: the lion lying on his side tangled in a rope net hung from a tree. No hunters are shown.

**Page 8.** Text: "The lion roared. The mouse heard it far away."
Picture: the mouse, ears up, small at the foot of a tree, with big curved sound lines in the sky.

**Page 9.** Text: "The mouse remembered her promise. She ran to the lion."
Picture: the mouse running fast through the grass toward the net.

**Page 10.** Text: "'Do not worry,' said the mouse. She began to gnaw the ropes with her small teeth. Nibble, nibble, nibble."
Picture: the mouse chewing a thick rope; thin strands falling.

**Page 11.** Text: "One rope came apart, then another. The net fell open. The lion was free."
Picture: the lion standing, the net on the ground, the mouse sitting on a rock beside him.

**Page 12.** Text: "'Thank you,' said the lion. The little mouse had said she would help, and she did. A kindness is never wasted."
Picture: lion and mouse side by side in a calm moment. Sun low behind them.

**Teacher questions.** "What did the mouse say she would do? Did she do it?" Frame: "She said ___. She did ___." Name her courage. Then: "Is the lion in charge? What shows that?"
