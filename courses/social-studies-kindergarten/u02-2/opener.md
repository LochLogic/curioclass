# Section 2.2 opener: choosing together

## What the section does

Days 13 and 14 answer "How can we choose together?" On day 13 the class names real classroom problems and draws two proposals. On day 14 it hears both, tries for a shared revision, votes with blocks, counts, and begins the first action. This is the year's informed action. The agreed arrangement runs in the class's routines until day 17. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and nothing is read by children.

## How the days run

Day 13: reopen the question, a small warm-up vote naming the class plant (never the action), the picture story The Paper Box told with two puppets, then picture cards of real spots. Children count hands to choose which problem to fix first. You draw two safe, accessible proposal cards from their ideas and, after class, start Our Decision Record with a "before" sketch from a marked spot on the floor. Day 14: hear each proposal and its reason, ask what change both could accept before any vote, then each child directs one identical block into a choice tower; compare, count, record. For a tie, revise and vote again; if still tied, try both in turn. Act that day, and record the result and any dissent as a reason, not a person.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A problem is a place or a thing, never a child. A fair choice is not a unanimous one: people hear reasons, look for something both can accept, and vote only if still apart. A vote does not make everyone agree (error B), and the loudest voice does not decide (error G). Choosing is not doing (error J): the plan comes after the count, and the first step happens the same day. Dissent is a reason set beside the plan, never a fault. Safety and access are never voted away.

## The errors, in plain words

- B: "We voted, so everyone wants it." Keep both wishes visible after the count.
- G: "The loudest picks." Let puppets voice each reason first.
- J: "We picked it, so it is done." Match each chosen step to an action.

## Supplies

Two paper-bag puppets, identical blocks (or paper squares), problem and proposal cards, Our Decision Record sheet, crayons, a floor mark for the before sketch. No student timer.

## A room with nothing

Hands or paper squares in two stacks, hand-drawn cards, and a record on any sheet. The puppet stories can be told from the page text with hand-drawn cards.

## Exit tickets

Both are new cases printed "invented for practice" and never used again. E13 uses a cushion story and three picture cards; E14 is a mark and a drawing of what the child did. The spoken "why" is heard from a rotating group of six.
