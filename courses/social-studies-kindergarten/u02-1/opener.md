# Section 2.1 opener: jobs and who counts on them

## What the section does

Days 11 and 12 open the unit "Doing our part", whose question is *How can each of us help our class?* Day 11 teaches what a job is and who counts on it. Day 12 is the first practice at finishing a real job helpfully and respectfully. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and nothing is read by children.

## How the days run

Day 11 begins with a picture story, Job Left Unfinished (a puppet waters a plant and leaves the jug in the walkway), and the question "What still needs doing?" You post the unit question, retrieve one rule from unit 1's Our Class Charter in pairs, teach three jobs on picture cards (line leader, plant waterer, door holder), and close with an exit picture. Day 12 models the door holder, gives every child a real job from a set of cards, lets them do it, and draws Our Jobs Chart together: what helpful looks like and what respectful sounds like. That evening you write one next step on each child's picture page. Children repeat their jobs in routines on days 13 to 17; about six are watched and dated each day, and only those later attempts count.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A job is doing your part for people who count on it. At five, a job is finished when the people who count on it can use what is left, so starting is not finishing. Respect means asking before taking what another child holds, and leaving things ready for the next person. Home jobs appear only as invented cases: homes differ, so no child reports a chore, and nothing about a family is inferred from chores.

## The errors, in plain words

- A: "I watered it, so I am done." The jug in the walkway is the lesson. Put an unfinished picture beside a finished one.
- H: "I was helping, so I took it." Practice asking first with puppets, then with partners.

## Supplies

Real plant and jug, two paper-bag puppets, three job cards and a set of job cards with several copies, picture pages, a large sheet for Our Jobs Chart, crayons, an envelope of sorting cards per pair, Our Class Charter. Every job is accessible: no lifting, climbing or blocking an exit. Jobs rotate with no job kept for a boy or a girl. Adults keep safety jobs (scissors, the door in a drill). No stickers, rewards or best-helper lists.

## A room with nothing

Real objects, two paper-bag puppets, job cards drawn on scrap paper, and a chart drawn on any large sheet. The picture story can be told from the page text with a hand-drawn card for each page.

## Exit tickets

Both are new cases printed "invented for practice" and never used again. The spoken "why" is heard from a rotating group of six each day; the rest are marked not yet heard and heard on another day.
