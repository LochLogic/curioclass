# Section 1.1 opener: wondering about our class

## What the section does

Two days, 1 and 2. On day 1 the class looks at Two Drawing Tables, hears the big question (What makes our class a good place to learn?), and starts a wonder wall where you write each volunteered wonder beside a picture. You also run the start-of-year check in ten minutes: three access observations, not a test. On day 2 the class hears Everyone Has a Place and sees that people like different things and still share one room. Nothing from these two days is scored; the scored explanation comes on day 9.

## How the days run

Each day is 20 minutes (30 on the longer route): a two-minute reopen, six minutes with the picture sequence, ten minutes of supported work, a two-minute exit. Day 1 swaps the supported work for the check. A child may pass and show an answer privately.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

- **A wonder is a question.** "I wonder why the bear is by the easel" is a question; "That is a bear" is a statement. We model the first.
- **"Matters to me" can be small.** "I want to know where things go so I can help" is a good personal reason. We want any real use, not a right answer.
- **A shared room works when each person can reach what they need.** Day 1 shows this with crayons. Day 2 adds that people differ and still need the same things: space, materials and a turn.
- **Pictured facts limit a reason.** Preferences differ, but "they need nothing" does not fit a picture of two children working.

## The errors, in plain words

- **A:** "It matters because the teacher says so." The child has not yet found a personal use. Offer two classroom uses of an answer (a place for coats, a job to do) and let each child point to the one they would use.
- **K:** "The two classmates share nothing." Lay two activity cards side by side and ask what the table, the room and the light do for each.

If a child names home or family in an answer, thank them and bring it back to our room. Wrong answers are voiced by a puppet or "someone might say", never by a named child.

## Supplies for the section

Paper, crayons, tape, chart paper for the wonder wall, a toy bear and an easel (or a drawn bear), two folded-paper puppets, torn-paper puzzle pieces, blocks, and the printed pictures: Two Drawing Tables (10 pages), Everyone Has a Place (10 pages), four room cards and the two exit cards. The record sheet has one row for each child and a column for how the child prefers to answer.

## A room with nothing

Draw the pictures on scrap paper and tell the words. Fold two paper puppets for the hook. Use the back of a poster for the wall, a lid for the easel and a drawn bear for the bear. One shared screen may show the pictures, but every child marks their own paper card.
