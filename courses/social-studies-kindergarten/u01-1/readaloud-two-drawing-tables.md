# Two Drawing Tables

A picture read-aloud for day 1. Ten pages. Before you show it, say who made it and what it is: "We made this picture for our class. The puppets are pretend." Show one page at a time and read the words under it. Children do not read; the only printed word is the title on page 1. Pause after page 5 and after page 10 to ask what each table lets a puppet do. Nobody has to answer out loud.

Drawing style for all pages: plain black line drawing on white, thick lines, no shading, no brand names, no printed words. Both puppets are folded paper: Tam has a round head and a small curved smile; Ro has a taller head with a pointed top. Both have two dot eyes.

## Page 1

**Picture:** A wide view of a bright classroom. Two small tables stand on opposite sides, one on the left, one on the right, with a window between them. Each table has one sheet of paper on it. Across the top, the title in large letters: Two Drawing Tables.

**Read:** In a room, there were two drawing tables.

## Page 2

**Picture:** The left table. Tam stands at it with both arms wrapped around a big tub of crayons, so big it almost hides Tam. Crayons of many colors poke out of the top.

**Read:** At the first table stood Tam. Tam loved crayons. Tam held the whole tub, every crayon.

## Page 3

**Picture:** Ro stands beside Tam at the left table with a clean sheet of paper. Ro's hands are open and held out. The tub is just out of Ro's reach, behind Tam's arms.

**Read:** Ro had a clean white paper. Ro had no crayons. Ro waited.

## Page 4

**Picture:** Close view of Tam's paper: a big round sun with long rays, drawn in many colors. Tam is smiling and drawing. The tub is still in Tam's arms.

**Read:** Tam drew a sun with many colors. Tam was not being mean. The tub was just in Tam's arms.

## Page 5

**Picture:** Close view of Ro's paper, still blank. Ro's head is tilted. A small question mark floats above Ro.

**Read:** Ro's paper stayed white. Ro wondered, "How can I draw?"

## Page 6

**Picture:** The right table in the same room. Tam and Ro sit side by side at it. A cup of crayons stands in the middle of the table, between them. Both have blank paper.

**Read:** At the other table, the crayons stood in a cup in the middle. The same room. The same two puppets.

## Page 7

**Picture:** Both puppets reach toward the cup at the same time, hands close but not touching. Each reaches with one hand.

**Read:** Tam could reach. Ro could reach.

## Page 8

**Picture:** Tam draws a sun. Ro draws a boat on wavy lines. Each holds a crayon. Other crayons stand in the cup.

**Read:** Tam drew a sun. Ro drew a boat. Both of them had crayons.

## Page 9

**Picture:** Both pictures, the sun and the boat, hang side by side on the wall. Tam and Ro stand below, looking up. The cup of crayons waits on the table behind them.

**Read:** Two pictures. Two puppets. One cup that both could reach.

## Page 10

**Picture:** Two small panels side by side. Left: the first table, with Tam holding the tub and Ro waiting. Right: the second table, with the cup in the middle and both drawing. A large question mark floats above, between the panels.

**Read:** Two tables. The same crayons. The same puppets. What does each table let a puppet do?

## For the teacher

After page 10, ask: "What does the first table allow? What does the second table allow?" Accept pointing at a panel. Then post the big question on the wonder wall (What makes our class a good place to learn?) and model "I wonder ..." with the toy bear and the easel.

Keep two frames for later: on day 3 and day 8 hold up page 7 or page 8 as a picture of a shared thing that both can reach.
