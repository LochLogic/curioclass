# Day 1: Our big question and the wonder wall

## Teacher page

### Before class

Tape chart paper up as the wonder wall. Put the toy bear by the easel and the toy bird on the sink room card. Choose today's six children.

### The day

#### 8 min. Hook and wonder wall

Whole class on the rug. Say: "We made this picture for our class. The puppets are pretend." Show Two Drawing Tables a page at a time, reading each page.

**Ask.** What does each table let Ro do? Expect: at the first, Ro waits and cannot draw; at the second, Ro reaches a crayon and draws. Pointing to a panel counts.

Post and read the big question: "What makes our class a good place to learn?" Model with the bear: "I wonder why the bear is by the easel." A wonder is a question; "That is a bear" is a statement, so we model the first. Then a reason: "Finding this out matters to me because I like it when everyone has a turn."

Show the bird on the sink room card and ask [[s01-R1]]: "What do you wonder?" Write each wonder on the wall beside a quick picture. No child must speak. Say a statement back once as a wonder.

#### 10 min. Start-of-year check

Not a test: pointing, signing or a communication aid is an answer. The six sit with you; the rest draw on the wonder card. Read aloud: "Draw something you wonder about our class."

With the six, [[s01-R2]]: two puppets reach for one puzzle piece; ask what could help both play, and why. Then pairs do [[s01-R3]]: "Choose a block. I will tell you my choice; then show or tell yours," trading twice. Note each answer and the support used. Others are noted on days 2 and 3.

#### 2 min. Exit ticket

Every child circles one spot on the room card ([[s01-X1]]); read aloud: "Circle the picture you most wonder about." Ask the six: "What do you wonder about our room? Why does our big question matter to you?" Any real use, small or large, is a reason. "Because you told us to" is error A: thank them and go on. Bring home answers back to our room.

### Other rooms

Nothing more: the materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No puppets: use two spoons. No crayon tub: hold a handful of crayons.

**Skip.** The wall and the class check.

- **5 min. The two tables.** One puppet holds every crayon and the other waits. Then the crayons sit in a cup between them.
  **Say.** "What does each way let the puppet do?" Expect: waits and cannot draw; with the cup, both can draw.
- **5 min. A wonder.** Say "I wonder why the cup is in the middle." Write what your child wonders.
  **Say.** "What do you wonder?" Expect: any question; say a statement back as "I wonder ...".
- **5 min. Draw it.** Your child draws the wonder on the wonder card.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s01-X1]] Draw a sink, shelf, window and rug. Your child circles one.
  **Say.** "What do you wonder? Why does finding out matter to you?" Expect: a question and any reason of their own.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: read pages 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 10. Longer: partners tell each other a wonder.

### Words and supports

Wonder: a question you want to find out. Frames: "I wonder ..." and "Finding this out matters to me because ...". A home language, signs, pointing or a dictated drawing all count.

### Tomorrow

Day 2 reads the wonder wall. If many gave error A, "because you told us to", it offers two classroom uses of an answer. Then Everyone Has a Place, room cards in pairs, and the partner-turn bridge for [[s01-R3]].
