# Section 2.4 opener: did it help, and what did we learn

## What the section does

Days 17 to 19 close the unit. Day 17 looks back at the class decision about a week on: did it help, and what would we change? Day 18 is the observation day: each child shows what they know about jobs, characters and the class decision, and answers "How can each of us help our class?" Day 19 re-teaches what the record showed and gives a brief fresh try. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and nothing is read by children.

## How the days run

Day 17: read Our Decision Record, children draw the "after" picture from the same marked floor spot, compare it beside the "before" sketch, confirm or revise the decision with one block each, then hear about fifteen children in forty-second conversations. The exit uses a reserved sketch you draw from another spot, never shown in the day's talk. Day 18: read The Puppet Basket, then each child marks separate rows of scene thumbnails for seven identification prompts, revises one mark, and about fifteen children are heard in conversation. Unheard parts are marked not yet observed. Day 19: small groups by error letter (at most two with you, the rest in pairs from a picture card), then a fresh try with The Rhythm Sticks.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A result is judged by evidence, not by having chosen it (error C). "It helps because it helps" is a claim with no reason (error F). A finding that the change did not help also meets the standard. Score only later dated attempts after a child has heard written feedback; first attempts are practice. Score civic understanding apart from speech, English or movement; a pointing, token, drawing or private exchange counts.

## The errors, in plain words

- C: "We chose it, so it worked." Compare a before and after detail.
- F: "It helps because it helps." Point to a detail in a named source.
- D and E: from section 2.3, revisited on day 19.

## Supplies

Our Decision Record, before and after sketches, the reserved sketch, blocks, seven response rows with thumbnails, tokens, the record sheet with group slips. No student timer.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn rows of scene sketches, paper tokens, and a record on any sheet. The stories can be told from the captions with a quick drawing for each.

## Fresh stories

The Puppet Basket and The Rhythm Sticks are new and withheld from teaching. Read the Basket's captions and describe the pictures neutrally; children find the evidence. They are invented examples, not records of a real class. Never rush a communicator.
