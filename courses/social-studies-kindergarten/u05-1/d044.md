# Day 44: Presidents, and holidays in order

## Teacher page

### Before class

Taught in Presidents' Day week. Lay the strip on the rug with the three saved cards. Pick today's six.

### The day

#### 3 min. Reopen

Post the unit's question on the wonder wall: "Why do we remember some days and some people?" Review: the wonder wall, from unit 1; first, next and last on a strip, from unit 4. Children put the three saved cards on the strip: first, next, last. Unmarked.

#### 4 min. Return and repair

If many missed day 43's exit (error A, error G), set a "day off" picture beside the King card, and a praise sentence beside a page detail; the class picks the one the page supports. Otherwise a small group does it.

#### 9 min. Teaching first

Four minutes: read Two Presidents. These pictures are ours, made in 2026. George Washington was the first president of the United States; the law names this day Washington's Birthday. Abraham Lincoln was a president long ago, in a hard time. He helped end slavery. On January 1, 1863, he issued the Emancipation Proclamation, which said enslaved people in the states fighting against the country were free. Many people call the day Presidents' Day. Sources: a reference article (read 2026-11-20) and the National Archives.

Five minutes: three cards, one sentence each, dates said, none printed. Independence Day remembers 1776, year of the Declaration of Independence. Memorial Day remembers people who died while serving in the military. Juneteenth remembers the day in 1865 when enslaved people in Texas learned they were free. Moss says "Memorial Day is for all the veterans." Answer: Veterans Day thanks everyone who served; Memorial Day remembers those who died while serving (error H).

#### 10 min. Supported work

Say: "This is a pretend class." Pairs set the seven cards on the strip mat with today marked: before or on today left, still to come right. Each pair says whom two cards remember. Hear the six, 60 seconds each. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

#### 3 min. Exit

Every child puts the two exit cards in order on the exit mat, the one first in the school year on the left ([[s05-X44a]]). Ask the six, 20 seconds each: "Pick one of your two cards. Who or what does that day remember?" ([[s05-X44b]]) The question stays posted.

### Other rooms

The materials line names each stand-in.

### At home

**Swap.** No cards: draw seven small squares. No strip: fold a long paper.

**Skip.** The strip.

- **5 min. Two presidents.** Draw a desk and a paper. Say: Washington was the first president; Lincoln was president in a hard time.
  **Say.** "Who does Presidents' Day remember?" Expect: Washington and Lincoln.
- **5 min. In order.** Draw two cards, one for November and one for January. Say each month.
  **Say.** "Which comes first in the school year?" Expect: November.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s05-X44a]] [[s05-X44b]] Ask the one that fits; wait.

### Shorter or longer

Shorter: four cards. Longer: a third card.

### Words and supports

President, holiday, honor. A point counts.

### Tomorrow

If many said Memorial Day is for all veterans (error H), day 45 opens with a thank-you wall beside a quiet flag. Day 45 begins the flag and the first Pledge words.
