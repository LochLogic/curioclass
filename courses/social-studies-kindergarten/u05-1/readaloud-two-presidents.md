# Two Presidents

A two-scene picture read-aloud for day 44, made by us from checked pages. One actor in each scene. Large pictures, one or two sentences read aloud under each. Say the source: the law names the third Monday in February Washington's Birthday, and many people call the day Presidents' Day. The pictures are drawings of people in old-fashioned clothes at a desk; they are our drawings, not portraits, and the exit and end cards use other pictures.

**Scene 1. A president at a desk (actor: Washington).** A man in old-fashioned clothes at a desk with a quill and a paper. Read: "George Washington was the first president of the United States. The law names this day for his birthday: Washington's Birthday." Say the source: a reference article read 2026-11-20.

**Scene 2. A president with a paper (actor: Lincoln).** A man in a tall hat holding a paper at a table. Read: "Abraham Lincoln was a president long ago, in a hard time. He helped end slavery. On January 1, 1863, he issued a paper called the Emancipation Proclamation. It said enslaved people in the states that were fighting against the country were free." Say the source: the National Archives page on the Emancipation Proclamation.

Say: many people call the day Presidents' Day. It is a day for remembering these presidents. Say that Lincoln helped end slavery, never that he ended it alone (a reference article read 2026-11-20 says he played a major role).

## Then three more cards, one sentence each (read aloud, dates said aloud, no date printed on a child's page)

- **Independence Day** (the card shows a drawing of a paper dated for July 4): "It remembers 1776, the year of a famous paper called the Declaration of Independence." Source: the National Archives page shows it dated July 4, 1776.
- **Memorial Day** (flowers beside a row of small flags): "Memorial Day remembers people who died while serving in the military." Source: a history article read 2026-11-19. Say: Veterans Day thanks everyone who served; Memorial Day remembers those who died while serving.
- **Juneteenth** (a calendar page with June 19 ringed): "Juneteenth remembers the day in 1865 when enslaved people in Texas learned they were free." This is the unit plan's line, said exactly. Sensitivity read with day 43.
