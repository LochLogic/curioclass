# Day 41 cards: the calendar card, the nation card and three practice cards

Large plain line drawings, thick outlines, no writing except the holiday name and the line "invented for practice" along the bottom of the practice cards. The teacher says "this is a pretend class" for the practice cards and reads every label aloud. The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Never shown in an exit (rule 4): the exit cards of day 41 are on their own sheet.

## Calendar card (teaching)

A page of a wall calendar with the second Monday of October ringed. Write the day's name exactly as your school's calendar names it. Say the name aloud and add: some places use another name for this day. The law names the day Columbus Day, and many states and towns observe Indigenous Peoples' Day instead or as well. Say the day is the same Monday.

## Nation card (teaching, credited)

Our own drawing, never a copy of anyone's page: a plain outline of an island on blue water with a ringed spot on its western end, the words *Martha's Vineyard, Massachusetts*, and the name *Aquinnah Wampanoag* in large print. Credit line, said aloud: "We drew this card after reading the Aquinnah Wampanoag Tribe's own page, Wampanoag History." Read, in the present tense:

- The Aquinnah Wampanoag still live on their land at Aquinnah, on Martha's Vineyard.
- The Aquinnah Wampanoag govern themselves and keep their traditions today.

Say who made the page (the tribe wrote it; we drew the card from it), what it is for (to tell their history in their own words) and who it is for (everyone). No headdress, costume or acting. Say the nation's name as its people say it; if you do not know, say that you are learning it. Where a nation lives nearer to your school with its own pages, show that nation instead and credit its page; the Aquinnah card is the fallback.

Model one claim aloud: "I think this day helps us remember Native peoples, because this card shows the Aquinnah Wampanoag, who live on Martha's Vineyard today." The card goes on the wall as the first of Our Holiday Cards.

## Practice cards for pairs (not the exit's)

Each pair gets three cards. Hear "I think ___ because ___" from the pair; a partner may speak for the pair.

**For Indigenous Peoples' Day:** (1) a wall map of Massachusetts with the island ringed; (2) a speech bubble over a pencil and a notebook, "a nation writes its own story"; (3) a loaf of bread.
**For Columbus Day:** (1) a globe with a curved line across the water; (2) a compass; (3) a plate of pasta (some Italian American communities keep the day as a tribute to their heritage, said aloud as a fact about some communities).

Sort rule for pairs: put the card that helps explain the day next to the day's card; leave the others in the bin.
