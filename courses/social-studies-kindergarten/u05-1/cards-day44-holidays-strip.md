# Day 44 cards: seven holiday cards and the school-year strip

A long paper strip read left to right and seven small cards, one for each holiday of the unit, each with a plain line picture and the holiday's name. Read every label aloud. The teacher says which cards are made up. The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Never shown in an exit (rule 4): the exit mat of day 44 is separate, and these cards are not its Presidents' Day or Memorial Day cards.

## The strip

A long paper strip with a picture of a school bus at the left (the start of the school year) and a picture of a summer sun at the right. Today's marker is moved along it. Earlier on the left, later on the right, as unit 4 taught; say that some home languages run the other way and that our class reads left to right.

## The seven cards, in order from the start of a school year (the teacher says the months aloud)

1. Indigenous Peoples' Day or Columbus Day (the name on your calendar), second Monday in October.
2. Veterans Day, November 11.
3. Martin Luther King Jr. Day, third Monday in January.
4. Presidents' Day, third Monday in February (the law's name is Washington's Birthday).
5. Memorial Day, last Monday in May.
6. Juneteenth, June 19.
7. Independence Day, July 4.

Source for every day and month: 5 U.S.C. 6103, read 2026-11-19. No date is printed on a child's page; say the months aloud.

## Supported work

Pairs set the seven cards on a strip mat with today marked: cards before or on today on the left, cards still to come on the right. Each pair says whom two cards remember ("I think this day remembers ___"). Hear the rotating six.

## Key (teacher)

The order above, adjusted to the day you teach. A pair may place the first three cards on the left of a day in the winter and the rest on the right. The point is whom each card remembers, not the order alone.
