# Day 42 cards: the record card and two question cards

Large plain line drawings, thick outlines. Read every label aloud. The teacher says which cards are made up. The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## Record card (teaching)

A paper held by a pair of hands, with a basket and the words *autumn, 1621*. The teacher reads: "Long, long ago, in 1621, English settlers at Plymouth held a harvest celebration. Wampanoag people were there too, and they brought food to share. The Wampanoag nations live in Massachusetts today." Say what a record is: words that were kept about what happened. Say that the records do not agree on every part and that Wampanoag people tell their own history on their own pages. Name the source aloud: a reference article that gathers what is known, read by us on 2026-11-19. No headdress, costume or acting out of the meal. Thanksgiving is a day coming soon; this is the record of one day long ago.

Do not use any Winslow line or any statement of a named historian: none has been checked on a free page, and the plan's two-voices part is reduced to the record sentence above and the sentence that Wampanoag people tell their own history.

## Two question cards (teaching)

Card A shows a flag and a hand waving above the words *Who do we thank on Veterans Day?* Card B shows a paper held by a pair of hands above *Who was at the 1621 meal?* Pairs match each question to a picture card and say "I think ___ because ___":

- For card A: a thank-you card, a flag, a balloon.
- For card B: two hands holding one basket of food, a ship drawn in outline, a pot on a fire.

Key (teacher): card A with the thank-you card; card B with two groups at one table, the basket the Wampanoag brought; the Wampanoag nations live in Massachusetts today.
