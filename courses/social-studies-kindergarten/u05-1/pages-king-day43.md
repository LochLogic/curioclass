# King pages and practice cards (day 43)

Two large pictures made by us, and three practice cards printed "invented for practice". Say first: "This is a pretend town. The real laws were like this." The pictures are looked at, never acted out. The unfair laws are named plainly: some laws kept Black and white people apart. No child is asked about family or about being treated unfairly. The marking scale on the class sheet is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The exit of day 43 has its own new pictures.

## Page 1: a pretend town (actor: the sign)

A park gate with two doors under one big painted sign. One door is for some people and the other for others. Read: "In a pretend town, a rule on a sign keeps some people apart from others. Long, long ago, in real places, some laws kept Black and white people apart. That was unfair." Say who made the picture (us), when (2026) and why (to show what an unfair law looks like).

## Page 2: working together (actor: the walkers)

A line of neighbors of many kinds walking side by side down a street, each holding a picture sign: a heart, an open hand, two linked hands. Read: "Martin Luther King Jr. worked with many others to change those laws. They worked peacefully. They walked together and spoke together." Say the source: the King Center's page about his life says he led a nonviolent movement for civil rights and that laws changed. Say that the day is on the third Monday in January and that we remember his work.

Model one claim: "I think we remember King's work, because this page shows people working together to change unfair laws."

## Practice cards for pairs (not the exit's)

Each pair sorts three cards and says "I think King's work was ___ because ___":

1. A crosswalk sign everyone follows (a rule everyone shares).
2. A sign over a slide that says only some children may use it (an unfair rule).
3. People signing one long paper at a table (people working to change a rule).
