# Section 6.2 opener: needs, wants, choosing and goods and services

## What the section does

Days 54 to 56 teach three linked ideas. Day 54: a need is something a person must have to stay well (food, water, shelter, clothing); a want is something a person would like. Day 55: there is not always enough for everyone, so people choose, and a trade happens only when both people say yes. Day 56: goods are things you can hold, services are things people do for others, and people pay for them with money they earn by working. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes. No child reads. A child answers by pointing, sorting, drawing, trading paper stickers or speaking.

## How the days run

Day 54 reopens the tool-only label and the hook of an apple and a kite on the rug. You show eight cards and two hoops, and Puppet Pip says the wrong ideas. Day 55 reopens one snow card and one sun card, then you pick one place on the table for a kite or a toy boat, and children each choose a paper sticker for one space and trade by asking and answering. Day 56 reopens "I like it" beside "only enough for one", reads the Jobs Book pages about Mina's stall and Davi's ride, then tables sort eight goods and services cards and follow a three-picture money chain. Each day ends with a picture mark and a spoken ask from a rotating six.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A need depends on the situation: a coat is a need in snow and shade is a need on a hot day, and a ramp is a need for a person who rolls. Nobody is asked what they have. A want is never wrong. Choosing is not a failure; it is what happens when there is only enough for one. A trade is an agreed swap and either person may say no. A good is an object people use. A service is an action done for someone. Money from work is one way people get what they pay with; some money comes in other ways, so you never say all money is earned.

## Care

No child is asked what they have, what they cannot have, or what their family buys. Stickers are the same for everyone and go back to the box. A no in the trade round is a fine answer and is never ranked. Invented people only.

## The errors, in plain words

- C: "Anything I want is a need." Pip says I want a kite, so I need a kite. Right: I want a kite; I need water.
- J: "Every person needs the same objects." Pip says everybody needs a coat. Right: a coat is a need in snow, shade is a need on a hot day, a ramp is a need for a pretend girl who rolls.
- D: "I chose it because it is best." Pip says the kite is the best, so he chose it. Right: there is room for one, so I choose.
- K: "Trading means taking." Pip grabs a star and says I traded. Right: a trade needs both children to say yes.
- F: "Any object someone touches is a service." Right: the shirt is a good; the mending is the service.
- G: "Money just appears." Right: Mina sells beans and earns money; she pays Davi for a bus ride.

A puppet says the wrong idea. A child's answer is never the example.

## Supplies

Eight needs and wants cards, two hoops, a card with one paper space, paper stickers (two kinds, the same for everyone) in a box, eight goods and services cards, three chain pictures, crayons and the unit record sheet. A room with nothing: draw the cards on scrap paper and tear a sticker from colored scraps.

## Exit tickets

Every exit is a new case printed "invented for practice" and never used in teaching. Every child marks a picture; the spoken part is heard from a rotating six. The marking scale is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
