# Two World Maps (day 23, and the taught pair of day 29)

Two large original maps of the same land and water, drawn for this unit. The land shapes are redrawn as large simple outlines from the public-domain Natural Earth land data (terms of use read 2026-11-19; geometry not inspected). The maps are ours, CC BY 4.0, and carry the foot line "Made with Natural Earth." Printed large, one pair for each pair of children, plus one for the room.

**Map 1, the Atlantic Ocean in the middle.** Land patterned (dots), water plain. A thick outline marks one large land shape that stands alone in the water (the maker's choice, checked against the land data; the plan's example is Australia). A raised or string line outlines it for touch. The teacher says: "This maker put the Atlantic Ocean in the middle."

**Map 2, the Pacific Ocean in the middle.** The same land and water, the same pattern, the same thick outline on the same land shape. The teacher says: "This maker put the Pacific Ocean in the middle."

**Art:** large outlines, high contrast, pattern not color alone, no names printed on the maps. The teacher says the ocean names aloud; children are not asked to name them. No projection words, no grid lines, no scale.

**Use on day 23:** lay both maps beside the globe. Trace the outlined land shape with a finger on the globe, then on each map: it did not move on Earth. Ask what is in the middle of each map and what each helps us see together. Never say one is the real map or the wrong map. The words are "the maker chose".

**Use on day 29:** the same pair, a taught pair scored with help (item 2); only the fresh globe and flat map half of that item is scored as independent.

**Facts the maps rest on:** the land shapes (Natural Earth, public domain) and the ocean names Atlantic and Pacific (NOAA, "How many oceans are there?", read 2026-11-19). No other fact is stated.

## Key (teacher)

Both maps show the same Earth: the same land and water, the same outlined land shape. The Atlantic map puts the Atlantic Ocean in the middle and the Pacific map puts the Pacific Ocean in the middle; the maker chose each middle. A map is not "different Earths" (error B).
