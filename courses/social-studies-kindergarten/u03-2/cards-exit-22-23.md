# Exit cards 22 and 23

Two new cards, printed "invented for practice" along the bottom edge. Large outlines, land patterned with dots and water plain, thick lines, no names, no brands. Land shapes are redrawn from the public-domain Natural Earth land data (terms read 2026-11-19). Never used in teaching (rule 4): the taught cases are the ball and flat outline of day 22 and Two World Maps of day 23.

## Card 22: a round Earth and a flat Earth

Say aloud: "This is a pretend card."

**Picture:** two outlines of Earth with Asia in the middle. On the left the outline is drawn wrapped round a ball (shaded so it looks round, one side showing). On the right the same land is drawn flat, edge to edge.

**Mark:** put a token on water on each outline. **Spoken:** "Show water on both. Tell one way these are alike and one way they differ."

## Card 23: the same island twice

**Picture:** two flat world maps of the same Earth. Map 1 has the Indian Ocean in the middle and map 2 has the Americas in the middle. The maker picks one island that sits near the middle of map 1 and near the right edge of map 2, checks it against the land data, and marks it with a small ring on both maps. Same land, same pattern, no names.

**Mark:** circle the island on each map. **Spoken:** "Show the island on each. Did the island move on Earth? What changed?"

## Key (teacher)

Card 22: tokens on plain water on both outlines. Both show Earth's land and water; one is round and the other flat; the round one does not show both sides at once. "The flat one is a different Earth" is error B; "the flat one is just a small place" is error I.

Card 23: the same island on both. No, it did not move on Earth; the map maker chose a different middle. "Yes, it moved" is error B; "the map with the island in the middle is only the island's place" is error I. Give a supported comparison, not projection words.
