# Section 3.2 opener: globes and maps, alike and different

## What the section does

Days 22 and 23 answer "What can maps and globes show?" Day 22 teaches that a globe is a small model of the round Earth and that a map is a flat drawing seen from above, and children find land and water on each. Day 23 sets two world maps, made differently, beside the globe, and the class talks about what each helps someone see. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and nothing is read by children.

## How the days run

Day 22 begins with a look down at a tray of objects and a flat drawing of the same tray: a map is a flat drawing seen from above. Then you turn the globe and find land and water, and find land and water on a flat world map, and say one way they are alike and one way they differ. Pairs put tokens on water on a ball with paper land shapes and on a flat outline. Day 23 sets Two World Maps beside the globe. One map has the Atlantic Ocean in the middle and one has the Pacific Ocean. You say what is in the middle of each, then trace the same large land shape with a finger on the globe and on both maps: it did not move on Earth. Pairs do the same and say what each map helps us see together. You scribe one sentence for each child: "Both ___, but ___."

## The ideas, at an adult's level

A globe is a small model of Earth. It shows land and water the way they sit, but only one side at a time. A flat map shows all of it at once, and that needs a choice: a round thing cannot be laid flat without cutting or stretching it, so the maker decides where the cut and the middle go. Putting a different ocean in the middle changes the page and not the Earth. Say "the maker chose", never "the real map" or "the wrong map". The reason for the choice is for you; children get the objects and the question "what does each help us see together?" No projection words, no scale or distance, no compass directions. You say the ocean names; no child is asked to name continents or oceans. No child is asked where a family comes from.

## The errors, in plain words

- B: "A map and a globe are different Earths." Match the same land outline across the globe and both maps with string or a token.
- I: "A map shows only a small place." Lay a world map beside the classroom picture map and name the different area each shows.

## Supplies

A globe if the school has one, otherwise a ball with paper land shapes taped on. A ball and a flat outline sheet for each pair, tokens, a tray of objects, Two World Maps (day 23), a long piece of string. Land is felt: raised or string outlines, rough paper for land and smooth for water, pattern and not color alone. A child who cannot see traces with your hand over theirs.

## A room with nothing

A ball with paper land shapes taped on, one flat sheet cut from the wrapped paper to show a cut is needed to flatten it, and two hand-drawn maps with different middles.

## Exit tickets

Both are new cases printed "invented for practice" and never used in teaching. Every child marks a picture; the spoken part is heard from a rotating group of six each day.
