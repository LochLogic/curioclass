# A House Is Built (day 36)

An original informational read-aloud about an invented crew and an invented house, read aloud by the teacher, who says it is made up. It describes a simple order in which a pretend crew works; it makes no claim about how every house is built. Four large cards, printed "invented for practice".

## Text

Workers clear the ground. Workers build the walls. Workers put on the roof. Workers paint inside.

## Cards

1. **Clear the ground:** a patch of bare earth and a worker with a shovel.
2. **Build the walls:** four low walls standing on the earth.
3. **Put on the roof:** the same walls with a roof on top.
4. **Paint inside:** one wall seen from inside, with a brush and a can of paint.

## How it is used

The cards go on a second strip, left to right, each tied to the one before it: "What happened just before the roof?" (the walls). A roof needs walls to sit on, and a painted wall must be there to be painted, so this order is a fact of the pretend house itself. Say first, next, last, before and after for two pairs: "The walls come before the roof." Moss shows error H by putting the painting before the walls.

## Key (teacher)

Clear the ground, build the walls, put on the roof, paint inside. The first is clearing; the last is painting.
