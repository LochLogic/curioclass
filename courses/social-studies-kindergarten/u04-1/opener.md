# Section 4.1 opener: putting events in order, and time words

## What the section does

Days 34 to 36 open the unit "Long ago and today", whose question is *Which change from long ago made the biggest difference to everyday work?* Nothing is answered in this section. Day 34 posts the question and the children's wonders, then teaches first, next and last with picture cards and teaches yesterday, today and tomorrow on the class calendar. Day 35 adds now, then, before, after, long ago, and last and next week, month and year, on a picture strip with a today marker. Day 36 reads a strip left to right, names it a timeline, and uses it for an invented story and for an informational read-aloud. Each day is 20 to 30 minutes, and no child reads.

## How the days run

Day 34 begins with two drawings we made, a washboard and a washing machine. Both help wash clothes. Ask once, "What might change about the work?" and write what children offer on the wonder wall; any child may pass, and nobody is asked about washing at home. Then you order the school day cards (morning, afternoon, night; the night card is the school under a moon) and three seed cards, saying "first, next, last" as you place each one. Pairs order a coat set and put a real class card on a small strip of yesterday, today and tomorrow.

Day 35 hands back one repair from day 34, then you move a today marker along the class calendar and a strip. Now is the marker, then is any card behind it, and before and after place any two events. Pairs set four cards of a pretend class, Room 9, on a blank strip and say one sentence each. Day 36 hands back one repair, then you read two short pieces aloud with picture cards: a story of Ria and a ball, and how a house is built. Each card goes on a strip, left to right, and the strip is named a timeline. Children then circle label cards on a dated picture log. It is practice only, and you write one relation to revisit on each log.

## The ideas, at an adult's level

To put events in order is to say what came earlier and what came later. A time word means something only against a reference event: "next week" is after today's marker on the class calendar, and "long ago" is a long time before this year. A bigger, brighter or favorite picture is not an earlier one. A child may say "yesterday" for anything in the past, so anchor each word on the calendar. Left to right is the way our class reads a strip; some home languages run the other way, so say so aloud. Use only school, nature and story events. Nobody reports a birthday, a trip or a chore from home.

## The errors, in plain words

- A: "Pictures can be ordered without following the events." Set a large card against the growth order and tie each event to the one before it.
- B: "Time words do not need a reference event." Point to today's marker, move an event to either side, and set a plan beside a finished event.
- H: "A later event comes before an earlier one." Place two events on the strip, earlier on the left, and say before and after for each pair.

## Supplies

Large movable picture cards, the class calendar with a ridged today marker, paper strips, the label cards, two puppets (Moss and Wren), wonder cards, crayons, and exit tokens. The strip and the cards can be felt: a child who cannot see touches each card and the marker. Large print and high contrast on every card.

## A room with nothing

Hand-drawn cards on scrap paper, a paper strip folded in thirds, and a calendar drawn on the board.

## Exit tickets

Every exit is a new case printed "invented for practice" and never used in teaching. Every child marks a picture; the spoken part is heard from a rotating group of six each day, and the rest are recorded not yet heard.
