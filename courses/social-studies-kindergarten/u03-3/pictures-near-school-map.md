# Picture map of places near school (day 24)

One copy for each pair, with a boxed key and a set of movable symbol cards. It is drawn from Our Place Record photographs of your own school and grounds, so draw what is really there. Pictures only, with at most one word under a picture. No child is in it, and no home.

**The photograph.** One Our Place Record photograph of the playground, taken by the teacher on a dated day, with no identifiable children. The teacher asks: "Who took this photograph? When? Why?" (The teacher took it, on the date written on its back, to remember what the playground looks like.)

**The picture map.** The same place drawn from above on a page with a thick border: the playground, the library and the park (or the three places near your school you name; an entrance or a visited place may stand in for a park). Draw only what is there. Each place is an outlined area with a small symbol in it. Keep the page the same way up on every copy, with a small sun picture at the top edge.

**The key.** A box at the page's edge, one row for each symbol with the symbol on the left and a small picture of the real place on the right: a heart for the playground, a hexagon for the library, a flag for the park. These three are the taught symbols; they are not used on any exit card (rule 4).

**Symbol cards.** The same three symbols as loose cards, each with a sticky back or a magnet strip, plus a plain blank card for the child's own. Raised stickers or string outlines for children who feel the page.

**Use:** pairs place a card, trace from the symbol to the key box and back, and say what is next to it: "The ___ is next to the ___." The teacher says "the heart stands for the playground, as the globe stands for Earth." Model one claim: "The library is next to the playground because the hexagon touches the heart." The rotating group also says where a symbol card is, each using a position word not yet used that day.

**What the map leaves out:** the teacher names two things in the photograph that the map does not show. Every map is a choice by its maker.

**Fit to your school:** the taught symbols stay the same; the places are yours. If your three places have different names, change the words and keep the symbols.
