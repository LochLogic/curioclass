# Exit cards 24, 25 and 26

Four new cards, printed "invented for practice" along the bottom edge. Plain line drawings, thick outlines, no writing except that line and at most a word under a picture, no brands. Never used in teaching (rule 4): the taught cases are the heart, hexagon and flag map (day 24), the shell tray and reading-corner model (day 25) and the unclear-key map (day 26).

## Card 24: the courtyard map

Say aloud: "This is a pretend card."

**Picture:** a courtyard seen from above on a page with a thick border and a sun picture at the top edge. A rectangle (the shed) in the middle. A diamond (the flower bed) right beside it. A circle (the rain barrel) in the far corner. A boxed key along the bottom: diamond means flower bed, rectangle means shed, circle means rain barrel, each with a small picture of the real thing.

**Mark (s03-X24):** point to what is next to the shed. **Spoken (s03-X24b):** "Show me in the key what it is."

## Card 25, first part: a view and a model

**Picture:** the left half is a window view: a tree, a bird feeder and a rock outside a window. The window view puts the tree at the left, the feeder in the middle and the rock at the right. The right half is a photograph of a block model of the same view: a flat card for the window and three plain blocks of the same shape and size in a row, in the same places as the three things. No key card, and no block is shaped like the thing it stands for, so only its place says which is which.

**Mark:** circle the block that stands for the tree (the left block). **Spoken, rotating group:** "How do you know that block is the tree?" The child's own model is the construction evidence.

## Card 25, second part: a wrong graph

**Picture:** four cups on the left. On the right a two-row graph card: row 1 has a cup picture and four marks; row 2 has a spoon picture and one mark.

**Mark:** put a finger on the row that is wrong, and say what is wrong with it.

## Card 26: the drawn map

**Picture:** a page with a thick border and a boxed key showing a small swing picture and a small fence picture. On the map a swing picture sits beside a fence picture, and a triangle sits at the other side.

**Mark:** circle the key picture for the swing and point to the swing on the map. **Spoken, rotating group:** "How could someone find the swing?" The child's own drawn map is the map evidence.
