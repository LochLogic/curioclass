# Graph cards and key cards (day 25)

Cards for the picture graph and the block model. Pictures only, with at most one word under a picture. The class counts its own patch of ground from an Our Place Record photograph, the window or the real patch under the school's supervision policy. None of these cards shows a case used on an exit (rule 4).

## The teacher's graph, from a tray (taught)

A tray holds three shells and no feathers. A two-row card is made on the board: row 1 has a shell picture at its left and one square for each shell; row 2 has a feather picture at its left and no marks. The teacher says: one picture for each, and a labeled place for none. The teacher counts aloud against the marks, adding or removing marks until each counted thing has one. This tray is never used on an exit.

## The children's graph card

A pre-printed card, one for each child: a title space at the top, two rows, each row with a picture at its left (a tree in row 1, a rock in row 2) and a line of empty squares along the row. The row names are pictures. The child puts one mark in a square for each tree and each rock counted, and leaves the rock row empty but labeled if there are none. The first graph is practice and is returned with one line of feedback.

## The teacher's reading-corner model (taught)

A block model of the reading corner: a tall block for the shelf, a flat block for the rug, a small block for the bin. Each block type has a key card, a small picture of the real thing on a card laid beside the block, so "this block stands for the shelf." The children's models use the same idea with their own key cards.

## Key cards for the children's models

One small card for each block type a child uses (hill, tree, rock, path, a classroom object), with a picture of the real thing. Blue paper and cloth or clay stand for a pond or a hill only if one was seen. No pond is invented.

## Feedback card (written that evening)

A small card with one picture and one line, written by the teacher on one feature, position or count for each child: for example, a picture of a tree with the line "Your count shows three trees. Put the third tree beside the rock." The card is read aloud to the pair on day 26 before the revise block.
