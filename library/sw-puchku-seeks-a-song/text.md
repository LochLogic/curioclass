![Picture for page 1](art/01.jpg)

From the window of her classroom, Puchku can see the jamun tree. It stands with its branches spread out, like it wants to give you a hug. Under its shade, even the hottest day feels cool.

---

![Picture for page 2](art/02.jpg)

But the tree is naughty – it drops fruits that leave bright purple splodges on Puchku’s uniform. There are also birds that hide in its leaves and slyly poop so that it goes  
SPLAT!  
on her head.

---

![Picture for page 3](art/03.jpg)

Every day, when it’s time for tiffin, Puchku runs to the jamun tree. Sometimes Dodla runs faster than her and gets there first. Boltu is always last. There’s always a butterfly or a worm or a flower that makes him forget the race.  
Today, Puchku beats Dodla to the jamun tree by three seconds. But someone else is under the jamun tree before them.

---

![Picture for page 4](art/04.jpg)

Who is that?  
Is it a bowl made of branches,  
twigs and leaves?  
And look! There’s someone in the bowl, with two little eyes and one  
little beak.  
“Hello,” says Puchku.

---

![Picture for page 5](art/05.jpg)

“Do you think it’s a baby pterodactyl?” Dodla asks Puchku. He likes the word pterodactyl. The “p” is silent, like a secret.  
“Maybe it’s a baby dragon,” says Puchku.  
“That’s a baby bird, dummies,” says Boltu, finally at the jamun tree.  
“But birds make noises,” says Puchku. “Pigeons coo, crows caw, parakeets chatter, peacocks squawk, kites screech. If this is a bird, why is she quiet?”  
Boltu scratches his head.

---

![Picture for page 6](art/06.jpg)

“You know how we talk, using words?”  
says Boltu.  
Puchku and Dodla nod.  
“Birds use song in just the same way,” says Boltu. “That’s how they talk to each other. So maybe this baby bird doesn’t know her song because she’s too small?”  
Dodla looks worried. “But without a song, the baby bird can’t talk to anyone! She’ll be so alone.”

![Picture for page 16](art/16.jpg)

At the end of tiffin break, the (BPD) 2 has no more brilliant plans.  
“What do we do now?” Boltu asks.  
“We’ll take her to Wildlife Didi,” says Puchku. And so they sadly walk to the staff room, to find the biology teacher in her usual corner.

---

![Picture for page 17](art/17.jpg)

“Puchku, Boltu, Dodla, what happened? You look sad,” asks Wildlife Didi.  
“We found a baby bird, but we couldn’t find the baby bird’s song,”  
says Puchku in a sad voice.  
“We looked everywhere!” says Dodla  
in a sadder voice.  
Boltu sniffs even more sadly.  
“It’s okay. You won’t always get it right.” Wildlife Didi gives Boltu a hanky.  
“But the baby bird still doesn’t have  
a song!” Puchku wails.

---

![Picture for page 18](art/18.jpg)

“But she has you, right? You won’t leave her because she doesn’t have a song like the other birds?” asks Wildlife Didi.  
Puchku, Boltu and Dodla shake  
their heads.  
“You just have to be patient,”  
Wildlife Didi tells Puchku with a smile.  
Puchku picks up the baby bird carefully and says, “Baby bird, you just keep your ears open. Any sound you like, you make it your song. And I’ll sing it with you. Okay?”
