![Picture for page 1](art/01.jpg)

In a village on  
the slopes of Mount Kenya in East Africa,  
a little girl worked in the fields with her mother.  
Her name was Wangari.

---

![Picture for page 2](art/02.jpg)

---

![Picture for page 3](art/03.jpg)

Wangari loved  
being outside.  
In her family’s food garden she  
broke up the soil with her  
machete.  
She pressed tiny seeds  
into the warm earth.

---

![Picture for page 4](art/04.jpg)

Her favourite  
time of day was just after sunset.  
When it got too dark to see the plants,  
Wangari knew it was time to go home.  
She would follow  
the narrow paths  
through the fields, crossing rivers as she went.

---

![Picture for page 5](art/05.jpg)

Wangari was a  
clever child and couldn’t wait  
to go to school. But her mother and father  
wanted her to stay and help them at home.  
When she was  
seven years old, her big brother  
persuaded her parents to let her go to school.

---

![Picture for page 6](art/06.jpg)

She liked to  
learn!  
Wangari learnt  
more and more with every book she read.  
She did so well  
at school that she was invited to study in the United States of America.  
Wangari was  
excited! She wanted to know more about the world.

---

![Picture for page 7](art/07.jpg)

At the American  
university Wangari learnt many new things. She studied plants and how they  
grow.  
And she remembered how she grew: playing games with her brothers in the  
shade of the trees in the beautiful Kenyan forests.

---

![Picture for page 8](art/08.jpg)

The more she  
learnt, the more she realised  
that she loved the people of Kenya.  
She wanted  
them to be happy and free.  
The more she  
learnt, the more she  
remembered her African home.

---

![Picture for page 9](art/09.jpg)

When she had  
finished her studies,  
she returned to Kenya.  
But her country had changed.  
Huge  
farms stretched across the land.

---

![Picture for page 10](art/10.jpg)

Women had no wood  
to make cooking fires.  
The people were poor and the children were hungry.

---

![Picture for page 11](art/11.jpg)

Wangari knew what  
to do. She taught the women how to plant trees from seeds.  
The women sold  
the trees and used the money to look after their families.

---

![Picture for page 12](art/12.jpg)

The women were  
very happy.  
Wangari had helped them to feel powerful and strong.

---

![Picture for page 13](art/13.jpg)

As time passed,  
the new trees grew into forests,  
and the rivers started flowing again.  
Wangari’s message spread across Africa.

---

![Picture for page 14](art/14.jpg)

Today, millions  
of trees  
have grown from Wangari’s seeds.

---

![Picture for page 15](art/15.jpg)

Wangari had  
worked hard. People all over the world took notice, and gave her a famous  
prize.  
It is called the Nobel Peace Prize, and she was the first African woman  
ever to receive it.

---

![Picture for page 16](art/16.jpg)

Wangari died in  
2011,  
but we can think  
of her every time we  
see a beautiful tree.
