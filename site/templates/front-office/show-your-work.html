The approach

Top down, one layer at a time.

Most curricula are written lesson by lesson and stitched together. Curio Class is written the other way: the whole K to 12 path first, then each course, then each unit, and only then the days.

Every layer is finished and checked before the next one starts, and each is written from the one above it. That way a day can always be traced back to the unit it serves, the course that unit belongs to, and the skill and standard behind it. When a layer changes, everything that rests on it is checked again.

Earlier drafts are kept out of sight while a layer is written, so old decisions cannot quietly steer new work. Differences between the new layer and older material are then settled one by one, each against a named source.

The layers

From skills to days.

  1. Skill ladders

    Every skill from kindergarten to grade 12, in teaching order, with the earlier skills each one assumes and the links between subjects.

    Checked against the state and national standards

  2. Course frames

    Each course's shape: its units in order, the question each unit asks, the standards each unit owns, and how many days each gets.

    Checked against the skill ladders

  3. Scope and sequence

    The school year laid out day by day: what each day teaches, what kind of day it is, and where students show what they know.

    Checked against the course frame

  4. Unit plans

    For each unit: the evidence students produce, the sources they read, the common misunderstandings to watch for and how to answer them.

    Checked against the scope and sequence

  5. Sections

    Each unit broken into short runs of days that share one goal.

    Checked against the unit plan

  6. Days

    The lesson itself: what the teacher does, what students do, the materials, the exit check and a page for families.

    Checked against the section and unit plans
The checks

What every layer has to pass.

Checks come in two kinds. Rules that can be counted are checked automatically, every time anything changes. Questions of judgement get separate reads, each with one job, and every finding is either fixed or answered with a reason.

✓

Standards

Every standard a course claims is taught, practiced and checked somewhere, and nothing is taught before what it builds on.

☰

Sequence

Units and days come in an order that makes sense, with no gaps, repeats or jumps between grades.

✎

Assessment

Students show what they know in ways that match what was taught, and every check has a clear answer key.

🔗

Sources

Every fact is traced to a named source, and every text is checked for its edition and rights.

⚖

Sensitivity

Topics about real communities are checked against that community's own published guidance.

⚠

Safety

Every hands-on activity is checked against named safety guides for the grade.

The process

Make, check, fix, then move on.

Every layer goes through the same five steps, in order, before the next layer starts.

  1. Make

    The layer is written from the one above it, following fixed instructions.

  2. Check

    Counted rules run first, then separate reads, each with one job.

  3. Fix

    Each problem is fixed at the layer where it began.

  4. Improve

    What the checks found goes into the instructions for the next layer.

  5. Close

    The layer closes only when every check passes.

After the last fix, every check runs again.

Fixed where it began.

Every problem is recorded with two places: where it was found, and where it began. A problem found in a lesson is often caused higher up. If a unit plan set up a practice task that gives away the exit check, the fix goes into the unit plan, and every other unit plan is checked for the same problem before more lessons are written.

  1. FixThe lesson and its plans are corrected. The practice changes, never the check.
  2. SweepEvery other plan at that layer is checked for the same problem.
  3. ImproveThe instructions that let it through gain a new check.

One grade at a time.

The planning layers cover every grade first. Then lessons are written one grade at a time: a grade's section plans, then its days, then a review of the whole grade before the next grade begins. A lesson only builds on lessons that are already written, so nothing claims something that is not there yet. In each subject's first grade, the first unit goes all the way to finished lessons before the rest is planned in detail.

Where things stand

Honest status.

The Front Office shows exactly which layers are done for every course. To be plain about the rest:

What has not happened yet

  • No part of the curriculum has been taught with students yet.
  • No working classroom teacher has reviewed it yet.
  • Most days are planned but not yet written.

Nothing here claims review or endorsement by any outside organization.

Base texts

The open texts behind the courses.

Where an openly licensed curriculum or textbook does a course well, Curio Class adapts it rather than starting over. Each adapted day names its source in a credit line. Social studies has no base text: it is written from primary sources and openly available records.
